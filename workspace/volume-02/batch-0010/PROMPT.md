The trust's account for the absence, itemised in pence and footed before a word of it goes on the page:** eleven markets lost or held late and thin, by custom, **29 marks 19 shillings 6 pence** (1,626d) · tolls not taken on the shut wharf road, one hundred and fourteen days, **3 marks 19 shillings** (372d) · three Tuesday barges held off, **4 marks 2 shillings** (216d) · cartage, stone, lime, oak and nails for the scour repair, **2 marks 9 shillings** (204d) · **a line with no figure against it at all — the scour at the third pier, nose, uninspected from the ninth of Embermonth, and not known by anybody in this office to be a cause eleven weeks out.** **Total: fifty marks, one shilling and sixpence — 2,418 pence.**

**And beside it, in a different book and never in the same sentence:** eleven marks and fourpence owed by a trust in abeyance, **two marks and three quarters** at eight pence a day on the cutting contract, and **twelve marks and ten pence** — sixteen weeks and two days of a copying post at three shillings a week, unclaimed, because he was not in the county to initial the returns, **and the post does not wait, and the county's surveyor took a man on in Thawmonth, and the post is a cage, and a cage is a place a man is sent for and also a place a man is sent away from, and he wrote that difference out in four lines in Fallowmonth and did not see the second half of it coming.**

**The scour under the nose of the third pier is a washtub and has been since the sixth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read the scour, and that is the line with no figure against it, and this chapter is where somebody does.**

**And the volume's Tovan beat, in a yard, in his own words, spent here and not before:** his brother has the six or seven things of an ordinary morning and cannot do them in that order and does not know the order, **and Tovan will not re-teach him his own brother, and says so, and it is not cruelty and it is the estrangement, and nobody in the yard has the word brother in a scene.** Eleven months ago he said a sentence in a gateway so that on the day he says nothing about it, his brother would not think it was his brother. **He has now said something, and it is not the other thing, and that is the damage and it is permanent in this volume.**

### Chapter 0098 — the twenty-sixth of Greenmonth, a Friday · the settlement, in the county's own book

**Goal.** The list is not burned. It is superseded, and the magistrate gives his own testimony.

**The county's own bench withdraws the magistrate's instrument in a room, with the physician's four-year-old finding read out and the three hands named.** The superseding is **entered as a fact and not as a defeat, because the magistrate says in the room that the list was the only instrument he had and that he is glad to be shown a second one and sorry it took four years and a stranger, and that is the whole of his testimony and it is in the county's own book.** Ash's dead stay Ash's. **Two hundred and fifty-four fired tiles are being re-ground and the clay wedged back, at a cost footed on a board, on a schedule, in the only season a county can afford, and about thirteen men are still out of work and the number is written down.** The softening has slowed and not stopped.

**And the thing that is not settled is the eighth ruled line, which is still empty, and which Mara Vey looks at for the second time in a year, and which she does not fill, and which is now the oldest document in the case and the one nobody has asked her about.** The brine weights are still cited and not filed, **the two day-book lines about them are a year apart and are read together, and the reason is written out again in full because the reason has changed: the roll is a ledger, and a ledger is a claim, and this office has learned this year from a county four hundred miles off that a claim about people is not a record of them.**

**Three hands, named, and the third is still a letter:** the rollkeeper's office at the assizes, the hand of the Ninebridge, and — in Chapter 99 — a creditor's press entered as a transaction at a farthing of salt. **No single office can add or remove a name, a person restored is restored when two of the three agree, a person removed is removed when two of the three agree otherwise, and the third can be wrong.** Nobody in this volume resolves the third hand.

### Chapter 0099 — the twenty-seventh of Greenmonth, a Saturday · the holding, and a name out of a copy

**Goal.** A roll of persons kept where nobody can take it out of the book, and one frightened signature read out in a fen.

**The holding is made in the country where the register is.** A creditor's press in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists takes the roll of a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths **as a transaction at a farthing of salt, entered as a transaction, with a date and an hour, in a fair copy, and the man who buys it is the salt merchant's brother in Harvestmonth and is named by trade and is not a villain.** The argument for it is minuted and the minute is put where a Crown's man will find it in about nine years.

**And in the same book, on the same leaf, and not in the same sentence as anything else: a name in a county four hundred miles off, entered in a creditor's fair copy and read in Merefen on the twenty-seventh** — **the person who signs the four-hundred-leaf register in a frightened hand twenty years after his grandmother did.** He is a name in a copy. **No want is stated for him, because the book does not have a column for it, and the absence of a column is the point, and the chapter says so in about four sentences and does not sentimentalise him.**

**The disposition schedule's county column opens halfway and is left.** Eleven other counties, eight of them with a boundary oath on a stone, and **the pattern is an arithmetic pattern and not a revelation, and it can be seen by exactly two people, a registrar who sold the book and a keeper who has a copy of a page of it, and neither of them can say what an oath has to do with a severance.** That is the next volume's question and it is stated here as a column and not as a revelation.

**The tile on the coping is not touched in this chapter and is not explained in this volume.**

### Chapter 0100 — the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth, YR 313, a Monday, a market day, the fourth hour, the lowest water of the year · the last image

**Goal.** The volume ends with a list that can be checked, a bell rung by three pairs of hands that are not his, and a man four hundred miles off who is alive.

**A scheduled message comes down the line from the County of Ash, on a named day, in a fair hand, and it is a list, and a list can only be checked.** It is the county's answer to term four: a struck person answered, and the person is restored, and the roll is in three hands. It carries **the two hundred and fifty-four tiles going back into clay at a cost and on a schedule, and a woman in the county who has written, in a hand that is not a clerk's, that she does not know whether that is a good idea and that she is going to do it anyway and would like somebody to write down that she said so.** The 2,906 is the county's own figure about itself. **The 456 does not appear in this chapter in any form.**

**On the near abutment there is a boy who is sixteen and has been the steward's apprentice for two years and is still not good at it, holding one end of a line, and beside him a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper, and at the far end of the line a tile-cutter of forty-one and a person nobody in Merefen has ever seen — and they ring the bell because that is the arrangement, and it is not Aren's.** The bell is on the Old Toll bridge with a band of forty-one rivets and a dull red seam in it. **He does not ring it.**

**A road warden of the Merefen bridge guild is on the Sedge Reach deck with a tool bag, counting load points out of habit, and his left palm is warm, and he does not need it, and nothing in the sound is his.** The scour is a washtub and has been since the sixth of Mudmonth and the bill is entered and the line with no figure against it now has a figure against it, **and the county has paid a man to look at it, which is the smallest thing in the volume and the only one that anybody in the book has been glad of.**

**And four hundred miles off, in a room above a kiln, a man of fifty-eight who fired the tiles in that kiln last spring does not know which kiln he fires, and his name is in three books, and the books are right about him, and no instrument in two countries will do anything about it, and the woman who reads for a living has written the whole of it down with the hour on it, against nobody, in a day-book over a weigh-house.**

**The last beat, and it is a constraint and not a licence.** Chapter 90 already spent this: a keeper of records read a four-hundred-word list in a fair hand, found nothing to argue with and one thing she could not put down, and wrote out the reason she is not going to ask, **and that reason is on the page and it is not a sentence about the man.** **Chapter 100 may not restate that reason as a speech, a paragraph of narration, or a found entry, and may not restate it in different words.** What is new is the entry: **the same reason, already written in Fallowmonth, entered a second time against nobody with the hour on it, in a day-book, in a hand that has stopped asking, and the volume ends on the entry and not on the reason.** The last line of the volume is not a sentence about the man.
