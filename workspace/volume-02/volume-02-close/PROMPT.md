## What this phase is for

## The seven-block audit — the spine of this close

**Volume 02 is five batches of ten chapters and the close must audit it as five blocks, and the audit must be against the outline's cards, not against the outline's intentions.** For each block, write: what the card promised, what the page delivered, what the deviation was, and whether the deviation improved or damaged the book. **Twenty-eight of the deviations were recorded at the time in the five batch entries — seven for Chapters 51–60, eleven for 71–80, twelve for 91–100 — and every one of them is in those files, not in memory, and your job is to decide which of them became canon and which of them should be corrected in the prose or carried forward as a fact about the plan.** A close that lists the deviations is not auditing; a close that says *this one is now the shape of the book and Volume 03 is written against it* is.

The five blocks, in order: **Block 1, Chapters 51–60, the form and the registrar** (the Stage 2 terms printed in an office's hand with the three-week figure in them; Halven's shield and the ledger; the second severance's index; the count that is eleven and not four; the refused crossing and the going). **Block 2, Chapters 61–70, Twenty-Three Days** (the stock count and the four hundred and fifty-six; the law; the physician's four pages; the five terms; the first fever; the return). **Block 3, Chapters 71–80, Ordinary work in Merefen** (the yard; the copying post; the bench; the election; the count answered; the crossing out). **Block 4, Chapters 81–90, The Nine Months** (the rite; the arrest in a private book; the softening; the coping; the child; the room that says it has no word; the road that did not open). **Block 5, Chapters 91–100, Eighty-Eight Days, and a Roll in Three Hands** (the return and the numbers; the breach; the second count; Dain; the second severance closing in the wrong country; the disclosure; the bill; the settlement; the holding; the last image).

## Hard requirements for the close

- **Do not write chapter prose.** No scene, no dialogue, no narrative. If you find yourself writing a sentence somebody would read aloud, put it in quotation marks inside a *document* the outline says exists, and stop.
- **Do not move the ending.** `outline/ending.md` is fixed. Every Volume 03 plan must be checkable against it.

- **Do not repair the breach.** Not in a scene, not in an aside, not in a document, and not by having either of them apologise.
- **Do not total the four thousand with the seven hundred and twenty, anywhere.** Four thousand is the Crown's figure; the villages are seven hundred and twenty by name, in the county's spelling, with `sum not stated` in the total line, and an eighth ruled line that is the city's and is still empty. Hushwater Reach in the path is near sixty and Hushwater in the main world is eighty, and the two are never printed together and no character notices that the names match.
- **Do not scale the 456.** It is a floor of a surplus, it has never been divided, multiplied, worked back from or entered on any slate, and the number being ground back into clay is **254** — arrived at by turning tiles over and looking for two with the same hand on the back of them, out of two kilns, one village and about half a wall of a third, and it is a floor the man with the ledger will not go past.
- **Do not put the private-keeper clause in anybody's mouth in an explanatory sentence.** It may be printed in an office's hand. Volume 03 is where he learns what it is being read as, and that is a plant, not an explanation.
- **Do not name the device on the tile on the coping.** It is the oldest object in the book after the bell and it stays that way.
- **A volume close that praises the volume is a bad volume close.** The audit has to find things, the "did badly" paragraph has to be specific, and **the two hardest things for this close in particular are that Volume 02's ending does not resolve the relationship the book is about, and that a volume which is a book about a records office may have been a book about forms.** Both are findings and both belong in the document.
