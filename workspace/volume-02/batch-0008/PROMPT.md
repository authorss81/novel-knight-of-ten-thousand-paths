**(b) The kilns close.** Two of them, in Thawmonth, because there is no wood and no money, and a county that has lost its employer has a choice between a wage and a list, and about forty men find out which. **A struck man and an employed man and a man whose kiln has shut are three different people and the room knows it and does not say so.**

**(c) The three hands** — the rollkeeper's office at the assizes, **the hand of the Ninebridge**, and **a fourth place that does not exist yet and has to be built out of a letter and an old press-mark: a creditor's office.** **Alis Merrow's counting house can hold a roll of persons in its press for a farthing of salt, because it held a chain of eleven sheets for a creditor who did not want them.** He is a man in another world and the only person who can do it, **and the price is the eleven sheets and a letter of conditions, and the fact that a roll of persons in a creditor's press is a thing that can be produced in a court in a country that does not exist — and Merrow's whole life's argument is that this is the worst possible thing, and he is going to do it anyway, and he will say so in a hall, and he will ask that the argument be minuted and the minute put where a Crown's man will find it in about nine years.**

**And the last beat of the three is the one Chapter 81 is about, and this block must plant it without spending it:** a working at the scale of two hundred people needs an anchor, a bound, a counterweight and a witness, and a bound is a promise. **He wrote the mistake down in a notebook on the nineteenth day of twenty-three, in the correct technical vocabulary, in tons, four months before he made it, and he did not act on it. The reader knows. Nobody in Ash knows. A later writer may not have him notice that he was right until Chapter 84, and Chapter 84's cost is that the remedy is his and the damage is his and the method was correct and it is still him deciding for a room.**
