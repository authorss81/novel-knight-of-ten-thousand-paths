**And the thing that is actually new is the four hundred and fifty-six: the two thousand nine hundred and six tiles on the roll, the ones that were fired twice, and the discovery that a fired tile can be *re-ground* — that a county can take a claim about a person and turn it back into clay, and that this takes a kiln, a cord of wood, and a decision.** **Ash begins to un-fire its own founding act, at a cost, on a schedule, with a bench, and it is the slowest and least dramatic and most permanent thing in the book, and it is the same motion a man out of Merefen carried out of a third slip of a ferry yard ten months ago and put down on a clamp in a filing room, and neither of them says so to the other.** Two hundred and fifty-four tiles are re-ground in Hearthmonth, in a county whose calendar has no Hearthmonth, so the writer must render the season in the county's own frame.

### Chapter 0090 — the ninth month and the last day of it · the climax, and it is a form

**Goal.** A form, a list, a stage that comes in a filing room, and a route that does not answer.

**The Second Door's terms are on the printed instruction in front of him in a filing room**, and the fourth of them — **one message, and it must be a list, and one object under a pound** — is met by Ash with a **scheduled list**, and the list is the county's own answer to term four, and it is four hundred words, **and it is not a message, it is a bill, and it is itemised, and a clerk in Merefen can check every line of it.** **The stage comes. It comes the way Stage 1 came: not as a reward, in a room, with a witness, and the room is a filing room and the person who witnesses it is the only other person in the county who can be asked.**

**And the cost, stated in a fair hand in a day-book with the hour on it: the second route does not answer when it is called.** The volume ends with a route that will not answer and a man four hundred miles away with no door. **No melody cost is added. The two carryovers of which he cannot tell which path gave him which are on the page, and the loss of the order of an ordinary morning is not this chapter's — that is Chapter 91's — and nothing in this block may spend it.**

**And the last beat: a list of restorations coming down a line in a fair hand, itemised, checkable, and a keeper of records reading every line of it and finding nothing to argue with and one thing she cannot put down — and she does not ask him about it, because she wrote in Fallowmonth that this office is not going to ask him again, and the reason is on the page and the reason has changed, and she is choosing the wound.** **The last line of the block is the reason she wrote it down, and the reason is not a sentence about the man.**
