## The thing this block is for

**Twenty-three days in a clay county that counts fires and has never counted itself, and a book that opens on a subtraction done out loud in a kiln shed.**

The engine is **not magic and not a bridge**. Nothing fails structurally in this block. No arch goes down, no glass breaks, nothing is forged that was not already true. What happens is that a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths and **two thousand four hundred and fifty people** has fired **two thousand nine hundred and six tiles** on its roll of persons, **because a county counts fires and does not count itself**, and a man with a ledger and a thumb black to the second joint says the second number out loud, and then says the difference — **four hundred and fifty-six** — and stops, and does not say what it is, and a room of nine people stops what it is doing.

**The subtraction must be printable and checkable by the reader in Chapter 61: 2,906 − 2,450 = 456.** The population is exact and is printed exactly, because an approximate population cannot be subtracted from anything. **The four hundred and fifty-six is a floor of a surplus, not a best estimate, and no figure anywhere in this volume may be got from it by scaling it.**

**The moral argument of the whole volume must not be made in this block. It must be made by a physician, in a drawer, four years old, and out loud in a room, and it is a medical finding and not a moral sentence: the softening travels from a mark to the person who reads it, and from a person to the marks of that person's household, and fastest along the marks that are read most, and the marks read most are the roll marks, because a clerk reads the roll every morning. The cure is therefore not fewer records. The cure is records that do not agree with each other.** A magistrate who is a decent man is going to be right about a fever, and nobody in this block may be able to say he is wrong about it, and the volume does not resolve this block — it asks for a week.

**Aren is not the mechanism here either, and this block is where that is tested in the hardest possible place.** He is a sett-cutter from a fen county in a county where roads are edged in fired tile, and the single craft this block needs from him is **re-pressing a soft tile**, which is a thing a child does and which takes four days to learn and which is, in the trade's own vocabulary, **the motion of a man setting a thing true against a thing out of true** — a carryover from Chapter 6 and Chapter 10 that comes home here, and **he does not get stronger; he gets a second use for the same hand, which is the whole of Stage 2.** He must not be given a revelation, a system message, a stage, a gate, or a right to speak for the county. **He is refused by the woman who is the hand of the Ninebridge in Chapter 59 and refused again by a room in Chapter 67, and both refusals are correct.**

**The three days' fever and the way of standing are not this block's subject. They are Chapter 70's, and Chapter 70 is the near end.** The block ends with him going, and it does not know what he will cost.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0061 — Ash, the first day · Aren

**Goal.** Let the county introduce itself by refusing to introduce itself, and get the volume's first arithmetic on the page.

**The chapter is the stock count, and the count is the chapter.** He is brought — walked, not driven, with the woman who is the hand of the Ninebridge and who still has not given him her name — into a kiln shed at Kilnholt, and the county's introduction to him is a stock count being done in an ordinary way. **Nine hundred and forty-one hearths on the roll. Two thousand four hundred and fifty people. Two thousand nine hundred and six tiles fired on the roll to date.** The tiles are fired in fours and **nine hundred and forty-one does not divide into anything.** The kiln clerk says the county has always counted fires and has never counted itself, and then says the people number out loud — **because he has counted it for the first time in nineteen years, in a room, because somebody asked** — and then says **four hundred and fifty-six**, and stops, and does not say what it is, and does not go on.

**Aren works it out in a minute and a half and says it out loud, and a room of nine people stops what it is doing.** He is not doing magic and nobody in the room thinks he is: he is a man who can add, and he adds out loud, and the room stops because nobody had said the number. **The chapter must make the reader able to do the subtraction themselves and must not explain what the number means for another forty pages.**

The room is a room of **the struck**. One of them is a man of fifty-eight who has been struck eleven months, and who says the number is right and that he knows what it is, and that it is his mother, and that she has been fired twice, and that the second time was in the winter, and that she was not asked.

**Resistances:** the kiln clerk's contempt for a sermon, stated as *I count and I do not preach*; the struck man's dignity, which is not asking to be rescued; and Aren's own instinct, which is to look for a load path and there is nothing here to look at.

**Ends** on the number in a room where nobody is speaking, and on the fact that a county has asked a stranger nothing and has told a stranger everything, which is the shape of the whole volume.

### Chapter 0062 — Ash, the second to the fourth day · Aren

**Goal.** The Roll of Persons at the assizes, Pernel Quist, and the law — shown as a working system, not as a horror.

**On the page, in the county's own terms:** a tile to a person. A pressed device, a name, a hearth, a trade, and a hand — **the last of them pressed by the person themselves in wet clay, so that the mark is theirs and not the office's.** A person is on the roll if two others witness them and their tile agrees with the clerk's entry. **A person is struck from the count, not from the roll**, and a struck person is alive and employed and **is not consulted**: cannot witness, cannot be witnessed, cannot be married into a hearth, cannot be buried in the county's ground, cannot be given a name at a kiln. Nobody in the county can state the difference between the two things.

**Pernel Quist, 44, rollkeeper, nineteen years in the job**, wants one roll and that it be right, and says out loud that two rolls is an admission the first one can be wrong, and she has nineteen years of evidence that nothing she wrote ever was. **She is hiding nothing.** She has a physician's finding from four years back saying her system is the vector, **and she has the finding in a drawer and has read it and has concluded that a physician's finding about reading is not a finding about a roll** — reasoning correctly, and wrong, and **the chapter must let both of those be visible from inside her head, without a narrator stepping in to say so.**

**Resistance:** the tile as a record that cannot be argued with, defended by the person who maintains it, in good faith, with the best argument available.

### Chapter 0063 — Ash, the fifth day · Aren

**Goal.** The magistrate, the list, and the first time anybody in this book asks Aren for his opinion instead of a service.

**Magistrate Colm Arreld, 54, of the assizes**, in a room with a stove and a clerk. He is not a villain and does not use a word like one. He wants the softening to stop before the spring, because the spring is when the clay is dug and the rolling is heaviest and the last two winters have been the worst. **The instrument is the List of Persons to be Struck: about two hundred names, eleven months old, and the offer is to burn it in the market square at the assizes in front of witnesses, because a struck person cannot be read and a person who cannot be read cannot carry the softening.** The county's own law provides for it and the clerk has the law open on the table and it is four lines and it is signed by three magistrates.

**Aren is asked what he thinks, by a decent man who wants his opinion.** That is the first time in this book anybody has asked him for advice rather than a service, and the moment the volume turns on. What he says is: *I would want to know what happens to the tile.* And the magistrate says: *the tile is fired, and then there is no name on it, and then there is a hearth with a fire in it and no reason anybody can give for the fire, and that is not a worse thing than a name that cannot be read.*

**And Aren, who has spent five months being the best reader of a room in a book about not doing that, finds that he has nothing — and the reason he has nothing is that the magistrate is right about the fever.** That is the moment the volume stops being about a man learning a lesson and becomes about a method that works.

**Ends** on the four lines of county law with three signatures on them, and on a man who has been asked a question and has discovered that his usefulness is the reason he is being asked.

### Chapter 0064–0065 — Ash, the sixth to the tenth day · Aren and Ilsa Soke

**Goal.** The hand of the Ninebridge, the road that is a record, and the single craft the volume needs from him.

**Ilsa Soke, 41, tile-cutter, the hand of the Ninebridge**, who keeps nine spans, four hundred yards of tiled kerb, and a road that is a record, and who is **the person who answered his question in Chapter 59** — and who is not a steward, because the word here is *the hand*. **Her want, in her own voice: that a road in this county is the only thing in it that is not an argument**, because a road is tiled and a tiling is a record and a record does not have an opinion, and she has spent nine years mending a road that is being cut up by people who will not say which half they want.

She gives him work on the terms that **he is useful and not a visitor.** The work is **re-pressing a soft tile** — a thing a child does, which he is too big for, which takes four days to learn, and which is the single craft this block needs from him. **A Bind does not travel and a motion does.** The motion he carried back from Bellweather in Chapter 10 — a hand setting a thing true against a thing out of true — **is the motion of a man re-pressing a soft tile, and it is on the right side of the world for the first time, and it belongs to a county and not to a city, and nobody in either world has taught him anything.**

**She does not give him her name in this block.** It is on a paper in a gateway in Merefen four hundred miles off and he has it in a notebook and he has never met the woman it belongs to. **If he uses her name in this block, Chapter 60's refusal is retroactively cheap and the chapter loses the only thing it has.** He may call her *the hand*, and she may not correct him.

**Resistance:** the four days of learning a thing a child does, and the county's suspicion of a stranger who is good at it in one hour.

### Chapter 0066 — Ash, the eleventh day · Aren

**Goal.** The struck, and the argument nobody in the county has been able to make.

**About two hundred people whose want is not money but to be asked.** **Ostry Vaile, 58, kiln-worker, struck eleven months**, who has said in a room that he would rather be left alone and uncopied — the anti-Archive position, and the most defensible sentence anybody says in the volume — and who then says the sentence the book needs: **a person who is struck has to be able to answer for himself, and if he answers he goes back on it, and if he cannot answer then he was never on it and we have been keeping a list of things.** He is right. He has been right for eleven months. **Nobody in the county has ever put that sentence in a form, and the reason is not authority: a form requires a column, and the column is *struck*, and there is no column in the world for *answered*.**

Also on the page, and both of them are load-bearing: **the four hundred and fifty-six**, and **the woman fired twice in the winter without being asked, and the second time, in the winter, by her own household, because a soft tile can be re-pressed by anybody with a thumb and a wet cloth.**

**Ends** on the missing column, which is a blank nobody has noticed, and which is the same shape as the eighth ruled line in a served schedule in Merefen four hundred miles away, **and nobody in this block may say so and nobody in this block knows it.**

### Chapter 0067–0068 — Ash, the twelfth to the eighteenth day · Aren and Hesper Lound

**Goal.** The physician's finding, four years old and in a drawer, and the first refusal in the volume that is Aren refusing to give advice he is asked for.

**Hesper Lound, 63, the county's one physician.** **Softening travels from a mark to the person who reads it, and from a person to the marks of that person's household, and it travels fastest along the marks that are read most, and the marks that are read most are the roll marks, because a clerk reads the roll every morning.** The cure is not fewer records. **The cure is records that do not agree with each other**: three copies, in three hands, in three places, none authoritative alone, a person restored when two of the three agree and removed when two of the three agree otherwise, and no single office able to do either. **It has been in a drawer for four years. It is a medical finding, not a moral argument.**

Lound's want, in his own voice: **that what he wrote four years ago be read in a room with a table**, and his complaint is that the only thing wrong with the county is that nobody has ever read a physician's finding out loud.

**Aren does not solve it in this block and is not permitted to.** What he does is refuse the burning of the list — and **refusing the list is wrong, and he knows it is wrong**, and the chapter makes him say the sentence the rest of the volume is built on: *if I tell this room not to burn that list, I am telling a county with a fever in it to keep a list, on my word, and I am the man who put the list there by arguing about it in front of witnesses, and I have been doing this for eleven years and I am not going to do it again in a hat.* **He asks for a week.**

**Asking for a week is the most expensive thing he does in the volume, and the first time in the book that he asks a community for time instead of taking it.** Two chapters because the finding has to be read out loud, argued, misunderstood by somebody who is clever, and misunderstood again by somebody who is kind, before the week can be asked for.

### Chapter 0069 — Ash, the nineteenth day · Aren and Ilsa Soke

**Goal.** Five terms, in the county's own hand, and a notebook entry in which a man writes down the mistake he is about to make, in the correct technical vocabulary, four months before he makes it.

**The terms are on a tile and in a book, because a tile cannot be copied out of and a book can** — and the county has understood something in four years that Mara Vey has understood for four.

1. The road is not to be made to carry a person.
2. No name is to be read out on the far side of the line by anybody from the north, and no name is to be written in the line's book at the north end.
3. The north is not to send anybody who can be asked to come and look.
4. **A person who is struck may answer, and if a person who is struck answers, the person is restored.**
5. The road is kept by a hand at each end and a witness at each end, and the witness at Ash's end is chosen by Ash.

**Term four is the whole volume and term four is what kills the road.** The mechanism is on the page and it is not magic: restoring a struck person requires the roll to be re-read; re-reading a roll is a working on a scale of two hundred; and a working at that scale needs an anchor, a bound, a counterweight and a witness, **and a bound is a promise, and a promise about two hundred people to one office is a shape the Gatehouse has refused before, and the shape it refuses is the shape a severance writ has.** He does not know that. He is a sett-cutter and he can see a load path, and he is about to put four hundred tons into a bed of river gravel.

**The volume says so, in his own trade's words, in a notebook, four months early, and he does not act on it. That is Chapter 69's whole content and it is the volume's most damning page: a man writing down the mistake in the correct technical vocabulary four months before he makes it, because he has written the mistake down before.**

**Ends** on the tile with five lines on it and a book with the same five lines in it, and on the fact that a county has written a road's terms in a hand nobody sent.

### Chapter 0070 — the fifth of Wolfmonth, a Tuesday · Mara, and the return

**POV Mara**, and the near end, and the block's close. He comes up at the first light of the fifth with a fever of three days — running the fifth, sixth and seventh, breaking on the eighth — and **with a way of standing that is a ferrywright's on a slip and that he cannot source.** The second answer is on the desk in Mara's fair hand with the hour on it. **The third thing he cannot tell her is the one that matters, and the block ends with the fever, the silence about the way he stands, and a page in the day-book in which she writes down that she has noticed and that she is not going to ask, because she wrote in Fallowmonth that this office is not going to ask him again.**

**Her reason, written under it, is the sentence the rest of the volume is built on: the not asking has changed from a protection into a thing she is doing to him, and the difference is that a protection is entered in a file and this is entered in a day-book, and she has noticed the difference, and she has noticed it late.**

Also on the page, and small: **Toban's yard, the four weeks and a day reduced by twenty-six days; the Sedge Reach scour, still unread, and a bench that has now waited eleven days for a third number; and the three Fridays of the copying post, which the county's surveyor has stopped holding.** **Do not resolve the copying post here — it is Chapter 71–77's cost and the bill's.**

**Ends** on the day-book entry, which is the last line of the block, and on the reader knowing that the not asking is now a wound and that nobody in this book is going to open it before Chapter 92.
