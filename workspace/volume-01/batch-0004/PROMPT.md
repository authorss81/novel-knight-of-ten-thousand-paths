## The thing this block is for

**Block 4 is the longest quiet stretch in the volume and there is no crossing in it. Say so in the prompt to yourself before you draft, and do not let the next batch open a door.** Between the sixteenth of Greenmonth and the twenty-second of Longlight, **thirteen weeks**, not one document crosses in either direction, not one person crosses, and not one thing arrives. No Gatehouse line, no panel, no system display, no warmth in the palm, no seam, no voice behind the eyes. **Volume 01's two-line Gatehouse budget was spent in Batch 0001 on the ringing and the return, and there is no third.**

The book's promise at its middle is that the other side is a society that has to put up with him. This block proves the second half of the promise: that **he is not the mechanism.** A city four days off goes on running its own business without him and is legible to nobody. A town loses a road and a market and a season's tolls and goes on being a town. A man with one crossing of the year left, pledged to a date, cannot get to a crack in a pier that has moved, and cannot be told, and cannot get there by any lawful route in the world.

**The volume's absence has to be felt as a cost and not explained as a rule.** The scarcity is the engine: a reader who has just watched a man spend nineteen days to buy one page is going to want the page to arrive. It does not. It cannot. And the things that do arrive are secondhand, late, and wrong in the way secondhand things are wrong.

## What this batch has to do

Put a man in a town where nothing has arrived and make the nothing cost him something in someone else's accounts. Take the road away in writing. Let a competent woman be competent in a room where being right is expensive. Let a frightened officer stay in post. Let the town blame him, because the town has a board and a clerk and no other story. And when Edric Hunn's first letter comes, let it be accurate, generous in the wrong way, and completely unhelpful.

**Batch goal.** The Crown declares contact illegal in writing. Rell is pressed from above and stays in post, frightened, correct, and unrepentant. The town eats the cost of a sealed road and a lost market and says why, out loud, in chalk. Mara argues for copies in more than one place while the Archive itself is subpoenaed, petrified, and then used. **Edric's first letter arrives and contains no offer, only an accurate summary of everything Aren has done, followed by an instruction to prevent further contact.** And news from the other side arrives late, secondhand, and two weeks out of date, through a barge mate who heard it from a toll-house steward, and it cannot be acted on.

**Batch midpoint. Chapters 35–36.** The Crown's instrument arrives, and the **petrification** of the Archive. A record office can be made to stop existing by paperwork without a single man being hurt: a seal, a date, a returnable order, and a question about who holds what. **Mara's competence is applied to her own side for the second time in the volume** — the batch's turn, not the protagonist's.

**Batch climax. Chapters 39 and 40.** **The stranger's report.** A man who was in Bellweather-Living in the last week of Aren Kest's nineteen days, coming down a channel with a load of stone, who heard from a toll-house steward that the marking of the second pier found the crack had moved — and who says so in a room in Merefen, in the wrong terms, with the wrong date, and is believed by about half the people who hear him. **Aren has to find out what it means and cannot, and cannot go, and the road is shut by a piece of his own signature.** Ends on a decision made without a crossing, with a man choosing to put a document in a place that makes him legible so that somebody else can hold it if he is not there.

**End type for Chapter 40:** decision, with the cost named in somebody else's accounts rather than in his own.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0031

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the twentieth and twenty-first of Greenmonth, Merefen: the yard, the Sedge Reach, the Old Toll abutment, Weir Street.

**Goal:** Be a man who works, in a town that has stopped asking him for anything, and find out what his own bench and his own brother need from him now that the road is shut and the money has stopped.

**Resistance:** The trust is in abeyance for a year, so the crossing has no owner, so the yard has no work off it. A tool chest is on the guild roll at three and a half. He has a wage advance at eight pence a day, six marks at a shilling a day, and eleven days of wages owed by a trust that has nothing in it. **A road warden who repairs nothing is a man with nothing to say at a meeting**, and the guild's own people know it. And the Sedge Reach — the structure the town knows him by — is holding, and is the only thing in Merefen that is holding, and he is on it every day and it is not going to fail.

**Information revealed:** what a bridge with no owner costs in ordinary labour; that the quarter-inch datum in the Archive's book governs twenty years of levels and that the guild has been quietly re-cutting its own mark; that the six marks on his wages are being carried by a man who cannot pay them and has said so in a room; and **that a town which cannot pay anybody is a town whose men are free, and what they do with that is the chapter's question.**

**Action/decision:** He does not go to the Archive, the watch, the magistrate, or the provincial town. He takes the work that is not on anybody's roll — a handrail, a kerb, a footing, a thing nobody owns — and he does it for nothing, in his own time, and he writes none of it down. And when a man from a factor's counting house asks him, in a yard, in front of four men, whether the road is shut for good, he answers the question that was asked and adds the load-bearing fact unprompted.

**Emotional change:** the difference between being asked for and being available, which he has never had to learn and now has.

**Resource change:** a reputation in a yard that is not a queue, and a man who has started giving away days he cannot bill.

**Ending type:** labour, and a cost. **Pull:** a letter arrives for somebody else in this town, and it is opened in a room he is standing in.

### Chapter 0032

**POV:** Mara. **Time/place:** the twenty-first of Greenmonth, the salt-loft rooms, and the town, with an order in it.

**Goal:** Get a set of records out of the building she is sitting in, before somebody with a lawful reason to want them has a reason.

**Resistance:** The Archive's founding instrument keeps **one set of records outside Crown custody**, and that is now the most dangerous clause in the county. A petitioner arrives with a Crown clerk behind him and a question that is not really a question: *who else holds copies of the Bellweather records?* And a man she has been careful with is named in a paragraph of somebody else's document, and the naming is not an accusation, and it is worse.

**Information revealed:** what a **subpoena** is in a county with no superior court inside a day's ride, and what it costs an office that has four pounds a year; **what petrification is** — a seal, a date, a returnable order, and a question about custody, which can stop a record office without hurting a single person; and that **the provincial court's copy of her file has a copy of an exhibit she cannot produce**, and that she promised a claimant a copy on the eighth and still has not got it.

**Action/decision:** She makes the copies. She does not make them in a hiding place and she does not make them in a hurry: she enters each one in the day-book, in the fair hand, with a date and an hour, and she gives one to a person who is not herself and is not a clerk, on the standing argument that a record held by one keeper is a favour and a record held in three places is a thing a court has to answer three times.

**Emotional change:** a woman who has been right four times discovering that being right is now a liability, and that the correct response is to make herself harder to remove rather than to argue.

**Resource change:** copies in more than one place, and a keeper of records who has begun to need other people more than they need her.

**Ending type:** discovery. **Pull:** the thing she is copied out of is a **memorial's second cousin** — a returnable order that names the office the Crown says does not exist, and it comes back on a day.

### Chapter 0033

**POV:** Tovan. **Time/place:** the twenty-eighth of Greenmonth, the yard, the wharf road, the market.

**Goal:** Keep four men in a yard through a month when the road is shut and the stone is finished, and find out what the market says about his brother when his brother is not in it.

**Resistance:** The factor's cutting is done or nearly done and a second load may or may not come. The **toll-house kerb** is work and is also a creditor's money in a creditor's book, and the yard's independence — the thing Tovan has spent a month building — is now on somebody else's page. And the market has three unruled lines on a board and a story about a man in a cart, and a town that has a sentence about the law being on its side and another sentence about a road warden who is not answering a summons, and the two sentences have joined up.

**Information revealed:** what a shut wharf road does to a factor's margin, a miller's cart, and a ferryman; that **a municipal trust in abeyance is a bridge with no owner and therefore a bridge with no crew**, and that a crew is not the same as an owner; and that the yard is the only place in Merefen where a man can be paid for a day's work without being asked what he thinks about a bell, and that this is now a thing people come to the yard to ask about.

**Action/decision:** He takes the work he does not like, again, and he says the thing out loud in the yard before anybody asks: what the yard is, what it is not, and what it will not be used for. And he does the one thing his brother asked him for in the second week of Greenmonth and nobody has thanked him for — he keeps a record of a thing set, with a date and an hour, in a book that is not a copy.

**Emotional change:** the completed beat of Batch 0003's second movement, cashed: the man the yard was built to keep out of the road is the reason the yard has work, and Tovan has stopped needing it not to.

**Resource change:** a yard with four men and a creditor's line in it, and a brother whose undertaking is in a week-book and a guild chest and a press.

**Ending type:** pressure. **Pull:** a **provincial factor** comes to the yard with a question that is really about a road, and asks it of Tovan, because Tovan is the man people ask.

### Chapter 0034

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the second of Longlight, Merefen, the Sedge Reach, and the provincial road.

**Goal:** Work a bridge season, and be a man with a name on a roll that is about to be asked about, and find out whether a road warden can be a useful stranger for a whole county instead of a scandal in one town.

**Resistance:** Longlight is bridge season and the river has dropped and there is more work in this county than there has been in four years, and **three of the four jobs are Crown work**: a schedule, a courier, and a clerk who wants a name on a sheet. He can be paid. He can be useful. And being useful in the main world at Crown expense is the exact shape of the thing he has to refuse, and refusing it costs him a year's work in the worst trade in the county.

**Information revealed:** what a road warden's office can be stretched to cover if a man is willing to be the man who signs; **the difference between a schedule and a finding**; that the Sedge Reach bench mark is a quarter-inch low and that every level in the county taken since the flood is wrong in the direction that makes a deck look better than it is, and that this is a nine-inch-wide fact that four generations will build on; and that **the town has started calling him a keeper, out loud, in a market, meaning the ordinary thing, and that he cannot correct it without explaining the other thing.**

**Action/decision:** He takes one Crown job and refuses two, and he says the reason for each in trade terms, and the reason for the one he takes is written down in his own hand. And he puts the level on the record himself, on the guild's own mark, with a date, and this time the guild's man does not stop him.

**Emotional change:** the moment a man who has been the scandal in his own town discovers he is useful to the thing he distrusts, and has to decide what that makes him.

**Resource change:** paid work, a name on a Crown sheet that can be read, and a level nobody can disprove.

**Ending type:** decision. **Pull:** the county's file is asked about — not by a man with a grievance but by an office with a form.

### Chapter 0035

**POV:** Mara. **Time/place:** the ninth of Longlight, the salt-loft rooms, and the court.

**Goal:** Keep the Archive standing through a week in which three separate people ask her, courteously, whether it exists.

**Resistance:** A **provincial clerk** arrives with a form and a returnable order and a question about custody that is not a question about records. The **county** puts the Archive's founding instrument into issue for the first time in four years. And a man in a stair has started saying that the keeper of the salt loft is a woman who has been writing down what a Crown did, and the town has started repeating the sentence in the only way it can, which is to make it a joke with a name in it.

**Information revealed:** what **petitioning, subpoening, and petrifying** are in a county with no superior court inside a day's ride, in the order they are used and why the third is the one that works; **what a returnable order is worth** and the eleven-day arithmetic of one; and that the Crown's own county file is about to be asked what it is for, by a man in a provincial office, for reasons nobody in Merefen can find out.

**Action/decision:** She is dull. She is duller than she has ever been, and it is a technique and not a personality, and she has never had to use it on her own office before. She enters everything, answers everything by the return date, gives nothing away that is not hers to give, and **sends one document out of the building to a person who is not a clerk and not a keeper, on the ground that a document left with a person is a thing a person can be arrested for and a document left with an office is a thing an office can be.**

**Emotional change:** the loneliness of being the only person in a room keeping a record, and the discovery that the correct response to being the only one is to make a second one.

**Resource change:** the Archive still standing, and a keeper who has begun to spend other people's money and other people's names.

**Ending type:** pressure. **Pull:** the order comes back with a name on it that the clerk at the provincial court read off a roll and did not have to look up.

### Chapter 0036

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the tenth of Longlight, the Old Toll road, the watch house, the wharf.

**Goal:** Find out what the Crown's instruction actually says, and get in front of the thing it is going to do to a man who followed it.

**Resistance:** **Captain Rell is pressed from above and stays in post.** He is given a paper with a seal on it, and the paper is not a threat, it is an instruction, and he carries it out. He is frightened and correct and unrepentant and he can be shown to be right on every count, and the reason he can be shown to be right is that the finding of the fourth of Greenmonth said the seizure was lawful, which was true, and which was his defence then and is his defence now. He has been relieved of a sergeant and his accrual and he has not been rewarded, and he says so, and it is not self-pity, it is a defence.

**Information revealed:** that **Rell has put the twenty-seventh of Mudmonth to the court himself and said on the record that he could not tell whether the paper or the road warden was lying** — so he is a frightened officer with a lawful custody and a documented doubt, and the doubt is in the file, and a man who has been instructed to prevent further contact is a man who now has a reason to be scrupulous; that **a Crown instruction to prevent contact does not require a court and does not name a law**, and that the town is not required to be told; and that the Old Toll post, the empty cradle, the green moss and the swept rectangle are still there, in a warmer month, with a seal that has not run in a hundred days.

**Action/decision:** Aren does not confront him. He asks him one question in a watch-house doorway, in trade terms, and the question is about custody, and it is answerable, and the answer is worse than a confrontation would have been.

**Emotional change:** the discovery that the man who took the bell is the man they send for, and that being the road warden now means being the person a frightened man asks whether he is right.

**Resource change:** an answer in a man's mouth, and a working relationship that is neither an alliance nor a war.

**Ending type:** discovery. **Pull:** the answer contains a date, and the date is a returnable order, and it comes back on the **fifteenth**.

### Chapter 0037

**POV:** Tovan. **Time/place:** the fifteenth of Longlight, the yard, the wharf road, the Old Toll abutment.

**Goal:** Keep a town's bridge open when the man who used to keep it has been told not to, and find out what a bench does to a road warden.

**Resistance:** The bench of the **Merefen bridge guild** has to decide whether to stand behind its own road warden, its own roll, and its own member, and every argument for doing it is an argument the bench has already made against Crown paper. **Reeve Aldis Fenn** is on that bench and her want is unchanged and she says it in one sentence: the road open, the markets kept, and this town able to govern itself without a subsidy out of a magistrate's chair. And the man on the roll is not in the room, because he has been instructed not to be near the matter, and the clerk has to enter that fact.

**Information revealed:** that a bench which votes about a Crown is a bench that has stopped being a bench, and that this bench has already taken a vote about a bond and no other; that **a bench can enter a man as absent and mean it as a protection**; and that the trust in abeyance means the crossing has no owner, and a crossing with no owner is a crossing with no crew, and there is a difference between a crew and an owner that nobody in the town has ever had to make before.

**Action/decision:** Tovan, who is a man on the roll and not a surety and not a road warden, says the true thing that is not an opinion, and then does not vote, because he is a party, and says so in a public room. And the bench votes on the only thing it can vote on, which is **a bridge.**

**Emotional change:** the second of the volume's two completed beats, and this time the man who says the true thing is the one who has to live in the town.

**Resource change:** a bench-minute, a road kept, and a brother who cannot be at a meeting about himself.

**Ending type:** pressure, and a lift. **Pull:** the Crown's returnable order comes back on the fifteenth, and it names the office of keeper of the Bellweather hinge, and the bench has a document.

### Chapter 0038

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the eighteenth of Longlight, Weir Street, the room above the cooper, and the provincial post.

**Goal:** Read what a marshal's office has actually written to him, and find out whether there is anything in it he can use, and then find out that there is not.

**Resistance:** **Edric Hunn's first letter arrives.** It is not sealed as an order. It is a letter, and it is accurate: a summary of everything Aren has done since the sixth of Mudmonth, in the order he did it, with the dates, including the things he has not told anybody — the passage in the hall on the eighteenth day, the field book on the table, the account filed in a filing room, the claim number entered against nobody. **It contains no offer. No restoration, no rank, no shield for the Archive, no access to a record.** It ends with an instruction to prevent further contact, and the instruction is the only thing in the letter that is a sentence with a subject and a verb, and it is competent and it is correct and it is not an argument, and it does not require a court.

**Information revealed:** that a man who kept a copy of a stranger's undertaking and a claim file and a seal-book's device and a press-mark **now knows all of it**, and has known it for some time, and has been waiting, and that Edric's eleven years of unexamined devotion are over in one page and it is he who ended them; that **a letter that is not an order cannot be refused and cannot be obeyed**, and that a man who answers it has agreed to something and a man who does not answer it has been reported to; and that the marshal's office has stopped asking Aren to stop and has started asking **who else he has told.**

**Action/decision:** He writes back. Not to the office — to the marshal, in his own hand, over four days, and the letter is the hardest thing he has written in eleven years, and it does not contain a refusal, and it does not contain a defence, and it contains the one thing he is actually able to give: **a date, a fact, and a condition, offered in the plainest form he can find, in the trade's terms, to a man who taught him the trade.**

**Emotional change:** the death of a devotion, conducted properly, in writing, by the man it happened to.

**Resource change:** a letter in a drawer on Weir Street that is not a surrender and not a refusal, and a Crown office that now knows the shape of him exactly.

**Ending type:** decision, and a cost. **Pull:** the letter goes to the post and the post goes to the provincial town, and a man in a provincial office reads the summary and writes a memorandum about **who else he has told.**

### Chapter 0039

**POV:** Mara. **Time/place:** the twenty-first of Longlight, the salt-loft rooms, and the market.

**Goal:** Find out where a report about the other side came from and how many people have heard it, and get to the bottom of it before the market decides what it means.

**Resistance:** A **barge mate**, in Merefen, in a tavern and then in a room, has something to say about a bridge. He was not there. **A toll-house steward in Bellweather-Living told him**, on a wharf, weeks ago, in the wrong words, about a marking, about a crack, about a number of inches. He is not a liar, he is a man repeating a thing he heard once, and he has the sequence wrong, and the date wrong, and the words wrong, and the wrongness is in the direction that makes it scarier and not less. And by the time Mara hears him, a market in a fen town has already turned it into *the bridge in the other world is going*, and four hundred people have heard the first wrong version and thirty have heard the second.

**Information revealed:** the shape of **news from the other side** — late, secondhand, lossy, and arrived through a man with a cargo and a grudge and a good memory for weather and none for dates; that the true report and the market's version differ by one load, one week, and one word; that **a stranger's report of a moving crack cannot be acted on by anybody in this county, including the man it is about**, and that the man it is about is the only person in the world who could read a pier and has been instructed not to leave; and that a claim in a Crown ledger, a case in a county court, and a bench minute are all documents about a bell and are all going to be read together by somebody eventually.

**Action/decision:** Mara does what she has always done and what the volume says is her whole trade: she puts the true version in writing, in the fair hand, with the date and the hour and the source and the name of the man who said the wrong one, and she does not correct the market, because a corrected market story is a market story with a correction in it.

**Emotional change:** the discovery that a keeper of records can be a person who is *wrong in public about a thing that is her whole life*, and that the only correction available is a document nobody reads.

**Resource change:** a report entered as reported, with its own limits written out in full, and a keeper who now holds the second firsthand account in the case.

**Ending type:** discovery. **Pull:** the two documents — a report from a man who was not there, and a page in a creditor's book four days off — are in the same building and have never been in the same room, and it is possible that a man could be shown both.

### Chapter 0040

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the twenty-second of Longlight, Weir Street, the yard, the Old Toll abutment, and the room above the cooper.

**Goal:** Decide what to do with the whole of it — a letter from a marshal, a market story about a crack, a bench minute, a trust in abeyance, a bond on a roll, and a crossing he cannot make.

**Resistance:** He cannot go. He has **one crossing of the year left** and it is pledged to a date, and the date is a marking in the middle of the year, and the report is about a marking, and if he spends it now he has nothing in the middle of the year and the woman at the ferry writes *not here*. The instruction to prevent contact is not a court order and does not need one. The old seat has been used twice and a glasswright will not sell a third, and there is no fresh Steppe glass north of Merefen before Longlight, and it is now the **middle of June** and the slip's price is four marks the pair and he has eleven days of wages owed by a trust that has nothing in it. **Everything he wants to do requires either a crossing he cannot make or money he does not have, and the third thing available to him is the thing he has never done.**

**Information revealed:** that the only power a man in his position has is to make himself legible, and that legibility cuts both ways and is worth the trouble; that a copy in three places is the same instrument three times and is why a keeper's rule is a rule; and that **an agreement can be kept by other people after the man who made it is not available**, which is the whole of what the Bellweather-Living consent with conditions was for, four days and a thousand miles ago.

**Action/decision:** He files the letter and the report and the bench minute and his own account — **the whole of it, in order, in one chain, with a date and an hour against every piece and a second witness who is not his brother and not a clerk** — in a place that is not his to keep, and he tells two people where it is, and one of them is a woman who has argued with him about a filing system since the sixth of Mudmonth, and one of them is a man who is a creditor's man and owes him nothing and is owed by somebody who does. He chooses a building over a person. He says why out loud, in a room, and the reason is that a person can be arrested for a document and an office can only be closed, and a closed office still has a door.

**Emotional change:** the completed movement of the block. He has stopped trying to be the mechanism and started making himself one of the things other people can hold.

**Resource change:** a file in three places with four dated pieces in it, a man in a bench-minute who will have to answer for a decision he did not make, and a crossing that is still being saved.

**Ending type:** decision, with the cost attached. **Pull:** a bell-shaped hole in a parapet, an eight-inch crack in an outer ring four days away, a claim with a blank line under the villages, a bench that sits again in a year, and **in a fold of the air on a night in Longlight, with four sets of hands on the anchor, a road that opens and a city that is deciding whether it wants it.**
