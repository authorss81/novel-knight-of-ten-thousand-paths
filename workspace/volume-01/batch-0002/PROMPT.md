## What this batch has to do

Give the reader the cost of the first crossing as it lands on other people's accounts, turn the Archive from a place that keeps records into a party in a case, and end on the decision that the second crossing has to be paid for in glass and money before it can be paid for in time.

**Batch goal.** The Archive becomes a party on Mara's terms. Rell's seizure is decided by a magistrate in a market town. A surviving Hushwater ferryman is told he is wrong by the one person best placed to tell him. A registrar's fee appears on the one record the office needs. The province renews its finding and everything filed after it is weaker. A glasswright sells honest anchor-glass at a price that ruins Aren's spring. And the path goes on for eleven days without sending one word, because a man at the first stage has no way to receive anything.

**Batch midpoint.** **Chapters 15–16.** The hearing on the seizure on the fourth of Greenmonth. Lysa Fenn rules that the seizure was lawful procedure, that a Crown seal is a Crown seal wherever the bell is, that the watch acted on a paper that predates the act, and that the paper's *existence* is now a matter for a different and larger office. Rell is not rebuked. The guild's municipal trust is not restored, because the Crown's custody is lawful. And a sealed order turns out to have produced two summonses and no authority at all, which is the batch's real discovery: the Crown can move a bell anywhere in Neyra without any office in Neyra being able to say who told it to. **The two summonses are already public** — Chapter 10 put the second one on a magistrate's table in full — so the midpoint's discovery is the *hole* under them, made a matter of public record with her name on it, plus **Bram Ottery's statement** naming the man who has been telling Merefen the bell carries the fever. The point of the midpoint is not that a paper is read out; it is that a document nobody was summoned to see turns out to be the only honest account of the morning.

**Batch climax.** **Chapters 19–20.** The renewal lands on the second of Greenmonth and the file is suddenly a year weaker; Jos Rusk refuses to be argued with and then, on the last page, asks the wrong question and gets an answer nobody in the room wanted; and the anchor-glass is bought — or not bought — and the sill is cut for the second crossing, or is not. **The batch must end on a decision, not a cliffhanger:** Aren decides to spend the last crossing of the season, or decides he cannot, and the reader must be able to name which and why.

**End type for Chapter 20:** decision, with the block's bill attached and the crossing's bill named.

**Day map, main world.** Ch 11 the first of Greenmonth · Ch 12 the first, afternoon · Ch 13 the second, the morning the renewal comes down · Ch 14 the third · Ch 15 the fourth, the hearing · Ch 16 the fourth, afternoon · Ch 17 the sixth · Ch 18 the seventh · Ch 19 the eighth · Ch 20 the eighth, evening. **The fifth of Greenmonth is a stated cut with nothing in it** — the market that fell on the fourth is cleaned up, the work goes on, nobody new comes, no new document arrives, and the bell is still where it is. **The ninth is the opening of Crossing 2 and is not in this batch.** Block 2 is Chapter 20 at the eighth hour of the eighth, and the ninth belongs to Chapters 21–22.

Markets fall on the fourth and ninth of each month and every fifth day after, so Greenmonth has markets on the fourth and the ninth: **the hearing of the fourth is a market morning**, and the square will be full, and half of them will be there because a bell went out of the town and a road warden said something impossible on a market morning. Use that.

**The volume's language rule, still in force.** "Keeper" is the corrupted Crown term and two provincial documents have applied it to Aren. **The word *custodian* stays unused in Volume 01.** Path-side locals say *steward*, and a steward maintains. ## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0011

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the first of Greenmonth, mid-morning, Weir Street, then Sedge Reach. The fever broke on the thirtieth and he has been up two days.

**Goal:** Be a road warden again. He has a shilling a day, eleven days of unpaid wages, a mark and a quarter in hand, a bond for six, a brother four days behind on wages **who has the four marks and will not be asked**, and a bell under a seal he cannot touch. He wants a job he can be paid for. **The four marks are not his — Ch 9 gives them to Tovan twice and Ch 10 restates it. Do not move them.**

**Resistance:** He cannot be paid for the work that is actually waiting, which is a scour hole on his own bridge, a bench mark, and a cut sill nobody has authorized. The trust's money is gone for the season — Ch 10's column is a fair copy of an account that has nothing left in it. The town has decided he is the sort who is not told, and now it has also decided he is the sort who says things. Two of his clients are the factor with eleven tons of paving setts and a man who wants a bridge pin cut and delivered by Thursday.

**Information revealed:** that the fever's price is now being invoiced; that the Old Toll sill's cross is a working scar and not a stain, and that no roadwarden in Merefen can mend stone with glass; and the anchor-glass slip's full terms: two Steppe discs, four marks the pair, ready in a fortnight, money before cutting.

**Action/decision:** He goes to the glasswright and is quoted, and the price is the price, and he does not have it. He does not borrow and does not explain. He asks what a disc of glass is *for*, in the trade's words, and is told, and asks the question that will matter later — whether anchor-glass can take a working — and is told that it can take a working the way a bridge takes a cart, and that it will also fail in a manner the maker does not choose.

**Emotional change:** Competence is back and money is not, and for the first time the reader sees that Aren does not know how to be in a room where he is not the best man, because nobody has needed one.

**Resource change:** no glass, no stitch, one day of pay, a name on a market square.

**Ending type:** cost. **Pull:** a woman at the loft has been asking for him, and the reason she wants him is a man in Merefen who is not going to leave quietly.

### Chapter 0012

**POV:** Mara. **Time/place:** the first of Greenmonth, afternoon, the salt-loft rooms, with a claimant in them.

**Goal:** Keep the office a record and not an argument, in a week when the province is about to renew, with a file she has promised to file.

**Resistance:** **Jos Rusk**, a Hushwater ferryman who survived and whose wife **Anwen Rusk** kept nine years of crossing book and whose daughter **Bryn Rusk** was eleven and aboard the last ferry out, is here to file. He is not stupid, he is not a symbol, and he is *wrong about the cause* — he believes the fever was carried in the water and that the low quarter took it and that the Crown buried the rest, and that is a better story than the Crown's and still not the truth. He does not want to be argued with. And the Archive's rule is that a claimant is not refused; he is heard, and the hearing is written, and the writing is the weapon.

**Information revealed:** **Anwen Rusk's crossing book** and what a crossing book can and cannot do — it records every crossing in a hand other men could check, and a hand that is regular proves that a boat went across, not why a village did not. **Bryn Rusk, 11.** Mara's personal refusal, made in front of a claimant, of the argument that would be easiest and worst: she will not use a child's name as a heading. **The erased are documents.** The rule that a roll built out of Ferris Oat's ledger must be a ledger and not a speech.

**Action/decision:** She hears him, takes the claim, and puts the cause in dispute in writing — *the record does not show the cause* — and she tells him plainly that this will cost him, because a claim with a disputed cause is a weaker claim than a claim with a wrong cause, and that she is doing it because the wrong cause can be disproved in a year and the right cause cannot be proved at all.

**Emotional change:** Mara makes grief do useful work, in front of a man who is grieving, and the reader should feel the cost of her competence on somebody rather than only on a file.

**Resource change:** a second claim number, a written dispute, and an enemy she made correctly.

**Ending type:** relationship conflict. **Pull:** Rusk does not shout. He asks, very quietly, whether anybody has told the ferrymen.

### Chapter 0013

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the second of Greenmonth, mid-morning, Merefen, the day the renewal comes down.

**Goal:** See what the province's renewal actually says, in a town that has already heard a worse version of it.

**Resistance:** The finding arrives by the ordinary post and is read aloud in a room because that is how a finding reaches a fen town, and it says what it has said every year: *no claim to Bellweather survives, and the crossing of the Alder Vale and its dependent places was not built and is not maintained.* Not destroyed. **Not built.** Eleven years of a sentence in which a city is a thing that was never there, and the difference between *erased* and *not built* is the whole of the case, and a man in a fen town has to be talked into understanding that it is better news and worse news at once. The renewal also carries a line about the renewal of emergency powers in the county, and a man who reads it correctly knows that the seizure of a bell is now routine.

**Information revealed:** the exact wording of a finding, and the fact that the Archive's thirtieth-of-Mudmonth exhibit is *not* what it was on the day it was made; that the crown's "dependent places" is a phrase that fits a city and fits seven villages and is used for both; and that the renewed emergency power is what put the watch on a bridge.

**Action/decision:** Aren does the thing he is good at: he reads the load, finds the load path in the paper, and tells a magistrate and an archivist exactly what the sentence is built to hold up, and what it will be asked to hold up next, and by whom. He does it for free, in a room, badly paid, and gets it wrong about one thing in a way that costs them.

**Emotional change:** He is the most useful man in the room again and the room is grateful, and the reader should feel the difference from Chapter 1.

**Resource change:** a document, a warning, and a new enemy who has never appeared and will not appear until Block 4.

**Ending type:** discovery. **Pull:** a letter with a fee on it, and the name of the man who quotes fees.

### Chapter 0014

**POV:** Mara. **Time/place:** the second of Greenmonth, afternoon, the salt-loft rooms, with **Sarn Oyelaran** in them for the first time.

**Goal:** Get the one record she needs — the Crown's own Bellweather salvage and clearance entries — before somebody discovers she has been asking for it.

**Resistance:** **Halven**, the registrar at the Crown registry in the provincial town, holds the Crown's Bellweather entries. He has already quoted her four shillings once and been paid. He now quotes a **fee for a missing-anchor ledger** — a ledger that should not exist, whose existence is a claim that anchors were kept and then not, and whose absence from the roll is itself the document. He is not a villain. He is a man with a post, a family, a legal exposure, and a price, and he is entirely reasonable. And the fee is not a bribe; it is a **charge for a search**, which is the ordinary way a Crown registry keeps itself from being blamed.

**Information revealed:** the missing-anchor ledger; that a registrar's fee is a real institution and not corruption; that Halven is a keeper of a public register and a keeper has no jurisdiction and cannot refuse a fee without a magistrate's order; and **Sarn Oyelaran's want**, which is a post of her own and which is the reason she is competent at a job she is being paid for badly.

**Action/decision:** Mara does not pay it twice. She writes to Lysa Fenn for an order to inspect a register without fee, which is a legal thing that takes eleven days, and she pays the small fee now to hold the date, and she is honest with Sarn about both facts.

**Emotional change:** The archive-as-argument tension from Chapter 12 becomes a money problem, and Mara has to admit that the office is poor and the fight is about a filing fee.

**Resource change:** a fee paid, eleven days of waiting started, and Sarn's loyalty half earned.

**Ending type:** political pressure. **Pull:** Sarn notices an altered date in the Crown's own roll, and says so out loud, and cannot say why it matters.

### Chapter 0015

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the fourth of Greenmonth, the ninth hour, the salt-loft room, market morning, the hearing on the sealed order.

**Goal:** Get the bell found, or found to be lawfully where it is, by somebody who is not him and not the marshal's office.

**Resistance:** **Captain Rell** under oath, correct, frightened, and right that a man who was wet through and grey on the twenty-seventh cannot be expected to say where he had been. **Lysa Fenn** on the bench in her own room, with a clerk, with the guild's notice of the eleventh in front of her, with the thirtieth's exhibit in the record, and with a crowd in the stair. The watch's seizure is lawful: the seal is the seal, the paper is the paper, the town is in the county, and the Crown may do what the Crown's paper says. The town will be told it was legal. The bridge guild's municipal trust is not restored, because lawful is lawful.

**Information revealed:** the mechanics of a lawful seizure and what it costs to undo one; the **three days**, from the sixteenth to the morning of the nineteenth, said aloud in a public room by the magistrate who will rule it lawful; that **Bram Ottery**, the watch sergeant, has written a statement nobody asked for, and it is a document that says what the watch did and why and who was present, and it names the man in the crowd who was told the bell was the fever's, which is a man nobody had thought to ask about. **That statement is the chapter's landing, not a middle beat** — set up the sergeant early, in Rell's own hearing, and let the room turn at the end.

**Action/decision:** Aren does not testify about Bellweather. He testifies about a bell: nine stone, a cradle, a bar through a headstock loop, and a crack. He gives the court a tradesman's account of an object and lets the object be the argument, because he has learned from Mara what a page has to carry.

**Emotional change:** He wins the smaller thing and loses the larger one, and the smaller one is real.

**Resource change:** a ruling, a written answer in somebody else's hand, and a legal door that is now closed on the question of the bell.

**Ending type:** cost, and a reversal. **Pull:** the watch-sergeant's statement, written on nobody's order, goes into the record — and it names a man in the crowd in this room who was told the bell would bring the fever back, and said it, and kept saying it afterwards. Nobody in the chamber had thought to ask that man anything, because he was not holding a document; he was holding a story, and he has been selling it in Merefen since the seventeenth, and the court cannot un-sell it in a morning. Do **not** end the hearing on the summons for the ninth of Greenmonth: Chapter 10 already put that letter on a magistrate's table in full, in front of Mara, on the thirtieth, and it has been public for six days. The summons and the matching seal can be *noted* by the clerk. The discovery this chapter owes the reader is the man in the stair.

### Chapter 0016

**POV:** Lysa. **Time/place:** the fourth of Greenmonth, afternoon, her own chambers, with Aren, and with the finding from the second.

**Goal:** Decide what a magistrate can actually do with two Crown seals, no authority, and a town that has been told the law was on its side.

**Resistance:** Her own leash. Procedure, and the discovery that procedure can hold a thing in place indefinitely while never touching it. She cannot refuse a Crown paper, she cannot enforce a municipal trust against one, and she cannot open a register without an order that will take eleven days. The **renewed emergency power** in the county is the actual subject of the hearing, and it is a provincial fact she cannot reach.

**Information revealed:** the shape of the legal machine between a town and a provincial office, drawn by a woman who likes it; the eleven-year-old salvage filing in her own hand, of which she says only that it is a different document from the bell, and that the difference is the first thing she was taught; and her own want, out loud at last, which is to make the law useful to people who cannot afford a title or a patron, and which is harder to say than to want.

**Action/decision:** She rules the seizure lawful, records the authority as unproducible, and does the one thing a magistrate can do: she makes the gap in the chain a matter of the public record with her name on it, so that the next office to try it has to answer for the last one. **She says the three days out loud in the room**, because that is the number the Crown cannot now unwrite, and because a record with a gap in it and a date in it is worth more to whoever comes next than a record with the bell in it.

**Emotional change:** A professional's cold comfort, honestly labelled as comfort, and a decision that costs a man a bell and saves him a principle.

**Resource change:** a public record of an absence, a date in eleven days, and a magistrate who is now a party.

**Ending type:** decision, with a mechanism named. **Pull:** eleven days, and in the meantime the office is poorer and the file is weaker and a man in a fen town is unemployed.

### Chapter 0017

**POV:** Tovan. **Time/place:** the sixth and seventh of Greenmonth, the yard, the Sedge Reach deck, the wages.

**Goal:** Pay four men and keep a bridge, with a brother who cannot be given work and a factor who will not stop asking.

**Resistance:** The work is done and there is no more of it, which is the thing a bridge carpenter dreads more than a flood. The factor's eleven tons are on a bank two miles up. The bridge bond is still on the guild's roll against a chest, and the guild's clerk has begun asking Tovan, as the brother, what his brother means to do. And **Pei Tarrow** comes to the yard, because a freight ferryman on the Old Toll channel has heard that the road is shut and the bell is gone and a man has been saying impossible things, and he has come to say what he came to say, which is that his mother was from Hushwater and he is not a symbol and he is not going to be one, and that if anyone in Merefen wants to use him they may use his eleven years of freight on a channel that is not his family's village and not anybody's claim.

**Information revealed:** Tovan's own want, at last, in his own voice, which is a paid yard and a bridge season and a brother who is not a road; the shape of the estrangement after the refusal — they are now, in public, a man who mends and a man who says things; and Pei Tarrow's want, which is freight, a channel, and eleven years of not being a symbol.

**Action/decision:** Tovan gives Pei the work and the wages and no sentence about Bellweather at all, which is the only correct thing available and which costs him the best conversation he will have had in a year.

**Emotional change:** Tovan finds out what he is for, and it is not his brother's mission, and the finding costs something.

**Resource change:** four men paid, a yard busy, one wage earned, and a street that is no longer entirely against him.

**Ending type:** relationship beat, and a lift. **Pull:** the tower at Merefen has a line of sight down the raised road, and a boy on the Sedge Reach deck reports that the old Bellweather bell rang on the far bank on the seventh, in daylight, with nobody near it.

### Chapter 0018

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the seventh of Greenmonth, evening, the Old Toll crossing, and then the room above the cooper.

**Goal:** Hear the bell, or hear nothing, and find out which he can live with.

**Resistance:** The sound is a report, from a boy, through a market, and it is a bell on a bridge in a fen town that rang in daylight with nobody near it — a fact that Rell will seize, a fact that the town will repeat, a fact that may be a boy, or the wind, or the same thing that happened on the eleventh. And there is no seam in the air, no warmth in the palm, no voice, no warmth, nothing. **He cannot send anything and he cannot receive anything and he has no anchor he may lawfully touch, because the bell is under a seal and the sill is a scar.** It is the seventh of Greenmonth, five days after the renewal, and the path has sent nothing in any of them, because a path does not write letters, and a man at the first stage does not have a door. Do not date this chapter by counting forward from the twenty-seventh: **Mudmonth's length is not stated anywhere in the bible or the outlines**, so a count from his return can be off by one. Count from the renewal, which is on the page.

**Information revealed:** the absence as an active cost; that the road in him is nominally open and is not usable; and that the fracture between a thing being open and a thing being available is the whole of his position and will be for forty more chapters.

**Action/decision:** He does the tradesman's thing: he goes and looks at the cradle, and the post, and the seal, and the sill, and the moss, and he writes down what he can see, and he does not touch the seal, and he does not try the sill. Then he sits down on the abutment and does the thing he has not done since the sixth of Mudmonth, which is to count load points under a structure he is not going to enter.

**Emotional change:** The elapse is gone and what is left is the work, and the reader should feel the difference between a man who is being refused and a man who has run out of road.

**Resource change:** a notch in the notebook, a bell report he cannot use, and eleven days of nothing.

**Ending type:** absence, and a small decision. **Pull:** the report has to go somewhere, and if it goes to the Archive it becomes a document, and if it goes to the watch it becomes a reason.

### Chapter 0019

**POV:** Mara. **Time/place:** the eighth of Greenmonth, the salt-loft rooms, and then the yard.

**Goal:** Decide what to do with a bell that rang with nobody near it, in a file that is now a year weaker than it was eight days ago.

**Resistance:** Sarn's altered date in the Crown's own roll. The renewal's damage. A claimant in the outer room who will not go away. And the discovery that the fee she paid bought her a *date of search* and not the ledger, and that the ledger's absence is a fact she can now put in front of a magistrate in eleven days, if she is still a keeper of an office that anybody listens to.

**Information revealed:** the altered date, named as altered, and what it is attached to — a clearance entry, in YR 302, which is the year the bell was lifted out of the river, and a man who writes a clearance entry a year late is a man who is copying somebody else's roll.

**Action/decision:** She does not publish the date. She files it, unargued, in the same way the seal was filed unargued, and she tells Sarn why: because a document that is only a document is a document nobody has to be persuaded about.

**Emotional change:** Her worst habit, seen from inside, and one small correction of it.

**Resource change:** a filed date, a fortnight's start on a glass order, and a keeper who has begun to need other people.

**Ending type:** discovery, and a turn. **Pull:** the glass is the crossing, and the glass is four marks, and there are two ways to get four marks in Merefen this week and only one of them is honest.

### Chapter 0020

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the eighth of Greenmonth, evening, the yard, then the Old Toll sill at the last light.

**Goal:** Pay for the glass, or decide that he cannot, and then say so out loud to the two people who will have to live with it.

**Resistance:** Four marks. He has a mark and a quarter, which is what a shilling a day and eleven days of unpaid wages leave a man. Tovan has the four marks and will not be asked and cannot be asked twice, and saying that out loud in front of a glasswright is the scene. The trust is spent. Chapter 19 ended on the fact that there are two ways to have four marks in Merefen this week and only one of them is honest, so the honest one is money that has to be owed and remembered by somebody other than Aren, and the batch should be clear about *which* before the glass is cut — do not let the price arrive from nowhere. The tradesman's dodge — a second bond against a tool-chest worth three and a half, on a chest a guild clerk has already priced at three and a half — is available and is a way of buying glass with a lien on his own tools, and a clerk will read it as faith again. **Ilvane Quarren** the glasswright will sell him anchor-glass at a price he resents and will not be talked down, and will say the true reason anchor-glass costs what it costs, which is that it takes a year to anneal properly and he will not sell a thing that will fail in a stranger's hand. And the sill has to be cut for the pin whatever else happens, because a scar in a sill is a scar forever and the next working will find it.

**Information revealed:** that the four marks is a spring's wages and a year of not eating; that the glass, cut well, will outlast both men; and that the Old Toll sill's cross is the place a crossing goes, and that the cross is also, on the evidence, a scar, and that a scar is where the anchor is.

**Action/decision:** He pays it, and the chapter ends on the sill being cut for the pin and the money going out of a fen town into a Steppe road in glass, and on the decision — spoken aloud, to a brother and a glasswright — that he is going to try again on the ninth, and on the quiet arithmetic of what that means: it is the second of three crossings this season, and the first one cost him nine days, three days of fever, a market, and a bell.
