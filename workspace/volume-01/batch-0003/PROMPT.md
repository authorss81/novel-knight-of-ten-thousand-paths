## What this batch has to do

Put a man on the other side of the door for the second time, with a date on it, and then have the other side tell him what it thinks of him. The book turns at the midpoint: the register names the man who signed the severance, and the living city refuses to be the case. The whole point of the reversal is that **the evidence exonerates him and indicts him in the same breath, and the party it is used against is the one he thought he was defending.**

**Batch goal.** He answers nobody's summons, he is a bridge back, and he arrives to find nineteen days of somebody else's morning. He works. He gets the counterfoil's match. He is asked to be a witness rather than a rescuer, and the asking is the price. The toll-house counting room produces the outgoing writ register, and the register names **Marshal Edric Hunn** as the signatory of the severance of *"the Alder Vale crossing and its dependent places"* on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301 — and a seal on that page matches the wax on the storehouse in Merefen. And then the guild tells him it does not want to be restored, and a city of four thousand explains what it would cost it to be saved by a stranger with a private road.

**Batch midpoint. Chapters 25–27.** Two beats, in this order, and the second is the real one.
- **The register page.** In the toll-house counting room, not the guild hall — the toll houses are the guild's political opponents and hold the other half of the writ. The page names the marshal. It is the same press as the Crown wax in Merefen, which means the mechanism that took a bell off a bridge in a fen town and the mechanism that severed a valley eleven years ago are the same office. The Crown can now be asked the question in public.
- **The refusal.** In the same stretch, the living city declines to be a case. It has its own dead, its own debt, and no interest in being appended to a Crown record; and the most useful witness in the world tells him plainly that a city rescued by the main world would be a city the main world owns. **Do not let the revelation be a reward. Let the reveal and the refusal land in the same block so the reader cannot have one without the other.**

**Batch climax. Chapters 28 and 30, with Chapter 29 between them on the other side of the door.** **Aren admits in writing that he was given a partial order, that he never reached the inner city, and that for three years he repeated the fever account and kept his own field notes in a coat instead of handing them over.** This is the batch's cost, paid by the man the book has been defending, and it is already partly on the page: Chapter 10 filed a sworn account with both omissions, so **do not file it again as a new document — extend it.** What is new is that he says it to the guild and to the people in the city, out loud, in his own voice, knowing what it will cost him with a bench of nine, and that the path's terms for his return stop being a favour and become an agreement. The third of three crossings is spent, and Chapter 29 is six days of Merefen going on without him, which is what the batch costs the reader in a different currency. **Ends on an agreement with a named counterweight and a price, not on a discovery.**

**End type for Chapter 30:** decision, with the agreement's terms named and the cost of the second crossing paid for in full.

### Chapter 0021

**POV:** Aren. **Time/place:** the ninth of Greenmonth, market morning, Merefen: Weir Street, the guild bench, and the Old Toll sill at the eighth hour.

**Goal:** Be in two places at once, and know which one he is in. The bench sits today on a bond whose surety is standing on a bridge he has cut, and the provincial town is expecting a keeper of the Bellweather hinge he has never been.

**Resistance:** He is not on a market morning. The bench is nine men and a roll and a vote, and a road warden who is not in front of it can be voted on. The **guild clerk** has to enter a wage advance as a debt and a witness's sight of a thing set, and he cannot enter the reason. **Reeve Aldis Fenn** sits on that bench and has been sent for, or comes, and the first thing she says is about the road and not the bell. Tovan is at the bench and is the brother and not the surety and has one sentence and it is the correct one. And the market is on the wharf road and the town is going to say in a square what a bench said in a room.

**Information revealed:** that the bond of the eleventh, six marks against a chest at three and a half, **is not discharged by a Crown seal on a post** and the bench has to decide what to do about six marks; that the bench can vote a surety into a room and that a man who is not in the room cannot answer; that the provincial clerk on the ninth will enter *absent*; and that **the Old Toll sill is a scar that has been used and the bench's decision does not change it.**

**Action/decision:** He does the tradesman's thing one more time and it is the last time: he goes and looks at the crossing, at the seal, at the seat he cut, at the two discs, and writes down what he can see, and he does not touch the seal and he does not try the sill, and he gives **Tovan a sheet with an hour on it** and asks him to keep it, which is the same request he made of a boy on a deck and is now a request of a man with a roll.

**Emotional change:** He leaves a town that is not waiting for him, on purpose, with a bench voting behind his back, and the reader should feel the difference from a man who was taken.

**Resource change:** a vote, a gap, a sheet of paper in a yard's week-book.

**Ending type:** departure. **Pull:** nineteen days and a city that has been up since the fourth hour, and a step on the north bank that is not the right step.

### Chapter 0022

**Goal:** Get to a bench and get terms, because the terms are the only thing he has that the city does not.

**Ending type:** negotiation. **Pull:** the toll-house man in the room has heard a foreign stranger offer a document, and the toll houses keep the other half of the writ.

### Chapter 0025

### Chapter 0026

### Chapter 0027

### Chapter 0030
