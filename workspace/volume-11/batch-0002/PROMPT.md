## 1. What this block is, and the one-sentence argument

`outline/volume-11.md` §8 Block 2: **nine counties have answered and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month are coming into the same two hands. The block's second cost is a record kept twice, and the two copies disagree, and the disagreement is not an error and is not resolved. Gault Prentice offers a compromise that is worse than his first offer and he knows it and says so.**

The argument in one sentence: **a form is a thing that finds out, and the finding-out is the asking, and a block in which two copies of everything are made is a block in which every copy is a second question asked of the same road.**

Where the block starts: the four heads in two counties, the sixteenth of an inch, about four hundred people at the end of a made lane on a return in two counties, nine counties asked and five answered, a chairman with a badge and two words in his mouth, a copyist of about twenty-six who has said one thing out loud in two counties, a keeper of records two hundred miles off who has refused fifty sheets once and has not been thanked, and a protagonist who is now written down in the back of a book of carts.

Where it ends: the two copies disagree about a place or about a figure, both are right, and a person who can be asked about either one cannot be asked about both.
