## What the ten chapters must do

Block 3, per `outline/volume-03.md` §8. **The block's frame: path time only, the first third of thirteen rollings, in a county that has just refused him and has no word for refusing. A council that refuses outside command a second time and says what it will accept instead. The salt, and the mechanism, said in trade terms by a person with a gauge column and not by a stranger. The one working anybody can do in this case, named out loud, refused, dated, and not explained. The seed vault. The tenant league. And the first proposal in the volume that is not his.**

- **Ch 121–123 · the first rolling · the council, and the terms, and the soil.** The council chamber over a tithe barn, about eleven people, a bench that argues about a boundary, **which refuses outside command a second time and means it, and says what it will accept instead, and what it will accept is the guild's terms, and the guild's terms are better.** **The four white villages, the salt, and the mechanism said in trade terms by a woman with a gauge column and not by a stranger: the water table has risen because a ditch has been moved a hand's width to the left every year for a hundred and forty years, and the salt has come up out of the ground it was always in.** And the question nobody in the room asks, which is the volume's question: **how do you keep a promise that has been kept for a hundred and forty years and is killing everybody who keeps it?** **The mechanism is already on the page and is not said by him: Chapter 120 entered it as *seen, not told*. The woman with the gauge column is the one who says it, and the saying is the batch's centre of gravity, and he says nothing in the room and is not thanked for saying nothing.**
- **Ch 124–126 · the second rolling · the cut, refused, and the struck, and the man who swore it.** Somebody in the room — **Delya Munn, who renews the oath-stone and is right about the history and wrong about the soil** — says the obvious thing in front of eleven people: *there is a working that would take the water out of that ditch in an afternoon, and the man off the road has done worse in a shorter time.* **And he says no, and the reason is not a rule, and the reason is a date: the last time one of these was cut, the anchor tore and a community lost contact for ever, and the man who cut it was told afterwards that the witness had been forged, and he wrote it down. Nobody understands the sentence. He does not explain it and may not.** **And the Marches' own struck: about sixty households, alive, on the roll, and not on the oath, and nobody has ever asked them what they swore, and the woman of about sixty on three hearths from Chapter 119 is in the room or is one of the ones who is not in the room, and the not-asking is now the batch's subject and not a detail.**
- **Ch 127–129 · the third rolling · the vault, the league, and a promise with a release in it.** **Sian Pugh and nine sealed jars of a wheat not grown here in forty years, and a wall with two earlier answers built up in it, and a woman of sixty-six who keeps a seed room and does not want the cooperative's thanks.** **Ordmar Gace and the tenant league, who wants a man to be able to say what he will not plant and not lose a field for it, and who is right about the wages and is on the council's side of every argument about money and against it on one.** **And the first proposal that is not Aren's, and it is the volume's turn: an oath that can be broken. Not lifted — broken, on purpose, in public, by the people who swore it, on a day named in advance, with a release in it, a consequence, and no office behind it.** It is the sentence a rule with no office behind it is for, and it is said first by a man who has not read a law.
- **Ch 130 · the fourth rolling · Ash's fifth term, used wrongly, and corrected down a line.** He offers the county the thing he knows: the fourth of the five terms of Ash, *a person struck may answer, and answering restores*. **It is the wrong instrument and he knows it in about a second and a half, because a person in Ash is struck from a count and a person in the Marches is not on an oath, and one is a wound and the other is a promise, and a promise cannot be cured by answering.** The correction comes down the line on a fair copy **nine days later, in a hand that is not a clerk's, from a woman four hundred miles off who keeps the road at that end, and it is two lines long and one of them is: *we did not lose our register, we lost our refusal, and a register is what you have instead*** — and he has read that in a fair copy once already and has never been told where it came from, and now he knows, and **the county does not know he knows.** **This is the volume's one conversion attempt and its refusal is the plant; do not spend it anywhere else in the block.**

## Hard constraints. Every one of these has been broken by somebody in this project before.

- **The calendar.** The anchor is Chapter 100's own last line: **the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth, YR 313, a Monday.** Every month from there to the twenty-fourth of Hearthmonth is **thirty days**. **Chapters 121–130 are path time and there is no main-world chapter date to state: no main-world month name, no real-world month name, and no main-world weekday may be spoken or written anywhere in the ten chapters, by anybody, in any register including a notebook entry, and no rolling may be converted into a day of this calendar by any character.** The Marches keep a year of **thirteen rollings counted from the first sowing, four weeks to a rolling, and the year is a year plus a rolling** — say it in the county's own words, do not convert it, and do not let a narrator convert it either. **The one conversion the volume attempts is Chapter 130's, and it must be refused in his own notebook, and the refusal is the plant.** Do not state how many rollings have passed in a main-world figure.
- **The money.** **Every money column is added in pence and footed on the page before the total is said aloud.** Do this arithmetic in front of the reader the way Chapters 108 and 116 do it, line by line, and then say the total. The Chapter 10 column's stated total of eleven marks and fourpence is canon and is not re-derived, and nothing may be scaled out of it. **Chapter 108's contract figures are fixed and are already on the page: ninety working days, four men at eight pence and a boy at four, thirty-six pence a day, three thousand two hundred and forty pence; two hundred and eighty-eight bushels of salt at nine pence, two thousand five hundred and ninety-two, and two hundred and sixteen bushels of seed at three pence, six hundred and forty-eight, which is the last fifth the guild's first hand gave up; and the yard's exposure of two hundred and eighty-eight pence for four men and three hundred and sixty for a boy, two hundred and eighty-eight and three hundred and sixty being six hundred and forty-eight.** The yard's four men are **in this county and are on the Marches' own calendar**; if you need a new figure, derive it in front of the reader and foot it, and a Marches figure is never set beside an Ash one or a Merefen one.
- **The banned sums.** **Four thousand and seven hundred and twenty are never added, anywhere, in any form, and the eighth ruled line in the served schedule is not looked at again and no character refers to it.** No Ash figure may appear on the page in Merefen or in the Marches: **941, 2,450, 2,906, 254 and the 456 are all out of bounds, and the 456 is not on the page in any form and is not derived from anything.** **The Marches' own figures are three and none of them is a hearth** — a gauge count, a book of lines, and a count of rollings or a count of ditches are the only figures available, and about sixty households on a roll is the volume's own and is not a hearth. **Hushwater Reach's near sixty and Hushwater's eighty are never printed together and nobody notices the names match.**
- **The words that are out of bounds.** ***Culling Order* does not appear in Volume 03 at all.** ***Gatehouse* may appear only as a measurement standard, quoted from his own notebook, and not as an institution anybody discusses.** *Bind* does not appear. ***Custodian* carries its `bible/terminology.md` meaning and **Aren is not a custodian and may not become one in this volume.**** The marginal instruction, the private-keeper clause, is **printed, quoted verbatim as Chapter 51 has it, cited in a file, and refused about in a gateway — and it may not be explained in a single explanatory sentence by anybody, and Sabin Dain may not be the person who explains it and may not change her want.** **Chapter 120's reply that neither accepts nor refuses may not be quoted back, improved on or resolved in this block; the refusal is a document in Chapters 148–150.**
- **The tile on the coping.** **Not touched, not looked at, not explained, not named, and the device is named by nobody.** No flash-back, no origin, no maker, no deathbed, and no scene whose engine is the tile. It is on the far side of a road that closes in Block 4.
- **The breach.** **Open. Not repaired. Not repaired in a scene, an aside, a document or an apology, and no mistake is to be made available to anybody. He gives her the numbers and not the morning. She does not ask, and the not asking is entered, and the three rules do their work, and the concealment is a habit and the habit is the cost, not the subject.** **The count of not-askings is nine and no tenth is entered in Volume 03** — if a Batch 0013 chapter is set in Merefen at all, which it should not be, this holds. **Dain's offer is not refused in this block** and the refusal is Chapters 148–150.
- **The brothers.** **The estrangement is not healed and no scene may heal it. `I am not going to re-teach you your own brother` is spent and may not be re-said, improved on, or alluded to as a thing to be taken back.** Each brother still has a page the other does not know about. **The yard is the only relationship in the book getting better, and in this block the yard's four men are in the county, on its own calendar, and are the best crew in it, and are not on the bench and are not on the oath and have never been asked what they swore.**
- **The cast.** **`outline/volume-03.md` §14 allows exactly TWO new names first named in prose in this batch and four unfilled slots: Ordmar Gace, 49, of the tenant league, who is right about the wages; and Aled Rhys, 27, a water-barge hand, who is the first person in the volume to use the word *refused* and mean it.** **Sian Pugh is Batch 0014's and may not be named in this batch** — she is *the woman of sixty-six who keeps the seed room* and no more. **A character established in `bible/characters.md` who has never had his name on a page counts as one. Nobody else gets a first name in prose.** Everyone else is established or addressed by trade: Coll Ferrow, Delya Munn, Thessa Wend, the man at the drawknife, Pei Tarrow, the boy of fourteen, the woman of about sixty on three hearths, the man who keeps the minute of the Marches council, the woman with a slate, the boy of fifteen, Bram Ottery, Reeve Aldis Fenn, Lysa Fenn, Mara Vey, Captain Rell, the bench-clerk, and the tile-cutter of forty-one.
- **The Marches' reasonableness.** **The council is not a rescue, is not an invasion and is not a symbol. The guild is not a rescue. Nobody is a monster and three of the four parties in this volume are better than he is.** The council refuses outside command because a promise renewed a hundred and forty times is killing everybody who keeps it, and it is right about the history, and **Delya Munn is right about the history and wrong about the soil and no one in the volume may call her a villain and she is the one who says the sentence about a working that would take the water out in an afternoon.** **The salt is the villain of the volume and it is not a person and is not named as one.**
- **Stage 3.** **Reached, not attempted, not offered as a plan, not priced, not described by anybody as a thing he could do if he chose — except that Chapter 124 to 126 names it once, out loud, in front of eleven people, and he refuses it, and the refusal has a date in it and a community in it and no explanation.** The cut is Volume 06's beat and is held. **In this block nobody says the word anywhere else, and no character in Chapter 121 to 123 or 127 to 130 refers to it, and the two lines that say it in 124 to 126 are said once each and are not improved on.**
- **No System panel spam.** At most one short Gatehouse-voice line in the whole batch, in plain trade language, and it must earn its place. Volume 03 Batch 0012 used none. **Nobody dies in this block, nobody is killed, no arch goes down, no glass breaks, and no unnamed person is arranged into a death for effect.** Block 4's flood and its boy in the water are Block 4's and may not be staged or foreshadowed as an event here.
- **Prose.** Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences. Vary the rhythm. Do not repeat *held, steady, plain, counted, breathed, wrote, nodded* as filler, and do not run the whole block on one metronome: **the Batch 0012 review found twenty-three instances of *the whole of* and about thirty of the *about nine seconds* construction across ten chapters, and a later block that repeats either tic at that rate has not varied its rhythm.** Do not deliver lore lectures. Do not let a chapter read as a list of beats: **Batch 0012 shipped thirty-five bolded narrator section headers and ten `---` rules across ten chapters, and that is the ceiling; a new batch should be lower, and the paragraph should carry the turn.** Do not use narrator-voice references to the book outside a physical object — **no chapter numbers, no "in this chapter", no "the volume"** — and that defect was found and removed in Batch 0011 and **came back three times in the first pass of Batch 0012**, so it is watched.
- **Then state the figures and check them.** Item 267 is the standing rule of this project: **a later phase states a figure, checks it, and records the check, and a state file is the least reliable document in this repository — take the fact from the page and then go and look at the page.** **The drift figure of Batch 0012 was written by the forbidden subtraction on the first pass, in the one number in the volume a man can check, and the review caught it.
