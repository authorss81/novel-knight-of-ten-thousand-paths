## What the ten chapters must do

Block 2, per `outline/volume-03.md` §8. **The block's frame: main world to the twenty-second of Rainmonth — twenty-one more days — and then path time. A count answered on the morning he goes out, the Marches' six terms, a line made ready, a crew that goes in seven days earlier on somebody else's calendar, and nine days in a county that has never refused anything, and a refusal that is correct.**

- **Ch 111–112 · the fourth and fifth of Rainmonth, a Monday and a Tuesday · Mara and the returned march, and a boy on the Old Toll abutment.** The road out is made properly, for the second time in the manuscript, and the page is about the difference between a road that is prepared and a road that is agreed. **A fair copy of the column of eleven counties goes out on a scheduled day in a fair hand and is not a message, and a message is not a route.** And the bell, rung, and the far end, and nothing on the coping. **A Monday is the day the far end keeps, so the far end keeps on this one** — the arrangement is intact after Chapter 110's wrong-day experiment, and what comes back is the same sentence the clerk of that assizes copied out, and there is still nothing on the coping that a keeper can be shown.
- **Ch 113 · the ninth of Rainmonth, a Saturday, a market day · Lysa Fenn and a second count, and it is answered on the morning he goes out.** A second proposed count, this time of oath-bound persons in a border county, returnable on a named day, with a space for a figure and no space for a name. **Her answer is the construction again and it works again, and the count of persons is answered with a figure about her own office, and the instrument is spent the moment it is answered, and the office's reply is a bigger count, in a woman's hand, dated, and it is not an instrument. No name in a Crown's hand for the second time.**
- **Ch 114–116 · the twelfth, thirteenth and fourteenth of Rainmonth · the Marches' six terms, and a line made ready.** The six lines on a board in the county's own hand, **and the board is a board in a fen county that four men carried four hundred miles because a board cannot be copied out of and a copy in a book is a thing a court can have.** The true copy already in Mara's day-book is the mechanism and must be used, not restated. **Term four is the price and term six is the door. Reeve Aldis Fenn refuses the witness line in the same words as the ninth of Embermonth, and the clerk enters *the reeve voted*.** **These six lines are already printed in full in Chapter 104 and their wording is fixed — copy it exactly and do not re-word, re-order or improve a line.**
- **Ch 117 · the fifteenth of Rainmonth, a Friday, not a market day · Tovan, and the crew, and a day nobody in Merefen can check.** **Toban's four men go in on the Marches' own road, on the Marches' own calendar, under the guild first hand's hand and the Marches' witness, on a day the Marches named — seven days before the road warden goes, and by a different road, and because a yard takes the work when the work comes.** The yard's book has the fact and not the paper. **Nobody in Merefen is told anything by anybody.**
- **Ch 118 · path time, the Marches, day one, the fourth hour · Aren, and the crossing out.** Two hands, a date, a line, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping the road, made up in about nine minutes with the hour against each of the nine. **And the second route does not open at all inside the ten days, because term six says the line is not to be opened on a day the Marches have not named, and the Marches named a day that is not his.** He comes up with nothing. **He does not know whether the road was refused or merely not kept, there is no procedure for a far side that has not decided, and nobody in either country says the word *failed* — and it is the first crossing in this case that has come back with no answer of any kind.**
- **Ch 119 · path time, the Marches, days two to five · the first sight of the place.** A stepped bank, the smell of a river in spate in the wrong month, and a country that is not a country of fires and does not count anything. **The county's introduction to him is a gauge column** — nine gauges, read twice a day since the first sowing, eleven years, in a hand that is not a clerk's, in a book in a hut at a weir, and nobody has ever asked to see it, and the council has never looked at it, and it is the only account of this county's water that exists. **Eight thousand and thirty-one lines, and the woman who keeps it is the woman of Chapter 104.** And a village where the ground is white, and a woman of about sixty on three hearths who is not on the oath, because the oath is renewed by the men who hold the water and she does not hold water.
- **Ch 120 · days six to nine · the refusal, and the stone, and the terms again.** **The road is refused on the terms he offered and accepted on the terms it offered, and the refusal is one question, asked by a woman who has never been asked anything, in about four seconds, and the answer is that a county that has never refused anything has no word for it and the word it has is the word for a thing that got lost.** And the stone, and the ditch, and a hundred and forty years. **And the notebook, and the first time he writes a drift figure in this volume, and the figure is minus eleven days — the warning is longer than the absence — on a nine-day path interval and a ten-day absence, and it is the first crossing in this case that has come back inside the warning, and he can check it, and what he learns from it is that a good result teaches a man nothing and a bad one teaches him everything, and that the ratio is the thing and not the figure. The half of it he cannot check, because the Marches' month is four rollings and this calendar's month is thirty days and there is not one of those in the other.**

## Hard constraints. Every one of these has been broken by somebody in this project before.

- **The calendar.** The anchor is Chapter 100's own last line: **the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth, YR 313, a Monday.** Every month from there to the twenty-fourth of Hearthmonth is **thirty days**. Chapter dates for this block: **Rainmonth 4 Mon (not a market day) · 5 Tue · 9 Sat (market) · 12 Sun · 13 Mon · 14 Tue · 15 Fri (not a market day) · 22 Fri (not a market day, the crossing out)**. **Merefen's market days are Saturday and Thursday. A chapter opens with both facts and does not reconcile them. A Friday is not a market day in this volume.** Path-side days are the path's own: **no main-world month name, no real-world month name, and no main-world weekday may be spoken or written anywhere in Chapters 118–120, and no rolling may be converted into a day of this calendar by any character.**
- **The money.** **Every money column is added in pence and footed on the page before the total is said aloud.** Do this arithmetic in front of the reader the way Chapter 97 and Chapter 108 do it, line by line, and then say the total. The Chapter 10 column's stated total of eleven marks and fourpence is canon and is not re-derived, and nothing may be scaled out of it. **Chapter 108's contract figures are fixed and are already on the page: ninety working days, four men at eight pence and a boy at four, thirty-six pence a day, three thousand two hundred and forty pence; two hundred and eighty-eight bushels of salt at nine pence, two thousand five hundred and ninety-two; two hundred and sixteen bushels of seed at three pence, six hundred and forty-eight; and six hundred and forty-eight of it payable on the return.** If you need a new figure, derive it in front of the reader and foot it.
- **The banned sums.** **Four thousand and seven hundred and twenty are never added, anywhere, in any form, and the eighth ruled line in the served schedule is not looked at a fifth time and no character refers to it.** No Ash figure may appear on the page in Merefen or in the Marches: **941, 2,450, 2,906, 254 and the 456 are all out of bounds in this block, and the 456 is not on the page in any form and is not derived from anything.** **The Marches' own figures are three and none of them is a hearth, and a Marches figure is never set beside an Ash one or a Merefen one.** Hushwater Reach's near sixty and Hushwater's eighty are never printed together and nobody notices the names match.
- **The words that are out of bounds.** ***Culling Order* does not appear in Volume 03 at all.** ***Gatehouse* may appear only as a measurement standard, quoted from his own notebook, and not as an institution anybody discusses.** *Bind* does not appear. ***Custodian* carries its `bible/terminology.md` meaning and **Aren is not a custodian and may not become one in this volume.**** The marginal instruction, the private-keeper clause, is **printed, quoted verbatim as Chapter 51 has it, cited in a file, and refused about in a gateway — and it may not be explained in a single explanatory sentence by anybody, and Sabin Dain may not be the person who explains it and may not change her want.**
- **The tile on the coping.** **Not touched, not looked at, not explained, not named, and the device is named by nobody.** No flash-back, no origin, no maker, no deathbed, and no scene whose engine is the tile. It may appear only as a coping on a river this county does not have, and it is on the far side of a road that closes in Block 4.
- **The breach.** **Open. Not repaired. Not repaired in a scene, an aside, a document or an apology, and no mistake is to be made available to anybody.** He gives her the numbers and not the morning. She does not ask, and the not asking is entered, and the three rules do their work, and **the concealment is a habit and the habit is the cost, not the subject.** Dain's offer is **not refused in this block** — the refusal is a document in Chapters 148–150 — but a reply that neither accepts nor refuses is in character and is on the page.
- **The brothers.** **The estrangement is not healed and no scene may heal it. `I am not going to re-teach you your own brother` is spent and may not be re-said, improved on, or alluded to as a thing to be taken back.** Each brother still has a page the other does not know about. The yard is the only relationship in the book getting better.
- **The cast.** **`outline/volume-03.md` §14 allows exactly TWO new names first named in prose in this batch and four unfilled slots: Coll Ferrow, 60, the first hand of the Marches bridge guild, who states his terms before he is asked, and who has been on the page un-named for eleven chapters; and Delya Munn, 55, of the Marches council, who renews the oath-stone and is the person who will not lift it, and who is right about the history and wrong about the soil and whom no one in the volume may call a villain.** A character established in `bible/characters.md` who has never had his name on a page counts as one. Nobody else gets a first name in prose. Everyone else is established or addressed by trade.
- **The Marches' reasonableness.** **The council is not a rescue, is not an invasion and is not a symbol. The guild is not a rescue. Nobody is a monster and three of the four parties in this volume are better than he is.** The council refuses outside command because a promise renewed a hundred and forty times is killing everybody who keeps it, and it is right about the history. **The salt is the villain of the volume and it is not a person and is not named as one.**
- **Stage 3.** **Not reached, not attempted, not offered as a plan, not priced, not described by anybody as a thing he could do if he chose.** The cut is Volume 06's beat and is held. In this block nobody says the word.
- **No System panel spam.** At most one short Gatehouse-voice line in the whole batch, in plain trade language, and it must earn its place. Volume 03 Batch 0011 used one, in a notebook, quoting the printed warning. Nobody dies in this block, nobody is killed, no arch goes down, no glass breaks, and no unnamed person is arranged into a death for effect.
- **Prose.** Natural paragraphs, generally two to six sentences. Vary the rhythm. Do not repeat *held, steady, plain, counted, breathed, wrote, nodded* as filler. Do not deliver lore lectures. Do not let a chapter read as a list of beats: **if you find yourself writing more than about four bolded narrator section headers in one ten-chapter stretch, cut some of them and let the paragraph carry the turn.** Do not use narrator-voice references to the book outside a physical object — **no chapter numbers, no "in this chapter", no "the volume"** — and that defect was found and removed once already in this block's predecessor and must not come back.
- **Then state the figures and check them.** Item 267 is the standing rule of this project: **a later phase states a figure, checks it, and records the check, and a state file is the least reliable document in this repository — take the fact from the page and then go and look at the page.** Nineteen figures introduced in Batch 0011 were contradicted in a later chapter and nine of them had to be re-derived.
