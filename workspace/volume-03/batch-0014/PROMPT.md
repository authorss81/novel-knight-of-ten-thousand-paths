## What the ten chapters must do

Block 4, per `outline/volume-03.md` §8. The frame: path time only, the second third of thirteen rollings. The guild proves the waterworks better than the north on the page and not in a stranger's mouth. The river comes in the wrong month. He takes command and is refused. The Ash road closes in writing. The arithmetic stops working.

- **Ch 131–133 · the fifth and sixth rollings · the guild, and the yard.** Coll Ferrow's guild opens a ditch in a day and a half that northern craft would plan a fortnight and a season, without a drawing, from gauge column and memory, twice wrong the same way and honest about it. The yard's four men are in the middle of it on the Marches' own calendar, the best crew in the county, not on the bench, not on the oath, never asked what they swore. The man at the drawknife speaks the fen county's distance aloud once in a gateway and nobody writes it down.
- **Ch 134–136 · the seventh rolling · the river changes course.** The Water comes into a different bed above the seed vault, in the wrong month, in about four hours, and nobody saw it coming. A timber causeway span built on four days' notice saves the near side of the vault and nothing else. The flood runs down the ditch system into the four white villages and salt comes up on the new land too. Nobody dies. A boy of fifteen is in the water for nine minutes and is got out by a man who will not be named, and it is not written in any book, and the boy's master copies the empty space. Do not stage Block 5's span or its limp here.
- **Ch 137–138 · the eighth rolling · the command and the refusal.** He takes command, the old move for the eleventh time, rightly, with bar and timetable at the fourth hour — and the council refuses him in about nine seconds, in the chamber's own form, entered as fact and not defeat, with eleven present and no argument. What is accepted instead is the guild's plan, written before he arrived and better than his, and he hands the road to Ferrow and the guild; the cost of it lives in a document and not in a scene.
- **Ch 139–140 · the ninth rolling · the notice and the arithmetic stopping.** Two things arrive on the same scheduled day and neither is a message. First, a four-line notice down the line from the County of Ash, itemised, checkable, in a fair hand, sent by the woman who keeps the road at that end for a bench that asked for it: entered at the fourth hour on a day this county keeps, containing no name per the fifth of five terms; its fourth line is the county's second term, forty-one words long, a refusal of him, and correct. He calls the second route once and the answer is the notice. Second, the column of eleven counties comes back down the line as fair copy, and the eight stones are not eight of one instrument: some fix field boundaries, some fix shares of water or seed, which are claims and not boundaries, renewed a hundred and forty years by living men. The three empty leaves are counties where nobody survived to swear. The finding, small and true, in two people in a room, out of a column: an oath renewed in public by living men is the only durable consent a rural county can produce, and the only countryside document that names a body, and a body can be served, which is why records gather where oaths are — not because oaths cause anything, but because an oath is a name. The finding that stops the arithmetic is this block's and may not be stated before Chapter 141 in any other form; Chapters 139–140 earn it on the page.
