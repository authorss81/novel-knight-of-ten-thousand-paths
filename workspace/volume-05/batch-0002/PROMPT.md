## The frame

**Main world is not in this block except as a distance.** The last main-world day written is the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, a Sunday, at the ninth hour, when a child of nine opened a road out of a fen county with a flat hand on a cold sill and handed a page to a man of thirty-two, and the page had a date on it and one word under the date, and nobody in that county read it aloud. **The block is inside the city. It opens on the arrival and the first fact about the light, and it ends with a ledger turned round and a child with a mark on one hand and the ninth refusal of four hundred years about to be given by somebody who is nine years old.**

**The path frame, and it is a different frame from the one the block before it used.** **The city keeps a count of days from the last renewal and no months, and a day is a day, and nobody in that city counts them except a man of sixty-six and a man of sixty-one, and the ledger's "three months" is a main-world conversion made once by a person in a notebook and not repeated here.** The first page of this block must say that in a character's mouth and not in the narrative. **No main-world month, no real-world month and no main-world weekday may be spoken or written in any city scene, in any register, including a notebook.** A city day is "the eleventh day" or "the fourth day" or "a count of days" and never a date.

**Path time, the first thirty days of ninety.** The volume's main-world absence is ninety days and the drift ledger for the volume is sixty-nine days of error against a printed three-week warning, about three quarters of the absence and the first narrowing in this case. **These are planning figures. This block has no drift figure to print, no drift figure is printed by this block, no character is told any of it, the printed warning is three weeks and has not changed, and no character in this block uses the word *failed* about a road.**

## What the ten chapters must do

Block 2, per `outline/volume-05.md` §8. **The arrival and the light · the council of eleven and the first refusal and the form of it · the mark, the ledger, and the seventh child · and one person in trade terms saying once what the volume is about.**

- **Ch 211–214 · the arrival, and the first fact about the light.** The light does not turn. It comes up out of the east and it stays where it is and it has stayed where it is for four hundred years, and the city's word for it is a plain noun and the block does not improve on it. **The first page is a man who has been in a valley that kept no hours walking into a city that keeps a standing one and getting the two of them confused**, and it is a page about a man being wrong about something ordinary. A city of about nine thousand people on a river: a wharf that works, a glass works with a furnace in it, a bread oven hot at the second hour and again at the ninth, a council of **eleven, not nine**, and no night. **The trade of the city is recognition: a person who can be named in a market here is a person somebody can be shown, and the city's whole defensive craft is to be unnameable, and a man who has been recognised four ways in one volume is, in this city, a man with a description.** He has a limp and a stick that is for four hundred yards of road and nothing else, and the stick is a problem in a city, and the fact that it is a problem is a trade fact and not a joke at his expense.
- **Ch 215–217 · the council of eleven, and the first refusal, and the form of it.** **The first refusal is not given in a room and is not given by a person and is not the block's best page: it is a form, produced by a keeper whose whole job is producing forms, and it is correct, and it is the eleventh refusal or the first of this volume, and the number is not in it.** **A man of sixty-six has received every refusal the city has given for twenty years and enters them in a book that cannot be shown to anybody, and the reason the book cannot be shown is that showing it is contact, and the reason it is not shown is therefore right, and the effect is that nobody outside the city knows why the city said no to anything, ever, and the city does not know that this is a fact about itself.** **One new name this block, and it is his: Dermit Crane, 66.** His want, in his own words, is to be asked a question that is not that question, **and he says it once, to a stranger, in about eleven seconds, and he is not complaining, and the volume's best page is a man who has noticed a thing nobody noticed and has nobody to tell.**
- **Ch 218–220 · the mark, the ledger, and the seventh child.** **Nobody in the city will explain what the roadmark is.** **A man of sixty-one keeps a ledger of them and has wanted, for most of his working life, to be asked to stop keeping it, and the want is said once in a yard in his own words and is not a speech: Osmund Cusk, 61, whose name was spent in Batch 0001 in the third column of a form in a provincial town and who now has a page.** The seventh child in the volume carries one **and the seventh child is not named, and the reason it is not named is the city's, and the reason the city's reason is the volume's is that the child is the post.** **The block's turn: a person in the city says the thing the volume is about, in trade terms, once, and is not improved on — that a person can be asked twice and a column cannot, and that this city has nine refusals in four generations and not one of them was given by somebody who could be asked again, and that the ninth is the first refusal in this city that a nine-year-old gave and it is the only one that cannot be filed.** That sentence is the block's single most important line and it is said once.
