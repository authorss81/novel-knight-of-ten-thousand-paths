## 4. What the block must contain

**The gift.** The shoulder drying in Longlight, and a party that is not a list. **Say out loud, once, in the mouth of a person in their own trade, what the party is: five people, or six, named by their trades and by nobody else, with no head, with no paper, and with a route they are walking because they each arrived at the top of a lane on their own business.** Longlight is the month a boundary oath-stone is renewed in this world and about nine people in nine counties know that and it is not relevant and somebody says so.

**The test.** Two of the nine households at the far end of that lane come to the line of nine stones with one outsider and no further. **Seven of the nine do not come, and the chapter says why seven and not nine in the households' own trade, and the reason is not cowardice and is not courtesy and is not fear of strangers.** The two say the reason for themselves. **The name budget for this block is one and `outline/volume-15.md` §13 reserves it for the person who keeps the far end of that lane.** Name them once, in the mouth of somebody, or in the narration the way `Dav Rundle` was named, and not twice.

**The cost.** **A crossing that turns out not to be there.** Somebody in the party, or one of the two households, says out loud that the thing the party came to do cannot be done from this side, and has no standing to say it, and is not argued with and is not thanked, and about four people in nine counties think the whole of this county's business is finished and about five do not.

**The hearing of the far side.** It is heard once more, at about the ninth hour on a clear day, and it is heard once only, and **a person says out loud that a thing heard once is not a road and not a place and not a fact anybody can build on**, and nobody agrees with him and nobody argues with him.

**The ordinary half, and it is long.** Two days out and two days back in Thawmonth-to-Longlight mud, the wall at the head of the made road and its nine yards of gap, the drove and its Thursday, the hives and the nine frames and the four weeks that make them back, the market street on a Saturday, the weigh-house, and about four hundred people who have heard four things and have not acted on them.

## 5. The ending of the block

**A completed beat and a new question.** The party comes down the lane. Two of the nine have been up to a line of nine stones and have not been past it. **The block's last page is not the shoulder and is not the stones: it is a person with a trade, at a market, saying one true thing about what a hearing is worth, and a second person answering it in their own trade, and the two of them disagreeing in the open about whether four hundred people who have heard something are in a different position from four hundred people who have not.** Nobody thanks anybody. The reader is left with a question the protagonist cannot ask, because asking it would be sending.

## 7. The house prose, and the four mechanical rules that are not taste

**The voice is in Chapters 0706, 0707 and 0710.** Read them before writing a sentence and match them: short declaratives, a flat cadence, a great many plain negatives, the census sentence where who-understood is the point, and physical work, weather and distance everywhere else.

1. **Zero em dashes, zero en dashes, zero semicolons, zero exclamation marks, zero single asterisks and therefore no italics, zero straight double quotes and zero curly apostrophes.** Speech takes curly doubles. Apostrophes are straight. Per-line bold parity even on every line. Every file ends in a newline.
2. **One footed money column per chapter**: ten items, priced 1 to 41, a mark at forty-eight pence in every conversion, and the coda that names the figures in the chapter that are not in it. **THE CEILING IS 332 PENCE AND IT IS ARITHMETIC: with ten distinct prices drawn from 1 to 41 the largest achievable total is 332, and no column may carry a printed total above it.** **The barred numerals are 14, 20, 26, 33, 38 and 41 and none of them may be a price, an over-figure or a printed total.** Foot the column four ways and check the conversion separately from the sum.
3. **`about` density.** Volume 14's last block ran 23.00 to 27.69 per 1,000 and no page at or above 28.00. **Volume 15 Batch 0001 ran 21.32 to 29.49 and two pages at or above 28.00 and that block does not claim to have met the band.** Aim for 22 to 27 and no page at or above 28. **Keep the census sentence for the two or three beats in a chapter where who understood is the point and write the rest as action, weather or silence.** Chapter length: Volume 15 Block 0001 ran at a mean of 3,240 and Volume 14's volume mean was 3,449. **Do not write below 3,000.**
4. **Every count attached to a printed speech is footed off the printed words beside it**, and where a claim cannot be counted off the words printed next to it, the claim is cut rather than left wrong. This is the single most repeated finding in this manuscript's reviews.
