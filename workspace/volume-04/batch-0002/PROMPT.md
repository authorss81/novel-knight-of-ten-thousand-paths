## What the ten chapters must do

Block 2, per `outline/volume-04.md` §8. **The roll is read, the hand is examined, the mechanism is said once in trade terms by somebody with no stake in it, the ninth generation is asked, and the asking is refused — correctly, in about four seconds, and the refusal is this volume's engine and not a defeat.**

- **Ch 161–163 · the winter book, and a hand nobody can name.** **Iven Sarr, 58**, who has kept it nineteen years, who wanted it read for six of them, who has had two readings and one of them lasted a night, and who at the end of Chapter 160 has said he has a fifth thing and is keeping it for a man who asked and has asked him a question that is not about the book. **The roll is nine generations deep: the day, the mark, the hand, and a fifth column with a name in it on the older spreads only. - **The mechanism, once, in trade terms, by a person with no stake in the Marches: the winter is kept by a working nine generations old, and the working is not a promise, and a promise cannot hold a thing for nine generations, and this is why the ninth generation is asked to keep it every year — because a promise has to be renewed or it stops, and a working does not, and the renewal is the price of the working being a promise at all.** It is said in the language of a valley that builds roads. **It is said once. It is not restated, it is not improved on, it is not summarised by anybody afterwards, and no narrator may underline it.**
- **Ch 164–166 · the two hands, the first asking, and the refusal.** **Wystan Roole, 34**, a bridgekeeper of the ninth generation, sixteen men in a craft guild with no work, who wants the road, and who is right that **a valley which cannot leave is a valley which cannot argue with anybody about anything**, and who said in Chapter 159 that showing three faults in a floor in about four seconds finishes the first day and that he was not going to ask him to do anything. **Nev Treen, 66**, of the eighth generation, who was asked what she swore and answered, and who is the reason the ninth generation has not been: **because she was asked in a room with a witness, and it took nine minutes, and it was the worst nine minutes of her life, and she has told the ninth generation so, and she told them it was survivable, and she was wrong, and she knows she was wrong.** She says that out loud, once, to a stranger, in about nine sentences, and **it is not an apology for anything.** **And the volume's first asking: he asks the ninth generation to be asked what they swore, and they refuse the asking. Not him. The asking.** In about four seconds, from a young man of twenty-two, and the reason is one sentence: **a name in a column is a thing you cannot get out of, and the only person anybody has ever been able to get out of one had written on the back of a leaf, two years before the return, that he did not put his name there.** **And then the second half, which is the volume's argument: they do not refuse the question. - **Ch 167–170 · the trade, the cost of it, and the main world without him.** Ordinary labour in a valley that cannot leave: **stone, timber, a road gang that is not allowed to build a road and builds a byre wall instead, and it is the best wall anybody in that valley has seen and it is the wrong wall.** The first drift figure of the volume, **written in the valley's own register and refused in his own notebook, because the valley keeps no months and he has a number and no frame, and the refusal is not improved on and is not the same refusal as Chapter 158's.** **And the main world runs on without him: a hearing in a room over a weigh-house on the ninth of Rainmonth, YR 314, a Tuesday, at the second hour, not a market day, for a delegation that is not to be sent for and cannot arrive, which stands, and which somebody has to sit in, and the person who sits in it is not him.** Chapters 167–170 may carry that hearing in a room of its own. **They may not put him in it, may not hold it early, may not adjourn it and may not refuse it.**
