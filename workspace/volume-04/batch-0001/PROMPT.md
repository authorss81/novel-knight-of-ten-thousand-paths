## What the ten chapters must do

Block 1, per `outline/volume-04.md` §8. **The frame: main world for six weeks, the crossing out on a Friday, and then the first sight of a valley that is physically stable and politically trapped.**

- **Ch 151 · the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, a Saturday, a market day, the fourth hour · the board under the stair · Aled Rhys. THIS IS THE VOLUME'S FIRST ACT AND IT IS NOT AREN.** A water-barge hand of twenty-seven from the Hearth Marches, in Merefen on the crew's account and paid — **he is not a new name, he is Volume 03's Aled Rhys of Batch 0013, and he said the true thing about his own trade in nine sentences in Chapter 146 and did not vote once** — **comes up two flights of a stair in Weir Street and reads a fair copy off a board under the bench, because anybody coming up two flights may read it, and asks for it to be copied, and nobody has to be asked.** The Archive copies it: **four hundred and eight words and a working on a second sheet**, and a keeper of records reads the whole of it aloud twice before she lets the minute-man have it, and the second reading is for a man from a county of clay who is in the room and is not the person the book is about. **The copy goes to a bench of nine four hundred miles off, which has been asked four times and has said yes four times and is not a bench, and the bench asks it a fifth question and the answer is no, and the reason is in the fair copy.** The road that opens is made of paper and carries nobody. **The man who built it may not write a name in the line's book at the north end — that is the second of the Marches' six terms, verbatim, and no line of the six may be re-worded — and the person who did not put the fifth asking is Reeve Aldis Fenn, and she is the reason the copy was worth sending.** And **the volume's ordinary work in the same chapter: stone in four carts, about forty men, pointing that is never finished on the day it is begun, and a man who gets up onto a deck off the gravel in three moves instead of two, which is a thing nobody in that yard can see, and a stick in his hand that is for four hundred yards of road and for nothing else.** Do not stage a conversation about the volume's argument. Stage a man reading a document and asking for a copy.
- **Ch 152–154 · the main world, six weeks · the county post, the hearing, the count, and the letter.** **A four-line notice comes down a road four hundred miles off and is not an answer to anything, because there was nothing to answer.** **The salt-loft bag is opened by a person who is not Aren** — a clerk, a keeper, a magistrate, or the office itself — **and getting it out is a party receiving a document, and it is not a scene in which he explains himself and it is not a reconciliation and there is no apology in it.** The clause that lands is the one the office left off its own sheet: **the number of persons the offer has been made out to, which is not stated on the page.** A proposed count of carrier obligations in a valley with no writ, returnable on a named day, **with a space for a figure and no space for a name** — beaten in nine minutes with the form's own construction by a magistrate who is frightened of her own answer, and **the office's reply is a bigger count, in a woman's hand, dated, and it is not an instrument. No name in a Crown's hand for the second time in two volumes, and there is still not one since the twenty-seventh of Fallowmonth.** **On the fourth of Longlight a letter arrives from a valley four hundred miles off that is in nobody's book, from a man of fifty-eight who keeps a winter book and has wanted it read by somebody else for six years, and the letter asks for a man who can read a hand and not a man who can cross — and the man who can read a hand is in the room, and it is a keeper of records who reads it and not the road warden, and she is asked to go and does not, and says why in four words.** Do not put the marginal instruction in anybody's mouth as an explanation. It may be cited in a return and refused about in a gateway and **Sabin Dain is not in this batch and may not become the person who explains it.**
- **Ch 155–157 · the seventh and eighth of Goatmonth, the eighth at the fourth hour · out.** **The road out is made properly, for the third time in the manuscript, and the page is about the difference between a road that is prepared and a road that is agreed.** A bell, rung, and the far end, and **nothing on the coping**. **A carrier is a name on a roll and the roll is nine generations deep and it is the only roll in the valley, and the ninth generation is on it from birth, and the woman of about forty-one who keeps the road at that end was struck and the county did not replace her in time, and the road is not there, and nobody is going to be sent to look, because the first of the six terms is that the road is not made to carry a person.** **His return stitch is not secure and the volume may not say why until Block 4, and the not-securing has to be visible in the preparation and not explained.**
- **Ch 158–160 · the valley · sixteen months begin, and the first sight of the place.** **A valley that is not starving.** The Burn runs, the stores hold, the births are normal, **and the winter has been keeping for nine generations and has never once broken, and the oldest person in the valley says it has never been asked whether it is wanted.** **The council meets in a room over a byre and argues about a road.** A young man of the ninth generation is described as a carrier in a way that makes him stop in a doorway. **The first chapter in the volume in which somebody recognises Aren as a carrier, and it is not by name, and it is a woman of about sixty-six who says the word before she has decided to, and he does not know what it means and does not ask — and the not-asking is this volume's first one, and it is a different not-asking from the one in Merefen, and it is going to cost more, and no character may notice the parallel.**
