## Detail on the must-fixes

**B1. The coda (the one that mattered).** The delivered coda put four sets of hands onto an anchor on a bridge "that is not in this world," folded the air "the way water folds," opened the road for about as long as it takes to say a thing that is not a treaty, shut a market that did not reopen, turned a ferry back, and closed on *And not one of them came through.* That is a Gatehouse opening, four days after Aren's decision, on the wrong side of the wall, and it spent Block 5's crossing-out and its failed oath off-stage before either was written. It was in no state file at all, and `current.md` and `batch-summaries.md` both claimed the batch had zero crossings. **The coda is recut, not removed.** The far side now does the thing it promised to do on the eighteenth day: a bench at the lowest water on the morning of the twenty-sixth puts a rule on the ring stone with two men and a clerk, the number is eight inches and was eight inches at the last marking, the woman who keeps that road keeps her hands open and nothing comes. The air does not fold. The only thing Bellweather-Living spends that morning on is whether the road is a road or a door, which it correctly puts off as the second question and defers to daylight. The chapter still ends on **About nine people did**, which is now a hook into Block 5's second question rather than a spent climax. The date the far side defers to was the seventeenth; item 77 makes it the **eighteenth**, and the coda now says the eighteenth.

**B4. The two distances.** The book uses **two hundred miles** for the provincial town and the county file (19 references) and **four hundred miles** for the far city (Ch 18, twice; Ch 34:167; continuity 248). Six references had them crossed. The two worst were in Ch 38, where Mara Vey is four hundred miles away *and* two flights up — she is in Merefen, in the building he is writing in. The other four put the provincial office four hundred miles off and placed Gallow Ford, a main-world bridge a barge mate can lie four days on at a towpath, four hundred miles the other way. All six now use the right figure, and the Ch 40 crisis is stated as what it is — **two kingdoms doing the same work with the same words** — rather than as a distance problem.

**B6. The clock.** Chapter 40 moved from the seventh hour to the eleventh and back twice. One clock now runs forward: **eighth** the arithmetic in the room above the cooper, **ninth** the decision on the sill, **tenth** the yard and the request for a line, **eleventh** the watch house and Rell's step, **twelfth** the salt loft, **fourth hour of the afternoon** the fourth thing written and then Millward, **fifth** the yard line as Tovan actually writes it. Two knock-on corrections: the yard line is now entered as an arrangement with an hour Aren brings down afterwards, because at the tenth hour the lodging had not happened yet; and the closing return up the Old Toll road is "after the yard had shut, with a lamp," not "at the ninth hour," which is the hour the decision was made and is now labelled as such.

**B11. The duplicated lecture.** Chapter 32's taxonomy (memorial / petition / order) and Chapter 35's (petition / subpoena / petrification) opened on the same two sentences and landed on the same two, four days apart inside one batch — the AGENTS.md lore-lecture failure delivered twice. **Chapter 35 keeps the machinery.** Chapter 32 now names the three in four sentences, gets the returnable date out of the paper, and Mara explicitly defers the explanation to a room in a month in front of a man who is a worse lawyer than her copyist. The chapter also had its date arithmetic wrong on the same page (eleven days from the eighteenth, nineteen days in the building, ten days after it is returnable — none of which is true); that is now three days in the building and eighteen days to the ninth.

**B20. The datum.** The reviewer's point was that "a hundred and thirty years of it" and "eight and three-quarters" have no support: the mark has been re-cut by hand three times in thirty-five years, each time taking about a quarter of an inch, and the recorded drift is a quarter of an inch. "Nine inches is four generations" is canon (Ch 31, item 96, thread 40) and was not touched. What changed is that it now has a mechanism, and it is the better one: **the stone is out a quarter of an inch and the nine inches is in the heads.** A man takes a level off the mark and writes it in his own book and does not write *this came off a mark that is a nail's width out*, because the book says *re-cut in hand*; so he rounds it, because a round number is what a book takes; and the next man rounds it again, because he cannot check it either. Four generations of men rounding in their own favour is where the hand's width is, and it is the heads that go on building for a hundred years. This is the same compounding Chapter 31's guild's man already described — a bridge built to the twenty-eighth year's figure and a reach set out to the two hundred and ninety-seventh — so the two chapters now agree instead of contradicting.

**B12, B13, B14, B27 — four documents that did not check out.** Millward's seventh line is word-for-word identical in Ch 35 and Ch 40, twelve days apart, and in Ch 40 it is read as Aren's. Rather than reword one, **Millward now recognises it**: *That is my line. You have written my line.* The repetition becomes characterisation. The guild's clerk's fifth *no reason given* was counted as the fourth and its three words described as seven. The reeve's "seven documents, all of them ours, not one written by a Crown" listed a survey mark on a Crown's schedule and a commendation the bench never entered; the list is now seven bench documents that exist, and she adds the two that are the Crown's as a separate beat, which is a better speech. Rell's four gifts included a line about a mark in a coin that does not parse and a seat handed to him on the tenth of Longlight in a scene that does not contain it; the gifts are now the number of the deck, the answer to his own custody question on the step, a seat he went and looked at unasked, and the county clerk's refusal.

**B26. Mara's four days.** Chapter 38 builds its cost on her finding out "in about a month"; Chapter 40 has her knowing since the eighteenth and confronting him on the twenty-second. The four days of him holding a Crown's letter and not coming up the stair is the whole beat, so **Ch 38's estimate is now the thing that is wrong** — *Inside a week, he thought, and he was wrong by four days* — and Chapter 40 says how a postman on Weir Street at the fourth hour in front of her own window gave it to her on the day it arrived, which also removes an unexplained knowledge. While in Chapter 40, "There is a letter in this building" contradicted her own day-book entry two pages later ("not received into this office and is not held in this office") and is now "in this town."

- **The deliberate *held* triple** at `36:125` — *a thing that is held by nobody … is a thing that was not held. Not unlawfully. **Not held.*** — and the phrase *held in use among a company of persons* in its quoted Crown documents. The nit was about density; the one literal hand-holding was reworded and the emphasis was left, because it is the chapter's best beat.
- **Chapters 20 and 30's "no fresh Steppe glass north of Merefen before Longlight."** Circular in Ch 34 and Ch 40, which are set on the second and the twenty-second of Longlight; **forward-looking and correct** in Ch 20 and Ch 30, which are set in Greenmonth. Only the circular instances were changed.
- **Ch 40 at 10,606 words.** The two blocks the review named were cut — the opening arithmetic, where three figures already given in Chapter 31 were collapsed into a single *three figures have not moved since the twentieth of Greenmonth* line, and the closing inventory, where seven dramatised things were replaced by the hand on the sill, the four days and the second place on the bridge — plus a redundant ring-stone paragraph. Chapter 40 is **10,585 words**. It remains the longest in the manuscript. It is the volume's climax, it carries the batch's chain, the lending, Millward's cost and the coda, and further cutting would start removing load-bearing material. Recorded as a known fact rather than an open defect.
