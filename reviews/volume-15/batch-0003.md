## Finding 1 — HIGH — the hand-off prompt misstated its own calendar, in the sentence that instructs the writer how to derive the datelines. **Applied.**

`workspace/volume-15/batch-0004/PROMPT.md` §3 supplied its own correct premises and then drew a wrong conclusion from them: Fallowmonth 30 = day 2525 and Embermonth the first = day 2526, both correctly stated, followed by *"The block therefore runs across the end of Harvestmonth and the whole of Fallowmonth and carries one month boundary, at day 2496."*

**Block 0004 is days 2491 to 2550 and it carries TWO month boundaries, at day 2496 and at day 2526, and it ends a quarter into Embermonth, not at the end of Fallowmonth.** The error is the worst shape this project has, because a wrong instruction is obeyed more reliably than a missing artifact, and it lands on the block that is meant to write the volume's climax, with the error to be inherited by Batch 0005, which inherits the datelines by name.

**The paragraph also printed no Embermonth weekday at all**, in the section that instructs the writer to *"write the ten datelines down before you write the first chapter and reconcile every one of them against the chain."*

**Repaired, and §3 now carries the whole window in one place.** Both boundaries are named and the count is emphatic. The derived chain is given to the block's last day: **Harvestmonth 30 = day 2495, a Thursday and a market morning; Fallowmonth the first = day 2496, a Friday, not a market morning; Fallowmonth 30 = day 2525, a Saturday, a market morning; Embermonth the first = day 2526, a Sunday, not a market morning; Embermonth 25 = day 2550; Embermonth 30 = day 2555, outside the block.** The block's two ends are now stated as a Sunday and a Wednesday, and the consequence is spelled out: **the last market morning inside the block is day 2546, Embermonth the twenty-first and a Saturday, so a chapter printed on the block's last day cannot carry a market-morning dateline.** The sixteen market mornings in the sixty days are listed — 2495, 2497, 2502, 2504, 2509, 2511, 2516, 2518, 2523, 2525, 2530, 2532, 2537, 2539, 2544, 2546 — together with the two month firsts, both non-marketing. The thirty-day-month prohibition was widened from Fallowmonth alone to Embermonth as well, since the block now runs into it.

**The review's own statement of the last day's weekday was wrong, and this is the second of the two errors in the review.** It gave *"Embermonth 25 = day 2550, a Saturday market morning."* **Day 2550 is a Wednesday and is not a market morning.** Derived from the anchor, not asserted: 2550 minus 2310 is 240, and 240 mod 7 is 2, so day 2550 is two days after the Monday at day 2310, which is a Wednesday; and the county's two market days are Saturday and Thursday. **The repair did not carry the review's figure into the prompt.** This is recorded rather than passed over because it is the same failure the finding is about, one level up: a weekday reached by carrying a number instead of deriving it.

## Finding 2 — HIGH — the same error was already standing in state, which is how the prompt inherited it. **Applied, and in two more places than the review reported — one of which is where the error began.**

`state/continuity.md` item 1642, the item the Batch 0004 prompt names as the source of the whole calendar, read *"The block crosses one month boundary, at day 2436."* **Block 0003 crosses two, at day 2436 and at day 2466**, and the item contradicted itself within four sentences by also recording that two of its own ten chapter days are firsts of a month. **A third sweep found the origin, and it is the prompt that wrote the block under review.** `workspace/volume-15/batch-0003/PROMPT.md` §3 read *"Longlight 30 is day 2435 and Rainmonth the first is day 2436, a Monday and not a market morning. Harvestmonth the first is day 2466 and is a Wednesday. The block therefore runs across the whole of Longlight and the first half of Rainmonth and carries one month boundary."* **That is wrong on both halves: Block 0003 runs across the whole of Longlight, the whole of Rainmonth and the first twenty-five days of Harvestmonth, and crosses two boundaries.** The prompt also omitted the step from Rainmonth the first to Harvestmonth the first entirely, so it never stated Rainmonth's length.

**So the error did not merely propagate forward into Batch 0004's prompt. **All three instances are corrected and no fourth of that kind exists** once the Batch 0003 prompt is counted. The three Volume 13 prompts that carry a *"one month boundary"* claim are each **correct** and were checked rather than assumed: Block 0002 runs days 1873 to 1914, from the eighth of Frostmonth to the nineteenth of Rainmonth, and crosses exactly one; Block 0004 runs days 1957 to 1998, from the second of Thawmonth to the thirteenth of Mudmonth, and crosses exactly one; Block 0005 runs days 1999 to 2040, from the fourteenth of Mudmonth to the twenty-fifth of Greenmonth, and crosses exactly one. **The phrase is not itself the defect, which is why it had to be checked in each file rather than swept for.**

**Nothing about the written block changes.** The ten datelines on the page are correct, two of them are firsts of a month, and both were written as firsts of a month; **the writer got the block right in spite of the frame, not because of it**, and the repair to the prompt is a record of what the block should have been told.

## What this review adds to the pattern, and it is a small and an ordinary thing

**The prose was clean and the records were not, again.** Fifteen mechanical and numeric checks came back clean, including a calendar re-derivation, ten money columns that foot, and fifteen barred words at zero. **Every one of the seven findings was in a hand-off or a state file, and four of the seven were one sentence each.** No finding changed a plot, a date on the page, a money figure, a name or an ending.

**But the finding that matters is that the same wrong sentence was standing in three files, and the review found one of the three.** A calendar error entered the Batch 0004 prompt, and it was inherited rather than caught, because the prompt's own §3 named `state/continuity.md` item 1642 as its authority and item 1642 held the identical claim in the identical form. **A derived figure was written into a prompt, and the prompt pointed at the record, and the record pointed at nothing that could contradict it.** That is a closed loop, and no amount of care inside the prose would have caught it. The three copies are now corrected together.

**And the review's two factual errors were both about figures and both were caught by going to the page.** It put a phrase in the wrong file, and it gave a wrong weekday for the day it was using to prove a point about wrong weekdays. **The repair carried none of them forward, and the calendar in the prompt is now derived from the anchor and printed to the block's last day** so that the next writer does not have to re-derive the half of the chain that was missing and will not have to guess the weekday of the block's last day at all.

**What was deliberately left alone, and why.** The twenty-five or so years of the standing prose debt in Volumes 09 to 12 is out of scope and is recorded as such in `state/current.md`. Volume 14's five closed blocks with no review record is a records gap for a phase that says it is closing that gap. The phase ledger is controller-owned. The sixteen demonstrative meta-language instances in closed volumes are a real defect with a named owner and no owner has been assigned to a batch that would write them. **Recording each of these with an owner is the whole of what a repair pass may honestly do about them, and a missing artifact is visible while a false claim about one is not.**
