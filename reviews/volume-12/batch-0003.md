# Review: Volume 12, Batch 0003 — the third pass over Chapters 571 to 580

**Reviewed artifact:** commit `ff7ca89` "novel: save writer work batch-0003", a repair pass over a finished ten-chapter block, not a draft. Four chapter files, five state files and the Batch 0004 prompt. **Findings: eight, all real, all applied. Two in the prose, six in the records and the prompts, and one of the six is a flag for the workflow owner on a controller-owned file that this project did not write.** No plot beat moved, no chapter was restarted, no dateline, weekday, money total, name or ending moved, and the block's plan is untouched.

**The review itself ran twice and the first run is not reconstructed.** The session that produced `logs/batch-0003.review.log` began as an exploration, found that the word counts did not agree between two instruments, chased that for several turns, established that the disagreement was an artifact of its own command rather than a defect in the files, and then returned the eight findings below. The gap between the two is recorded here rather than papered over, and the log is gitignored, which is the reason this file exists.

## What the review verified, and did not touch

- **Every word count reproduces on the stated basis.** Block 0003 = 18,234 before this repair, per chapter 1774 / 1769 / 2017 / 1726 / 1957 / 1680 / 1823 / 1873 / 1980 / 1635; manuscript 1,644,389 in 580 chapters; Volumes 01 to 11 = 1,588,852; Volume 12 = 55,537. The review's own first suspicion was a 4-word discrepancy and it was the reviewer's instrument that was wrong; see finding 5.
- **The sitting repair is chronologically sound.** The cycle runs bar → blossom → set → swell → pick. The observed run swell → pick → bar → blossom → set is a valid rotation, and 0567 and 0568 in the pick sitting correctly precede 0571 to 0573 in the bar sitting, 0574 and 0575 in the blossom sitting, and 0578 to 0580 in the set sitting. The three swell-to-pick repairs point backwards correctly.
- **The 0579 removal of a witness is grounded** in 0567's placement of the man of about fifty-one at a terrace from about the two-mile mark until the seventh hour, with the hand on the lip at about the fifth hour.
- **The 0574 addition is supported by the page.** 0568 has the woman of about sixty-four tell Aren Kest *I know when my day is finished because I count*, which is the disclosure 0574 has her disclose afterwards, and the woman of about thirty-eight is nowhere in 0568.

## Findings and dispositions

**1. The next dispatch would have overwritten finished prose, and the prompt did not say so. Applied, and it is the finding that mattered.** `workspace/volume-12/batch-0002/` had been marked `.done` by the controller, so the runner's first unsatisfied prompt was `batch-0003`, whose ten chapters were already on disk. Its prompt opened *This run writes Chapters 571 to 580 and nothing else* and acknowledged only that 551 to 570 were finished prose; it asserted the opposite of the truth about its own chapters. The mitigation — count the chapter files first — lived in `state/current.md`, `state/continuity.md` 1381 and 1382 and thread 374, none of which the prompt directed a writer to read. The mitigation was also the same run's mitigation, since 51 of the 53 batch directories under `workspace/` carry a `.done` or a `.retired` and only 0003 and 0004 carry none. **Applied: `workspace/volume-12/batch-0003/PROMPT.md` now opens with five paragraphs that state the chapters are written, name the file range and per-chapter figures, name the missing marker and the census, direct the writer to count the files and read the Batch 0004 prompt first, forbid writing 551 to 600 and forbid creating a second prompt for a batch that has one, and say what the run is for instead. The brief below the warning is untouched.** The same one-paragraph warning was added to the Batch 0004 prompt, whose chapters genuinely do not exist yet, because a prompt that is about to be fired is the only place a warning costs nothing. Recorded at `state/continuity.md` 1392 and `state/open-threads.md` 383.

**2. An undocumented prose change in Chapter 0574. Applied to the records, not to the page.** The pass changed four chapter files and its narrative said *five sentences in three of Batch 0003's chapters* and *no other sentence was touched*. Both claims were false: 0574 had gained a speech of about sixty words. The same claim was in `state/current.md`, in the batch summary, in the Chapter 574 summary and in the Batch 0004 prompt — and the first two are the files a writer reads first. An auditor checking 0573, 0578 and 0579 would never have learned that 0574 was in the pass. **Applied: the true count is seven changes in four files — two sentences in 0573, one word in 0578, three sentences in 0579, and one added speech in 0574 — and it is now printed in all five places, with the welded-speech repair and the disclosure described where they happened.** The general form is at `state/continuity.md` 1388: a pass that changes more than it describes has not read its own diff, and a record that describes a smaller number than the diff is not conservative, because a successor reading it will look for five and stop.

**3. That addition is a character disclosure with no continuity item. Applied.** It establishes that the woman of about sixty-four told a stranger *I count* and learned only that morning that there was anything else in it, which changes what a habit of thirty years has been and lands two sittings and about eight days after the scene it recalls. Items 1382 to 1387 covered the other repairs; none covered 0574. **Applied: `state/continuity.md` 1389 is the disclosure as canon, with what a successor may not do with it, and the chapter summary now carries the disclosure in the form the page gives it.** The finding was also the one that let the review check the addition for support, and it held: 0568 is the scene, 0568 has the words, and the woman of about thirty-eight is not in 0568.

**4. The recorded rationale misdescribes the defect it repaired. Applied to the records; the prose repair stands.** `state/current.md`, `state/continuity.md` 1383 and the batch summary all said the swell sitting is *earlier in the year than the pick sitting* and that *as printed the valley remembered an event before it happened*. Those two cannot both be true, and the second is wrong: a reference from the swell sitting to a day dated in the pick sitting points backwards, so the printed text mis-dated a day, and nothing remembered anything before it happened. The three speakers were also consistent with each other and wrong only against `chapter-0567.md`, so the block rule cited at 1383 — that no character converts one sitting into another — was not what was violated; the writer was. **Applied: 1383 is marked superseded in part and `state/continuity.md` 1390 states the correction, with the general form — the explanation of a repair is itself a claim about the book, and it is the one claim in a repair that no instrument checks and no reader of the chapters will ever contradict.** The same correction is in `state/current.md`, in the batch summary and at thread 379.

**5. A recount-instrument hazard in four files nobody had touched. Applied, and the review's own first suspicion turned out to be its own error.** Four chapter files carried no trailing newline: `volume-06/chapter-0271`, `volume-06/chapter-0275`, `volume-10/chapter-0497`, `volume-10/chapter-0500`. Per-file counting — the basis every state file in this repository states — was correct at 1,644,389, and a concatenated `cat` of the five hundred and eighty files returned 1,644,385, four short, because the last token of each of the four was glued to the first token of the next. **The hazard was not the four words. It was that the standing rule in this project is that a figure which does not reproduce is the error, so the next pass would have been licensed to spend itself reconciling a four-word discrepancy in four files in closed volumes, and the likelier outcome is that it would have moved a figure in five state files and a prompt instead. Applied: all four files now end in a newline, no prose was touched in any of them, no per-file word count moved, and the two bases now agree.** Recorded at `state/continuity.md` 1391.

**6. Two smaller record defects. Both applied.** (a) The Chapter 574 summary read *stood where the other woman is standing* where the page reads *stood where you are standing*, an inserted word that changes the referent; the summary now follows the page. (b) The added speech in 0574 had been welded onto the end of the speech before it inside a single line, which passes a bold-parity gate at three pairs and reads as a patch; it now has its own paragraph and its own attribution, which is the book's form for a second turn and the only form in which two named women in one scene can be told apart without counting the turns.

**7. The review output was not landing in `reviews/`. Applied.** There was no `reviews/volume-12/` directory at all, and none for `volume-08` either, so Volume 12's review left no durable record and the findings existed only in a gitignored log. **This file is the record. `reviews/README.md` is indexed.** The Volume 08 gap is real and is not repaired here, because reconstructing a review nobody performed is the failure this directory was created to stop.

**8. `state/phase-ledger.json` is not tracking reality. Flagged, not written.** It reads `currentPhase: phase-000-bootstrap`, `status: planned`, `attempts: 0`, `range: null` and `actualModel: null` against five hundred and eighty chapters and three hundred and seven commits carrying the `novel:` prefix. Phase selection, retry and timeout read that file. **It is controller-owned and this project did not touch it. The finding is flagged to the workflow owner and recorded at `state/continuity.md` 1392 and `state/open-threads.md` 383, where a successor will meet it.**

## Two more, found by the repair while checking what the review had already checked

**Neither was in the review's eight, and both are this project's standing class, and both are in the state files rather than in the book.**

**9. A rate certified on one basis and carried on another.** The `about` count in four of the five files the third pass wrote is printed as 494 case-insensitive and 491 lower-case at 27.09 and 26.98 per 1,000, on a stated basis of *a case-insensitive whole-word numerator over a whitespace denominator*. The numerator was taken by splitting on the space alone, which glues a line-opening `about` onto the token that ended the line before it, so the pair does not reproduce on the basis the file names; on that basis the figures are 500 and 496 in 18,240 words at 27.41 and 27.19, and the space-split count is 495. The Batch 0004 prompt carried the same rate in a third form, 495 in 18,121 words at 27.32. **The number is not wrong and the sentence describing it is, and a successor who re-derives it on the stated basis gets a different number and concludes the file is broken.** The honest pair is now printed everywhere with the method named, and the old pair is recorded as a first printing rather than quietly replaced. This is the class `reviews/volume-10/batch-0001.md` already records, where a rate was certified on one basis, carried on another, and three files quoted a figure that was neither.

**10. An index that pointed two runs of items at the wrong volume.** `state/current.md`'s rolling-window paragraph, the one that tells a writer which run of continuity items and which run of threads belongs to which batch, labelled 1318 to 1327 and 319 to 327 as Volume 12's verification pass. The section headers those numbers point at are both headed Volume 11. **Corrected in place with the correction named in the file, and it is the purest instance of the class in this whole review: a sentence in a state file that is wrong about the book, in a repository where the book is on disk to be read, and no recount, no sweep and no diff will ever see it.**

## What this review adds to the pattern

**It is the fourth review of a repair rather than of a batch in this project, and the second of one in this volume, and the target changed nothing about the failure modes.** Six of the eight findings are records: an undocumented change, a record asserting a smaller change than the diff, a disclosure with no canon item, a rationale that described the defect backwards, a stale marker claim, and a figure whose stated basis does not reproduce. Two are prose-adjacent, and both are in a paragraph a previous pass added. **No finding was a plot defect and none required a story change, which is the shape `reviews/README.md` states for this project as a whole: no review has found a plot defect in the delivered prose, and every finding that has changed a chapter has been a continuity, arithmetic, formatting or voice slip.**

**The two findings worth keeping are 2 and 4, and they are the same finding at two scales.** A repair pass that changes seven things and describes five has not read its own diff; a repair pass that explains its own repair in a way the repair's own logic contradicts has not read its own explanation. **In both cases the prose was right or nearly right and the sentence about the prose was wrong, and in both cases the sentence about the prose is what the next writer quotes.** That is the tenth instance in this project of a figure or a claim being asserted in a state file and inherited as a rule, and it is the one class that no recount, no sweep and no diff check will ever catch, because the file that carries it is not wrong in a way a number can be wrong in.

**And finding 1 is the cheapest and the most expensive in the same pass.** A prompt that is about to be fired is read by exactly one person, once, and it is the only artifact in this repository whose contents are the last thing standing between a dispatcher and a finished block. It had been wrong about its own chapters since the batch was written. It now says so, in the first four paragraphs, in the imperative, before the brief.
