## The structural finding: four ledgers had grown to 4.8 MB

**`state/continuity.md` was 1.94 MB and about 373,000 words, growing about 1,100 words a chapter, which is roughly a third of the 1.14-million-word manuscript.** `chapter-summaries.md` was 1.19 MB, `batch-summaries.md` 1.03 MB, `open-threads.md` 661 KB. Between them they were being carried whole into every phase, which is the exact opposite of what AGENTS.md asks a rolling summary to be. The cause is visible in the git history: the last writer commit added thirty-one lines to `continuity.md` and deleted none.

**The repair put all four on a rolling window.** Each now keeps the global material and the whole of the current volume and nothing older, and everything older is in `state/archive/` as a **byte-for-byte copy**, verified against the pre-repair file by exact slice comparison. The four files went from **4,815,259 characters to 506,131**, and all 869 continuity items, all 70 chapter-summary sections, all 60 thread sections and all 109 batch-summary sections are still findable under the numbers they always had.

**Three things about the window are worth stating because they are the parts that are easy to get wrong.** First, **nothing was renumbered and nothing was deleted, only moved**, so an item number means the same thing in the archive as in the live file. Second, **four global sections sat at byte offsets that would have swept them into the archive** — `## Ending constraints` and `## Knowledge control` in `continuity.md`, and `## Final-volume questions` and `## Planning status` in `open-threads.md`, all of them after the first batch section rather than before it. They are live in the live files now, and they are still in the archives too, because the archives are copies. **A global section appearing in both places is harmless and a global section appearing in neither is not, and an offset-based cut will always get this wrong.** Third, **the window does not move again until the Volume 07 close**, because a close audits the volume it is closing and needs the volume whole.

## Two things left standing, with the reason

**`state/phase-ledger.json` is inert.** After four completed batches it still reads `phase-000-bootstrap`, `planned`, `attempts: 0`. **It is a controller file owned by GitHub Actions and this pass did not touch it and must not**, and it is recorded here instead: if the workflow is not writing it, the ledger is not tracking phase state, and AGENTS.md's own batch checklist calls for a phase-ledger update that is not happening. **That is a question for whoever owns the workflow and not a finding against the prose or the plan.**

**The chapter files are unchanged, deliberately.** Every finding was in a record, and the reviewer that produced findings 1 and 2 was reviewing the prompt, not the chapters. Repairing prose that passed every check it was given would have been motion, and the standing rule this project has arrived at across eleven reviews — that a repair pass is not a safer thing to review than a batch — cuts both ways: a batch with nothing wrong in its prose does not need a repair pass.

## Verdict

**The twelfth data point, and the cleanest prose and the worst bookkeeping this project has produced in the same batch.** No plot defect, no continuity defect on the page, no craft finding at all, and a manuscript that is measurably better controlled than the batch before it. **And six findings, every one a self-report about the manuscript that did not survive re-measurement, three of which the review itself did not catch.**

**What this batch adds to the pattern is the cheapest and the most dangerous class yet, and it is a new one.** Eleven reviews have found the same three things: a continuity slip, a false claim about an artifact, an unmeasured number. This one found **an unmeasured number in the batch's own report of the batch, escalated into a mandate for the block after it, in a prompt whose §6 forbids exactly the confusion that produced it.** A wrong number in a state file is caught by the next person who counts. A wrong number in the *opening warning* of the next block's prompt is obeyed, quoted, and cited as precedent, and by the third block it is a tradition.

**The rule that would have prevented all six is already written down in this project, and the batch had it in front of it: a figure whose basis was not recorded is a figure nobody can check including its author.** Five of the six were counted once and copied. The sixth was counted and the count was then inflated into a headline, and the inflation is the new part. **A batch that reports a sweep must report the number it got, and a block that inherits a number from a predecessor must re-count it before it reasons from it, and the correction belongs where the number was so the next reader sees both.**
