## Finding 1 — an impossible count, repeated across a chapter boundary. **Applied.**

`chapter-0619.md` struck the page's tally as *three spaces with nothing at all in them, and **about four of those three** are refusals and one of them has not been reached*. Four of three. `chapter-0620.md` repeated it — *about four of those three blanks are men who read it and would not, and one of them is a line the page has not reached*. Both instances were **pre-existing**, present in `HEAD~1`, which means the pass that claimed to have *verified every instrument* and found exactly one real defect missed both.

**Repaired to two of three in both chapters.** The hedge goes with it, and that is not tidying: `outline/volume-13.md` §15 item 13 states that **the word *about* may not stand in front of a figure somebody in the scene could have counted.** Two refusals and one unreached line is the state the page is actually in, and it is the state the block's own load-bearing mechanic requires — `chapter-0612.md` gives the argument in a woman's mouth (*if a refusal is a blank and a blank is a page nobody has got to yet, then a road that everybody refuses has been shut by a printing press*) and `chapter-0613.md` gives it as the finding (*a man who read that page and said no comes back as a blank*). The unreached line is what makes the blank unreadable, and it is kept.

**Where the review went further than the file can follow, and the reason is recorded rather than argued.** The review held that both pages *contradict the plan*, because §8 Block 2 says *three refusals and two signatures and one road that agrees*. **It does not, and the volume says why on the page.** The plan's three refusals are the **contested figure**, not the page's state: `chapter-0613.md` has a man of about thirty-one from a road office say *you have three refusals* and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet answer *I have three blanks, and I cannot tell the difference, and neither can you, and that is the point and you have just walked into it*. Three blanks on the page, three refusals in a road office's mouth, and the volume's whole subject is that the second is an inference the first cannot support. Correcting the page to three refusals would have deleted the argument to satisfy the sentence that states the argument's target.

## Finding 2 — a meta-narrative frame break, and there are two of them, not one. **Applied.**

`chapter-0619.md` said *this is the third time in about six weeks that a person **in this manuscript** has said a true thing*. **This is the second instance in Volume 13 and the sweep that found the first did not find this one**, because the review ran a literal search for `this manuscript` and not a sweep of the frame-break class. ## Finding 4 — the flagged sentence told the reader the significance instead of dramatising it. **Applied, and the rewrite removed a second defect the review did not name.**

The review flagged `chapter-0619.md`'s *which is the fourth time a figure has gone down that lane in a coat in about six weeks, and the first three were a number of barrows, a note on the back of a copy, and a sheet that said which line came back with what in it*, correctly, as exposition in place of an event. **The clause was also impossible, and in a way no count could see.** The third item in that list is the sheet described at `chapter-0620.md` — which is **the following day**. Chapter 0619 claimed to be the fourth journey and listed as already past one that had not yet been written.

**The rewrite removes the forward reference by making the man decline the tally, which is what he is.** What he wrote on the eighteenth is not a figure but *a thing a person had asked for*, and a man established at line 69 as *a man who has spent a month watching one figure get made out of another figure* does not put the two in one line. **This makes `chapter-0620.md` correct as it already stood**, and that chapter is untouched: *which is the third time **a number** has gone down that lane in a coat in about six weeks, and the first two were a number of barrows and a note on the back of a copy*. **The distinction the repair draws is the one 0620 was already drawing and could not be seen to be drawing** — 0620 counts numbers, and a request is not a number. **A repair that made 0619 agree with 0620 by renumbering 0619 would have destroyed a correct page to fix an impossible one.** **The repair's own first draft dated the barrows journey to the ninth, on the strength of `chapter-0614.md`'s dateline; that was wrong, because the barrow number on the page is `chapter-0604.md` and 0614's is a boy's report of a habit, and the draft was corrected before anything was recorded. ## What this review adds to the pattern

**The eleventh data point, the twelfth, the thirteenth and the fourteenth all found the same three classes — continuity, arithmetic and voice — found no plot defect in the delivered prose, and each found one new thing about the instruments rather than about the pages. This one is the first whose new thing is that a figure can be right and unwritable-down, and it is the first in which the review's own central sub-claim was declined.**

Three things here are new.

**One: a pass that verified every instrument did not read the page, and the two defects it missed were both pre-existing and both in the chapter it had just rewritten.** It found a real outline violation in `chapter-0619.md` and repaired it well, and it certified that file clean of everything else. Both defects it missed were in that same file, and the second of them — the coat tally reaching forward to a sheet written the next day — is not a slip of arithmetic but an **event ordered wrongly across a chapter boundary**, which is the class no count in this project can reach. **Verifying an instrument against a file is not the same as reading the file, and the difference shows up in the one file the pass had open.**

**Two: a count of *N of those M* is a machine checkable defect and this project had never written it down as one.** The `about` before it is separately forbidden by §15 item 13, so the phrase was doubly barred and was still printed twice, on two consecutive days, by a block whose own records discuss the blank/refusal ambiguity at length. **The check is one regular expression and it is now written into the successor prompt as a standing first-move sweep.**

**Three, and it is the general form of the other two: the review found `this manuscript` and did not find `this book`, in the same volume, because it searched for the string it had been given rather than for the class.** `reviews/README.md` recorded in Volume 11 Batch 0005 that **a rule about register is not a rule about vocabulary**, and this is the first case in this project of the same class being missed by searching for a token rather than for the frame. **A sweep that names its patterns is a sweep that will keep finding the one it named; a class needs a class.**
