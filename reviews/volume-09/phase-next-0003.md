## What this review adds to the pattern

**Across eleven batches, no review has found a plot defect in the delivered prose.** Every finding that changed a chapter has been a continuity, an arithmetic, a formatting or a voice slip, and every finding about a record has been a record about a record.

**What this one adds is a new instance of the cheapest and most frequent class, an unmeasured number, and it has a twist the project has not seen before: the number was wrong in a file that was right.** Continuity 1029 said ten money columns and prompt §4 said eleven, and the file a writer reads first and trusts was the one that was wrong. `state/current.md` said thirty-three recurring totals and the list it points at holds thirty-two. **`outline/volume-09.md` §5.4 said the round that carries nine hundred sheets a month runs two rounds a year, and the round runs twice a month, and the error was in the plan and had been inherited by the prompt and was about to be inherited by the page.** This is the inverse of the Volume 07 Batch 0005 finding and it is the same finding: **a close or a block that audits itself by comparing its own state files against each other will find nothing at all, because the file with the right figure is the one nobody compares to.**

**And the second thing it adds is the plainest possible statement of why a repair pass needs its own review, in a block that had one anyway.** Finding 10 is a repair that was applied to one of two instances of its own defect nine lines apart and recorded as complete, and nothing about it was visible to any instrument in the project, and the only thing that caught it was a reviewer reading Chapter 404 against the state file that claimed Chapter 404 was clean. **A repair pass is not a safer thing to review than a batch. It has the same defects on a smaller sample, and it has one the batch does not: it is trusted.**
