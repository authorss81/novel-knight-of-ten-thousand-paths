# Volume 05 — Outline phase 0001 — review of the Volume 04 close and the Volume 05 outline

**Seven findings, all applied, none requiring a change of plot, and two of them would have put a false fact on the page in the first ten chapters.** This file is the durable record `reviews/README.md` asks for; the reviewer's own output is in a gitignored log, and this is the sixth time that has cost the project its reasoning. Every figure below was re-counted on the page by the repair pass before it was acted on, and none of the reviewer's numbers were taken on trust.

**Scope reviewed.** The Volume 04 close phase and its product: `outline/volume-05.md`, `outline/volumes/volume-04-close.md`, `workspace/volume-05/batch-0001/PROMPT.md`, and four state files. **No prose exists past Chapter 200 and none was written, so this is a review of a plan and not of a batch, which is the shape of the thing the reviewer was asked to look at.** The reviewer verified the phase's arithmetic against the two hundred chapters on disk and found the manuscript totals, the five batch columns, the `nine` and `about` ledgers, the four device sweeps and the crossing arithmetic all exact. ## F1 — High. The batch's primary length instruction points at batches that do not exist

**Found.** `workspace/volume-05/batch-0001/PROMPT.md:3` and `outline/volume-05.md` §15 item 14 both told the writer to *write full scenes at the length of Volume 04's Batches 0011 and 0012*. **Volume 04's batch directories are `batch-0001` to `batch-0005`; batches 0012 to 0015 are Volume 03's, and no `batch-0011` exists anywhere in `workspace/` — 0011 is the Volume 02 close, stored as `volume-02-close`.** The writer's main length calibration was therefore unresolvable, and the fallback in the same sentence was a different volume's blocks.

**Applied.** Both instances retargeted to Volume 04's Batches 0001 and 0002, which the prompt now gives with their counted figures of 33,045 and 33,385, and the five-column list is relabelled as Volume 04's five batch columns rather than as the two batches just named. The batch count figure was independently confirmed against the directory listing.

## F2 — High. A Wednesday was booked as a market day

**Found.** `PROMPT.md:52` listed *the fourth (Wed, market)* of Embermonth. **Fallowmonth 25, YR 314 is day 0 and a Monday, and the month is carried at thirty days, so Embermonth 1 is day 6 and a Sunday, and Embermonth 4 is day 9 and a Wednesday.** Markets in this county are Saturday and Thursday, twice a week, a rule the same sentence's own first clause states, and the same list correctly marks Embermonth 5 as a Thursday market and Embermonth 7 as a Saturday market. The defect was a market on a day the county does not trade, in the block whose own instruction is *a chapter does not get to want a day*, and on a day that carries the bench minute, the yard's book and the money columns.

**Applied, and the arithmetic was checked both ways before the change.** The list now reads *the fourth (Wed, not a market day)*. **This was not a cosmetic fix: the volume's market count depends on it. The four markets before the crossing are days 3, 5, 10 and 12, and a market on day 9 would have made five and broken the `four before the crossing and fourteen after the return` line in §17's crossing table.** The §17 table was re-footed from day 0 forward and holds at 43 market days in 151 days, 4 before the crossing and 14 after the return inclusive of the return morning.

## F3 — Medium. A fifth-block count was presented as a volume figure

**Applied.** The basis is now named in the line — Volume 04 Batch 0005, Chapters 191 to 200, the tenth-chapter block the batch is measured against — and the fifty-chapter volume column is given alongside it, with the reason a writer needs both.

## F4 — Medium. `cut` was a substring count inside a whole-word ledger

**Found.** `PROMPT.md:69` said *`cut` is 35 in the fifth block and all thirty-five are ordinary.* **Whole-word `cut` in the fifth block is 29; 35 is the substring count.** The gap was located rather than assumed: the six are *been cutting* four times, *the cutting* once and *a cutting* once. `outline/volume-05.md` §15 item 14 describes all of its device counts as whole-word counts, so the prompt was asserting a device budget with the wrong counting rule behind it, and *all thirty-five are ordinary* was a claim about a token set nobody had measured.

**Applied.** The prompt now gives 29 for the fifth block and 116 for the volume, as whole words, and the correction is recorded on the face of the line. **The twenty-nine were read, and all twenty-nine are ordinary uses — a line, a stroke, a figure, a number, hair.** The claim survives, on twenty-nine instances instead of thirty-five. `outline/volume-05.md` §15 item 14 gained the same warning, because a baseline quoted without its counting rule is how a substring becomes a device in a chapter.

## F5 — Medium. The §17 chain was a selected list presented as a checkable chain

**Found.** `outline/volume-05.md` §17 promised the table existed *so that any later writer can check any single figure in it by counting*, and listed 16 of the volume's 43 market days. **Eight of the fourteen days of Block 1 were missing — days 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 11 — as were 9 of the 14 post-return markets, which matters to Batch 0005, which has to write a 48-day tail.** Every date and weekday that was listed is correct.

**Applied.** Days 0 to 13 are now given in full, because Block 1 runs on all of them and a batch that must reconstruct a weekday it was not handed will get it wrong — and did, which is F2. The table now states what it is: the first fourteen days complete, the rows from day 17 onward selected anchors, sixteen named of forty-three, with the instruction that the chain between two rows is a cycle of seven and a block needing a day the table does not print counts it from the nearest row above and says which. The new rows were generated from day 0 and checked against the day-0-Monday anchor.

## F6 — Medium. "The seventh child" had two referents, and the outline is the one that is right

**Found, and the finding was inverted on inspection.** `outline/volume-05.md` §5.2, §8 Block 2 and §17 all make the seventh child the city's child and the post, and §17's crossing table makes *the seventh child in the volume* the living anchor the return is made through. `PROMPT.md:36` then ruled that the Chapter 210 nine-year-old was **not** that child — *two different children in two different places and no chapter may join them* — and coined *the seventh child of this block*, an ordinal with no referent in the plan.

**The outline does not have the conflict the prompt was recorded as having; the prompt invented it.** §13's cast plan states in its own words that Block 4 is carried by four returned faces and *the seventh child*, and describes that child as *one who was established in Block 0001 and has never had a page* — and Block 1's card is the last page of Chapter 210, where the road is opened by a person who is not Aren and who is nine. **The outline's single child is therefore established in Block 1, carries the city's mark and gives the ninth refusal at nine in Block 2, and is the anchor of the return in Block 4, and all three of those are the same person.** The prompt's version would have doubled the volume's unnamed nine-year-olds and left Block 4 with a fifth face who had never been on a page.

**Applied.** The prompt now says the Chapter 210 child **is** the seventh child in the volume, and the *seventh child of this block* ordinal is gone. **Recorded in `outline/volume-05.md` §16 as a sixth item, because the finding belongs with the other recorded conflicts and because a batch prompt overruleing a cast plan is a failure mode worth having in the document: a batch prompt may not overrule a cast plan, and where the two disagree the outline is the plan.** No character, plot point, date or cast name moved.
