**Embermonth the fourteenth, YR 323, a Tuesday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and a man of thirty-two with a stick beginning to stay off that floor on purpose.**

A Tuesday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the fourteenth of Embermonth with about nine people in it and a hard north wind coming down it off the shoulder. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Tuesday, and the ordinary part is that a made road on a Tuesday in Embermonth carries about one cart in nine of what it carries on a Saturday and that the four hundred people who know about those boards are not any of the nine hundred.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the ninth of Embermonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is seventy-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that seventy-nine has not moved in five days and that about five days is the interval at which this county says any figure out loud at all.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for five hundred and fifty-two days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that five hundred and fifty-two days is a year and seven months and that a thing done every day for a year and seven months is a trade and not a decision.

The practice has run two hundred and one mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of Embermonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is two hundred and seven mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the fourteenth of Embermonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred mornings is the first figure in this county that is exactly round and that a round figure is a figure somebody could count on a good day.

There are ten things and about nine people on that floor, and the number is said by two of them twice in the hour before light, and a thing is held when those two people have said out loud in the same hour that they are holding it and are both holding it again on the next morning. Nine of the ten things have a person against them and two voices. One of the ten has nobody against it and never has, and it is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is on the back of a board in a cart shed four days off. Eight of the ten were held on the morning before this one and two were not, and the one of the two that has a person against it has not been said out loud at the head of its track since the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, and nobody in nine counties has asked that person anything about it, and about four have said that a person who has said nothing for about nine months is still a person on a list and that is a different thing from a person taken off one.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days in Mudmonth and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is two hundred and seventy-one days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is two hundred and fifty-two days. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and fifty-two days shut is most of eight months and that most of eight months is long enough that the road has stopped being a road and become the way things are, and five have said that about nine houses four hundred yards behind nine hurdles would like them to stay and said so once through a shut door and nobody has been up there a third time.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is three hundred and nineteen days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that three hundred and nineteen days is most of a year and twenty days and that twenty days over a year is nothing anybody can put a figure on, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades and that about nine feet is also the width of the made road and the length of the gap in that wall, and about four have said that it does not close over.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences and two copies of a roll are nailed one over the other on the frame of them. The notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and ninety-three days past, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and ninety-three days is over six months, and five had not, and four have said to each other that a notice six months and thirteen days past its own date is a thing nobody has to decide anything about, because there is nothing left on the page to decide.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that four papers out of one office in about a year is an office that files, and five say that filing is not holding, and about four have been saying that about four hundred people in that street could tell you which of the four they have seen and about nine hundred along that made road could not tell you how many there are.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper, and that not one of those nine has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name either, and five have said that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold.

The roll is on those boards seventy-two days and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down, and its nine lines are empty on it and on a second copy over it, and about four hundred people have walked past it and about nine people have stood at it and nothing has been written on it by anybody. The second copy came back in the ordinary post on the first of Embermonth with one sentence on a folded sheet inside its covering paper in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had begun to fill again. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them, and about four have said that a stone with nine hundred names on it is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone into that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times in one morning and does not touch a frame has found a third way of saying no, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said that nothing in that yard is on any board in nine counties.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that the flat of it has gone to a mat and that a mat of that kind is under it now and not on it, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line is about four and a half acres, and about four have been saying that it will not close over in this summer either.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs that day and has not come down them on a Tuesday in about four and a half years. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is not the record and is not in any document this county keeps.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four people in nine counties had seen the thumb-drag across the end of the top line and read it for a moment as the first stroke of a letter, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it.

Then at about the ninth hour of that Tuesday the man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane and stood in front of those boards and had not touched either copy of the roll, and that was the whole of what he had come down for, and he told nobody that, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who comes down a lane to look at a thing and tells nobody why is a man who has already decided what he is going to do and has not said the word for it. He had stood there about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and had read the Tuesday printed at the head of it, which is the twenty-third of Fallowmonth and is twenty-one days gone, and he had gone back up the lane without going up the made road past the ninth mile stone.

He had told nobody that he meant to be off that floor for eleven mornings and he had not written it down, and the eleven were the eleven mornings a practice runs on between the first of a month and the seventeenth of the one after it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that eleven is not a round number and that a man who has picked eleven rather than ten rather than nine has been counting something.

The fourth hour of that Tuesday morning came before the light and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people, and the man of thirty-two with the stick was one of the seven and said it, and he was going to be one of the two on that floor for the last time for eleven mornings. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about a roll of nine lines and nothing about a Tuesday that came and shut nothing is written down anywhere either.

He had meant to finish the work of about four and a half years in this county by finding out who was standing on that floor when he was not on it, and there were about nine people in nine counties he had asked once each and not one of them had said they would do anything without a second morning, and that had been the finding and it had cost him the standing. He had been wrong about the morning before the light was properly up, and the wrongness was that he had believed a floor with seven people on it was a room with him in it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a room with one man in it and a floor with nine people on it are two different arrangements of the same number and that only one of them goes on when he goes down a made road.

The cost of that Tuesday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about nine minutes standing at a board and had a hot band across the knee by the seventh hour that did not go down again that day, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is not coming back this winter or any winter, and four people in nine counties have said since that about four months ago a man was planning around half an ear and that by the fourteenth of Embermonth he had stopped planning around it and is planning with it, and that those are the same amount of deafness. And in the standing fact that he cannot be near the works without somebody standing with him, which is about four and a half years old and has not been argued about and is not going to be.

The ordinary part of that Tuesday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and put no board out, and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Tuesday in Embermonth is dark and there is nothing off the dry slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the fourteenth of Embermonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fourteenth of Embermonth is a Tuesday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that street had worked out inside nine seconds that the lamp has been lit about four hundred times and nobody has ever asked who lit it.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile are the second set of people in this county with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that nine households have nine names between them and a form has one line.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about three hundred and nineteen days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Tuesday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about a roll of nine lines, nothing about a Tuesday that shut nothing, and nothing about the eleven mornings is written down anywhere either.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who decides to be absent for eleven mornings and tells nobody is a man nobody thanks, because being absent on purpose is not a thing a county can be told about. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for two hundred and one mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and he was one of the two of them and was about to find out what that floor did without it.

He went down that four hundred pace lane at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and nine feet of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it and was still shut by nine hurdles put there by the people at the end of it who would like them to stay, and the four lines of chalk under that hood were still four and were still not writing, and the leaf at the back of that day-book still carried nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.
