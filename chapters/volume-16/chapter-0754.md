# Chapter 0754

**Hearthmonth the fifteenth, YR 322, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, a cart of lime that came up from the north two days before standing on a weigh-house floor for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and a man of forty-one who has not answered a question in four days.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is the loudest thing in nine counties. Four hundred people were in that street by the second hour of the fifteenth of Hearthmonth and two hundred of them stopped what they were doing, and the frost came off the ruts about an hour after that. Four people in nine counties had worked out that a man had asked nine people for one thing each in a fortnight, and five had not.

A cart of lime came up about nine hundred yards of a cart track and eleven miles of made road on the Thursday before, which was the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood on that weigh-house floor for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and went on up the made road with about four hundred pounds of it in it. The interval between it and the one before it is fourteen days, and it has been fourteen days for about nine years and about two hundred and thirty times.

The man of about fifty-four who keeps that floor said the number of days out loud at about the ninth hour, to four people in and five out, and nobody stopped him.

“**Fourteen days, and it has been fourteen days for about nine years, and the last one was two days ago, and there is one coming on the twenty-seventh of Hearthmonth and I have said that out loud every morning for nine years and nobody has ever said it back to me.**”

He said the date the way a man says a word he has said a great many times, without weight, and then he waited, and about four people in that market street noticed that he waited and five did not. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has said a date out loud every morning for nine years is a man with a calendar nobody else in nine counties keeps, and five have said that the twenty-seventh of Hearthmonth is a Thursday and that nobody in that street had ever said so back to him in nine years.

The man of forty-one was asked on the eleventh of Hearthmonth, four days ago, at about the ninth hour, in that market street, in the open, out loud, in his own trade, for one thing each. He has not answered and has not refused and has not said that he will not. About four people in nine counties have said since that four days is not long, and five have said that a man who has said ten words in eleven years and is asked for eleven is not refusing. About four people in nine counties have said since that the count on that floor four days off has been four since the fifth of Hearthmonth and has not moved, and five have said that the rule says two people and there is one of the two who is not there, and that nobody on that floor can tell which one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and four feet high. The man of forty-one keeps a drove and a byre and that wall, and has said ten words in eleven years, and the tenth of them was at that gate in the winter before last and was about a bar. One man has been carrying the stone for that gap two miles in his own hands for two hundred and eighty-three days, and the last yard is about nine feet of a course and he has not started it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

Four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it has been across that lane gate at the head of that made road since Mudmonth, and the drove goes up past it through the gap at the low end of that wall on every Thursday. The twenty-seventh of Hearthmonth is the Thursday before next, and the drove will go up through that gap and not through that gate. About four people in nine counties have said since that a drove is the only thing in nine counties that turns up on a Thursday whatever anybody has done, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked a drove what it thinks of a bar.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing where it stands, and a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years. Four people in nine counties have said since that about nine hundred yards of it is standing on the same shoulder as about nine hundred yards of dead grass, and that the two are not the same nine hundred yards, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked him which end of his own line he meant.

That scar is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is fifty days old. The dead grass inside its own width is standing up out of a hard frost higher than the standing grass either side of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a scar that stands up higher than the grass round it is a scar that will be visible from the made road at the bottom of that shoulder in every frost for about three years. Five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever walked to the bottom of that shoulder to look at it.

The practice has run fourteen mornings and the number is four, and it has been four since the fifth of Hearthmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that a number that stands still for ten days while two people are asked about it every day is a number that has become a habit, and five have said that a habit is not a count. About four people in nine counties have said since that the rule has a hole in it, and that the hole is that a number which goes down goes down without a sound.

The room over that weigh-house was open because it is a Saturday, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs at the seventh hour and went back up and did not carry anything. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled. The lamp at the foot of those stairs is lit at the fourth hour every morning and is not lit at the fourth hour of a market morning, because there is no fourth hour on a market morning that anybody is awake for.

The inn at the high end had four beds full and put no price out on any board, and the woman of about fifty-one who keeps that house came out into it at about the fifth hour and stopped nine feet short of those boards. A chandler nine doors down put a cloth over a bench rather than write anything at all on it.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about four hundred people in that street put a thing down and pick it up again. A man with a barrow of lime went the length of it four times and put about two hundred pounds of it down at one door and took it up again at two others. A carter at the low end got a wheel off a cart and put it on again with a bar in about nine minutes. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick at about the seventh hour of that morning, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The two mile stones between the seventh mile stone and the ninth that had come out of line are in line where a man of thirty-two put them about the middle of Harvestmonth with a bar and a lever and no mortar. About four people in nine counties have said since that the ninth mile stone is cleaned twice a week and the other two are not cleaned at all, and five have said that a stone nobody looks at is a stone nobody can be asked about.

The man of thirty-two with a stick stood at the high end of those boards for about an hour that morning and read the number in his own head, which was four, and it had been four for ten days while he had asked nine people in nine trades for one thing each. He had believed the number would go up as the asking went on. He had not said that out loud to anybody and he had not written it down, and it was the fifth thing in four and a half years he had wanted to be true and had not examined, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who asks nine people in a fortnight and gets four of them has not found out that the other five are his, and five have said that he found it out on a made road about eleven weeks ago.

Then the part of that Saturday that was said out loud, and two things in it were, and neither of them was about a road.

A person at that third door asked him one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, kindly, and it was not about a road and not about a wall.

“**What are the ten things?**”

He had a longer answer ready to that one than to any other, because he had been carrying the list of the ten in his own head for about eleven weeks and could have said the whole of it in a market street in about a minute, and what stopped him was that nine people in nine counties could then go and ask the nine of them, and he would have handed nine questions to nine people and got back nine answers he did not want.

“**Nine of them are roads somebody in this county keeps and one of them is nine hundred yards of grass four days off, and I could tell you the nine and I am not going to, and you have not asked me a question and you have asked me the right one.**”

Nobody said anything back to him. Four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a man has said the number nine and then stopped, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man who will not say what he is holding is a man who has understood that the man who says it is the one who gets asked, and five have said that this county has understood that for four and a half years and has never once done it.

The leaf with one thumb on it that named this month is fifty-eight days old, and it was read out at those boards on the seventeenth of Wolfmonth in seventeen words. About four people in nine counties have said since that a cart came up that made road on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, and that a cart coming up is the opposite of a place shutting its own end, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever been to the place. About four have said that a leaf is not a road and a cart is not a leaf, and five have said that both of them came up the same made road in different months.

Then this is the whole of what this county has on the fifteenth of Hearthmonth, and nothing about any of it is in a document. A number of four is said out loud twice on the fourteen mornings it has run and it has not moved in ten days. A cart of lime came up two days ago on the fourteenth day after the last one, and there is another due on the twenty-seventh. A man of forty-one has been asked for one thing each and has said nothing for four days. A man with a name has said a date out loud and nobody has said it back to him. And about nine hundred yards of grass four days off is on the back of a board in a cart shed, and about nine hundred yards of broom is standing on the same shoulder, and neither of them is going to close over.

The light went off the west bank first on that Saturday and found the upper edge of those boards about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. The lamp at the high end of them had been standing lit since the fourth hour and went out with the light, and about two hundred people in that street went out of it into about five trades and about two hundred stayed at the boards.

A Crown's cart came up four days of made road on the Wednesday before with paper in it and stood outside the Crown's road office at the low end of that street for one day, and went on up the made road at about the sixth hour with about four hundred pounds of wool in it. About four people in that market street had worked out that this is the sixth cart in about twelve weeks to come up that made road with paper in it, and five had worked out nothing. Nobody at the low end of that street has ever opened the paper, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a county that has been sent six pieces of paper in twelve weeks and has opened none of them is a county that has decided something and not said it.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side. One of the nine households that own them has about nine hundred yards of dead grass nine hundred yards above his own wall, and a young man of about nineteen has about nine hundred yards of standing broom on the same shoulder. About four people in nine counties have said since that there are three things on one shoulder, four hundred yards apart on the ground, that belong to nobody, and five have said that one of the three is a scar and one of the three is a line of broom and one of the three is a wall with a yard of it missing.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fifteenth of Hearthmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was not over the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping was not rung. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that there are about four hundred people on one bank of that water and about four hundred on the other, and that the two numbers have been the same for as long as anybody has counted, and five had not.

The road office at the low end of that market street has the printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth in it still, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it, and that is thirty-eight days. About four people in nine counties have said since that the same office has now asked this county for two things about one wall in two different forms and has had no answer to either, and five have said that about nine people in nine counties could name the man who holds that wall this morning, and that the naming of him is not the same thing as the answering of it.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and about as wide as two hands, and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and has not gone past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them, and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and no third household ever came up and nobody asked it for a reason. About four people in nine counties have said since that a scar which stops at a boundary somebody drew nine hundred years ago is a scar that has been given a place to stop, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked the nine households whether they knew.

The first of Goatmonth is in sixteen days, and the fourteenth first of a month in a row will arrive on a Monday with nothing at those boards. About four people in nine counties have worked out that a first of a month is the one morning in this county on which about four hundred people are not in that street and therefore about nine people are, and five have said that nobody has ever used it for anything.

Then the fourth hour of that Friday before, which is before the light. Four people in nine counties knew that four of them were going to be on a floor four days off, and about nine hundred did not. The number that was said out loud on that floor that morning was four and it was said twice. The man of about thirty-four with a satchel said it first and the woman of about fifty-one said it again, and neither of them had written it down. About four people in nine counties have said since that two people who say a number in the same hour in the dark are two people who cannot check each other after the light, and five have said that a number in the dark is worth less than a number in the morning, and about four have said that this county has never had a figure worth either.

About nine hundred yards of a cart track goes north out of that market town and up the made road for about eleven miles and then stops being made, and about a mile of it after that is a cart track with a stone in the middle of it. About four people in nine counties have said since that a road that stops being made is not the same thing as a road that is shut, and five have said that about four people in that market street have been waiting eleven weeks for the two things to come apart, and about four have said that the ninth of the nine stones is nine hundred years old and is the other half of the same question.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a number of four that has not moved in ten days, four days of a man's silence that is not a refusal, about two hundred and thirty carts of lime of which one is due on the twenty-seventh of this month, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, about nine hundred yards of broom that will not either, a leaf that named a month fifty-eight days ago, and the use of one ear and the use of one leg, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
