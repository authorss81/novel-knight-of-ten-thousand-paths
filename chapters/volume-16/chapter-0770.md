# Chapter 0770

**Mudmonth the forty-ninth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with seventy-two days behind, and a floor that said no number that morning and will say none after.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the forty-ninth of Mudmonth with about four hundred people in it and sun on the stalls. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them dried to the colour of old bone. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Thursday, and the ordinary part is that a hard road carries and nobody remarks it either.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday itself at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is fifty-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that fifty-nine is a figure one short of sixty, and five have said that nobody counts drove to round numbers.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about five feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and seven days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and seven days is a year and six weeks of carrying.

The practice has run ninety-five mornings and no number was said on the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one, and this is the last day of seventy-two and the practice does not run on it. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water, and about four people in nine counties have said since that ninety-five mornings with four at the end is a practice that knows what it is, and five have said that knowing is not holding, and about four have said since that the knowing came when the road under it went.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and thirty-nine days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and thirty-nine days is near five months, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all and that the ninth has been held by nobody for a hundred and thirty-nine days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and seventy-four days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and seventy-four days old is half a year of standing, and five have said that standing is not holding and never was.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is forty-eight days past and is a Friday gone, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that forty-eight days past a date is a dead paper, and five had not, and about four have said since that nobody enforced it in this county, and that no gate was shut on it anywhere in seventy-two days.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that will ask until it is answered, and five have said that this county has nine clerks it cannot put a thing in front of and cannot call a hearing with.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that seventy-two days ending on a market morning is a good place to have held one's tongue throughout.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen went in among them at about the eighth hour and found the ninth skirt blown sideways and set it straight. About four people in nine counties have said since that a skirt blown sideways in Mudmonth sun is wind and not water, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that setting a skirt straight is the third time he has kept that saying without saying it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water had laid nearly all of it flat. About four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom at the end of Mudmonth is a field waiting for June, and five have said that waiting is all anybody does with it.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with chalk on her hands from ruling. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and no head was filled or ruled anywhere in seventy-two days.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that market street to close seventy-two days and to learn what they added to, and that was his goal for the morning and he carried it to the boards past the stalls. He had meant to count the sayings and the numbers and to carry the total home, and to say out loud what the seventy-two days meant. He had been wrong about the morning before the ninth hour was out, and the wrongness was that he thought seventy-two days would add to an answer, and seventy-two days adds to a question, and the question is what a practice is when the road under it goes. The cost of it was in the leg from standing the market through with the stick planted, which drove the ache up through the knee into the hip by the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which took the market noise until it sang with it and he had to turn his good side to every speaker.

At about the ninth hour of that Thursday Renn Ottery came down to that market street with chalk on his thumb, and about nine people saw it because a man who keeps a floor does not chalk his thumb on market mornings. He stood before the boards and said it out loud in his own trade, to four people in and five out, the way he says his figures, and held up the thumb with the chalk on it.

“**The floor says four and the floor has said it seventy-two days in all weathers, and four lives in nine heads and on no paper, and water took four of us once and will take us again.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a floor described as a thing that can be taken, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. About four people in nine counties have said since that a figure in nine heads is a figure nowhere when the heads cannot meet, and five have said that meeting depends on a road, and about four have said since that a practice that has to be written down to survive a road going has never yet been written down anywhere.

He went back four days up the made road that afternoon with the chalk still on his thumb, and at the weigh-house door he stopped and bent with difficulty, for he is about fifty-four and the road had tired him, and made four short lines with the chalk on the stone sill of the door, where the rain does not reach and where no room is and no paper is. The lines are about four inches long and side by side, and they mean four, and they are the first mark anybody in nine counties has made of the practice anywhere that is not a room and not a paper. About four people in nine counties have said since that four lines on a sill is not writing, and five have said that it is the beginning of it, and about four have said since that a practice that needs a made road will need a mark that needs no road, and that this is the first person who has made one, and nobody asked him to and nobody thanked him for it.

The man of thirty-two saw the four lines at about the fifth hour, standing nine feet off in the yard with the stick in his hand, and understood all of it there in the sun with chalk dust on the sill. A number said twice in the dark lives in heads, and heads are stopped by water, and lines on stone are not stopped by water. He had thought not writing was honesty, and not writing is a road going out from under honesty, and the honesty has to be carried another way. He had been wrong about writing for seventy-two days, and the wrongness had cost him nothing yet and would cost him everything later, and the cost sat in him then as a lightness behind the knee-ache and a clearing in the good ear, as if the county had spoken once through chalk. Nobody thanked the man of fifty-four for the lines, and about four people in nine counties have said since that unthanked lines last longer.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had all four of its beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day for about nine hours.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Mudmonth is dim and there is something off the dry slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the forty-ninth of Mudmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the forty-ninth of Mudmonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Thursday by anybody. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a dry causeway at the end of Mudmonth is summer coming.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that seventy-two days ending with four lines on a sill is a county learning to count on stone, and five have said that stone is not paper and no roll was filled.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one, and this is the last of seventy-two days. That is the whole of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that seventy-two days with four lines at the end of them is a beat finished, and five have said that finished is not mended.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who closes seventy-two days by watching chalk is a man nobody thanks, because watching is not doing. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for ninety-five mornings by nine people saying numbers twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice forty-eight days past its date that had shut nothing and taught one thing, and the one thing is that a practice which needs a made road is a practice that a person can switch off by ruining the road.

He went out of that market street at about noon with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand and about nine miles of ache in it, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The first of Mudmonth had come and gone with about nine people saying they would put no name on four lines of a roll, and nobody had asked them to and nobody is going to, and nobody in nine counties is going to shut a gate on it either. He had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody in seventy-two days, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of those seventy-two days is a notice come and gone with no roll made, nine sayings nobody asked for, nine days of water taking four of nine, a number of four in seven heads and four lines on a stone sill, a practice found to need a made road, one thing that went quiet a hundred and thirty-nine days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
