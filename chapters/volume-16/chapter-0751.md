# Chapter 0751

**Frostmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 322, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour in that market street with nobody in it at all, and three people on a floor four days off being asked for one thing each.**

A Monday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and there was nobody at all in that market street at the fourth hour of the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it. The boards at the low end of that street stood in the dark with nothing on them. Nine doors down, the cloth was still over the bench.

Then four days off, and about nine hundred people along that made road did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part is that nothing about a Monday is visible on a road.

A drove went up that lane on the Thursday before, which was the twenty-second of Frostmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is thirty-eight drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and about four feet high. The stone is quarried two miles off on the low side, and one man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for two hundred and sixty-four days, and his hands have been the colour of the stone since about the first of Frostmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

Five frames of brood in a walled yard half a mile below that wall are under about nine straw skirts for the winter. The skirts went on about the ninth of Fallowmonth, one stone on each, and the young man of about nineteen who keeps those nine hives has not been inside one of them since. About four people in nine counties have said since that a skep under a skirt in Frostmonth is a thing nine people in nine counties can describe, and five have said that nobody has asked him what is under the skirt.

About nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing on that east rise where it stands. The broom has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth. A line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years, and that one is dead in its own width in a winter that is not over, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a standing line of dead broom is harder in a hard winter than a standing line of dead grass. Five have said that nobody has asked him which of the two he meant on the morning he said it.

That scar is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is thirty-one days old. It is on the top of that shoulder, from the ninth of the nine stones down to the place where the nine-foot line in the grass begins and the kerbed road starts, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off, about nine feet long and about nine inches wide. About nine people in nine counties have stood in front of that board and nobody has asked the roadkeeper for it.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand. They were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth, which is a hundred and two days and ninety days, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to. Four people in nine counties have said since that three months of not arguing with a thing is longer than anybody in this county has gone without asking twice about anything.

The ninth mile stone on that made road was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The man who cleans it cleans it on a Tuesday and a Saturday, and the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth is a Monday and he was two days short of it. Nine people in nine counties know that he does it. Five have ever seen him do it.

Then the floor, which is the only floor in nine counties where a figure has been kept out loud every morning for about four and a half years. It is about four paces long. It is the floor of a weigh-house with a board on the wall outside it, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come past it in a good month, and Frostmonth is the month they come past clean.

Aren Kest was on that floor at about the fourth hour of that Monday, with a woman of about fifty-one who keeps the inn at the high end of that market street and a man of about thirty-four with a satchel he has not opened. He had come four days up the made road on the Sunday with the stick in his right hand and his left hand hanging at his side where anybody could see it, and he had asked none of them anything on the way.

Four days on a made road had changed what the leg was for. It had been the leg he put his weight on at the fourth hour and it was now the leg he dragged, and he had slept two nights in a barn and one in a doorway and had learned in those three nights to sleep with his back to a wall. The ear he turns to people had been cold for three of the four days and had made him turn his head further than he wanted to at every gate he came through. About four people in nine counties had worked out inside about nine seconds that a man who walks four days and asks nothing is a man who is going to ask something. Five had not.

He had come here to ask three people for one thing each, and he had been wrong about the number before he started out. He had come thinking nine people in nine trades would say yes. He had come thinking that a man holding a thing alone gets asked where his figures come from, and that two people holding it gets the same question asked of two people and one of them has usually never had it. He had written nothing down on the road, because a man with a page in his coat is a man who can be shown his own plan, and he had kept the ten things off the page in his own head for four days so that the first thing anybody heard from him would be the ask and not a rehearsal of the ask.

Then he asked, in the open, in the market street at the sea end, where a man with a barrow had just put about two hundred pounds of lime down at one door and taken it up again at two others.

“**I hold about ten things and I cannot hold ten on my own and I am asking the three of you to hold one each between you and to tell me the number out loud at this hour on the morning you meant to, and I am not going to say what the ten things are and nobody is going to be thanked for any of it.**”

The woman of about fifty-one had a cloth in both hands when he finished. She wiped them down her apron in one movement, which took longer than it needed to take, and then she said it back to him on the floor and not to the street.

“**I have a house open at the fourth hour every morning of this year and I will have one of yours open too, and I am not going to be thanked, and I want a fire laid for four people and not nine.**”

The man of about thirty-four with the satchel put his thumb under the flap and did not lift it, and he answered standing the way a man answers when he has decided the answer before he opened his mouth.

“**I have been four days up a made road with nothing in the satchel and I will hold one of yours and put it in the satchel, and a man who puts a thing in a satchel is a man carrying it, and I am not going to be thanked for carrying a thing I asked to carry.**”

The man of about fifty-four who keeps that floor did not answer for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. He has a board on the wall outside that floor and about four hundred and nine of the sheets in that month go through his hands, and he stood with his back to the weigh-beam and looked at the three of them, and then he said it.

“**I have had a figure out loud on this floor every morning for about four and a half years and nobody has ever asked me where I get it, and I will hold one of yours and say the number at this hour, and I am not going to be thanked, and there is one thing I am not going to do and I will tell you what it is when I know what it is.**”

Then he gave them the rule, and he gave it to the floor and not to the three of them, because a rule given to the three of them is a rule that belongs to them by the second hour and a rule given to a floor belongs to whoever stands on it.

“**A thing is held when two people have said out loud in the same hour that they are holding it, and both of them are still holding it on the next morning they said they would be, and that is all of it and I am not going to improve on it.**”

The number was then said out loud on that floor at about the fourth hour of the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth by one person and said again by a second, and both of them said three. It went fast. It went so fast that the man of about thirty-four said it before the woman of about fifty-one had finished getting her breath back from the last of it, and the man of about fifty-four said it a second time because the rule says two people, not two people who have finished breathing.

About four people in nine counties had worked out inside about nine seconds that a man of thirty-two with a stick had got three people to say a number out loud in the same hour. Five had not. About four have said since that a number said twice in the same hour is not a record, and five have said that it is the only kind of count this county has ever had.

At about the ninth hour the man of about fifty-four said his own figure out loud, to four people in and five out, and nobody stopped him.

About four hundred and nine sheets came past that floor in Frostmonth. He had said every one of those out loud, every morning, for about four and a half years, and until this week nobody in that street had ever asked him a figure about anything.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side. About nine hundred trees on that low side went out of blossom in the second week of Fallowmonth, and the fruit came down into four hundred yards of grass and was still in it under the first frost. The owners are nine households, and not one of them has ever asked anybody anything about anybody who walks past its gate.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter. The twenty-sixth of Frostmonth was a Monday, and the lamp went on at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and about four people in nine counties had worked out inside about nine seconds that a Monday in this county is the one day of the week the lamp is lit. Five had not. Nobody in that market street has ever asked a person at a gate at the near end of that water anything at all.

He had come four days to put ten things into nine people's mouths and he had got three of them into one floor in about ten minutes, and he stood in the dark at the sea end of that street afterwards and understood for the first time how little he had chosen. He had expected to have to work for it. He had built four days of road around the idea that a man who asks for a thing in a market street gets refused, and gets refused slowly, and that the refusal would be the honest part. Instead there had been three yeses before the light, and no argument, and no trouble of any kind, and the number was three, and there was nothing on any page anywhere in nine counties that said three.

The ordinary part of that Monday went on around him. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. Two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and put no price out on any board.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had put a second lamp at the foot of them on the Friday of the week before and had it lit at the fourth hour, as she has lit it every fourth hour since. It is the second lamp in that room, and it is on thirteen stairs, and there was no lamp on them before. About four people in that lane had worked out that she paid for it out of fourpence a year. Five had not. The four of them on the floor four days off did not see that lamp and were not told about it.

Then he stood on that floor and went along the whole of the month in his own head, because he had come four days to find out whether a number could be kept by people who had not been asked where they got it, and he wanted to know what he had actually started with. This is the whole of what this county has on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nothing about any of it is in a document. A number has been said out loud twice in the same hour on a floor four days off and it is three. A figure of about four hundred and nine sheets came past that floor in a month, and the man who keeps it has been saying figures out loud for about four and a half years and has been asked for one of them. Nine hundred yards of dead grass is on the back of a board in a cart shed. Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths. And a hand lamp went on a coping on a Monday afternoon for the first time in this county's winter, and about four people noticed and five did not.

Nine of the ten things he had come to talk about were in that list. The tenth was nine feet wide and nine hundred yards long and was on the back of a board in a shed four days off, and there was nobody in nine counties he could ask to hold it, because nobody in nine counties knew it was there.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the lamp at the high end is lit at the fourth hour and on a Monday the fourth hour is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Nobody in that street has ever turned it out early.

The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past the ninth has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them. The two mile stones between the seventh mile stone and the ninth that had come out of line are in line where a man of thirty-two put them about the middle of Harvestmonth with a bar and a lever and no mortar, one of them about two hundred yards back from where it had been and the other about nine feet. The second of the two has not walked out of line since, and nobody in nine counties has asked him and nobody has been told.

The four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it is still across that lane gate at the head of the made road, and the drove has gone up past it through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of wall on every Thursday since the second of Mudmonth, and has never once gone through that gate. About four people in nine counties have said since that the gate is not the road, and five have said that nobody has ever asked the man who bars it what the gate is for.

About nine hundred yards of a cart track goes north out of that market town and up the made road for about eleven miles and then stops being made, and about a mile of it after that is a cart track with a stone in the middle of it that a man put there about nine years ago. A cart of lime has come up that track and onto that made road on every second Thursday for about nine years and about two hundred and thirty times, and the last one came on the twenty-ninth of Frostmonth, which was the Thursday before, and it stood on that weigh-house floor for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and went on up the made road with about four hundred pounds of it in it. It is the only thing in nine counties that arrives on a day anybody here can name in advance, and it is the only date in this county that a man keeps without being asked to keep it.

Then the ninth hour came, and the market street was still not a market, and about nine people were in it and four of them were at the boards with nothing on them and five were not. A person at the third door asked him one thing out loud, kindly, and it was not about a road and not about a wall.

“**Is it going to be all right?**”

Nobody said anything back to him. About four people in that market street had heard a man ask three people for one thing each and five had heard a rule about two people saying a number out loud in the same hour, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nobody in nine counties has consoled him and nobody has thanked him. Five have said that this county has never had anybody in it ask three people for one thing each and be given it before.

He had about four answers ready and used none of them, which is the only thing about this Monday that he had practised for. The rule said he was not going to improve on it, and the practice said a number is only a count if two people will stand in the dark at the fourth hour and say it twice, and both of those had been easy to say out loud to three people at once on a floor, and neither of them had been tested on anything yet. Nine of the ten things had a person who could be asked for it. The tenth had a board in a shed and no person at all, and a rule with two people in it cannot be given to a board.

**And the cost of that Monday is three people who have each been asked for one thing and have each said yes in their own trade, about four hundred and nine sheets in a month that a man keeps out loud and nobody has asked him about, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, a hand lamp on a coping that about four people noticed, and the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a rule that two people have to say a number out loud or it is not a count, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
