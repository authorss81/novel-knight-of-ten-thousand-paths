# Chapter 0768

**Mudmonth the thirty-seventh, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with the shoulder drying, and five people where nine stood.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the thirty-seventh of Mudmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts moving again. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them dried pale by sun. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Saturday, and the ordinary part is that a road drying is a road mending without anybody mending it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the thirty-fifth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and one man went with it. That is fifty-seven drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that a drove after water is a field starting again.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about five feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and ninety-five days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three hundred and ninety-five days is a year and a month of mornings.

The practice has run eighty-seven mornings and no number was said on the thirty-seventh of Mudmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it has been four since the twenty-seventh, and about four people in nine counties have said since that four held over ten days of drying is four tested, and five have said that tested is not mended.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and twenty-seven days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and twenty-seven days is a road gone quiet in earnest, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and sixty-two days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and sixty-two days old is the county's oldest lesson, and five have said that lessons are not holdings.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is thirty-six days past and is a Friday gone, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that thirty-six days past a date is a date dead, and five had not, and about four have said since that a dead date with four unnamed roads is still a date.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that will keep, and five have said that this county keeps better.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that thirty-six days past a notice is a good time to have said nothing.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick that morning at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it. The clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep, and the eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the ground had dried enough that the young man of about nineteen went in among them at about the eighth hour and lifted one skirt and set it back. About four people in nine counties have said since that lifting one skirt after water is checking and not keeping, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that checking is how a man keeps that saying.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water had laid nearly all of it flat. About four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom drying is a field of thatch with nobody to thatch, and five have said that nobody has asked for thatch.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the day-book under her arm. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that market street to ask two of the four who stopped whether they were coming back, and that was his goal for the morning and he wrote it nowhere and carried it in his head past the stalls. He had meant to ask kindly and to take no for an answer, and to learn the shape of four without pressing it. He had been wrong about the morning before the ninth hour was half out, and the wrongness was that he thought asking was a thing he could do on a market morning among stalls, and asking on a market morning is performing, and two men asked among about four hundred hearers cannot say yes or no freely. The cost of it was in the leg from standing through the market noise waiting for a gap that never came, which set the knee to a deep throb, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which took stall cries until they blurred into one long ache behind the eye.

One of the four was at the third door at about the tenth hour buying twine, and the man of thirty-two stood beside him and said it low, in his own trade, to one person, with about nine people hearing anyway.

“**The floor says four now, and it said eight before the water, and will you say it with us when the road hardens?**”

The man looked at his twine and then at the boards with the notice dried pale on them, and said one thing back, short, and went on winding his twine.

“**I will come when the road is hard, and not before, and that is all I will say on a market morning.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a maybe said where hundreds hear, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to either of them. About four people in nine counties have said since that a maybe on a market morning is a no with good manners, and five have said that good manners is the only thing a market morning allows, and about four have said since that the asking cost the asker and taught him where not to ask.

He walked away from the stall with the leg loud in him and the ear aching, and understood about half of it there among the carts and the other half on the four hundred pace lane where it was quiet enough to think. A man who asks among hundreds gets an answer shaped by hundreds, and the answer he got was shaped kindly, and kindness on a market morning is a refusal with its coat on. About four people in nine counties have said since that a refusal with its coat on is still a refusal, and five have said that a coat keeps nobody warm, and about four have said since that the floor will say four on Monday whether the road is hard or not. Asking among hundreds is asking the hundreds, and the practice is said by two and heard by seven, and a market is the wrong room for it because the room over the weigh-house is open on markets and the practice does not run on them for that reason. He had thought persistence was asking everywhere, and persistence is asking in the right hour, and the wrongness had cost him a morning of standing, and the cost sat in the knee and in the side of the head where noise had driven in through the deaf side until it pulsed. Nobody thanked him for not pressing, and about four people in nine counties have said since that not pressing on a market morning is work nobody sees.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street in about nine inches of wet and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had all four of its beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Mudmonth is dim and there is something off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirty-seventh of Mudmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Saturday by anybody. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a dry causeway after nine days of water is a road given back.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that a dry road with nine households on it is a county whole again, and five have said that whole is not held until four say it.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years.

Then the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a Saturday after water is a morning for drying and not for numbers.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who asks wrong and learns is a man nobody thanks, because learning is not giving. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for eighty-seven mornings by nine people saying numbers twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice thirty-six days past its date that had shut nothing.

He went out of that market street at about noon with the leg bad and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The shoulder steamed dry in patches under about nine seconds of sun at a time, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a market morning after water, a question asked wrong among stalls, a maybe for an answer, eighty-seven mornings behind and a hard road ahead, a number of four held over a market and on no paper, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet a hundred and twenty-seven days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
