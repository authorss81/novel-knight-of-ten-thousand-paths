**Wolfmonth the ninth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it and a floor four days off that said no number that morning, and a man of thirty-two with a stick who has had blood on the inside of his left wrist for four days and has told nobody.**

On the fifth of Wolfmonth in the dark, which is four days ago, he had gone with the man of about fifty-four who keeps that weigh-house floor down the made road and off it about nine hundred yards to the ninth of the nine stones, and the man of about fifty-four had stood about nine feet off with a stick that was not his own and had not gone past the ninth of them, and what is beyond the ninth of those nine stones is not on the page of this chapter and has not been on the page of this county for about a year. The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it was a foot wide and half an inch deep and had frosted over. The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was taken off and put back on, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in among the frames and lifted one skirt and set it back and stayed looking at it for about nine minutes. The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat and the first frost has gone into the mat of it and whitened it. The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm. On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it, and he was about nine feet off on the fifth of Wolfmonth and did not see anything at all.

At about the ninth hour of that Saturday the man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane into about four hundred people with his right hand shut over the inside of his left wrist, and that was the whole of what he had come down for, and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to find out how many mornings it cost a floor for him not to be on it, and it was five mornings since the first of Wolfmonth and the number had been said on every one of them by two people who were not him, and nobody had gone and looked to see whether it was being said properly. He had been wrong about the morning before the light was properly up, and the wrongness was that he had believed a number said without him is the same number said with him, and about four people in nine counties have said since that it is and it is not, and that the not is the only part of it anybody learns anything from.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and two hours standing in a market street with a hand shut over a wrist, and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour that did not go down again that day. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent now and is not a discovery this winter, it is the arrangement he has. And in four days of blood on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything and that nobody in nine counties knows about and that he has not put in any document and has not said out loud to anybody at all.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the frost had put ice in the hub of it and that is what took the nine minutes. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Wolfmonth is dark and there is something off the frosted slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the ninth of Wolfmonth either.
