# Chapter 0755

**Hearthmonth the twenty-first, YR 322, a Friday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with five people on it, and a roadkeeper of about fifty-two putting a date and a figure on one thing each out loud in a market street.**

A Friday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning. About nine people were in that market street by the ninth hour of the twenty-first of Hearthmonth, four of them at the boards with nothing on them and five not, and the bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it.

Then four days off, and about nine hundred people along that made road did the ordinary part of a Friday, and the ordinary part is that nothing about a Friday is visible on a road.

A drove went up that lane on the Thursday before, which was the twentieth of Hearthmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is forty-two drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one.

The man of about fifty-two who keeps four miles of made road under contract for about nineteen years was asked on the nineteenth of Hearthmonth and said yes on the twentieth, and the number said out loud on that floor at the fourth hour of the twenty-first is five, and it has been five since the twentieth. About four people in nine counties have said since that a roadkeeper who has been under contract for about nineteen years is the fifth person in nine counties to say yes, and five have said that he is the only one of the five who said what it costs.

He said it in that market street at about the ninth hour of the twentieth, to one person, in his own trade, standing with a bar in his hand because he had come across from the kerb.

“**I will hold one of yours until the first of Mudmonth and not one day past it, and I have asked that office for nine years to pay me for nine hundred feet of kerb and nobody has ever answered me, and a man who is not paid is not holding, and I am holding this one on the same terms I hold the kerb.**”

Nobody said anything back to him. About four people in that market street had heard a date said out loud with a figure behind it, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that the first of Mudmonth is a date nobody in this county has named a thing against, and five have said that about nine people in nine counties have named the first of Mudmonth as the end of something in about nineteen years, and about four have said that nobody has ever told the rest of the county what it is.

The man of thirty-two with the stick was nine feet off when that was said and heard all of it. He had come four days up the made road to ask nine people for one thing each, and he had built the ask in his own head as a thing with no condition in it, because a condition is a thing a person can be let out of and he had wanted nine people who could not be let out. A man who says yes and gives a date has taken the thing he was asked for and put it somewhere else, and it is now held on a contract with an office four hundred miles off that has never paid for nine hundred feet of kerb. He understood that on the twentieth of Hearthmonth, four days after asking the fifth man, and that understanding was the whole of what he got out of that week.

The man of forty-one was asked on the eleventh of Hearthmonth and has now said nothing for ten days. That is not a refusal, and nobody in nine counties calls it one. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has said ten words in eleven years and is asked for the eleventh is not refusing, and five have said that he is the only one of the nine people in this county whose silence is doing work. About four people in nine counties have said since that the count on that floor has moved from four to five because a roadkeeper put a date on it and not because anybody asked him twice.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and four feet high. The stone is quarried two miles off on the low side, and one man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for two hundred and eighty-nine days, and the man of forty-one bars that gate on the lane side with four feet of ash and two stones on the end of it and has barred it since Mudmonth. The drove goes up through the gap at the low end of that wall every Thursday and has never once gone through that gate.

The practice has run nineteen mornings and the number is five. About four people in nine counties have said since that nineteen mornings is nineteen mornings, and five have said that nineteen mornings is a fortnight minus a day, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the first morning was the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth and the nineteenth is the twenty-first of Hearthmonth and that is twenty-six days. Five have said that nobody in nine counties has a piece of paper with either date on it.

The man of about fifty-four who keeps that floor said his figure out loud at about the ninth hour, to four people in and five out. It is eight days since the last cart and there is one due on the twenty-seventh of Hearthmonth, which is six days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a county cannot tell that a thing has stopped until after the day it was due, and five have said that this county has been unable to call a hearing since the first of Goatmonth last year, and about four have said that a thing that has not stopped yet is a thing nobody writes down.

The leaf with one thumb on it is sixty-four days old, and it was read out at those boards on the seventeenth of Wolfmonth in seventeen words, and the words were that a place shuts its own end before Hearthmonth and is not asking anybody for anything about it. About four people in nine counties have said since that Hearthmonth is halfway through and nothing at this end of nine counties has shut, and five have said that the place that wrote it is not in this county. About four people in nine counties have said since that a cart of lime has come up from the north on the last four second Thursdays and about two hundred and thirty times before them, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked a cart where it comes from.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand. They were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth, which is a hundred and twenty-seven days and a hundred and fifteen days, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to. About four people in nine counties have said since that a third man is being asked for the same thing and has said nothing for ten days, and five have said that the three of them are now the only three people in this county who have been asked for one thing each. About four have said that two of them said no in the open and one of them has not said anything at all, and that is a worse position to be in.

The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and put no price out on any board, and the woman of about fifty-one who keeps that house came out into it at about the fifth hour and stopped nine feet short of those boards and would not come nearer. A chandler nine doors down put a cloth over a bench rather than write anything at all on it.

The ordinary part of that Friday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. About four people in nine counties have said since that a street of nine people and a bolt of cloth is the whole of what a county is on a Friday, and five have said that the bolt has been the same argument for four and a half years and nobody has ever asked what it is worth.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, which was the day before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The two mile stones between the seventh mile stone and the ninth that had come out of line are still in line. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man who cleans a mile stone twice a week and has put two stones back in line has never once been asked to do either, and five have said that about nine people in nine counties know that he does all three, and about four have said that a thing nobody asks for and everybody knows about is the only kind this county has ever had.

That scar is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is fifty-six days old, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. The roadkeeper of about fifty-two is the man who drew it on the fifth of Wolfmonth, and has said out loud in thirty-five words that he is not going to draw it again and not going to give the board away. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man who will not draw it again is a man who knows that the second drawing is how a thing gets sent somewhere, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has asked him what he thinks it will be sent to.

Then the part of that Friday that was said out loud, and it was one thing, and it was about the rule, and nobody in nine counties has asked about it.

A person at the third door of that market street asked the woman of about fifty-one one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, kindly, and it was not about a road.

“**What happens when one of them stops?**”

She had been nine feet short of those boards since the fifth hour and came the rest of the way back to her own door to answer it, and she said it flatly, at about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and it was the answer of a woman who has kept a house open at the fourth hour for nine years and has been keeping one at the fifth hour for four and a half.

“**The number goes down by one and nobody hears it go down, and that is the whole of the answer, and I have kept a house open at the fourth hour for nine years and I have never once known who was in it.**”

Nobody said anything back to her. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a woman who keeps a house open has just said that the rule has a hole in it, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that the rule was written by a man of thirty-two on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth in fifty-two words, and that a woman of about fifty-one has found the hole in it in forty-four, and five have said that neither of them has written it down. About four people in nine counties have said since that this county has now had two people tell it the same thing in different words in twenty-six days and has not put the two things together.

He was at the sea end of that street when she said it and he heard the whole of it, and it was the fourth thing in about four and a half years that a person in this county had found a hole in something of his before he had found it himself, and he had spent four days of made road believing that he had asked the question and everybody else had only answered it.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the fourth hour of a Friday is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-first of Hearthmonth is a Friday and the lamp was not put there. The water was not over the causeway and the hand bell was not rung. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that a lamp that is put out on a Monday afternoon in the winter is the only thing this county does on a Monday that is not work, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked the man who lights it why he does it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing, and a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years. About four people in nine counties have said since that the standing line and the dead line are about a mile apart on the same shoulder, and that one of them is nine feet wide and the other is nine feet wide, and five have said that a young man of about nineteen left the end of his own line standing on purpose and nobody has ever asked him which end. About four people in nine counties have said since that the end of a line is the part a man is least likely to want cut.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. The fruit that came down into four hundred yards of grass in the second week of Fallowmonth was still in it under the first frost. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine households own two miles of a made road in this county, and that one of them has a neighbour nine hundred yards above him with a mile of stone on the high side, and five have said that neither of the two has ever been asked about the other.

The road office at the low end of that market street has the printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth in it still, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it, and that is forty-four days. About four people in nine counties have said since that a county which has been asked once for a name has not sent it, and a county which has been asked twice for a name will be asked a third time, and five have said that about nine people in nine counties could name the man who holds that wall this morning and that about nine people would name him in a market street. About four have said that naming a man in a market street is not the same as putting a name on a piece of paper, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked whether it is.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee, and the tops of them are all at the same height, and the dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them. About nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them, and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years. About four people in nine counties have said since that a boundary nine hundred years old that a scar has stopped at is a boundary that a scar was told where to stop, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked who told it.

Then the fourth hour of that Friday, which is before the light, and four people in nine counties knew that five of them were going to be on a floor four days off and about nine hundred did not. The number that was said out loud on that floor that morning was five and it was said twice. The man of about thirty-four with a satchel said it first and the man of about fifty-four who keeps the floor said it again. About four people in nine counties have said since that the man who has said a figure out loud every morning for four and a half years said another man's number this morning, and five have said that a floor is a place where two people who do not agree about a thing can both be heard saying it, which is about the only use a floor has that a room does not.

**And the cost of that Friday is a rule with a hole in it that two people have found in twenty-six days and neither of them has written down, ten days of a man's silence that is not a refusal, a roadkeeper who will hold one thing until the first of Mudmonth and not one day past it, about two hundred and thirty carts of lime of which the next is due on the twenty-seventh and has not been late yet, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, and the use of one ear and the use of one leg, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
