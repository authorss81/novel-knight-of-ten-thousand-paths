# Chapter 0784

**Harvestmonth the twenty-seventh, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it and a floor four days off that said no number that morning, and twenty-five days of a roll of roads on a board with nothing on any of its nine lines.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh of Harvestmonth with about four hundred people in it and about nine carts along both sides and the sun off the west bank at about the fourth hour. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them hard and white and the roll nailed over the top of it with four nails in the frame. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Thursday, and the ordinary part is that a Thursday market is about two thirds of a Saturday market and about twice a Monday, and that about nine hundred people along a made road do not know which of the three they are standing in.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was this day, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is seventy-three drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and four have said since that seventy-three is the first of the figures in this county that has gone up three in nine days and that nobody has said why.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for five hundred and five days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that five hundred and five days is a year and five months and that five months is a season a man can plan a thing inside and has not planned one.

The practice has run one hundred and sixty-seven mornings and no number was said on the twenty-seventh of Harvestmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water, and four people in nine counties have said since that a practice which has run one hundred and sixty-seven mornings has been said out loud on about a hundred and sixty-seven mornings and has been written down on none of them, and five have said that a figure nobody can check is a figure that a county can lose in about four months without noticing that it had it.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is two hundred and twenty-four days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is two hundred and five days. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and twenty-four days is most of a season and that two hundred and five days shut is most of a year, and five have said that a shut track the people at the end of it shut themselves is the only shut track in nine counties nobody has a paper for, and about four have said that about nine houses behind it are not a body and have not been asked for an account.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is two hundred and seventy-two days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that two hundred and seventy-two days is about nine months and that nine months is the length of a debt nobody in this county has been paid for, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades and that neither of them has anybody holding it.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences and a roll is nailed over the top of it. The notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and forty-six days past, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and forty-six days is close to five months, and five had not, and four have said to each other that a form with a Tuesday in it is past the point where a county can pretend it has not been given.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files it, and five say that filing is not holding, and about four have been saying that the roll is the third paper out of one office in about a year and that three papers for one name is not a question any more, it is a habit, and about nine people in that street could tell you which of the three they have seen.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper, and that not one of those nine has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name either, and five have said that about nine people who said no once are not a county and that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had dried in about nine hours of sun and taken a heel again. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board he had set on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them at about the seventh hour, and the young man of about nineteen went in among the frames and lifted one skirt and set it back and stayed looking at it for about nine minutes. Four people in nine counties have said since that a young man of about nineteen who looks at one skirt for about nine minutes is looking at a thing he will not say out loud, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said that nothing in that yard is on any board in this county and nobody has to act on it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that flat broom has now been down about five weeks and that five weeks of roots is the point after which cutting it would be a different job and not a rescue, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line is about four and a half acres and about four have been saying that it will not close over and is not going to be cut this year either.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill and went down the four hundred pace lane to the market again at about the tenth. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

The roll had been on those boards twenty-five days and about nine of its nine lines were empty and about nine people in that street had stood at it, and about four hundred people had walked past it, and not one person in nine counties had put a name against a road on any of the nine lines, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that twenty-five days is most of a month and that most of a month is how long a thing takes to stop being news, and five had not.

At about the ninth hour of that Thursday morning the man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane to stand at those boards and to see whether a month of a roll had put anything on any of it, and that was his goal for the morning and he kept it past the eleventh hour. He had meant to hear what a county said after it had been given three answers that do not fit and to be able to say out loud which one about four hundred people had kept, and he had not found out, and he did not ask, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who goes to a board to hear what a county kept has not understood that about four hundred people is not a county, and five have said that about nine people stopped at that board on the twenty-seventh of Harvestmonth and about four hundred went on and that nine is the number this county keeps and that nine is not the number it needs.

About nine people asked him four different things that morning at those boards and he answered none of them, and the four questions were whether the roll was going to be filled in, who was going to fill it in, what happened on the Tuesday, and whether the hurdles at the head of the cart track came down. Four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a man who is asked four things in nine minutes and answers none of them has said something, and five had not, and nobody in nine counties commented on it.

The roadkeeper of about fifty-two was at those boards at about the tenth hour with a bar in his hand and about nine people round him and he said nothing out loud at all that morning, and about four people had worked out inside nine seconds that a man who claimed out loud three weeks ago that a bar will get up a shut track and said in the same sentence that he would not be the one to try it has nothing to say about a form, and five had not, and he went down the made road toward the coast at about the eleventh hour without trying anything.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four of its four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day for about nine hours.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Harvestmonth is dim and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the twenty-seventh of Harvestmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-seventh of Harvestmonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Thursday by anybody. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that four hundred people who have never been across a water and about nine hundred people who have never been told about a shut track are both kept by nobody and both are on the roll, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile are the second set of people in this county with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that a form wants one name against a line and nine households have between them nine names and that does not fit on one line.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about two hundred and seventy-two days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a market morning is a morning for questions and about four hundred people and is not a morning for a number said to seven on a floor four days off.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who is asked four things and answers none of them is a man nobody thanks, and about four have said that this is the third time in a year that the whole of what he did in a market street was not speaking. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and sixty-seven mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and a roll of nine lines had been on a board for twenty-five days with nothing on any of them and a Tuesday twenty-six days off on it.

He went out of that market street at about the noon with the leg bad and nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and the roll on those boards had a Tuesday in it and no name in it and the roadkeeper had said nothing and the man of thirty-two had said nothing at all.
