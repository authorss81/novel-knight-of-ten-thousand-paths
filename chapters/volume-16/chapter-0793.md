# Chapter 0793

**Embermonth the thirtieth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it, the last day of a month that carried twenty-nine days before it, and a lamp that came down thirteen stairs and went back up them again without anybody asking for it.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the thirtieth of Embermonth with about four hundred people in it and the wind gone round to the west and the boards at the low end standing with the notice on them gone soft with the wet and two copies of a roll nailed one over the other on the frame of them. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up the thirteen stairs of it, and the cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of the last market morning of a month, and the ordinary part is that the last Thursday of a month is the morning on which about nine hundred people find out what they owe somebody and about nine hundred find out that they do not.

A drove went up that lane four days off on that same Thursday, the thirtieth of Embermonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and two men went with it. That is eighty-two drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and four have said since that eighty-two is the last figure of a month anybody in nine counties will say out loud and that the first of the next one is a Friday and a figure nobody here will say at all.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and is about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for five hundred and sixty-eight days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that five hundred and sixty-eight days is a year and seven months and that seven months is the length of time after which a man carrying stone every day is not carrying it for anybody.

The practice has run two hundred and twelve mornings and no number was said on the thirtieth of Embermonth, because the room over that weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one, and about nine people in nine counties have said that a practice which keeps its own hours is a practice and not a habit. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water, and it is four on this morning as it was on the first morning of about a year ago, and four people in nine counties have said since that a number which has been four for about seven months is a number nobody in this county has to be reminded of, and five have said that a floor with nobody on it is the only way to be reminded of nothing.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and eight of the ten were held at the fourth hour of the morning before this one and two were not. The first of a month is tomorrow and it is a Friday, and it is the twenty-first first of a month in a row, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the first of a month is the day this county used to put something in a street and stopped about four and a half years ago, and five have said that twenty-one is not a record and is not a refusal and is not a holding.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days in Mudmonth and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is two hundred and eighty-seven days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is two hundred and sixty-eight days. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and eighty-seven days is nine and a half months and that nine and a half months is a season and a half of a road being quiet, and five have said that about nine houses four hundred yards behind nine hurdles would like them to stay and said so once through a shut door, and nobody has been up there a third time.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is three hundred and thirty-five days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that three hundred and thirty-five days is most of a year and twenty-five days short of a whole one and that twenty-five days is nearly a month of a thing nobody has been asked to hold, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades, and about four have said that it does not close over.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences and two copies of a roll are nailed one over the other on the frame of them, and about nine people read it that morning and about four hundred did not stop. The notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is two hundred and nine days past, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that two hundred and nine days is one day short of seven months, and five had not, and four have said to each other that a paper one day short of seven months past its own date has been overtaken by the year it was printed in.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that four papers out of one office in about a year is an office that files, and five say that filing is not holding, and about four have been saying that nobody in nine counties has answered any of them and that about four hundred people in that street have stopped counting them.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and are in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper, and that not one of those nine has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name either, and five have said that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold.

The roll has been on those boards eighty-eight days and its nine lines are empty on both copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and about four hundred people had walked past it that morning and about nine had stood at it and had said nothing out loud at it. Four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that eighty-eight days is a season of weather on a board, and five had not, and about four have said since that a paper which has been through a whole month of rain and a whole month of dry is a paper nobody looks at any more, and that is not the same as a paper nobody has answered.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it was a foot wide and half an inch deep and had taken a heel again. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and four have said that a roll of nine hundred names with no date on it is the only roll in this county that cannot be used against anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times and does not touch a frame has found a third way of saying no, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and four have said that nothing in that yard is on any board in nine counties.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing on it, and the water laid nearly all of that flat. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that the mat of it has gone into the ground at the edges and that a mat with the ground coming up through it is a mat that will be under it next year whatever anybody does, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line is about four and a half acres and four have been saying that it will not close over in this summer either.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs twice that morning and went back up twice, and about nine people at the tables watched her do it and about five did not. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none that morning, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is not the record.

At about the fifth hour of that Thursday the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs with that lamp in her hand and set it on the trestle table by that weigh-house floor, where about nine people were standing because it is a market morning and people wait about at a weigh-house. The man of thirty-two with a stick was at that table with a tally-stick in his other hand and the woman of about twenty-nine was in the lane with the day-book under her arm, and the copyist put the lamp down in the middle of them, took the wet cloth off the table, shook it out and hung it on the rail, put a dry one on the table and went back up those thirteen stairs, and she said nothing to anybody about it.

Then at about the seventh hour the same lamp went back up those stairs and the room it belongs to was open, and the man of thirty-two and the woman of about twenty-nine had not said one word to each other about the lamp at any point in the day and had not looked up when it was set down. About four people in that lane had worked out inside nine seconds what a lamp at the foot of a stair coming down and going back up on a morning when the room was open is, and five had not, and nobody in nine counties has said anything about it since, and nothing about it is in any document this county keeps, and about four people have said that the person who asked was a third person and that is the whole of what a third person is for.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it and is not going to, and about four people in that lane had worked out inside nine seconds that a press drawer is about eleven inches deep and that a stick of chalk is about three inches of it.

At about the ninth hour the man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane to stand at those boards on the last market morning of a month, and that was the whole of what he had come for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to find out who was on a weigh-house floor at the fifth hour of a market morning when the practice does not run, and about nine people were, and one of them had brought a lamp down thirteen stairs for them and taken it back up. He had been wrong about the morning before the ninth hour was half out, and the wrongness was that he had believed the thing to be looked at was a practice and it is not a practice, it is a floor that people stand on when there is nothing else to stand on.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and then pick it up again about nine minutes later. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Embermonth is dim and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the thirtieth of Embermonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirtieth of Embermonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there, and about four hundred Mondays have gone by with it not being put there on any day but Monday. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a lamp on a coping and a lamp on a trestle table are both things a county puts out for somebody and that neither of them has been asked for.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile are the second set of people in this county with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and four have kept saying that nine households have nine names between them and a form has one line and that about nobody has been asked to make them fit out of any of them.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about three hundred and thirty-five days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and about four people in nine counties have been saying that a lamp came down thirteen stairs at the fifth hour of a market morning and went back up at the seventh, and that this is not in any book in this county and is the only thing in about seven months that is in nobody's mouth either.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who stands at a trestle table with a tally-stick on a market morning is a man nobody thanks, because the tally-stick is not his and the table is not his. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for two hundred and twelve mornings by nine people saying a number twice in the dark, and he was about to be off that floor for eleven mornings and had told nobody that either.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and the roll was still on those boards with eighty-eight days on it and nine empty lines on each of two copies, and tomorrow was the twenty-first first of a month in a row and nothing would stand in that street for it.
