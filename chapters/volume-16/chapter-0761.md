# Chapter 0761

**Thawmonth the eighth, YR 323, a Wednesday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with nine people on it, and a man from a mill lane saying out loud that a thing is being done to a road.**

A Wednesday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was dark at the fourth hour of the eighth of Thawmonth with about nine doors shut along it and the boards at the low end standing with nothing on them. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the cloth nine doors down was still over the bench where it had been since the middle of Hearthmonth. The hearing had not sat on the first of the month and nobody in nine counties had expected it to, and that was the fifteenth first of a month in a row.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Wednesday, and the ordinary part is that a road in Thawmonth is wet underfoot and nobody marks it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the third of Thawmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is forty-eight drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that a drove nobody watches is still a drove.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and thirty-six days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man carrying stone for three hundred and thirty-six days is past asking.

The practice has run fifty-three mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the eighth of Thawmonth is eight, and it has been eight since the eleventh of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that fifty-three mornings is a practice and not a habit, and five have said that the way to tell is whether the road under it holds, and about four have said since that the road under it is four days of made road in Thawmonth and Thawmonth is the month that finds out.

Two of the ten things are not held. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is fifty-five days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that a track nobody has been up for weeks is a thing nobody can hold, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all and that the ninth has been held by nobody since the thirteenth of Hearthmonth.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and three days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and three days old with no name against it is one of the four roads in this county that notice is going to shut, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has been asked to name any of the four.

A printed notice came down that made road in a cart in the ordinary way before the twentieth of Goatmonth and was read out loud at those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is in twenty-three days and is a Friday, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that twenty-three days is not a long time to find four names, and five had not.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it, and that is more than ninety days. About four people in nine counties have said since that the same office has asked this county for one name twice in two forms and has had no answer to either, and five have said that answering the second one answers the first one without anybody deciding to.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that two men nobody asks anything are the reason the county can still say no.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them, and the two mile stones between the seventh and the ninth that had come out of line are in line and have not walked.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen has said out loud twice that he is not going to make more bees than the bees he has got. About four people in nine counties have said since that five frames under nine skirts in Thawmonth is a thing that waits on the weather, and five have said that the weather in Thawmonth waits on nothing.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing, and a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years. About four people in nine counties have said since that a standing line and a dead line a mile apart are the two things in this county that will not close over, and five have said that nobody has asked which of the two matters.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that floor to hear the number said twice and to do nothing else, and that was his goal for the morning and he held it the whole way through the dark. He had walked the four days up the made road with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand, and the wet had got into the seam of his boot by the second mile and stayed there, and he had told himself that a morning of listening was a morning of work. He had been wrong about the morning before it started, and the wrongness was that he thought listening was a thing he could do without being seen to do it, and a man standing on a floor with eight other people in the dark is seen by all of them. The cost of it was in the leg by the sixth hour, which had gone from a dull ache at the fourth to a sharp pull behind the knee, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had filled with the sound of rain on slate until nothing through it came clean.

At about the ninth hour of that Wednesday a man from a mill lane four miles to the north came into that market street on his own business and stopped at the third door and said a thing out loud that nobody at those boards had said before. He keeps a driftway for beasts between two pastures, and he had about nine households behind him and a council that had voted the month before to bar the driftway at both ends until the frost was off. He stood with his coat wet through and his hands red after about nine miles of rain, and about four people in that street stopped to hear him because a man who comes four miles in rain has come about something.

“**They are lifting the bars off my driftway on Friday to get a cart through with paper in it, and my council voted it shut until the frost is off, and the cart is not theirs and the paper is not mine.**”

The man of thirty-two heard all of it from nine feet off, and the rain ran off the brim of his hat and into his collar while he listened for about nine seconds longer than he needed. About four people in that market street had heard a man in a trade say out loud that a thing was being done to a road, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

Aren Kest asked the man one thing, in his own trade, standing still in the rain, and it was not the council he asked next.

“**How many households walk that driftway, and will they say out loud what they want done with it?**”

The mill man looked at him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and the rain made lines down his face that could have been anything.

“**Nine households, and they have said it once each to me and not to anybody else, and they want it shut until the frost is off the way the vote said.**”

About four people in nine counties have said since that a man of thirty-two asked the households and not the council on the eighth of Thawmonth, and five have said that asking nine households in rain is a longer road than asking three men in a dry room, and about four have said since that the asking is what cost him, because a council writes a thing down and households remember who came.

He went north the next morning before light with the mill man, four miles through wet grass with the stick sinking at every step, and asked all nine households one by one at their doors for about four minutes each what they wanted done with the driftway. Three doors were shut and he waited at each of them until somebody came, and at the fourth door a dog came out and he stood his ground and the woman of the house laughed at him and then answered. By about the fifth hour he had nine answers from about nine doors and they were all the same, and his coat weighed about twice what it had weighed in the morning and the leg would not take his weight on the first attempt at any stile. He had been wrong about the time it would take by half a morning, and the wrongness cost him the market street at noon and the hearing boards and a dry hour he had meant to use for the stones. The cost sat behind his knee all the way back and in the side of his head where the rain had got in through the deaf side until voices came to him as if down a wet lane.

At about the ninth hour of that Thursday he stood at the third door again and said what the nine households had said, out loud, to four people in and five out, and he did not go to the council room at all.

“**Nine households on that driftway want it shut until the frost is off, and their council voted it shut, and a cart with paper in it does not lift bars that nine households asked to have kept on.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a man say what nine households wanted, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him. About four people in nine counties have said since that no route was opened on the eighth of Thawmonth and none was shut either, and that a man asking households instead of a council is not a decision, and five have said that it is the only thing in this county that has ever stopped a cart without moving a bar.

The ordinary part of that Wednesday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again after about nine seconds. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street in about nine inches of wet and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes after about four tries without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a full house with no board out is a house that expects nobody and turns away none.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards after about nine seconds of light, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the fourth hour of a Wednesday in Thawmonth is still dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades with about nine errands between them and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody for the rain holding off, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a Wednesday with no market in it is a day nobody is thanked on.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the eighth of Thawmonth is a Wednesday and the lamp was not put there. The water was not over the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred people live at this end as well, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that a driftway four miles north with nine households behind it is the same shape as two miles of wall with nine households behind it, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked either nine what they want twice.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years. About four people in nine counties have said since that a boundary nine hundred years old is the only thing on that shoulder that anybody here can date, and five have said that nobody has ever asked the nine households whether they knew.

Then the fourth hour of that Wednesday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was eight and it was said twice. The man of about thirty-four with a satchel said it first and the woman of about fifty-one said it again, and the other seven on that floor heard the same number at the same hour on the same morning, and the day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who walks four miles in rain to ask nine households is a man nobody thanks, because a council would have thanked him and households do not. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for fifty-three mornings by nine people who said a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice twenty-three days off that wanted four names on a piece of paper, and the two men who could have said about nine names between them had said nothing to each other for years and one of them had not been asked a question at all.

He went out of that weigh-house at about the sixth hour with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. He had four days of road behind him and four miles of wet grass in front of him and then four days again, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Wednesday is a driftway four miles north with nine households behind it, nine answers got one by one at wet doors, a cart with paper in it that did not lift a bar, twenty-three days to the first of Mudmonth, a number of eight in nine heads and on no paper, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet fifty-five days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
