# Chapter 0775

**Longlight the fourteenth, YR 323, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and a man with a stick at a stone trough at the bottom of a cart track.**

A Monday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the fourteenth of Longlight with about nine people in it and dust on the boards. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them dry and hard. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part is that Longlight dries a road faster than it wets one and that a Monday in it is about four hours of good going and about nine hours of dust.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the fifty-eighth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and one man went with it. That is sixty-two drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that sixty-two is a figure nobody would put on a board.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and thirty-two days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and thirty-two days is a year and seventy days over and that seventy days is one season of stone.

The practice has run one hundred and fifteen mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of Longlight is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is one hundred and twenty-one mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the fourteenth of Longlight, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that one hundred and fifteen mornings is close to four months and that about four months is what a thing needs before somebody asks where the figure went, and five have said that nobody has asked because nobody has to.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and fifty-one days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and thirty-two days, and the man of thirty-two had been about nine feet short of those nine hurdles at about the ninth hour of the Monday before and had not been near them since. Four people in nine counties have said since that the ninth thing is not dead, and five have said that not dead is not a holding and that a road nine people keep shut is not a road anybody holds, and four have said since that the ninth thing has been looked at twice by one man and is on a page nowhere and is in the mouth of nobody.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and ninety-nine days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and ninety-nine days old is past half a year, and five have said that about half a year is the length of a scar that stays and not the length of a scar that goes.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is seventy-three days past and is a Friday gone, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that seventy-three days is about two months and a fortnight, and five had not, and four have said since that two months and a fortnight is how long it takes for a date to stop being news and become a habit of not asking.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five have said that filing is not holding, and four have said since that a request for a copy is a request for a person as well and that this county has answered neither half of it.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that two men wrote no twice and one woman has kept nine houses shut for nine years and there is not one clerk in nine counties who knows any of the three things.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, which is the other of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and the dust had put about an inch of grey in it. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen went in among them at about the eighth hour and found nothing shifted and came out and shut the gate behind him with his hand on it, which is a thing he does about nine times a week. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who shuts a gate with his hand is a man who has begun to notice gates, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and four have said since that nine times a week is nine hundred times in the third month.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom will not close over, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of it is a thatch nobody has offered to take, and four have said since that four hundred people in a market town have never once walked over the middle of it.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and no head was filled or ruled anywhere in that week, and about nine people in that street had counted thirteen stairs more than once without arriving at a different number.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the eleven miles from that cart track in about nine hours with the leg in a state he had not had since the water, and had meant to go on down the four days of made road to the market town and say one thing out loud about nine houses, and that was his goal and he carried it the whole way. He had meant to say it in a market street on a day when about four hundred people were in it, because a market street is the only room this county has and he had never once used one for anything a person had not already asked him. He had been wrong about the way to say it before the second mile was behind him, and the wrongness was that he had believed a thing about four hundred people hearing it, and four hundred people who hear a thing in a market street are about four hundred people who have heard a carter turn round, and the asking cost the asker and the telling will cost the teller, and this county has nine people who found that out on the first of Mudmonth and no clerk who has. The cost of it was in the leg, which had gone from a hot band to a dead weight below the knee by the fourth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had been at a low note all the way down and had not come back to anything he could use.

At about the seventh hour of that Monday he came to the stone trough at the bottom of that cart track, where the made road ends, and there was a man of about thirty-one at it with two buckets and a rope, and his name was Abel Chinn, and he filled the trough at about twice a week and carried water up eleven miles on his own back because there is no other water at the top of that track that a house will drink.

The man of about thirty-two stopped at the trough and put the flat of his stick down to get his breath, and the man of about thirty-one spoke first, in his own trade, to one person, and nobody had asked him anything.

“**Nine hurdles foot to foot and our own hands in September, and I count what does not come up, and I have carried one woman over them and they went back the same evening.**”

Four people at that trough had heard a man at a water trough give a count of a shut track without being asked, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who keeps a trough about a mile from nine hurdles is the only person in nine counties whose trade has made him count them, and five have said that a count nobody asked for is the first kind of record there is, and about four have said since that about nine years of counting what does not come up a cart track is a figure nobody in nine counties has asked for and never will.

Then the man of thirty-one said the one thing he had been working up to since about the fourth hour, and it was seven words, and he said it to one person in his own trade.

“**Are you going to write it down?**”

The man of thirty-two with a stick said it back, short, in his own trade, to one person, and it was nineteen words.

“**There is nowhere in this county I could write it down that a clerk would ever look at it.**”

The man of thirty-one did not argue with that and did not agree with it, and he filled the second bucket, and then he said one more thing, and it was twenty-six words, and he said it to the trough as much as to the man.

“**Then do not write it down. Write it on something and put it where the rain cannot get at it, and do not tell me where.**”

Four people in nine counties have said since that this is the second time anybody in that county has put a thing to a person about a thing done on their own account and the first was on the Wednesday before, and about four have said since that a man at a trough who tells a stranger to write something down and not to say where is a man who has given away about the only thing he has that nobody has asked him for. Five have said that about a hundred and thirty-two days and about nine hurdles and about two buckets of water a week is the whole of the paper in that place, and about four have said since that the two of them will never speak again and neither of them will ever say that they did.

The man of thirty-two went on down the four days of made road after that and got into that market town at about the noon of that Monday with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand and about nine miles of ache in it, and he did not say the thing about nine houses in the street, because the street had about nine people in it and about five of them were behind shut doors. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who carries a thing eleven miles to a street and then does not say it has not wasted the eleven miles, because he has learned which streets are rooms and which are about nine people in the open air.

The ordinary part of that Monday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Longlight is dim and there is nothing off the dry slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the fourteenth of Longlight either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fourteenth of Longlight is a Monday and the lamp was put there that afternoon, and the water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a lamp lit on the second Monday running is a habit and not a light, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile have never once been asked what they think about a roll, and five have said that about four hundred yards off there are nine households that have never once been asked either, and about four have said since that this is the shape of the whole thing and that the shape is that the asking has to happen at somebody's own door.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and the ground round them had dried in the sun of that afternoon. Four people in nine counties have said since that a thing nobody will look over is kept better by weather than by anybody, and five have said that a rota of about nine years is a kind of wall.

Then the fourth hour of that Monday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing a man said at a trough about eleven days ago is either.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man who is told at a trough that he has nowhere to write a thing is a man nobody thanks, because being told where you cannot write is not an invitation. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and fifteen mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice seventy-three days past its date that had shut nothing.

He went into that market street at about the noon with the leg bad and did not go out to the ninth mile stone, and the boards at the low end had the notice on them and about nine people behind them, and the copyist of about twenty-six had the register under her arm and was going up thirteen stairs that were not hers. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has come back from a road with the whole of it in his head and nobody in his county to put it in is a man who will have to find somebody to put it in, and four have said since that nine days is a long time to carry a thing and not put it anywhere, and five have said that this is not a cure and that it is a job and that a job is what a man has instead of a road.

**And the cost of that Monday is about eleven miles down and four days up in a week, a dead weight below the knee by the fourth hour, a man at a trough who counts what does not come up, seven words asked and nineteen words answered and twenty-six words given, a thing carried down a made road and not said in a street with about nine people in it, a ninth thing that is looked at and not written and not open, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
