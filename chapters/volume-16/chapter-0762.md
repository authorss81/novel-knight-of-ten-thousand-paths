# Chapter 0762

**Thawmonth the sixteenth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour at the boards with a cart tally in them, and a floor four days off that said no number that morning.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the sixteenth of Thawmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts at both ends. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice still on them where it had been put on the twentieth of Goatmonth. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and nobody had lifted it, and about four people in that street had stopped expecting anybody to.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Thursday, and the ordinary part is that carts go down wet and come back wet and nobody writes it down.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday itself at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is fifty drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that fifty drove is a round figure nobody counted on purpose.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and forty-four days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three hundred and forty-four days is a figure nobody says out loud twice.

The practice has run fifty-eight mornings and no number was said on the sixteenth of Thawmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is eight, and it has been eight since the eleventh of Goatmonth, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a number held over a market day is a number held on trust, and five have said that trust is the whole of the practice.

Two of the ten things are not held. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is sixty-three days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that sixty-three days without a cart is a road speaking in the only voice it has, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has gone to listen to it.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and eleven days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and eleven days old is one of four roads with no name against any of them, and five have said that the first of Mudmonth is in fifteen days and is a Friday.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. About four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that fifteen days is two market weeks and a day, and five had not, and about four have said since that a market street does its thinking on market mornings and forgets it by noon.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that the same office has asked twice in two forms and has had no answer to either, and five have said that nobody in nine counties is going to answer the second one on a market morning either.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a market morning is the worst morning to ask a man anything, because there are about four hundred witnesses.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen was at the yard wall at about the seventh hour looking at the skirts and not lifting one. About four people in nine counties have said since that a young man who looks and does not lift is keeping his own word to himself, and five have said that he said out loud twice he would make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that two sayings is a promise even if nobody heard the second.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing. About four people in nine counties have said since that a standing line in Thawmonth is wet at the root and dry at the top, and five have said that nobody has asked what that means for the cutting.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with a tally in her hand. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that market street to buy nails and to hear what a market thinks, and that was his goal for the morning and he told it to himself plainly on the way down the lane. He had about nine nails in mind and about four questions, and he meant to spend coin on the first and nothing on the second. He had been wrong about the morning before the ninth hour was out, and the wrongness was that he thought a market morning was a morning for hearing, and a market morning is a morning for selling, and nobody tells a man with a stick anything until he has bought something. The cost of it was in the leg by the tenth hour from standing on wet stone without moving, which had set the knee to throbbing behind the cap, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had filled with stall noise until the woman at the third door had to say her price twice and he heard it only the second time on his left.

Renn Ottery was at the boards at about the ninth hour with his tally-stick, and about four people in that street stopped because a man who keeps a floor does not bring his stick to market. He stood with the stick across his hands and said his figure out loud to four people in and five out, the way he says it on his floor, and the rain had spotted the wood dark.

“**No cart since the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, and that is sixty-three days, and I have never once been one short in about four and a half years until this one, and I am one short still.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a man say he was short and stay standing, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about nine people at the stalls heard the whole of it over the noise. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man saying a shortfall on a market morning is doing the same thing he does on his floor, and five have said that a market is not a floor and the saying lands different.

Pell Arkwright was nine feet off with lime dust still in his cuffs from two months back, and he had come down about eleven miles on his own business again. He looked at the tally-stick and then at the boards with the notice on them, and said one thing in his own trade, to one person, with about nine people hearing.

“**Nine houses voted on the eleventh of Hearthmonth and I was the ninth vote and I told them I would not be the tenth, and I have carried that about two months, and the carts do not go while the hurdles are across.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a man of about twenty-seven name his vote and his limit in the same breath, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that a ninth vote who will not be a tenth is a man holding a line nobody drew for him, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has asked him to draw it either.

The man of thirty-two stood nine feet off and heard both halves, and understood about half of it there at the boards and the other half about a mile up the made road later in the day when the noise had gone out of his head. A figure said on a market morning is not a floor figure, because a floor figure is said to nine people who know what it means and a market figure is said to about four hundred who do not. He had thought a market would hear arithmetic the way a floor does, and he had been wrong, and the wrongness followed him all the way down the four hundred pace lane. The cost of standing through it was a knee that would not bend clean on the stairs and an ear that rang with stall cries until past noon, and he sat on the coping at the low side of the lane for about nine minutes with the stick across his knees until the ringing thinned.

At about the eleventh hour he went back to the boards and said one thing out loud, to four people in and five out, standing in his own trade with lime dust on his boots from the floor four days off.

“**The floor is one short and the track is shut by nine hurdles, and the two are the same thing found twice, once by counting and once by walking, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a man put counting and walking side by side, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him either. About four people in nine counties have said since that putting two findings together on a market morning is the nearest thing this county has to a meeting, and five have said that a meeting that cannot be called is still not a meeting.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again after about nine seconds. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street in about nine inches of wet and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had all four of its beds full and put a board out on the made street for the first time in about nine days, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a board out on a market morning is a promise of supper.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards after about nine seconds of light, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Thawmonth is still dim and there is something off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody for the market, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a market morning is a day for prices and not for thanks.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the sixteenth of Thawmonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there. The water was not over the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Thursday by anybody. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred people live at this end as well, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine households owning two miles is a roll nobody has written, and five have said that a roll of people is not a roll of roads and the notice wants the second.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years. About four people in nine counties have said since that a rota kept nine years is a practice nobody wrote down, and five have said that this county is learning what those cost.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That had happened about twice a week for about ten weeks, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a practice that stops twice a week and starts again is either strong or lucky, and five have said that nobody knows which.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who buys nails and says a finding at the boards on a market morning is a man nobody thanks, because the nails are paid for and the finding is not. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for fifty-eight mornings by nine people who said a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice fifteen days off that wanted four names on a piece of paper.

He went out of that market street at about noon with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand and nails in his pocket, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. He had about nine miles of wet in his boots from standing, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a market morning with about four hundred people in it, a tally-stick carried to market, a shortfall of one said out loud where a floor cannot hear it, a ninth vote named again after about two months, fifteen days to the first of Mudmonth, a number of eight held over a market and on no paper, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet sixty-three days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
