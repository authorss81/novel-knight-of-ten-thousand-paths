# Chapter 0783

**Harvestmonth the tenth, YR 323, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and the third of three answers to a roll of roads given out loud in a lane with about nine people in it.**

A Monday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the tenth of Harvestmonth with about nine people in it and low cloud on the tops and the boards at the low end standing with the notice on them hard and white and the roll nailed over the top of it. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part is that a Monday in the tenth day of Harvestmonth is a day about nine hours of light in it and about nine hundred people on a made road all of whom have somewhere to be that is not this county.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the sixth of Harvestmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is seventy drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the same seventy went up four days ago, and four people in nine counties have said since that a drove which has not moved in four days is a drove somebody has stopped counting, and five have said that about nobody in nine counties except the man who counts it.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and eighty-eight days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about four hundred and eighty-eight days is sixteen weeks and that sixteen weeks is a quarter and that a quarter of a year is the length of time it takes a man to decide that a thing he has carried every day for a quarter of a year is his to stop.

The practice has run one hundred and fifty-five mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the tenth of Harvestmonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is one hundred and sixty-one mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the tenth of Harvestmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that about one hundred and fifty-five mornings of a number in two voices is the only thing this county can show anybody who comes down that made road with a question, and five have said that two answers given out loud in a market street in four days have gone about nine miles and the number has gone none at all.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is two hundred and seven days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and eighty-eight days. Four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and eighty-eight days is six months and that six months is the length of time after which a place stops being somewhere a person has been and becomes somewhere a person has heard of, and about four have said that a shut road with nine hurdles on it that people put there themselves is the only shut road in nine counties nobody has a legal hold on.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is two hundred and fifty-five days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that two hundred and fifty-five days is eight months and that eight months is longer than a pregnancy of anything in this county except a debt, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades and that nobody has to act on either.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences and a roll is nailed over the top of it. The notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and twenty-nine days past, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and twenty-nine days is about four months and nine days, and five had not, and four have said to each other that a form is a notice with a line on it and that a line is a place a person can be put.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files it, and five say that filing is not holding, and about four have been saying that the roll is the third paper out of one office in about a year and that three papers for one name is not a question any more, it is a habit.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper and none of them has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name, and five have said that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold and that about seven months of that is now the shape of this county.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board he had set on the skirt stones at the gate was taken off and put back on again that morning, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has taken honey about nine times and not touched a frame has found a third way of saying no, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said that nothing about that yard is on any board and that nobody has to act on it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that flat broom has now been down about a month and that a month of not being cut is a month of roots, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line is about four and a half acres and about four have been saying that it will not close over and is not going to be cut this year either.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs that day and has not come down them on a Monday in about four and a half years. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four people in nine counties had seen the thumb-drag across the end of the top line and read it for a moment as the first stroke of a letter, and nobody has read the four lines out loud and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it.

At about the ninth hour of that Monday the man of about fifty-four who keeps that weigh-house floor came out of the weigh-house door with the tally-stick in his hand and about nine people were in that lane, and he said one thing to the man of thirty-two with a stick, short, in his own trade, to one person, and it was sixteen words, and about four of the nine heard it.

“**Ask your keeper about the fourth line, she has the book and I have the floor.**”

Four people in that lane had heard a man say the word keeper to another man about a woman in a room thirteen stairs up, and about five had not, and nobody said anything back to either of them, and nobody in nine counties has said since that the word was wrong or that it was right. Four people in nine counties have said since that two people in four and a half years have between them a room a man in a lane calls hers about half a mile from where she is standing, and five have said that a word said once in a lane is not a thing anybody has to do anything about.

Then the man of thirty-two with a stick had come up that four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour to stand at the foot of those thirteen stairs and to have said nothing to anybody, and that was his goal for the morning and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to hear the third thing that had been said about that roll and to be able to say out loud whether it was the same as the other two, and he had not known before the ninth hour was half out that the third thing was going to be said in this lane. He had been wrong about the morning before the light, and the wrongness was that he had believed a refusal had to be about a road to be a refusal, and a road is the one thing a form cannot take from anybody who has none.

At about the ninth hour of that Monday the woman of about twenty-nine came down four of those thirteen stairs with the day-book under her arm and stopped on the step, and there were about nine people in that lane and about four hundred yards of made road behind them and about nine hundred people along it who had not heard anything. She said one thing out loud, short, in her own trade, to about nine people, and it was forty-five words, and nine of them heard all of it.

“**A form nobody is paid to fill is a form a county cannot fill, and there is nobody in nine counties paid to govern, and about fourpence a year is what a person is paid for the side of a book the record is on.**”

Four people in that lane had heard a keeper of records say that a form nobody is paid to fill is a form a county cannot fill, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to her for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. Four people in nine counties have been saying since that this is the third answer given to the roll in five days and that it is not about a road at all, and five have said that a refusal to govern is not a refusal to fill in a line and that fourpence a year is the whole of what anybody in this county is paid for keeping the only book in it.

Four people in nine counties have been saying since that the three answers cannot be held together and that no county of about two thousand people can hold two of them, and that the first says a road with no name against it is a road nobody can shut, and the second says a keeper's name is a thing that is his and is not to be entered under another hand, and the third says a county that will not fill a form is a county that will not govern, and five have said that about a county which cannot shut a road and cannot fill a line and will not pay anybody to do either is a county that has about four months of paper left in it, and about four have said that none of the three is wrong and that is the difficulty.

The man of thirty-two with a stick said nothing at all, and about nine people in that lane worked out inside nine seconds that a man who has stood at the foot of thirteen stairs four times in about a year has never once been the one who spoke, and five had not. She went back up and shut the door at the top of those stairs and the bolt went on from the inside about nine minutes after she had gone up, and he did not knock and did not go up and did not ask her what the forty-five words had been for, and nobody in nine counties asked her either.

The ordinary part of that Monday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped, and one of them was carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had about three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street that day.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Harvestmonth is dim and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the tenth of Harvestmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the tenth of Harvestmonth is a Monday and the lamp was put there that afternoon. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred people on a far bank and about four hundred people in a market street is the same number twice and that nobody in nine counties has put the two fours together.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile are the second set of people in nine counties with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and about four have kept saying that a form wants one name against a line and nine households have between them nine names and that does not fit on one line.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about two hundred and fifty-five days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Monday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about the three answers is written down anywhere either, and no head was filled or ruled anywhere in that week and the copyist of about twenty-six has been told about none of it.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who stands at the foot of a stair while a woman says the third thing out loud is a man nobody thanks, because the whole of what he did was not being in front of her. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and fifty-five mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and three people had said three different things out loud about a roll of roads and he had said nothing at all.

He went down that lane at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and the roll on those boards had one line for this county with nothing on it, and about nine lines and about nine empty spaces and one Tuesday in Fallowmonth on it, and the four lines of chalk on that sill were under the hood where the rain does not reach and still were not writing.
