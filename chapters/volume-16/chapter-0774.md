# Chapter 0774

**Longlight the seventh, YR 323, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and nine hundred yards of cart track gone over for the second time in nine days.**

A Monday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the seventh of Longlight with about nine people in it and low cloud on the tops. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them gone soft again with the wet. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part is that a Monday in Longlight is the first day of a week with about nine hours of light in it and about nine hours of not.

Then about eleven miles north of that weigh-house, at the head of that cart track, from about the sixth hour to about the ninth hour of the same morning, a man of thirty-two with a stick stood nine feet short of nine hurdles and did not sit down once.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the fifty-eighth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is sixty-one drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that sixty-one is a drove that has been counted twice because the last two figures were close together.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and twenty-five days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and twenty-five days is four and a half years of one man walking two miles with his own arms.

The practice has run one hundred and ten mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the seventh of Longlight is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is one hundred and sixteen mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the seventh of Longlight, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that one hundred and ten mornings of a number in two voices is the only thing this county has built in two years, and five have said that about one hundred and ten mornings is about a third of a year and that a third of a year is a thing you can lose.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and forty-four days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and twenty-five days, and he had stood about nine hundred yards short of those nine hurdles for about three hours on that Monday and would stand there again for about three more. Four people in nine counties have said since that the man who goes and looks went twice in nine days, and five have said that going twice is not about four hundred yards and about nine hours, and four have said since that about nine days is the shortest interval anybody in nine counties has ever used to be sure of a thing.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and ninety-two days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and ninety-two days old is two thirds of the way to being a year, and five have said that about a year is the length of a lease and not the length of a life.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is sixty-six days past and is a Friday gone, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that sixty-six days is two months and three days, and five had not, and four have said since that two months and three days is how long it takes a thing that stopped to be mistaken for a thing that began.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five have said that filing is not holding, and four have said since that about four and a half years of walls in this county have never once had a drawing of anything above them.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has stood for three hours on a rise has learned one thing and that one thing is about the only thing anybody learns at a rise.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, which is the other of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had begun to fill again. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen went in among them at about the eighth hour and lifted one skirt and set it back and stayed looking at it for about nine minutes. Four people in nine counties have said since that a young man of about nineteen who looks at one skirt for about nine minutes is looking at a thing he will not say out loud, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that about five frames and about nine skirts is a man saying no for the third time without speaking.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom will not close over whatever the summer does, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line will be there next year and the year after, and four have said since that the only thing in this county that is certain is a width.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and no head was filled or ruled anywhere in that week, and the press in that room has stood open with no date on it since a keeper put it there four and a half years ago.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come up that cart track the second time in nine days at the fourth hour of that Monday and had meant to stand at the head of it until the light went and find out whether the ninth thing was a thing with nobody in it, and that was his goal and he kept it for about three hours. He had meant to come down with a number of people in it and to be able to say the number out loud in a market street, which is what a figure is for and the only use he has ever had for one. He had been wrong about the six days between the two trips, and the wrongness was that he had decided on the Tuesday what he was going to find on the Monday, and a man who decides what he will find does not find it, he recognises it, and everything he saw on that rise for three hours was the Tuesday coming back with a different coat on. The cost of it was in the leg, which had a hot band across the knee by the seventh hour and took him to the ground on one knee at about the eighth hour and got him up on the second attempt, and nobody saw it but him, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had gone from a high thin note to a low one he could not sit inside for nine minutes at a time.

At about the ninth hour of that Monday, and about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, one of the nine houses put a light in one window, and it was the fourth house from the left in the near row, and four people in nine counties had seen nine houses with no light in them from the top of that rise in October and five had not, and nobody had looked at them from the same place at the same hour since.

The man of thirty-two went off the rise and down the four hundred yards and knocked twice on the door of that fourth house at about the ninth hour, and there were nine seconds of nothing and then there was a voice against the inside of the door.

It came first, short, to one person, and it was fourteen words, and the door did not move.

“**I can see your stick from here, so do not come up the yard.**”

He said it back, short, in his own trade, to one person through a shut door, and it was thirty-seven words.

“**I am not coming up the yard and I am not going to touch a hurdle. I have been at the head of this track since the fourth hour and I want one thing said out loud.**”

Then the voice came again against the inside of the door, and it was twenty-nine words, and it gave him the only name anybody in that place has given to anybody from this county in nine years.

“**Nine houses and I am the fourth, and my name is Wyn Fell, and the hurdles went in by our own hands and we would like them to stay.**”

Four people in nine counties have said since that a shut road kept shut by nine households about four hundred yards apart is a road with no name against it, and five have said that a road with no name against it is the only thing the Marshal's hand in that notice cannot use, and four have said since that nine people who put a name on no roll on the first of Mudmonth did the right thing on the first of Mudmonth, and that this is a woman who has done four and a half years of a different thing with the same result, and that neither of them knows the other exists. Five have said that about a road nobody can name is a road nobody can be shut, and that this is the ninth thing in this county being not nothing and also not anything anybody can do with.

The man of thirty-two stood on that step for about nine minutes and asked nothing else and put nothing in writing and took nothing out, and the door did not open, and the light in that one window went out at about the same time as the light went off the west bank. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has been told a thing he cannot use, and who asked for one thing out loud and got one thing out loud, has had the first honest minute of this county in about nine days, and five have said that about nine minutes on a shut step is a long time to hold still when the whole of what a man wants is to be told that a road is not a road.

The man of thirty-two came back down the four hundred yards at about the tenth hour and past nine hurdles that were where nine hurdles had been on the Tuesday and on the twenty-ninth of Mudmonth and on the first of Longlight, and he did not touch one, and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one had gone to about the powder you could blow off a slate. Four people in nine counties have said since that the route at the head of that cart track is not opened, and five have said that the nine hurdles are not lifted, and about four have said since that four and a half years of this county's argument is still standing where it was with its boots on.

The ordinary part of that Monday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had all four of its beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day for about nine hours.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Longlight is dim and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the seventh of Longlight either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the seventh of Longlight is a Monday and the lamp was put there that afternoon, and the water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a lamp lit on a Monday in the third month of a wet year is a lamp a man has lit about nine times, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on a mile of stone and a mile of orchard and nine houses about four hundred yards apart is eighteen of something, and five have said that eighteen is not a number anybody in nine counties has put in one place, and about four have said since that about nine households on the mile have never once been counted with the nine about four hundred yards off, and that nobody in nine counties has ever been in a position to put the two nines in one place.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and the grass in that scar is dead to its own width and about nine feet across and standing outside it.

Then the fourth hour of that Monday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about nine houses is either, and nothing about the twenty-nine words said through a shut door four hundred yards behind nine hurdles is either.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who is told the road he came to look at is shut by the people at the end of it is a man nobody thanks, because being told is not being given anything. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and ten mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice sixty-six days past its date that had shut nothing.

He came down off that rise at about the tenth hour and slept at the trough with his back to the hedge and nine days of thinking in him, and did not go up that track a third time and was not going to be sent up it a third time either. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open, and four people in nine counties have said since that nine people keeping a road shut is not a thing anybody can hold and is not a thing anybody can take.
