# Chapter 0765

**Mudmonth the first, YR 323, a Friday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with nine people on it, and the date printed on the notice come round at last.**

A Friday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was dark at the fourth hour of the first of Mudmonth with about nine doors shut along it and rain moving sideways past the boards. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them where it had been put on the twentieth of Goatmonth. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Friday, and the ordinary part is that Mudmonth work is wet work and nobody hurries it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the thirtieth of Thawmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is fifty-two drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that fifty-two drove is a year of Thursdays all but one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and fifty-nine days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three hundred and fifty-nine days is a year all but six days.

The practice has run sixty-nine mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the first of Mudmonth is eight, and it has been eight since the eleventh of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that sixty-nine mornings ends on the date on the notice, and five have said that a practice meeting its date is either ready or finished, and about four have said since that nobody knows which until the second.

Two of the ten things are not held. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is seventy-eight days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that seventy-eight days is not a quarter of a year whatever anybody says, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all and that the ninth has been held by nobody for seventy-eight days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and twenty-six days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and twenty-six days old with no name against it is to be shut from this morning, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has shut it.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences, and this is the morning of the date on it. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is this morning and is a Friday, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a date come round with no roll made is a date that passes like any other, and five had not.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that will ask a third time, and five have said that this county will answer the third the way it answered the first two.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the first of Mudmonth is the wrong morning to begin.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep, and the thaw had put a skin of silt over the low side of it that had not been there a week before. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the thaw had softened the ground round the yard wall so that the stones of it shone. The young man of about nineteen walked the wall at about the eighth hour and touched each skirt stone with his foot to set it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that setting stones with a foot is a spring habit, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing. About four people in nine counties have said since that Mudmonth rain will flatten standing broom before the month is out, and five have said that flat broom is still uncut broom.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day, and the lane was empty enough that the drip off the stair hood was the loudest thing on it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to those boards to see the date come round and to do nothing about it, and that was his goal for the morning and he kept it past the ninth hour with his hands in his pockets. He had thought for weeks that the first of Mudmonth would bring somebody with authority to shut a gate, and he had braced for it the way a man braces for weather. He had been wrong about the morning before the boards were even in sight, and the wrongness was that he thought a notice behaves like an order, and a notice is not an order, and nothing came down that made road all morning but rain and one empty cart going the other way. The cost of the bracing was in the shoulders, which had been up round his ears since the fourth hour, and in the leg, which had locked from standing braced on wet stone, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had gone from dull to aching with the sideways rain in it.

At about the ninth hour of that Friday about nine people came to those boards one by one out of about nine trades, and each of them stood before the notice and said one thing out loud, short, in their own trade, and none of them touched the paper. Renn Ottery was first, with his hands empty of his tally-stick for once, and the rain ran off his hat while he spoke.

“**I keep a floor and not a road, and my name goes on no roll.**”

The roadkeeper of about fifty-two was second, with a bar in his hand, and he said it to the boards and not to anybody.

“**I keep four miles under contract and nine hundred feet unpaid, and my name goes on no roll.**”

The woman of about fifty-one was third, and she had the tally-book she was copying out of still under her arm, and she set it on the boards face down before she spoke so that nobody could see a page of it.

“**I keep the copy of a roll I am not allowed to have, and my name goes on no roll.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a satchel was fourth, and he said it to the notice and not to the street.

“**I carry what nine other men carry and I have asked for nothing in ninety days, and my name goes on no roll.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six was fifth and had come down those thirteen stairs for nothing, and she said it without stopping.

“**I keep ten questions with no answer under any of them, and my name goes on no roll.**”

The young man of about nineteen from the walled yard was sixth and had left a skirt lifted behind him, and about four people in that street had never heard him say a whole sentence.

“**I keep five frames under nine skirts and bees do not read notices, and my name goes on no roll.**”

Three more came after him out of about nine trades, a carter, a woman who kept the third door, and a man who swept the lane behind the boards, and each of them said it in turn and short, and the sayings were all of the same shape, and about four hundred people in that market street heard at least one of them, and five heard all nine. About four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine counties said out loud on the first of Mudmonth that they would put no name on four lines of a roll, and five have said that nobody in nine counties asked them to and nobody is going to, and about four have said since that a refusal nobody asked for is still a refusal.

The gate question was answered by nobody at all, and no bar was lifted anywhere in that county and none was put on. The cart track at Wethergill stayed shut by its nine hurdles, and the driftway four miles north stayed shut by its bars, and the made road stayed open because nobody shut it, and the notice stayed on the boards getting wet. About four people in nine counties have said since that a date with nothing behind it is a day like any other, and five have said that the whole of the pressure is that about nine people could fill four lines and about four hundred do not know the roll exists.

The man of thirty-two stood through all nine sayings with his hands in his pockets and said nothing at all, and that was the whole of his work that morning. He had come braced to stand against authority, and there was no authority to stand against, and his bracing ached in him like a held breath. He understood at about the tenth hour, walking the four hundred pace lane in rain, that he had mistaken the shape of the threat for months, and that the threat was not that somebody would come to shut roads, and that it was that nobody would come and the roads would stand unnamed until somebody four hundred miles off wrote them shut. He had been wrong about the direction of the weather, and the wrongness had cost him a morning of bracing, and the cost sat in the shoulders and the knee and the side of the head where rain had driven into the deaf ear until it throbbed. Nobody thanked him for saying nothing, and about four people in nine counties have said since that saying nothing on the first of Mudmonth was the only correct answer, and five have said that nobody knows.

The ordinary part of that Friday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the fourth hour of a Friday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the first of Mudmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the first of Mudmonth is a Friday and the lamp was not put there. The water was high under the causeway but not over it, and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Friday by anybody. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred people live at this end as well, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine households with nine sayings on a wet Friday is a county speaking, and five have said that speaking is not writing and no roll was filled.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and on the morning of the first of Mudmonth nobody in nine counties had been past them to find out whether the rota had been kept or not.

Then the fourth hour of that Friday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was eight and it was said twice. The man of about thirty-four with a satchel said it first and the woman of about fifty-one said it again, and the other seven on that floor heard the same number at the same hour on the same morning, and the day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who stands with his hands in his pockets while nine people speak is a man nobody thanks, because he did none of the speaking. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for sixty-nine mornings by nine people who said a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice dated this morning that wanted four names on a piece of paper and had got none.

He went out of that market street at about noon with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The rain had eased and the shoulder four days off showed dark against the sky, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Friday is a date come round with no roll made, nine sayings in nine trades before wet boards, no gate shut anywhere in the county, seventy-eight days since the last lime cart, a number of eight in nine heads and on no paper, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
