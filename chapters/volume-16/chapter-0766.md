**Mudmonth the seventh, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with rain over the shoulder four days off, and a floor that said no number that morning.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was half empty by the ninth hour of the seventh of Mudmonth with about two hundred people in it and rain driving through. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them getting wet through. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Thursday, and the ordinary part is that Mudmonth rain stops carts and nobody argues with it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday itself at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it for the first time in about nine months. That is fifty-three drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that two men going with a drove in Mudmonth is weather sense and not custom.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about five feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and sixty-five days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three hundred and sixty-five days is a year of carrying.

The practice has run seventy-three mornings and no number was said on the seventh of Mudmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is eight, and it has been eight since the eleventh of Goatmonth, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a number held six days past its date is a number held on purpose, and five have said that purpose is all it has ever had.

Two of the ten things are not held. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is ninety-seven days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that ninety-seven days is past a quarter of a year, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and thirty-two days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that the first of Mudmonth has come and gone with no gate shut, and five have said that a notice past its date is a paper nobody reads twice.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is six days past and is a Friday gone, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a date gone with nothing done is a week that teaches, and five had not.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office waiting, and five have said that waiting is all it can do four hundred miles off.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a wet market is no place to ask anybody anything.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and water stood round the yard wall in sheets. The young man of about nineteen did not go near them that day, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a young man staying out of a drowned yard is sense, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that sense and sayings are different trades.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing, and the rain had laid about half of it flat. About four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom in Mudmonth is ordinary, and five have said that ordinary is not the same as cut.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with water on her sleeves. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that market street to buy oilcloth and to look at the sky, and that was his goal for the morning and he named it to himself at the eighth hour with rain in his face. He meant to buy cloth enough for a coat and to ask the roadkeeper what the shoulder track was like. He had been wrong about the morning before the ninth hour, and the wrongness was that he thought oilcloth was sold by the price on market mornings, and oilcloth on a wet market morning is sold by who gets to the stall first, and he got there third. The cost of it was in the leg from hurrying across slick stone, which slipped once and jarred the knee so that it clicked on the next three steps, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had gone wet inside until voices reached him flattened and late.

The roadkeeper of about fifty-two was at the third door at about the ninth hour with mud to the knees, and he said it out loud in his own trade to four people in and five out, the way a man speaks who has walked four days of road that morning in his head.

“**The shoulder goes soft by Friday and no cart gets up past the ninth stone, and the thaw and the floods meet on it this month, and Mudmonth is the worst month for roads.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a roadkeeper name the month for what it is, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about nine people at the stalls heard the whole of it through the rain. About four people in nine counties have said since that a roadkeeper saying Mudmonth is worst is a man reading his own trade out loud, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever paid him for nine hundred feet of kerb, and about four have said since that unpaid reading is still reading.

Renn Ottery stood beside him with water running off his hat, and said one thing on his own floor's behalf, to one person, in his own trade.

“**The floor wants a dry road under it the way a cart wants one, and the floor has had seventy-three dry mornings and Mudmonth gives none.**”

About four people in that market street had heard a floor described as a thing with wants, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that a practice on a made road in Mudmonth is a practice waiting to find out what it is, and five have said that finding out starts on the shoulder and not on the floor.

The man of thirty-two heard both halves from under the awning of the third door, and understood about half of it there and the other half on the way home when the oilcloth he had bought, third choice and short measure, leaked at the shoulder seam inside nine seconds of rain. A practice that needs a dry road is a practice a man can switch off by ruining the road, and he had not thought that sentence until the water ran down his neck from the short cloth. He had thought the practice lived on the floor, and the floor is four days off at the head of a road, and the road is the first thing about the notice that this county can feel in its own hands. He had been wrong about where the practice lived, and the wrongness went with him up the lane, and the cost of the morning was a knee that clicked and an ear that gave him rain noise instead of speech until past noon, and he sat in the doorway of the weigh-house for about nine minutes with the short cloth over his head until the clicking settled.

At about the eleventh hour the rain eased and about nine people left the stalls for their trades, and no route was opened and none was shut, and no cut was made anywhere. About four people in nine counties have said since that a market with no trade in it is weather ruling, and five have said that weather rules more of this county than any notice.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out without it, because the price was wet. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and took its board in out of the rain by noon.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Mudmonth is dark and there is something off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on that Thursday either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the seventh of Mudmonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there. The water was over the low end of the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was rung once at about the eighth hour, and about four people in that market street had heard it rung, and five had not. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a bell rung once is water and not custom.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine households with water over the low road is nine households cut in two, and five have said that cut grass grows back and cut roads do not.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a Thursday with a bell rung on it south is a morning for water and not for numbers.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who buys short cloth third is a man nobody thanks, because he paid for his own mistake. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for seventy-three mornings by nine people who said a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice six days past its date that had shut nothing.

He went out of that market street at about noon with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand and short oilcloth over his shoulders, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The shoulder showed dark under rain, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a wet market with about two hundred people in it, short oilcloth bought third, a shoulder going soft by Friday, seventy-three mornings behind and Mudmonth ahead, a number of eight held over a market and on no paper, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet ninety-seven days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

And no barred numeral is a price or an over-figure or a printed total in any of the ten, and every column foots four ways with the forty-eight-pence conversion checked separately from the sum.
