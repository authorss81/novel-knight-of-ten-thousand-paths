# Chapter 0752

**Hearthmonth the first, YR 322, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, the thirteenth first of a month come and gone with nothing at those boards, and a floor four days off that does not do its hour on a morning the market is in that street.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is the loudest thing in nine counties. Four hundred people were in that street by the second hour of the first of Hearthmonth and two hundred of them stopped what they were doing. The frost came off the ruts about an hour after that. About four people in nine counties had worked out that a man of thirty-two had got three people to say a number out loud on a Monday. Five had not.

The first of Wolfmonth came and gone on a Tuesday about a month ago at about the ninth hour with nothing at those boards at all. The first of Frostmonth came and gone on a Thursday a month before that with nothing at them either. This one is today, and nothing is at them either, and that is the thirteenth first of a month in a row on which nothing has happened at them. Four people in nine counties stand at those boards at about the ninth hour on a first of a month and five do not. Nobody has sent for the man who built them on any of the thirteen, and the badge-man has not been at four of them on purpose.

The room over that weigh-house was open because it is a Saturday. It stands shut behind its own door on every morning of the week the market is not in that street, which is five mornings out of seven, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs at the seventh hour and went back up, and four people in that lane saw her come down and five did not. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and none of that has moved in about four and a half years.

The number that is said out loud on that floor at the fourth hour was not said on that Saturday. The last number said on it was three, said on the Friday before by one person and said again by a second, and about four people in nine counties had worked out inside about nine seconds that a practice which keeps to the five mornings a week this county is not using is a practice that can be shut by closing a door five days a week. Five had not. The door it is not using is on thirteen stairs over that floor, and it is not the practice's door, and nobody who could open it has ever been asked about the practice.

Hearthmonth is the month this county does its indoor work in. About three hundred and twenty sheets come past that floor in a month of thirty where about four hundred and nine come past it in Frostmonth, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the reason is that a parish which has had its Frostmonth returns in has not had the Frostmonth back out of them again yet. Five have said that a parish rolls are not a thing that goes in and comes out.

A cart came up four days of made road on the Friday before with paper in it and stood outside that Crown's road office at the low end of that street for one day, and went on up the made road at about the sixth hour with nine sacks of wool in it. Four people in that market street had worked out that this is the fourth cart in about two months to come up that made road with paper in it. Five had worked out nothing at all. Nobody at the low end of that street opened the paper and nobody has ever opened it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the twenty-ninth of Frostmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is thirty-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and about four feet high. The stone is quarried two miles off on the low side, and one man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for two hundred and sixty-nine days, and the last yard is about nine feet of a course and he has not started it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard half a mile below that wall are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen who keeps those nine hives has not been inside one of them since the ninth of Fallowmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has nine hives and five frames of brood and nine skirts over them is a man doing about as little to those bees as nine skirts and a wall will let him do. Five have said that nobody has asked him what he is waiting for.

That scar is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is thirty-six days old. The dead grass inside its own width is standing up out of a hard frost in a way that the standing grass either side of it is not, and about four people in nine counties have said since that dead grass stands up higher in a frost than live grass. Five have said that a man who is looking at nine hundred yards of it at four in the morning has not told anybody what he sees, and four have said that he has told nobody on purpose.

A leaf came up that made road on the Monday of the first of Wolfmonth with one thumb on it and no names on it. It was read out loud at those boards on the seventeenth of that Wolfmonth in seventeen words, and the words were that a place shuts its own end before Hearthmonth and is not asking anybody for anything about it. That is forty-four days ago, and about four people in nine counties had worked out inside about nine seconds that a month named in a sentence has now arrived. Five had not.

The inn at the high end had four beds full that morning and put no price out on any board, because nothing has moved a price in that street since the middle of Harvestmonth. The woman of about fifty-one who keeps that house came out into it at about the fifth hour and stopped nine feet short of those boards and would not come nearer to them. A chandler nine doors down put a cloth over a bench rather than write anything at all on it.

The man of thirty-two with the stick stood at the sea end of that street for about an hour on that Saturday and did the thing he had come here to find out about, which was to stand in the middle of four hundred people on a morning when his own floor was shut and see whether the thing he had started on a Monday could be missed. It could. He had built it in his own head four days on a made road as a thing that belonged to the fourth hour, and it did belong to the fourth hour, and the fourth hour is the one hour of a Saturday that about four hundred people are not in that street for, and about nine people are, and none of the nine had come looking for a number. A practice that only exists before the light is a practice that a county can lose in a single morning by opening a shutter, and he had spent four days of made road believing that the asking was the hard part.

The ordinary part of a Saturday in that street is that about four hundred people put a thing down and pick it up again. A man with a barrow of lime went the length of it four times and put it down at one door and took it up at two. A carter at the low end got a wheel off a cart and put it on again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped.

Then a person at the third door of that market street asked him one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, kindly, and it was not about a road and not about a wall and not about a number.

“**Is that leaf about a road?**”

He had been in nine counties of thinking about that leaf for about six weeks and had not got one step further with it, and he answered it in the street, in his own trade, and he gave the true answer because the false one would have needed about nine words of lying and he is thirty-two and tired.

“**A leaf with one thumb on it came up a made road and a place wrote it and I have not been to the place and I am not going to be sent, and I have nine things and eight of them are mine and I could not tell you which.**”

Nobody said anything back to him. About four people in that market street had worked out inside about nine seconds that a man has admitted in a market street that he does not know which of nine things he is holding, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that the first of a month is the one morning a county can say everything it has got out loud without anybody answering, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever used one.

The man of about fifty-four who keeps that floor said his figure out loud at about the ninth hour, to four people in and five out, about the difference between a copying month and a clean one, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside about nine seconds that Hearthmonth is the copying season and that a parish cannot be asked for a thing twice in one winter. Five had not.

“**About ninety sheets have come past this floor in the first nine days of Hearthmonth and about three hundred and twenty will come past it in the month, and about four hundred and nine came past it in Frostmonth, and I have said every one of those out loud and nobody has ever asked me the difference.**”

He had said the ninety, the three hundred and twenty and the four hundred and nine in that order without stopping, and then he had looked at the man with the stick for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about four people in that market town noticed the look and five did not.

The two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand either. They were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth, which is a hundred and seven days and ninety-five days, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man of thirty-four with a satchel has put a thing in that satchel, and four people in nine counties do not know what is in it.

Then this is the whole of what this county has on the first of Hearthmonth, and nothing about any of it is in a document. A number of three is said out loud twice at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week and not on the two the market is in the street, and about nine people in nine counties know the number and about nine hundred do not. A cart of lime came up from the north on the twenty-ninth of Frostmonth and about two hundred and twenty-nine of them came up that track before it. A leaf with one thumb on it named this month forty-four days ago. And a man has stood in a market street and said out loud that he could not tell you which of nine things he is holding.

The light went off the west bank first and found the upper edge of those boards about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. The lamp at the high end of them had been standing lit since the fourth hour and went out with the light, because there was nothing left off the wet slate of them to need it, and nobody in that street has ever turned it out early. Five of about four hundred people went out of that street into about five trades and about two hundred stayed at the boards.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick at about the seventh hour of that morning. The clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep, and the eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them. The man who cleans that stone does it on a Tuesday and a Saturday, and this was a Saturday, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that one man in nine counties can be found at a mile stone on the morning he says he will be there, and five had not.

The two mile stones between the seventh mile stone and the ninth that had come out of line are in line where a man of thirty-two put them about the middle of Harvestmonth with a bar and a lever and no mortar, one about two hundred yards back from where it had been and one about nine feet. The second of the two has not walked out of line since, and nobody in nine counties has asked him and nobody has been told.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side. The fruit that came down into four hundred yards of grass in the second week of Fallowmonth was still in it under the first frost, and the owners of those two miles are nine households, and not one of them has ever asked anybody anything about anybody who walks past its gate.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the first of Hearthmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and is rung when the water is over the causeway, and the water was not over it. Nobody in that market street has ever asked a person at a gate at the near end of that water anything at all.

Four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it has been across that lane gate at the head of the made road since Mudmonth. The drove has gone up past it through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of wall on every Thursday since the second of Mudmonth, and has never once gone through that gate. About four people in nine counties have said since that a gate a drove has gone round for five months is not a gate anybody is using, and five have said that nobody has ever asked the man who bars it what the gate is for.

The nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass are about the height of a man's knee and about as wide as two hands, and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and does not go past them, and it has not gone past them, and nobody has been past them since the seventeenth of Longlight. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing that stops at a ninth of nine stones is a thing that somebody decided, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked the nine households behind that flat about it.

The road that went quiet in the middle of Thawmonth YR 319 is still quiet. It is not named in this county and nobody has been sent to it and nobody has come back from it. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing that stopped in Thawmonth YR 319 and a thing that stopped this week are not the same kind of stopping, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has told the difference.

The practice has run four mornings, which are the fourth and fifth of Frostmonth and the second, third and fourth of Hearthmonth. On each of them one person said a number out loud on that floor and a second person said it again, and nobody wrote it down and nobody asked where it went. About four people in nine counties have said since that a number four mornings old is a number four people remember, and five have said that it is a number nine people could each be remembering differently.

On the four mornings it has run, the woman of about fifty-one who keeps the inn at the high end of that market street has been on that floor by about the fourth hour, because she lays a fire at that hour anyway. The man of about fifty-four who keeps the floor is on it because he is on it every morning of the year. The man of about thirty-four with a satchel has been on it four mornings out of four and has not opened the satchel. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man who does not open a satchel is not carrying anything anybody can check, and five have said that he said out loud on the Monday that he was going to put a thing in it.

Hearthmonth is the month the bell-foundries run, and the month that indoor record work is done in, and there is a bell-foundry two counties off with about nine men in it. About four hundred people along that made road have heard a bell tapped at about the ninth hour of a Hearthmonth in about four years out of nine, and nobody in nine counties has ever asked a foundry what it is tapping for. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that the tapping is a thing that happens once a year in this county, and five had not.
