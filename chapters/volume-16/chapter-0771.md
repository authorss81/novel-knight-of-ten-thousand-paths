# Chapter 0771

**Mudmonth the fiftieth, YR 323, a Friday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and a man of thirty-four with a stick going to the other side of a door to look at four lines of chalk.**

A Friday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the fiftieth of Mudmonth with about nine people in it and rain gone off the boards. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them dried to the colour of a tooth. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Friday, and the ordinary part is that a weigh-house floor is four paces long and about nine people know what goes over it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is fifty-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that fifty-nine is a figure one short of sixty again.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and eight days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and eight days is a year and a bit of carrying.

The practice has run ninety-eight mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the fiftieth of Mudmonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is one hundred and four mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the fiftieth of Mudmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. About four people in nine counties have said since that a practice four and a half years old has a figure in seven heads and no other place at all, and five have said that seven heads is about one bad week from four, and about four have said since that about four and a half years of saying a number into the dark is the whole of what this county has built.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and twenty-seven days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and eight days. About four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and eight days is a season shut, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all and that the ninth has been held by nobody for a hundred and eight days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and seventy-five days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and seventy-five days old is most of half a year of standing, and five have said that standing is not holding and never was.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is forty-nine days past and is a Friday gone, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that forty-nine days past a date is a paper that has stopped working, and five had not.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five have said that filing is not holding, and about four have said since that the drawing asked for in the second one is the ninth thing in this county and that nobody has drawn it.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that forty-nine days past a notice is a good time to have held one's tongue.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had dried hard enough in the sun to take a heel. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them, and about four people in nine counties have said since that about nine hundred names and about nine readable is the ordinary rate of this county and is not a thing anybody has asked to be raised.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen went in among them at about the eighth hour and found one skirt shifted and set it straight. About four people in nine counties have said since that a skirt shifted sideways in Mudmonth wind is wind and not water, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that setting a skirt straight is the third time he has kept that saying without saying it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. About four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom at the end of Mudmonth is a field waiting for June, and five have said that waiting is all anybody does with it and it is not going to close over.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and no head was filled or ruled anywhere in that week, and a stick of chalk is in a press drawer in that town and nobody has asked the woman of about twenty-nine where a chalk came from.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come four days up that made road to look at four lines of chalk on a stone sill and to learn what a mark is, and that was his goal for the day and he carried it to the weigh-house door in the rain. He had meant to find out that the lines were writing, and to go back down the made road with that in his hands, and to be the man who had understood the first mark. He had been wrong about the lines before the rain was off his coat, and the wrongness was that a mark is not a letter and four lines side by side about four inches long say one thing to the man who made them and nothing at all to anybody who comes after. A key is what makes a mark into a letter, and nobody had given the sill a key and nobody in nine counties was going to. The cost of finding that out was in the leg, which went from four days of made road to a deep grind at the knee when he bent to the sill and stayed ground, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had the drip off the hood in it and turned the drip into a bell that would not stop.

At about the fifth hour of that Friday the man of thirty-two stood in the yard of that weigh-house nine feet off its door with his stick in his right hand, and the four lines were where he had seen them. Nine days of rain had been at them. The top end of each line had run about a quarter of an inch down the stone and set there as a pale bead about the width of a grain of wheat, and the left ends had gone soft and were the colour of the sill, and about four people in nine counties would have said that chalk on a sill is writing, and about five would have said that the rain is the only clerk this mark will ever have, and about four have said since that a clerk nobody can pay is a clerk who takes payment in weather.

The man of about fifty-four was in the doorway behind him with a lamp-hook in one hand, and he had been in the doorway about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and he had not said anything.

The man of thirty-two said his question first, short, in that man's trade, to one person.

“**What does the floor say when nobody is at it?**”

The man of about fifty-four answered it out loud, in his own trade, to one person, and it was thirty-eight words, and he did not come out of the doorway.

“**It says it, and it has said it about four and a half years, and a figure nobody has written down is a figure that dies with the man who said it, and stone is not a man.**”

About four people in that yard had heard a keeper of a floor say what the floor says to nobody, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. About four people in nine counties have said since that the man who keeps a figure is the only person in nine counties who is ever asked what a figure is for, and about four have said since that being the man who goes and looks is a job and not a thing anybody thanks a man for, and five have said that a job is what a man has instead of a road, and about four have said since that about four miles of made road and about eleven more of track is a long way to walk for a figure that nobody has asked you for.

The man of thirty-two stood there with the pale beads at the tops of four lines and understood about half of it in the yard and the other half would take him eleven miles. A figure nobody has written down dies with the man who said it, and there are seven of those men on that floor and one of them is fifty-four, and the four lines on the sill are not writing and they are also not nothing, because they are on stone and stone is not a man. He had thought four lines were the beginning of writing it down, and four lines are the end of a thing somebody did on his own account with a thumb and no key, and the practice is going to have to be written down by somebody who has never been asked for it and who will not be asked for it. The cost of the understanding was a lightness behind the knee-ache for about nine minutes and then a settling, and a clearing in the good ear that he had not asked for and did not know what to do with, as if a door had been opened somewhere behind him. Nobody thanked the man of fifty-four for the lines and nobody thanked him for the thirty-eight words, and about four people in nine counties have said since that unthanked lines last longer than thanked ones.

The ordinary part of that Friday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and about four people in that street counted the nine minutes on the frame of him and five did not. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street, and about four people in nine counties have said since that an inn with three of four beds full on a wet Friday in Mudmonth is about as busy as this county gets.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Friday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the fiftieth of Mudmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fiftieth of Mudmonth is a Friday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Friday by anybody. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a dry causeway at the end of Mudmonth is summer coming.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine households and a fourth day of water in Mudmonth is a county waiting, and five have said that waiting is the whole of what this county is doing.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will. Four people in nine counties have said since that a line of nine stones about nine feet across is a line and not a gate, and five have said that a line and not a gate is the thing a person can walk about nine yards and stop at.

Then the fourth hour of that Friday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a figure in seven heads is one cart away from being a figure in none.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who goes and looks at four lines of chalk is a man nobody thanks, because looking is not doing. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for ninety-eight mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice forty-nine days past its date that had shut nothing and taught one thing.

He stood in that yard about nine minutes after the man of fifty-four had gone back inside with his lamp-hook, and then he said out loud, to nobody and in his own trade, that he was going up to the head of that cart track in the north on Monday, and that nobody had sent him and he was not going to be sent. About four people in that yard would have said a man should not say a thing like that to a wet yard, and five heard nothing at all because they were about four days off. He had meant to leave the sill alone and go down the made road with a piece of understanding in him, and he had understood instead that a mark with no key is worth nothing to a county and that the only way to know whether nine houses have people in them is to walk about eleven miles of made road and about nine hundred yards of cart track in daylight and stand there.

He went out of that yard at about the sixth hour with the leg grinding and the drip-bell still going in the ear the stick is on, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of that track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and he was going on Monday without a message from anybody.

**And the cost of that Friday is four lines of chalk gone soft in nine days of rain and a key nobody gave them, a floor that says four into an empty room, a man of fifty-four saying thirty-eight words in a doorway, one thing that went quiet a hundred and twenty-seven days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
