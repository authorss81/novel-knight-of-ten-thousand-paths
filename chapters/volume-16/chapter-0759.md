# Chapter 0759

**Goatmonth the twentieth, YR 322, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, a printed notice read out loud at those boards that nobody stopped, and two people who said yes to one thing each on the ninth and the tenth of Goatmonth and were not asked.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is the loudest thing in nine counties. Four hundred people were in that street by the second hour of the twentieth of Goatmonth and two hundred of them stopped what they were doing, and the frost came off the ruts about an hour after that. Four people in nine counties had worked out that a piece of paper had come up four days of made road, and five had not.

The cart that carried it came up that made road on the Friday before at about the sixth hour and stood outside that Crown's road office at the low end of that market street for two days, and went on up the made road at about the sixth hour on the Sunday after with about four hundred pounds of wool in it. About four people in that market street had worked out that the last three carts to stand at that office have stood there for two days each, and five had worked out nothing, and this is the ninth cart in about eighteen weeks.

The practice has run thirty-nine mornings, and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the twentieth of Goatmonth is eight, and it has been eight since the eleventh of Goatmonth. Two of the eight said yes on the ninth and the tenth of that month and were not asked for it by anybody. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man can be one of ten things without anybody putting him there, and five have said that a practice which grows by people putting themselves in it is not a practice anybody is holding. Four people in nine counties have said since that about two hundred and thirty carts of lime have come up that made road for about nine years because a man put his cart on the track every second Thursday, and nobody ever asked him to.

The notice was read out loud at those boards at about the ninth hour of that Saturday by the copyist of about twenty-six, in the open, in her own trade, and nobody stopped her. It is in two sentences, and it came down the ordinary post from the office four hundred miles off, and it carries the Marshal's hand at the foot of it and no name.

About two hundred of the four hundred people in that street stopped what they were doing while she read it, and about four people in that market street had heard a notice read out loud, and five had not.

“**By the first of Mudmonth every road in nine counties is to be entered in a roll with the name of the person who keeps it set against it, and any road in nine counties with no name against it is to be shut.**”

She read the second sentence out loud and nobody stopped her and nobody thanked her.

“**The roll is to be made up in the county and sent down the made road, and the county is not to keep a copy of it.**”

Nobody said anything back to them. About four hundred people in that street had heard a road is to be shut. About two hundred of them stood still and about two hundred went on what they were doing, and about four people in nine counties have said since that about two hundred people heard a thing about a road and went on arguing about a bolt of cloth. Five have said that about nine people in nine counties have said the word shut out loud this morning, and that eight of the nine are people who are holding one of the ten things.

The first of Mudmonth is in forty-one days and it is a Friday, and Mudmonth is sixty days long in this county, and the thaw and the spring floods overlap. About four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a road that is shut by the first of Mudmonth is a road that is shut in the worst month for roads, and five had not. Four people in nine counties have said since that a paper which chooses the worst month for roads is a paper which has been written by somebody who knows the month, and five have said that the notice is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office at the low end of that street. It asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it, and that is seventy-three days, and the two papers are about four feet apart on a table in one room. Four people in nine counties have said since that the same office has now asked this county for the same name twice in two forms in seventy-three days and has had no answer to either, and five have said that one of the two asks for a name and the other asks for a name on a roll, and that a county which answers the second one has answered the first one without deciding to.

A man of about thirty-two with a stick was at the high end of those boards with his stick in his right hand and his left hand hanging at his side where anybody could see it. He said nothing at all while the notice was read. The badge-man of about fifty-eight with no head stood at the sea end of that street and said nothing at all. The copyist of about twenty-six went back up thirteen stairs and did not carry anything. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that three people who are holding three of the ten things stood in one street on a Saturday morning while a notice about roads was read out, and five had not.

The roadkeeper of about fifty-two has said out loud that a wall is held by the man who repairs it, and that he is not going to hold nine hundred yards of wall for nothing, and he has said out loud that he will hold one of the ten until the first of Mudmonth and not one day past it. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has put a date on a thing he is holding, and the date is the date on a notice, is a man who has read the notice and has said so out loud, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has asked him whether he read it. Four people in nine counties have said since that he did not have to be asked, because he said it out loud on the twentieth of Hearthmonth, five days before the notice came up the made road.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before at about the sixth hour and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is forty-six drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and four feet high, and one man has been carrying the stone two miles in his own hands for three hundred and eighteen days. The man of forty-one has been asked for one thing each on the eleventh of Hearthmonth and has now said nothing for thirty-nine days, and nobody in nine counties has asked him anything since and nobody is going to. Four people in nine counties have said since that a notice which says a road with no name against it is to be shut has been read out in a market street, and the man who could put the name on it was in that market street, and said nothing at all.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen who keeps those nine hives has not been inside one of them since the ninth of Fallowmonth. Four people in nine counties have said since that the second week of Mudmonth is forty-eight days away, and that nine hives under nine skirts is a thing a young man of about nineteen will have to look at in forty-eight days. Five have said that he said out loud twice that he is not going to make more bees than the bees he has got, and nobody has asked him what he is going to do with the five he has.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing, and a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years. Four people in nine counties have said since that about nine hundred yards of standing broom is on a shoulder above a wall with a yard of it missing, and five have said that both of those things are a mile from nine new hurdles at the head of a cart track, and that all three of them are about four hundred yards apart on the ground and belong to three people who have never spoken.

The room over that weigh-house was open because it is a Saturday, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs at about the seventh hour and read the notice at the boards at about the ninth hour and went back up. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled. About four people in that lane had worked out that a woman who reads a notice out loud at the boards and comes back up thirteen stairs has put it in a room with ten questions and no findings under any of them, and five had not.

The ordinary part of a Saturday in that street is that about four hundred people put a thing down and pick it up again. A man with a barrow of lime went the length of it four times and put about two hundred pounds of it down at one door and took it up again at two others. A carter at the low end got a wheel off a cart and put it on again with a bar in about nine minutes. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and put no price out on any board.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick at about the seventh hour of that morning, and the eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them. The two mile stones between the seventh and the ninth that had come out of line are in line and have not walked. Four people in nine counties have said since that a notice about names and two mile stones that nobody has ever named are about a foot apart in subject matter and about four hundred miles apart in every other way, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked.

Then the part of that Saturday that was said out loud, and it was one thing, and it was asked by a person at the third door.

A person at that third door asked him one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, kindly, and nobody in that street stopped it, and it was not about a road and not about a wall.

“**What is a road with no name against it?**”

He had been standing at the high end of those boards for about half an hour with the paper in his head and the man of forty-one in it, and he answered it in the open, at the boards, in his own trade, and he gave four of them away and kept the rest, and about four people in that market street noticed that he gave four and five noticed nothing at all.

“**It is nine hundred yards of wall with a yard missing and about nine hundred yards of broom standing on the shoulder above it and a bar of ash in a gate and about nine houses behind a hurdle, and I know of four, and I am not going to say which.**”

Nobody said anything back to him. About four people in that market street had heard a man say he knows of four, and five had not. Four people in nine counties have said since that about four hundred people heard a notice about names and about nine heard the answer, and that about four of the nine are the people who are holding the four. Five have said that this county has spent twenty-nine days learning to say a number out loud twice, and has spent one morning learning that the number it is being asked for is four.

He had said four and had not said which, and the reason was not kindness and was not modesty. It was that the man who bars the gate with four feet of ash was standing about nine hundred yards up a made road at that moment and had said nothing for thirty-nine days, and that if a name had gone into about nine heads in a market street then four hundred people would have it by the second hour of the next market morning, and the notice does not say a name has to be said out loud in a street. It says it has to be put on a piece of paper in this county and sent down the made road. A man who is silent is a man who has not been asked. A man who has been asked in front of four hundred people has been asked in front of four hundred people.

The light went off the west bank on that Saturday about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them had been standing lit since the fourth hour and went out with the light. About two hundred people went out of that street into about five trades and about two hundred stayed at the boards.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twentieth of Goatmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was not over the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping was not rung. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a notice about shutting roads was read out in a street where about four hundred people can hear a bell and have never once heard it rung, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said since that a notice which asks for the name of the keeper of every road in nine counties cannot be answered out of a roll of nine households that owns two miles of one of them, and five have said that the owners of those two miles do not know the name of the man who keeps the kerb on the made road below them. Four people in nine counties have said since that a roll of roads is a roll of people, and that four people in nine counties hold every road anybody here could name.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee, and the tops of them are all at the same height, and the dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them. About nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years. About four people in nine counties have said since that a notice about names has arrived at a county that keeps a nine-year rota of beasts on a flat and has never once put the rota in writing, and five have said that the rota is kept in nine heads and that a head is not a name. About four people in nine counties have said since that this is the second place in this county where a thing is kept in heads, and one of them is a number on a floor.

A man of about twenty-seven, who came down that made road on the Tuesday before the week before and was answered about a sheet instead of about a cart, is in that market street this morning. He is about nine feet off the boards and he said nothing while the notice was read. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that the only man in nine counties who has been to the place that wrote the leaf is standing in the street the leaf's contents were read out in, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man of about twenty-seven has now heard a notice that says a road with no name against it is to be shut, and that the hurdle across that road has no name against it, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has asked him whether the hurdle is his.

Then the fourth hour of that Friday before, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was eight and it was said twice. The man of about thirty-four with a satchel said it first and the woman of about fifty-one said it again. About four people in nine counties have said since that eight has been said on about twenty mornings, and that the two people who say it are the same two people every morning, and that the other six are only in the number. Five have said that a number said by the same two people about two hundred times is a number two people agree about. About four have said that this county has had a practice for thirty-nine days and has not once had a morning on which the two people saying the number were not the same two people, and that this is the other hole in the rule and the bigger one.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a printed notice with a date on it that says a road with no name against it is to be shut, four such roads in one county, a man who can say which four and will not, a figure on a floor that is two short, thirty-nine days of a man's silence at the head of a made road, a county that is not to keep a copy of the only paper it has been sent in four and a half years that it is asked to answer, about nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, and the use of one ear and the use of one leg, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
