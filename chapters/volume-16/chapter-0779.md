# Chapter 0779

**Rainmonth the sixteenth, YR 323, a Friday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and a woman of about fifty-one at the high end of that street asking a man of thirty-two for a figure he has not got.**

A Friday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the sixteenth of Rainmonth with about nine people in it and the wet gone out of the boards. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them soft at the corners and nine people had gone past it without reading it that morning. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Friday, and the ordinary part is that the second week of Rainmonth is the week the roads stop being about water and start being about time.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the second of Rainmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is sixty-seven drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and four have said since that sixty-seven is two weeks past sixty and that two weeks is the length of time it takes a drove count to stop being news.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and sixty-four days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and sixty-four days is a year and five and a half months of carrying and that a year and a half of it was carried before anybody in this county knew there was a wall.

The practice has run one hundred and thirty-eight mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of Rainmonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is one hundred and forty-four mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the sixteenth of Rainmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that one hundred and thirty-eight mornings is close to five months of the same two voices and about nine other people standing there, and five have said that about five months is long enough for a figure to become a habit and not long enough for a figure to become a number.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and eighty-three days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and sixty-four days, and four hundred people in a market street had heard one sentence about it on the twenty-sixth of Longlight and nine hundred people along a made road had not heard it at all. Four people in nine counties have said since that a thing said once at a table has been in this county for about sixteen days and about nine hundred people still do not have it, and five have said that about nine people is the number that has it twice.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is two hundred and thirty-one days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have said since that a thing two hundred and thirty-one days old is close to eight months and is not going to close over, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades and that neither of them is the ninth thing.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and five days past and is a Friday gone, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and five days is fifteen weeks, and five had not, and four have said since that fifteen weeks is the length of a quarter and that a quarter is the length of time an office has for deciding about a county.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five have said that filing is not holding, and about four have said since that about fifteen weeks is three quarters of the way to a second quarter and that neither paper has been answered and nobody has asked why.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and five days is fifteen weeks since two men wrote no and nobody in nine counties has done anything about it or is going to.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had dried again and taken a heel. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board was still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times that morning with a honey pail and had not touched a frame. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man going in and out nine times with a honey pail and not touching a frame is a man taking honey and not keeping bees, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that about five frames and about nine skirts and a man taking honey and not keeping bees is about the whole of that yard in the second week of Rainmonth.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom has now been down through the whole of Mudmonth and about sixteen days of Rainmonth, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of it is about four and a half acres and about four have said since that about sixteen days is about a fifth of the way to the end of the wet and that nothing about it has been decided.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a stick of chalk is in the drawer of that press and nobody has asked the woman of about twenty-nine for it.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come up that market street at about the ninth hour with the leg still carrying the eleven miles of the week before in it, and that was his goal for the morning and it was to get through one market morning without saying anything, which was the ninth time in a row he had set himself that. He had meant to walk the four hundred pace lane and go down the made road without being asked anything by anybody, because he had worked out inside nine seconds at the fourth hour that a thing said out loud in a street is a thing that has to be followed by a figure, and he had no figure. He had been wrong about the morning before the ninth hour was out, and the wrongness was that he had believed the work was finding the ninth thing and the work was always the number, and a number is what his whole trade was made of, and a thing found with a leg and an ear and no arithmetic is a thing and not a figure. The cost of it was in the leg, which took the length of that street twice and had nine feet of ache in it by the second length, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which took the sound of an inn doing about nine beds and turned it into a hum it would not sit inside.

The woman of about fifty-one who keeps that inn at the high end of the street came out of the door with a board about four feet long under her arm, and the board has a line ruled across it with nine spaces on it, and about four people in that street had seen that board before and about five had not. She had a copy of a tally in front of her that she is not allowed to have, and she does the work of a woman of about thirty with about half the hands, and nobody has asked her about either of those things.

She said it first, short, in her own trade, to one person, and it was twenty-seven words.

“**I keep a board with a blank for every house on that road, and I cannot fill nine blanks without nine figures, and you went up there.**”

The man of thirty-two with a stick said it back, short, in his own trade, to one person, and it was thirty-two words.

“**I got one light in one window from four hundred yards and I did not count a house, and if I had counted a house nobody would have believed it off me.**”

The woman of about fifty-one turned the board over in her hands and looked at the nine spaces on it and did not look at him, and then she said the third thing, in her own trade, and it was thirty-eight words.

“**Then it is a thing you have got and not a number, and four hundred people in that street have a thing and not a number, and a number is the only thing that goes on a board.**”

Four people in that street had heard a woman who keeps an inn say that a number is the only thing that goes on a board, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to her for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. Four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time anybody in that county has asked the man of thirty-two for a figure out of him, and five have said that about four and a half years ago everybody in nine counties stopped asking him for a figure because he could not say where one came from, and about four have said since that now he has a thing nobody asked him for and cannot give it out as a number, and that about nine months is how long it takes for a man to be the wrong kind of useful.

The woman of about fifty-one put the board back under her arm and went in, and the nine spaces on it were blank, and they were blank on the Friday before and they are blank now, and four people in nine counties have said since that a board with nine blanks on it is the ninth thing in this county in a second shape, and five have said that four hundred yards is not a figure and nine seconds of light is not a figure and one person at a door is not a figure, and four have said since that the ninth thing is on a stone sill and on a market morning and in four hundred mouths and is on no board at all.

The ordinary part of that Friday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street, and the man at the second door had turned about nine boards over and put about nine of them back the way they were.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Friday in Rainmonth is dim and there is nothing off the dry slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the sixteenth of Rainmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the sixteenth of Rainmonth is a Friday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Friday by anybody. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that the second week of Rainmonth is the week the causeway starts to matter again, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile have been asked nothing about a roll for about fifteen weeks and five have said that nine households about four hundred yards behind nine hurdles have been asked nothing about it for about nine years, and about four have said since that the difference between the two nines is about nine miles of made road and about one year of nobody asking.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about two hundred and thirty-one days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Friday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, except on a leaf at the back of that day-book that carries no line and no ruling and is not the record, and four people in nine counties have said since that a figure a woman wrote on the back of a book in her own hand on the fifth of Rainmonth has now been in nine counties for eleven days and nine hundred people have not heard of it.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who is asked for a figure and has not got one is a man nobody thanks, because having not got one is the whole of what happened. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and thirty-eight mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice a hundred and five days past its date that had shut nothing and had never once been asked for again.

He went out of that market street at about the noon with the leg bad and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who came into a market town with a finding and left it with a hole in it is a man who has spent about nine days learning that a road nobody can name is a road nobody can count either, and five have said that about a hundred and sixty-four days shut and nine hurdles and about nine blanks on a board is a thing and not a number, and about four have said since that the ninth thing is not dead and is not open and is not on a board and is in no roll and that is a position and about a position is the whole of what this county has got.
