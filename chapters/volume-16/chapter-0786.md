**Fallowmonth the thirteenth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it and a floor four days off that said no number that morning, and ten days between that market and the Tuesday printed on a roll of roads nailed to a board at the low end of that street.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the thirteenth of Fallowmonth with about four hundred people in it and about nine carts along both sides. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them hard and white and the roll nailed over the top of it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Saturday, and the ordinary part is that a made road on a market day in Fallowmonth carries about nine times what it carries on a Tuesday and that nine hundred people along it have no idea that a Tuesday ten days off is printed on a board four days south of the coast.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the eleventh of Fallowmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is seventy-five drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and four have said since that seventy-five is three more than the last figure said out loud in that street and that three is a number nobody has an opinion about.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for five hundred and twenty-one days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that five hundred and twenty-one days is a year and five months and that a man who has carried stone two miles on his own arms for a year and five months is not a man anybody in nine counties can put a name against on a form.

The practice has run one hundred and seventy-eight mornings and no number was said on the thirteenth of Fallowmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water, and four people in nine counties have said since that a practice which has run one hundred and seventy-eight mornings has a figure in about nine mouths and no figure at all in any hand, and five have said that about the fourth hour of a morning nobody is awake to hear it and that a thing said to nobody who is awake is a thing said to the future.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is two hundred and forty days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is two hundred and twenty-one days. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and forty days is eight months and that eight months is the length of time after which a stopped thing stops being late, and five have said that about nine houses four hundred yards behind nine hurdles would like the hurdles to stay and have said so once through a shut door and are not going to be asked again.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is two hundred and eighty-eight days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that two hundred and eighty-eight days is most of a year by nine days, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades and that neither of them is going to be held by anybody before the winter, and about four have said since that it is not going to close over whatever a summer does.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences and a roll is nailed over the top of it. The notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and sixty-two days past, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and sixty-two days is about five months and a half, and five had not, and four have said to each other that a notice that has been past its date for about five months and a half has nothing left to threaten anybody with except a form.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files it, and five say that filing is not holding, and about four have been saying that the roll is the third paper out of that office in about a year and that about nine people in that street could tell you which of the three they have seen and that five of them could not tell you what a drawing is.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that the last of those two men said what he said about six months ago and that about six months is a long time for nobody in nine counties to have asked either of them anything, and five have said that about the same six months a roll of nine lines has been nailed to a board and filled in by nobody and stood there through about nine hundred people.

The roll had been on those boards forty-one days and its nine lines were empty and about four hundred people had walked past it and about nine people had stood at it and nothing had been put on any of it, and the Tuesday printed at the top of it was the twenty-third of Fallowmonth and was ten days off. Four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that forty-one days is six weeks and that six weeks is longer than a notice and about twice as long as a form ought to be left on a board, and five had not.

At about the seventh hour of that Saturday morning the man of thirty-two with a stick had cleaned the ninth mile stone with a bit of stick about four hundred yards up that made road, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had dried in about nine hours of sun. Then he had come down the made road to that market street at about the ninth hour to stand at those boards and to see what a county does with the last market morning before a Tuesday, and that was his goal for the morning and he kept it past the eleventh hour. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past the ninth has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

He had meant to hear somebody in that street say what was going to happen on the Tuesday, and about four hundred people were in that street and about nine of them said one thing each and none of them said what was going to happen, and about four people in nine counties have been saying since that a Tuesday ten days off a form and a street with nobody in it who knows what a Tuesday is are two different weeks, and five have said that about nine of them said one thing each about the roll and that none of them said one thing about the Tuesday, and about four have said that a county which cannot say what it will do about a Tuesday is a county which has decided to do nothing, which is a decision.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has taken honey about nine times in one morning and not touched a frame has run out of ways to say no out loud, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said that nothing in that yard is on any paper in nine counties and nobody has to act on it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that flat broom has now been down about seven weeks and that a dry week in Fallowmonth has not put one of them up, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line is about four and a half acres and about four have been saying that it will not close over in this summer either and that about nine hundred yards is going to be there next year.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour and went down the four hundred pace lane to the market at about the tenth. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody, and a letter from that office is on the low side of it with a crease in it where it has been folded and unfolded about nine times.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four of its four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day for about nine hours, and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Fallowmonth is dim and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the thirteenth of Fallowmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirteenth of Fallowmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Saturday by anybody. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that four hundred people who have never been across and about four hundred people who walked past a roll for forty-one days is about eight hundred people who have not been asked anything, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile are the second set of people in this county with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that a form wants one name against a line and nine households have between them nine names and that does not fit on one line, and that about nine households have not been asked and are not going to be asked to make anything fit.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about two hundred and eighty-eight days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a market morning is a morning for questions and about four hundred people and is not a morning for a number said to seven on a floor four days off, and about four have said that the last market morning before a Tuesday is the morning a county would use if it were going to do anything and it is not.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who cleans a mile stone at the seventh hour and then stands at a board for two hours saying nothing is a man nobody thanks, and about four have said that this is the fourth time in a year that the whole of what he did in that street was not speaking. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and seventy-eight mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and a roll of nine lines had been on a board for forty-one days and a Tuesday was ten days off and about four hundred people had walked past it.

He went out of that market street at about the noon with the leg bad and nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and the roll on those boards had a Tuesday in it and no name in it, and the man of thirty-two had not told anybody what would happen on the Tuesday and nobody in that street had asked him twice.
