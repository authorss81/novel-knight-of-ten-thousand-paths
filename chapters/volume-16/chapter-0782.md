# Chapter 0782

**Harvestmonth the eighth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it and a floor four days off that said no number that morning, and two of three answers to a roll of roads given out loud in that street inside four days.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the eighth of Harvestmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts along both sides. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them hard and white and the roll nailed over the top of it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Saturday, and a made road on a market day in Harvestmonth carries about nine times what it carries on a Tuesday.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the sixth of Harvestmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour of that afternoon, and two men went with it. That is seventy drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up that lane, and four have said since that seventy is a round figure and that a round figure is a figure somebody is waiting to see the next of.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and eighty-six days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and eighty-six days is a year and four months of carrying and that five have said a year and four months is not a life.

The practice has run one hundred and fifty-three mornings and no number was said on the eighth of Harvestmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water, and it is four on this morning as it was on the first morning of the seventy-two days, and four people in nine counties have said since that a practice which stops for about four hundred people in a street and runs for seven people on a floor is a practice with about seven people in it, and five have said that four hundred is not a number anybody in this county has any use for before the noon.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is two hundred and five days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and eighty-six days. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and five days is a season and a half, and five have said that a hundred and eighty-six days shut is about half a year and that about half a year is how long it takes a county to stop going to look, and four have said that the two figures come off two different days and a man who mixes them has been to neither place.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is two hundred and fifty-three days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that two hundred and fifty-three days is most of a year and a month and is past the point where anybody calls a thing new, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades and that neither of them is going to be held by anybody before the winter, and about four have said since that it is not going to close over whatever a summer does to it.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences and a roll is nailed over the top of it. The notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and twenty-seven days past, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and twenty-seven days is four months and about nine days, and five had not, and four have said to each other that a form nailed over a notice is a way of saying the notice was the mild part.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files it, and five say that filing is not holding, and four have been saying that the roll is the third paper out of one office in a year and that three papers for one name is not a question any more, it is a habit, and about nine people in that street could have told you which of the three they had seen.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper and none has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name either, and five have said that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had begun to fill with the grit that comes off a made road in a dry week. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen went in among them and lifted one skirt and set it back and stayed looking at it for about nine minutes. Four people in nine counties have said since that a young man who lifts one skirt and puts it back is looking at a thing he will not say out loud, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and four have kept saying that five frames and nine skirts is a trade and not a finding and nobody has to act on it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that flat broom in the first full week of Harvestmonth is about nine hundred yards of standing thatch nobody has cut, and five have said that nine feet across and nine hundred yards long is about four and a half acres, and four have been saying that it will not close over in the summer either and is not going to be cut in the summer either.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

The roll had been on those boards six days and about four hundred people had read it, and nine lines of it are ruled and nine of them are empty and one of them has this county on it, and about nine people have stood at it and said nothing out loud, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that six days is most of a week and that most of a week is not long enough for anybody in nine counties to be asked anything about it, and five had not.

At about the ninth hour of the Thursday before, which was the sixth of Harvestmonth, a woman of about forty-six who keeps a lime kiln four miles north of that weigh-house and a stone-walled yard behind it stood at those boards with lime dust to the elbow and said one thing out loud, short, in her own trade, to about nine people who had stopped, and it was thirty-one words, and about nine of them heard all of it. Her name is Brida Lenth, and about four people in that street had known the kiln and not known the name on it, and five had not known either.

“**I have burnt lime for this county nine years and carried the stone for it on my own arms, and I am not going on a line under somebody else's hand.**”

Four people in that market street had heard a woman say that she is not going on a line under somebody else's hand, and about five had not, and nobody said anything back to her for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. Four people in nine counties have been saying since that this is the first refusal anybody in this county has given to a form and the first one given about a thing that is hers, and five have said that lime and a stone-walled yard is a trade nobody has asked a question in since the first of Mudmonth, and four have said that the sentence is about a hand and not about a road and that nine counties of clerks will not know what to do with a refusal in those terms.

Four people in nine counties have said since that the woman of about forty-six has been paid for nine years of lime in about four and a half of them and that nobody has asked her about the rest, and five have said that a person who is owed money and has said one thing out loud on a market morning is the whole of what this county has built in nine months, and four have kept saying that a refusal about a hand is not the same refusal as a refusal about a road.

Then at about the ninth hour of that Saturday morning the man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane to stand at those boards, and that was his goal and he kept it past the tenth hour. He had meant to hear the two things said at those boards in four days and to say out loud whether either was the same as the other, and he did not say anything at all about either of them, and about four hundred people in that street worked out inside nine seconds that a man standing at a board with a stick in his hand for an hour has said nothing, and about five had not.

Pell Arkwright was about nine feet off with lime dust still in his cuffs and he had come down eleven miles on his own business for the third time in about a year, and he looked at the roll on the boards and then at the four nails in the frame of it, and he said one thing out loud, short, in his own trade, to about four hundred people, and it was forty-two words, and about nine people at about the stalls heard the whole of it over the noise.

“**A road with no name against it is a road nobody can shut, and a shut road at Wethergill has been shut nine years on nobody's paper, and I am not going to be the one who puts a name on yours.**”

Four people in that market street had heard a man of about twenty-seven say that a shut road is a road nobody can shut, and about five hundred had not and were looking at a bolt of cloth off a stall, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. Four people in nine counties have been saying since that this is the second refusal given to the roll in four days and that it is the answer the people at the end of that cart track have been living nine years without being asked for it, and five have said that nine households four hundred yards behind nine hurdles are not a body and are not going to be, and four have kept saying that a shut road with no name against it is the only kind of road that notice cannot use, and that about two hundred and thirty loads of lime out of Wethergill have been unpaid for about four and a half years and that a man who is owed money has a reason and his reason is the one everybody in that street has got.

The man of thirty-two said nothing back to that, and about four hundred people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that the man who went up that track twice has not said whether a shut road is safe, and five had not, and four people in nine counties have said since that two answers in four days that cannot both be held in one county is the finding, and five have said that about the finding is that it does not say which of the two is right, and nobody said so out loud.

The copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the tenth hour with a sheet in her hand and went past the man of thirty-two with the stick and put the sheet into the hands of the woman of about twenty-nine and not into his, and did not stop at either of them and said nothing about it, and about four people in that lane had worked out inside nine seconds that a copyist who knows where a sheet goes hands it to one person and not to another, and five had not, and nobody in nine counties commented on it at all, and about four people who saw it have said nothing since about it.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had about four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day for about nine hours.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Harvestmonth is dim and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the eighth of Harvestmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the eighth of Harvestmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Saturday by anybody. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a water nobody has crossed in nine years and a cart track nobody has gone up in a hundred and eighty-six days are both kept by people no roll has a line for, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile are the second set of people in nine counties with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and four have kept saying that a form wants one name against a line and nine households have between them nine names, and that does not fit on one line.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody is going to, and that line of dead grass is about two hundred and fifty-three days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a market morning is a morning for two answers given out loud to about four hundred people and is not a morning for a number said to seven.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who stands at a board for an hour and says nothing is a man nobody thanks, because the nothing was the whole of what he did, and four have said that this is the second time in a year he has been the one in the room who did not speak and that a room which needs a man not to speak is not a room he is in. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and fifty-three mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and two people had said no out loud to a roll of nine lines and he had said nothing at all.

He went out of that market street at about the noon with the leg bad and nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and two refusals had been given to a roll of roads in four days in one street and neither was his and he had made no third.

**And the cost of that Saturday is two answers given out loud in four days in one street that no county can hold both of, a woman of about forty-six who said one thing about her own hand on a Thursday and a man of about twenty-seven who said one thing about a shut road on a Saturday and two hundred and thirty loads of lime unpaid for four and a half years, a copyist who handed a sheet to one of two people and not to the other and nobody who saw it said anything, four hundred people who heard one of the two and five hundred who heard neither, a practice run for one hundred and fifty-three mornings and stopped this morning for four hundred people, one thing that went quiet two hundred and five days ago and has been looked at by one man, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
