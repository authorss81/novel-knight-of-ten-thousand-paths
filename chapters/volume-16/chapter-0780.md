**Harvestmonth the first, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with a floor four days off that said no number that morning, and a man of thirty-two with a stick at the foot of thirteen stairs on the last day of seventy-two.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the first of Harvestmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts along both sides and the sun off the west bank at about the fourth hour. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them hard and white and nobody at them, and nine people had gone past it that morning without reading it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Saturday, and the ordinary part is that the first of a month in Harvestmonth is the first market day of the year anybody in that county puts a board out in the sun.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the eleventh of Rainmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is sixty-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and four have said since that sixty-nine is a figure one short of seventy and that a drove count stops being a thing anybody in nine counties has an opinion about somewhere about sixty.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and seventy-nine days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and seventy-nine days is four and a half years and three months of carrying over that, and that three months is the time a man takes to decide that a ninth yard is not a season.

The practice has run one hundred and forty-eight mornings and no number was said on the first of Harvestmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water, and it is four on the last morning of seventy-two days as it was on the first, and four people in nine counties have said since that one hundred and forty-eight mornings and a number that came to four and stayed at four is a practice and not a growth, and five have said that four is what is left after nine days of water and two things that came back.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and ninety-eight days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and seventy-nine days. Four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and seventy-nine days is about six months shut and that about six months is the length of time it takes a county to stop going to look, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all and that one man has looked at the ninth thing twice and told about four hundred people once.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is two hundred and forty-six days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have said since that a thing two hundred and forty-six days old is most of a year, and five have said that most of a year is not a figure of anything and that the figure of it is that nobody was ever asked to hold it, and about four have said since that it is not going to close over and that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and twenty days past and is a Friday gone, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and twenty days is four months, and five had not, and four have said since that a notice past its date by four months and shut nothing is still the thing that decides this county, and that four months is the time a date takes to become the reason nobody has to decide anything.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five have said that filing is not holding, and about four have said since that four months is close to half a year and that about a half is the point at which an office stops asking a county and starts asking a county about itself.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that nine people in nine trades said one thing each out loud on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper, and that not one of those nine has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name either, and four people in nine counties have said since that a refusal which is also a refusal to govern is the shape of the thing this county is going to have to answer, and five have said that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had dried in about nine hours of sun and taken a heel. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board was still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times that morning with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has taken honey about nine times and not touched a frame and not added a sixth has said no three times now without speaking, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that about four and a half months of five frames under about nine skirts is a trade and not a finding and nobody has to act on it.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom has now been down through a whole Mudmonth and about two weeks of Rainmonth and about four days of Harvestmonth, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of it is about four and a half acres and about four have said since that it will not close over in the summer either and is not going to be cut in the summer either.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries a figure and a date and the names of nobody, and four people in nine counties have said since that twenty-six days is the length of time a thing written in one hand on the back of a book has been in nine counties without anybody asking to see it.

The roadkeeper of about fifty-two was at those boards at about the ninth hour of that market morning with a bar in his hand and nine people round him, and he said one thing out loud in his own trade, to about nine people, and it was thirty words, and it was not about lifting anything, and about nine people heard all of them.

“**A bar will get up that track if a barrow can and I have said so out loud and I am not going to be the one who tries it.**”

Four people at those boards had heard a roadkeeper say that a bar could get up a shut track, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. Four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time anybody in this county has claimed out loud that a shut road can be made to speak, and five have said that about a claim is not a hand on a hurdle and that the same man said in the same sentence that he is not going to be the one, and about four have said since that a man who can make a road speak and will not is a man who has become about the most dangerous thing in this county, which is a man who knows how.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to the foot of those thirteen stairs at about the tenth hour of that market morning to stand in the lane and to have said nothing to anybody, and that was his goal and he kept it until about the eleventh hour. He had meant to end seventy-two days with something that a person in that lane could use on a Monday, and there was nothing a person in that lane could use on a Monday, and there are two marks in nine counties and one of them is on the back of a book and the other is four short lines of chalk gone soft in the rain. He had been wrong about the ending before the seventh hour, and the wrongness was that he had spent about nine months learning that a thing somebody can name is not a thing anybody holds, and had gone up a lane to find out whether a road that has gone quiet is a road that is dead, and it is not, and there is nothing whatever he can do about it with power, and a man of thirty-two who has found that out is not a man with a finding and is a man with a job. The cost of it was in the leg, which took nine minutes standing on the flags at the foot of those stairs and then nine paces to the wall and back, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had taken about four hundred people and about four hundred carts that morning until the whole market was one sound with a high note in it, and which he has been half deaf in on that side since the fourth month and will be half deaf in on that side when this is over.

The woman of about twenty-nine came down four of those thirteen stairs at about the eleventh hour with the day-book under her arm and stopped on the step, and he was nine feet off at the wall and neither of them said anything about the leaf at the back of it or about anything else, and four people in that lane had worked out inside nine seconds that two people nine feet apart with a shut door above them are not going to say a thing about a book, and five had not. Then she went back up and shut the door at the top of those stairs and the bolt went on from the inside about nine minutes after she had gone up, and he went down the four hundred pace lane and out into that market street with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had all four of its beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day for about nine hours, and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Harvestmonth is dim and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the first of Harvestmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the first of Harvestmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Saturday by anybody. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that the first of Harvestmonth is the first day of the year this county can see the far bank from, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile and nine houses four hundred yards behind nine hurdles are two nines about nine miles apart and that a refusal is now the only thing either of them has, and five have said that about nine households with a wall and a road and no name is a position and about a position is not held.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about two hundred and forty-six days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have said since that the first of Harvestmonth is the eighteenth first of a month in a row and that nothing at those boards has happened on any of the eighteen, and five have said that about eighteen is not a record and is not a refusal and is not a holding.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man who goes and looks and comes back with nothing anybody can use is a man nobody thanks, and that this is the correct thing and is not a complaint. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and forty-eight mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice a hundred and twenty days past its date that had shut nothing.

He went out of that market street at about the noon with the leg bad and nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it and was held by nobody, and the printed notice on those boards had been past its date by four months and had shut nothing yet and was going to shut nothing this year either.
