# Chapter 0769

**Mudmonth the thirty-ninth, YR 323, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and two people choosing a thing on a lane in short words.**

A Monday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth with about nine people in it and sun on the boards. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them dried pale. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part is that a drying road carries and nobody remarks it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the thirty-fifth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and one man went with it. That is fifty-seven drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that fifty-seven is a figure nobody celebrates.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about five feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and ninety-seven days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three hundred and ninety-seven days is a year and five weeks of carrying.

The practice has run eighty-nine mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth is four. Two of the four who stopped have come back and stand on that floor again, and two have not, and the floor holds seven where it held nine. About four people in nine counties have said since that seven is more than four, and five have said that seven is still less than nine, and about four have said since that a practice growing back is a different thing from a practice holding, and that the growth rate is not anybody's to set.

Two of the ten things are not held. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and twenty-nine days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and twenty-nine days is past a third of a year, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all and that the ninth has been held by nobody for a hundred and twenty-nine days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and sixty-four days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and sixty-four days old is the county's own unheld acre, and five have said that nobody has been asked to hold it.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is thirty-eight days past and is a Friday gone, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that thirty-eight days past a date is history, and five had not.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five have said that filing is not holding.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that thirty-eight days past a notice is a good time to have held one's tongue.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen went in among them at about the eighth hour and worked through all five with his hands bare. About four people in nine counties have said since that bare hands in May is confidence, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that working five frames bare-handed is how a man keeps that saying without saying it again.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water had laid nearly all of it flat. About four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom in sun is thatch going to waste, and five have said that waste is a word buyers use.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled. Her name is Mara Vey, and it had not been printed in this county for years, and about four people in that lane had known it all along without saying it, and five had not.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that floor to say the number and to walk the lane after, and that was his goal for the morning and he kept it through the fourth hour with the seven. He had meant to say four and hear it said again, and then to clean the ninth stone, and then to say nothing else all day. He had been wrong about the day before the fifth hour of it, and the wrongness was that he thought saying the number was the work, and the work was what two of the seven did after it without being asked, and he had asked for nothing and got more than he asked for. The cost of the morning was in the leg from standing the fourth hour on a drying floor that still gave underfoot, which wrenched the knee sideways once so that it burned for an hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had gone from roar to a high thin ringing that made the second saying of four sound doubled.

At about the sixth hour of that Monday, after the number had been said twice on that floor, two of the seven did a thing they had not been asked for. The roadkeeper of about fifty-two took the bar he carries and set it across the soft place in the track below the floor where carts had been cutting, and the woman of about fifty-one took broom straw from the flat and laid it over the soft place for footing. Nobody had asked either of them for bars or straw, and both of them did it in about nine minutes in their own trades without a word, and about four people in nine counties have said since that two people mending a road on their own account is a practice mending itself, and five have said that nobody in this county has ever asked a person to account for a thing they did on their own account, and about four have said since that this is the first morning anybody could have, and nobody did.

At about the fifth hour of that Monday, which is the hour at which a lamp at the foot of thirteen stairs is lit, the man of thirty-two with a stick was standing nine feet off the foot of those stairs in the lane. Mara Vey had lit it at about the fourth hour and it was standing lit at the fifth, and she was in the doorway. About four people in that lane had worked out inside nine seconds that a man of thirty-two had stood there twice before, and five had not.

He said it first, short, in the open, to one person, and he did not call it anything.

“**The lamp is a room, and I walk past it.**”

She said it back at once, short, in her own trade, to one person, and she did not come down the step, and she did not call it anything either.

“**The room is yours to pass, and come in dry.**”

About four people in that lane had heard two people choose a thing in short words, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to either of them. About four people in nine counties have said since that a lamp become a room is two people saying what neither calls anything, and five have said that nothing about it is in any document. Nothing went in the register, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head stays empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it.

He went in out of the lane into the foot of the stairs, out of the rain that fell for about nine seconds harder that had started again, and stood under the lamp with his stick dripping, and understood about half of it there in the dry and the other half would take months. A room that is his to pass and to come into dry is a room shared without a name for sharing, and a lamp that is a room is a household nobody announces. He had thought the lane was a place to walk past, and the lane is a place to come into, and the wrongness of years sat in him lighter all the way up the four hundred paces. The cost of the evening was a knee gone stiff from the floor's soft wrench and an ear ringing thin into the night, and he sat under the stair lamp for about nine minutes with his head tipped to his good side until the ringing settled enough to hear rain.

The ordinary part of that Monday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the fourth hour of a Monday in Mudmonth is still dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on that Monday either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth is a Monday and the lamp was put there that afternoon, and the water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a lamp lit on a Monday after water is ordinary, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that a room at the foot of thirteen stairs is a narrower holding than two miles, and five have said that narrower holdings are held tighter.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years.

Then the fourth hour of that Monday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about the lane is either.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who is told to come in dry is a man nobody thanks, because dry is not a gift. He had come into this county years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for eighty-nine mornings by nine people saying numbers twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice thirty-eight days past its date that had shut nothing.

He went out of that lane at about the sixth hour with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The rain had stopped and the stair lamp burned behind him, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Monday is a floor of seven saying four twice, two mendings nobody asked for, a lane with a lamp in it, short words saying a room, thirty-eight days past the first of Mudmonth, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet a hundred and twenty-nine days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
