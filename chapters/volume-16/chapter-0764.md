# Chapter 0764

**Thawmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour at the third door with a second question about a driftway, and nine households saying out loud what they want.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of Thawmonth with about four hundred people in it. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the press standing open, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice still on them. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Saturday, and the ordinary part is that Saturday trade is loud and Saturday work is quiet.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the twenty-third of Thawmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is fifty-one drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that fifty-one is a figure with no roundness to recommend it.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and fifty-three days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three hundred and fifty-three days is a year all but twelve days.

The practice has run sixty-four mornings and no number was said on the twenty-fifth of Thawmonth, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is eight, and it has been eight since the eleventh of Goatmonth, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a Saturday without a number is a morning the floor rests, and five have said that a floor that rests twice a week is a floor that lasts.

Two of the ten things are not held. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is seventy-two days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that seventy-two days is past mending by waiting, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and twenty days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and twenty days old is the oldest unheld thing in this county, and five have said that the first of Mudmonth is in six days and is a Friday.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. About four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that six days is less than a market week, and five had not, and about four have said since that a street that can count to six can count what six means.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office talking to itself, and five have said that this county is the wall it talks at.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that six days to a date is no time to learn asking.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the second week of Mudmonth will be the first time anybody in nine counties has had to look at nine skeps under nine skirts in nine days of thaw. The young man of about nineteen has said out loud twice that he is not going to make more bees than the bees he has got, and about four people in nine counties have said since that two sayings will be tested in the second week of Mudmonth, and five have said that bees do not read notices.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing. About four people in nine counties have said since that standing broom in a market week is a thing buyers walk past, and five have said that nobody buys what nobody will cut.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the day-book under her arm and put it back again. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that market street to stand by what he had said about the driftway, and that was his goal for the morning and he set it at the third door before the ninth hour. He had said nine households wanted the bars kept on, and he meant to say it again if the cart came, and to put his own body where the bars were if it did. He had been wrong about the morning before the tenth hour, and the wrongness was that he thought the cart would come on a market morning, and the cart with paper in it comes on no day anybody can name, and standing ready for a thing that does not come is a different cost from standing against it. The cost of it was in the leg from three hours on stone without sitting, which had gone numb to the ankle and then painful again all at once, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had taken the market noise until his own voice sounded far off to himself.

At about the ninth hour of that Saturday a carter nobody in nine counties knew stopped at the third door with an empty cart and asked about the driftway bars, and about nine people heard him because an empty cart on a market morning is a question. He said his cart was hired for paper and not for lime, and that Friday had come and gone without him, and that he wanted to know whose bars they were.

“**Whose bars are those on the driftway, and who pays for the cart that waits on them?**”

About four people in that market street had heard a hired man ask who pays for waiting, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about nine householders from the mill lane who had come down for market stood along the wall hearing the whole of it.

The mill man stepped forward and said it out loud in his own trade, with eight of his neighbours behind him, and each of them said it after him one by one, short, the way people speak who have already said it at their doors.

“**Ours, and we keep them on until the frost is off.**”

“**Ours, and the vote said it.**”

“**Ours, and the beasts come first.**”

About four people in that market street had heard nine households say out loud that a thing was theirs, and five had not, and the carter looked at the nine of them and then at the man of thirty-two with a stick, and nobody said anything back to any of them for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine households saying it on a market morning is the same nine answers he got at wet doors, and five have said that saying it where a hired cart hears it is a different thing from saying it at a door.

The man of thirty-two said nothing at all through the whole of it, and that silence was the hardest work of his week. He had come ready to put his body where the bars were, and nine households had put their own voices there instead, and his readiness was left standing with nowhere to go. He understood at about the tenth hour, sitting on the coping with the stick across his knees, that he had asked well and then come to be thanked without knowing it, and that coming to be thanked is a different thing from coming to stand. He had been wrong about what the morning needed, and the wrongness had cost him three hours on stone for nothing, and the cost sat in the knee and in the side of the head where market noise had packed the deaf ear until it ached. Nobody thanked him for standing there, and about four people in nine counties have said since that not being thanked was the whole of his pay.

At about the eleventh hour the carter turned his empty cart in the street and went back the way he had come, and the bars stayed on four miles north, and no route was opened and none was shut, and no cut was made anywhere. About four people in nine counties have said since that a cart turned by nine voices is a road kept open by being kept shut, and five have said that nobody in nine counties knows what that sentence means, and about four have said since that the nine households know.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had all four of its beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a full house on a Saturday is ordinary.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Thawmonth is dim and there is something off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on that Saturday either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-fifth of Thawmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was not over the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Saturday by anybody. About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred people live at this end as well, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine households owning two miles and nine households holding a driftway is eighteen households holding one county together, and five have said that nobody has counted eighteen.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years.

Then the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over the weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a Saturday with nine voices on it is a better number than eight, and five have said that voices are not numbers and cannot be held.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who stands ready and is not needed is a man nobody thanks, because readiness is not work until it is. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for sixty-four mornings by nine people who said a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice six days off that wanted four names on a piece of paper.

He went out of that market street at about noon with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. He had stood three hours and said nothing, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Saturday is nine voices saying one thing each on a market morning, an empty cart turned in a street, six days to the first of Mudmonth, a number of eight held over a market and on no paper, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet seventy-two days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
