# Chapter 0781

**Harvestmonth the second, YR 323, a Sunday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and a Crown's cart going up the made road in the ordinary post with a roll in it.**

A Sunday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the second of Harvestmonth with about nine people in it and the sun not up over the east wall until about the seventh hour. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them gone soft with the wet and hard again with the dry. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Sunday, and the ordinary part is that a made road on a Sunday carries about a ninth of what it carries on a Saturday and carries it in one direction only.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the twenty-ninth of Rainmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is sixty-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and four have said to each other that about sixty-nine is the same figure this county had on the last day of the last seventy-two days and that a figure which does not move on a Sunday is the only kind anybody here keeps.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and eighty days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about four hundred and eighty days is a year and four months of one man walking two miles with his own arms, and that five have said that a number which has not moved since the last time it was said out loud is a number this county is not asking anything about.

The practice has run one hundred and forty-nine mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the second of Harvestmonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is one hundred and fifty-five mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the second of Harvestmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that the practice runs on the five mornings a week that room is shut and that this is the hundred and forty-ninth morning of it and that about nine hundred people along that made road do not know it exists, and five have said that a thing four hundred and eighty days old that nobody has heard of is still a thing.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and ninety-nine days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and eighty days. Four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and eighty days is about half a year shut, and five have said that about half a year is the length of time it takes a county to stop counting, and about four have said that the two figures come off two different days and that mixing them is how a number gets to be a lie.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is two hundred and forty-seven days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that a thing two hundred and forty-seven days old is most of a year and one month, and five have said that most of a year is not a figure of anything and that the figure of it is that nobody was ever asked to hold it, and about four have said since that it is not going to close over whatever a summer does to it.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is a hundred and twenty-one days past and is a Friday gone, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that a hundred and twenty-one days is four months and one day, and about five had not, and four have said to each other that a paper four months and a day past its own date has stopped being a threat and become a habit, and five have said that a habit is a thing a county sleeps beside.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five say that filing is not holding, and about four have been saying that the second paper was made to be answered by the first one, and that nobody in nine counties has answered either.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each out loud on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper, and that not one of those nine has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name either, and four people have kept saying that a refusal which is also a refusal to govern is the shape of the thing this county is going to have to answer, and five have said that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had dried in about nine hours of sun and taken a heel. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board he had set on the skirt stones at the gate on the Saturday was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone into that yard about nine times that morning with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has taken honey about nine times and not touched a frame is a man keeping a third saying no, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said that a board on a skirt stone is a board he expects to want back.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom has now been down through the last day of an Embermonth and a Wolfmonth and a Frostmonth and a Hearthmonth and a Goatmonth and a Thawmonth and a whole Mudmonth and a whole Longlight and a whole Rainmonth and two days of Harvestmonth, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of it is about four and a half acres and about four have been saying that it will not close over in the summer either and is not going to be cut in the summer either.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs on a Sunday and has never come down them on a Sunday in about four and a half years. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Then the Crown's cart came up that made road in the ordinary post at about the ninth hour of that Sunday, and it did not know what it was carrying, and the man who drove it had not been told what was in it either. Four people in that market street had seen that cart go up four times in about a year and five had not, and nobody in nine counties has ever been told what is in it when it goes up, and about four have said to each other that a cart in the ordinary post is the only kind of thing in this county that arrives without being asked for.

It stopped at the boards at the low end of that street and a man in a coat with a Crown's button on it got down and unrolled a sheet on the boards over the top of the notice and drove four nails into the frame of them with the heel of his hand. The man of thirty-two with a stick came down the four hundred pace lane at about the tenth hour to see what a Crown's cart unloads and did not get there until it had gone, and the eleventh mile stone was about four hundred yards back from that corner and he had not cleaned it and the cart was not going up that made road any further.

The roll is one printed sheet with a ruled line for every one of nine counties and the fourth of the nine lines is this one, and there is a space on each line for one name and no other thing, and at the top of it in print it says that the name required is the name of the person who keeps the roads of that county, and that a road with no name against it on the twenty-third of Fallowmonth is to be shut. That is a Tuesday, and it is fifty-one days off, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that fifty-one days is about seven weeks, and five had not.

At the foot of that sheet there is a covering paper folded over the back of it, and on the covering paper there is the Marshal's hand, which is a name and is written out in full, and it is Edric Hunn. Four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that a name written out in full at the foot of a form is a name somebody is going to be answerable to, and five had not. The man of thirty-two looked at it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and did not ask about it, and nobody in that street asked him anything, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who reads a name at the foot of a form and asks nothing is a man who has learned what asking costs in about four and a half years.

About nine people in that street had stood at those boards that afternoon and looked at it and gone away, and one of them was a woman of about fifty-one who keeps the inn at the high end and who said one thing out loud to nobody in particular about a copy of a roll she is not allowed to have, and about four people heard it and five did not. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that a roll of roads came into this county in the ordinary post and that about nine people looked at it and went away, and five have said that about nine people is not a county and that about nine people on a Sunday is not even a street.

About nine people who said one thing each out loud on the first of Mudmonth did not come and stand at those boards that afternoon, and nobody has told them the roll is there, and nobody in nine counties is going to tell them. Four people in nine counties have been saying that a refusal nobody hears about and a form nobody is told about are the same county, and five have said that they are not the same, and about four have said since that a thing you refused on the first of a month and a thing that arrived on the second of another are two different sizes of the same silence.

The ordinary part of that Sunday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had about two of its four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day for about nine hours, and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Sunday in Harvestmonth is dim and there is nothing off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the second of Harvestmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the second of Harvestmonth is a Sunday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Sunday by anybody. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a road in nine counties and a water with four hundred people on the far side of it are both held by nobody and both are on somebody's list, and five had not.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on a mile of stone are the only people in this county with a wall to hold and no form to fill in, and five have said that about nine households have not been asked what they would do with a roll and about nine households are not going to be asked, and about four have kept saying that a form that reaches a house nobody asked about it is a form with a name on it that somebody chose.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about two hundred and forty-seven days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Sunday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people. The day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about a roll of roads is written down anywhere either.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who stands at a board with a nail in it and says nothing is a man nobody thanks, because nothing is the whole of what he did. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for one hundred and forty-nine mornings by seven people saying a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice a hundred and twenty-one days past its date that had shut nothing, and there was a form on the boards with a name written out in full at the foot of it.

He came down the four hundred pace lane at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and nine feet of ache in it, and he had meant to ask the man who drove that cart one question about what was in it, and he had not got there in time to ask it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who misses a question by a quarter of an hour will miss it by a week next time. Then he did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and the roll on those boards had one line for this county with nothing on it and a name written at the foot of the covering paper that is not anybody's name in this county.
