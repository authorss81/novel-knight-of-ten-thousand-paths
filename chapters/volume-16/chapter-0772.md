**Mudmonth the fifty-fifth, YR 323, a Wednesday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, and eleven miles of made road walked north by a man with a stick in about nine hours.**

A Wednesday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the fifty-fifth of Mudmonth with about nine people in it and mist off the boards. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them gone soft with wet. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and five had not.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Wednesday, and the ordinary part is that eleven miles of made road in Mudmonth takes a sound man about six hours and takes a man with a stick about nine.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is fifty-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth for the second week running, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that a drove that does not go up is a drove that has found somewhere else.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for four hundred and thirteen days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that four hundred and thirteen days is a year and a bit and about six weeks over.

The practice has run one hundred and two mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the fifty-fifth of Mudmonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is one hundred and eight mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the fifty-fifth of Mudmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that a number said four times in a week in the same two voices is about the thinnest thing this county holds, and five have said that thinnest is not empty, and about four have said since that about four and a half years is long enough for a habit and not long enough for a record.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is a hundred and thirty-two days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is a hundred and thirteen days. Four people in nine counties have said since that a hundred and thirteen days is about a season and a half shut, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and eighty days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and eighty days old is about two thirds of the way through a year of doing nothing to it, and five have said that nothing at all is what has been done to it.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is fifty-four days past and is a Friday gone, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that fifty-four days is nearly two months of a paper, and five had not, and about four have said since that about two months is enough for an office to write a second one.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that two papers asking for one name is an office that files, and five have said that filing is not holding, and four have said since that the same office asked for a drawing about what is above a wall in Frostmonth and has not had one since.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the whole of a county's courage so far is about nine sayings in nine trades and two refusals in nobody's hand.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before at about the seventh hour, which is one of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep and had begun to fill with mud. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen went in among them at about the eighth hour and found nothing shifted and came out again and stood looking at the yard wall for about nine minutes. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who looks at a wall for nine minutes in Mudmonth is a man thinking about something other than bees, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and about four have said since that about nine minutes of standing is not a figure of bees and is a figure of standing.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said since that flat broom drying in Longlight is a field of thatch with nobody to thatch it, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line is a great deal of standing for a thing nobody has asked for.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and no head was filled or ruled anywhere in that week, and the latch on that room had been oiled once that month by somebody who left no mark of having done it.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come to that weigh-house four days up the made road to sleep one night under a roof and to start north on the Wednesday morning before light, and that was his goal for the day and he kept it to about the ninth hour. He had meant to walk eleven miles of made road and arrive at the bottom of that cart track with his leg the same as it was and his ear the same as it was. He had been wrong about the day before the eighth hour was out, and the wrongness was that he had thought walking eleven miles north was his own business, and eleven miles of that made road is four miles of another man's contract and the man of about fifty-two keeps those four miles under a written piece of paper and nobody had told the man of thirty-two that a made road has an owner. The cost of it was in the leg, which went from an ache to a hot band across the knee by about the sixth hour and would not go down again that day, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which took eleven miles of wind off the tops of the ditches into about nine seconds of ringing at once.

He set out from that weigh-house at about the fifth hour with the light not properly up and got to about the sixth mile stone by the light, and the four days of made road were wet to the axle in the ruts and hard on the crowns, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a made road is about one crown and two ruts and a man with a stick uses all three. Then the eleven miles went away one at a time and by about the eighth hour he was above the last of the walls and the lane behind it was a cart track with a stone in the middle of it, and the stone was about the height of a knee and was the ninth mile stone, and the clean ring at the foot of it had about nine inches of mud in it and he cleaned it with the back of his thumb on the way past.

At about the eighth hour on that Wednesday the man of about fifty-two was at a soft place in that made road about a mile short of the weigh-house with a bar in his hands and a barrow beside him, and he was turning the stones of a place where carts had been cutting. The man of thirty-two came round the bend with his stick and the man of about fifty-two looked at the leg and then at the road ahead of him.

He said it first, short, in his own trade, to one person, and it was thirty words.

“**You are not on my four miles and the road is wet, and a stick going down a made road is a hole in my day and not a guest.**”

Four people in that lane had heard a man put a walker in his place on his own ground, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to him. Four people in nine counties have said since that the man of thirty-two was told where he was standing about four days after he began asking nine people in nine trades to do things, and five have said that asking is a licence and a licence does not carry eleven miles, and about four have said since that about a year and a half ago he asked that same man for nothing and was refused once and did not ask again, and nobody has asked him anything since.

The man of thirty-two stood in the wet at the edge of another man's four miles and asked the second question, and it was the first time anybody in that county had asked anybody that. He said it in that man's trade, to one person, standing out of the rut.

“**You barred that soft place below the weigh-house and laid straw over it, and nobody asked you, so tell me why you did it.**”

The man of about fifty-two set his bar down on the stones and looked at the man of thirty-two's knee and not at his face, and answered it out loud, in his own trade, and it was nineteen words.

“**Carts were cutting it and I keep four miles, and nobody asked me to and nobody was going to.**”

Four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time anybody in that county has asked a person to account for a thing they did on their own account, and about four have said since that it cost the man of about fifty-two more than the bar cost him, because a man who has given an account has a thing to keep and a man who has not has only the doing. Five have said that a man who mends a road and cannot say why is a man who will mend it once and think twice after, and about four have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each on the first of Mudmonth and nobody has asked those nine either.

The man of thirty-two went on north after that at about the ninth hour and got to the bottom of that cart track, where the made road ends and the track begins and there is a stone trough and a gate that is not shut. The track is about a mile of made road past the trough and then about nine hundred yards of cart track with the stone in the middle of it, and it goes up past the ninth mile stone and on over a rise where from about the fourth hour in the afternoon in clear weather a man can see nine hurdles across the head of it. Four people in nine counties have said since that about nine hundred yards of cart track is not a road to anybody whose work is not on it, and five have said that it is about nine hundred yards to the man of thirty-two, and about four have said since that about one mile before it and about a mile after it there is no light of any kind in Mudmonth.

The man of thirty-two stood at the gate at about the ninth hour that Wednesday with the light going and could not make out the hurdles at all, and he did not go up the track in the dark with a leg that had gone hot at the knee and an ear that had been ringing since the sixth hour, and he slept on the dry side of the trough with his stick across him and his back to the hedge. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who turns back at a gate in Mudmonth light is a man who will be at the head of it at the fourth hour on another day, and five have said that about two days is not a long time to think about nothing.

The ordinary part of that Wednesday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the fourth hour of a Wednesday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the fifty-fifth of Mudmonth either.

About four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that high water under a causeway for about nine days is the shoulder's own arithmetic.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Then the fourth hour of that Wednesday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by seven people.
