# Chapter 0792

**Embermonth the twenty-third, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it and a floor four days off that said no number that morning, and a woman of about fifty-one answering one question in her own trade.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the twenty-third of Embermonth with about four hundred people in it and carts along both sides. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had come off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice on them gone soft with the wet and two copies of a roll nailed one over the other on the frame of them. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a market Thursday, and the ordinary part is that a drove goes up that lane at about the sixth hour while about four hundred people are still buying string in a street four days off from it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on that same Thursday, the twenty-third of Embermonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and two men went with it. That is eighty-one drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up, and four people have said since that eighty-one is two more than the figure this county said out loud about nine days ago and that two is the kind of number nobody argues with.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for five hundred and sixty-one days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that five hundred and sixty-one days is a year and seven months and a bit, and that a bit is the part of a figure nobody puts in a form.

The practice has run two hundred and seven mornings and no number was said on the twenty-third of Embermonth, because the room over that weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. The number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water, and it is four on this morning as it was on the first morning of about a year ago, and four people in nine counties have said since that a practice which stops for about four hundred people in a street and runs for nine people on a floor is a practice with about nine people in it, and five have said that four hundred is not a number anybody in this county has any use for before the noon.

There are ten things and about nine people say the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and eight of the ten were held at the fourth hour of the morning before this one and two were not, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the ninth of those ten has a person against it and that the person has said nothing out loud at the head of that track since the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, and that the tenth has nobody against it and never has and is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long on the back of a board in a cart shed.

Two of the ten things are not held, and four more went unheld for nine days in Mudmonth and two of the four came back and two have not. One of them is the Wethergill end of that cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is two hundred and eighty days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is two hundred and sixty-one days. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and eighty days is most of nine months and that most of nine months is a season and a bit, and five have said that about nine houses four hundred yards behind nine hurdles would like them to stay and said so once through a shut door, and nobody has been up there a third time and nobody lifted one.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is three hundred and twenty-eight days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that three hundred and twenty-eight days is over a year by a month and a bit, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of standing broom and about nine feet of it across are the same kind of number in two trades and that nobody has been asked about either.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences and two copies of a roll are nailed one over the other on the frame of them. The notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is two hundred and two days past, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that two hundred and two days is over six months and about twenty-two days, and five had not, and four have said to each other that a form that has been past its own date for six months and twenty-two days is a form nobody has to answer in order to be safe.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. Four people in nine counties have said since that four papers out of one office in about a year is an office that files, and five say that filing is not holding, and about four have been saying that nobody in nine counties has answered any of the four and that four unanswered papers is a habit an office keeps.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that about nine people in nine trades said one thing each on the first of Mudmonth and none of them touched the paper, and that not one of those nine has since offered any other person in nine counties a place to put their name either, and five have said that a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold and that about seven months is now the whole of what this county has.

The roll has been on those boards eighty-one days and its nine lines are empty on both copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and about four hundred people had walked past it that morning by the ninth hour and about nine had stopped. Four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that eighty-one days is nearly three months on a board, and five had not, and about four have said since that a form which sits on a board through a whole season of weather is a form that has become part of the street, and that about four hundred people in that street have stopped seeing it the way they have stopped seeing the cloth nine doors down.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it was a foot wide and half an inch deep and had begun to fill again. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and four have said that nine hundred names cut by people who did not know each other is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was taken off and put back on that morning, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in among the frames and lifted one skirt and set it back and stayed looking at it for nine minutes. Four people in nine counties have said since that a young man who lifts one skirt and puts it back is looking at a thing he will not say out loud, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and four have said that five frames under nine skirts is a trade and not a finding.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide was left standing, and the water laid nearly all of it flat. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that the mat of it is thicker now than it was a fortnight ago and that a thicker mat is a mat that has stopped moving, and five have said that about nine hundred yards of a nine-foot line is about four and a half acres and about four have been saying that it will not close over in this summer either.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is not the record.

At about the ninth hour of that Thursday a carter of about thirty-eight came into the inn at the high end of that street with his coat open and asked the woman of about fifty-one who keeps it one question, kindly, in his own trade, about what a person does with a second copy of a roll of roads that is nailed up at the low end of the street. About four people in that room heard all of it and about five did not.

“**I keep a tally of what goes out of that door and what comes in, and I have had a copy of that roll since the second of Harvestmonth, and I am not going to be asked twice what I do with it.**”

Four people in that room had heard a woman say she was not going to be asked twice, and about five had not, and nobody said anything back to her for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. Four people in nine counties have said since that a woman who keeps an inn has about four hundred chances a week to be asked a question she has already answered, and five have said that about four hundred chances is what makes a person refuse in public rather than in a lane, and about four have said that this is the first thing anybody in this county has put into a room that a roll exists in two places.

Then the carter asked her what to call her, because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and she told him, and it is Bessie Dellow, and about four people in that room had known the inn and not known the name on it, and five had not known either.

“**Bessie Dellow, and I keep this house, and the copy is in a drawer under the stair and nobody has ever come up it.**”

Four people in that room had heard a woman give her name in a market morning and about five had not, and nobody said anything back to her, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a person who has been in this county for about nine years and has never had to put a name on a form has now put one in the air in a market morning, and five have said that is not a thing anybody in nine counties has to do anything about, and that about four hundred people in that street went on buying string.

Then at about the ninth hour the man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane to stand at those boards with the market going round him, and that was his goal for the morning and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to hear what four hundred people said to one another about a roll that has been on a board for eighty-one days with nothing on it, and about four hundred people said nothing to one another about it, and nine had stopped, and he had been wrong about the morning before the ninth hour was half out, and the wrongness was that he had believed four hundred people in a street is a room if the street is full, and it is not, and this county has now had that said to it twice.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a board and stand at it for about two hours, and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had taken about four hundred people and carts until it sang and had not let him hear the bell on the gate post of the inn when it was rung at the noon.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the same carter had asked a question at the third door before he did it. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Embermonth is dim and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the twenty-third of Embermonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-third of Embermonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung on that Thursday by anybody, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that a woman with a copy of a roll and four hundred people who cannot get to a causeway are two kinds of person with a paper they cannot use.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile have not been asked about a roll and have not been asked about anything else, and five have said that nine households is nine names and a form has one line, and four have kept saying that a county which cannot fit nine names on one line is a county about to ask somebody to make them fit.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is about three hundred and twenty-eight days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over that weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a market morning is a morning for one thing said out loud in a room and is not a morning for a number said to nine.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who stands at a board for two hours on a market morning with about four hundred people going round him is a man nobody thanks, because what he did was stand there. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for two hundred and seven mornings by nine people saying a number twice in the dark, and a woman of about fifty-one had put a name to herself in a room that morning and he was not in the room.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and there was a thumb of lime gone to powder on the shoe of the top one, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and the roll was still on those boards with eighty-one days on it and nine empty lines on each of two copies and a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second one that nobody in nine counties has claimed.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a woman of about fifty-one who said one thing out loud in a room in her own trade and gave her name, about four hundred people in a market street who went on buying string, about nine who stopped at a board, eighty-one days of a roll with nothing on it, a practice stopped that morning for four hundred people and run for nobody, one thing that went quiet two hundred and eighty days ago and has been looked at by one man, one thing that has never been held by anybody, about nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
