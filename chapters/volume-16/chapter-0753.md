**Hearthmonth the ninth, YR 322, a Sunday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with four people on it, and a man of about fifty-four being given his name out loud in a market street because somebody asked him for it.**

Nobody thanked anybody and she asked it out loud, in the open, in that market street, at about the ninth hour of the fourth of Hearthmonth, to one person, and nobody stopped her, and nobody thanked her, and it was 27 words.

“**Where does the number go after the morning? I write things down and I have never had anything to write down that two people said out loud.**”

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two with a stick answered it out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 53 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**It goes in the two people and it goes nowhere else, and that is deliberate, and a room with ten questions and no finding under any of them is a room that has learned what happens to a thing that gets put in it, and I am not asking you to unlearn that.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in nine counties have said since that a man of thirty-two has told a copyist that a number he wants kept is going to be kept by two people and no paper, and five have said that that is the second time in four and a half years that he has refused to put a thing in a room, and four people in nine counties have said since that a number said out loud twice in the same hour is a count for that hour and for nobody afterwards, and four have said that the first morning of the practice was the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth and the tenth is the ninth of Hearthmonth and that is fourteen days, and five have said that nobody in nine counties has a piece of paper with the first morning on it.

“**The last lime was on the twenty-ninth of Frostmonth and the next is due on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth and it has been fourteen days between every cart for about nine years and about two hundred and thirty of them, and I have said that out loud about nine hundred times and nobody has ever asked me how many.**”

Then the part of that Sunday that was said out loud, and it was one thing and it was about a man who keeps a floor.

Nobody thanked him and a person at the third door of that market street asked the man of about fifty-four one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, kindly, and nobody stopped it, and it was not about a road and not about a cart and not about a number.

“**What is your name?**”

Nobody said anything and he answered it out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 29 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**Renn Ottery, and I keep this floor, and I am fifty-four, and I have been saying my figures out loud on it for about four and a half years.**”
