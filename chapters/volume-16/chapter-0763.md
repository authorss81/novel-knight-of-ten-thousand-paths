# Chapter 0763

**Thawmonth the twentieth, YR 323, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with nine people on it, and a hand lamp going on a coping four days south that afternoon.**

A Monday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the twentieth of Thawmonth with about nine people in it and carts nowhere. The bolt on the room over the weigh-house had gone on from the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it, and the boards at the low end stood with the notice still on them. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part is that Monday work is done out of sight and nobody counts it.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the sixteenth of Thawmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it. That is fifty drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one go up, and about four have said since that a fiftieth drove with no witness is a thing that happens whether anybody watches or not.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for three hundred and forty-eight days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three hundred and forty-eight mornings of carrying is a wall built out of mornings.

The practice has run sixty-one mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the twentieth of Thawmonth is eight, and it has been eight since the eleventh of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that sixty-one mornings is eleven mornings past fifty, and five have said that a number held ten mornings past fifty without moving is either held well or held by nobody noticing, and about four have said since that nine people noticing is not nobody.

Two of the ten things are not held. One of them is the Wethergill end of a cart track with nine hurdles across it and nine houses about four hundred yards back, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, which is sixty-seven days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. About four people in nine counties have said since that sixty-seven days is past two months and a week and is not a season yet, and five have said that the tenth thing has never been held by anybody at all and that the ninth has been held by nobody for sixty-seven days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is a hundred and fifteen days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. About four people in nine counties have said since that a thing a hundred and fifteen days old will not be named by the first of Mudmonth, and five have said that the first of Mudmonth is in eleven days and is a Friday.

A printed notice is on those boards in two sentences. It asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. About four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that eleven days leaves one market week and four days, and five had not.

The printed request of the fifteenth of Frostmonth is in that same office, and it asks for the name of the person who holds that wall and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it. About four people in nine counties have said since that two papers ninety days apart asking for one name is an office that has learned nothing, and five have said that this county has taught it nothing either.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that eleven days to a date on a notice is the wrong time to start asking.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, which is the other of the two mornings of the week he cleans it, and the clean ring at the foot of it had taken a week of rain and been cut twice. The clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep, and the eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the young man of about nineteen lifted the edge of one skirt at about the eighth hour and put it back without reaching in. About four people in nine counties have said since that lifting an edge and putting it back is a whole winter's work in one motion, and five have said that he has said twice he will make no more bees than the bees he has got.

The broom on that east rise has been off the hill since the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing. About four people in nine counties have said since that Thawmonth rain gets under standing broom and loosens it, and five have said that loosened broom is still broom nobody will cut.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day, and the damp had got into the paper on the boards at the low end of that lane and taken the corner off the notice. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down to that lane to walk past the lamp and to say nothing to anybody on it, and that was his goal for the day and he owned it to himself at the fifth hour with his hands in his pockets. He had meant to walk past and be gone before the light, and to clean nothing on the way back and to have said nothing at all by the time he got to the ninth stone. He had been wrong about the lane before the fifth hour came, and the wrongness was that he thought a lane with a lamp on it was a lane he could pass through unseen, and a man who has walked past a lamp about fifty times has been seen about fifty times. The cost of it was in the leg from kneeling at the stone, which had stiffened so that getting up took two tries with the stick braced, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which had caught the wind off the low side until the coping bell chimed somewhere and he heard it only as a thin tremor.

In the afternoon of that Monday the hand lamp went on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south, put there by the keeper in the ordinary way for the winter. The twentieth of Thawmonth is a Monday and the lamp went on there that afternoon for the first time in about nine days of rain, and nobody in nine counties has asked why a lamp goes on a coping on Mondays, and about four people in that market street had worked out that a lamp nobody asks about is a lamp that works.

At about the fifth hour of that same Monday, which is the hour at which a lamp at the foot of thirteen stairs is lit, the man of thirty-two with a stick was standing nine feet off the foot of those stairs in the lane again. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had lit it at about the fourth hour and it was standing lit at the fifth, and she was in the doorway with a bundle of tallies in her hand that she had been sorting. About four people in that lane had worked out inside nine seconds that a man of thirty-two had stood there once before about a month back, and five had not.

He had had about four sentences ready for years and had said none of them, and what he said instead was about the room above and not about himself, and he said it to the stairs and not to her, about nine feet off.

“**Your room is shut five mornings and open two, and the floor does the work of it on the five, and I have never asked what the two are for.**”

She said it back at once, in her own trade, to one person, and she did not come down the step, and the lamp burned between them at the foot of the stairs.

“**The two are for the room and the five are for the floor, and the room is mine and the floor is yours, and I know you have never asked.**”

About four people in that lane had heard two people say a thing about a room and a floor, and five had not, and nobody said anything back to either of them for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. About four people in nine counties have said since that a room and a floor named in one breath is the nearest thing to an answer this county has, and five have said that neither of them said what the answer was for. Nothing about it went in any document, and the register gained no entry, and the day-book stands at eleven entries.

He walked back up the lane with the leg dragging and the stick striking sparks off nothing, and he understood about half of what had been said there and the other half about a mile up the made road the next morning when the ache had settled. A room that is shut five mornings is a room that trusts a floor, and a floor that carries sixty-one mornings is a floor that wants a room to come back to. He had thought the lamp was a thing lit for stairs, and he had been wrong, and the wrongness sat with him all the way past the ninth mile stone. The cost of standing those nine feet through it was a knee gone hot and an ear gone dull, and he slept that night with his back to the wall of a barn and woke with the damp in the joint so that the first step took three tries.

The ordinary part of that Monday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had three of its four beds full and put no board out on the made street.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the fourth hour of a Monday in Thawmonth is still dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on that Monday either.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. About four people in nine counties have said since that a room and a floor with nine households behind each of them is two nines that have never met, and five have said that they had said it once before on the twelfth of Goatmonth and again on the twentieth of Thawmonth, and about nine seconds of each was on a lane.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine stones at one height is a count nobody wrote, and five have said that counts nobody wrote are the only ones that last.

Then the fourth hour of that Monday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was eight and it was said twice. The man of about thirty-four with a satchel said it first and the woman of about fifty-one said it again, and the other seven on that floor heard the same number at the same hour on the same morning, and the day-book in the room over the weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled.

He was not thanked for any of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who cleans a stone and stands nine feet from a lamp is a man nobody thanks, because the stone is clean and the lamp was already lit. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and the practice he had started on a floor in Frostmonth had been run for sixty-one mornings by nine people who said a number twice in the dark, and there was a printed notice eleven days off that wanted four names on a piece of paper.

He went out of that lane at about the sixth hour with the leg bad and the stick in his right hand and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. He had a stone cleaned and a sentence said and a sentence answered, and he had told a market street once that he had not been to the place at the head of the cart track and was not going to be sent, and nobody had sent anybody, and the hurdles were still across the top of that cart track, and there was a thumb of lime on the top one of them.

**And the cost of that Monday is a stone cleaned at the seventh hour, a lamp put on a coping four days south in the afternoon, a room and a floor named in one breath each on a lane, eleven days to the first of Mudmonth, a number of eight in nine heads and on no paper, a practice with two holes in it that nobody has written down, one thing that went quiet sixty-seven days ago and has not been looked at, one thing that has never been held by anybody, nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
