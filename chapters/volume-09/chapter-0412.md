# Chapter 0412

**The eighteenth day of Longlight, YR 318, a Thursday and a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the eleventh hour, in a meeting room over a store in a township of about nine hundred people two days off that made road, where a market runs on two days in seven and this is one of them, and about two hundred people came in off six villages for it, and the room is eleven paces by nineteen and the chair is on a platform at the top end and the hat is on a shelf behind the door and both of those are about a hundred and forty years old.**

## One

The room has a chair on a platform and a hat on a shelf and a book in which what was agreed is written, and the room has had both for about a hundred and forty years, and the room uses them for a pound, a common oven, a set of nets, the repair of a water trough, and a list of other things that is a list of about nine over four generations.

**The way the room works is that anybody may put a name in the hat, and anybody may come and look at the names in the hat before the names are read, and about nine people out of about nine hundred put a name in every year, and each of the nine has the thing for a month, and when the nine are done they are done again the next year with different names, and the chair has gone round about twenty times in a hundred and forty years and it has never once had anybody's name against it for longer than a month.**

That is the whole of the arrangement and it is a good arrangement and about nine people in nine counties have at some point said so in those words.

**A man of about thirty-two with a limp asked the room to take a fifth thing, and he did it by putting it in the ordinary way, at the third hour, after the trough, and he said that it was a thing nine counties do and that he had not come to explain where it came from because he does not know, and that the room was a room and there is not another kind of thing a thing like that wants.**

## Two

The room agreed to it in about four minutes, and the agreement was not a favour.

**A man of about thirty-four who keeps a net said that if a thing is done in a room, the room is the place for it, and that if a thing is said in a room, the room is the place for it, and that this is not a thing nine counties does and is a thing people do, and the two are not the same and the second is the easier one.**

**A woman of about sixty-three said that the room had said things out loud before and that the last one was a boundary in Fallowmonth of the year the old man died, and that a thing said out loud in a room is not kept by the man who says it, because about forty other people heard it, and that this is the only kind of keeping this room has ever had and it has never once had to look for it.**

Then the room did what rooms do, and it turned the thing over to the chair, and the chair turned it over to the hat.

**And this is where the arrangement that has worked for a hundred and forty years found the one thing it had never been asked about, and it found it inside about nine minutes, and nobody in that room had ever needed to know it before.**

## Three

The fifth of the eight is four words long and it is the one nobody can do on purpose.

Say it in a room.

**And a room is a room and not a hearing and not a bench and not a court, and the fifth is not a thing that is said into the air. It is said to somebody. Somebody in that room is meant to hear it and to go out of that room different, and the person who says it is not the person it is for, and that is the whole of what the fifth one is and it is the only one of the eight that has a person on both sides of it.**

The hat had nine names in it on the third of a month and it had nine names in it on the eighteenth, and two of the nine were not of that township, and one of the two had not stood in that room since he was nineteen.

**A man of about thirty-eight who lived seven miles off in the next fold had put his name in twice, and his wife had put it in once in the second week of a month in Mudmonth because he had been out on a job and had not been in the room, and neither of the two of them had said anything about it, and the room had not asked, because the room has never had to ask who is in the room before the hat is read, because everything the room has ever put in that chair has been a job and a job does not mind being done by anybody.**

**A man of about thirty-eight who takes a drove to a market a day and a half off has gone on the first of a month since he was nineteen, and the room sits on the first of a month, and nobody in that township has seen that man in that room on the first of a month in about nineteen years, and about nine people in that room could not have told you that, and one of them was asked in the fourth hour and worked it out in about a second and a half and said nothing for the rest of the day.**

**And the room had a good answer, and the good answer is that a man can say a thing before he goes, and there is an hour before the drove, and the room agreed to it in about nine seconds, and nobody in the room noticed that they had agreed to a thing about a man who is a day and a half off and would be a day and a half off on the night.**

**And then a woman of about twenty-nine who was in the room and had not said anything for two hours said, in the ordinary way, that the man of about thirty-eight had been asked about the chair in a kitchen in a town about nine miles off four days ago and had said that he would not take it, and that he had not said why, and that the room had not heard that from anybody and she had heard it from a man who had been in a kitchen, and that a name in a hat and a man who has already said no are not the same thing and only one of them can be read from the back of the room.**

Nobody asked her where she had heard it, and nobody asked her twice, and the woman of about sixty-three took the name out of the hat with a piece of chalk and put a line through it and wrote the other eight in, and the eight went round, and about nine hundred people in that township did not know that any of it had happened, and the man of about thirty-eight seven miles off was mucking out a byre and did not know that his name had been struck out of a hat in a room he has not been in since he was nineteen, and will not know until somebody tells him, and nobody in that room is going to.

## Four

A woman of about forty-nine said the thing that nobody had said, and she said it to the man of about thirty-four with the net and not to the man with the stick, and it is the fourth time in about nine days that a man of thirty-two with a limp has been told something in a room by a person who had not been asked to tell him anything, and he did not answer it.

**“The man in the chair is not the person it is for,**” she said. “**And you cannot put him there by drawing him out of a hat, and if you can, then you have made a room into a bench, and this room has been a room for a hundred and forty years.**”

Nobody improved on it. A man of about thirty-four said that the room could be asked to say it in a month when the drove was not going, and the woman of about forty-nine said that he could, and that a thing said in a month when the drove was not going was a thing said into the air, and that a room which says things into the air is a room with a habit in it, and that this room has never once had a habit in it.

**And the hat stayed on the shelf and the nine names stayed in the hat and the man of about thirty-eight seven miles off went on with his drove on the first of a month and will go on with it, and there are three months in every twelve with nobody in that chair at all, and nobody in that township can tell you which three they will be.**

**And a man of about thirty-four with a net said one more thing at about the tenth hour, and it was the only thing anybody said in that room all night that was not about the chair, and what he said is that the room has just agreed to be the only room in nine counties that says a thing out loud on purpose, and that the room is going to find out inside about four years whether that was a good idea, and that it will not be able to find out from anybody, because there is no other room to compare with.**

## Five

The cost of the night is a column and it was got up in a room that does not keep a column, which is a thing about four hundred years and one water trough and about nine counties.

**A room at six pence for the night, a lamp at two pence, a sheet of paper at one penny, a pound of wood at three pence, a loaf at one penny, cheese at two pence, four quarts of small beer at four pence, and a tallow candle at three pence: six, two, one, three, one, two, four, three is twenty-two pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and twenty-two pence is a little under half of one, and the woman of about sixty-three who keeps the book took the whole of it out of a box on the shelf and would not put it in the book, and said that the book is for what was agreed and not for what it cost, and that the trough has been in the book since her husband's time and the lamp has never been in the book once.

**And about two hundred people came into that township off six villages for a market on a Thursday and about nine of them went into a room over a store and agreed in about four minutes to say a thing they had never heard before once a month from a chair, and about nine hundred people in that township did not go into the room and were not asked to and were not going to be, and the room has about nine hundred people in it and about nine of them and that is the whole of what a public lot has ever been anywhere in this kingdom, and it has never once been a thing anybody outside the room could be in, and there is no form on which a person outside the room is asked whether they would like to be in it.**
