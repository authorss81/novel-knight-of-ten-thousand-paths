# Chapter 0434

**The thirteenth day of Fallowmonth, YR 318, a Friday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the tenth hour, at a stone in a hedge on a fold of ground thirty miles south of a market on the salt, where nine stones stand in two miles and about two hundred people live inside them and the man who walks the two miles has walked it since he was eleven years old.**

## One

He asked the question at the third stone, and he asked it the way a man asks a thing in the ordinary way, standing on the wrong side of a hedge with his stick in his hand.

"**Did anybody come up that drove on the second or the third of this month.**"

The man of about forty-one stood with his hand on the stone and said nothing for long enough that a crow would have gone over, and the silence is not him deciding whether to tell the truth. **He is a man who has been asked about a drove about four times in thirty years and the answer takes him a moment because it is not his to give in the ordinary way. A drove is not his. A boundary is his, and the difference between those two is the whole of his life and he has never had to explain it to a stranger with a stick.**

"**A cart went up it on the Monday and did not come back down it,**" he said. "**And a man went up it on foot on the Tuesday and did not come back down it either, and I did not see his face because I was at the sixth stone by then and the sixth stone is four hundred yards off the line of it.**"

"**You did not see his face.**"

"**I have not seen his face in thirty years and there are about two hundred people inside this boundary and I could not tell you the face of one of them and I could tell you the day and the hour and which stone was on the path side of which.**"

## Two

**And then he said the sentence, and it is twenty-one words, and it is the whole of what he said about the taking, and he said it to a man who had not asked him a question about the taking.**

"**I can tell you what came over and I cannot tell you what anybody did on the far side of it.**"

And he was right and it is the answer a boundary gives and no other answer in nine counties is as good and none of the others knows that it is giving it.

**A boundary of two miles and nine stones, walked by a man, is the only record in nine counties that can say whether a person has been there twice.**

Not the register. Not a day's book. Not the clerk's schedule of nine things in a yard, and not a form with three heads and no line for a name, and not a slate in a wall. **A boundary can say that a man came over on the Tuesday and not on the Monday, and it can say that a cart came up the drove and did not come back, and it can say it with nine stones and a man's feet and no paper at all, and it has been saying it in about two hundred people's ground for four generations.**

**And that is the thing about it, and he did not say it and he was not asked.**

A boundary is a record of crossings. **And a record of crossings is a record of persons, and a record of persons is the thing that a column with a name under it is, and a column with a name under it is what a man of thirty-four with a sheet went to look for in a form in the ordinary way seven weeks ago because a place with no person against it cannot be found on the ground by anybody.**

So the only thing in nine counties that can refuse a person is a thing that can also find one, and it has been both for about four generations, and nobody in that fold has ever had to use it for either, and about two hundred people live inside it and a boy in new boots does not have to walk it and was told so about two months ago.

## Three

**And the cost of asking that question in that fold is not the answer and it is not the two miles.**

The man of about forty-one went back to the third stone after the stranger had gone and put his hand on it again, and the reason he did that is not sentiment. **You touch a stone to know that it has not moved, and a boundary man checks his stones the way a man checks a wheel, and it is not a feeling about his mother's ground or anything of that sort, and anybody who says it is has not walked one.**

Then he went up to the fourth stone and the fifth and the sixth and the seventh and the eighth and the ninth, and he did it at the pace he has done it at since he was eleven, and he was back at the first stone at about the eighth hour.

**And on the way he worked out what a boundary is for, and he had never had to work it out before, and it took two miles.**

A boundary is for a dispute. **A boundary is for two people who both think the same field is theirs, and the nine stones settle it because neither of them wrote them down and neither of them can move them without the other one seeing.**

**It is not for a stranger who wants to know what a man in a coat did in a yard eleven days off with a cart that went up a drove and did not come back. And a boundary that has never been asked the second question has one job, and the moment somebody asks it the second time the nine stones are a list of who comes and goes, and about two hundred people inside them are on nine stones.**

He said that to nobody. **He is not a man who says things to people about what a boundary is, and there are about two hundred people in that fold and not one of them has heard him say anything about it at all, and one of them told him on a Thursday in Rainmonth that there was a boy in new boots who did not have to, and he said a short thing about the boy and did not say the other thing.**

## Four

**And what is on the two miles, and what is not, is worth having exactly.**

There is the practice, and it is the third of the nine, and it has been held in that fold since before the grandfather of the man who holds it now. **Nobody asked the man of about forty-one to hold it. It came to him when his father died, and his father had it from his father, and the third of the nine is *never a person twice*, and it is written on the back of a door in that fold and the door has been painted over it.**

There is a boy in new boots who does not have to walk it. There are about two hundred people. There is a byre, a stream, a lane, and a hurdle that goes across another lane twice a day nine miles off in a dale, and that is a different answer and it is not a worse one and it is not being weighed against this one.

**And nothing in the fold was improved on on the thirteenth of Fallowmonth, and nothing in the fold was refused, and nothing in the fold was ranked, and about two hundred people went on doing what about two hundred people do, and the only person in that fold who had been asked anything in about thirty years went home at about the eighth hour and had not been asked anything else.**

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is the longest walk of the ten days before it and it is three days of a horse.

**a tallow candle, a second horse's feed, a second cart's fare, a room, a third horse's feed, a cart's fare, a horse's feed, a fire, a bed, a quill and ink, a loaf, a sheet of paper, a quart of small beer: two, nine, four, six, nine, four, nine, three, two, seven, one, one, two is fifty-nine pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and fifty-nine pence is eleven pence over one, and thirty miles south on a Friday is three days and two nights on a horse that is not his and a mark and eleven pence of the difference came out of a man's own hand.

**And a boundary of two miles and nine stones can say whether a man has been there twice, and about two hundred people live inside it, and a boy in new boots does not have to walk it, and the man who walks it has walked it since he was eleven and has never been asked to use it for anything but a dispute, and on the thirteenth of Fallowmonth a stranger with a stick asked it the other question and the other question is a question a boundary cannot answer, and the man who walks it said so in twenty-one words and went back to the third stone and did not say one word about it to anybody.**
