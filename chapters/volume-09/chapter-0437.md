# Chapter 0437

**The twenty-third day of Fallowmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the second hour and the seventh hour and the twelfth hour, in the same paved yard behind the house of assizes, where the yard is bare to the middle of it, the two-room building in it is standing open, and the circuit was sitting in that town on the afternoon of that day and a cleared yard is a yard with nothing in it.**

## One

He came in at the top of the second hour on foot and he had come in on the Saturday and had slept in a barn four miles off with about nine other men in it, and the yard was bare to the middle of it and about nine feet of the wall at the back of it was still standing with the building against it.

**The door was off its sill and lying face down in the yard where somebody had put it out of the wet on the Monday morning, and the sill stone was out and against the wall, and the crowbar was on a cart and not on its bracket, and the bracket was empty and about eleven men from the first of the month were standing about in a cleared yard waiting to be told what was left.**

A man of about thirty-four with a sheet was there at about the second hour with a paper under his arm and he had a fair hand and he was not in a hurry in the way a man is in a hurry on the third of a month.

**And about two handcarts came into that yard at about the seventh hour, and four men, and a rope, and about nine feet of dressed stone, and a day-book on a stool, and nine packets of paper with string round them on the lower boards of a shelf that was coming down off its brackets in the next hour.**

## Two

**And the transfer is the whole of that morning and it took about an hour and it is not worth a paragraph of anybody being impressed by it.**

The packets came off the lower board first and went onto the left handcart. The stool came out and the day-book came off it and went onto the right handcart with the stool upside down on top of it. **A dressed stone about four feet long and about a hand thick came out of the doorway at about the eighth hour on two men's hands and went onto the left handcart on top of the packets, and the two handcarts went out of the gate to a cart standing on the made road, and everything that was in that building went out of it on two wheels and a rope.**

**Nobody burnt it, nobody read it, nobody counted it, and the nine packets with string round them were not on the paper with the seal on it and were not counted at all, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote a second line under his first line that said the building was cleared and did not say what had gone out of it, and that second line is accurate in the same way the first one is.**

And nobody said a word about the four men of the second of Fallowmonth and nobody said a word about the four men of the twenty-third, and the two groups are the same four and about nine men in that yard do not know that and one of them is a man of about thirty-two with a limp.

## Three

**And here is the thing that the chapter is for, and it took four seconds, and it was offered to him and he did not take it, and the man who offered it did not know that he was offering the third thing anybody has ever put in front of him that he has had to decide about.**

The left handcart had about nine packets on it and a dressed stone on top and it wanted to go about thirty feet to the gate and the ground is flagged and the flagged yard of a house of assizes is not level, and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar put his shoulder under the handle of it and got it four feet and then stopped.

"**You could take that end and it would save a trip.**"

That is eleven words and it is the ordinary thing to say to a man standing in a yard with nothing in his hands at the eighth hour, and it was said to a stranger, and no man in that yard knew anything else about the man he said it to except that he had been in the yard twice in a fortnight and had a stick and had not been in anybody's way.

**He said no. He said it in about one word and he said it the way a man says no to a thing he was not asked to do and has not offered to do, and he did not give a reason, and the reason he did not give a reason is that there were four possible reasons and every one of them was about himself.**

He could have carried about nine packets and a dressed stone thirty feet and been about four minutes late getting to the road, and doing it would have made him a man who helped, and a man who helped is a man who was there on purpose. **A witness is a thing a person is and not a thing a person is put in.**

That is seventeen words and it is the whole of what he had about it.

**And the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar took the other end himself on his own back and got the cart to the gate, and neither of them said anything else, and about nine men in a cleared yard watched a cart go out of a gate and went back to standing about.**

## Four

**And the twenty-third of Fallowmonth is the day he worked out that he was the connection, and it took about four seconds, and he did not work it out in a speech and he did not work it out to anybody and he worked it out in about nine feet of a yard with a stick in his left hand.**

**A receipt in a fair hand in a yard two days off, with a place on it and a figure of men on it and a trade on it, and the same fair hand on four figures at the bottom of a column on four feet of board in a kitchen nine miles on, with no head on the column and about three hundred and seventy figures on it and no way of saying whose any of them are.**

**Those are the same kind of thing. They are the same kind of thing in the same order and they are the kind of thing this has spent thirty chapters arguing against, and a man who has been in nine rooms in nine counties in about four months and has never once seen both of them at once saw both of them inside a fortnight because four men carried about forty sheets out of a shed with a door that was not locked, and one of those four men had once been twenty-three and had carried glass.**

He did not say one word about it to anybody that day or the next day or the day after that. **He did not write it down. He had a page with eleven lines on it and no words on any of them and he did not add a line, and the reason he did not add a line is that a line on that page is a thing he can be asked about, and a receipt is a thing anybody can be asked about, and a column with a head on it is the worst of the four things that can happen to a number and he has got a ninth of one of them in his coat already.**

## Five

The cost of that Monday is a column and it is three days and it is a day of a horse and two nights in a barn and a handcart that is not in it.

**A horse's feed at nine pence on each of the three days is twenty-seven pence. A bed at two pence on each of the three nights is six pence. A tallow candle at two pence on each of the three nights is six pence. A quart of small beer at two pence on each of the three days is six pence. A loaf at one penny on each of the three days is three pence. A fire at three pence on two of the three nights is six pence. Two handcarts hired for the day at four pence are eight pence. A room at six pence on two of the three nights is twelve pence. A sheet of paper at one penny and a quill and ink at seven pence are eight pence: twenty-seven, six, six, six, three, six, eight, twelve, eight is eighty-two pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-two pence is about one mark and two-thirds, and thirty feet of a flagged yard at the eighth hour is not in that column and would not have been in it if he had taken the other end of a cart.

**And about forty-one sheets went out of a building on the third of a month and a day-book and nine packets and a stool and a dressed stone went out of it on the twenty-third, and one man of thirty-two with a limp was in that yard on both days and carried nothing on either of them and was offered one end of a handcart and said no in one word, and he is the only person in nine counties who knows that two of those three things are the same kind of thing, and he is not going to tell anybody, and the notebook in his coat is eleven lines with no words on any of them and it is going to stay that way.**
