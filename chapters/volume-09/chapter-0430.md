# Chapter 0430

**The first day of Fallowmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the seventh hour and the eleventh hour, in a street four hundred yards long in a fen town of about four thousand people, where a market runs on two days in seven and this is neither of them and about two hundred people are out of their houses, and a place called Coldharbour, a figure of four hundred and seventy, and a trade of salt carting are being used in four counties, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp is in the street with a sheet of his own in the inside pocket of a coat.**

## One

About two hundred people out of about four thousand were out of a street four hundred yards long on the first morning of Fallowmonth, and about a hundred and sixty of the two hundred had gone nine miles to a chapel and about forty had gone nowhere in particular.

**And all three of them were being said in about nine houses in four counties before the fourth hour of that morning, and not one person who said any of them was looking at a form.**

At about the fourth hour a man of about twenty-six who carts salt out of a town of about two thousand people on the salt was in a yard at the near end of that street and said the trade to a boy of about eleven who was holding a cart pole, and the boy said the name of the place back to him, and the man of about twenty-six said the figure, and the boy did not know what the figure was for and asked and was told it was the people, and the boy said that then there was a great many at Coldharbour.

At about the seventh hour a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a second-hand stall had a slip of paper with three heads cut about forty years ago on it in a box to keep pins in, and about nine people saw it during the day and about four of them said the name out loud.

**And at about the ninth hour, at the far end of the same street, a man of about thirty-three with a barrow put a barrow-load of glass down on the wrong side of a threshold because the man who had ordered it had gone home, and stood there about a minute and a half, and did not say anything to anybody, and about two hundred people in a market town of about two thousand on the salt have heard that man sing all nine lines, and about nine of the two hundred heard the last of them, and there is no list in any of nine counties with either of the two men of that street on it.**

## Two

**The three have been in four counties for about twenty-nine days, and that is what in use means, and it is worth being exact about because it is not what anybody in that street would have guessed.**

The place went into a form on the twenty-second of Rainmonth and the figure on the twenty-fourth and the trade on the twenty-fifth, on three different days, in three counties, by three sets of people, and it came out of a room over a shop on a fixed Saturday in a band of about a hundred and four sheets tied with a cord.

**It is in a fen county that has a room over a weigh-house in it with a door on a lane. It is in the county on the salt where a market runs on two days in seven. It is in the county the kilns are in, two days west, and about nine people in that county have never heard of it and not one of the four hundred and seventy is in that county at all. And it is in a fourth county that has a made road in it and nothing else in common with any of the other three, and there is a man in that county who has been paid nine pence a day for three days in four since a month in Wolfmonth and who was not told one word of it.**

The last two counties got it off a slip of paper left at a counter for anybody going that way. **That is the ordinary way, and it is the whole of the machinery, and there is no machinery.**

## Three

**And the ninth line is still ruled and empty, and nobody in nine counties has been near it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has not been back to the room that holds it since the eighteenth day of Rainmonth, which is about six weeks ago, and he knows it is there, and a room let by the day has been let about nine times in six weeks.**

**It runs about nine feet along that board. Four hands have ruled it. The last of them belonged to a man of about twenty who was paid four pence to rule nine lines on a morning when there were eight words to put in them, and the ninth has been empty for about two months, and it is not empty because nobody had anything to say. It is empty because there is nothing in nine counties that will go in a line like that one. About forty people heard all nine of them in a market in Greenmonth with a load of dressed stone standing in the street. Nine of the forty heard the last one properly. One man of about thirty wrote it on the skin of his own hand and covered the writing with a bar of soap that evening and with the bare skin of it the next morning.**

**A road is not a place and has nobody in it and cannot be written on a form at all, and a place with a figure and a trade on it can be written on every form there is, and nine counties are being sent about nine hundred sheets a month out of the room where that is done, and the three of them are in four of the nine and the ninth line is in a room over a cooper's shop that is let by the day.**

## Four

**And here is what he had in the inside pocket of a coat on the first morning of Fallowmonth, and he wrote it himself and it is about nine inches by six and it has three lines on it and no head, and the three lines are not a place, a figure and a trade.**

They are the twenty-second of Rainmonth, the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth, and against each of the three of them a man of about thirty-four with a sheet, and that is all of it. **Three days and one name, and the name is not anybody's keeper and is on no roll in four counties, and it is the only record in the world of when the three were entered and by whom, and it is in a coat, and it was written by a man with a limp sitting on a bench on a made road in the eighth hour of an evening on the eleventh day of Harvestmonth, which is about three weeks ago.**

**And nobody in that street knows he has it. There are nine people in nine counties who could have written that sheet and about four of them have thought about writing it, and the clerk of about nineteen could produce it out of a file in a quarter of an hour and would not think of it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet is on a made road about nine miles off and has not spoken to him since the twenty-first day of Rainmonth.**

## Five

**And the man of about thirty-three with a barrow came up that street at about the tenth hour with a barrow-load of glass in it and a fold of paper in the inside pocket of a coat that is not his, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp was about nine feet off the same side of the same street, and the two of them passed each other at about the ninth pace and neither of them said one word, and about a hundred and sixty people in a town of about four thousand were nine miles off at a chapel and about forty were standing about.**

**And that is the whole of it and it is the last of it, and neither of the two men was asked anything by anybody on the first morning of Fallowmonth, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow has not opened what he is carrying and the man of about thirty-two with a limp has not given his sheet away and is not going to, and the last morning of that month is a morning and not a page of anybody's column, and about four counties are carrying three things that are all true.**

The cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the shortest one in about a month and it is not in the sheet.

**A loaf at one penny, a bed at two pence, a tallow candle at two pence, a horse's feed at nine pence, a room at six pence, a fire at three pence, a second loaf at one penny, a sheet of paper at one penny, a quill and ink at seven pence, and a quart of small beer at two pence: one, two, two, nine, six, three, one, one, seven, two is thirty-four pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and thirty-four pence is about two-thirds of one, and the whole of what a Sunday costs a man who is not going to give anybody a sheet of his own.
