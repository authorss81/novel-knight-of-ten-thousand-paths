# Chapter 0444

**The twenty-fifth day of Embermonth, YR 318, a Friday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the eleventh hour, in the same meeting room over a store in a township of about nine hundred people, where the room is taken by the day and there is a book about a hundred and forty years old on a shelf behind the door that nine people have now decided shall not have anything to do with them, and nine people are in it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is in it and is the tenth, and the youngest of the nine is seventeen and has not shown anybody anything in four years.**

## One

**Nobody asked her. That is the first thing and it is the whole of the arrangement that made it possible, and it happened because a woman of about fifty with a board had asked for a book on the Friday before last and had been refused, and a room that has refused one thing in a fortnight does not ask a second thing in the next one.**

The woman of about seventeen said it anyway, at about the sixth hour, standing up, in the way a person says a thing they have had in their mouth for four years and have never said.

"**There is a road nine miles long and it is about nine miles west of this room,**" she said. "**And there is a gate at the end of it, and there has been a gate at the end of it for about four hundred years, and the road has not been a road anybody uses for about four hundred years, and there are people on the far side of that gate.**"

She stopped there for a while and the room let her.

"**A woman is on the other side of that gate and she is my mother, and I came to this side when I was nine, and I have not been down the far half of that road since I was nine, and I have not seen her since I was nine, and I am seventeen.**"

**And that is the whole of what she said, and nobody asked her how she knew, and the man of about thirty-four with a net had his mouth open and did not use it, and the reason he did not use it is that he had asked a question on the Saturday and had been given an answer and the answer had been no, and a room that has been given a no holds on to it for a while.**

## Two

**And here is what she did not say, and it is worth having exactly why, and the reason is not modesty.**

She did not say where the road is. She did not say which of the eight the other seven of the nine are, and the nine miles is the one figure in what she said that a person could get up and check, and none of the nine has a chain or a chain's man or any reason to walk nine miles to find out. She did not say how many people are on the far side of the gate, and when the woman of about fifty-eight with the slate said, in the ordinary way, that a figure would be useful, the woman of about seventeen said that a figure is the one thing that is not wanted, and gave the reason in eight words, and the reason was correct.

"**A figure is how you get sent to.**"

She did not say how she knew that there was a gate at the end of nine miles of a road that has not been a road for four hundred years, and nobody asked, and that is the ninth thing that book of about four hundred entries has never been shown for.

**The book is made out of sheets she buys and folds and it is four years old and it is about four hundred entries and it has about nine marks in it in a hand that is not hers, and she has never shown it to anybody in four years and she was not going to show it in that room, and she was asked for it on the Friday before last by a man of about thirty-four with a net and she said no, and he did not ask twice.**

## Three

The man of about twenty-two with a handcart was the one who said the thing that made the next meeting happen, and he said it at about the eighth hour and he said it to the woman of about seventeen and not to the room.

"**I carry things to doors,**" he said. "**That is what I am for. I have carried glass, lime, a barrow of sand and a dressed stone four feet long about four hundred times in four years and every single one of them went to a door with somebody standing at it. And if there is a gate nine miles off with somebody on the other side of it, then I am the one person in this room who could go and stand at it, and I am not going to, and you are not going to ask me to, and here is why.**"

He put his hand flat on the bench.

"**I do not know what is on the far side and neither does she, and a man of about twenty-two with a handcart who turns up at a gate that has been shut for about four hundred years is a man who has been somewhere he cannot say he has not been, and a road with a man on it is a road somebody can write down.**"

**And that last thing he said is sixty-seven words, and the whole of what he said at the eighth hour is a hundred and sixty-eight, and the woman of about fifty with the board wrote the number of them on the back of her left hand and did not tell anybody then and has not told anybody since, and the ninth word and this one are in the same mouth in the same week and the mouth does not keep either of them.**

Nobody sent anybody. Nothing was opened. The gate at the end of nine miles of a road that has not been a road for about four hundred years is exactly where it was on the twenty-fifth of Embermonth and it will be exactly where it is on the twenty-fifth of a month in the year after this one, and nine people in nine counties know there is a gate at the end of it, which is nine because there are nine of them in a room, and the tenth of that room heard it an hour ago and is not one of the nine.

## Four

**And here is the finding of the twenty-fifth of Embermonth, and it is not about the gate, and nobody in that room was looking for it.**

The man of about thirty-two with a limp did not say one word about the mother.

He was the tenth. He had told four of the nine himself and he was the only person in that room who knew where nine of the eleven places in nine counties were, and a room of nine that had just been told there was a gate at the end of a nine-mile road was a room in which the only person worth asking was a man with a limp, and he sat on a stool by the door from the second hour to the eleventh hour and said nothing at all, and he did not write anything in the eleven lines, and he did not take the sheet of nine inches by six out of the inside pocket of his coat, and nobody asked him for it.

**And the reason he said nothing is the reason he has said nothing for about four weeks about a receipt, and it is a reason about himself and there are four possible reasons and all four of them are about him.**

If he had said one word in that room, then a thing a person of about thirty-two with a limp had found out about a gate would be a thing that people knew he had found out about, and a man who is on no list in nine counties is on no list for a reason, and the reason is that there is nothing on him, and the moment there is one thing on him he is a person with a thing.

**A receipt is a record, and a record with a place and a figure of men and a trade on it is the thing nine counties have been arguing about for about a year, and a gate is a place, and a mother is a person, and those three are the three heads on a form cut about forty years ago in a room two counties off by a man who is not alive.**

**A man who is on nothing is the only kind of man there is who can hear all three of those in one afternoon and be asked about none of them, and he has been that man for about four months and it has cost him everything he had, and it is the only thing he has done in about four months that nine counties did not have to pay for.**

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is six days after the day a word was said out loud in that room and it is the last column of that kind he keeps before the winter.

**a loaf at one penny, a sheet of paper at one penny, a second sheet of paper at one penny, a bed at two, a second bed at two, a third bed at two, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, a quart of small beer at two, a second quart of small beer at two, a cart's fare at four, a second cart's fare at four, a horse's feed at nine, a second horse's feed at nine, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a broom at five, a tin of nails at five and a bushel of oats at four.** One, one, one, two, two, two, three, three, two, two, four, four, nine, nine, two, two, five, five and four is sixty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and sixty-three pence is a mark and fifteen over**, and nine miles of a road that has not been a road for about four hundred years is not in it and is not going to be.

**And a boy of seventeen stood in a room and said three things and was not asked one question, and a book of about four hundred entries in four years has still never been shown to anybody, and a gate at the end of nine miles of road is still shut, and people live on the far side of it, and about thirty-four people live in a field and a lane at this end of it, and no figure of the far side has ever been printed on anything at all, and that is not a rule anybody set and it is a property of the absence.**
