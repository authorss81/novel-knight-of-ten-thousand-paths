# Chapter 0424

**The twenty-fifth day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the third hour and the seventh hour and the tenth hour, in the office over the shop where a form with three heads has had two lines filled in since the twenty-second and the twenty-fourth of that month, on a morning when about nine hundred people are in the street four hundred yards long below it, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar is at the rail with a docket in his hand that nobody has looked at, and he is there for about four minutes and he is the reason the third line is what it is.**

## One

The third line wants a trade. **A place and a figure are on the form in a hand of about thirty-four and both of them are true, and the third line has been empty for two days, and a man who has not slept went round two counties on the Thursday and came back with a word.**

He did not go and find a trade. He asked, in a yard in Merefen on the Thursday, a man of about twenty-six who carts salt out of the town of about two thousand people on the salt, and the man of about twenty-six said the trade on that road is salt, and he said it the way a man says the weather, and then the man of about thirty-four asked the second one, a man of about fifty-three who takes loads the other way up the same road, and the man of about fifty-three said the same thing in different words.

**So the third line got salt carting, written at the third hour of a Saturday in a room over a shop by a man of about thirty-four with a sheet, and it is not a lie, and there is a town two days off where salt is put into boats and about two hundred carts a week go up a made road past a field with a gate on it, and the nearest trade to that field has been salt carting since before the field was a field.**

That is the whole of how the third line was filled and it took a man of about thirty-four about nine minutes in a yard and about four minutes at a counter.

## Two

**And here is the four minutes at the rail, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar is the reason the third line says what it says and would have said it anyway.**

He had come in off the salt road with a load of glass and was standing where he stands, and the clerk of about nineteen asked him which road he had come in by, as he asks everybody at that rail, and the man of about thirty-eight said the made one, and the clerk said he would want a docket, and the man of about thirty-eight put a piece of paper on the counter and did not look at the form and did not read the two lines on it.

He read the three heads.

**He read the three heads the way a man reads a sign on a road he has been down a thousand times, and the third one is a trade, and there was a word on the third line already, or there was a clerk about to put one there, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said twelve words.**

“**A trade on a field is a licence and not a description.**”

And it is twelve words, and it is the whole of what he had to say about it, and he said it to the counter and not to anybody, and then he took his docket and went out, and the door of that office is on a street four hundred yards long and a market was in it.

## Three

**Nobody in that room had the standing to ask him what a licence is, and the reason is not that they did not want to and it is that they could not.**

The clerk of about nineteen is nineteen and is in his second year and cannot ask a man of about thirty-eight who has been on the strength of the county's own road since he was about seventeen why the county's own form is wrong, and would not be thanked for it if he did.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet is on the same strength and is a month behind the other man in years of service and has a form in front of him with a place and a figure on it, and the whole of the standing a man of about thirty-four has in that room is the standing of a man who is holding a form somebody else started.

And the man of about thirty-four could not ask him either, and for a different reason, and the reason is a Saturday in the previous month in a room over a door on a lane, and about nine feet of floor in front of a rail, and a long sentence about a thing with a head on it and a name under it, and a mason of about sixty-eight saying nine words to a bench-board.

**So a man of about thirty-four heard a man of about thirty-eight say the true thing about a column and did not ask him a question about it, and the man of about thirty-four went on being the man who is right about roads and wrong about forms, and the two of them have been on the same strength for years and have never once had a conversation about a form, and neither of them is anybody's villain, and about two hundred people in that street know that both of them were in that room.**

## Four

The form was finished by about the ninth hour, and a form with a place and a figure and a trade on it is a thing that goes out, and it goes out on the fixed day, and the fixed day is a Saturday, and the last fixed day was the Saturday before this one.

**That is the whole of the timing and nobody arranged it. Three errands, four days, and a form that came complete seven days before the day it goes out on, and the clerk of about nineteen put it in the middle of the next band without being asked and without thinking about it, because that is where the finished ones go, and there were about a hundred and four in that band and about a hundred of them were other people's.**

And a toll is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and a bar is not a rate and a return is not a toll, and nothing in this kingdom has ever put two of those on one sheet at once, and a licence is not a trade and a trade is not a description and a place is not a name and a name is not a person, and every one of those five is in nine counties every day, and about nine years of a county's own road have never had the three of them on one piece of paper and there is nobody in that room who thinks of the morning as the first time.

## Five

**The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the officer's again and the crowbar man is not in it and does not know that he is not in it.**

**A sheet of paper at one penny, a loaf at one penny, a quart of small beer at two pence, a horse's feed at nine pence, a bed at two pence, a tallow candle at two pence, a room over a shop at six pence, a fire at three pence, a man of about twenty hired to copy the third line out fair at four pence, a second handcart for four miles at seven pence, and a second sheet at one penny: one, one, two, nine, two, two, six, three, four, seven, one is thirty-eight pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and thirty-eight pence is about four-fifths of one, and the whole of what it costs to be right about a road and wrong about a form is a third of a mark and a bed and a handcart.

**And a form with a place called Coldharbour in the first line and four hundred and seventy in the second and salt carting in the third went out of that room on a fixed Saturday in a band with a cord round it, in the middle of about a hundred and four sheets, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room into nine counties, and one of the three on that form is a field, one of the three is the number of people who went down nine miles of a road in a day and a half, and one of the three is the nearest trade to a gate, and every one of the three is a true thing, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said twelve words about it at a counter and went out through a door into a market and took his docket with him and did not turn round.**
