# Chapter 0439

**The twenty-ninth day of Fallowmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the seventh hour and the tenth hour, at a yard in a town of about nine hundred people and at the gate of a lane that crosses a field, where a man of about twenty-six is loading, a man of about twenty-two with a handcart is waiting, and a woman of about fifty-eight has the yard swept every morning of the week whatever the week is.**

## One

**There were three counts made on that Sunday in three places about eleven miles apart, and not one of the three was made for any reason anybody could have given, and not one of the three people knew that the other two were counting.**

A man of about twenty-six who carts salt out of a town of about two thousand people on the salt has been up a made road for about nine years and takes loads up it and back and he counts what goes up a road, because a man who does not count what goes up a road does not know whether he is being robbed.

**Carts into the yard behind the assizes from the second of the month to the tenth of it was nine. One a day, on the nine days there is traffic, which is what a made road in a town of about nine hundred people does, and the four of the nine on the days a market is on the road. And one of the nine went in on the morning of the third with a cart and came out on the same cart with a satchel on it, and the other eight came out as they went in.**

A man of about twenty-two with a handcart was hired on the morning of the second of Fallowmonth to take a load out of that yard and did it in about an hour, and a man who is hired by the hour counts what he carries in fives, because counting in fives is faster and he was paid by the hour.

**Forty-one sheets go on a handcart as eight fives and one. He said that to a man in a yard on the twenty-ninth of the month, unasked, in the tone of a man saying what day it is.**

A woman of about fifty-eight has had the yard swept at about the fourth hour every morning for about nineteen years and has swept it herself, and a woman who sweeps a yard counts the men who come into it, because a yard that is cleared for a circuit has about eleven men in it in the last week and a yard that is not has about four.

**Men in that yard on the second of Fallowmonth was four, and on the twenty-third of Fallowmonth was four, and on about nine other mornings in between it was eleven, and she did not know that four of the four had a paper with a seal on it and she has never asked any of them what they were in her yard for and she is not going to.**

## Two

**And the eleventh of the eleven is Ivo Serrat, who is seventeen and carries glass between kilns and has been carrying it for four years, and he can read figures and not words, and he has a book of about four hundred entries in it that he made himself out of sheets he buys and folds, and he has never shown the book to anybody in four years and nobody has asked to see it and nobody is going to.**

He was in the yard at about the seventh hour on the twenty-ninth with a hand-basket of glass and there was nothing in it to deliver, because the man who had ordered it had gone home, and he is a man who has been paid nothing and told nothing and left standing about four hundred times in four years.

**And he counted, because that is what he does, and he counted three things, and none of the three of them was given to him.**

**Four men, off the mouth of the woman of about fifty-eight, which is a number he does not know he has.**
**Forty-one sheets, off the mouth of the man of about twenty-two, which he was told on the same morning and did not hear because he was counting a load.**
**Nine carts, which he worked out himself, because he has been up a made road for four years at about nine miles a day and he knows what a made road in a town of about nine hundred people does.**

**And then he counted the fourth thing, and it is the one that is his, and nobody asked him and nobody told him and no person in nine counties knew he was doing it.**

The building in that yard has its door off its sill and its contents gone and its shelves on the wall, because a cleared building has its fixtures. **The shelf is nine feet long and two boards high on iron brackets, and the top board is bare, and the lower board is bare, and on the lower board there are nine worn places about the width of a hand in a line down the middle of it, and the wood in them is paler than the wood either side and they have been there about nine years.**

He counted them with his thumb. **Nine. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine. And he put the side of his hand on the top board and there is nothing on the top board, because loose sheets do not have string round them and a string does not wear a board and a string is what a packet is for.**

## Three

**And here is the arithmetic, and it is the last arithmetic anybody does in this, and it is a boy's, and it is done in a doorway in about four minutes with his thumb on a piece of wood.**

He knows what about forty sheets weighs because he has been carrying glass for four years and a hand-basket of glass is about as much as he can lift and a sheet is lighter than a pane by a long way and about forty sheets is about a basket.

**Nine of about forty is about three hundred and sixty.**

And then the second half of it, and this is the half that is his and not anybody's.

**About three hundred and sixty sheets went out of that building on two handcarts on the afternoon of the twenty-third of Fallowmonth, on top of a stool and a day-book and a dressed stone, and nobody counted them, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote a second line under his receipt that said the building was cleared and did not say what went out of it.**

**And about forty-one sheets went out of it on a satchel on the second, and the receipt is accurate, and the receipt is a receipt for about a ninth of what was in the building, and the nine worn places on the shelf board are the only record in nine counties that there was ever any more than forty-one.**

He said it out loud in the doorway, to nobody, in the tone of a boy saying a number he has got, and it is twenty-two words and nobody heard it.

"**Nine of about forty is about three hundred and sixty and I have got it in my book and it is mine.**"

**And then he wrote it in the book, at the end, in his own hand, on a page of figures with no head on it, and the book has about four hundred entries in it and it is four years old and about nine marks in it are in a hand that is not his and a mark is a mark and he has never asked anybody what a mark is and he is not going to.**

## Four

**And the thing he found out by counting, and nobody told him it, and he is seventeen, is the one that nobody in nine counties is going to be told either.**

**Four men, nine carts, forty-one sheets. Forty-one sheets is one basket. A boy of seventeen can carry one basket nine miles in a morning and he does it four days a week. And about three hundred and sixty is nine baskets. And a handcart was brought to that yard on the morning of the third and went out on the same cart with a satchel on it and the cart was not loaded at all.**

**So four men brought a cart to a town of about nine hundred people to move about one basketful, and they were at it from about the third hour to about the seventh hour, and they counted it twice, and a boy of seventeen has been carrying one of those basketfuls nine miles four days a week since he was thirteen.**

**A man who is in a hurry does not count a basket twice. A man who does not know what is on a shelf does not bring a cart.**

He got that out of three numbers on the same afternoon, and **it is the reason nobody in nine counties is going to be brought to anything, and it is the reason the man of about thirty-four with a sheet is not a villain, and it is the reason the volume is not going to say who sent four men with a paper and a crowbar to a door that was not locked, and it is also the reason the man who sent them is not going to be found, because the man who sent them is a person who did not think about a shelf at all.**

## Five

The cost of that Sunday is a column and it is two days and it is the last column in the block that has a horse in it.

**a bed, a second quart of small beer, a sheet of paper, a room, a quill and ink, a candle on the road, a fire, a second loaf on the road, a quart of small beer, a second cart's fare, a cart's fare, a second loaf, a bed on the road, a horse's feed, a tallow candle, a loaf on the road, a third horse's feed, a loaf, a second horse's feed: two, two, one, six, seven, two, three, one, two, four, four, one, two, nine, two, one, nine, one, nine is sixty-eight pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and sixty-eight pence is a mark and twenty pence over, and about three hundred and sixty sheets is not in it and never will be.

**And four men and nine carts and forty-one sheets and nine worn places on a shelf board, and about three hundred and sixty is the last number that does any work in nine counties in this, and it is in a book of about four hundred entries that a boy of seventeen made himself out of sheets he buys and folds, and the book has never been shown to anybody and it was not shown to anybody on the twenty-ninth of Fallowmonth, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was in that yard at about the seventh hour and did not see the boy count the board and will not be told.**
