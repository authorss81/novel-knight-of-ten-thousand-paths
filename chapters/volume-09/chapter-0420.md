# Chapter 0420

**The nineteenth day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in four places that are about eleven miles, about thirty miles, about nine miles and about two hundred miles apart, on a day the county does not trade, and the four places are a township, a fold of ground, a dale, and a room over a door on a lane, and the four of them are not joined here, and the four are not added, and a yard keeps its count in work and a week and a county keeps its count in Saturdays and Thursdays and the two are not converted.**

## One

A Sunday is the day the county does not trade and about two hundred people out of about four thousand in that town were out of their houses, and about a hundred and sixty of the two hundred went to a chapel about nine miles off, and about forty went nowhere in particular and stood about, and the bench did not sit, and the room over the cooper's shop was let to nobody and was shut, and the room over a door on a lane was open because it is open on every day of the week and a man of about fifty-eight who keeps the minutes came in at about the second hour to close a window that had been open since Saturday, and there was nothing to close up and he shut it anyway and went out again.

**And in four other places four other mornings happened, and they are set out as four mornings and not as one, because making them into one would be the whole of what a county has spent four hundred years arguing against, which is that four things done by four people who have not met are not one thing done by one person.**

## Two

**In a township of about nine hundred people, two days off that made road, a room over a store eleven paces by nineteen has a chair on a platform and a hat on a shelf, and a thing none of the nine hundred had heard of before was taken on on the eighteenth of Longlight, which was a Thursday, and this is the first day of the month the chair was going to be filled, and the man who fills it in that township is a man of about thirty-eight who lives seven miles off in the next fold and who takes a drove to a market a day and a half off and who has gone on the first of a month ever since he was nineteen, and the platform was bare, and the room had known it would be bare, and about nine people in that room had arranged a good answer on that Thursday, and the good answer was that a man can do a thing in the hour before a drove goes, and there was an hour, and nobody was in it.**

**Thirty miles south, in a fold of ground with a byre in it and a stream about nine feet wide, a man of about forty-one mended a hurdle at the second rail of the byre fence and a boy of about nine took a stone out of a wet hole with both hands and set it back leaning, and neither of them left the fold, and a Sunday is not a market day, and about two hundred people in that fold were in their houses, and the nine stones of that boundary stood where the last four generations of men had walked them, and about four inches of one of them stood where a clerk of about sixty in a town two hundred miles off had put it, and the number of inches is in that clerk's head and is in neither of the two kinds of minute he keeps, and he does not know it is wrong, and nobody has ever asked him, and nobody in that fold has had a boundary to argue about for about thirty years.**

**Nine miles off a made road, in a dale of about nine hundred people, a man of about forty-four who farms the near side put a hurdle across a lane at about the seventh hour and took it back across at about the ninth, and the lane runs four miles with a stream beside it and about two hundred acres of grazing off it, and about nine hundred people got an ordinary Sunday done in a valley that sends no cart up that lane, and about two of the nine hundred know that a man with a stick went down that lane and back on a Friday and was fed at two doors and asked nothing, and neither of the two knows why, and there is no form anywhere in this kingdom on which a person puts down not wanting one, and the whole of what that dale has is a hurdle and a sentence and about nine hundred people who have never had a reason to want either.**

**Two hundred miles off, in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, a keeper of records of twenty-nine stood at a board on a Sunday morning with a day-book open in front of it, and the day-book has nine entries in it of a thing nobody asked, and it was not opened, and a press has stood open since a day in a month that nobody in that building will put a date to, and nine words came back off a road about two days off a coast asking whether the first of the eight is still being done anywhere, and the nine words are in a drawer with about four hundred other returns and have been there about six weeks, and she has not answered them, and she is not going to answer them this morning either, and she is the only person in nine counties who has read that question twice.**

## Three

**And here is what that Sunday found, and it found it without anybody looking, and it is not about the eight and it is not about the ninth and it was not found out by anybody who went looking for it.**

There are four things in nine counties that a person can point at in the rain, and they are not the whole of it. **A boundary walked by one man who is forty-one and whose father walked it and whose grandfather cut three of the nine stones, and a boy of nine in new boots who has been told in nine words that he does not have to. A chair in a room that a hat fills once a year and that has been empty once. A plate in front of about nine people a week at a roadhouse, kept for thirty-one years on four feet of board with no head on it by a woman of about fifty who will not read a figure out loud. And a hole in a wall with nine figures and no head in it under a stone on a bench in a shed nine feet by seven, kept by a man of about nineteen who can count a barrow of bricks and cannot read.**

**And here is what the four of them are attached to, which is a bar of nine feet of oak at the head of a made road, and a cart, and about a hundred and four sheets on the fixed day out of a room with a door on a lane, and about nine hundred sheets a month into nine counties the other way, and the two are not the same figure and are not added, and eight words on eight of them, and three of the eight nowhere at all because three people said no in three weeks, and one of the eight in a clock case above the milking pails in a byre with two older ones in it, and one in a drawer in a market town, and one in a coat that is not his on a road four miles west of a town of about two thousand on the salt.**

**And not one of the people who hold any of this is on a list, and no two of them has ever met another of them, and about four of them have been asked nothing by anybody in about forty-one days, and the whole of the arrangement is in nine mouths and one board with no head on it and one boy's own book of about four hundred entries that he cannot read, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp is the only person alive who knows all of it, and he is on no list, and there is no form anywhere in nine counties that has his trade against any of the nine, and if he were not there in a year's time there would be eight places and a bar and a cart and about nine counties with nothing in them that anybody could be asked about.**

**And that is not a finding anybody made. It is what a Sunday is, in four places, when the county does not trade, and nobody made anything of it and nobody explains it and nobody admires it, and the four paragraphs above do not touch, and a toll is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and a bar is not a rate and a return is not a toll, and there is not a clerk in this kingdom who could put a pound of lime and a sheet of paper in the same column, and nobody has ever tried.**

## Four

**And the cost of that Sunday is a column, and it is in one place, and it is the only one of the four that has ever had one, and it is the year and not the Sunday, and the three others have never had a column and are not going to get one and are not added to the fourth.**

A farrier at one shilling and four pence is sixteen pence. A mason of about thirty-eight at four shillings to re-cut a mark on a boundary stone is forty-eight pence. A pair of a boy's boots, second hand, at two shillings and two pence, is twenty-six pence. **Sixteen and forty-eight and twenty-six is ninety pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence and that is one mark and forty-two pence, and not one of the two hundred has ever seen that sum written down anywhere, and about four of them could go through it item by item from memory and would not need to see it, and a pair of boots is a thing a man pays for and not a cost anybody else is carrying, and it is in the same column as a farrier because the man who paid for it is the same man and not because it is the same kind of thing.**

**And the other three places cost three pence, nothing, and the price of a cart, and those three are not added to the ninety and the ninety is not divided by three, and a yard keeps its count in work and a week, and a county keeps its count in Saturdays and Thursdays, and a fold keeps its count in a year and one man, and the three frames have never been added to one another in four hundred years and this is not the afternoon they start.**
