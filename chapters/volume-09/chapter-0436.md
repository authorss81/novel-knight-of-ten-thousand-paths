# Chapter 0436

**The nineteenth day of Fallowmonth, YR 318, a Thursday and a market day, and the third hour and the seventh hour and the eleventh hour, at a roadhouse nine miles on from the kilns on the made road, where about nine people come through the door in a week, and a woman of about fifty has stood in front of that fire for thirty-one years putting a plate in front of whoever comes in before she has asked them a single question.**

## One

The board is four feet long and it is against the wall behind the fire and it has been against that wall for about thirty-one years, and it has no head on it.

**It has about three hundred and seventy figures on it, at about nine to the inch, going down the board in one column, and the column is about forty inches of a board that is four feet long, and about four of them at the foot of it are in a hand that came in with glass.**

**There are three hands in the whole of it. One is hers and has been hers for thirty-one years. One belongs to a woman who is dead and was dead before the board was nine years old. And the third one belongs to a man who was about twenty-three about eleven years ago and used to come into that room with a load of glass four days a week and put his figure at the bottom of the column and go out, and she told him to do it the first time and never told him to do it again and he never needed telling, and there are four of his on the board and they are the last four and the last of the four is about eleven years old.**

She does not know what he became. **He went out of that room about eleven years ago and he was not on a roll then and he is not on one now, and a woman who cannot read does not know what a man of about twenty-three became, and she has never once wondered, because a plate in front of a person and a figure on a board are two different trades and she has only got one of them.**

## Two

A man of about thirty-four with a sheet came into that room late in the day on the second of Fallowmonth, after the yard, and gave her a piece of paper he had written out again that same afternoon in his own hand.

**He gave it to her because she is the only person on eleven miles of that road who hands a stranger a written thing when a stranger pays for something, and a man who has taken about forty sheets off a shelf has a receipt on him that he has to put somewhere, and she is the ordinary answer to a question he did not have to ask.**

**He said seven words, and it is the whole of what he said.**

"**Keep this and do not lose it.**"

And she cannot read, and she said so, and he said that did not matter, and he went out. **That is the ordinary way and it is not a kindness. A receipt that is left with a woman who cannot read it is still a receipt, and a receipt that is read is a conversation, and a man in a hurry on a market morning does not stop for a conversation in a kitchen nine miles off a made road.**

## Three

**And here is what happened on the nineteenth, and it took about nine minutes, and she did it and nobody asked her to and nobody could have asked her to because there is nobody in nine counties who could have put the question.**

The man of thirty-two with a limp came in at about the seventh hour and had a plate put in front of him, and she did not ask him anything, and that is the practice and it has held for thirty-one years and it is the eighth of the nine and it is held by a woman who does not know she is holding anything.

**Then she put the piece of paper down on the table beside the plate, and she did not ask him to read it either, and she said one thing about it, and it is twenty-four words.**

"**He put four of them on there and he was not asked to and he was not thanked and they are still on it.**"

And then she went behind the fire and got the board down off the wall and set it against the table with the back of it towards the room, which is what you do with a board of four feet in a kitchen, and she put her finger on the last four figures, and she put her finger on the top figure on the paper.

**She cannot read the words on the paper and there are about four lines of them and they are a fair, clear, ordinary hand. She can read figures, and she has been looking at a column of them for thirty-one years, and she does not need to read a hand to know a hand.**

It is the same hand. **A two that has a flat foot on it and a seven that has a stroke through the bar, and about three hundred and seventy figures on a board, and one on a piece of paper, and about nine counties in between, and the last of the four went on that board about eleven years ago, and the paper is about sixteen days old, and the two are eleven years and a fortnight apart in a kitchen where nobody has ever written down a date.**

## Four

**And a figure with no head on it is a thing with two properties that a figure with a head on it does not have, and the woman of about fifty found both of them out in about nine minutes at a table in her own kitchen, and she has not told anybody either of them and she is not going to.**

**A figure with no head cannot be refused. She cannot refuse him those four. He did not ask her for them and he does not know they are on that board and there is no line on that board that says whose any of them are, and if he came in and asked her for the four she would have to say that she does not know which four, and she does know which four, and she would say so, and then she would have to hand over a figure with no head on it, and you cannot hand over a figure with no head on it because there is nothing to put in the hand.**

**And a figure with no head cannot be taken back either, and the same absence is the reason. There is no name against his four and no date against them that she could name out loud and no line and no head, and about four marks in the column are in his hand and about thirty-one years of the column have never had a head, and the only person alive who could say which of the three hundred and seventy figures in that room belong to the man of about thirty-four with a sheet is a woman who cannot read and has been keeping it for a woman who cannot read.**

He is named on a receipt in four counties. **And four of his figures are in a kitchen nine miles on from the kilns with no head on them and a fire in front of them, and neither of those two things can find the other one, and the man who wrote the receipt does not know that a figure of his is nine miles off in a column of three hundred and seventy, and the woman does not know that the man who wrote the receipt is the man who put four numbers on her board eleven years ago, and she does know, and she knew about a minute before he came in and she has not said it to him and she is not going to.**

## Five

The cost of that Thursday is a column and it is a market day and about nine people came through the door and she fed all nine of them and not one of the nine is in this.

**a loaf, a tallow candle, a bed at two pence, a second bed at two pence, a horse's feed, a quill and ink, a fire, a second sheet at one penny, a room, a sheet of paper, a loaf on the road, a quart of small beer, a handcart for nine miles: one, two, two, two, nine, seven, three, one, six, one, one, two, six is forty-three pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and forty-three pence is five pence under one, and a plate in front of about nine people is not a line in this column and has never been a line in any column in nine counties and is the only one of the two that anybody in that room would call a cost.

**And four feet of board stood against a table for about nine minutes with a finger on the last four figures, and about three hundred and seventy of them have no head on them and about four of them are in the hand at the foot of a receipt in a yard seventeen days off, and the board went back on the wall behind the fire at about the eighth hour, and a woman of about fifty washed up, and nobody in nine counties will ever be shown both of those things at once except one man, and he was in the room and he did not say a word about it to anybody.**
