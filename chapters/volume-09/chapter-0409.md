# Chapter 0409

**The first day of Longlight, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the seventh hour and the ninth hour, in a field about four miles off a made road in a fen county, where a boundary oath-stone stands in a hedge with a figure cut into its face that is twenty-one years old, and a man of about sixty keeps two kinds of minute and is not going to keep a third, and about thirty miles south of that town a man of about forty walked nine stones alone this morning, and nobody in that fold knows that anybody else walked anything today.**

## One

**Longlight is when a boundary oath-stone is renewed and the whole of the renewal is that the neighbours walk it and a figure is cut into the face of the stone with the year, and the stone goes back in the hedge.**

This one is in a hedge at the corner of two fields and a lane, and it is about three feet and it is not dressed and it was never dressed, and the figure on it is a cut like a bracket and a stroke, and the stroke is the year and it is twenty-one years old, and it is eleven years overdue, which is why there are about nine people in that field on the first of Longlight and about four of them live in one of the two fields.

**It is not the stone that the records speak of, and nobody in that field has ever heard of the other one, and there is about nine inches between them and they have nothing to do with each other.**

The clerk is about sixty. He came at about the fourth hour with a bag, and he has kept two kinds of minute for about nineteen years, and the two kinds are these.

**The first is a walk.** Who came. In what order. Which side of a hedge they stood on.

**The second is a figure.** What was cut into the stone and when.

**And he is not going to keep a third, and he said so at about the seventh hour in seventy-one words, and he is a functionary in a field and about sixty and has been for nineteen years, and the seventy-one words are the best thing anybody said to a man with a stick in about nine days.**

“**I have got the walk and I have got the year,**” he said, “**and if I wrote down the names to go with them then in about nine years there would be a man in a town two hundred miles off who could tell me that a field of about nine acres had nine people in it on a Monday in Longlight, and he could not tell me one thing about the hedge.**”

## Two

**They walked it before the stone came out.** A boundary oath-stone is not a place, it is a mark on a line, and the line is about two hundred yards in that field and a lane and two fields, and about nine people walked it in about forty minutes, and about four of the nine live in one of the two fields and about two live in the other and about three live in the lane, and a man of about sixty walked it at the front with a bag and did not hurry and did not go round anything.

**And the first of the nine was a man of about sixty-seven who farms the field on the near side, and he was forty-six when the figure on the face of that stone was cut, and he is the reason it is eleven years overdue, and nobody in that field has ever asked him about that either.**

## Three

**The stone had to come out of the hedge and go back into the hedge and a dressed block cannot be lifted out of a hole in a hedge by hand without about two men and a lever and a length of board, and about nine yards of it, and the field is soft on the lane side because the lane is the low side.**

**Tovan Kest is the man of twenty-five and he owns the crews and the yard four hundred yards up a road in that town, and he is one of nine men in it, and the yard sent a barrow and two men and a length of board, and the yard does not do work in Longlight that it has not agreed to, and the man of twenty-five agreed to it in about nine seconds because a barrow going four miles for a day is not a job anybody has to think about, which is the whole of his opinion of it and he was right.**

He came with the barrow. He did not come to see the man of thirty-two with a limp and he was not asked to and he was not asked why he was not, and they had three sentences together in about four hours and all three of them were about load.

“**Put a board under the near wheel,**” Tovan said.

“**It is soft for nine feet and then it is not.**”

“**It is soft for nine feet going and it is hard coming,**” he said, and that was the whole of the craft in that field and Aren Kest had not thought of it and would not have thought of it, and Tovan Kest put a board under the near wheel and the barrow came out of a hedge with a three-foot stone in it without anybody lifting it and nobody shouting.

**And that is all that happened between the two of them and there is no more of it, and neither of them has said one word about any year, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand would not believe it either way.**

## Four

**The mason came at about the fourth hour and he is about forty-three and he brought an apprentice of about fifteen and he is not the mason of about sixty-eight and he does not know there is a mason of about sixty-eight.**

Cutting a year into the face of a field stone is not masonry. **It is a chisel held at about eleven degrees and about nine blows with a mallet behind it, and a man who does it wrong cannot be told and cannot tell himself, and the difference between the two of those is the whole of the trade.**

**The clerk cut it. Not the mason. The clerk has cut this figure about nine times in nineteen years and he does it with the mason's tool and the mason's assistant holds the head of the chisel and does not swing it, and the mason of about forty-three stood about nine feet off and watched and did not offer, because a man who offers to do that has told you he does not know which of the two of you does it.**

The stroke went in at about the seventh hour. **A bracket and a stroke, the bracket the same bracket as the one twenty-one years old and about a third of an inch to the left of it, and the stroke four cuts, one for each of the four figures of the year, and a mason of about forty-three said afterwards that it was good work and the clerk said it was the same work he has always done and the mason said that was not the same thing and did not press it.**

## Five

**What the stone goes back into is a hole in a hedge with about nine inches of play on one side, because a boundary stone has never been anything except a thing that somebody can find from a field.**

That is the whole of it and it is nine inches of play and about four inches of it goes in facing the lane and about five goes in facing the two fields, and the number matters and the number is not written down and there is no second person who knows it, and if that number is lost then the stone is a stone in a hedge, and there is about nine hundred of them in this county and about four hundred in this parish.

**And the clerk knows the number and nobody else in that field does, and he is about sixty, and his two kinds of minute do not hold it, and he said so himself in fifty-seven words at about the ninth hour when a man of twenty-five asked him, not because it was wanted but because it is nine inches and a man with a barrow was standing over it.**

“**Four inches to the lane,**” the clerk said. “**And I have not written it down, and I am not going to write it down, and if I lose it then a field of about nine acres and a hedge of about forty yards will be argued about in about eleven years, and the arguing will not be about the hedge.**”

“**Why not.**”

“**Because the arguing will be about which of nine men said what,**” the clerk said, “**and I have been the man who writes down which of nine men said what for nineteen years and I have watched what it turns into, and it turns into the thing itself, and the hedge goes.**”

## Six

**The cost of the day is a column and there are two things in it and the third thing is not in it.**

**A mason at three shillings and sixpence is forty-two pence, and his assistant for a day is in that and not on top of it, and two men off a yard of nine men at six pence a man is twelve pence, and forty-two and twelve is fifty-four pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence.** And a clerk is not paid out of any part of it, because a clerk is paid by a county and not by a field, and that has been the arrangement for about nineteen years and nobody has ever proposed another.

**And a barrow went four miles and came four miles and a man of about sixty cut a year into a stone that is in a hedge at the corner of two fields and a lane, and a figure went in that is about a third of an inch from the last one, and about nine people stood in that field and about four of them live in one of the two fields, and none of them knows the number of inches, and a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag did not come, and a man of about fifty-eight who kept a store for nineteen years did not come, and the two of them have not been in that field in about eleven years and one of them is dead.**

**And the second kind of minute says the year, and the first kind of minute says who came, and there are two kinds and there is not going to be a third, and about nine inches of a stone in a hedge is held in the head of a man of about sixty, and the eight words are in a coat in a town four miles off, and none of these two things has ever been in the same room and there is no form in this kingdom that puts them in the same room, and Longlight is over, and the stone stays in the hedge for another year.**

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp walked back about four miles to a town of about four thousand people on a road that is hard coming and soft going, on the first of Longlight, in a year that does not turn until he is sixty, with four pounds in his coat and a sheet of eight words he is not carrying anywhere, and there is a yard four hundred yards up a road in that town that he did not go into, and about nine men were in it, and one of them is twenty-five and told him to put a board under the near wheel, and that is the whole of what is between them and it is a board and it worked.**
