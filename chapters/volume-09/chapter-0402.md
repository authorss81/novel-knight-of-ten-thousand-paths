# Chapter 0402

**The first day of Greenmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a back room over a cooper's shop on a market street four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people in a fen county, where a market runs on two days in seven and this is one of them, and there are about ten people in the back room and about nine hundred in the street, and the street is not the subject and the room is.**

## One

The room was taken for the day. **A cooper's back room is nine paces by fourteen and it has a bench-board in it, a bench-board four feet off the ground, a stove that is not lit because it is Mudmonth's turn to be Greenmonth's and a stove in a back room is a thing a man asks about, and there is about nine feet between the door and the board and ten people in nine feet of it and one of the ten is standing in the doorway with his back to the street.**

**The nine words were on the back of a sheet.** Not nine words, eight, in a hand that belonged to a man of about twenty who had been hired for the day at four pence and who had asked twice whether he was to put a head on it and had been told twice that he was not, and the sheet is the back of a sheet because the front of it had somebody else's arithmetic on it and the front was somebody else's because the front was a form.

The eight, in the order they were sung, in chalk on a millimetre of dust that was on the bench-board and in a pen where there was no dust:

> **Ask the far side.**
> **Two books.**
> **Never a person twice.**
> **Read it yourself.**
> **Say it in a room.**
> **Leave the name off.**
> **Anybody may come.**
> **Feed the stranger first.**

**And under the eight, at the bottom, in the same pen, a ninth line ruled in and nothing on it, because the man of about twenty was told to rule nine lines and there were eight words.**

## Two

The first one was asked at about the second hour and it was put to the room by a man of about thirty-three with a barrow, and the question was who keeps it.

**Four people answered and the four of them were not known to each other and had never been in a room, and the man of about forty-seven who was standing in the doorway with his back to the street said afterwards that he could tell they had not been in a room by the shape of the answers.**

“**My uncle does it,**” the woman of about fifty at the salt warehouse said. “**He has a yard at Cask and when there is a load to bring he asks the far side whether they can have it before he sets off and not after he has set off.**”

“**I ask the far side whether they want it now or at the turn of the day,**” a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said, “**and if they want it at the turn of the day I come back at the turn of the day and it is half a mile further and I am not paid for the half mile.**”

“**I do not ask anybody,**” the man of about thirty-one with the canvas bag said. “**I ask my own mouth what I already knew, which is not asking anybody, and I have been doing that about twenty years.**”

“**I asked a man on a road in Harvestmonth,**” a woman of about thirty-four said, “**whether I might take a load of stone off his ground, and he said go on, and that is the whole of it and he would not remember me.**”

**Nobody in that room said where they had got it, and none of the four of them had the words, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that out loud twice, once after the fourth answer and once after the eighth, and it is the only thing he said in eight hours that was about the song at all.**

## Three

**The eighth one took the room for about forty minutes and gave that room its finding.**

Feed the stranger first. The woman of about fifty at the salt warehouse said that she had put a pot on at the back of her house in Embermonth of the year before last because a man came in off the towpath with no shoes and no business and about four o'clock in the day, and that she has done it every day since, and that she has a figure.

“**I have a figure,**” she said. “**I have had a figure since the eleventh week. I do not write it anywhere. I know it the way you know how many times you have gone down to the pump.**”

“**What is the figure,**” the man of about twenty said, with the pen.

“**I am not going to tell you,**” she said, and then she said why, and the why was about nine words and it is worth having exactly. “**If I have a figure and you have a figure, then there is a number of me, and I am a woman who puts a pot on.**”

“**It is eleven,**” said a man of about thirty-four who had said nothing for two hours.

“**It is a number,**” she said. “**That is all it is, and you are all going to write it on something, and I have watched that happen to a thing I told about four years ago and I am not doing it twice.**”

**Nobody in that room wrote eleven down, and that is the first thing anybody in this county has said out loud in a room and not had written down, and the reason there is no record of it anywhere is that the man of about twenty had the pen off the board by the time she had finished, and he put it in his pocket, and he is a man of about twenty, and he will be a man of about fifty in this town.**

## Four

**The ninth line was asked about four times in that room and it was answered four times in four different ways and none of the four answers was about the ninth line.**

“**It is a name,**” the woman of about thirty-four said, and that was the first answer, and it was wrong and it was the answer nine people in nine counties would have given.

“**It is a place,**” the man of about thirty-eight with the crowbar said, and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar knows the difference between a place and a name because he has stood in both.

“**It is not a word at all,**” said the man of about thirty-three with a barrow, and then he stopped, and about four people looked at him, and he said that he had heard a man of about thirty-two with a limp say so in a road in the second week of a month and that he had not understood it then and did not now.

“**It is a line,**” said the man of about twenty-seven who had come in with a load of glass and had been fed a plate before anybody asked him his name, and he was the only person in the room who had not been asked anything at all before the fourth hour, and he said, when he was asked about it, that a line ruled for nothing is a line somebody meant to fill in, and that there is a boy on a road who has about four hundred of them in a book, and then he stopped, and did not say the boy's name, and about half the room noticed that he did not say it and about half of them did not notice at all.

**And the ninth line stayed ruled and empty and about nine feet long, and nobody knew what it was for, and the man of about twenty ruled it because he was told to rule nine lines.**

At about the fifth hour the man of about twenty asked the question that the rest of the room had been working towards for three hours without saying it, and he asked it with the pen out.

“**Do I want a column,**” he said.

The room did not answer for about a minute.

“**No,**” Aren said. “**Not for a name.**”

“**Then what is it for,**” the man of about twenty said, and he was not being difficult, and he had been hired at four pence and was owed four pence and would be paid at the eighth hour whether or not the sheet was finished.

“**It is for a word and it is for a word because a word on a sheet is the only one of the eight that is going to be here in fifty years,**” Aren said. “**And a name in a column is a handle. That is not an opinion. A man of about fifty-eight with a key kept a store for nineteen years and somebody wrote down his trade in a column and had a go at a place of about nine hundred people on a river four hundred miles off with a sheet and a figure in it, and the column did it, and nobody had to break anything.**”

“**And the eight of them would go in one column with a name under them,**” the man of about thirty-three with the barrow said, “**and you would have nine rows and one of them would be a man and eight of them would be a thing a man does.**”

“**I know.**”

“**Then what is the sheet for,**” the man of about forty-seven said. He had not spoken for two hours and he had said the thing that everybody in the room had been agreeing with for two hours and that nobody was going to be able to write down.

“**It is for us,**” he said, “**and it is worth having and I will not argue with you about it. But you are all going to go home and forget eight lines of chalk, and four of you will have forgotten them by the time you are down the street, and in forty years there will be a woman at a kiln who knows the first one and a man at a road who knows the second one and neither of them will know that the other one is a thing, and that is how you get a rule that is a rule in one place and a habit in four hundred and a piece of a document in a drawer in a county that has been looking for it since the year it was written.**”

**And that is the whole of the argument and it went on for about nine minutes and it was not resolved, and the man of about forty-seven said afterwards that he had not expected it to be and that he had been in nine rooms about a sheet in about four months and this was the first one where anybody had said the other thing out loud.**

## Five

**There was a woman of about twenty-nine in that room the whole of the day.** She came in at about the second hour and she stood on the left of the board where the door does not open on anybody, and she did not have a question and she was not asked one, and at about the seventh hour a man of about thirty-four asked her what she made of it, out of the ordinary way, in the tone of a man asking a person in a room, and she said that she had nothing, and he said sorry, and that was the end of it.

**Nobody has ever asked her what she makes of anything, and there is a form in this kingdom for asking a person a question and it has a head on it and the head is four words long, and the form is not the reason and the form is not the reason at all.**

## Six

At about the eighth hour the man of about twenty was paid four pence and he put the sheet on the bench-board and asked who it should go to.

**There were ten people in the room and eight of them could have taken it and every one of the eight had a reason, and four of the reasons were good and one of the reasons was that a man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that if it stayed in the room it was on a bench-board, and a bench-board is a thing in a town that a bench can come into, and a bench sits on Saturdays.**

The sheet stayed on the bench-board. **The room let the light out and the fire did not need lighting and nobody put the eight words anywhere else, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that he would be back in a fortnight because the cart comes on the fixed day, and it does, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow does not ride the cart, he walks beside it, and he told nobody that and was not asked.**

**A sheet of paper with eight words on the back of it and no head on it is in one place, and there was a plan for nine, and the nine is a plan and not a fact, and the one place is a bench-board in a back room over a cooper's on a market street four hundred yards long, and the door is not locked and the room is let by the day.**
