# Chapter 0427

**The eighth day of Harvestmonth, YR 318, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, nine miles on past four lime kilns on a made road that runs between a market and about four hundred people, in a house with about nine feet between the kitchen and the front room, where a slip of paper with three things on it has been on the counter for five days, and a boy of seventeen with a hand-basket of glass has been coming into that room four years and has been fed about four hundred times and has never once been asked for anything, and on this morning he was asked for nothing and did the thing himself.**

## One

The slip had come in with a carrier on the Saturday and had been left at the counter for anybody going that way, which is the ordinary way, and it is a printed strip about four inches by two with three things on it and three heads cut about forty years ago in a room two counties off.

**Coldharbour. Four hundred and seventy. Salt carting.**

The woman of about fifty did not read it and did not turn it over and did not put it anywhere, and she left it on the counter the way she leaves everything anybody brings in, and about nine people a week come through that door and about four of them looked at it in two days and about one of the four can read.

Ivo Serrat came in at about the second hour with a hand-basket of glass, and the woman of about fifty put a plate in front of him, and he ate, and he said that there was a number on the paper on the counter, and that is the whole of what he said.

## Two

**The boy cannot read the words. He can count, and the counting of a person who cannot read the words is a different thing from the counting of a person who can, and the difference is that he does not know which things on a page are numbers and which are not until he has counted them and found out.**

He stood at the counter for about a minute and a half and did not touch the slip and did not pick it up. He looked at it the way a man looks at a row of hooks.

"**There is a four, a seven and a nought,**" he said. "**And there is a nine under it, and the nine is not like my nine.**"

**And that is the whole of what the arithmetic in that room was, and it was nine minutes long, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who was at a table by the fire did not understand one word of it and did not interrupt it once.**

"**That seven is not yours,**" the woman of about fifty said, from behind the plate.

"**It is mine,**" Ivo Serrat said. "**I do it with the bar across the top. I have done it four years. I have got about four hundred of them in a book I made myself and every seven in that book is made that way and anybody who has seen two of them would know it.**"

**And the seven in four hundred and seventy is his, and it got there in the ordinary way and there is no man anywhere who could say how.**

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet did the sum on the back of one of the nine pieces of paper, because the nine pieces were on the counter and the one nearest his hand was the boy's, and he turned it over and added the nine figures on the back of it and copied the total off the back onto a form with three heads. The nine pieces went into a file. The file went into a room with a door on a lane. The form came out on a fixed Saturday in a band of about a hundred and four sheets.

**And a hand cannot be dated, and a piece of paper cannot be asked who was standing at the counter when it was turned over, and the whole of what happened to that seven took about four seconds on the twenty-fourth day of Rainmonth in a room over a shop and it cannot be proved by anybody, ever, in any of the nine counties.**

## Three

**And that is where the seven words came out, and they are the only seven words he said in that room and he did not know they were seven.**

"**I have got that mark nine times.**"

**He said it about the ninth hour, with the plate empty and the book out of the inside of his coat and shut, because the book is always shut and it is never opened in that room, and he has never shown it to anybody in four years, and nobody in that room asked to see it, and nobody is going to.**

The mark is a mark neither of them can read. **The woman of about fifty has it on four feet of board in a kitchen nine miles off with about three hundred and seventy figures on it and no head on it, and he has it nine times in four hundred entries in a book he made himself, and about nine marks in the two of them are the same and this is the tenth, and the person who could tell them what it is is a mason of about sixty-eight about forty miles off who is out of work and does not know that either of them has it.**

Nobody explained it. Nobody asked him to read the book out loud. He did not offer.

## Four

**And then he did the arithmetic, and it is the only arithmetic anybody has ever done on a book of numbers about a thing nobody has ever measured, and it is arithmetic and not a crossing, and nothing was opened.**

He counted the book. He had never counted it. He counted the entries at about the ninth hour and got about four hundred and ten, and he counted them again at about the tenth hour and got about four hundred, and he said that a book of figures nobody can read out loud cannot be counted twice the same, and he was not complaining about it.

**And then he counted the nine.** Nine entries in about four hundred, and the intervals between them, counted in days, are all the same number, and the number is a number of days, and the sum of nine equal intervals is nine times that number.

"**A road is the only thing you can count in days,**" Ivo Serrat said. "**A field is not. A field you can count in acres and you can count in seasons. You can only count a road in days, and you only get to count one if somebody made it to be gone in a day, and somebody counted it, and they did it about four hundred times.**"

**And that is the identification, and it is an arithmetic, and it identifies a thing that is on no sheet in nine counties: a road that is nine miles and is a day's walk and is a thing that was gone in a day on purpose, four hundred times, by people who were going somewhere. It is not a road to a field. It is a road that was built to be used and shut and left, and about four hundred years ago it stopped, and nobody in nine counties knows the day it stopped, and it is not in his book and it is not going to be found out from him.**

He did not go down it. He has not been down the far half of it. Nobody asked him to and nobody in that room thought of it and the road is four miles on and nine miles off and there is nothing at the end of it but a field with a gate on it.

## Five

The cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is a boy's column and a boy's is the smallest thing in this kind and it is the only one that has ever been right.

**A second loaf at one penny, a hand-basket mended at three pence, a bed at two pence, a tallow candle at two pence, a quart of small beer at two pence, a loaf at one penny, a boy's wages for a day at four pence, a fire at three pence, a second quart of small beer at two pence, and seven days of the same at four pence a day: one, three, two, two, two, one, four, three, two, twenty-eight is forty-eight pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence, and that is a mark exactly, and it is the whole of what a week of a boy who carries glass between two places and cannot read his own book costs anybody.

**And a slip of paper on a counter with three heads cut about forty years ago has a seven in it that a boy of seventeen has been making since he was thirteen, and about four hundred and seventy is a figure of persons at a place that has about thirty-four people in it, and a road that is a day's walk and was shut about four hundred years ago is on nine hundred sheets a month in four counties, and a boy counted his own book for the first time in four years and got two answers and said so, and nobody asked him a second question and he did not offer one, and the nine marks in the two collections are ten now and the tenth is in a file two hundred miles off and nobody in nine counties will ever see all three of them at once.**
