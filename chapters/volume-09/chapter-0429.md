# Chapter 0429

**The thirteenth day of Harvestmonth, YR 318, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the eleventh hour, at a kerb four miles west of a market town of about two thousand people on the salt, on a made road that about two hundred carts a week come up whether anything is lying across it or not, and today something is, because today is one of the four days in seven, and a man of about nineteen has been at one end of it for two hundred and fifty-nine days, and about nine carts will come up it in about four hours and about fifty-four came up it on the last Sunday.**

## One

Three figures and not a fourth, and the three have not changed since the day he came off the cart, and one of the three is the only one anybody has ever kept a count of.

**Nine feet of oak. Nine pence a day. And a coat that belongs to somebody else with the wages of it twisted up in paper in the left pocket of it. He has counted the twists. He has never counted the days, and there is no other column of figures in that shed and there has not been one since he came.**

The working, and it is the only arithmetic about his year that any person in that town has ever set down, and it is the third time it has been set down and it has been set down by him and not by anybody else.

**Two hundred and fifty-nine days is thirty-seven weeks, and the bar is up four days in seven, and thirty-seven weeks is a hundred and forty-eight up-days, and the one day in seven he is not paid for is the Sunday, and there are thirty-seven of those in thirty-seven weeks, and a hundred and forty-eight less thirty-seven is a hundred and eleven. There are about a hundred and eleven twists of paper in the left pocket of a coat that is not his.**

And it cross-checks, and the cross-check is the reason it is on the page. **On the twelfth day of Rainmonth there were two hundred and twenty-eight days of it, which gave about a hundred and thirty up-days of which about ninety-eight were paid. Thirty-one days is four weeks and three days, and four weeks is sixteen up-days of which twelve are paid, and the three of them are two up-days of which one is paid, and about a hundred and thirty and eighteen is about a hundred and forty-eight, and about ninety-eight and thirteen is about a hundred and eleven.**

## Two

**A Wednesday is a day the bar is up and he is paid and about a fifth of a Sunday's carts come up it, and that is the whole of what a Wednesday is at that bar.**

About nine carts come up that made road in about four hours on a Wednesday, and about fifty-four come up on a Sunday, and a Friday is about nine, and the largest thing that comes up it is nine hundredweight of dressed stone on a cart and about two of those a week come up a day and a half off, turn round at the far end of the made road and come back the same way.

**The four hours between the second hour and the sixth hour are the whole of his trade. He has a lever and a fulcrum and a second and a half to use them in for each one, and about nine times in four hours the two of them agree without either of them saying anything, and there is no negotiation on that road at that bar because a man of about nineteen has nine feet of oak and about a second and a half.**

## Three

The man of about thirty-three with a barrow came up that road at about the sixth hour on the Wednesday and pulled up nine feet short of the lever, which is nine feet further than he has come in about four months and the same nine feet as the last two times he came this way, and he had a fold of paper in the inside pocket of a coat that is not his, thirty-nine days old and still folded, and he had not opened it.

**And this time he asked, and it is the first time anybody has asked him anything at that bar since a man of about thirty-three with a barrow put a sheet on the end of it on a Friday in Greenmonth and told him he had been told not to say what it was.**

“**What is on the sheet on that bench.**”

And the man of about nineteen looked at him and did not answer for about four seconds, and the four seconds are not him deciding whether to lie, and a man of thirty-three with a barrow watching his face in those four seconds could not have told the difference and was watching it.

“**I do not know and I cannot read and nobody has ever asked me either.**”

That is fifteen words and it is the whole of what a man of about nineteen said to a man of about thirty-three with a barrow at a bar on a Wednesday, and it was believed, and about two hundred people in a town of about two thousand people on the salt would have believed it.

**And it is the wrong question, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow asked it because he is a man who asks the next thing, and the right question was what the far end of that nine-mile road is called, and the man asking it has been up that road for about nine years and has not been down the far half of it either, and the reason is the same ditch, and he is not going to now.**

## Four

He did not ask anything else. **He has been carrying a sheet for thirty-nine days and has not opened it, and he has now found out that there is a second one on a bench in a shed nine feet by seven under a stone, and he is not going back into that shed to lift the stone, and he has not been inside that door since a Friday in Greenmonth, and there are two pieces of paper in that county with nine figures on them and no head on them and a date at the bottom and not one person in four counties can read either of them.**

The sheet on that bench has been under that stone since the fourteenth of a month in Greenmonth, which is about eighty-nine days, and it has lifted twice off the bench in that time and the stone is the reason it is on the bench and not on the floor of that shed.

**Behind the stove in that shed is a bundle the size of a small dog, and it has not been touched for about two hundred and nine days, and it is the only thing in that shed that belongs to the man at the bar. The bar is the yard's and the coat is somebody else's and the sheet on the bench is nobody's, and the bundle is his and he has not opened it. He has a sister of twenty-two about forty miles off on the road the cart came in on and the two of them have not spoken in about a year, and about forty people in that town know it and not one of them has said a word about it. About four hundred yards west of that shed a boy of about eleven with a pail stands on the shoulder of a ditch at every hour of the daylight, and about two hundred carts a week go the long way round that quarter of a mile, and about two hundred people know exactly why they do and not one of them has ever started it.**

## Five

**And here is the whole of what the thirteenth day of Harvestmonth cost, and the bar's own money is not in this column because the bar's money is a year and not a day and it is printed above and it is not added to anything here.**

**A cart at four shillings is forty-eight pence, and about two carts of glass and lime went up that road that day that nobody asked him to take, which is ninety-six pence. A barrow-load of lime at eighteen pence, and he had one and got paid for it in a yard. A bed at two pence, a quart of small beer at two pence, a loaf at one penny, a tallow candle at two pence, a handcart mended at three pence, a wheel iron at eight pence, a broom at five pence, a quart of oil at four pence, and a sheet and a quill at three pence: ninety-six, eighteen, two, two, one, two, three, eight, five, four, three is one hundred and forty-four pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and forty-four pence is three marks.

**And about a hundred and eleven twists of nine pence in the left pocket of a coat that is not his is nine hundred and ninety-nine pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence, and not one of those four figures is written down anywhere in four counties, and the man who could put them together is a man who cannot read, and he has stood at one end of nine feet of oak for two hundred and fifty-nine days and has not asked anybody for a hand with a sum, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow was nine feet off him at about the seventh hour and believed him, and went on up the road with a barrow-load of glass and a fold of paper he has not opened and a second one he now knows about, and neither of the two of them ever will.**
