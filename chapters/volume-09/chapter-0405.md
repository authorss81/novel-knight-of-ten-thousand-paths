# Chapter 0405

**The fourteenth day of Greenmonth, YR 318, a Friday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the eighth hour, at the head of a made road four miles west of Marl, a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a bar of nine feet of oak has been going down at the second hour and coming up at the sixth on four days in seven since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and a man of about nineteen has been standing at one end of it for a hundred and seventy days, and the county does not trade that day and about two hundred carts a week come up that road whether it is up or down, and about nine hundredweight of dressed stone on a cart is the largest thing that comes up it, and one man was at the head of it in every hour of the daylight.**

## One

The bar is up on a Monday and a Wednesday and a Friday and a Sunday and down on the other three, and it comes up in about four seconds on a lever and a fulcrum and a man of about thirty with a mallet cut the two sockets in that kerb in about two hours and the sockets are four inches deep and the road is nine feet wide.

**He is paid nine pence a day for the three days in seven it is up. He is not paid for the four hours. He is not paid for the four months of the year when nobody comes, and a person who is not paid for the four months and is not paid the four hours is a person this kingdom has no rate for at all, and he asked for the three days out loud in a yard in the second week of a month in Wolfmonth where nine men were setting terms, and nobody agreed to them and nobody disagreed with them.**

**This is a Friday and the bar is up and he is paid nine pence, and the nine pence is in a twist of paper in the left pocket of a coat that is not his, and he has not emptied that pocket in a hundred and seventy days.**

**A bar that is up four days in seven for a hundred and seventy days gives about ninety-seven, and he is paid for three of the four, and that gives about seventy and three, and there are about seventy and three twists of paper in that pocket, and he has counted the twists and not the days, and counting the twists is the only column of figures he has ever kept.**

The shed is a yard's and it is nine feet by seven and there is a stove in it and a bench against the far wall with a blanket on it, and the bench is there for a reason a clerk in a town two hundred miles off would not understand and about two hundred people in that town do.

**The morning of that Friday was the hundred and seventieth and it was the way the other hundred and sixty-nine were.** The bar goes down at about the second hour and it goes down in about four seconds on a lever and a fulcrum, and on a Friday about nine carts come up that made road in about four hours, and on a Sunday about six times that many, and each cart is somebody else's afternoon, and a man of about nineteen has about a second and a half to say whether a cart goes round the bank or back down the road again.

**He does not wave anybody through and he does not wave anybody back. He stands at one end of it and he looks at the cart and the cart looks at the bar, and about nine times in four hours the two of them agree without either of them saying anything at all, and it is the only part of that road on which there is no negotiation, and there is no negotiation because a man of about nineteen has a lever and nine feet of oak and about a second and a half to use them in.**

## Two

**The man of about thirty-three with a barrow came up that road at about the sixth hour and stopped about nine feet off the bar, which is nine feet further than he has come in about four months, and he had a sheet of paper in his hand and he put it on the end of the bar.**

He said nothing for a moment. The bar came up about a second later on the lever and the man of about thirty-three did not step back far enough and the chain swung and did not catch him.

“**From a room over a cooper,**” he said. “**And I am told to leave it and not to say what it is and I have been on this road about nine years and I have never left anything for anybody and I did not know until this morning that I could.**”

“**What is it.**”

“**I do not know,**” the man of about thirty-three said. “**I was told to leave it and I was told the same by a woman who is not the one who wrote it, and I have got a barrow and a load of glass in it and I am doing this on the way to somebody else.**”

Then he said the thing that made him stop coming up that road, and he said it in about nine words and he did not know that he had said anything.

“**I am not to say,**” he said. “**And there is a ditch four hundred yards west of that bar that takes a loaded barrow down about two inches, and I came up here three times last winter and I did not come a fourth, and I have never told anybody that, and I have not got a word for it either.**”

**And the man of about thirty-three with a barrow put his hand on the bar and looked at the sheet on it and did not touch it, and the sheet was face up, and the writing on it was at the top.**

## Three

**The sheet has nine figures on it.** Not nine words. **Nine lines, and each line has a figure at the front of it, and the figures are in a column down the left, and there is nothing at the top of the sheet at all, no head, no seal, no signature, and a date at the bottom in the same hand as the figures and larger than the figures.**

The man of about nineteen looked at it for a while.

“**That is a number,**” he said.

“**That is nine numbers.**”

““**They are numbers,**” he said, “**and I cannot read them. I can count to a hundred and I can do a sum of two things and I cannot read, and those are three separate things and only the last one of them is the one that is the matter here.**”

**And there is a thing about a man of about nineteen who has been on no roll in four counties for one hundred and seventy days, and it is not that he is stupid, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow worked it out inside about four seconds and it is worth having exactly.**

“**You cannot read.**”

“**I can count,**” the man of about nineteen said, and he said it without any weight on it at all, in the tone of a man correcting a thing about the weather. “**I can count a barrow of bricks. I have been counting cartloads off that road for a hundred and seventy days and there is a man who used to take four and he stopped in Thawmonth and it was nothing to do with me.**”

“**Can you read the day at the bottom.**”

**He put his finger on it. It was the fourteenth of a month, and the number was two digits, and he looked at the two digits for about as long as a man looks at a thing he has decided is going to be a mistake.**

“**I know which day it is,**” he said, “**because there is one of these most days.**”

## Four

**About eight people in that town of about two thousand have worked out that a man of about nineteen who came off a cart on the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth ought to have gone home on the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth, and about four of the eight are wrong by a day and about four of them are right, and not one of the eight has ever said one word to him about it, and there is no form on which to say it, and a man who says it to a man of about nineteen who is standing at a bar is a man who has come four miles to be a fool in front of a bar.**

**The four that are right have it right for a reason and the reason is not in that town.** His four months were set in a yard in the second week of a month in Wolfmonth by nine men, and the four months were four months, and the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth is four months off the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and a man of about nineteen has counted that off the front of a shed about four times in about five months and got it right every time.

**And the four who are wrong by a day are all wrong in the same direction, and that is the half of it that matters.** The four months began on a day that nobody said out loud, in a yard, standing at eight, while it was holding a span over a beck, and nine men set a date and did not say it, and a date that has never been spoken in a room is a date that about nine hundred people in nine counties are carrying differently in their heads, and about four of the eight who know have it wrong and all four of them have it wrong the same way, and a man who has watched four people make one mistake the same way learns the mistake in about nine days.

**The man of about thirty-three with a barrow had the sheet in front of him and a day on it that he could read and he did not read it out, and the reason he did not read it out is that he was told not to say what the sheet was, and a man who has been told not to say what a thing is does not go on to say what day it is on.**

That is the whole of what happened on that road on that Friday, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow went on four miles to somebody else's door with a load of glass in a barrow and the ditch on his left, and did not come back up that road again that week, and about four hundred yards further on he stopped and looked back and then went on.

The man of about nineteen put the sheet on the end of the bar, then took it off and put it flat on the bench inside the shed, and put a stone on the corner of it, because the stove is against that wall and the draught in that shed comes off the floor about a foot and the sheet had already lifted once.

## Five

**A bundle has been in that shed for about a hundred and twenty days and it has not been opened in about a hundred and twenty days and it is not a bundle a man packs for four hundred miles and it is not a bundle a man packs for one mile.** It is behind the stove and it is about the size of a small dog and it is the only thing in that shed that is his.

**He has a sister of twenty-two in the county he came off the cart from and he has not spoken to her in about a year, and about forty people in that town know it because the cart came off the north road and the sister works on the same road, and about nine hundred yards west there is a ditch that takes a loaded barrow down about two inches and a boy of about eleven with a pail has been on its shoulder every hour of the daylight, and about two hundred carts a week have gone round that quarter of a mile every day since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and every cart in them is somebody's afternoon, and nobody in that town has complained about it once and nobody is going to.**

**And on the end of a bench in a shed nine feet by seven there is a sheet of paper with nine figures on it and no head on it and a date at the bottom that nobody has read out loud, and a stone on the corner of it, and a man of about nineteen who cannot read and has not asked anybody to read it and is not going to.**

**The nine figures are nine places, and the date is a day, and the man of thirty-three with a barrow did not read the day out, and a hundred and seventy days is a long time to be standing at a bar with a date in a room two hundred miles off and not know what is written on the end of your own lever.**
