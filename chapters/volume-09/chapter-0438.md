# Chapter 0438

**The twenty-fifth day of Fallowmonth, YR 318, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the third hour and the eighth hour and the eleventh hour, at three places about eleven miles apart: a roadhouse on a made road with a plate on a bracket over its door, a yard off that same road, and a kerb four miles west of a market town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a man of about nineteen has been at one end of nine feet of oak since a day in a month in Wolfmonth of the year before.**

## One

**The three copies are the whole of what a man of about thirty-four with a sheet did that nobody has found out about, and it took about a minute and it was the ordinary way and he is not going to be asked about it.**

On the second of Fallowmonth he wrote his receipt and then he wrote it again, and then he wrote it a third time, because a receipt that sits in a book in a yard where a building has just been emptied is a receipt about a building and not a receipt about a taking.

**So he went round that yard at about the seventh hour and gave a copy to every person in it who had come in from off the road, and there were three of them, and he did not ask any of the three whether they could read, and not one of the three had come into that yard from off the same road as anybody else.**

A woman of about thirty-seven who keeps a roadhouse eleven miles off on the made road, and who hangs a plate out on a bracket over her door when there is a hot dinner and takes it in at dusk, got one. **She has had it twenty-three days and it is in the till box under a pound of silver and she has shown it to four people this month and not one of them can read it and neither can I.** That is twenty words and it is the whole of what she said about it, and she said it to a man with a stick who had a plate put in front of him first, and she would have said it to anybody.

## Two

**The second of the three is a man of about thirty-three with a barrow, and he was in that yard on the second of Fallowmonth with a load of glass on the barrow, and he is the only person in that yard who had come in off a road he was not going back down.**

He put the copy in the inside pocket of a coat that is not his, on top of a fold of paper that is also in the inside pocket of a coat that is not his, and the fold had been there since a fixed Saturday in Rainmonth, which was eighty-one days ago, and he had not opened it in eighty-one days.

**He opened the other one. That is the whole of what he did and it is the only time in about three months that he has looked at anything anybody handed him.**

He did it in a yard at about the eighth hour on the twenty-fifth because a yard is where a man with a barrow stands. **The copy is about eleven inches by four and it has a date on it at the top and a figure on the second line and a figure of men on the third and a trade on the fourth, and a place on the first, and a name at the foot of it, and he can read every word of it, and he read it in about the time it takes to read anything.**

**Forty-one sheets. The two gates. Lime carting. Eleven men. And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet.**

And then the finding, and it is not a finding about the four men, and it took about four seconds.

**It says what was taken and where it was taken from and on which days and by whom, and it does not say one word about why, and it does not say who sent them, and it does not say what a sheet is, and it is the most complete piece of paper about anything that has ever been handed to him and there is not one thing on it he can do anything with.**

He put it back in the pocket on top of the other one. **He did not open the other one, and it is a sheet of about nine figures with no head on it and a date at the foot of it, and not one person in four counties can make anything of it, and neither can he, and he had made a rule about that in a yard about three months ago that not looking is the same as not saying, and on the twenty-fifth of Fallowmonth he looked at one of the two and not at the other and the rule held.**

## Three

**The third of the three is a man of about nineteen at a bar four miles west of a market town of about two thousand people on the salt, and he got the copy out of the same yard and out on the same road and was given it on the made road four miles west by a man who had been told by a man in a yard that there was nobody on that stretch of road he could give it to, and there was not.**

**He put it in the inside of his coat on the second of Fallowmonth and it is in the inside of his coat now, and he cannot read, and he has never asked anybody to read anything for him, and nobody has ever asked him anything since a man with a barrow asked him one question in a month in Harvestmonth and believed him.**

And the bar is the only one of the three in this that anybody in nine counties can put a figure on, and the figure is not his to say.

**The bar was put up on the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth of the year before, and it is nine feet of oak, and it is up four days in seven on a Monday, a Wednesday, a Friday and a Sunday, and this is a Wednesday, and he is paid nine pence for three of those four and the one he is not paid for is the Sunday.**

**It is three hundred and one days. It is forty-three weeks exactly. Forty-three weeks of four up-days in seven is a hundred and seventy-two, and the Sundays in forty-three weeks are forty-three, and a hundred and seventy-two less forty-three is a hundred and twenty-nine.**

And it cross-checks, and the cross-check is on the page because a man who cannot read is the only person in that town who has ever worked this out.

**Two hundred and fifty-nine days was on the thirteenth of Harvestmonth and that was thirty-seven weeks. This is six weeks after that and six weeks is forty-two days, and two hundred and fifty-nine and forty-two is three hundred and one, and three hundred and one is forty-three weeks, and a hundred and eleven and a hundred and eighteen is a hundred and twenty-nine.**

**There are about a hundred and twenty-nine twists of paper in the left pocket of a coat that is not his, and every one of them is nine pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence, and a hundred and twenty-nine of them come to twenty-four marks and nine pence, and not one of those two figures is written down anywhere in four counties, and the man who could put them together is a man who cannot read, and there is a receipt in his coat that he cannot read either, and about a hundred and thirty-one days ago a sheet with about nine figures on it and no head on it went under a stone on a bench in a shed nine feet by seven and it is still under that stone.**

## Four

**And the distribution at last was done by people who were not sent, and that is the whole of the finding of the twenty-fifth, and it took no longer than the eighth hour.**

Three copies went out of a yard into four counties in about a minute, into the inside of three coats and a till box. **A woman who cannot read showed it to four people who cannot read. A man who can read looked at every word of it and could do nothing with it and put it back. A man who cannot read has had it in a coat for twenty-three days and has not asked anybody about it and is not going to.**

**And not one of the three was sent anything, and not one of the three was asked to carry anything, and not one of the three has anybody's name, and the man who wrote the three copies does not know that there are three.**

**A sheet of about forty-one sheets of paper about a road went out of a shed in a satchel, and about forty-one sheets came out of it into eleven places in about a month, in a country on the salt and a fen country and a kilns country and a country that has a made road in it and nothing else in common with any of the other three, and eleven people counted it in eleven different ways and eleven people have not met and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a shop into nine counties with a head on them, and about forty-one of them went out of a shed with no head on anything at all and were more use than any of the other nine hundred.**

## Five

The cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is three places and a bar in it and the bar's money is not in it.

**a broom, a fire, a loaf on the road, a wheel iron, a quart of small beer, a bed, a handcart hired for the day, a second horse's feed, a horse's feed, a loaf, a room, a quart of oil, a tallow candle, a quill and ink, a sheet of paper: five, three, one, eight, two, two, four, nine, nine, one, six, four, two, seven, one is sixty-four pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and sixty-four pence is a mark and sixteen pence over, and a wheel iron and a broom and a quart of oil are the price of a bar that has been worked about a hundred and seventy-two times.

**And about a hundred and twenty-nine twists of nine pence in the left pocket of a coat that is not his is twenty-four marks and nine pence, and that is printed outside this column and added to nothing, and the two are not in one column.**

**And three copies of a piece of paper with a place and a figure of men and a trade on it are in four counties, in a till box under a pound of silver and in two inside pockets, and a woman who cannot read and a man who cannot read and a man who can read and does not know what any of it is for, and about nine counties have a head on a column and about forty-one sheets went out of a shed with no head on anything, and the three of them are all still where the man of about thirty-four with a sheet put them and he does not know they are there.**
