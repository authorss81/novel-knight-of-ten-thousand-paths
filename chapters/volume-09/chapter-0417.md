# Chapter 0417

**The ninth day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Thursday and a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the twelfth hour, in a fold of ground about thirty miles south of a fen town, where there is a byre and a stream and a boundary of two miles with nine stones in it, and about two hundred people live in the fold and a market comes into it on Thursdays, and about two hundred of the two hundred are in it today, and a man of about forty-one walked that boundary alone on the first of Longlight nine days ago with a boy of about nine in the fold with him, and about two hundred people in that fold know the boy did not walk it and not one of the two hundred knows why.**

## One

He came into the fold on a Thursday because a Thursday is the day about two hundred people are in one place in a fold of two hundred, and a thing said to a man of about forty-one on a Thursday is said in front of nine people, and he had a week to decide whether that was a reason to come or a reason not to and he came.

**The man of about forty-one was mending a hurdle at the second rail of the byre fence and the boy of about nine was taking a stone out of a wet hole with both hands and putting it back at a lean, and neither of them had been told anybody was coming and the man of about forty-one was not surprised and did not put the hurdle down.**

They had talked about it at the third hour of a Friday in Greenmonth, on a lane, in about nine minutes, and the man of about forty-one had said that he had had nine years to say a thing to that boy and had got to the end of the nine years about twice, and about two hundred people in that fold do not know that either of those sentences has been said, and nothing was ended on a lane nine days ago and nothing was ended in the nine minutes before that and nothing is going to be ended in a field in Rainmonth.

## Two

He said it in the fourth hour, in the middle of a job, about four hundred yards down the boundary from the near stone, with the stream about nine feet wide on the right and a boy of nine about thirty feet off with a stone in his hands, and it took about nine seconds and it is nine words and it is the whole of what he had to say.

“**You do not have to. I have, thirty times.**”

**And he did not say it twice and he did not say it again for about two hours, and the boy of nine put the stone back in the hole and leaned it and wiped his hands on his legs and came about eleven feet nearer and stood there, and neither of them said anything for about two hours, and about two hundred people in that fold had their market in the middle of it and about nine of them came past a hedge and saw two men about eleven feet apart not talking, and about four of the nine thought it was nothing.**

Then the boy asked one question, and it was the only one he asked that day, and it is the question a fold of two hundred people has never had to ask.

“**Then who walks it.**”

**And the man of about forty-one gave him the price of the thing at the sixth hour, standing at the second stone, and he gave it without softening it and without adding to it, and the price is that there is nobody.**

**A boundary is not looked at. It is checked, on foot, over years, and what is being checked is whether it is where the last four generations of men put it or where anybody says it is, and a man checks that by the ground and not by the sky, and the boy of nine could do it before he could count to nine.** About four hundred yards along there is a place where four generations have all walked a foot to the left of where a man with a chain would put it, and the man of about forty-one said so at about the fourth hundred yards without being asked, and pointed at it from about nine feet off, and would not go and put his own foot in it. The nine stones are four old ones, two of them older than anybody in that fold, and three of them cut by his grandfather and set leaning, and the other two are the two that the stream takes under it for about four months a year, and there is no list of which month, and there is no second person in that fold who knows the answer to that.

**He has been wrong four times in thirty years and knows where all four are and can put his hand on all four, and there is nobody in that fold who could do that, and there is nobody in that fold who knows that there are four. In three of the four a stone stood out of place the following year and a man of about fifty named somebody had been near it and said nothing about it, and in the fourth a girl of about eleven told her mother, and her mother came to his gate about a week later and told him, and he was thirty-one and did not say thank you and has not said thank you to her since.**

**And that is the whole of the stock of knowledge in that fold about where its own boundary is, and it is in the head of a man of about forty-one, and it is not on anything, and it is not in either kind of minute a clerk of about sixty keeps in a town two hundred miles off, and it goes when he goes.**

**And the boy of nine asked a second question at about the seventh hour, at the near stone, and it was not a question anybody had expected and it was about his mother. He asked whether his mother knew, and the man of about forty-one said no, and the boy asked what she would say if she knew, and the man of about forty-one said that his mother would say nothing to him and a great deal to his father's people, and that his grandfather's people would have said he was a soft head, and that he has made his peace with being said that, and that the boy is nine and does not yet know that a thing said once in a fold of two hundred people is said in about nine houses by the following week and that about four of the nine will have heard the wrong version, and that the wrong version is already the one his mother's sister has.**

## Three

Then he gave the boy the second thing, and he gave it in nine words as well, and about two hundred people in that fold did not hear either sentence and about one man in a fold of two hundred people is the whole of what a Thursday in Rainmonth came to.

“**Say you have not been asked. That is all.**”

**And the boy of nine said that nobody was going to ask him anything because he was nine, and his father said that the asking was the whole of it and the not being asked was the other half, and that there are about four people in that fold who will say to a boy of nine, before he is twenty, that they have not been asked to do it and that it can wait, and the boy said he would remember that and did not say he would do it.**

**And a man of about forty-one did not tell his mother, who is sixty-three and lives four hundred yards off the byre and has been told nothing, and that is a decision he made in about four seconds on a Thursday in Rainmonth in a fold of about two hundred people and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man tells his mother that he has stopped being the second of nine stones.**

## Four

The cost of the year is a column and it is the boundary's and it is got up in a fold that has never had a column in it and will not have one, and the man of about forty-one does it out of his head once a year and has never once written it down.

**A farrier at one shilling and four pence to shoe a horse that takes the boundary on the near side is sixteen pence. A mason of about thirty-eight at four shillings to re-cut the figure on the third stone, which is not a year and is a mark his grandfather cut, is forty-eight pence. A sack of quicklime at two pence. A man of about twenty at four pence for a day carrying the sand up the wet place. Sixteen and forty-eight and two and four is seventy pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence and that is one mark and twenty-two pence, and about two hundred people in that fold have never seen that figure and about four of them could tell you every item in it.**

**And the boots are not in it. A pair of a boy's boots, second hand, at two shillings and two pence, which is twenty-six pence, and the man of about forty-one paid for them on the Thursday at the market cross and gave the boy the money for the second pair as well because the first pair was four inches too long and had been for a year, and the boy put the new boots on in the byre at about the seventh hour and did not put the old ones anywhere, and about two hundred people in that fold saw a boy of nine in new boots on a Thursday in Rainmonth and about nine hundred people in nine counties do not know it and there is no form on which a man enters a pair of boots.**

## Five

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp stood in a fold of ground thirty miles south for about nine hours and did not ask a single question about the boundary, and the reason he did not ask is that the man of about forty-one had already asked the only question anybody can ask about a boundary and had answered it out loud in a lane in Greenmonth, and a man who has answered a thing that plainly is not asked it again by a stranger in a field, and the price of that restraint is that he will go away from this fold with nothing new except nine seconds of it in a lane that he was not in.**

**And a boy of nine in new boots walked about forty yards of that boundary on his own that afternoon with nobody, and got as far as the second stone and turned back, and the man of about forty-one did not follow him and did not stop him and was mending a hurdle about nine hundred yards off and did not look up twice in about forty minutes, and about two hundred people in that fold will know by the end of the year that the boy of nine did not walk the boundary, and not one of the two hundred knows why, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person puts down that a man walked two miles alone with his own son in the fold with him and told the boy in nine words that he did not have to, and that the boy asked the only question, and that the answer was nobody.**
