# Chapter 0449

**The eighteenth day of Wolfmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the ninth hour, in a meeting room over a store in a township of about nine hundred people, where about two hundred people come into that township off six villages on a Thursday and on a Saturday and not one of them came on that Sunday, and the room is eleven paces by nineteen and is taken by the day, and there are nine people in it, and there is no tenth, and there has not been a tenth since the thirtieth of a month in Embermonth.**

## One

**Nine people are in a room and they have nothing to do, and that is the whole of the eighteenth of Wolfmonth, and it is the first time in about a month that any of the nine has said a sentence in that room that was not about a thing somebody had asked for.**

There is nobody's name against the chair this month. There is no name in that hat for any of the nine. There is no book, because the book is about a hundred and forty years old and is on a shelf behind the door and its keeper is a woman of about sixty-three who is four miles off and who has not been in this room since the day before a man of about thirty-four with a net put a whole sentence into it in a month in Longlight, and nobody has told her what has happened in here in about a month and nobody is going to.

**And the nine have not agreed anything since the eighteenth of a month in Embermonth, which was a Friday, and the reason they came on that Sunday is that they said on that Friday that they would come, and nobody wrote it down, and a thing said in a room of nine is kept by the eight who heard it and by nobody else.**

A man of about thirty-four with a net came in at about the second hour and put a pail in the passage and did not say why, and a woman of about forty-four came in at about the third hour with lime on her hands and sat down and said that she had a draw on the Thursday and was going home for it.

## Two

**And the room did what the room does, because a room that stops doing what it does is a room with a purpose in it, and this one has not had a purpose in it since about nine years before a woman of about sixty-three decided against the bolt.**

The list on the shelf has been looked at. There is a water trough out at a place about half a mile off and about nine men are going to it on the Monday, and the second is nets at a stream, and the third is a door frame at a farm, and there are four names in the hat and each of the four has the thing for a month, and one of the four is a woman of about thirty-eight who has put a name in twice and has never stood in the chair, and the room knows that and has not done anything about it and is not going to.

**The man of about thirty-four with a net is one of the nine and he keeps that list, and he put it on the bench and read out the four and then took it off the bench and put it back, and the nine of them did not discuss it, and he did not ask them to, and a thing a room does not discuss is a thing the room is doing and not a thing nine people have taken over.**

**The chair has been empty three months in every twelve for about a hundred and forty years and there are three months in this coming year that nobody in that township can tell you which they will be, and the hat is on the shelf behind the door and it has four names in it and none of the nine put one of them there.**

## Three

**And a receipt came into that room on the twenty-eighth of Embermonth without anybody carrying it, and it is a record with a place on it and a figure of men on it and a trade on it in that order and it is in a fair hand and it is accurate, and it cannot be given back, and the nine of them had it in front of them in a sentence, and they did not have the paper.**

The man of about thirty-four with a net said it at about the fifth hour, and he said it as a fact about the room and not as a thing that wanted doing, and it took about nine seconds.

“**A receipt is a record. A record is a thing with a place on it and a number of men on it and a trade on it, in that order, and that is the shape nine counties have spent a year arguing against. There is one of them under a door in a town of about nine hundred people. It is true. It cannot be given back. It says at the foot of it who took it.**”

**Nobody agreed with him. Nobody disagreed with him either. A man of about forty-one with a boundary said that a thing you cannot give back is not a thing anybody can be asked to give, and a woman of about fifty-eight with a slate said that a figure with no head on it cannot be refused and cannot be handed back for the same reason, and the man of about twenty-two with a handcart said that a receipt is a thing you carry to a door and a thing you do not carry to a door is not a receipt, it is a piece of paper with a hand on it, and the mason of about sixty-eight said nothing, and the woman of about seventeen said nothing, and the man of about nineteen said nothing.**

**And the woman of about fifty who keeps a roadhouse nine miles on from the kilns was in that room, and the four feet of board with about three hundred and seventy figures on it and no head on it is in her kitchen, and the same hand is on the bottom of that column and at the foot of that receipt, and she is the only person alive who could say which of the three hundred and seventy are his, and she has not said it and is not going to, and nobody in that room asked her and nobody in that room thanked her and nobody has ever thanked her.**

## Four

**And here is what nine people can do, and it is worth having exactly why, and it is a short list and the room did it in about four minutes.**

They can meet. They can say a thing out loud and eight other people will know it and none of the eight will have it written down. They can refuse a thing together and a refusal that nine people make at the same time in a room is a harder thing to undo than a refusal one person makes in a yard, and that is the whole of their strength and it took about four minutes to find out and about a month to be sure of.

**They cannot hold anything. A thing nine people hold is a thing nine people can be asked about, and a thing nine people can be asked about is a thing with a handle on it, and the ninth of the nine is a name that has a handle on it now, in four counties, in the mouths of children, and about forty people heard it in a market in a month in Greenmonth and about nine of them heard it properly, and nothing about that can be put back and nothing about it was anybody's fault.**

**They cannot keep a book. There is a day-book two hundred miles off with nine entries in it of a thing nobody asked, and it has not been opened on any day anybody keeps, and a tenth is not a gift. There is a register with seven entries in it and every one of the seven is a question and not one is a finding. There is a page under a fourth step of an outside stair with nine things on it and a bench that sits on Saturdays, and nobody has put a tenth asking of that bench in nineteen years and nobody is going to.**

**And they cannot add anything to anything. About nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane with a head on them, and about a hundred and four of them go out on a fixed Saturday in a band of a hundred and eight with four taken out of it, and those are two counts of two different things and the nine of them in that room heard both of those figures about a month apart and neither of them is a figure of persons and neither of them is the same as either of the other, and the day-book's nine and the page's nine and a schedule of nine things in a yard behind a house of assizes are three different nines and none of them is a fourth.**

**And so the eighteen of Wolfmonth was a Sunday that the county does not trade, in a room of eleven paces by nineteen, with nine people in it, and the ninth of the nine is not in it and will say it again if he is asked, and a chair stood empty on a platform at the top end and a hat stood on a shelf behind the door with four names in it and the ninth of the nine was not one of the four.**

## Five

The cost of that Sunday is a column and it is not his and it belongs to the man of about thirty-four with a net, and it went in a box on the shelf and not in the book, and the woman of about sixty-three who keeps that book is four miles off and did not see it happen.

**a third bed at two pence, a pound of cheese at two, a second tallow candle at two, a fire at three, a pin at three, a third fire at three, a cart's fare at four, a room at six, a second room at six, a second quill and ink at seven, a wheel iron at eight, a second wheel iron at eight, a third horse's feed at nine, a second horse's feed at nine and a farrier at sixteen.** Two, two, two, three, three, three, four, six, six, seven, eight, eight, nine, nine and sixteen is eighty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-eight pence is a mark and forty over**, and what that room is taken for went into a box on the shelf and not into the book, and the trough has been in that book since her husband's time and the room has never been in it once, and a woman's four feet of board with about three hundred and seventy figures on it and no head on it is not in it and is not going to be in anything anybody keeps.

**And nine people were in a room and they had nothing to do, and nine of them talked about a receipt and not one of them agreed about it, and a woman of about fifty sat in that room with the only hand in nine counties in her head and nobody knew she had it except the person who already knew, and about nine hundred people in that township did not know that nine people had met seven times in a room over a store, and there is no form in this kingdom on which that would go.**
