# Chapter 0416

**The seventh day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Tuesday and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the seventh hour and the tenth hour, at a single arch over a beck nine miles off the kilns on a road that is not on any sheet, and at a drain arch in a wall at a house in the next village about four miles off that arch, where a mason of about fifty-one and a boy of about fourteen have been at it for two days and a mason of about sixty-eight who has not worked in about eleven years came to look at it and was not asked to do any of it.**

## One

The first of the eight is a builder's sentence and it is four words long.

Ask the far side.

**And it is a builder's practice and it is about four hundred years old and it is sung in a yard of four lime kilns two days west and it is nowhere else in nine counties, and there is not one piece of paper in this kingdom with it in it except a man's mouth, and what it means is that before you touch the far side of a job you find out who is over there, and the reason it is a practice and not an instruction is that the finding out takes a day and a half and there is no form for it and nobody can be paid for it and there is nothing to write at the end of it.**

He had it in his coat in a hand nobody in that room recognised, nine words, no letter and no seal, and a place in the county on the back. **Whether the first of the eight is still being done anywhere.** It came in on the same cart as eight sheets of paper, and a woman of twenty-nine read nine words in about the time it takes to say nine words, and then she sat down on the edge of a bench-board with the paper in her hand for about a minute and a half, and nothing was written on anything, and the paper went into a drawer with about four hundred others.

**And she has not answered it. That is a decision and it is two hundred miles off and it was made in a room with a door on a lane and it has not changed in about thirty days, and nobody in nine counties is going to answer it for her, and the man of about thirty-two with a limp was not going to be the one and he knew that before he started.**

**The arch is nine miles off the kilns and it is one span with no parapet on the ring and about nine feet of dressed top course on each approach and nothing on the arch itself, and the beck is about eleven inches over the sill and the ring is about nine feet above the water, and the mason of about sixty-eight got himself out from under it in about eleven seconds, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp took rather longer than that, and neither of them was offered help and neither of them asked for it, and that is the second time in about two months and it is not going to stop and it is not a thing either of them would call a thing that has happened to him.**

## Two

The mason of about sixty-eight was at the arch at about the fourth hour because he goes there, and he goes there in a wet year and a dry one and he has not been under it since the year it went up and he is sixty-eight and there is not a second time.

A drain arch in a wall at a house in the next village is a different job from the arch over the beck, and it is a small one, and it is the only kind of work in this district there is at the moment, and a mason of about sixty-eight who is the nearest man alive who can lay a ring came to stand about nine feet off it for about an hour and a half.

**And the reason he came is worth having, because it is the reason nothing in the four days changes. A ring over a beck is his trade and a drain in a garden wall is not, and the man four miles off does drain arches, and the top course of that arch over the beck is out of level and needs two men and four days and a new stone and a barrow, and the man who should do it is about forty miles off and will do it for four shillings and sixpence a day, and the man who can do it for four shillings and sixpence a day and is standing nine feet away is the one nobody sent for, and has not been sent for, and would come if he were asked and has not been asked and did not ask.**

**He was not asked to do any of it. The mason of about fifty-one and the boy of about fourteen knew who he was by about the fourth minute, and neither of them said so, and they carried on, and the boy looked at him once about the eighth minute and then did not look again.**

He asked the question at about the seventh hour, in the plainest words he had, standing on a cart of sand with his stick against his knee.

“**Does anybody still ask the far side.**”

## Three

And the mason of about sixty-eight did not know that it was the first of eight, and did not know there were eight, and did not know it was in a song in a yard of four kilns two days west, and took it as a question about a job, which is what it sounds like when a man says it standing next to a wall with a hole in it.

“**I do. I have done it four hundred times.**”

**And that is nine words and it is the answer to a question that came back from a road about two days off, and the man who said it is sixty-eight years old and is out of work and has been for about eleven years and did not know he was answering anything, and the apprentice laid the next stone and did not look up.**

He said the rest of it to the mortar, not to anybody, and it took about four minutes.

**“A drain is a job with a far side and the far side is a man in a house with a garden in it, and I go to the door and I ask him whether he knows what I am doing and whether he minds, and he says yes or no, and I do it either way, and I have done that four hundred times and about four hundred men have said yes.**”

**“And the arch is nine miles off and the far side of that is a field and a beck and about nine hundred yards of bank that a man of about forty-four has farmed since before I was thirty, and I have said the words to about nine men in forty years and not one of them has come back to tell me anything, and I have not stopped asking, and the reason I have not stopped asking is that I do not know what a wall is until I have asked the man on the other side of it whether he minds.**”

Nobody improved on that and nobody agreed with it and the mason of about fifty-one said, at about the eighth hour, that he had never heard of it and never had, and that his father's people did a thing with a word and that he could not remember what the word was, and the boy of about fourteen said that he had never heard of it either, and there was nothing else said about it for about two hours.

**And a man of about sixty-eight has said out loud, for the first time in about forty years, that he asks the far side, to a stranger, beside a wall he was not asked to look at, and the reason he said it is that a stranger asked him a question about a job.**

## Four

He did not write it down. He has a book and it has eleven lines on a page in it with no words in them, and he wrote nothing in it about a mason of about sixty-eight and a drain arch in a wall, and the nine words of the letter are not in his coat, because about thirty days ago a woman of twenty-nine put them in a drawer in a room with a door on a lane along with about four hundred other returns, and that is what a room over a weigh-house does with a thing that has nine words on it and no letter and no seal and a place in a county on the back, and he has them in his head and cannot be asked out of it and would not want to be.

**And the answer is in this kingdom and is four hundred years old and is spoken by one man about four times a year to about four men, and it is nine miles from a room that has had a copy of the question in a drawer for about thirty days, and nobody in nine counties is going to carry it from the field to the drawer, and that is not a failure of a plan, because there is no plan, and there is a man of thirty-two with a limp who has heard the answer and knows the question and does not know what to do with either, and he is not going to say one word about having not known what to do with either, and about four hundred people in nine counties did not hear either thing and are not going to.**

## Five

The cost of the two days is a column and there are two things in it that are a wage and three that are not, and one of the two men on that job is not in it and does not know that he is not in it.

**A mason of about fifty-one at four shillings and sixpence a day for two days is fifty-four pence twice, which is a hundred and eight pence. Nine sacks of quicklime at two pence is eighteen pence. Three loads of sand at one penny is three pence. A barrow of stone at six pence. A hundred and eight and eighteen and three and six is a hundred and thirty-five pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence and that is two marks and thirty-nine pence, and the boy of about fourteen is in the hundred and eight and not on top of it, and that is the arrangement and has been the arrangement since either of them could remember.**

**And a mason of about sixty-eight stood about nine feet off that wall for about an hour and a half and was not asked to do any of it and did not do any of it and did not say so, and about four shillings and sixpence a day is the rate he said in a field at that arch on the fifth of a month in Greenmonth, and he is a man who is about forty miles off it, and the whole of what he has out of the year is about four hundred times a question asked at a door about a garden, and he is owed nothing by anybody, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man is owed a day for turning up.**
