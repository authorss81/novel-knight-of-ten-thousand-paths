# Chapter 0406

**The seventeenth day of Greenmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the second hour and the third hour and the fifth hour, thirty miles south of a fen town, in a fold of ground with a byre in it and a stream about nine feet wide and a boundary about two miles long, where about two hundred people live and a man of about forty has walked that boundary on the first of Longlight every year since he was eleven years old, and a boy of about nine is in the fold with him, and a boundary of two miles with nine stones in it is walked by one man and is not walked by the boy.**

## One

**The practice is the third of the eight and it is a short one and it is the shortest and it is the one that is broken least and most completely at the same address.**

Never a person twice.

**What it means, and it means this in about nine counties and it means it in about four hundred places where nobody has heard of the eight: the person who keeps a thing and the person who uses a thing are not to be the same person in the same season, because a person who writes a figure and then spends against it will write a figure that spends.** That is the whole of the practice. It is about four hundred years old, it is about a forge, and it is the reason that in about nine counties no man is both the clerk of a store and the man who draws from it.

**In this fold it has meant, for about a hundred years, that one man walks the boundary and nobody walks it with him.**

## Two

His father died in a winter. **He was eleven. He was the eldest of three and the other two were nine and six, and the boundary is two miles and it is walked on the first of Longlight and a boy of eleven can walk two miles, and he did, and he did it again the next year, and the year after, and he has done it about thirty times.**

“**Nobody asked me,**” he said, and he said it in the first four minutes, standing in a fold with a byre in it, to a man of about thirty-two with a limp he had met in a lane four miles back.

“**I know that.**”

“**You do not know it,**” he said. “**You have been told it. I did not hear it until I was standing in a yard at eleven with about four other people in it and a woman of about sixty-nine said, you are your father, and that was the whole of the asking and I have had about thirty years to think about whether that was an asking.**”

“**Was it.**”

“**It was him asking me,**” the man of about forty said, “**and she did not wait for an answer, and I have never known since whether I answered.**”

## Three

**He is good at it, and that has to be said plainly and early, because the whole of the difficulty of the next three hours is that it works.**

**The boundary is about two miles and it is marked by nine stones of which four are old and two are about a hundred and forty years old and three are his grandfather's, and the stream crosses it once at a place that is under water about four months in the year, and he goes at the point where the water is not, and the year the water is somewhere else he goes at the water, and there is no list of which, and he has been wrong about four times in about thirty years and each of the four times somebody caught it before the season turned.**

He said that himself, in the third hour, without being asked and without any sign that it was a boast.

“**Four times in thirty years,**” he said, “**and I know where all four are and I can put my hand on all four, and there is nobody in this fold who could do that, and there is nobody in this fold who knows there are four.**”

“**Then how do you know they caught it.**”

“**Because in three of the four the year after there is a stone that is not where it was,**” he said, “**and a man of about fifty named somebody put it back and never said so, and the fourth time it was a girl of about eleven who told her mother, and her mother came and told me, and I was thirty-one.**”

**They walked about nine hundred yards of the two miles, which is a quarter of it and no more, and it took about a quarter of an hour at a pace that was not a walk and was not a stop and had no name for itself.**

**A boundary walked is a thing you do with a man of about nine on your left and a stream on your right, and every nine or ten yards one of you turns round and looks back, and you do not look at the man, you look past him at about four feet off the ground. The thing being checked is whether a boundary is where the last four generations of men have walked it and not where anybody says it is, and a man looks at the ground and not at the sky for that, and the boy of about nine could do it before he could count to nine, and about four hundred yards along there is a place where the last four generations of men have all walked a foot to the left and the man of about forty said so, and pointed at it, and would not go and look at it.**

“**That is the one nobody argues about,**” he said, “**and the two that are in the water are the ones that get argued about, and it has been the other way round for four generations because a stone under water is a stone you have to go and look at, and a stone on dry ground is a stone you can see from a lane.**”

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp walked nine hundred yards of a boundary at a pace set by a man of about forty-one and a boy of about nine, and his right leg went at about the six hundredth yard and he said nothing about it and neither of them mentioned it, and about two hundred people in that fold have no idea that any of it happened today, and about nine of them are in the byre and about four of them live in the house the boundary goes past.**

## Four

**The boy of about nine was in the fold for the whole of it and was not asked anything by anybody, and he was not shy about it, and he was doing a job, which was walking a boundary stone out of a wet hole with both hands and putting it back at a lean.**

Aren asked him what he would do when he was his father's age.

**The boy of about nine said that his father was forty-one, not forty, and he was corrected and did not argue, and that is the only thing anybody in that fold disagreed about in about an hour.**

Then the boy said the thing that made the hour, and he said it about thirty feet off with both hands in a hole in the ground and he did not look up.

“**Are you going to tell him to do it,**” the boy said, meaning his father, and he meant the walk.

“**I have not asked him anything.**”

“**He is not going to want you to,**” the boy said.

**And a man of about forty with a boundary of two miles and nine stones and four errors in thirty years stood about thirty feet off with a sheet of paper in his hand, and the whole of the last thirty years of that fold came out into the air in about nine seconds by a boy of about nine who had not been asked either.**

## Five

**The man of about forty has a boy, and the boy is nine, and the practice says that when the man is forty-one the boy is the man, and that is what his grandfather's grandfather did, and the man of about forty has thought about it every year for about nine years and has said nothing to anybody about it, including his own mother, who is sixty-three and has been told nothing.**

“**I have had nine years,**” he said, at about the fifth hour, and it is the only time in about three hours that he spoke about himself in the first person, “**and I have got to the end of them about twice, and both times I decided I would tell him when he was old enough to be told, and he is nine, and I have decided twice that nine is the age, and I have decided it twice this year.**”

“**Tell him what.**”

“**That he does not have to do it,**” the man of about forty said, “**and that nobody in this fold has ever not done it, including me, and that I would be the first and that his grandfather's people would have said I was a soft head for saying it, and I have made my peace with being said that because there is going to be one soft head or there is going to be nobody in four generations and I would rather there was a soft head.**”

**And then the man of about forty refused the sheet, and the reason he gave is the best reason anybody gave in about nine days, and it took him about twenty seconds to say and it is worth having exactly.**

“**Never a person twice,**” he said. “**I know what it means. It means I am not to be the one who keeps a thing and the one who uses it. And I have just told you I have kept this one for thirty years and I use it four times a year, and I have been the second of those for thirty years and nobody here ever noticed, and that is the safest I have ever been in my life.**”

“**The sheet is not a rule.**”

“**It is in a song,**” he said, “**and I have heard the song, my father's people sang it, and the second of the eight is two books and the third is me, and if I take a sheet off a man with a stick and put it on a bench in this fold then I am a person twice, and I will be a person twice in a song and in a room, and there is no way to be the man who walks it and the man who has it, and that is the whole of what my father taught me in about nine words before he died.**”

## Six

**And the sheet stayed in Aren Kest's coat, and about nine hundred yards of a boundary in a fold of ground thirty miles south was walked on the first of Longlight by one man and not by the boy, and about two hundred people in that fold know that the boy did not walk it and not one of the two hundred knows why, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man can write down why he walked a boundary on his own when his own son was in the fold with him.**

**And the byre took a calf on the Thursday, and about two hundred people in that fold did the ordinary week of a fold of ground with a byre in it, and one man of about forty-one in it had refused a sheet of paper four days before and defended a practice that has been broken every generation for a hundred years without anybody noticing, and there is nobody in that fold who will ever hear either of those things.**

**And the practice goes on being broken, and the man who is breaking it has told nobody, and about a year from now there will be a boy of about ten in that fold who has been told by his father that he does not have to do it, and that is a fact about about one man in about two hundred people, and it is the whole of what happened in that county in the second week of Greenmonth.**
