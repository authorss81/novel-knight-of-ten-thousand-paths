# Chapter 0407

**The twenty-second day of Greenmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a market town of about two thousand people on the salt, where the market runs on two days in seven and this is one of them, and about nine hundred people are in a street about two hundred yards long with a rope at one end and a bar at the other that goes up on five days in seven because the market trades on two, and about four hundred people of the about nine hundred are there about a cart, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow is one of the four hundred.**

## One

**The market runs on two days in seven and the rope at the one end of that street is what marks the two, and the bar at the other end goes up on five because the market trades on two, and about two hundred yards of street between them is where about nine hundred people stand on a Saturday and about four hundred of them stand about a cart.**

The cart was a barrow-load of dressed stone off a man of about thirty-four who had come nine miles with it, and it was in the middle of the flat side with nine carts going round it, and the man of about twenty-six had been standing about nine feet off it for about eleven minutes.

**The argument was about a load of stone and it had been going for about eleven minutes and it was the fourth such argument that day and it was not going to be settled by anybody with a cart, because a cart is worth arguing with.**

A man of about twenty-six had brought nine hundredweight of dressed stone off a barrow-load and the stone had come up the made road and across the flat and the man of about twenty-six had found his own stone in a stack on the flat side of the market bar with another man's name chalked on the corner of it. **He had not taken it. He had stood about nine feet off it for about eleven minutes with a man of about forty-three who had come for his own load, and the two of them had got as far as saying the words *that is my corner* four times, and the four times were the same four words.**

**And the man of about thirty-three with a barrow put his barrow down in the middle of the flat side of that street, in about nine feet of ground with nine carts in it going round him, and he sang the nine lines.**

## Two

**He sang it well. That is the fact about it and it is the whole of the trouble. A man who carries glass and lime for a living has a voice that about four hundred people in a market street have heard at seven o'clock for four years, and he sang nine lines in about eleven seconds with a barrow wheel against his boot, and the bar went down between the third and the fourth, and nobody sang the ninth line with him because nobody there knew there was a ninth line.**

**About forty people heard it. About nine of them were inside about nine feet and heard the words. Of the other about thirty-one, about half heard a man making a noise on a beat, and the rest heard nothing at all, and the nine are the ones this block is about.**

**He got the ninth line right, and he had never in his life heard a ninth line, and that is the second thing that is wrong with this and the first thing that is right.**

**The first eight he has had for about four years, from a yard of four lime kilns two days west, where four people draw a kiln in nine rounds and a boy of about seventeen wheels barrows between the second and the third of them and comes in off a made road with glass in a hand-basket twice a week, and where a woman of about forty-four has sung the first eight at a draw since before he could lift a barrow.**

**The ninth is his own, and he has never once thought about where he got it, and it is the name of a road in this county that his own father used to send him along in a cart, and there is one road in this county with that name and there is a sign on it that has been painted out, and a man does not have to be told a thing about his own road to be able to say it.**

**And that is the whole of it. A woman of about forty-four has held the ninth word for nineteen years because saying it in a yard of four kilns puts nine people in a position where they can be asked, and a man of about thirty-three got the same nine words in the same four years by never once being in that yard, and the difference between them is that one of them was in the room and the other one was on the road, and neither of them chose anything.**

**And he said the name of a road in a market street where a cart of stone was standing with a corner chalked on it, and about forty people heard it, and a road's name that is said where a load is being settled is not a road's name any more.**

## Three

The man of about twenty-six looked up. The man of about forty-three did not.

“**Who said that.**”

“**He did,**” said the man of about thirty-three with a barrow, not turning round, and he was already picking his barrow up again.

“**Who is he.**”

“**He is a man of about thirty-two with a limp and he is not from this market and he is not anybody in this street,**” the man of about thirty-three said, “**and I have said nine lines because I have nine lines and I have said them about nine times in about four years and I did not think about it.**”

**And that is the whole account of it, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow did not know even then what the ninth line was worth, and he has never known and is not going to find out, and about four hundred people in that market have not seen him since and one of them was asked about it in a street in another market about nine weeks later and could not remember where he had heard it, and that is another thing that happened and not because anybody did it.**

Aren Kest was about forty yards off, at the cross end, with a stick he could not lean on, and he had heard the whole of it.

**He had written four lines in the back of a book in a room over a cooper's seven days before, and one of the four was a rule, and the rule was that the ninth copy was not to be put in a room and was not to be carried by anybody who would be asked about carrying it.**

**And the man of about thirty-three with a barrow had been asked about carrying it, in a room, by a woman, on a Friday, and had been told not to say what it was. The rule was broken before it was written and he broke it again on a Saturday in a market street, and the reason is that a rule about who is asked about a thing does not reach a man who is asked once and obeyed, and the whole of what a rule of that kind can do is catch somebody who is nobody.**

**And that is the finding, and it took him about a second and a half on a bad leg in a market street, and it is the first thing in about nine days that has gone into him rather than out of him.**

He got to the flat side of the street in about four minutes on a leg that takes longer going than it does coming, and the man of about twenty-six and the man of about forty-three had both stopped talking, which is what happens to two men when a third thing arrives.

“**Say the twenty words,**” Aren said.

The man of about thirty-three with a barrow said them back to him flatly, as though reading a rate off a board.

“**A road’s name only stays a road’s name as long as nobody says it where a load is being settled,**” he said. “**And that is not a thing I made up, it is the fifth of the eight and it is the one I have never once been able to do.**”

## Four

**Nobody put it back. That is the finding and it took about nine minutes to establish and no one wanted to establish it.**

**About four people in that street had heard all nine lines. About nine more had heard the last one only. The man of about twenty-six had a corner with a chalk name on it and about nine feet of ground and a man of about forty-three and no sheet anywhere in this kingdom, and the four of them, by the end of the seventh hour, had a name of a road.**

**A mason of about sixty-eight had said that name in a field beside a beck to about nine men in forty years, and every one of the nine had been a stranger, and a stranger hears a thing once and forgets it in a week. A man of about thirty-three with a barrow said it in a market street on a Saturday to about forty people, and thirty-one of them were within sixty feet of a nine-hundredweight load of dressed stone with a corner chalked on it, and about nine of them were inside nine feet.**

**Nine is the number that matters and it is the number of people in this kingdom who have the ninth word correctly, and it is now somewhere between nine and fifty, and the figure is not a figure anybody can hold, because nine of the fifty are in three counties and the other forty are in one street and will have been in nine more streets by the first of Longlight.**

A man of about forty-three said, to nobody in particular and in the tone of a man settling something with himself before he goes home, that there was no such road. **And a man of about twenty-six said there was, and that his grandfather had a field by it, and that it went nine miles, and that it was nothing now, and that it had been a harbour once.**

**And in about two hundred yards of a market street on a Saturday, in about four minutes, a name that had been a road became a road that a grandfather had a field by, and that is what happened to it, and it happened in the ordinary way, and nobody in that street raised their voice at anybody.**

**And by the seventh hour there were about nine people in that county who had a thing about it that they did not have at the second.** A woman of about nineteen said it out loud to a man of about thirty who was buying nothing, and the man of about thirty did not write it down and wrote it on the back of his own hand, and about four hours later a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar saw it and asked whose hand it was, and the woman of about nineteen said she did not know and did not want to and was sorry.

**And the man of about thirty put a bar of soap over it in the evening and did not put anything over it in the morning, and the whole of the difference between a name in a song and a name in a county is about nine hours and a bar of soap.**

## Five

**The man of about thirty-three with a barrow was seven days late for the sheet on the bench-board and he came for it on the fifteenth of the month and he took it and he did not open it, and he has not opened it and will not, and it is in a drawer in a house in that market town and it is a list of about nine places with about nine figures in front of them and no head on it and a date at the bottom, and the man who wrote the figures is two hundred miles off and does not know they exist.**

**And the ninth copy of the eighth word went to a bar four miles west of that market on a Friday, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow put a sheet on the end of it and could not read a word of it and told a man of about nineteen that he had been told not to say what it was, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said a name in a market on a Saturday and told a man with a stick that he had said nine lines about nine times in four years and had not thought about it.**

**Nothing was taken. Nothing was seized and nothing was lied to and nobody was compelled and no hand was raised and no door was shut, and a name that had been a road for about four hundred years was said out loud in a market by a man carrying glass, and about nine people heard it properly, and the whole of the price is going to arrive later and is not going to be anybody's fault and is not going to be stoppable by anybody who was in that street.**

**And the man of about thirty-two with a limp stood at the cross end of about two hundred yards of market street with a stick he could not lean on and worked out, in about the time it takes to cross that street badly, that the plan had been right about everything except the one thing it was about, and that the plan had put eight words into eight places and had not once put a person in charge of saying them out loud, and that a person who says them out loud in a market is not a keeper of anything and cannot be written down as one, and that this was the best possible outcome available and that he was the only person in that street who knew it was the best possible outcome, and nobody in that street had asked him and nobody was going to.**
