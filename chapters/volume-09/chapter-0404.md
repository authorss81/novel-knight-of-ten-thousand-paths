# Chapter 0404

**The tenth day of Greenmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour, in a room over a cooper's shop on a street of working yards in a fen town of about four thousand people, where nine men keep a yard four hundred yards up the same street and a man of thirty-two with a limp has a room over the cooper and has had one for about three years, and a keeper of records came down on the cart on the fixed day and is in this room and is on the cart back on the fifth morning, and about two hundred miles off there is a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane and a press standing open in it.**

## One

The sheet was still on the bench-board.

**It had been on the bench-board for nine days and the room had been let four times in those nine days and the cooper's wife had seen it every time and had not moved it and had not swept it, because a sheet of paper with a bench-board under it is furniture and furniture is not swept round, and she said so when she was asked, and she was right.**

**Eight words in chalk and pen, no head on them, and a ninth line ruled in and nothing on it.** The chalk had gone at the top three because the top three had been nearest the edge and the pen had gone at the bottom because the bottom had been dusted. **There were about two feet of daylight between the words and no two of the eight were alike, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow had said he would be back in a fortnight and it is not a fortnight yet.**

The keeper of records was called Mara Vey and she is twenty-nine and four years and a half and she came down on the cart on the fixed day with about four hundred sheets in a banded bundle and she has been in this room for three of the five days she is here.

**She is the only person in nine counties whose whole occupation is the second copy of something, and she has spent about four years and a half making them, and she is the person in this room who knows more about what a copy is than anybody in it by about nineteen years, and she is the reason the argument in that room happened.**

**She came down on the cart with about a hundred and four sheets in a band and the band is broken at one corner and the corner is tied with a cord, and she has carried a load like that up and down about nine times in about four years and has never once had it weighed, because a bundle is a bundle and a banded bundle that weighs is a bundle somebody has counted.**

**The yard four hundred yards up that street is not mentioned in that room at all, and the man of twenty-five who owns it is not mentioned in that room at all, and the reason both of those are true is that there is a rule in that yard which says that nothing that belongs to a man goes in the yard's book and only a thing set, and a rule that says a thing is either set or it is not a thing has no room in a conversation about a sheet of paper, and that is not a fault in the yard.**

## Two

The plan was made at about the third hour and it took eleven minutes and it is a good plan and it is not the plan anybody wanted.

**Eight words into eight places.** Not a copy into eight rooms. A copy into eight places, and the eight places to be chosen for what they are and not for what is convenient, and the sheet itself to go to the eighth and not to be a copy of the sheet, and a person in each place to have the words and to do whatever it is they already do with them.

“**Eight places,**” Mara Vey said. “**And you have four.**”

“**I have two and a half,**” Aren said. “**The kilns, the arch, and a fold of ground about thirty miles south where a man of about forty has held the third one since he was eleven, and a woman of about fifty with no business in the world at a roadhouse nine miles on from the kilns who has been feeding strangers for about thirty years and has a figure for it that she will not give anybody.**”

“**That is four, and two of them are people who will say no to a sheet of paper, and the third is a man who cannot read.**”

“**I know.**

“**Then the eight are not chosen yet,**” she said, “**and that is the correct number of places to have, and I want to say the rest of it and then I want to be wrong in a room and not in a column.**”

## Three

**She was wrong about one thing only, and she put it wrong for about nine minutes, and the nine minutes is the whole of what she wanted that day, and it is said once.**

“**You have not said where the ninth one goes,**” she said, “**and you are going to say that you will hold it, and I am going to tell you why you cannot, and you are going to tell me that you have thought about it for nine days.**”

“**I have thought about it for nine days.**”

“**Then say it,**” she said, and he did, and it is worth having as it was given.

“**I will hold it, and there is nobody else who can be asked for it, and the reason is that about four hundred people went without a road for four months because a man of about fifty-eight kept a store for nineteen years and a clerk wrote his trade in a column, and there is not a single one of those four hundred who could be asked now if I was doing it instead, and I would be doing it with a better hand, and I would be in nine counties instead of four.**”

He said it standing up, in ninety-four words, and he did not say it as a man making a case for himself.

“**You are the person least able to hold it,**” Mara Vey said, “**and I am the person everybody in nine counties would look at second, and I have a door on a lane and a number and a deed of thirteen words and a press that has stood open for longer than anybody in that building will put a date to, and if the ninth one is in that room it is not a ninth one. It is a first one with a witness.**”

There is a silence of about four seconds in that room and it is the only silence in about three hours.

“**That is the argument of the last four months turned round,**” Aren said, “**and I have been on the other side of it and I did not think of it.**”

“**I have been on this side of it for four years and a half,**” she said, “**and there is nothing in this room that is going to change my mind about it and I would like that said out loud, because I have said things in rooms that were soft and I have had them quoted at me for about a year and a half.**”

## Four

**And then she refused, and she refused the second of the eight, and nobody in the room had asked her to hold anything.**

“**Two books,**” she said. “**That is the second one, and you were going to bring it to me and you have not said so and I have known for about two days that you were going to, because it is the only one of the eight that a person like me could keep.**”

“**It is the only one of the eight anybody could keep twice.**”

“**It is the only one of the eight anybody could find,**” she said, “**and the whole of what that line means is that a second copy is kept by somebody the first copy's keeper does not know, and I have a list. I have had a list since the first year. It is not a list of people, it is a list of places, and there are nine places on it, and I know which nine rooms, and I could produce it inside a quarter of an hour, and a person who could produce it inside a quarter of an hour is a person holding eight copies in one hand.**”

“**You would not be the first keeper.**”

“**I would be the first person anybody asks,**” she said, “**and I have spent four years and a half learning to be a person nobody has to ask, and it is the only piece of good work I have ever done, and I am not giving it up for a rhyme.**”

## Five

At about the ninth hour, on the third of the five days, they came back to it from the other side, and the other side was a thing neither of them had thought of, and it came out of a list of nine things that the keeper of records keeps in her head and not on anything.

“**Who is hardest to send a form to,**” she said.

“**A man on no roll.**”

“**A man on no roll, in no yard's book, on nothing that goes out on a cart, in no place anybody has a reason to be,**” she said, “**and who is therefore the only person in four counties a form cannot reach, and a man who has nothing is not a keeper of anything and cannot be written down as one, and the four counties cannot find him and I could not find him and I have a list of nine rooms and he is not in any of them.**”

**And a man of about nineteen has been standing at one end of a bar of nine feet of oak at the head of a made road four miles west of Marl since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and he is not on a roll in four counties and is not in a yard's book and is not on the Crown's own series, and about two hundred people in a town of about two thousand people within four miles of him know that, and about eight of them have worked out that his four months came out on the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth, and about four of the eight are wrong by a day, and not one of the eight has ever said one word to him about it.**

**And there is no form in this kingdom that can be sent to a bar, and there is no column a bar can be entered in, and the fourth column of the Crown's own series is headed Keeper and takes a person by that person's trade, and a man who stands at a bar is a trade, and the trade is written down and the man is not, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow has been the one that came and stopped coming.**

Aren wrote four lines in the back of a book of his own that had eleven lines on a page in it with no words in them, and he wrote them in about four minutes, and one of them was a place and one of them was a day and one of them was a man who could not be asked about and one of them was a rule.

**The rule was that the ninth copy was not to be put in a room and was not to be carried by anybody who would be asked about carrying it, and the rule was hers, and he wrote it down because she had said it out loud and he wanted the words he had written to be hers, and she looked at the four lines for about nine seconds and said that one of them was wrong and would not say which and went out to the yard.**

The cart back is on the fifth morning and it is a fortnight and it is a fixed day and the keeper of records goes up on it with about a hundred and four sheets in a band and about nine hundred in total going the other way in nine counties, and the band is broken at one corner and the corner is tied with a cord and not with the band.
