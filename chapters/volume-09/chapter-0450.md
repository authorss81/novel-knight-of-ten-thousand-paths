# Chapter 0450

**The twenty-fifth day of Wolfmonth, YR 318, a Sunday, and a day the county does not trade on at all, and the third hour and the seventh hour and the tenth hour, in the upper room over a store in that township of about nine hundred people, where a market comes in off six villages on two days in seven and where a woman of about sixty-three keeps a book and is four miles off, and nine people are in the upper room, and it is given up at dusk, and there is nothing on any road in nine counties.**

## One

**Nine people were in a room on that Sunday and four of them had come nine miles and one of them had come thirty miles and one of them had come in a cart hired for the day, and the man of about thirty-four with a net had come half a mile and had a pail in the passage again.**

**They had nothing to do for the first hour and none of the nine pretended otherwise, and a woman of about forty-four at a kiln said that she had come because she had said she would and not because there was anything, and a man of about forty-one with a boundary said that a thing you have said you will do is a thing you do, and that the two of those are the same thing and that people who say they are different are the ones who are lying.**

**And three of the nine had carried a number into that room without being asked to and not one of the three wanted to.**

The man of about nineteen at a bar had been at one end of nine feet of oak since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth of the year before, which is fifty-one weeks and four days and four days short of a full year, and fifty-one weeks of four up-days in seven is two hundred and four, and the Sundays in fifty-one weeks are fifty-one, and two hundred and four less fifty-one is a hundred and fifty-three, and the four days over are a Wednesday, a Thursday, a Friday and a Saturday, and a Wednesday and a Friday are up and a Thursday and a Saturday are down, and one hundred and fifty-three and two is a hundred and fifty-five, and he is paid nine pence for three of the four and a hundred and fifty-five nines is one thousand three hundred and ninety-five pence, and a mark is forty-eight, and that is twenty-nine marks and three pence, and it is in nobody's column and it is not in anybody's.

The sheet under a stone on a bench in a shed nine feet by seven was a hundred and ninety-one days old that morning, and the paper in the inside pocket of the man of about thirty-three with a barrow was a hundred and forty-one days old and has never been opened, and the receipt in the inside of the boy of nineteen's own coat was eighty-three days old and he cannot read it.

**And not one of the three said a word about any of it and the other six did not ask, and that is what eight days in a room have come to and it is not on a sheet.**

Then the man of about thirty-four with a net put the pail in the right place and the nine of them did the thing they had come to do, and it took about forty minutes, and it is not a thing anybody would put in a book.

## Two

**The thing they decided was decided at about the sixth hour and it is the whole of what that Sunday was for, and it is a decision and not a road, and nobody in that room was happy with it, and the woman of about fifty-eight with a slate was against it and said so, and the other eight did not go against her for the sake of not wanting it, and she knew the difference.**

The decision is this. **That there is no ninth meeting, and no tenth, and that if two of them ever stand in a room together again it will be because two of them turned up for something else, and that nobody is to arrange it and nobody is to hint at it and nobody is to take a room in advance.**

The man of about twenty-two with a handcart said the reason and it was the reason he had given on the eighteenth of Embermonth and nobody in that room had improved on it since and nobody was going to.

"**Nine of us have met,**" he said. "**A man with a form can find that in about four years, and if he cannot find the room he can find the nine, and we have each got about nine days of the year in which somebody who has a form would be stood in front of us about a street we walk on. And there is not one thing in nine counties that we cannot do without meeting again.**"

Nobody improved on it. A woman of about fifty-eight with a slate said that she had come eight times for nothing and that she was the one it cost most, and that she was not going to be thanked and did not want to be, and that a figure of men in a yard in a town of about nine hundred people is going to stop being chalked on a slate in about nine years when there is nobody left to chalk it, and there is nothing anybody can do about that on a Sunday in Wolfmonth.

The man of about forty-four at a kiln said that she was right and that it was the first time in about a month that anybody had said anything in that room that was true and cost her something, and then said that she would not have said it if she had not been asked, and the woman of about fifty-eight said that was the correct arrangement and that was the whole of what a room is for.

**And the eight practices stay where they were, and the two that have no keeper do not get one, and the four ways of holding a thing are not improved on and not refused and not put in order, and the sixth of the eight is still not in a place that anybody can be sent to, and nothing about any of the nine went on any sheet on that Sunday or on any other day, and there is no head on any of it and no crown on any of it and no office and no register and no hat with a name in it for any of the nine.**

## Three

**And a woman of about seventeen asked the man of about twenty-two with a handcart for the ninth thing at about the seventh hour, in front of eight people, and he said it, and that is the last time it is said in that room and he will say it again if he is asked anywhere else.**

"**What is the ninth.**"

He said it once and did not wait to be asked for it again, which is a different thing from the nineteenth of Embermonth, and both of them knew it was.

"**Coldharbour.**"

**And it is the name of a road nine miles long that is on no map because a road is not a place and has nobody in it, and about forty people heard it sung in a market in a month in Greenmonth and about nine of them heard it properly, and it has been said out loud in four counties since, and a man of about thirty has it on the back of his own hand under a bar of soap and nothing over it, and none of that can be put back and none of it was anybody's fault and nobody in that room said one word about it.**

"**I am not keeping it,**" he said. "**I said it three times on one morning in Embermonth because I was asked three times, and this is the first time I have said it since, and I have not written it down and I have not got anywhere to write it, and if you ask me on a road in ten years I will say it again.**"

**Two people were asked on that Sunday and one of them said the word and one of them did not.**

The man of about nineteen at a bar was asked second and he said no.

"**I have been asked one question in four months and believed, and being asked in a room in front of eight people is a different kind of being asked.**"

**That is twenty-nine words and it is the same answer he gave on the nineteenth of Embermonth and he is nineteen, and the man of about twenty-two with a handcart looked at the floor while it was said and did not take it as a thing about himself, because it was not.** The mason of about sixty-eight was in that room and was not asked and did not volunteer, and the woman of about thirty-seven with a plate on a bracket over her door in a roadhouse eleven miles on was not in that room and has not been told and there is no form on which she could be, and the person who did not say the word on that Sunday has not said it to anybody since and is not going to.

## Four

**And here is what is in that room on that Sunday, and none of it was improved on, and none of it was taken, and none of it was given away, and none of it has a crown on it.**

A chair on a platform at the top end, empty, and about a hundred and forty years old, and three months in every twelve with nobody in it and nobody in that township able to say which three.

A hat on a shelf behind the door with four names in it for a month in Hearthmonth, and one of the four is a woman of about thirty-eight who has never stood in that chair, and none of the nine put one of those four names there, and not one of the nine has ever put a name in that hat, and eight days in that room is the whole of what any of them has done in it.

A door with a bolt on the inside of it that has not been drawn since a woman of about sixty-three decided against it in about nine seconds, in a room that is the only room in nine counties that says a thing out loud on purpose.

A boundary of two miles and nine stones in a fold thirty miles south, walked by one man who is forty-one and whose father walked it, and a boy of nine in new boots who does not have to.

A day-book two hundred miles off with nine entries in it of a thing nobody asked, shut, unopened on every day anybody keeps, and a tenth is not a gift, and a woman of twenty-nine wrote four lines on a sheet with no name at the foot of it a fortnight ago and put it in a bag with the letters in it and has not told anybody and is not going to.

**And four feet of board with about three hundred and seventy figures on it and no head on it, in a kitchen nine miles on from a yard of four lime kilns, in the wall of a woman of about fifty who cannot read, and nobody thanked her.**

## Five

The cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-two with a handcart's, and he is paid by the hour and he was hired for that day and he carried nine sacks of quicklime to a farm about four miles off and came back with the barrow empty, and the room is given up at dusk and the keeper goes home and the column goes in a box on the shelf and not in the book.

**a handcart hired for the day at four pence, a second handcart hired for the day at four, a quart of small beer at two, a second quart at two, a tallow candle at two, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, a bushel of oats at four, a tin of nails at five, a broom at five, a wheel iron at eight, a ream of paper at eight, a cart's fare at four, a second cart's fare at four, a farrier at sixteen, a room at six and a second room at six.** Four, four, two, two, two, one, one, one, three, three, four, five, five, eight, eight, four, four, sixteen, six and six is eighty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-nine pence is a mark and forty-one over**, and what that room is taken for went into a box on the shelf and not into the book, and nine sacks of quicklime at two pence a sack is not in it and is carried at somebody else's cost, and nine sacks of quicklime is a day of work and is not a figure of persons.

**And nine people were in a room on a Sunday that the county does not trade and they decided that they would not do it again, and the ninth word was said once more in a mouth that is not keeping it, and a mason of about sixty-eight was in that room and did not say it, and a chair stood empty on a platform at the top end and a hat stood on a shelf behind the door, and a woman of about fifty went nine miles home to four feet of board with no head on it, and about nine miles west of that township a man of about nineteen had his hand on a lever at about the seventh hour and then went and had his supper, and the lever and the fulcrum were cold, and the twenty-sixth of Wolfmonth is a Monday, and at the second hour of that morning he will put his shoulder under nine feet of oak and take it down in about four seconds and at the sixth hour he will bring it up again, and he has been at one end of it on four days in seven since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth of the year before, and there is not one crown on anything in that township or on that road.**
