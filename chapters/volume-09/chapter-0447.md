# Chapter 0447

**The eleventh day of Wolfmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the tenth hour, in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane in a fen county about two hundred miles off a street of working yards, where a press has stood open since a day that nobody in that building will put a date to and nobody shuts, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room into nine counties with a head on them, and about a hundred and four go out in one band on a fixed Saturday, and the two are counts of two different things and are not added, and Mara Vey is in that room and a man of about thirty-two with a limp is not and has not been in about four months.**

## One

**The drawer is the second one down on the left and it is about eleven inches deep and it holds about four hundred returns and nothing else, and every one of the about four hundred is a thing a person sent in from a road and about nine in ten of them were sent in because somebody told them to send it in.**

They have been going in since the seventh of a month in Longlight, and a cart goes out on the fixed day and is back a fortnight later, and the drawer is emptied into a bag with the letters in it and the bag goes on the cart, and the letters go by the same cart and the letters are answered and the returns are not.

**She is the keeper of records. She is twenty-nine and she has had the room four years and a half and the room is hers out of a deed of thirteen words, and it is a body corporate with a door, and a number, and the capacity to sue and to be sued, and fourpence a year, and the thirteen words are good and she has read them about four hundred times and they have never once told her what to do with a drawer.**

A press stands open in the corner and it has stood open since a day she will not put a date to, and the reason is that nobody who has been in that room in four years and a half has wanted to shut it, and the reason nobody has wanted to shut it is that a press in that room has never in her time held anything anybody could lose.

## Two

**One of the about four hundred has nine words on it and it is the only one of the about four hundred she has ever read twice.**

"**Is the first of the eight still done anywhere.**"

That is nine words and it has no letter with it and no seal on it and a place in that county written on the back of it in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and it came off a road about two days off a coast, and it was put in that drawer on the fourteenth of a month in Longlight, which was a Sunday, and the cart went out on the Saturday and the Sunday's went in the bag on the Monday and that is the ordinary way.

**Twenty-three weeks exactly, and no day over, and no half-day, and she has it by the Saturday count on the wall that a man who keeps a weigh-house has had there since before she came, and she checked it twice on the Saturday and once on the Sunday and it has not moved.**

**And here is the whole of what a keeper of records can do with nine words in a drawer, and it is worth having exactly why, and the reason is that she does not know what the first of the eight is.**

She has four years and a half of returns in that room. She has a day-book on the shelf with nine entries in it of a thing nobody asked, and it has not been opened on any day anybody keeps, and a tenth is not a gift. She has a register with seven entries in it and every one of the seven is a question and not one of the seven is a finding, and it was read aloud once in that room in a month in Longlight two years back and has not been read aloud since.

**She does not know what the first of the eight is. She knows there are eight of something because about nine hundred people a month send her things and about one in a hundred of those things is a sheet with eight words on it and no head, and she has four years and a half of that in a press that stands open, and she could not tell you what the first of the eight is and she has never once asked anybody.**

## Three

**And the answer is nine miles from that door and she does not know it is nine miles away, and that is the whole of the twenty-three weeks.**

The answer is a mason of about sixty-eight who stands under an arch over a beck about nine miles off a yard of four lime kilns and asks the far side of it, out loud, in the tone of a man asking a wall, to somebody who has nothing to do with him. He has asked it about four hundred times. He asked it the last time of all on the seventh of a month in Rainmonth, out loud, to a stranger who had asked him a question about a job, and the stranger did not carry the answer anywhere and told nobody and is not going to.

**Nobody carried it nine miles to a drawer. Nobody has ever carried it. A mason who asks the far side of a wall four hundred times is a man with a habit, and a habit does not travel, and the one time in about four years that a stranger wrote his habit down and sent it to a room in another county, the room it went to could not read the question, and that is the correct way for that to have gone and it is a bad way.**

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp stood in that same field on the sixth of a month in Harvestmonth, which was about three months before this Sunday, and carried nothing back, and wrote nothing in a book of eleven lines with no words on any of them, and is two hundred miles off and does not know that a mason nine miles from a door has been asked the only question in that drawer's four hundred that could have been answered inside a day.**

## Four

**And this is the decision, and it was made at about the sixth hour of a Sunday, and it is hers, and nobody else in that building knows about it, and she has not sent anybody to find out and is not going to.**

She wrote four lines on a sheet of paper.

**And what the four lines say is what that room holds. About nine hundred sheets a month into nine counties. About a hundred and four in one band on a fixed Saturday, and the two are not added. A press standing open. A drawer with about four hundred returns in it, of which about nine in ten were sent in by somebody who was told to send them in. A day-book with nine entries in it of a thing nobody asked. A register with seven questions and no findings.**

She wrote them in about nine minutes and she read them twice and she did not put a name at the foot.

**A record with a place on it and a number of men on it and a trade on it is the shape of every one of those, and that is the shape nine counties have been arguing about for about a year, and she has been the woman who sends about nine hundred of those a month for four years and a half, and she has never had to say what any of them were for, and she has never been asked by anybody, and this is the first return that has ever gone out of that room that is not a return.**

And she did not put the nine words into the round, and the nine words are still in the second drawer down on the left under about three hundred and ninety-nine other pieces of paper, and the four lines went into the bag with the letters on the morning of the fixed Saturday, and the cart went out, and the bag is on the cart, and the man who asked the question is about two days off a coast.

**And what he will get is a description of a room he has never been in, written by a woman who cannot answer his question and is not pretending to, and it has no name at the foot of it, and a sheet with no name on it cannot be got at and cannot be enforced and cannot be corrected, and the woman who wrote it is the keeper of records and that is the one thing she has ever made that she cannot be got at about, and she did it on a Sunday in about nine minutes and it took her four years and a half to be able to do.**

She refused the second of the eight in that room, in a short answer, to a man with a stick, and the second of the eight is a rule that there are two of any thing, and a second copy is a copy in a room she has to answer for, and she is the person in nine counties for whom that is the whole of her job.

**And she is not going to tell him about the four lines, and he is not going to hear about them, and that is the second thing in about four months that has happened to him that he was not told about and had no part in, and neither of them has said one word to the other and there is nobody to say it to.**

## Five

The cost of that Sunday is a column and it is not his and it is hers and it is the first one she has kept in about four years and a half and she has kept it on the back of a sheet that came in a bag from a county on the salt.

**Two pounds of wood at three pence, two fires at three, five loaves at a penny, two pounds of cheese at two, three quarts of small beer at two, three tallow candles at two, four sheets of paper at a penny, two quills and ink at seven, a broom at five, a ream of paper at eight, a wheel iron at eight, a bucket of tar at eight, three carts' fares at four, and a tin of nails at five.** Three, three, three, three, one, one, one, one, one, two, two, two, two, two, two, two, two, one, one, one, one, seven, seven, five, eight, eight, eight, four, four, four and five is ninety-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and ninety-seven pence is two marks and a penny over**, and about nine hundred sheets a month is not in it and is added to nothing, and about a hundred and four sheets on a fixed Saturday is not in it either, and a drawer with about four hundred returns in it is not a column and is not going to become one.

**And nine words have been in that drawer for twenty-three weeks exactly and no day over, and there are about nine hundred people on a road about two days off a coast who will get a piece of paper with four lines on it and no name at the foot, and about nine miles from that door a man of about sixty-eight put his hand on the ring of an arch over a beck and asked the far side of it in nine words to nobody, and that is the first of the eight, and she has been the keeper of records for four years and a half and she does not know what the first of the eight is, and she has written four lines about a room instead.**
