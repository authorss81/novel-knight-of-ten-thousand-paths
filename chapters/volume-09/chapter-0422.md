# Chapter 0422

**The twenty-second day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the eighth hour and the twelfth hour, on a lane and in a field about two hundred yards off a made road nine miles from a fen town of about four thousand people, in a county of about two thousand people on the salt, where the market sits on a Thursday and a Saturday and the county does not trade on any other day of the week, and the same made road goes four miles the other way to a kerb where a bar of nine feet of oak comes up four days in seven and about two hundred carts a week go past that kerb, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room in the first town and none of them has ever been to the second.**

## One

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet left before the light and was on the field at about the fourth hour with his coat done up and a stick he did not lean on, and he had nine miles of made road and then four of lane, and he came the last of the four on foot because the lane takes a cart about two feet below where a cart ought to be.

**The field is about forty acres and it is good grazing and about nine acres of it is wet at the bottom where a creek used to run, and the creek is a ditch now and it is about eleven inches deep and about nine feet wide and it goes down to a hard standing of about nine yards square that a man of about thirty-four was told is where the boats came up.**

The farm gate is on the made road and the lane comes off the made road two hundred yards north of it and crosses the field and goes to a stile and then goes on for another half mile and joins a road that is not on any sheet.

**That is the whole of the ground and there is a place on it. There has been a place on it for about four hundred years and for about forty of those it has been a field, and the name of the place is the name on the slip of paper two inches by one, and the name of the road at the other end of the half mile is the same name, and no man in that field has ever been asked whether the two are the same thing and neither of them would think it was a question worth asking.**

## Two

The man who farms it is about forty-four and he was at the gate at about the fourth hour and he was not surprised and did not put down the bucket he was carrying, and the man of about thirty-four asked him, in the ordinary way, what the place was called.

"**It was a harbour once and it is my field now and both of those are true,**" he said, and it is seventeen words and it is the answer of a man who is not lying and is not being careful either.

"**There is a road of the same name half a mile up that lane.**"

"**There is,**" the man of about forty-four said. "**My grandfather took glass up it. There is a sign on it that somebody painted out and I have never seen the sign and I have never asked what it said, and I would not take it down again if I could see it.**"

**And he said all of that at a gate with a bucket in his hand and he went on with the bucket, and he is a man of about forty-four who farms about forty acres and has a ditch at the bottom of it that used to take a boat, and he has never in his life entered anything anywhere and never will.**

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote the name down on the form at the fourth hour of that day, sitting on the second rail of the gate with the sheet on his knee, and the name he wrote was the name the man of about forty-four said, and the man of about forty-four did not ask what it was going on a form for and the man of about thirty-four did not tell him.

## Three

**And here is what the place is, and it is a finding and not a forgery, and there is no man to catch and nobody is going to be brought to anything.**

The place is a harbour. It was a harbour about forty years ago and before that for about three hundred and sixty, and the boats came up the creek that is a ditch, and the salt went out of it and about two hundred carts a week still go up the made road past the top of that field with salt or stone or glass on them, and the field is called the name because the field is where the harbour was, and that is the ordinary way a place gets a name and there is nothing in it that anybody would have to apologise for.

**What the office did with it was not to make it up. The name was on a slip and the name is the name of a real place with a real history and a man on it who is about forty-four and who answered a question in the ordinary way in seventeen words. That is where the whole of it starts, and it starts with a man being asked one question and answering it, and it starts nowhere else.**

The thing that is new is the second half mile. **Nine of those words have been on a road in this county for about four hundred years and the road is nine miles and it is on no sheet because a road is not a place, and the road is nine miles from this field and about a man of thirty-four's drive away, and a man who farms this field and a man who drives that road have never had a reason to put the two together, and there is nobody in nine counties with a reason.**

## Four

He walked back the four miles of lane because a cart is two feet below where a cart ought to be and a man with a stick is not, and he came in at the made road at about the eighth hour and stood about a quarter of an hour, and then he went on the nine miles, and he got in at about the twelfth hour in a fen town of about four thousand people where about nine hundred sheets a month go out into nine counties.

**The first line on that form was a place, and it was a real place, and it had a man on it, and the man had answered when he was asked, and the office had got a place.**

And a form with a place on it and nothing else on it is not a form that goes out. The clerk of about nineteen said so at about the ninth hour on the Wednesday, in the ordinary way, and the second line wanted a figure and the figure wanted to be a figure of persons, and about four hundred yards of that county has a lane across it with a stile in it, and about nine hundred people in nine counties do not know that a figure of persons is wanted at Coldharbour, and nine of them are going to be asked about it in a lane and not in a room.

**A place is easy. A place is the easiest of the three, and it took a man of about thirty-four one day and a bucket and seventeen words, and the whole of the office's invention is going to be done on the other two lines and neither of them is going to be a lie.**

## Five

The cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is two days of a man who is not paid for days, because a man on a county's strength is paid by the week and a week does not stop.

**A loaf at one penny, a horse's feed at nine pence, a market toll for a barrow at two pence, a bed at two pence, a quart of small beer at two pence, a sheet of paper at one penny, a handcart hired for the last four miles at eleven pence, a fire at three pence, a dinner at one penny, and a second loaf at one penny: one, nine, two, two, two, one, eleven, three, one, one is thirty-three pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and thirty-three pence is about two-thirds of one, and a handcart at eleven pence is the only line in that column that anybody thought about, and he thought about it for about a minute at a gate with a man holding a bucket.

**And a place went on a form in a room over a shop on the twenty-second day of Rainmonth, and the place is a field of about forty acres and about nine acres of wet ground at the bottom of it, and it has a name that has been on that ground and on a road for about four hundred years, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room and the first of the three is a true thing written down by a man who was not asked whether it was true and could not have known.**
