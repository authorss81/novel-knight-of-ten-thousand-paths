# Chapter 0425

**The fourth day of Harvestmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour, in a market town of about two thousand people on the salt and in a fen town of about four thousand people about nine miles off it, nine days after the third of three things was written in an office over a shop and two days after that sheet went out in a band of about a hundred and four sheets tied with a cord, and the three are in use in four counties, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow is in a yard in the first of the two towns at about the seventh hour.**

## One

The first person who said all three out loud in a room was not the man who wrote them, and he was a clerk of about twenty-three in a room over a saddler's about nine miles off, and he said them because a form with three heads has to be read out to somebody before it is copied, and that is what a form is for.

**Coldharbour. Four hundred and seventy. Salt carting.** He read them the way a man reads three things off a counter, and the woman in that room with him wrote them on a sheet of her own with three lines ruled on it and the same three heads cut about forty years ago in a room two counties off.

**And about four people in four counties had said all three out loud in a room before that Monday was out of its first hour, and none of the four had written any of them, and about a hundred and forty people had heard one of them in a yard or a shop or a kitchen, and nobody who said any of the three out loud in a room in the first nine days had any idea that they were saying a name.**

The county that made the form is the fen county, and it is a county of about four thousand people in a town of about four thousand people with a market in it two days a week. **The other three are the county on the salt where a market runs on two days in seven, the county the kilns are in two days west, and a fourth that has a made road in it and nothing else in common with any of the other three, and the four of them are about a day's ride apart and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of one room into all four.**

## Two

**Nobody can say who made it, and that is the finding and it took nine days to establish and nobody wanted to establish it.**

About nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a shop into nine counties and a hundred and four of them go on a fixed day, and one sheet in that hundred and four had three things on it that a man of about thirty-four had found in three counties in four days, and the sheet is in four counties now, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet is a man who is on the strength of a county's own road and answers to a man about eleven years older than him, and the man about eleven years older than him does not know it is in the band.

**A thing that a clerk copied is a thing two rooms have. A thing that a clerk copied twice is a thing three rooms have. And nobody in any of the rooms can point at the man who started it, and the man who started it can point at the four days, and the four days are his own and he will say so if anybody asks, and about nine people in four counties are right about where it started and about four hundred and seventy people have it in a mouth and a mouth is not a roll.**

## Three

The man of about thirty-three with a barrow put a load of glass down in a yard in a town of about two thousand people on the salt at about the seventh hour, and a man of about fifty-three who takes loads the other way up that road came through the yard and said, in the ordinary way, that he had been to the field on Tuesday.

“**I have been to the field,**” the man of about fifty-three said. “**They have put a number on it.**”

Then he asked the man of about thirty-three with a barrow whether he knew the place, and he said he did, and he said he came up that road about nine years, and he said the name without being asked for it because it is the road he comes up.

“**Then you will get work,**” the man of about fifty-three said. “**A place with a figure on it gets carts put on it. That is how it is done. I have seen it twice.**”

**And the man who asked was not an officer and was not on the strength of anything and was a man of about fifty-three who takes loads up a made road and has taken loads up that road for about thirty years, and there is no form in this kingdom on which such a man is anybody's business, and he had read the three off a sheet that a clerk of about twenty-three read out in a room over a saddler's about nine miles off, and he was not told where it came from and did not ask.**

The man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that he did not know what a figure on a place got a man, and the man of about fifty-three said that neither did he and that it did not matter, and went out of that yard with an empty cart.

**And that is the moment the three stopped being a form, and it happened in a yard, and it took about four minutes, and it was said by two men who are neither of them a keeper of anything and one of whom has a sheet in the inside pocket of a coat that is not his and has not opened it.**

## Four

A man of about thirty-two with a limp had been in that town on the Saturday before, in the morning the band went out in about a hundred and four sheets tied with a cord, and he was in it again on that Monday, and he was not in that yard on either day and he did not hear one word of it.

On the Saturday he had come off a road at about the second hour and had gone to a room over a door on a lane where a bench sits on Saturdays, and the bench had four lines in its minutes and the four lines were about a watercourse, and he stood in about nine feet of floor in front of a rail and said nothing at all, and a man of about fifty-eight who keeps the minutes looked up once and did not look up again.

**And that is what he came for. There is a thing in four counties now and he is the only person alive who can say where the three of it came from, and there is a bench in nine counties that sits on Saturdays and has been asked nine things in about nineteen years, and the argument for putting it to that bench is that a bench is where a thing gets said out loud, and the whole of the argument is about four minutes long and he made it to himself on a Tuesday, and he put it away on the Saturday and took it out again on the Monday and said it to nobody on either day.**

## Five

**And here is what he decided, on the Monday, in a street four hundred yards long, and it is the decision and it is not a good one, and nobody knows he made it.**

He decided that he would be at the gate of that field on the Wednesday morning and that he would ask the man who farms it what the place was called, in the ordinary way, the way a clerk of about nineteen asks, and that he would not say one word to that man about a form, a figure, a trade or a county, and that he would not take anything back to any room in any county in his own hand or anybody else's.

**He is a man of thirty-two with a limp, and the last time he got up a bank was the seventh of a month in Longlight and there is not going to be another one, and the gate is nine miles of made road and four of lane off a made road, and he is going to walk it on a Tuesday and sleep in a barn and be at that gate at about the fourth hour on the Wednesday, and he has four days between the office's first line on the twenty-first of Rainmonth and the county having all three of them, and the four days are the twenty-first, the twenty-second, the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth, and the three are in four counties and he is going to a gate.**

The cost of that Monday is a column and it is his and it is the cheapest he has kept and it is the last one he keeps for a while.

**A loaf at one penny, a bed at two pence, a horse's feed at nine pence, a second handcart at eleven pence, a room over a shop at six pence, a quart of small beer at two pence, a tallow candle at two pence, a fire at three pence, a sheet of paper at one penny, a second loaf at one penny, and a man of about twenty hired to walk four miles and back at four pence: one, two, nine, eleven, six, two, two, three, one, one, four is forty-two pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and forty-two pence is under one, and a man who has spent the ninth of that Monday walking four miles into a county he does not trade in has still six pence in his hand, and he keeps it, and there is nothing in nine counties he can do with six pence on a Tuesday.
