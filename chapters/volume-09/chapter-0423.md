# Chapter 0423

**The twenty-fourth day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Friday and not a market day, and the fourth hour of the twenty-third and the fourth hour and the tenth hour, at a stile on a lane across a field called Coldharbour about two hundred yards off a made road in a county of about two thousand people on the salt, where nine people counted who went over it from the fourth hour of a Thursday that was a market day to the tenth hour of the Friday, and a day and a half is what a count takes when there is no roll to read, and there is no roll.**

## One

A figure of persons at a place is a thing a county can have, and this county had never had one at that place, and the reason it had never had one is that there is no roll anywhere with anybody in it who lives in that field, and the parish ends about a mile short of it and has ended about a mile short of it for about a hundred and forty years.

**The man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not go back to the office to say so. He went and found out what could be counted, which is the ordinary way, and what could be counted in a day and a half on a lane in a field was the people who went over the stile on it, and so nine people sat down at a stile with pieces of paper on their knees for a day and a half and counted.**

The nine were the man of about thirty-four with a sheet, and the man of about forty-four who farms the field, and two men of his who were about thirty and about nineteen, and a woman of about thirty-one who lives at the second house down the lane, and a man of about fifty-eight who goes to Marl on a Thursday and comes back on the Saturday, and a man of about twenty-two with a handcart, and a boy of about seventeen with a hand-basket of glass, and a girl of about thirteen who had been told to sit there.

**None of the nine is on a roll. Two of them are on nothing at all. The field is about two hundred yards off a made road and the lane goes half a mile past the stile and joins a road that is on no sheet in this county, and every one of the nine lives inside four hundred yards of a place that a county can put a name to and none of them has ever been asked anything by anybody with a form.**

## Two

The count was done with nine pieces of paper and a piece of chalk and about nine hours a day, and it was done in the ordinary way and nobody cheated and nobody counted twice, and the figures were added on the back of a sheet at the fifth hour on the Friday by a man of about thirty-four who can add and has added in his head in a yard and at a bench and on a cart.

**Fifty-eight, forty-four, thirty-nine, seventy-one, thirty-three, sixty-two, forty-seven, sixty, fifty-six.** Fifty-eight and forty-four is a hundred and two, and a hundred and two and thirty-nine is a hundred and forty-one, and a hundred and forty-one and seventy-one is two hundred and twelve, and two hundred and twelve and thirty-three is two hundred and forty-five, and two hundred and forty-five and sixty-two is three hundred and seven, and three hundred and seven and forty-seven is three hundred and fifty-four, and three hundred and fifty-four and sixty is four hundred and fourteen, and four hundred and fourteen and fifty-six is four hundred and seventy.

**And the number is four hundred and seventy, and it is the best count that has ever been made in that lane, and it is honest, and it is the worst number that has ever been put in a form, and neither of those is a matter of anybody lying.**

## Three

**The thing they counted was not what anybody thought it was, and this is the whole of the finding, and it takes about a day and a half to establish and about four seconds to state.**

What went over that stile in a day and a half was four hundred and seventy people. What that figure was about, and is about now, and will be about for as long as that form is in existence, is **how many people went down nine miles of a road and came back, and about nine tenths of the four hundred and seventy were on that road and not on the field, and the field is a mile and a half off the far end of it and a man of about thirty-four could not tell you that on the twenty-first day of Rainmonth and did not ask.**

The man of about forty-four who farms it said, on the Friday morning, without being asked and about as a man says the price of a thing, that there are nine houses in that lane and about four people in a house.

**Nine houses and about four to a house comes to about thirty-four, and there are about thirty-four people in that field and in that lane, and none of them was asked where they were going on the Thursday, and the reason none of them was asked is that they were not asked anything at all, they were asked to sit at a stile and count, and they did it, and the doing of it was the whole of what was asked and it was done well.**

So the form got **four hundred and seventy** in the second line and the second line wants a figure of persons, and a figure of persons is what it got.

**And nobody lied. The nine counted and the count was true. The man of about thirty-four added the nine and the sum was true. The clerk of about nineteen wrote the second line because the second line had a head on it and a figure under it, and the head says a figure of persons and four hundred and seventy is a figure of persons, and a form cannot tell the difference between a figure of persons and a figure of people, and neither can an office, and neither can the nine hundred sheets a month that go out of that room into nine counties.**

## Four

The boy of seventeen wrote his figure in his own hand and the man of about thirty-four took the nine pieces of paper and kept them, all nine, and the boy's was one of them and it was in a hand that is his and that he has been using since he was thirteen.

**He did not know he had done anything. He was not asked to do anything except count, and he counted, and his figure is sixty, and he wrote it in a way that has about four hundred other figures in it in the same shape, in four feet of a hand-basket route and about four hundred entries in a book he made himself and has never shown anybody.**

He said, at about the ninth hour on the Friday, that the count was four hundred and seventy, and he said it to nobody in particular and in the tone of a man saying what day it is, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet added the nine together, wrote the total in the second line, and did not look at the nine pieces of paper again, and the nine went into a file, and a file is where a great deal in this county ends up.

**A hand that has been used for four years, on a lane, in a field, in a day and a half, is the hand of a boy of about seventeen, and there is nothing in this kingdom that can tell a man's hand from anybody else's, and the second line of a form with three heads on it is written in a hand and the hand is a thing that travels, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room and the second line goes out on all of them that want a figure.**

## Five

**The cost of the count is a column and it is the count's and not the form's, and it is got up by the man of about thirty-four out of his own head and has never been written down by anybody at all.**

**A sheet of paper at one penny, a loaf at one penny, a bed at two pence, a barrow hired for the day at four pence, a man of about twenty hired to sit at a stile for a day and a half at four pence, a tallow candle at two pence, a quart of small beer at two pence, a fire at three pence, a horse's feed at nine pence, and a second sheet at one penny: one, one, two, four, four, two, two, three, nine, one is twenty-nine pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and twenty-nine pence is a little over half of one, and that is what the paper and the eating and the sitting came to, and it is the cheapest item in that column and it is the one that did the damage.

**Nine people gave a day and a half apiece to that stile and one of them was a boy who counts and cannot read and one of them was a girl of about thirteen who was told to sit there, and about thirty-four people live in that lane and about nine hundred people go down a road in a day and a half, and the two figures are not close and there is no figure anywhere in nine counties for how far apart they are, and the larger of the two is in a form with three heads on it in a room over a shop in a street four hundred yards long, and it is going to be in about nine hundred sheets a month for as long as the form is in use, and there is nobody in nine counties who can go back to a stile on a lane and ask the nine who counted whether they counted the people who live there or the people who went past.**
