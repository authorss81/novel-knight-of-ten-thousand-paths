# Chapter 0414

**The third day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Friday and not a market day, and the sixth hour and the ninth hour and the thirteenth hour, at a hurdle across a lane nine miles off a made road, in a dale of about nine hundred people where nothing has been asked of anybody in about forty years, and the hurdle has no lock on it and has never had a lock on it and is put across twice a day by a man of about forty-four who farms the near side and who does it before he has had his breakfast.**

## One

It is nine miles from the made road and nine miles back and there is no made road for the last four of them, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp who has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight went the four of those four miles along a lane and round, and took about four hours, and stopped twice, and neither stop was at a gate.

**The dale is about nine hundred people and a stream and about two hundred acres of grazing and a chapel that was rebuilt in the year the old mill stopped, and the people in it have not been asked for anything by anybody in about forty years, and the reason is a sentence of nine words that a man of about forty-four said standing at the hurdle without being asked to say it.**

“**A man with a form stayed here four years.**”

And he said it and then he put the hurdle back the other way and went to his breakfast, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp stood in a lane with nine miles of walking in front of him and nine miles behind him and did not improve on it, and nobody who came down that lane after him improved on it either, and there is nobody in that dale to improve on anything.

## Two

Nobody in that dale was unfriendly, and that is the finding and it took nine miles of walking to establish it, and it is a different thing from the other three places and he had been reading it as the same thing for about forty-one days.

**A woman of about thirty-one came down the lane at about the seventh hour and put a plate in front of him at a door and asked him where he had come from, and he said he had come from a town about nine miles off, and she said there was a market on the Thursday and did not say anything else about it, and she did not ask him what he wanted and she did not ask him who he was, and he ate and she washed the plate and he went out and said thank you and she said nothing at all, which is the correct answer to thank you at a door in a wet lane and is not unfriendliness.**

**A man of about nineteen who was in the lane with a dog looked at the stick, and looked at the road behind him, and worked out in about four seconds that a man who walks nine miles and does not know the way back does not come to do a thing for a plate, and did not say anything, and the dog did not bark, and that is the whole of the cost of a stranger to a place with no gate: one plate, one look, and about four seconds of arithmetic.**

Then a man of about fifty-eight came out of the second house and asked him what he was doing, and it was the plainest question anybody had asked him in about nine days, and he said he was walking, and the man of about fifty-eight said that he was welcome to walk, and that nobody came down that lane and that there was nothing at the top of it, and that the last four people who came down it had had forms and one of them had stayed four years.

**“A man with a form stayed here four years,”** the man of about fifty-eight said, **“and the one before him stayed nine months, and the one before that did not stay, and we have not had a form down that lane since and we are not going to want one.**”

That is not the same nine words and it is forty-four of them and it adds nothing and takes nothing away.

**And a man of about nineteen with a dog was still on the lane at about the ninth hour and looked at him a second time and looked at the road behind him again, and the dog did not bark either time, and about four seconds had become about nine, and he worked out the second nine in about a second and a half, and the arithmetic on that lane is now done and will not be done again by anybody for about nine years, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp could not have stopped it if he had stood still all day.**

## Three

**And here is what he got wrong, and he got it wrong in a lane, on foot, at about the eighth hour of a Friday in Rainmonth, and he did not get it wrong in a room and did not get it wrong in a market and did not get it wrong anywhere else in about forty-one days.**

He had been carrying, since a room over a cooper's shop on the first of a month in Greenmonth, a thing that he called three refusals, and the three refusals were: a woman who would not have a sheet of paper in a kiln yard, a man who would not hold a thing and use it, and a woman who read seven of eight out loud to an empty room. **And he had been carrying them as three doors, and the doors had faces on them and the faces had said no, and he had been waiting for a fourth face.**

**There is no fourth face. A place with no gate does not say no. It has a hurdle and a man of about forty-four who farms the near side, and the hurdle is across twice a day, and there is nobody in nine hundred people who is the one to be asked and there never has been, and not being able to be asked is not a refusal and cannot be put in the same line with a refusal, and the reason it cannot is that a refusal is a thing a person does and this is a thing a place has not got.**

He stood in the lane with that and did not like it and did not argue with it, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp has been wrong about four times in about four years and has been wrong about this one in about four minutes.

## Four

**And the thing that made it a place and not a refusal is that nobody there argued with him, and about four people said something kind to him, and one of them fed him, and about nine hundred people in that dale have no idea anybody came.**

Then a woman of about twenty-nine came out of a cottage about four hundred yards down the lane and walked up behind him and stood there while he worked the stone out of the ground, and when he had got it out she asked him whether he was going to take the long way round, and he said he was, and she said that the long way round is past the chapel and the short way round is the bank, and that a man with a stick who takes the long way round past the chapel is a man who is being careful and a man with a stick who takes the bank is a man who wants to be asked to be helped, and that she had been in that dale nineteen years and had seen about four of each.

**He took the long way round. It is half a mile longer and it took about forty minutes and the light went at about the eighth hour, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight and is not going to, and the half mile past the chapel is the only reason the lane at that place has a stone out of it.**

The cost of the day is a column and it is the smallest one anybody has kept in nine counties, and a rate is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and neither of the two is anything to do with a plate.

**A loaf at one penny at one end of the lane and a quart of small beer at two pence at the other: one, two is three pence.** Nine miles out and nine miles back and four of the eighteen a lane, and one plate he did not pay for, and the whole of what it came to.

**And he did not ask for anything in that dale, and nobody in that dale asked him for anything, and about nine hundred people got their ordinary week done in a valley that does not trade on a Friday and does not send a cart up that lane in Rainmonth, and about two of them know a man of about fifty-eight said a sentence of forty-four words at a gate in the last four hours, and about none of the two knows why, and nothing in this kingdom has a form on which a person enters that they do not want a form, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp has now got four answers, and the fourth is a family and a chair and a hat and nobody, and the one he walked nine miles to and nine miles back to is the nobody, and he had been carrying it in the same line as the other three for about forty-one days, and a place with no gate is not a refusal, and there is nobody in nine hundred people to refuse him and there never has been.**
