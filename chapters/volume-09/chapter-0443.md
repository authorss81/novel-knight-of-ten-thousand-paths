# Chapter 0443

**The nineteenth day of Embermonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the ninth hour, in the same meeting room over a store in a township of about nine hundred people, where about two hundred people have come in off six villages for the market and are in the street below and cannot hear anything that is said in that room, and nine people are in it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is in it and is the tenth.**

## One

**The ninth of the nine is a name and it is the only one of the nine that is a name, and it was said out loud in a market in another county in a month in Greenmonth, about five months back, by people who did not know what they were saying, and it cannot be taken back, and the only thing anybody in nine counties can do with that is go on saying the word.**

**Eight of the nine are things people do and not words, and they are not in any order anybody put them in.** The first and the fourth are both a mason of about sixty-eight at a single arch over a beck nine miles off a yard of four lime kilns, and the first of them is that he has asked the far side of it about four hundred times and the fourth of them is that he will not be told what a dressed stone says. The second is held by nobody at all and was refused by a woman of twenty-nine who has a door on a lane and a deed of thirteen words and fourpence a year. The third is a man who walks two miles and nine stones so that no person is put on a paper twice, and there is a boy of nine in that fold in new boots who does not have to. The fifth is a room over a store in a township of about nine hundred people. The sixth is not in a place anybody could send a man to anywhere and has not been for about four hundred years. The seventh is a yard of four lime kilns with a cold fourth kiln at the gate. And the eighth is a woman who puts a plate in front of a stranger before she asks him a single thing.

**And the ninth is the name of a road, and a road is not a place and has nobody in it and cannot be written on a form at all, and there is no line in this kingdom that takes a road, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet worked that out in about nine seconds in a yard in a month in Rainmonth and would not say it twice and did not say it again.**

The room had been at it since the fourth hour on the Monday and it had got to the ninth and stopped, which is what a room does.

The woman of about thirty-seven who keeps a roadhouse eleven miles on from that yard on the made road and hangs a plate out on a bracket over her door when there is a hot dinner is not in that room and has not been in it. What is in the room is this: that nine people know the ninth word and that the ninth word has nowhere to sit.

## Two

The man of about twenty-two with a handcart was sitting on the bench under the platform with his hand on the frame of the door, the way he sits, and the room asked him.

Nobody asked him a question. A woman of about fifty with a board said his name, and then said the word, and then stopped, and that was the asking.

He said it back.

“**Coldharbour,**” he said.

“**Again.**”

“**Coldharbour.**”

“**Once more.**”

“**Coldharbour.**”

**And that is the whole of what the ninth of the nine came to, and it took about four seconds, and he did not sing it and nobody asked him to and there is no tune in that room and there has never been a tune in that room, and the two parts and the beat of a bar coming down on a rim are about nine miles west of that room and are not in it and were never going to be in a township of about nine hundred people on a Saturday.**

Then he said the thing that made it work, and he said it to the room and not to the man of thirty-four with the net, and nobody had asked him for it.

“**I am not keeping it,**” he said. “**I said it three times because I was asked three times. A man who is keeping a thing gets asked once and then writes it down, and I have not got anywhere to write it and I would not know what to put at the top.**”

**And that is fifty-one words and a person of about seventeen in that room wrote the number of them on the back of her hand and did not tell anybody, and a person of about seventeen can read figures and not words, and she did not tell anybody because there was nobody to tell.**

## Three

And then they asked the mason of about sixty-eight, and he said no, and this is the whole of it.

He had said no on the Monday to a question about the arch. Nobody expected him to say anything else and nobody was waiting for him to.

The man of about thirty-four with a net said the word, once, and then stopped and waited, and he waited longer than he waited for the handcart man, because the man of about thirty-four with a net is a man who has been in a room that has said things out loud for about two months and has got into the way of it.

The mason of about sixty-eight looked at him.

“**No,**” he said.

“**You have not heard what I was going to ask.**”

“**I know what you were going to ask,**” the mason said. “**And I said on Monday that I am not going to be asked in a room, and I have not said anything else since Monday, and a word is not a stone and I do not know why I would say one and not the other.**”

Nobody said the word again in that room for the rest of the day.

**And a man of about thirty-four with a net did not say it twice to get a second person to say it, and that is the only thing in that room that nine counties got right by accident and on purpose at the same time, and he did not know he had done it and nobody has told him.**

## Four

**And here is the whole of the ninth thing, and it is worth having exactly why.**

A name that is written down is a handle. A name that is sung in a market in front of strangers is nine people who heard a road. **A name that is said in a room of nine people who have agreed that nothing about them goes on any sheet is a name that eight people heard and one man is holding for nothing.**

The man of about twenty-two with a handcart is not a keeper of anything. He holds no practice. He is not on a list in the county the bar is in, and he is not on a list in the county the kilns are in, and he is not on the county's own series anywhere, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane with a head on them and he is not one of the nine hundred.

**He is also the only person in that room who is paid to carry things and is not carrying anything, and that is why it went to him and not to anybody else, and nobody in that room worked that out and the man of about thirty-four with a net said afterwards that it had been luck.**

It was not luck. **A man who is hired by the hour will carry a thing to the door of a person who is not in the room, and will not keep it, and will say it again if he is asked, and will not write it down, and that is four things that between them are the only protection anybody in this kingdom has ever found.**

And the mason of about sixty-eight is the ninth thing in a different way. He is the man who could have held any one of the nine, because he has been asked four hundred times and has answered four hundred times and is the only person in that room who has ever been made to answer a question in front of strangers. He is the most qualified person in the room and he is the one room will not use, and that is not a loss and it is not a failure and it is the correct answer.

## Five

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is his and it is a market morning and about two hundred people were in the street below and not one of them knew that anything had been said in that room.

**a loaf at one penny, a sheet of paper at one penny, a stove at two, a lamp at two, a quart of small beer at two, a tallow candle at two, a pound of wood at three, a pin at three, a handcart hired for the day at four, a broom at five, a room at six and a ream of paper at eight.** One, one, two, two, two, two, three, three, four, five, six and eight is thirty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and thirty-nine pence is nine under one**, and a ream of paper at eight pence is the only thing in that column he has bought in about four months that is not bread, a bed, a fire or a light.

**And a word that is the name of a road nine miles long and a field that a man of about thirty-four with a sheet entered on a form in a month in Rainmonth have the same nine letters and are two different things, and about nine counties are using both of them, and no person in that room said one word about that, and it is the ninth thing about the ninth word and it is not the end of it and it is not the beginning of anything, and the sheet of paper in his coat with three lines and no head on it is nine inches by six and he did not take it out.**
