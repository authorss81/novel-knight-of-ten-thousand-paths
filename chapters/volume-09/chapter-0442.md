# Chapter 0442

**The eighteenth day of Embermonth, YR 318, a Friday and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the ninth hour, in the same meeting room over a store in a township of about nine hundred people, where the room is eleven paces by nineteen and is taken by the day, and the woman of about sixty-three who keeps the book takes what it is taken for out of a box on the shelf and has never once put it in the book, and nine people are in it and a man of thirty-two with a limp is in it and is the tenth, and there is a book on a shelf behind the door in which what is agreed in that room has been written for about a hundred and forty years.**

## One

**The woman of about fifty-eight came with a slate.**

It is a slate about the size of a chopping board and there is a figure on it and no head on it, and the figure is the number of men who came into a paved yard behind a house of assizes in a town of about nine hundred people on a morning about three weeks ago, and she has written it in chalk and she wipes it off at noon and writes it again the next morning, and she has been doing that for about nineteen years and she has swept that yard every morning of that nineteen years and she is the only person alive who knows what the yard is for.

"**I am not asking for a room,**" she said. "**I am asking for a number to be somewhere that is not me. I am fifty-eight. I have a sister in a township who is sixty-two and she cannot read and I have nobody after me who would know what eleven in a yard means, and I would like it written down once, anywhere, by anybody, and that is all I have come for.**"

**And that is a want about the world and not about this room, and it is the only want in that room on that day that anybody could have given, and the room did not give it, and it is the only one of the three things in that room on that day that anybody could have given anybody, and the reason the room did not give it is a thing that had not been said out loud in eleven paces by nineteen until the fourth hour.**

## Two

The woman of about fifty kept a roadhouse nine miles on from the kilns and had a board four feet long on the wall of her kitchen with about three hundred and seventy figures on it and no head on it. She had come nine miles for this and she had not eaten.

"**What would the paper be for,**" she said.

She said it to the middle of the room and not to anybody, in the tone of a woman asking a price.

Nobody answered her for a while. The man of about forty-one with the boundary was looking at his own boots. The mason of about sixty-eight put his stick down against the wall and then picked it up again and did not lean on it.

The man of about twenty-two with a handcart answered her, and he answered her from the bench at the end where a man sits who is not going to be asked anything, and it took him a moment to find it and then it came out in one go.

"**It would be for whoever is not in the room,**" he said. "**That is what paper is. I am hired to carry things to people standing further off, and nobody has ever once asked me what was inside them, and that is the arrangement and I would not want it different.**"

**And that is forty-nine words and it is the whole of what he said on the second day and he did not say one word of it on the first.**

## Three

**And then a woman of about seventeen who carries glass said the thing that made the rest of the day, and she said it standing up, and she is seventeen and she had not eaten either and she had come nine miles.**

"**Can I have the book,**" she said.

Nobody in that room understood her for about four seconds.

"**Not to write in it,**" she said. "**To have it. To take it off that shelf and put it in my basket, and bring it back on a day, and have it in my basket for about two days, and then put it back.**"

The woman of about fifty with the board said that the book was a hundred and forty years old and that it was the only thing in that township that nine hundred people all agreed about.

"**I know what it is,**" the woman of about seventeen said. "**I have not come for it for me. If it is on a shelf in this room then this room is a room nine people have been in, and if the room is a room nine people have been in then there is a list somewhere with nine on it, and I have been on the fourth sheet of a band a hundred and eight with four taken out of it and I did not know, and I want to have been in this room myself and seen the shelf.**"

**And nobody improved on that and nobody argued with it and the nine of them spent about four hours on it, and the shelf is still behind the door and the book is on it, and the woman of about sixty-three who keeps that book is not in that room and is four miles off and is not coming, and nobody told her anything and nobody is going to.**

## Four

**The thing they agreed was said in seven words and it was not written down, and here it is, and the room has it.**

"**Nothing about it goes on any sheet.**"

The woman of about fifty-eight with the slate said that was not fair to her and it was the only person in that room who said so, and then she took the slate off her knee, turned it over, and put it under her arm, which is what a woman does with a slate at about the ninth hour.

**And the cost of it was said out loud by the man of about twenty-two with a handcart, in forty-seven words, and he said it because he is a man who is hired and hired men are the only people in a room who know what anything is worth.**

"**Nine of us have met and nobody can unmeet us, and a man with a form can find this room and find the nine of us in it in about four years, and that is the price and we pay it now and not in four years.**"

The man of about forty-one with the boundary said, and it is the only time in about nineteen years that anybody has heard him say anything of the kind, that if that was the price then he was in it for two miles and nine stones and no further, and the woman of about forty-four from the kilns said that she was in it for a draw on Thursday and no further, and the mason of about sixty-eight said nothing at all and was in it.

**And the nine of them then agreed on the one thing they did agree on, which is that each of the nine will keep whatever they write, or whatever they do not write, and that none of the nine will send it to the other eight, and that there is no room in nine counties that any two of them can be found in twice running, and that the man of about thirty-two with a limp is the tenth and that the tenth knows four of the nine and that the tenth is not told anything more.**

Nobody wrote any of it down. He had bought four sheets of paper at a penny a sheet in a shop on the ground floor that morning and he had all four of them in his coat at the ninth hour and he wrote nothing on any of the four, and the eleven lines in the other book stayed as they have been all year.

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is his and it is the only one in about four months that has four sheets of paper in it and not one word on any of them.

**a sheet of paper at one penny, a second sheet of paper at one penny, a third sheet of paper at one penny and a fourth sheet of paper at one penny is four pence. A bed at two, a tallow candle at two, a pound of wood at three, a cart's fare at four, a bushel of oats at four, a tin of nails at five, a broom at five, a quill and ink at seven, a bucket of tar at eight and a horse's feed at nine.** Four, two, two, three, four, four, five, five, seven, eight and nine is fifty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and fifty-three pence is a mark and five over**, and what that room is taken for is not in that column either, because the woman of about sixty-three who keeps the book takes it out of a box on the shelf, and the trough has been in that book since her husband's time and the room has never been in it once.

**And a woman of about fifty-eight went home nine miles with a slate wiped clean, and a book about a hundred and forty years old is on a shelf behind a door, and nine people agreed that nothing about them is going on any sheet, and four of those nine are in four counties and one of them is a boy of seventeen with a basket of glass on his arm and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow carried that basket nine miles two days ago and put it down in a passage and did not say what was in it.**
