# Chapter 0433

**The seventh day of Fallowmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour, at a yard of four lime kilns two days west of Merefen, where the second kiln of the four was drawing, about forty people were out of a country of about four hundred, and the fourth kiln at the gate has been cold since the spring.**

## One

She was told nine times in four days and she can give the four days exactly, and she can give it exactly because there is a tally cut into the jamb of the kiln door and every draw is cut into it, and a woman who has cut a mark in a door for nineteen years can count the days between two marks without stopping to think about it.

**A man of about forty with a cart of lime said it on the third, at the gate, in the ordinary way, because he had been two days off and a thing that happens in a yard is a thing you say at a gate. A woman of about thirty-four who had come for lime said it on the fourth, on her own doorstep, to the woman of about forty-four, because a woman standing on a doorstep with a bucket in her hand is a woman saying a thing. A man of about fifty who buys stone and stone is in his trade said it on the fifth, in a yard, in the middle of an argument about a price.**

And then six more, and the six were not six more facts. **Three of them said the same as the first and two of them said it wrong and one of them added a thing that was not in it, and the one who added a thing was a man who had heard it from a boy and the boy had heard it from a woman at a well.**

Nine times in four days, and not one of the nine asked her anything, and not one of the nine was wrong in a way that mattered, and the one who added a thing was the only one of the nine who added a thing.

## Two

What was said, in the nine tellings, is this.

**There is a yard behind a house of assizes in a town of about nine hundred people. There are two rooms standing in it with a shelf in them and about four hundred years of paper on the shelf. The door has no lock on it and has not had one since a woman died there in a winter. Four men came with a paper with a seal on it and took about forty sheets off the top board and put them in a satchel and went.**

That is it. **Nobody said a name and nobody said a number and nobody said why, and about four hundred yards of a makeable road separate that yard from the gate of this one and about four people have been up it in their lives.**

**And the thing that made it go nine times in four days is not that it is strange. It is that it is a door with no lock on it. A yard with paper in it is a yard with paper in it, and four men with a seal is a thing that happens and a man can nod at it. A door with no lock on it is a thing that nine people out of four hundred can say something about, and every one of the nine said it in the middle of something else they were doing.**

She said the thing she said about it at about the sixth hour on the Saturday, to a man of thirty-two with a limp who had come into that yard at about the second hour with a stick in his left hand and had not asked for anything, and she said it once and she did not say it again.

"**I have not been to that yard and I have not sent anybody and I do not want to know whose it is.**"

**And that is twenty-three words, and it is the whole of what she said about it, and she was not defending herself, because nobody had said she had done it, and she was not refusing an offer, because no offer had been made, and she was putting a thing in the right place and the right place was in front of a man who was not going to write it down.**

## Three

**And the finding of that Saturday is not about the yard and it is not about the door, and it took four days of counting marks in a door jamb to establish.**

A yard of four kilns is a place where about forty people are out of a country of about four hundred, and about nine people come through the gate of it in a week, and there is a market on the Saturday in a field at the low end of the yard, and a thing said at that gate on a Tuesday is said on the Wednesday at the second house down, and on the Thursday at a well, and on the Friday in a market, and on the Saturday it is in about nine mouths.

**That is the whole of the machinery. It is not machinery. It is about nine people in a lane telling each other what a stranger in a coat said at a gate, and there is no clerk in it and no sheet in it and no band and no cord, and about four hundred people live in that country and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a shop in a fen town into nine counties, and the two of those are the same size and neither of them was designed.**

And the second finding is worse and it is about the door, and it took about four minutes.

**Anybody may come. That is what the yard is for, and it is the oldest of the nine and it is held by nobody and it needs nobody, and about nine people a week come through that gate and are fed and are asked nothing. It is the one of the nine that has no keeper, and the reason it has no keeper is that there is nothing in it to keep.**

**And a door with no lock on it is a thing that the same nine people can be asked about, and there is a difference between being let in and having nothing to hide, and about four hundred people in that country had never had to work out which of the two they were doing.**

## Four

The second kiln drew from about the fourth hour to about the twelfth and gave about two days of lime after it, and the four people who draw a kiln do it to a beat, and the beat is a thing four people have done for longer than the yard has been a yard, and one of the four is a woman of about forty-four and one of the four is a man of about nineteen and a boy of seventeen with a hand-basket of glass was in the yard with a load and was fed and was asked nothing and has been fed at that gate about four hundred times in four years.

**Nobody in that yard said the name of the road. Nobody in that yard said the figure. Nobody in that yard has ever had a reason to say either of them and the slip with three things on it is about nine miles off in a box to keep pins in, and about four of the four hundred people in that country have heard the place and not one of them was in a room with a form when they heard it.**

The buyer from eleven miles off came on the Friday and looked at the face of the cold fourth kiln from the road and did not come in, and that is twice a year and has been twice a year for about nine years.

**And that is a market morning, and about nine hundred people were in a street four hundred yards long in a fen town nine miles east of the nearest of them, and not one of the nine hundred was at a kiln, and the two of those are four days and nine miles apart and there is no line between them.**

## Five

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is a walking column and he walked it in a day and a half.

**a horse's feed, a cart's fare, a quart of small beer, a quill and ink, a second sheet at one penny, a bed, a handcart for four miles, a sheet of paper, a fire, a room, a second loaf, a tallow candle, a loaf: nine, four, two, seven, one, two, five, one, three, six, one, two, one is forty-four pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and forty-four pence is four pence under one, and he was fed at that gate and paid nothing for it and a plate of a woman of about forty-four is not a line in this column and is not in any column in nine counties.

**And nine people told her one thing in four days and six of the nine said it the way it happened and one of the nine added a boy and a well to it, and about four hundred people in that country can now say what happened in a yard two days off, and not one of them was there, and not one of them is the woman who ordered anything, because nobody ordered anything that anybody in that country knows of.**
