# Chapter 0418

**The twelfth day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, at the head of a made road four miles west of Marl, a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where nine feet of oak has lain across nine feet of a kerb since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth and comes up on four days in seven, and it is up on this one, and a man of about nineteen has been standing at one end of it since that day, and about fifty-four carts came up that road in about four hours today, a Sunday being about six times as busy as a Friday at that bar, and the largest thing that comes up it is nine hundredweight of dressed stone on a cart.**

## One

The same three figures, and they are the same three because there is no fourth, and a man of about nineteen has been counting one of them and not the other two for a long time now.

**The bar is nine feet of oak. He is paid nine pence a day. And the pocket of a coat that is not his has the day-money in it as twists of paper, and he has counted the twists and not the days, and counting the twists is the only column of figures he has ever kept.**

The derivation, and it is the only arithmetic about his year that anybody in that town has ever done.

**From the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth to the seventh of Longlight is a hundred and ninety-three days. In those days that bar stood up about a hundred and ten times, and he is paid for three days in four of the days it is up, which comes out at about eighty and three, and about eighty and three twists of paper were in that pocket on the seventh of Longlight, and nobody has counted them since except him and he has not counted them since.**

**From the seventh of Longlight to today is thirty-five days more, which is five weeks exactly, and five weeks is twenty up-days and fifteen paid days, and eighty and three and fifteen is about ninety-eight. Two hundred and twenty-eight days at four up-days in seven gives about a hundred and thirty, and three paid days in four of those gives about ninety-eight, and there are about ninety-eight twists of paper in the left pocket of a coat that is not his.**

**And the fifth of the four that he is not paid for is this one. The bar is up on a Monday and a Wednesday and a Friday and a Sunday and he is paid for three of the four, and the one he is not paid for is the Sunday, and a Sunday is about six times as busy as a Friday, and the pocket does not get a twist today and will not get one tonight, and nobody has ever asked him about it and he has never asked anybody, and about two hundred people in that town know that he stands at that bar and about four of the two hundred know which day he is not paid for and about none of the four have said a word to him about it.**

**And a man of about nineteen has been on that bar for a hundred and fifty-eight days and has not once been told that a thing is wrong with him, and the standing of that bar in that town is a thing about nine hundred yards of kerb and about two hundred carts a week and about four feet of a drain, and there is a kerb bar in a market four hundred miles off and a bar across a market street in a town of about two thousand on the salt, and this is the only one of the three anybody writes anything down about, and it is written on a bench nine feet by seven with a stone on the corner of it, by a man who cannot read it, and the reason there is any writing at all about that bar is that there is a man on it who is on nothing.**

## Two

The money of it, and it is the only sum about that bar that anybody has ever added.

**Nine pence a day for about ninety-eight days is eight hundred and eighty-two pence, which is eighteen marks and eighteen pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence, and there is not one figure of that sum in this kingdom on a page or a board or in a mouth, and he cannot read and has never been able to read and can count a barrow of bricks and can do a sum of two things, and those are three separate things and only the last one of them is the one that is the matter here.**

**And there is a sheet of paper on the bench inside a shed nine feet by seven under a stone, and it has nine figures on it and no head on them and a date at the bottom, and it has been on that bench for about fifty-eight days, and the draught in that shed comes off the floor about a foot and the sheet has lifted twice and a stone holds it, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow put it there on a Friday in Greenmonth and was told not to say what it was and has not said and is not going to.**

## Three

The man of about thirty-three with a barrow came up that road at about the fifth hour and stopped about nine feet off, which is nine feet further than he has come in about four months, and he had a fold of paper in the inside pocket of a coat that is not his and had carried it about eight days and had not opened it.

He looked at the bar for about a second and a half and then he looked past the bar, into the shed, at the bench, and the sheet under the stone was face up on the end of the bench and the writing was at the top of it.

**And he did not ask what the sheet was. He has been asked about carrying a thing and told not to say what it was, and a man who has been told not to say what a thing is does not go on to ask a stranger at a bar what a different thing is, and that is a rule and he is the man who most needs it and he is the man it does not reach, and the reason it does not reach him is that a rule about who is asked about a thing only reaches somebody who is asked.**

He stood there about four seconds and then he went on up the road with a load of glass in a barrow and the ditch four hundred yards west on his right, and about four hundred yards further on he stopped and looked back and then went on, which he had done once on that road before, in the spring, and had not told anybody that he had done it either.

**And at about the sixth hour, about two hundred yards up the road, a man of about thirty-four with a barrow of his own caught up with him and asked him why he had stopped at the bar instead of going round the lane, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that he had wanted to look at the bar, which was not true, and the man of about thirty-four said that the bar is up on a Sunday and there is nothing to look at, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said nothing for about a quarter of a mile, and then said that he could not have said why he had stopped, and the man of about thirty-four said that a man can always say why he stopped, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that he could not today, and that was the end of it and neither of them said anything else for about nine miles.**

## Four

**And about eight people in that town of about two thousand are right or wrong about a date, and they have been for about a hundred and fifty-eight days, and about four of the eight are wrong by a day and about four of them are right, and a Sunday is a day that parish goes to the next parish, and about eight of the eight of them are in the next parish today, in four different houses, at about the ninth hour, and not one of them came up that road and not one of them said a word to a man of about nineteen at a bar about the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a person says that to a man standing at a bar and nobody is going to be the first.**

**And a bundle has been in that shed for about a hundred and twenty days and has not been opened in about a hundred and twenty days, and it is behind the stove and about the size of a small dog and it is the only thing in that shed that is his, and he has a sister of twenty-two in the county he came off the cart from and has not spoken to her in about a year, and about forty people in that town know the year and the reason and neither of them knows why he has not gone, and she is on the same road the cart came in on and he has not been near it in about a year, and none of the eight who know about the date knows about the sister and none of the eight who know about the sister knows about the date, and about nine hundred yards west of that shed a ditch has been taking a loaded barrow down about two inches since the middle of Harvestmonth, and a boy of about eleven with a pail has been on its shoulder at every hour of the daylight ever since, and the quarter of a mile round that shoulder is the reason one particular barrow man with glass stopped coming up that road in a winter, and he is not the man of about thirty-three with a barrow who put a sheet on the end of that bar in Greenmonth and who has not stopped and will not, and about two hundred carts a week go round the shoulder and about two thousand people in that town know why and nobody has ever started it.**

**The cost of keeping that bar is a column and it is got up by a man of about nineteen out of his head once a month and has never been written down.**

**A new pin for the lever at three pence, a quart of oil at four pence, a load of sand at nine pence, a broom at five pence, a tallow candle at two pence, and a bucket of tar at eight pence: three, four, nine, five, two, eight is thirty-one pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence and thirty-one pence is about two-thirds of one, and the whole of that was found in a yard about four miles off and paid for out of a twist of paper, and there is one more twist in that pocket tonight than there was on Friday and the ninth pence of today is not in it and will not be.**
