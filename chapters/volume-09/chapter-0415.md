# Chapter 0415

**The fourth day of Rainmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the fifth day of the same month, a Sunday and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the ninth hour, on a cart on a made road and at a byre eleven miles off a lane, about forty miles from a town of about two thousand people on the salt, in a fold with about nine acres in it and a stream about nine feet wide in it and about four people living at the byre, and the cart goes out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane on the fixed day and it came back a fortnight ago and it is going out again today, and nobody sent the man of about thirty-three with a barrow and he did not ask.**

## One

He got on the cart at a crossroads about eleven miles off the room and he was not asked and he did not ask, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man volunteers for a delivery.

**About a hundred and four sheets went out of that room on the fixed day in a band with four taken out of it, tied with a cord, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow was in the middle of the load for about two hours, and about the second hour he picked a sheet up off the top of the band, held it at arm's length, put it back, and said that he knew those eight lines and could not have said from where, and nobody asked him where, and he said that he had said nine lines out loud once in a market in the spring and had never got to the bottom of it and was not starting on it in a yard.**

**And then he was given a fourth thing at the last moment by a woman of twenty-nine who did not put a head on it and did not say where it went, and he put it in the inside pocket of the coat that is not his, and he has carried it about a day and a half and has not looked at it, because he was told not to say what the sheet was and he decided some while ago that not looking is the same as not saying, and that decision is a good one and is about the only thing about the last eleven days that is entirely his.**

The cart went about two days and he was put down at a crossroads at about the fourth hour of the second day, and the two days of his own trade were gone, and the ditch four hundred yards west of a bar in another county was not his ditch and there was no bar on this road.

**The two days were on a made road and about four hundred carts a week go up it and the cart went at about four miles an hour and stopped four times in two days, once for a ford and twice for a cart that had come off in the middle, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow got off it at each stop and put a shoulder against the back of it and did not say anything to the driver, and the driver did not say anything to him, and that is the whole of what he did for two days for a woman of twenty-nine who did not ask him to.**

## Two

The place is a byre and about nine acres and a man of about fifty-three and his wife, and the county ends about two miles short of it and the parish ends about a mile and there is no roll anywhere with him in it.

**He came out of the byre wheeling an empty barrow and asked whether a sheet of paper with eight words on it had come, and the man of about fifty-three said that one had, on the Saturday a fortnight ago, and that a man had put it down on the table and said nothing at all and had gone, and that the woman had read it out loud twice and had put it away in the clock case, and that nobody had come for it since.**

Then the man of about fifty-three said the thing that is the whole of that day, and he said it in the tone of a man telling somebody how many hurdles he has to mend.

“**There have been three of them.**”

**The byre is about thirty feet by about fifty and there is a milking row of nine stalls in it and the clock case is on a shelf above the pails and the shelf is at the height of a man of about fifty-three and not at the height of a woman, and he said that his wife had put it there and that he had not moved it and would not, and that she read it out loud twice and that the second time was in front of a man of about twenty-two who was looking for a hoe and who heard all eight and who has not been back to that byre since and lives nine miles off, and that about four people in that county have heard eight words out loud in a byre this year and three of the four did not know they had heard them.**

## Three

There is a stone in the byre wall about four feet up and it is not in the wall, it is on a shelf inside, and the shelf is above the milking pails, and there were three folds of paper on it and one of them was about a fortnight old and one of them was about four years old and one of them was about nine years old, and they were folded in four and they had been folded in four by three different hands.

**The man of about fifty-three has never known what the three of them are for and has never asked and has not thrown them away, and the reason he has not thrown them away is that a sheet of paper with writing on it is not his to throw away, and he has been in two counties that are not this one and a house that is not his family and he has never in his life had anything in his hand that anybody would have come for.**

He was told the reason in about nine seconds and he did not argue with it and he did not agree with it, and the reason is a thing about nine counties rather than a thing about this byre: **a room two hundred miles off keeps a list of nine places, and the list is not a list of people, and the three of them in that clock case and the two of them in a drawer in a market town and the one in a coat on a cart are five of the nine, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow has been in the room where four of the nine were said out loud in about nine seconds by a woman of twenty-nine, and it did not occur to anybody in that room, including him, that a list of places is a list of the places where nobody can be found.**

“**She brings them to places,**” the man of about fifty-three said, **“and she does not bring them to anybody, and I have had two of them in nine years and the one in between was for a man in a house two counties off and I know that because I have asked, once, in the first year.**”

## Four

The barrow man asked, at about the sixth hour, whether it was going to keep coming, and he could not answer that, and he has never been able to answer a question of that shape in his life and did not know until about the sixth hour that he could not.

**Nobody sent him. There is no list with his name on it in a room two hundred miles off, he is on no roll in four counties, and the sheet in his coat is the only piece of paper in this kingdom that has his whole errand in it and he has not read it, and a man of about fifty-three with nine acres asked him a question and got nothing back and did not press him, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow put his hand in the inside pocket of a coat that is not his about a second later, for a twist of string he had been looking for since the second hour, and there was a fold of paper in it, and it was not the twist of string.**

He rode back to the crossroads at about the ninth hour and the cart had gone and he walked the two days home and the two days were four now, and about a week of his own trade was gone, and there was a fold of paper in the inside pocket of the coat that is not his the whole of the way, and he did not open it and he is not going to.

## Five

The cost of those four days is a column and he paid it himself and there was nobody to pay it for him and no sheet anywhere that has a word about it.

**A cart at four shillings a day for two days is forty-eight pence twice, which is ninety-six pence. A horse's feed at three pence a day for two days is six pence. His own bread and cheese at three pence a day for two days is six pence. A bed at two pence and a quart of small beer at two pence. Ninety-six and six and six and two and two is one hundred and twelve pence, and a mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and twelve pence is two marks and sixteen pence, and he was paid four pence a day for the two days the cart was moving, which is eight pence, and he took the eight, and there is a barrow-load of glass in a yard about nine miles off that has been standing since the Tuesday.**

**And three sheets of paper with eight words on them are in a clock case above the milking pails in a byre two miles short of a county, and one of them is about a fortnight old, and the man of about fifty-three has been reading the same eight words about once a fortnight for nine years without knowing that there are any others, and he has never once in nine years been asked whether he is a keeper of anything by anybody at all, and he is not, and there are about nine people in that county who know he has a clock case with paper in it and about four of them have told him it is a form.**
