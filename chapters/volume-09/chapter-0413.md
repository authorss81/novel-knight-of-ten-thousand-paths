# Chapter 0413

**The twenty-seventh day of Longlight, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a room over a door on a lane in a fen town of about four thousand people, where a bench sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week, and has been asked nine things in about nineteen years, and about nine hundred people are in the street below and the bench does not sit because of them and has not for about nineteen years.**

## One

The bench is nine men and it is not a court and it does not have a chair, and it has a board and a rail and about nine feet of floor in front of the rail, and the nine feet is the part that matters, because a man who has business with that bench stands in the nine feet and not at the rail, and every man in that room has stood in the nine feet at some point in his life and about four of them had not been in the room for years.

**The bench has been asked nine things in about nineteen years, and the nine is on a page under the fourth step of an outside stair in another building in the same county, and the page is not moved, and the bench has never been asked a tenth thing and is not going to be asked one by anybody who has read the page.**

The man who keeps the minutes is a man of about fifty-eight, and he has kept them a year, and he is not the clerk of anything and has never been, and the minutes go round.

**That is the whole of the rotation and it is about nineteen years old and it works, and it is a rotation because a clerk of that bench is a year of a man's life and not an office, and about eighteen men in that county have been the clerk of it before him and about eleven of them did not want to be and not one of the eleven said so in the book because the book is for what was agreed.**

**And the man of about fifty-eight, who is on his second year and has four months left of it, said the thing about himself at about the third hour without being asked, and he said it in forty-seven words, and nobody in that room improved on it and a boy of about nine was asleep against the rail.**

“**I have not been asked whether I will do a third. Nobody asks. It comes round and you are in it, and that is the arrangement and it is a good one, and if I said no they would have to write down that I said no.**”

**And that is the whole of what a rotating clerk is and it is the second of the four ways a thing can be held without a keeper, and he is in it for four more months and will not be in it after, and about nine counties have never asked him about it and about two hundred people in that town know which four months of which year he is the clerk of it.**

## Two

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet came in at the fourth hour with a sheet of about nine lines on it and a question, and he stood in the nine feet, and he was not the first person to stand in the nine feet that morning and he was the third person to come off a made road that day, and the other two had a load and a dog.

**He put the sheet on the rail and asked whether the bench would take up one of the eight, and he said it as a man putting a business, and the sheet went on the rail face up and there were eight words on it and a ninth line ruled and nothing on the ninth line, and about four men in that room read it and about five did not because they were talking.**

Then the bench was asked nothing, and the reason the bench was asked nothing is that two men from the Crown's own road were in that room and this bench is not the Crown's, and a matter with an officer in it is not a matter for a bench, and the officer of about thirty-eight said so in nine words and did not raise his voice about it.

**“An officer in it makes it not bench work.”**

And it is correct and it is nineteen years of a rule, and the sheet came off the rail and went into the hand of the man of about thirty-four and the bench was asked nothing at all.

## Three

What happened instead happened on the floor, which is what the floor of that room is for, and it took about nine minutes and it is the whole of what was done with the sheet.

**A man of about twenty-seven who sells nails and had been in the room for about four minutes said that the thing on the sheet is eight words and a ruled line with nothing on it, and that it is not a business of the county, and that if the county takes up eight words then the county has to be told by somebody what happens to the ninth line, and that a county which is told nothing by anybody cannot take anything up.**

**And the man of about fifty-eight who keeps the minutes asked him one question, which was whether the man of about thirty-four had come to the right room, and the man of about thirty-four said that he had, and the man of about fifty-eight said that he had, in his own opinion, not.**

Nobody improved on either of those and nobody wrote anything down, and the sheet went back into the man's coat, and the other two of the Crown's own road went out at about the fifth hour and took nothing and asked nothing, and the bench rose at about the seventh hour, and the minutes for that Saturday have four lines in them and the four lines are about a watercourse.

**And at about the sixth hour the man of about thirty-four with a sheet came back into the nine feet on his own, without either of the other two, and put the sheet on the rail again face down, and the man of about fifty-eight did not look up out of the book, and the man of about thirty-four said that he was not asking the bench anything and wanted to say one thing into the nine feet and off the rail and out, and the man of about fifty-eight said that the nine feet was for that.**

**And he said that the thing the sheet was asking the county for was a place and a figure and a trade, and that this county can put a place and a figure and a trade on about nine hundred sheets a month and does, and that a road has none of the three, and that a road with a figure and a trade on it would be a thing that could be asked after, and that he did not want it asked after because of about nine people who have no way of being asked after, and that he had been turning it over for about four days and that this was the first time he had said it out loud and that he was not going to say it a second time to anybody at all.**

**And the man of about fifty-eight wrote one line in the book, and the one line is that a person stood in the nine feet and said a thing, and the thing itself was not written, and the sheet went off the rail face down and into the man's coat for the second time that day, and the bench went back to what it had been doing, and about two hundred people in that town know that two men from the Crown's own road were in that room on a Saturday, and about none of the two hundred know what either of them said in it, and the Crown's own series in that town of about four thousand has no column for that either, and it is not going to get one from a Saturday.**

**And the count on the page under the fourth step of an outside stair in another building in that county stands where it has stood for about nineteen years, and a day-book two hundred miles off has nine entries in it of a thing nobody asked, and neither of those two numbers was added to the other anywhere in this kingdom and neither of them is added to a figure of persons.**

**And the man of about thirty-four with a sheet walked out of that room with the bench at about the seventh hour and out of the lane at about the eighth, and did not get on a horse, and stood in a yard about two hundred yards off for about nine minutes in the rain, and then went into an office about two hundred yards the other way and put a sheet of nine lines into a drawer and shut the drawer, and nobody in that office said anything to him, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that office into nine counties, and about nine of them have nine words on them, and the other about eight hundred and ninety of them have a place and a figure and a trade on them, and that is the whole of the difference between the two nines and nobody in that office has ever written it down.**

## Four

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the officer's own, and he paid it and it was not put to the bench and the bench was not asked whether it should be.

**A sheet of paper at one penny, a horse's feed at nine pence, a loaf at one penny, a bed at two pence, a quart of small beer at two pence, and a tallow candle at two pence: one, nine, one, two, two, two is seventeen pence.** A mark is forty-eight pence and seventeen pence is a little over a third of one, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet paid it in a market town about nine miles off and slept in a room over a stable and said on the Monday that the county had got the sheet back, which it had, and that this was the ordinary end of an ordinary morning, and that it is not the first time in about nineteen years and it will not be the last that a thing has been carried nine miles into a room and nine miles back out of it.

**And about nine men in a room over a door on a lane went back to a watercourse at about the seventh hour, and about nine hundred people went out of a street four hundred yards long, and a bench that sits on Saturdays went down for six more days, and about nine men in it keep a book on a turn, and about eight of the nine are not this year's turn and are not going to be next year's either, and about nine people in that county have been asked something by that bench in about nineteen years and there is a page under a fourth step that says nine, and the newest of the nine is about two years old and the oldest is about nineteen, and the bench does not keep a third kind of minute and has never been asked to.**
