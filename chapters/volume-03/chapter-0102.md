# Chapter 0102

The first of Longlight, YR 313, was a Wednesday and it was not a market day, and two letters came up the hill in the same bag, and one of them was three days in the post from a city two hundred miles off and the other one was eleven days in a waggon from a county four hundred miles off, and neither of them was addressed to the same person.

The first was for the keeper of records. The second was for a yard.

Mara Vey read hers first because it was the shorter and because it was dated, and a dated thing in a letter is the only part of a letter that has not yet been ruined by the road.

It was a small flat hand that had been copying for thirty years.

*Keeper of records at Merefen in the north. I am an officer of the third press in the provincial town and I have read a press register every working day since I was thirty-one and I am writing to you because there is nobody else in this country who has ever written to me about a register and that is a fault in the country.*

*I will put it in four parts and the fourth is the one that matters.*

*One. A return of the series R.C. has been entered on a shelf in this office in a season you would name in a moment. It came from a border county on the west side and it is the ordinary paper of a village and not the office's own. It is a good return. It is filled in properly except in one column.*

*Two. The column is the fourth and it is headed Keeper, and the name in it is in a hand that is not the hand of the man the return is about, and the man is a seed-carter of a village in that county, and he did not write it and has never been asked whether he did.*

*Three. The office has relied on it. Not this office — an office, up the corridor, in the ordinary way, and the relying was done by a person doing the ordinary thing their own desk did, and the thing their desk does with a fourth column is treat what is in it as an office. I have asked. I was told it is a standard practice. I have been in this building nineteen years and I have never seen it relied on and I have seen it filled in perhaps forty times and I have not seen it once relied on, and I do not think that means it has changed, I think it means the second time a thing is done is the first time somebody depends on it.*

*Four. **A register answers what was entered on the shelf and nothing else.** I put that at the foot of a letter to you two years ago, in a different matter, and I have had two years to improve on it and I have not, and I am going to say the other half of it this time, which I did not say then: **a return is a fact about a person and not a claim about a person, and the difference is that a person can be asked about a fact and cannot be asked about a claim, and there is nothing in this kingdom I can put in front of a man in that county to ask him anything.**

*I am not going to send you the return. I have taken a fair copy of the leaf and I will send it if you ask me for it, and if you ask me for it I will put my name in the request, and I would like you to understand what you would be doing if you asked: you would be taking a leaf out of a press that belongs to an office, in a form that says why, with my name on it, in a matter about a man in a county four hundred miles off who has done nothing. I have been weighing that for two days and I have decided to send it unasked, which is not a rule and is a thing I am doing.*

*It is yours, and I am the third press, and the register answers what was entered on the shelf.*

*Ivet Salter.*

Mara Vey read it twice, which is her habit, and then she did a thing she almost never does, which is that she took a sheet of plain paper and copied the fourth part out entire, in her own fair hand, without the salutation and without the signature, and put the copy in the press with a claim number against nothing on it, and the reason is in the day-book and is one line and is not about Salter.

*First of Longlight, YR 313, sixth hour. Copied out entire from a letter of the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth, which is three days in the post, thirty-one years a copyist, third press, provincial town, the fourth part, being a sentence I have had a copy of for two years in a different matter and have never put next to anything. I have put it next to something today and I am not entering an opinion against the man, because I have not got one and have not asked him anything, and if he is in the third press in a border county and has done nothing, then the sentence is the only instrument in the world that would reach him, and it is a sentence.*

Then she wrote back to the third press on the second of Longlight and asked for the fair copy of the leaf, and signed the request, and dated it, and entered the hour against it, and did not put a name in it.

---

**The second letter was for a yard on Weir Street and it was addressed, underneath, to the keeper of records at Merefen in the north, and the yard has no clerk, so it went up the two flights of a stair over a weigh-house on the morning of the first and a man of twenty-five carried it and stood in a doorway holding it out like something hot.**

"To the yard," said Tovan Kest, reading over his brother's shoulder. "And to you. **The same wrapper.**"

"And the same hand," said Mara Vey. "Sit down, both of you, and do not stand in my door."

---

The wrapper was a board-rubbing gone soft at the corners, which told her the road before she read a word of it, and inside there were two sheets and the two sheets were not copies of each other.

The first was a paper of business and it was very short and it was dated, as far as it could be, in a way she had not seen on a letter before: not a day and not a month but a position in a year. *Written in the second week of the rolling that begins with the low water, in a year we keep from the first sowing.* And it gave a distance — *four hundred miles by the road we use and eleven days in a waggon with one river crossing, and the crossing is a bridge, and the bridge is not ours* — and a distance is the only thing in the letter that can be checked, and she checked it against a road-book on the shelf and it is four hundred miles, and she wrote the two figures in the margin of nothing with a pencil and then rubbed the pencil out, because a number she cannot explain is a number she does not put in a book.

*To the yard, and to whoever keeps the records in the fen.*

*We are eleven households of a council in a border county, and the council has no writ and no bell and no clerk of the Crown, and we are writing to a yard and not to a man because a yard keeps things and a man does not.*

*We have a water. It is in spate in the wrong week of the year and it has been for two years, and the water is not the trouble. The trouble is that the boundary of this county is the water, and the boundary has been sworn on a stone in the field where it is sworn, every year, in public, by the men who hold the ditch, since before anybody now living was born, and the men who hold the ditch are the men who swear, and a thing that is sworn every year is a thing that has to be paid for every year, and we do not know what it is being paid for, and the four villages at the bottom of the ditch system have gone white in the ground.*

*We are not asking you to believe that. We are telling you what a village looks like when it has been told the truth about a ditch for a hundred and forty years.*

*We want a bridgewright. Not a Crown man. Not a bench. A man who has held a structure in water and can be shown a hole and say what is under it. The county's own guild says it can do that work and it is right, and we would rather have the county's guild than a stranger, and we have asked the county's guild three times for a wage and been refused three times, twice by a landlord and once by our own bench, and the last refusal was six weeks ago and it was a good refusal and it was correct and the man who refused us is the man we are writing to about.*

*So we are writing to a yard four hundred miles off, because a yard is a thing that has to eat and we are a river county and we have salt and seed and no coin, and we will pay in what we have at a rate to be agreed, and we will not send a man to look at our ground before we have an answer.*

*If the answer is no, write the word and we will not write again this year.*

Mara Vey read it twice and put it down flat on the table with both hands on it.

"That last line," she said, "is in a fair copy of a county bench's minute four hundred miles off and it is four lines long and I have it in this room on the second shelf, and I have never told anybody in this county where I got it, and you have just both of you read a different letter that ends the same way."

Nobody said anything about that for about four seconds.

"And the other sheet," said Tovan Kest.

The other sheet was the return. It was a single leaf of printed paper, five ruled columns, and it had been folded and unfolded so many times that it had gone soft along the folds, and it was not a copy. It was the office's form, sent to the county, filled in by the county, and it had come back.

*Object. Date. Person having knowledge. Keeper. How often.*

*Object: the Water, and the ditch system of the four lower villages, and the standing water in the low ground at Sallow Marsh.*
*Date: in the second week of the rolling that begins with the low water.*
*Person having knowledge: **Aren Kest, road warden, in the roll of the Merefen bridge guild, and no other office.***
*Keeper: **Oswin Rill, seed-carter, of Nether Dray in this county.***
*How often: twice a day, and the same man has not read this line before he wrote it.*

And on the reverse, in the office's hand, printed at the foot of every form of the series, the instruction, and under the instruction, in a smaller type and still in the office's hand, a worked example.

Mara Vey put her finger on the fourth column and then took it off it and put it on the third.

"That is you," she said. "In the third column. In this county's own hand. And it is a sentence you said to a woman in a yard on Weir Street on the first of Embermonth of the year before, and it is here as a fact about a watercourse in a county four hundred miles off, and it is the only true line on the page."

"I did not write that," said Aren Kest.

"No," said Mara Vey. "**The person having knowledge** column is not yours to write and you did not write it. Read the small print under the instruction."

He read it. It was the instruction, and then it was headed *Example, as printed in the office's own hand, being how a county is to enter the fourth column where no office of keeper is found in the county schedule*, and the example ran:

*Where no office of keeper is found: enter the person having knowledge as the keeper, with the words he uses for himself and the order he is in it in, and the entry shall not be construed as the creation of an office. Example: **Aren Kest, road warden, in the roll of the Merefen bridge guild, and no other office.***

"So a county clerk did that," said Tovan Kest.

"A county clerk did exactly what the sheet told him to do," said Mara Vey. "And I want both of you to sit still for a moment, because I have been a keeper of records for four years and a half and I have never once seen the answer to this before and it has been on a table in front of me for four minutes."

She turned the leaf over and over again.

"That example is the only worked instance the office has ever printed, and it is a year old, and it is a real man, and it is a real county, and it is on the foot of every form the office sends out. **Every county that has ever filled in that column has been shown how, and the way that has been shown is a man's own name, in his own words, saying what he is and what he is not.** And the one time a man has answered this office's own column in his own words, in a yard, in front of four people, the answer has been printed at the foot of the form as the model for the whole series. It is not a case. It is a **standard**."

Nobody said anything.

"And the county copied the office's example into the third column and wrote a man's name into the fourth," she said, "and one of those two is a printed sentence and the other is a man, and the office will read the fourth."

---

**And that is the whole of the reading, and the rest of the first of Longlight is a yard.**

Tovan Kest read the letter of business four more times after Mara Vey had gone down the stair, and he answered none of it that day, and he put it in the press on the shelf in the yard where the week's work is, and on the Thursday he sent word up the hill by the boy of fifteen that a yard would read a paper properly at the ninth hour on a market morning and that anybody who wished to be in the room when it was read was welcome and would not be asked why.

**Nothing that belongs to a man goes in this yard's book, and only a thing set.**

That is the rule and it has been the rule for nineteen years and it is on the wall in the yard in the hand of a man who is dead, and it is the reason there is no name of Aren's in Toban's book and has never been one, and the reason the four days from the fifth of Wolfmonth are in it in a hand Tovan's brother would know is that those four days are the yard's and not a man's: *eleven days, and the stone is gone and the wall is going, and I have told him this morning and I am not going to tell him again.*

A contract is a thing set. Two sheets of paper with a rate to be agreed on them, delivered to a door, is a thing set. It is not a man's errand and it is not a man's favour and it is not a thing a yard does because a man it is related to went out of the county eighty-eight days ago and came back with grey clay in the seam of his bag.

And on the morning of the ninth of Longlight, at about the ninth hour, in a room over a wharf with the bench of nine in it, Toban's yard read the Marches' paper and the Marches' return in public, and eleven of the council of a county he had never heard of were in the room, and the answer the yard gave was about the rate, and the rate is the yard's own and is in a book in the yard with four seasons of it in, and the number was in the book and not in Toban's mouth, and he put the book on the table open at the page and let the man at the drawknife read it out.
