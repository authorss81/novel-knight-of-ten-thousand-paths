# Chapter 0130

The fourth rolling came in with the Water still high and the council’s book holding three things it had never held before: a page in the gauge woman’s hand, a question about sixty households raised and not decided, and a tenant’s proposal entered without opinion.

Aren walked the bank alone in the early light, thinking of Ash. In that clay county a person struck was removed from a count, alive and employed and not consulted, and the fourth of its five terms said a person struck might answer, and answering restored. He had seen men answer and return. He knew the instrument. The Marches were not Ash, but the shape of the unasked pressed on him, and he resolved to offer what he knew, though offering another county’s remedy to this one had failed him before.

He asked leave at the sitting and got it. He stood where Gace had stood and spoke carefully.

He offered the fourth term of Ash, that a person struck might answer, and answering restores. He said any household not on the oath might answer for itself before the council, and answering would restore it to the oath’s protection, and the restoration would be entered with two witnesses whose names stood in the book. He laid it out as a bridgewright lays out timber, piece by piece, and sat down.

He knew it was wrong within a breath and a half. The knowledge arrived the way a misstep arrives on a plank, before thought. A person in Ash was removed from a count. A person in the Marches was not on an oath. One was a wound and the other a promise, and a promise could not be mended by answering. Answering belonged to a roll that could be read twice. The Marches had a stone and a ditch and forty men, and no roll for an answer to land in. He had offered the wrong instrument, and the room knew it before he finished explaining why.

Munn said gently that the council thanked him for the thought and would not take it, because taking it would make the oath a count, and a count was a thing an office could hold, and this county had no office and would not make one to hold itself. Ferrow said the guild would not witness answers because witnessing belonged to the oath’s own men. The slate woman asked whether answers would be entered on the roll with a mark, and answered herself that there was no mark for answering, only for swearing, and the sheet had no room for a sixth space. The minute-man entered the offer and the declining as facts, with the hour.

Aren inclined his head and withdrew it, and the sitting moved on. Nobody reproached him. That evening he wrote in his notebook that he had attempted the one conversion the rolling allowed, from a county of counts to a county of promises, and had been refused, and the refusal was correct, and he would not attempt it again. He wrote that a register was what a county had instead of a refusal, and that he had no register to offer these people and they had not asked for one.

Nine days later a fair copy came down the line, unasked for and with nothing sent after it to say it was coming, in a hand that was not a clerk’s. The hand was the woman four hundred miles off who keeps the road at that end, and the leaf said at its foot, in the same hand, that it was a true copy of four lines taken out of a minute of a bench in the clay county at the far end of that road, and gave the room the minute was kept in and the hour, so that anybody who doubted it could go and stand in the room and look.

The first line gave the reading of the line and the hour it was kept. The second line said: we did not lose our register, we lost our refusal, and a register is what you have instead.

He had read that sentence once before, in a fair copy in a room over a cooper, without knowing where it came from. Now he knew where to go and look, and he knew the woman, and the leaf carried two more things at its foot in the copyist’s hand, because a copyist cannot resist telling a reader what she is copying out of: that in nineteen years that office had entered two things against a person by name and this was the second, and that the woman’s fourth column said *wife* and was not a trade, and that the entry was not a removal. It had come four hundred miles to correct him, in the hand of a woman who keeps a road and had no business correcting a bridgewright and did it anyway. He folded the leaf and put it in his notebook, and told nobody in the Marches what he knew, because the county did not know he knew, and knowing was his burden and not theirs.

The rolling closed with the proposal still on the table, the jars still sealed, the sixty still unasked, and the ditch still eleven yards off the trough. He was at the slot after the sitting, listening to the barn settle, thinking that the attempt had been his and the correction had come down a line, and that the distance between those two facts was his schooling.

He spent the evening with the yard’s men, helping plane a sill for the staging, saying nothing of fair copies or far counties. The drawknife man told stories of fen work, of fortnights lost to weather and wages footed in pence by lamp-light, and the younger men listened the way apprentices listen, storing method. Aren planed and listened, content for an hour to be hands and not reasons.

Later alone he opened his notebook and wrote the refusal of conversion plainly: no rolling would be rendered into days of his own calendar, no count of rollings into figures of months, because the two frames shared nothing and paper could not make them share. The nine days to the fair copy were nine days on the line’s own keeping, and that was as much as the paper would carry without a man inventing it.

Then he entered one line under that, which was the first decision he had made in this county: that he would carry nothing else to that chamber. He had brought a term out of a clay county that worked on a count, and it had been refused in about a breath and a half, and the refusal had been right, and there was no third thing in the bag for that room. A man who keeps offering after he has been told the offer is wrong is not a bridgewright, he is a nuisance with a good reason, and the reason does not make him less of one. He put the hour against it, because he put the hour against everything, and then he read the two lines together and thought that the sentence four hundred miles off had beaten him to it by nine days, and that a woman who keeps a road had done in a gateway what he had needed a notebook for.

The last of the light went off the Water and the drawknife man came out to the staging to see how the sill lay, and found Aren still there with the plane in his hand.

“You have been in this county four rollings and the nine days before them,” he said. “I am not asking how long you are staying. I have two men after your own day is done and a wage question, and a man who says he is leaving in the morning is a man I will not put on a staging at seven.”

“Then put me on it at seven,” Aren said, “and I will tell you honestly that I do not know what I am doing in the spring. Nobody in that chamber has asked me for one thing, and I carried one thing up those stairs, and it was wrong, and what the county has said in its own hand is better than it. So I will hold your line. And I will not come up those stairs with anything in my hands again unless a person on it asks me for it by name.”

The drawknife man took the plane off him and set it down on the staging boards, and looked at the sill, and did not say anything for long enough that the river got louder. Then he said that in all the years he had worked in a yard there had been two men who said a thing like that to him, and that one of them had been wrong about it and the other had been a fool, and that he would take the sill as evidence and not as a promise, because a yard that took promises kept promises in a book and the county it worked in had not got one.

“Seven, then,” he said, and went in.

Aren was in the dark at the end of a staging above a brown river in a county that had refused him twice and kept him anyway, with a fair copy folded into his notebook that he had told nobody in the county he had, and three marks in a stone wall behind the chapel that nobody had asked for, and the certain knowledge that whatever road he had come four hundred miles to open was not going to be opened by him, and that he was going to be here at seven in the morning.
