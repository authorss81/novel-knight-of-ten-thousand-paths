# Chapter 0117

The fifteenth of Rainmonth, YR 313, was a Friday and it was not a market day, and a yard on Weir Street that has been working since first light on a Tuesday can be very quiet on a Friday morning, and the four of them were out of the gate at about the sixth hour with a cart and no load on it at all, and that is the one thing in that street that anybody would have noticed, and nobody did, and it is going to stay that way.

The cart was Toban's and it went out of Merefen on the western road, and there were four men on it and their tools and their beds and a good deal more of it than four men need for eleven days, and one of the four was fifty-two years old and had a cart of his own on the Old Toll channel and had left it in that yard, hitched, with his name on it in chalk, which is a thing men do not do.

---

**And the thing nobody in Merefen can check is the day, and it is the day because a county four hundred miles off named it, and it named it by saying when the men would be on its road.**

That is the whole of the arrangement and there is no other part of it.

**They named it by the men arriving.** Not by a number. A sheet came up the hill on the fourth of the month saying that a day is named for four men of a yard and that the day is the day those four men will be on that county's road, and that no day has been named for a line, and that there will not be one until the line is made. And it is signed at the foot by the council and by the first hand of that county's bridge guild, whose name is Coll Ferrow, and it is in a good hand, and it came sixteen days up the hill, and a delegation that came the same road in a waggon had said the same thing in the same words eleven days' walk away twenty-five days before.

Eleven days to the crossing, they said, and both of the men who said it said it twice.

A Friday in Rainmonth in this county is a Friday and is not a market day, and in that county the day those four men are on their road is a day in a month this county has not got, in a year counted from the first sowing, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will convert one of those into the other, and the yard's book says so, and the yard's book says it in a line with no figure on it.

And the eleven days was itself the second-hand number, and the man at the drawknife, who is the only one of the four who was in that yard on the ninth of the month before, said so at about the fourth hour and got told by the other three that the number was good.

"It is the number the delegation walked," he said. "Two of them came it and one of them said the waggon was quicker and the other said the waggon did not go the whole way, and the delegation was in this county five days and I fed all of them, and one of the two I never saw for two days and I did not ask him where he had been, and I have thought about not asking him every day since the eighteenth of the month before, and I am not going to be the man who guesses a number out loud on a Friday morning."

"Then we take eleven," said Tovan Kest, "and we keep for twelve, and if the ground is bad on the twelfth we will have eleven days of it done, and if it is good we will be idle a day, and a day idle in a foreign county costs me three hundred and fifty-two pence divided by eleven, and I have not divided it and I am not going to."

And they were on the western road by the seventh hour and the county was behind them inside a mile, and the four of them talked about nothing for about two hours because that is what four men do in a cart on a road they have not been on.

Then the man at the drawknife said: "There is a woman in that county who has read the water twice a day for eleven years and has never once been asked for a line of the book. That is on the sheet, and I read it on the bench at the tying-up end, and I have been turning it over since the fourth of the month before, and I cannot get past the part of it where somebody has been getting up twice a day in the dark for eleven years and has not been asked. I have been in this yard since I was eleven and I know what that is. **It is not a thing anybody is given for. It is a thing a person does because the person is the one who is there.**"

"Then somebody had better ask her," said Pei Tarrow.

"**Then somebody had better ask her," said the man at the drawknife, "and it will not be me and it will not be us, because the sheet says the north is not to send anybody who can be asked to come and look, and if we go and ask her we are the man who was sent to ask, and there is a difference and they have written it down and we have read it out twice in a hall."**

And the first camp was on the seventh evening in a field of cut stubble behind a farmhouse, and it rained, and the tarpaulin came off the load in about four minutes because the tarpaulin was on top of the tools and the tools were on top of the beds.

They got the beds dry. That is what a crew does on the first night and it takes about a quarter of an hour and nobody speaks during it.

**And then the man at the drawknife took out the page from the yard's book that he had copied out on the tying-up bench on the Thursday, on the back of a contract sheet, because the yard's rule is a fact set and a fact set goes in the book and the yard's book is four hundred miles off and the copy in a man's boot is not the book and is not a rule and is the only thing four men will have on a road like that.**

*Four of us, eleven days on the road, at the yard's rate of eight pence a day: thirty-two pence a day. Thirty-two for eleven days is three hundred and fifty-two pence. The sheet pays the crossing and not the road. The figure for the road is not on the sheet and the figure is nothing.*

"Three hundred and fifty-two," he said. "Out of the yard. That is the road and that is the keep and that is what the four of us eat for eleven days, and I have said it out loud in a field in a county I cannot name a village in, and if that is the worst thing that happens to us in ninety days then I will consider this the best contract this yard has ever had."

Nobody improved it. A carter's boy came across the stubble at about the ninth hour with a dog and asked whether they were for the market at the third stile, and the man at the drawknife said they were, and the boy said the market was a day, and went away, and the man at the drawknife wrote nothing on the page, and the not writing was correct, because a day a boy told a man is not a date and a date is what that page is for.

And the keep was told to them standing up in the yard at about the fifth hour, which is the master's rule and has been for nineteen years, and it took about ninety seconds and one of the four wrote it on the back of his hand in chalk, which is also the yard's habit and is nineteen years old and is not the rule.

"Eleven days on the road before we are on their ground," said Tovan Kest. "The crossing is theirs. The road is ours. **Here is the road.**"

*Four of us, eleven days, at the yard's rate of eight pence a day: thirty-two pence a day. Thirty-two for eleven days is three hundred and fifty-two pence.*
*And the sheet pays the crossing and not the road, and the figure for the road is not on the sheet, and the figure is nothing.*
"So it is three hundred and fifty-two pence out of that yard, and that is seven marks and sixteen pence, and I am not going to put that in the same sentence as the eleven marks and fourpence the trust owes us, and that is on purpose, and I would like it noticed that it is on purpose."

Nobody noticed it, and he looked to see whether the man at the drawknife noticed it, and the man at the drawknife was loading a bar he was not going to need for two days and was not looking up, and that is the whole of what happened between those two men on that morning and it is in the book as a fact and not as a want.

And Pei Tarrow wrote the figure on the back of his hand and then wiped it off about a mile out, and said the thing he had come four hundred miles to have said, and it was said to a man of twenty-nine and not to the master.

"I have been in eleven markets in eleven years," he said, "and I have been in every one of them as a man somebody had a thing to say to. **And I have got into that cart this morning and I have gone four hundred miles to a county I cannot name a single person in, and I have not asked one question about it, and I have wanted to, since the fourth of the month before, and I have not asked, and I would like somebody to notice that I have not asked.**"

"You have not asked," said the man at the drawknife.

"I have not asked. And the reason is that a yard takes the work when the work comes, and the work came, and I am not going to stand in a gateway in Weir Street being a man with opinions about a county I have not seen while four hundred miles of road waits. **And I have been a man with opinions about a county I have not seen for eleven years and it has cost me every cart I have ever had.**"

The man at the drawknife said, after a while, and it is the one thing he said in a cart that morning and he did not say it to the master:

"She is in a village called Sallow Marsh and I have not got the rest of it and I do not want to guess at the rest of it in a cart. **And if we go and ask her, we are the men who were sent to ask, and there is a line on a board in a hall in this town that says the north is not to send anybody who can be asked to come and look, and I have read that line out loud in that hall twice and I am not going to be the reason it is true.**"

Then the gateposts went by, and the western road, and the country of Merefen behind them for about a mile, and then not.

And they went under two hands and not under the master, and the master stayed, and the two hands were Coll Ferrow at the far end of a line four hundred miles off and the man at the drawknife at this end of it, and the man at the drawknife was on the western road with a bar he did not need until the crossing, and there was no hour in it and there was no line in it and there is a sheet in a chamber over a tithe barn four hundred miles off with the first hand's name at the foot of it, and the county does not know the yard's name and has never once asked for it.

The witness at the Marches' end was chosen by the Marches and is not named on anything, and the four men were told that and were told one more thing, which is that if the man at the far end of the line is not there on the day, the line does not open, and it does not fail, and nobody is to say afterwards that it failed.

"That is a rule about a door," said Pei Tarrow. "Somebody in that county has a door."

**"Somebody in that county has a door and the key to it and it is not the same door every day," said the man at the drawknife, "and if it is not there the door is not shut, it is a day nobody opened, and I have been in this yard nineteen years and the only thing I have ever been sure of is that a thing that is not opened on the day it was going to be opened is not the same as a thing that would not open."**

Then the road went west, and the four of them were on it, and the cart was light, and the master's brother came out of the yard gate at about the seventh hour with a book in his coat and a bag and a chalk stub and walked four hundred yards up the wharf road to a bridge he does not hold, and he did not go four hundred miles that morning, and he went out of the county seven days later, and the two of them had said eleven words to each other across a bench-board in nineteen months and every one of the eleven had been about a length of hemp.

**And nobody in Merefen was told anything by anybody on the fifteenth of Rainmonth, YR 313, or on the sixteenth, or on the twenty-second, and the county does not know the yard's name and the yard's book does not have the name of a man in that county in it except the first hand's, and there is a road on a Tuesday in a bag in a room over a weigh-house four hundred miles off, and nobody has laid a hand on it.**
