# Chapter 0106

The sixteenth of Longlight, YR 313, was a Thursday and a market day, and the market in Merefen runs in one street with the water on the left of it, and by the third hour it had been under the window of a room over a weigh-house at the north end of the town for two hundred years, and on this particular Thursday about nine people went past that window and looked up at it, which was four more than usual, because on the ninth the delegation of a county that is not on any map in this kingdom had asked a room full of forty people a question and the answer had gone round the market by the second hour.

Aren Kest came up the two flights at about the fifth hour with a wet sleeve and a notebook in the inside of his coat, and the door on the lane was open, and there was a cup on the sill with cold tea in it that she had put there for him on the ninth and had not cleared away.

"I have been working on the rules," said Mara Vey, before he had got his coat off.

"I have been trying to think of a way not to do this," he said.

"Then you have wasted a day and I have not, and put the bag down."

---

**What she had been working on was a sheet of fair copy, ruled in her own hand, headed in her own hand, and it is the shortest thing in that office and the only thing in four years and a half of that office that has been entered by two people on the same morning.**

*Three rules of this office. Entered against the office. Entered against no person. The sixteenth of Longlight, YR 313, sixth hour.*

**One.** A cost is entered before it is incurred, in the book of the person who pays it and not the book of the person who decides it. An entry made before is not a promise and is not a confession.

He read that one twice and then said, "That is mine, and you have got it better than I had it."

"You had it as *a man pays his own costs*," said Mara Vey, "which is a rule for a man. This one is a rule for a book."

"That is worse, and it is right, and I would not have got there in a year."

**Two.** A thing that nobody dissents about is entered as though somebody had dissented, with the name and the hour.

"No," said Aren Kest.

"It is not to be your name," she said, without looking up. "It is to be the name of the thing that dissented. There is a county boy of nineteen in this county who copied a reason onto a served schedule and asked whether it would still be on the sheet in a year, and it is, and that is the only precedent in this office and it is a good one and it is about eight months old. **A keeper who enters a dissent on behalf of a person is making a record and not a record of a person, and is not to do it.** The clause is not to stop the thing being entered. It is to stop me putting a man's objection in a book and calling it his."

"Whose objection," said Aren Kest.

"Whose objection is on the line," said Mara Vey, "and I am not going to say, and you are not going to make me, and that is the first rule of working with me and it is older than the three."

**Three.** An authority is entered with the words that made it. An authority that nobody can name the source of is entered as a habit; and a habit is a thing a county does to itself when nobody is looking.

Nobody in that room said anything for a while, and out in the street a carter went past shouting about a horse.

"That one is the whole of it," said Aren Kest, "and it is the only one of the three that is not a rule about a book."

"It is a rule about a book," said Mara Vey. "**It is the only one of the three that is about this office, and it is about me, and I am entering it against the office and not against me because if I enter it against me it is a document about a person and this office does not keep those any more.** And I have been at this desk four years and a half and there is an authority in this county that nobody can name the source of, and I know what it is and I cannot put it in a sentence."

"Then it is the right rule," he said.

"It is the right rule and it is written by a woman who is frightened, and I am going to put that in the document too, because the day somebody reads this sheet in nine years they are going to want to know whether the three rules were written by two careful people or by two frightened ones, and the answer is both, and the frightened ones are the ones who wrote them."

She wrote the third rule out, and it ended the way it ended, and neither of them said a word about where the last clause of it had come from, and there is no reason in the world for a reader to know, except that a woman in a plain coat with a damp hem said them in a yard on Weir Street on the first of Embermonth of the year before, at about the seventh hour, to a man and four others, and this keeper of records was not in the room and had never told this man that she had them in her own hand.

**And there were four lines copied in her fair hand six months ago, in a book in the press in this room, on a leaf about a county four hundred miles off, and he has read them, and she knows he has read them, and neither of them has ever said whose they are or where they came from, and there is no copy of them in this office that anybody could take out of the building and use.**

---

**And what came next is the part of the morning that the sheet exists for, and it is the reason the sheet is the shortest thing in the office and the only thing in it that has two people's handwriting on it, and neither of them wrote the second half.**

Beneath the three rules, in the same fair hand, in the same ink, and with the hour against it:

*Entered on the same morning, by the keeper, and against no person: that these three rules were made this morning because of the ninth entering of the not asking on this page, and that they do not end it, and that a rule about what is to be written down is not a rule about what is to be done.*

*That a procedure built around a thing that is not being said is a procedure and not a cure, and that it can be worked in for a long time by two people who both know exactly what it is built around, and that the two of them have been working one for about four years and a half and the other for about eleven days, and that neither of them has said the thing out loud in this room and that neither of them is going to.*

*And that the day this office stops entering the not asking is not the day these three rules have done their work, and that this office will not know which of the two days it has reached.*

"Mara—"

"Do not," she said, not unkindly. "If you say thank you I have to write down that you said thank you, and if you say you are sorry I have to write down that you said you are sorry, and we would both be doing a thing we have agreed not to do. **Give me the numbers.**"

---

**And he gave her the numbers, because he always gives her the numbers, and it is the reason the two of them have worked for four years and a half.**

He took the notebook out and did not open it, which she noticed, and she wrote what he said with a pen and put the hour in the margin.

"Two roads held. One of them opens when it is opened and the other one does not answer when it is called, and it has not answered since the first of Embermonth of the year before, and there is no procedure for a far side that has not decided, and there is not going to be one, and I have stopped expecting an answer and started noticing the absence."

"Numbers, Aren."

"Twenty-six days of absence on the first, and eighty-eight on the second. Twenty-one days is the figure printed at the foot of the terms in an office's hand, and I have been out of this county one hundred and fourteen days against a printed twenty-one, and the printed figure has not changed and the office cannot say whose figure it is, and I have said that in a room twice this year and nobody in this county can do anything about it and I am not going to say it a third time in a month."

"And the error."

"Five days on the first and sixty-seven on the second, and the ratio of the error to the absence is about a quarter on the first and about three quarters on the second, and it is not a constant, and I wrote down in my own book two volumes ago that the error is in me and scales with the work, and I have now been out long enough twice to be able to say that I do not know whether that is a fact or a thing I wanted to be true."

"That is the first sentence you have said to me in a year that you did not know was true," said Mara Vey, "and I am entering it, and I am entering that I have entered it, and I am not entering an opinion about it, and you may look at the page at any time and you may not take it out of this room, and that is rule one and it is the whole of rule one."

She wrote for about a minute and a half and stopped and looked at the page and then at the window.

Then she said: "The thing you came in with in the inside pocket of your coat on the ninth was not a bridgewright's thing. I saw the corner of it when you took your hand off the table. And I have known since the fifth of Greenmonth that there is a morning, and I have not asked you about the morning nine times now, and I am entering the ninth, and I am entering that I did not ask, and there is no reason on the line and there is not going to be one."

She wrote the entry and the entry was four lines and it was the ninth and the last line of it was the same as the last line of the eight before it, because a keeper of records does not improve on a sentence she has said eight times.

And then she put the pen down and squared the three rules against the top of the page, and read them out once, all three, in the voice she uses for reading a fair copy, which is a voice that does a great many things and one of them is making a thing sound older than it is.

Then she signed it, which is not a thing a keeper of records does to a page in her own book, and then she ruled a line under the whole of it and wrote at the foot, in the same fair hand:

*This is a true copy. It is entered in the day-book of this office, which is the only book in this office that cannot be served. It is not to be copied out of this room and it is not to be read out of it, and anybody in this county who is interested in it may come up the two flights and read it here, which is the practice of this office and has been since the first year.*

**Which means that the third rule is now a page in a book that cannot be served, in a building that has a door and a number and the capacity to sue and to be sued, and that anybody at all may walk up two flights of a stair on a market day and read the sentence about an authority nobody can name the source of, and a habit, and a county, and nobody looking, and take it away in their heads and use it at the bench or in a court or in a gateway, and neither of the two people who wrote it will ever be asked whether it is true.**

That is the whole of the sixteenth of Longlight, YR 313, and it was the first morning in a year that the two of them had done a piece of work together that was not about a wound, and it was not a reconciliation, and neither of them called it anything, and the work will still be in that room in nine years doing exactly what it was made to do.

---

**And at about the ninth hour there was a knock, and the man who keeps the minute of a council in a border county came up two flights of a stair with a board-rubbing under his arm and a wet sleeve, and he had three days left in this county and a post on the Saturday and had come to ask one thing and had asked it in about nine seconds.**

"I want the six lines of my council's paper in a copy that a court could have," he said. "Not to take away. To be in a book that is a person's and not an office's, in a county that has one, because a copy in a book in a room is a thing a court can have and a copy in a chamber over a barn is a thing a court cannot, and I have four hundred miles and no office and my clerk says a court would want it and I am not going to argue with a court from a barn."

Mara Vey made the copy in about a quarter of an hour and it is in the press in this room and it is a true copy of six lines of chalk off a board that came in a wagon, and the man who keeps the minute read it twice before she had ruled the line under it and then said:

"You will want to know which of the six is the price."

"I have four months in this office to work out which of the six is the price," said Mara Vey, "and I have a guess, and my guess is not a line anybody expects."

"Which."

"**The sixth.** *The line is not to be opened on a day the Marches have not named.* And I have been sitting on that for three weeks and I cannot say what it is a door to, and I have written it in the margin of the fair copy in my own hand, and the margin says: *a condition of this office, and the office does not know what it is a condition of, and it is entered against nobody.*"

She entered it, and the man read the margin and did not argue with it, and he said that in his county a door is not a thing you build and it is a thing you agree when to open, and that his county has been agreeing it for a hundred and forty years and has never once written down what it is a door to, and then he said that he would like to be in the room on the nineteenth and he would not be, because he was going home on the Saturday and there were three days of him left and he was going to spend one of them in a field watching a woman of thirty-eight read a gauge, and that this was not an errand of his council and that she was not to enter it.

"Enter it," said Mara Vey.

"I did not say that."

"You said you would like to be in the room on the nineteenth," she said, "and I have entered that, and I have entered that you are going to a field on your own account, and the second of those is against no person and the first of those is a man of a council telling me where he intends to be on a day, and I will not have a man in my book without a date, and you may not leave this room having told me a thing and not let it in."

He let it in. It is in the day-book with the date and the hour and a name on it, and it is the first name out of that county that has been in this office in four years and a half, and there is no Crown's hand anywhere near it.

---

**And at about the eleventh hour Aren Kest came down the two flights and stopped on the bottom step, in the lane, in the market noise, and stood there for the length of a cart going past.**

The third rule was right and he had known it was right before she finished writing it, and it was the only one of the three that was about him, and he had let her write it.

That was the ninth day of a habit that had begun on the fifth of Greenmonth with a sheet of paper with twelve months on it that she wrote out and tore up in front of him, and it had cost nothing that anybody could put a figure on, and he had a warrant of a road warden's rank with a district and a clerk and eleven marks a year in a sealed envelope in a press two hundred miles off, and a woman with a filing system had told him in writing not to take it and had said she did not think he would not, and he had not told the keeper of records about any of it, and she had just written down a rule about authorities that nobody can name the source of and had signed it and had read it out in a fair copy voice, and he was going to go down to a yard on Weir Street and cut a dressed block for a crossing that a bench cannot pay for.
