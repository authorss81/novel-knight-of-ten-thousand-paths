# Chapter 0143

The fourth week of the twelfth rolling is the week the stone has been renewed in for a hundred and forty years, and the day named in the tenth rolling came round on the third morning of it, and it came round fine, the way a day does when four hundred people have known about it for three weeks and have had nothing to do about it until it arrived.

The sky was low and grey and not raining. The Water was at the trough. The field before the trough was the same field it has always been, which is a long field with a boundary at the top of it and a stone at the west end of the boundary and nine men who have to be in the field before the hour.

They were all in it at the third hour and the room under the tithe barn was empty, which had happened four times in nine rollings.

Munn did the renewal and she did it the way she has done it since she was thirty-six, and the order had not changed in nineteen years, and there is a man of fifty-two who has been in this county since the fourth week of the seventh rolling and has not heard it before and who said afterwards that it was the only thing anybody in the county had ever said to him that he had not been able to do afterwards.

It is a long thing and it is in the county's own words and it names a field and a boundary and a season and a year, and it does not name a man, and at the end of it the nine men put their hands on the stone in the order they have always put their hands on it, and the last of them was a man of sixty-three who has put his hand on that stone since he was nineteen and who said afterwards, in the gateway, to nobody in particular, that his hand had been in the same place on the same stone since he was nineteen years old and that today it was in the same place and that the thing it was swearing was a different thing.

That is true and nobody in the county understood it, and Munn understood it, and she stood there and said nothing, and she had known since the second rolling of the year before that this was going to happen and she had said in that room that she would have to stand in a field in the fourth week and not say that nine men were saying true words for the first time in a hundred and forty years.

She did not say it. She has kept that since the second rolling and she kept it again, and it cost her the only thing she has ever wanted to say in a field.

Then the release.

It is not many words. Ordmar Gace wrote it and Munn would not let him say it and said it herself, from the front of the field, with her back to the trough, and her voice carried about four hundred yards and did not need to carry further.

“This oath has been sworn in this field in this year and in the years before it. It is broken today, in this field, by the nine men who have sworn it, on a day this county named a rolling ago, and it is broken so that it can be broken again, and a thing that cannot be broken again is a thing that has been kept so long it has stopped meaning anything.

“Any household in this county may declare itself loosed from the boundary as it has been sworn. It may work its own ground to spare the soil. It may say what it will not plant without forfeit of lease. And the consequence of breaking this oath is a list, and the list is of households to be helped with seed and with labour, and the four villages on the white ground go on it whole, and the council has entered at the head of that list that anybody with a copy of it and a copy of the roll can turn a household into a person, and has done it knowing.”

And then, because she is the woman who renews the stone and has been right about the history for fifty-five years and is not going to be wrong about it in public on the day she breaks it:

“The stone stands. It is the only evidence this county has that anybody ever agreed with anybody. Do not touch it.”

They dug the boundary in the four days before that morning and the five days after it, and that is nine days of work, and the council's account carries nine days of work and not ten, and the day of the breaking is in the account as a day on which the men of the length went to the field and did not dig, and Ferrow said out loud, to the room, before the work started, that he wanted the day in the account as a day with nothing in it, because a plan that is worked on the day the ground stops taking a barrow is a plan that has been written by a man who has never stopped working.

It is a mile of boundary and it is done by the people on both sides of it, and that is the only part of the ninth rolling's work that the tenth rolling's plan changes, and it changes it because a boundary that is a channel cannot be re-cut by one side of it.

The forty ditchmen split at the trough in the morning and went up the bank in two files, the men of Sallow Marsh north and the men of the other three villages south, and about nine hundred yards of bank on each side of the trough was opened to the old depth in a day and a half, and the east side of the mile was four hundred yards behind the west side by the second evening, which is normal, and which means that for about four hours on the second evening there was a stretch of the boundary with a new ditch on one side of it and a hundred-and-forty-year-old ditch on the other.

Ferrow was on that stretch. He sent the drawknife man down it with a lantern and the man came back and said the old head was nine feet off the new one where they met, and Ferrow said the figure, and the figure was in the book with the hour on it, and it is the second time in nine rollings that the guild's own two numbers have been nine feet apart at a meeting of an old ditch and a new one and the first time it has been the other way round.

The second night the water came up to the top of the new ditch and stopped there and did not go over, and about forty men watched it stop, and a man at a sluice said a sentence that went into four languages in the county that week and is not worth translating.

A promise that can be broken is a promise that can be tested. That is the tenth rolling and the eleventh and the twelfth, and it was tested nine days after it was made, in the same season, in standing water, on the same bank, by the same forty men and four men out of a fen county and a stranger with a bar he is not entitled to.

The sowing went in in the last week of the twelfth rolling, in the four white villages, and the minute-man entered it as a sowing and not as a return, and entered the words that make the two things impossible to join afterwards.

*Entered, in the last week of the twelfth rolling, that the four villages of the white ground have sown, and that it is a sowing and not a return, and that the roll of the households of this county is a roll of households and is not a return, and that the year ends in the fourth week of the thirteenth rolling, and that a sowing in this county has never before been entered in this book because the record of a sowing in this county has been a stone and the stone is a boundary.*

The four villages sowed into ground that had been under water for three weeks in the seventh rolling and had been re-opened in the ninth day of a nine-day job in the last week of the twelfth, and the last of the digging was on the sixth day of the sowing, which is wrong, and everybody in the room knew it was wrong, and the ground had to be turned before the rain and the rain came on the eighth day.

Aren Kest was in that field on the second day of the sowing and he was not asked to be and he was there because forty men were there and a man with a bar who is not on the roll stands at the end of a line and digs, and the minute-man entered him as one of the men on the bank and entered no name, which is a thing he has done eleven times now and did the twelfth on that day without being asked.

The woman of about sixty on three hearths sowed the middle hearth and let the lowest one lie, and the beans that had been in the lane in the seventh rolling were not in it, and she did not put anything in the lowest one at all, and the man at the drawknife went past her and said nothing to her, which is a thing a man at a drawknife does.

At the second hour of the fourth day the boy who reads the gauge came off the water with his boots in his hand and a piece of paper and said that the woman had sent him, and Ferrow read it and did not put it in the guild's book and did not put it in the council's either, and he read it out loud on a bank in front of about thirty men, because he has said since the seventh rolling that a reading is a fact and a fact is only a reading if somebody says it out loud.

It said that the water at the head ditch had come up in the night and gone down in the morning and come up again, and that on the second rise it was above the mark she keeps in her own head, and that she had put the mark against a stone on the bank and the mark was where she put it and not where the water was, and that she wanted four men to look at it in daylight and that she had not asked and would not ask.

Ferrow sent four, and they went, and they came back in the third hour and said nothing that was worth saying at the time, and the man at the drawknife said afterwards, in the gateway, that a painted foot on a board is a painted foot lower than the ground it is in, and that if the mark moves then the mark is wrong and if the mark does not move then the water does, and that he had been in this county for five rollings and had never before seen a thing be measured by somebody who wanted it to be wrong about herself.

It is in the eighth rolling already. He said it twice in two rollings and neither of them improved on the other, and nobody in that gateway said so, and the guild's book has no line about the mark and the council's minute has no line about the mark, and the only account of the water in that county is in a hut on a weir in a hand that has been the only one for eleven years.

The plan held. That is the sentence for the twelfth rolling and it is not a small one, and it needs the other half of it said in the same breath, because a man who reports only the half he wanted is doing the thing this whole account is about.

The plan held and two of the four villages' ground is worse than it was, and the salt has not moved, and the water table is still a mile wide, and a man at a sluice who has scraped ground for forty years put it the way he puts everything in this county, to a stranger, in the plainest words available.

“You have stopped it walking,” he said. “That is not the same as stopping it being here. You have put a stop to a hundred and forty years of it being somewhere else, and a hundred and forty years of water does not go away because you have stopped moving it, it goes down, and it takes four years to go down, and there is nothing anybody in this county can do in the four years except not make it worse, and half of them will not manage that because half of them are the reason it moved.”

Aren asked him what the four years looked like from where he was standing, and the man at the sluice said the first crop off two of the four would be a failure and would be in four years' time or a season's, and that the ground that was white before the Water came and the ground that is white now are the same white, and that one of them is going to grow something and the other one is going to grow a stone, and that a man in a county cannot tell them apart from where he is standing and can from where he is not.

Nobody in that field asked him how he knew which was which, and he did not say, and the words that came with it were that a hundred and forty years of a hand's width a year is a mile and a bit, and a mile of water standing on the low side of a bank is a water table a mile wide, and a water table comes down when the thing that is feeding it stops, and it comes down at the rate it came up and not any faster, and nobody in this kingdom has ever yet found a way to make a water table come down quicker than it went up.

He said that last part to a stranger and then looked at him as if he had heard himself.

“Nineteen years,” he said. “I have been standing on the low side of that bank for nineteen years and I have watched a ditch walk a hand's width a year and I have never once had anybody ask me how deep it was, and I have never once had anybody ask me how long it would take to bring it back, and if you had asked me either of those in the first week of the year before last I would have given you a figure and I would have been a man with a figure in a county where a figure in a book is a claim, and I have thought about that for about a minute and I am going to go and dig.”

He went and dug, and he was on the bank for the rest of that week and for the four that followed it, and the last week of the twelfth rolling is the week the four villages finished their sowing and began to wait, and the Water at the trough was two feet down and going down, and the pond above it had gone down two inches since the ninth rolling and the salt under it had not moved at all.

And a mile of boundary in the Hearth Marches had been re-cut in nine days by the people on both sides of it, at a cost of one thousand seven hundred and fifty-two pence of which the county had none, and the difference between that and the four hundred and eighty pence of the four hundred and forty yards in the fifth rolling was one thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence, and it was in two books, and the one with nothing in a money column in it was in both of them.
