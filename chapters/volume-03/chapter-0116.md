# Chapter 0116

The fourteenth of Rainmonth, YR 313, was a Thursday and it was a market day, and a yard on Weir Street that has four men signed onto a contract for ninety working days in a county four hundred miles off has a market Thursday like any other market Thursday, and the difference is that the four men had been in that yard since the fourth of the month before and knew things.

Tovan Kest had them at the tying-up bench at the sixth hour with the terms out on the bench-board in a fair hand, because the sheet itself went out of this county in a bag on the second of this month and is now in a minute book over a barn four hundred miles off, and what is in this yard is four lines with figures on them and an hour, and the boy at fifteen copied all four and copied the hour, and copied the empty space under the line about the seed as well, because that is what the boy does.

**"It is not signed by them yet," he said, and the sentence is still true and the paper it is on is still four hundred miles off, and the man at the drawknife had got to the stage where he said nothing back to it at all.**

Then he put his hand flat on it, which is not a thing he does, and said:

"Read your own line out. Not mine. Yours."

---

**The man at the drawknife is twenty-nine, has been in that yard since he was eleven, and does all of the arithmetic, and no book in this county has his name in it, and this one has not got it either, because in nineteen years nobody in that yard has ever entered a man.**

"*Ninety working days, at the yard's rate of eight pence a day a hand, four hands, and the boy at four, being half.* And then: *paid in the county's coin at the crossing and not in goods*, which is the guild's third term, which a man of sixty from that county said out loud in the hall over the wharf in this town on the ninth of the month before, and **which we lost.**"

"You lost it," said Tovan Kest.

"We lost it and we are going."

"Yes. And here is the part I have not said to the four of you, and I am saying it on a market Thursday because on a Saturday I would be saying it to be fair to myself."

And what he said took about nine minutes and it is in the week-book for the fourteenth with the hour against it, and it is the only page in that book in nineteen years that nobody has asked to be corrected after it was said.

*Four men, ninety working days, at the yard's rate of eight pence a day: thirty-two pence a day. Thirty-two for ninety days is two thousand eight hundred and eighty.*
*The boy, ninety days, at four pence a day: three hundred and sixty. **The boy is not going and his name is not on it and I am not going to have it.***
*Two thousand eight hundred and eighty and three hundred and sixty is three thousand two hundred and forty pence. That is the sum on their paper and it is the sum on mine and I have added it four times this week and had the same answer four times and I am still frightened of it.*
*What the county is paying, at the county's own figure for the county's own salt: two hundred and eighty-eight bushels at nine pence. Two hundred and eighty-eight times nine is two thousand five hundred and ninety-two. There is no seed, because I would not carry it, and I said that in a gateway in front of these four and I meant it and I would say it again.*
*Two thousand eight hundred and eighty, less two thousand five hundred and ninety-two, is two hundred and eighty-eight pence. That is what the four of you are short by, at their price for their salt, in their own coin, on their own ground.*
*And the boy was three hundred and sixty. Two hundred and eighty-eight and three hundred and sixty is six hundred and forty-eight.*
*And six hundred and forty-eight pence is the last fifth of the whole sum, which is the one the man from the Marches kept on the twenty-third of the month before and then gave up in a gateway in this street, in front of these four, for a reason about a promise, and which nobody in this yard wrote down and which I have now written down, and **the two six hundreds and forty-eights are the same number and they are not the same money.***
*Which means that a man of sixty from that county gave away, in a gateway, exactly what this yard is out of pocket by, and he did it for a reason he would not say twice, and he is four hundred miles off, and this yard's book holds the fact of that and not the paper, and the paper is in a minute book over a barn four hundred miles off and the getting of it would take eleven days and a man who has never been to that county would be told to come back.*

He shut the book.

"Two hundred and eighty-eight pence is six marks, and it comes to six shillings a man, and I am not going to let that be the reason any of you takes salt instead of coin," he said, "and I am not going to divide it, and I am not going to call it a fifth, and I am going to say one thing about it and then I am going to cut stone and if anybody brings it up again this week I will put them on the cart."

And the one thing he said was said to the man at the drawknife and not to the four, because the man at the drawknife is the one who will have to hold a yard together for ninety days with a number in it that is short, and the four will be four hundred miles off and cannot do anything about it.

"You will hear a thing in about three weeks and I am telling you now so that you can remember you were told. **A man from that county is going to send word, or somebody is, and there is no post that comes out of that county at any price, and what will come is a man on a road with a horse or a boy on a cart, and he will be a man who has been sent by somebody with no office, and he will say the salt is bad, or the salt is short, or the crossing was not there when we came to it. And not one of those three will be a lie.** And when it is said I want you to hear it and not to answer it, and to bring it to me, and not to settle it out of the yard's own pocket, because a yard that pays a claim on a contract it has not been paid for is a yard that has bought the contract twice."

"And if it is not one of those three," said the man at the drawknife.

"Then it will be a fourth thing, and I do not know what the fourth thing is, and that is the honest position of this yard on the evening of the fourteenth of a month I have a name for, and I have put it in the book in that order and you can read it in the morning."

**And on a market Thursday about eleven people came into that yard, because a market Thursday is the one day in a week in which a man in a market town is on the same street as a yard, and they came because a man from a county four hundred miles off had been in this yard for a fortnight in the month before and had been fed, and what they came with was the second-hand version of him, and the second-hand version was that Merefen has a bridgewright.**

The man at the drawknife said nothing at all about that for about two hours and then said: "We have a road warden with a vote. He is not a bridgewright, he is a man who is good at a structure and has been eleven years in a county that cannot pay him, and if anybody in that market says Merefen has a bridgewright I would be obliged if one of them would come and stand in this yard and be one, because I have a yard and a cart and four men on a sheet of paper for the morning and I have been the yard since I was eleven."

Nobody came, and he did not say anything about it afterwards, and the market went on, and the four men went on loading, and that is the whole of what a market day is for in a yard that has signed for a season four hundred miles off.

And Pei Tarrow, fifty-two, with a cart of his own on the Old Toll channel, who has been called a symbol for eleven years by eleven people in eleven different years and who has not once asked any of them for anything, said the true thing about a yard, and it took about nine seconds, and he said it to the four and not to the master.

"Ninety days is not a season," he said. "A season is when the work comes. Ninety days is a gap between two things and there is nothing in it, and a man who takes a gap is a man out of work in the middle of it, and in the middle of it he is a man who took a contract he cannot keep."

"You said that on the twenty-third of the month before," said the man at the drawknife, "in this yard, and you have said it twice, and I have written the number in the book both times."

"I have said it three times," said Pei Tarrow, "and I am not going to say it a fourth, and I am going to say the other half of it because the other half is why I am going. **Eleven years I have stood in this yard and nobody in this town has ever put a question to me. I am going four hundred miles to be a man in a yard that has my wage in it in a book, and I do not know what the ground is and I have not asked, and I have never in my life not asked, and that is new and I do not like it.**"

Then he looked up at the master.

"And I want the ninety days in the book with the hour, and I want to be back before the sowing, and I want it in the same hand as the figures, and I do not want my name in the book in the same line as anything else anybody in this yard has ever been called."

"It goes in with the hour," said Tovan Kest, "and it goes in on the same line, because there is no other line."

And at about the ninth hour the master's brother came into the yard with a book in his coat, eleven inches by eight, and asked for forty yards of tarred hemp, and the asking took ninety seconds, and neither of the two of them said one word about the sixth of the month before, or about the ninth of Embermonth, or about the twenty-second, or about a sheet in a bag that has gone to a provincial town two hundred miles off, and a yard where that has been true for four years.

"Forty yards is in the bin and it cost a pot of beer," said the man at the drawknife, "and the beer is in the minute now, which is a thing I never thought I would do."

"It is in the minute because the reeve asked for everything," said Aren Kest.

"That is not why it is in the minute," said the man at the drawknife.

"No," said Aren Kest. "It is not."

He cut the hemp and measured it on the bench and the boy at the bench-board copied the cut and the length and the hour, and copied the empty space under the line about the seed as well, because that is what the boy does, and at about the eleventh hour the boy was told that he was not going and did not say anything about it, because he had been told on the thirtieth of the month before in front of four men and there was nothing new to say and he is fifteen and he has been copying the empty spaces his whole life.

**And the last thing on the page for that Thursday is a line with no figure on it, and it is the sixth such line in that book in a year, and the yard's rule is that a line with a figure on it is a thing set.**

*Four men go on the fifteenth, on the road of that county, on the day that county named, which is the day they arrive, and the day they arrive is not a date. It is a number of days on a road I have never been on and it is eleven days to the crossing and I have been told eleven twice by two men who came it and believed the number.*
*And the road warden goes on the twenty-second, which is seven days after, and by a different road, and the reason for that is not mine and he has not told me the reason and I have not asked, and I have not asked because I have a page of my own in this book with an hour against it and no figure on it and it is about a date of his that is not this county's date, and I do not write on a page that has his name nowhere on it about a thing he has not told me.*

*And the crew's keep for the eleven days on the road is ours and not theirs, and I have not told them that yet, and I will tell them on the morning, and they should hear it standing up.*

Nobody in that yard, in that street, in that town, or in any office in that county knew on the evening of the fourteenth of Rainmonth, YR 313, that four men were going out of it in the morning, and **nobody in Merefen has been told anything by anybody, and the reason nobody has is that a yard's book holds the fact of a thing and not the thing, and the paper is four hundred miles off in a chamber over a barn, and a fact that is set down in a yard in one town and cannot be got at by anybody in another is the only kind of record in this case that has ever worked, and it is working by accident, and the man who wrote it down nineteen years ago did it because a thing that belongs to a man goes in the man's own book and not in a clerk's.**
