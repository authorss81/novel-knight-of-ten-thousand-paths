# Chapter 0112

The fifth of Rainmonth, YR 313, was a Tuesday and it was not a market day, and the mail went out of the lane behind the weigh-house at about the seventh hour with two things in it that were not letters, and a man with a tool bag watched it go from the top of the stair because the stair is the only place in that building from which a lane can be seen and he has watched a post go out of it perhaps forty times and has never once been in it.

**And the thing he was making was not a road, and he knew that by about the third hour, and the knowing took him nine minutes to say out loud, and those nine minutes are what the day was for.**

The north end of a road is a place a man can stand in. He had stood in it. The wharf road at the rail, the rail with the cold off the water on it, the tool bag at his feet, the Sedge Reach four hundred yards up the same road holding a fortnight after a mended parapet, and the six pence that two men had been paid twice out of a surveyor's box for reports nobody had asked for.

The north end of a road is also a book, and the book was the reason, and the book is a thing nobody has ever seen.

---

He had ruled it himself, on the eleventh, in a room over a cooper, on the back of a cost sheet because there was nothing else of a size. Eleven inches by eight, four columns, ruled in his own hand with a straight edge off a joiner's rule and a nib, and the four columns had no headings on them, and that was not an oversight and had taken him about a quarter of an hour to arrive at.

*What the line is. The day it may be opened. The hour. Whose hand is on it.*

And no column for a name. That was the second line of the six, in the county's own words, on a board of lath and lime that four men had carried four hundred miles: *No name is to be read out on the far side of the line by anybody from the north, and no name is to be written in the line's book at the north end.* He had read that one eleven times in three weeks and had not known what it was for, and on the ninth of the month before, at about the ninth hour, he had said yes to the fourth line in about nine seconds in a room in which nobody was looking at his hands, and his hands had been behind his back so that nobody would see them move.

A book with a column of names in it is a roll. He had spent four years and a half learning from a woman who kept one what a roll is, and he was not going to rule a column of names at this end of a line into a county that is not on any map in this kingdom, and if the far end could not be got to keep a book at all then the line was not made and the honest thing was to have a book with four columns and no names in it and to say so.

So the fifth column, at the bottom, under a ruled line, was narrower than the other four, and it was for the hour, and the hour went in every time a thing was done to the line and never in a lump, and a line with a column for names on it is a list, and a list has people in it who know they are in it.

And at the fourth hour and a half on the day before, which was a Monday, the bell went on the Old Toll bridge four hundred yards up that road, and it went because that is the day it goes, and the whole county knows on which day it goes.

It is a bell with a band of forty-one rivets round it and a dull red seam in the metal where the band was closed, and it is rung by a boy of sixteen with two years of an apprenticeship who is still not good at it, holding one end of a line, and beside him a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper and has a bar he is not using.

The arrangement is a Monday. It has been a boy and a ferrywright since the twenty-fourth of Fallowmonth and a boy and a carpenter since the ninth of Hearthmonth, and it is a Monday because that is the day the far end of the line keeps, and the whole of the arrangement is in a page at the head of the yard's book for the week with the hour against it in a hand somebody would know, and nobody wrote a word in it about who rang the bell and nobody has written a word in it about who arranged it.

They rang it because that is the arrangement, and the boy leaned on the third stroke, which is the thing a boy does who is not good at it, and the carpenter did not take the line, and the man at the rail at the wharf listened to the whole of it and did not go up the road, because the arrangement is not his and he does not ring it and nothing in the sound is his.

And at the far end of the line, four hundred miles off, on a coping on a river this county does not have, something was struck at the fourth hour and a half on a Monday, and a Monday is the day the far end keeps, and it kept.

Two people were on it. One of them is a tile-cutter of forty-one and one of them keeps the road, and they did not strike it and they were not asked to, and the sound is not a bell and it is not nothing, and it came across four hundred miles in a second and a half and went back the same way.

And the same sentence went into the minute of the assizes of that county, in a clerk's flat hand, and it is the third time that sentence has been entered and it will be entered again:

*Entered: that at the fourth hour and a half, at the north coping of the second span, a sound was made such as the two men present could not name, and that neither of them made it, and that no instrument in this county is known to them that maketh it, and that the same was entered on two former occasions, and that the men present are the same two, and that the coping itself is not described in this entry because no person present has asked what is on it and none of them is charged with it.*

That last clause was in the minute on all three occasions and it was in none of the three clerks' own hands, and it was the only part of the sentence anybody had ever read twice.

**And a keeper of records in a room over a weigh-house four hundred miles from the coping was told about it on the fifth of the month, in the evening, by the man who had heard the whole of it from a rail at the other end of a different road, and she wrote it down with the hour against it, and there is nothing on the coping that she can be shown.**

Not because it is hidden. Because the coping is in another county and the sentence about it is in a minute in another county and a minute is a fact about a room, and a room is not a thing anybody can serve, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will take a thing out of a minute and put it into a hand.

"That is the third time," she said.

"It is the third time."

"Then it is a thing that happens on a Monday, and you have found out what happens on a Monday, and I am going to write the word *Monday* in this book against no person, because a day of the week is the only part of it that is not a fact about two counties, and a day of the week is not a fact about a coping, and if I write the day down in this county's book then the day belongs to this county's book, and there is one Monday in a week and it does not matter which county is holding it."

And in the afternoon he walked four hundred yards up the wharf road to the Old Toll, which he has not done on a Tuesday in about a year, and the boy was on the abutment.

The boy is sixteen. He has two years of an apprenticeship and he is still not good at it, and he was on the north abutment doing something to the cradle with a bar and a wedge that nobody had asked him to do and that was plainly right, and the bell was above him with its band of forty-one rivets and its dull red seam, and it hangs nine inches higher than it did in Mudmonth of the year before, because a man took nine inches off the cradle on a bench's own vote in Fallowmonth and nobody has ever said so.

"You rang it yesterday," said Aren Kest.

"I rang it yesterday," said the boy.

"On the day."

"On the day," said the boy, "and on the day before that I rang it on the day before that, and I know what you are going to say and I have said it to him and he said it was not a reason and then he held the line anyway, and you can ask him yourself if you want, and he will tell you the same thing twice in the same words, because that is what he does with a thing he has decided."

"You could have asked me," said Aren Kest.

**"No," said the boy, and did not stop working, "because you are a man in a guild and I am two years of an apprenticeship and if I asked you I would have been asking a man in a guild whether I might do the thing that is in the page at the head of the yard's book, and the page does not say who it is for. It says a Monday. I would rather break it and find out than ask and be told."**

A bar went in and the cradle came up a sixteenth of an inch and went back.

"He has been at it since the fourth of the month before," the boy said. "Not you. The day I rang it wrong. He has not said one word to me about it and he has told two men in this yard that I did it on purpose, and I did do it on purpose, and I do not know yet whether I would do it again, and I have not asked him because he will give me a straight answer and I would rather not have a straight answer about it until I know what I think."

And nothing in that exchange put a question about the far end into the mouth of anybody, and nothing in it mentioned a coping, and nothing in it mentioned a county four hundred miles off, and a man who has a notebook in his coat did not ask the boy a single question about the day the sound arrived or who heard it or what it was, and the reason he did not ask is written in the notebook and the notebook is in the coat and the coat was on a hook by the door of a room over a cooper for three hours that afternoon.

He looked at the sill on the way back, because you cannot help it. A dressed block and forty yards of tarred hemp through the abutment on a splice cut out of a cooper's bin, and the fourth course above it is open the whole depth of the joint the width of a hand, in register, and scarred, and left open by a vote of nine men on the fourth of Frostmonth on a casting vote, over the objection of a man who keeps eels, so that a man standing on that deck can look down and see the mark.

**No glass. There is no glass in this county and there is not going to be a third seat.** The anchor he came back on is a stone with a cut in it and a seam of tarred hemp, and it is warm about a quarter of an inch under the surface in the middle of a wet afternoon, and he put his palm on it for about a second and a half and took it off, and it is not needed, and it has not been needed since the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth.

**And at about the seventh hour that evening a man with a tool bag made a thing that took nine minutes, and it is the second time in his life he has made one, and the first one was made by two men in a room over a cooper on a night in the ninth month of a year he counts from, and that one had a witness at each end who was not the man who kept the road, and so does this one.**

*A line. A hand at this end. A hand at that end. A date. The hour. A witness at this end who is not the man who keeps the road. A witness at that end who is not the man who keeps that road. The book at this end. The book at that end.*

That is nine, and he wrote them in a list in the back of the notebook, and the eighth and the ninth are the two books, and the two books are where the trouble sits, because a road is a line and two hands and a date and a witness at each end, and there is no column anywhere in that for what is written down about it, and he has never once in four years and a half known what a keeper of records is going to do with a thing that was made properly by two men in a room.

And under the nine, the honest second half, which is the half a notebook is for:

*Which of these nine can I make at this end. Seven. The two are the date and the book at that end, and the date is the one the sixth line is about, and the sixth line says the line is not to be opened on a day the Marches have not named, and a sheet came up the hill yesterday naming a day for four men of a yard and naming no day for the line, and there will not be one until the line is made.*

*And a message is not a route. I have been carrying that about since the fourth hour yesterday and I have got it into a sentence now and the sentence is that the four things are a line and two hands and a date and a witness at each end, and a sheet that names one of them is a message, and a message can be read by anybody it is handed to, and a route cannot be read by anybody at all, and I have spent four years and a half in an office that exists to hold the difference.*

*And the eighth of the nine is a book at this end with four columns and no column for a name, and the ninth is a book at that end that nobody in this county has ever seen and cannot be served, and the two of those are not the same kind of object, and one of them is in a room in Merefen that anybody may come up two flights and read and the other one is four hundred miles off in a county that has no office in it at all, and I cannot make the ninth.*

*And the two I cannot make are the date and the book at that end, and the second of those is four hundred miles off, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will let me lay a sheet of paper on that bench.*
*And I am not a hand. I have not been a hand on anything since I do not know when, and I have a page at the head of my brother's week-book that says a fifth line and has no figure on it, and I do not know that he has written it, and if I knew I would not be able to do anything about it.*

He shut the book and went down to the wharf and cut a dressed block for a crossing that a bench cannot pay for, and it was the Tuesday after a Monday, and on the Monday the bell had gone at the fourth hour and a half on the day the far end keeps, and the day before that, on the thirtieth of the month before, a boy of sixteen had rung it on a Thursday to find out whether the far end would hear it on the wrong day, and the far end had not heard it and had not been asked anything about it, and neither fact is in a book anywhere, and one of them is the arrangement and the other one is a boy, and the arrangement is the arrangement and the boy is sixteen.
