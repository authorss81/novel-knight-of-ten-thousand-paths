# Chapter 0105

The thirteenth of Longlight, YR 313, was a Monday and it was not a market day, and a letter came to the rooms over the cooper on Weir Street at about the second hour, in a bag with the county post, and the letter had been in the bag four days, which is one day more than the number Mara Vey has said out loud in a filing room in this county for a year.

The envelope was the office's and the office's is a good envelope. The seal was a device of a hand laid flat on a book and it was not broken, it was *cut*, with a knife, along the left edge, by somebody who wanted the contents and did not want the shape of them.

She is the one who does that. He has seen her do it eleven times.

Inside there were three things and they were not in order of importance, they were in order of folding.

The letter is dated the ninth of this month and it is the thirteenth, and he did the subtraction twice and got four both times, and then he noticed that he had noticed it, and that a keeper of records four hundred yards away has said out loud in a filing room a year ago that the gap between the day a thing is dated and the day it arrives is the only fact in her business she has never been able to do anything about, and that it had been three days then and three days in the ninth month, and that this one is four, and that the office has never once asked her to be exact about it and that he now is, for a letter from the same office, and that there is no good reason for the difference and that the difference is the whole of his morning.

---

**The first thing was one page, dated, printed at the head with the number of the series, and it is the first dated form out of that office to reach this county since the ninth of the same month a year ago, and that one is in force and is not superseded, and it came from a stranger.**

*Office of Provincial Registration. R.C. series, standing offer, made out to a person named in the fourth column of a return and holding a road under the marginal instruction of this office.*

*And at the foot of it, in the same hand and the same type and not in a line with anything else, because that is how the office prints things that are not the point of the sheet: **Returned. This instrument of the ninth of the same month in the year last past remains in force and is not superseded, and bars the assembly of any body of this county in respect of the object named in it.***

Aren Kest read that four times, and then he went and got the second sheet out of the wrapper, because the first thing on it was an instrument barring the assembly of any body of this county, and the object of it was not named on the sheet, and he has wanted to know what the object was since the twelfth of Greenmonth, when a woman two hundred miles off described four Crown's instruments back to a room in this county and this was one of them and nobody in that room could say what it was about.

He has been in this county eleven years and he has watched a room over a weigh-house become a body corporate with a door and a number. He has watched a bench of nine be asked four times. He has watched about forty people stand in a hall over a wharf on the ninth of this month and listen to eleven householders from a county that is not on any map in this kingdom, and a man of sixty state his terms in the middle of a floor, and a woman of thirty-eight say the number of lines in a book nobody has ever asked to see.

**And there is an instrument in force, in an office's hand, from the ninth of this month a year ago, that bars the assembly of any body of this county in respect of the object named in it, and the object is not named anywhere he has ever been able to see, and the instrument is dated, and a dated instrument is the only kind that a court can do something about.**

He wrote one line in the notebook, and the line was: *I do not know what the object is. I have four candidates and no instrument and I am not going to guess in a book. Ask somebody who is not me. — And then he crossed the last three words out, because there is nobody in this county who is not him at anything, and that is the first time in eleven years he has been able to write that sentence without it sounding like a boast.*

*And a road warden's rank, of the grade used in the eastern counties, with a district, with a stipend of eleven marks a year, with a clerk, and with the right to send men. The grade is a command and is not a custody. It is the grade given to a man who is answerable to a person and not to a community, and it may be held by a man who was never appointed by anybody, and the reason of that is that the office has found that a man appointed by a community may refuse.*

*A record of yourself, sealed. It will be kept by this office. It will be produced by this office to any body that asks this office a question about you, and it will not be produced to you at any time while it is sealed, and the seal is the office's and not yours, and the reason of that is the same reason as the grade: that a record a person cannot see is a record a person cannot contradict.*

*For both of the above, the eastern paths.*

*This offer was made out in the ninth month of the year three years last past and has been held open since, and the number of persons to whom it has been made out in that time is not stated on this page.*

Aren Kest read the first sheet four times and then went and got the second sheet out of the wrapper and read the first one again with the second on the table, and then put it face down and did not pick it up again for about an hour.

The reason the offer says *for both of the above* is not that the office has noticed what he wants. It is that the office has read its own marginal instruction, which says that where no office of keeper is found in the county schedule, the person having knowledge shall be entered under the column headed Keeper, and the entry shall not be construed as the creation of an office — and a man who is entered in that column, and who has written *and no other office* in the box underneath it, is a man who has refused an office in the only language the office has ever printed.

**So the office has decided that the cheapest way to get a keeper is to give him one.** He is not being recruited for what he can do. He is being recruited for what a form did to him, and the office has worked this out from its own paperwork, and it worked it out correctly, and he has been in this county eleven years and it is the first time in his life that a Crown document has been right about him for a reason that was about a form.

---

**The second thing was a fair copy of one leaf of a return, and it is the thing the offer is attached to, and it is the same leaf the Bellweather Claims Archive has had on its table for nine days, and the office on the other side of it does not know that the office on this side has it.**

*Object. Date. Person having knowledge. Keeper. How often.*

And in the margin, in a second hand, and the hand is an office's hand and the writing is recent:

*Fourth column relied upon as the office of record for the object. Return forwarded. No further returnable date.*

That is nine words and an office has built a county out of it.

Below the four columns there is a box. It says *State the office, if any*, and there is nothing in it, and there has never been anything in it, and the reason there is nothing in it is a sentence a woman said in a yard on Weir Street on the first of Embermonth of the year before and which is now the reason this leaf is four hundred miles wide instead of four inches.

And in the column headed *Person having knowledge*, in a county clerk's hand four hundred miles off:

*Aren Kest, road warden, in the roll of the Merefen bridge guild, and no other office.*

He put his hand over that line and then took it away again, and the reason he took it away is that he wanted to be sure of what was written and not of what it did, and a man who wants to be sure of what is written has to be able to see it.

**The entry beside the fourth column is a man who wrote *I do not keep this* on the back of the same leaf, two years before it, and meant it, and the office has had that leaf in a press on a shelf in a city two hundred miles off and has never turned it over.**

The third column is a different thing and it is worse. *And no other office* — the whole of it, in the order he is in it in, saying what he is and what he is not — was said in a yard on Weir Street in the rain, in a gateway, in front of four men, on the first of Embermonth of the year before, by a woman with a pen she had to unscrew from somewhere, at the seventh hour, to a man who did not want an office. He did not write it and she wrote it for him and she told him why. **It has been in print since that morning at the foot of every form of the series as the worked example of how a county is to fill a column, and it is on this leaf a second time in a third hand four hundred miles away, and nobody copied it from the original, because nobody in that office knows there is an original.**

And nobody in that office has read it, and he knows they have not, because a man who has read it does not write *No further returnable date.* A man who has read it stops. **And no other office** is the only line in this whole business that was ever written to be refused, and the refusal is exactly the thing that has not happened, because the office did not need a man to read it. The office needed a column.

---

**And the third thing was a single sheet folded twice, and it is not part of the offer, and it is in the same hand as the rest of her work and it is the only thing in the envelope with nothing official on it.**

*This is not the office's. It is mine, and it is dated today, and I have not put the date of it with the date of the offer because I do not want the two to have one date between them.*

*The offer was made out three years ago and has been sitting in a drawer in the fourth press with a number of persons against it that is not stated, and I found the drawer in the ninth month of last year because I was looking for something else, and I read it, and I recognised a man described in it from a description in a file, and the description in the file is a set of four columns, one of which he filled in himself.*

*I am going to send this to you and not to your bench, and I am going to tell you that I do not believe it is going to work, and I have not said so to my own office and I am not going to, and I would like it entered in whatever book you keep that I have said it.*

*Here is my reason and it is the only thing I have that is worth the paper.*

*An office that cannot get a name will get a figure. An office that cannot get a figure will get a rank. A man who takes the rank is a man who can be asked where a road is, and when he cannot say, the office will have a man who is bound by a record he has never read, and the record will be the strongest thing anybody has ever held about him, because he will not have read it either. That is not a threat. It is how a filing system works. I have watched it work on two men and one of them was a registrar and it took him eleven years to understand what had happened to him and he never once got the record back.*

*You have spent two years refusing to have a rank. I have read the returns and there are eleven of them and in nine of them you gave this office a number and in two of them you gave it nothing at all, and I know what a nothing costs an officer, because I have paid it out of my own eleven months twice, and both times I did it because somebody in a room told me it was standard.*

*Do not take it. I would like it minuted that I told you not to and that I do not think you will not.*

*Sabin Dain.*

He read that sheet four times, which was the most he had ever read anything of hers, and then he got a piece of paper and wrote a reply.

It was two lines long and it was the hardest thing he wrote that year, and it is in a notebook and not in a letter and the notebook says so:

*Thirteenth of Longlight, seventh hour. One question from this, and the question is the only one I have to ask her, and I am writing it down before I do anything else with the page, because a man who answers an offer before he has asked one question has already accepted it in his own head and is now choosing his words.*

*The question: how many of the roads the office calls the eastern paths does the office know I hold, and where did it get the phrase.*

*Because there is a category in that office called the eastern paths and it is older than me and it is in a file and it is not a count, and if the office has a category and not a count then it cannot act on me in particular, and if it acts on me in particular then somebody has been in a room and has looked at something, and I would rather know which of the two it is and I would rather know in a document.*

He did not send that. He wrote it and put it in the notebook and it is not the reply he sealed.

The reply he sealed was three lines and it is in a file in the provincial town and it may be read there in about nine years:

*Received. I am not able to answer on the eastern paths and I am not able to refuse them, because I do not know what they are.*

*I have this county's copy of a fourth column and I will send a copy of it and nothing else.*

*Road warden, Merefen.*

---

**And at about the ninth hour he went down to the yard on Weir Street and did not tell his brother that there had been a letter, and at about the eleventh hour he stood in a gateway and did not go up the two flights of a stair, and at about the sixth hour in the evening he did a thing he has never told anybody about doing, which is that he looked at the bottom of the form.**

There is a habit. He has never spoken of it to any living person. It is this: when a Crown sheet of any kind comes into his hands he turns it over, and if there is nothing on the back he runs a finger along the foot of the last column, because a column that has been ruled and left empty is a hole somebody made on purpose, and a hole somebody made on purpose is the only kind of writing that has never once been wrong.

He turned the enclosure over. The back of the fair copy was the office's own letterhead, and along the foot of it, under a rule, in the smallest hand on the page, was a line of office furniture that is on every sheet the office has ever printed:

*Retained. Series R.C. Eastern. Two classes.*

And he stood in a gateway on Weir Street and looked at that for longer than a man looks at a line of furniture, and then he went up his own stairs and did not write it down, and the reason he did not write it down is that a line like that in a notebook is a thing that has to be explained, and the only person he has to explain it to is four hundred yards away in a room over a weigh-house, and he has three things he is not going to explain to her and one of them is a letter that came this morning.

**He has had the words on the back of that form in front of him three times in his life. The first time was four years ago, in a shed in a county above this one, at about the fourth hour, because a man there could not read and brought the sheet to a yard and asked somebody in the yard, and the somebody was him, and he read it out and it meant nothing to him then and he wrote nothing down. The second was the first of Embermonth of the year before, in this yard, in the rain, with four men in it, and it took two women in two counties to get the first half of it as far as him and he got the second half on his own and only the second half. The third is this morning, in a gateway, on the back of a fair copy, and he has understood the office in it this morning and not the clause, and the office is not the clause, and there is a difference between understanding a thing and being able to use it and he does not yet know which of the two he has done.**

The notebook entry for the thirteenth of Longlight, YR 313, is four lines long, and the fourth of them is the one that is the whole of what he has got.

*Thirtieth of Greenmonth I asked a gateway in this county whether there was anything from a county I had never heard of, and I asked it for no reason, and it turned out to be the reason, and that is the second time this year that a question of mine with no reason under it has been the one that mattered. The first was a woman asking me about a woman in a clay county and not asking me about myself. I have a habit of looking at the bottom of a form and I have never told a living soul I have it, and this year it has found me two documents, and the reason it works is that a hole somebody made on purpose is the only writing that has never lied to me, and I have never once in thirty-two years been able to work out whether that is a good thing to be or a bad thing to be.*
