# Chapter 0118

The steps up out of the road were clay, cut into a bank, and there were nine of them and the fourth one had gone soft, and the fourth hour is not a morning anybody starts a thing in and he started it on the fourth step because it was the only dry thing within a mile and he had been on his feet since the second hour.

The country came up at him from the bottom. It was not a country of fires. In four years and a half he had crossed into four places and three of them had wanted a fire kept in it, and this one wanted the water read twice a day, and the smell off it was a river in spate in the wrong part of the year, and there was a smell of turned hay under the spate which is a smell a fen county knows and this is not a fen county.

Rolling country, eleven villages, a market town with a low roofed hall on a tithe barn, household fields and commons and a ditch system cut in a grid that a man could see from where he was standing, and every one of the ditches in that grid was straight, and straight ditches are made by men who are not in a hurry, and one of them had been moved.

**And the thing that took the whole of the first hour was not the country. It was that there was no count of it.**

He has been into four places in four years and a half and three of them wanted something counted — a fever, a list of hearths, a column, a roll of persons with a figure at the foot of it — and this one wanted the water read twice a day, and there was no register of it, and no roll, and no return, and no office in this kingdom that a return out of this county could go to.

A county that is not counted is a county that can still be described, and a county that is counted is a county that has become a figure, and he had spent nine days of the last year in the second kind and four hours in the first, and the difference between the two is why he came, and he could not put it in a book because it is a paragraph and not a fact.

A man in a field will tell you how many houses are in a parish, out loud, in nine seconds, and every one of them will be wrong by a different amount, and not one of them will be frightened of being wrong, because being wrong about a number you were never given is not a thing anybody can be got at. He wrote one line of that down and crossed it out, because a man nine hours into a country four hundred miles off does not get to write paragraphs about a country.

He got the book out on the fourth step at about nine minutes past the fourth hour and made up the nine in nine minutes, and seven of them took him about four seconds each, and the two he could not took him the rest of the time and neither of them got a figure, and he wrote the hour against each one because the trade says to and because he has never once been able to find a reason not to.

*One. The line. It is the ditch at the third stile out of the market town where the grid turns and turns back, because that is the only length of water in this country that anybody has ever put a road across. **Entered at the fourth hour, and the line is a ditch, and a ditch in this county is a boundary, and I have been brought four hundred miles to look at a boundary and I have been telling myself for a fortnight that I was coming about a road.***
*Two. The hand at this end. **Coll Ferrow**, first hand, nineteen men, one of them his brother-in-law, and he states his terms before he is asked and he has said them twice and both times they have been better than anything I have brought. Entered at the fourth hour and nine minutes.*
*Three. The hand at the other end. One of my brother's four, and his name is not a thing that goes in a yard's book because the yard's rule is that nothing belonging to a man goes in the yard's book and only a thing set, and so I have not got it and I am not going to get it out of a man by asking him for it. He is on a road. He went on the day the Marches named, and I am on a day they have not, and a man can be a hand at an end and be four hundred miles short of it at the wrong hour, and that is not an argument, it is a calendar.*
*Four. The date. **Not entered.***
*Five. The hour. The hour is entered against every one of the nine and the hour is the only column of that book that is full.*
*Six. A witness at this end who is not the man who keeps the road. There is one. He was asked in a gateway in a fen county and asked for it in the yard's book in my county's hand and not his own, because a clerk four hundred miles off copied his writing down once and not the daylight, and everything that came of it came of the copy.*
*Seven. A witness at that end who is not the man who keeps that road. Chosen by them. He is here. He has not said one word in four minutes and he does not intend to and he does not have to, because they chose him and the fourth of the six is the price and the sixth is the door, and both of those are shut in this county's own words and I did not write them.*
*Eight. The book at this end. I have it. Eleven inches by eight, four columns and no column for a name, and the fifth column under a line for the hour, and I ruled it on the back of a cost sheet in a room over a cooper because there was nothing else of a size.*
*Nine. The book at that end. **Not entered. I cannot enter it. There is no instrument in this kingdom that will let a man lay a sheet of paper on that bench over there, and the county has no office in it at all, and that is not an insult to the county, it is the reason the sixth line exists and the reason the copy of the six lines in a book in my county is a thing a court can have and this one is not.***

He shut it and looked at the fourth line, which is the one with nothing against it, and the fourth of nine is the date.

---

And a woman of thirty-eight of Sallow Marsh came up from the low ground to the fourth step and stood about two yards off and did not come closer, which is a thing a person does when she has decided to wait to be spoken to.

"You have been on that step eleven minutes," said Thessa Wend. "There is a bench at the weir and the water is in spate and the weir is running and you are going to want to stand somewhere that is not going to be under you by noon, and that is not a courtesy, it is a fact about the ground, and you will find out the way you find out everything in this county, which is by putting your hand on it."

**And then she said the thing that the whole of the fourth hour of the first day of a crossing in this case is about, and she said it without stopping, and she had been in the county all her life and had not said it in that room before, and the reason she said it to him was not politeness. It was that he had a book out and a book is a thing a person in this county is allowed to say a number into without owing anybody anything afterwards.**

**"And the sixth of the six is this: the line is not to be opened on a day the Marches have not named. And the Marches named a day, and they named it by saying when four men of a yard will be on their road, and the four men went on a day and you came on a different one, and there is no arithmetic here and there is no arithmetic anywhere."**

"Then I am a day late," said Aren Kest.

"You are a day late, and so is the day you came on, and both of those are the same fact. A county that has kept a promise in a field a hundred and forty times keeps a day the way it keeps a day. **It is not a thing that is short of you. You are not a man it is waiting for. You are a man who has come on a day nobody agreed to come on, and the whole of the difference is that nobody here is going to be unkind about it, because a county that was unkind about days would not have lasted a hundred and forty.**"

**"Then there is no road," said Aren Kest.**

**"There is a line and two hands and a witness at each end and a book at your end," said Thessa Wend, "and there is no road, and a day is the only part of it that is theirs, and you have the one part you cannot make."**

"You have written seven of them," she said.

"I have written seven of them."

"Then you have all of it but the one line, and I have watched a man try to make seven of them into nine for about nine minutes and I have not helped him, because nobody has ever asked me anything and I have wanted somebody to ask me for eleven years and I am not going to start with a man who is going to write things down."

And at about the fifth hour he did the one thing he has told nobody in this book he would do, which is that he called the second road, once, and he has called it perhaps forty times in four years and a half and it has not answered since the first of a month two years ago, and this time it did not answer either, and the difference is that this time he was on the far side of something else and not in his own county and had a reason to expect nothing and got it.

Nothing. Not a refusal, because a refusal is an answer with a person in it. Not a *not kept*, because a not kept would have a date on it and a *not kept* is still a thing somebody did.

There is no procedure for a far side that has not decided. He has known that for two years and has written it down twice and there is nothing under either entry, and he wrote the rule on the page above the nine, in his own hand, in the ordinary way, because a man who has been let down by a road forty times is entitled to one line about it:

Nobody in either country is to say the word about it. Not afterwards, not kindly, not in a letter, not in a book, not in a minute, not in nine years. A road that does not open on a day is a day that was not opened. That is the whole of the fact and there is no other fact, and the moment a person puts a word on it, the word is a thing somebody chose, and the thing somebody chose is a thing somebody can be shown.

And under it, in the same hand, one line, and it is the only line in that book that is not about a road:

*A road with one end is a length of string.*

He wrote the hour against the page as well, because the trade says to, and the hour was the only column of that book that was going to be full by the end of the week, and everything else in it was a thing four hundred miles off or a thing nobody had named yet.

He came up with nothing.

Not a refused road, not an unkept road, not a road anybody had said anything about. At about the seventh hour the woman of thirty-eight read the weir post and said eleven at the weir post and nine at the Sallow foot, and wrote both in a book in a hut at a weir that has eight thousand and thirty-one lines in it, and nobody has ever asked her for one, and the council has never looked at it, and it is the only account of this county's water that there is.

**And the rest of those nine days happen without one.**
