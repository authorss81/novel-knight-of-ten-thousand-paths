# Chapter 0115

The thirteenth of Rainmonth, YR 313, was a Wednesday and it was not a market day, and on that Wednesday a road warden of the Merefen bridge guild spent the day doing the one kind of work there is that never appears in a roll: he got a thing ready, and the getting ready took three days and about two marks and twelve pence, and a bench of nine that has been asked four times and has said yes four times paid for it out of a chest that had two marks in it on the thirtieth of Greenmonth, which is the whole of what was in that chest, and the reeve asked that the minute carry that as well.

**And the day is in the yard's book and the book is the yard's and the column is footed on the page before anybody says the total, because a yard does not say a total it has not added, and Tovan Kest does not add in his head on a wet morning, and the man at the drawknife is twenty-nine and has been in that yard since he was eleven and does all of the arithmetic and writes it down twice.**

*Thirteenth of Rainmonth, YR 313. The line at the north end. What it cost to be ready.*

*Four hands, three days, at this yard's rate of eight pence a day a hand: thirty-two pence a day, and thirty-two for three days is ninety-six.*
*The boy, three days, at four pence a day, being half, and he is fifteen and he copies the empty space under a number as well as the number: four pence a day, and four for three days is twelve.*
*Ninety-six and twelve is one hundred and eight pence.*
*Which is nine shillings. Which is two marks and twelve pence.*

"And that is what it costs," said the man at the drawknife, "to have a thing ready that is not going to be used for a fortnight. Forty yards of tarred hemp would be another two shillings and I have got forty yards of hemp out of a cooper's bin and it cost me nothing and I am not going to put it in a county's minute at nothing, and if the minute wants the hemp it can have the hemp at what I paid for the bin, which was a pot of beer, and that is a bad rate and I am putting it in."

The reeve's minute carries two marks and twelve pence and carries the beer as well, in the clerk's hand, with the hour, and it is the first time in the roll of that hall that a beer has been an item.

---

And the book itself has been ruled since the eleventh of the month before, in a room over a cooper, on the back of a cost sheet because there was nothing else of a size. Eleven inches by eight. Four columns and no headings, and a fifth under a ruled line and narrower than the other four, and no column for a name, and a boy of fifteen at a bench-board has the rule of the fifth one by heart and can say all five without looking.

What the three days went on was the line itself: forty yards of tarred hemp off a cooper's bin laid along the parapet of the Old Toll and chalked at four places, and the four marks on the parapet where the two ends of it stop being this side, and a day of a man at the drawknife with a bar putting the two ends of the hemp into the abutment where they will not be a thing anybody can pull.

The boy copied the four marks and the four lengths and the hour. And then he copied the nine as well, out of the back of the notebook, because a list of nine things is a thing set and a thing set goes in this book, and the man at the drawknife read it, and put his thumb on the fourth item and the ninth item, and said nothing, and did not copy them.

"Which of those can you make at this end," said the man at the drawknife.

"Seven," said Aren Kest. "**The date and the book at that end. Everything else I can make with a nib and a rule and a man who is not the man who keeps the road.** And the date is the sixth line, and I have got a sheet four hundred miles off that names a day for four men of a yard and does not name a day for me, and the book at that end is in a county that has no office in it, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will let me lay a sheet of paper on that bench."

And the witness at this end was fixed on the thirteenth, in a gateway on Weir Street, in about a minute and a half, and the man at the drawknife was in the gateway and heard both halves of it and has entered the fact of it in the yard's book with the hour against it and not one word of the reason in it, which is what that book has done for four years.

Bram Ottery. A watch sergeant, and the watch of this county is a thing with a captain and a stove and a commendation on the wall, and the sergeant is not the captain and has no office and has been in that house eleven years, and he was the witness at this end on the ninth of Embermonth of the year before at the fourth hour for a road out of this county, and he was paid fourpence a week out of a private box, and he did not draw it, and he said then and says now that the yard's book is not to have it.

He said two things in the gateway and the second one is the reason he was asked and not anybody else.

"First: I will stand at that end on the day and I will be a man off a bridge and not a man in a guild, and if the man who keeps the road at this end is in a guild then I am the wrong man and I will not do it."

"You are the right man," said Aren Kest, "because I am on the roll of the guild and I do not keep any road in this county, and if the man at this end is me then a witness at this end is a witness who can be asked what he thinks of me, and I am not going to have that in a line four hundred miles long."

"Second," said Bram Ottery, "the last time I did this I did it in daylight in a market and I wrote down that I had been in daylight in a market, and a clerk four hundred miles off copied the writing down and not the daylight, and everything that came of it came of the copy. **So I want it in your book in this county's hand and not in mine, and I want it in the book before I am asked and not after.**"

It went into the yard's book at about the tenth hour on the thirteenth of Rainmonth, and the boy at the bench-board copied it out with the hour, and the entry is a fact and not a want, and the man at the drawknife put the fourpence a week in the same page and did not draw it and did not enter it either, and both of those things are the yard's rule and the yard's rule is nineteen years old and is painted on a wall in the hand of a man who is dead: **nothing that belongs to a man goes in this yard's book, and only a thing set.**

And in the afternoon he cut a dressed block for the Old Toll parapet at the bench's own cost, and the bench has been asked four times for the Old Toll and has been given two marks and a book and the fourth asking took five minutes, and this block is the first thing anybody in this town has cut for a crossing in about a year and there is no money for the second one and everybody in the guild knows the date of the day there will be no money for it, which is when a man at the wharf says it.

He dressed it on the trestles and did not charge for it and entered nothing about it, because a block is not a cost and a cost is what the hour against it is for.

And a peer of the wharf road was on the deck with a bar he was not using and watched him, the way a man watches a thing he has decided to be able to do himself one day, and after a while said: "You will be out of this county in nine days and there is a man in your brother's yard who has been putting a bar through oak for eleven years and he does it better than you and you know he does it better than you."

"He does it better than me," said Aren Kest.

"So you have told him," said the peer of the wharf road.

"I have never told him that in my life and I did not tell him today and I am not going to," said Aren Kest, "and it is not modesty, it is that the one time I said a thing like that to him he took it as a thing that needed answering, and he was right to, and it cost him about a year."

**And the peer of the wharf road wrote that down on the back of a tally-board in his own hand, and it is in a yard's book in this town, and it is a fact about a man and not about a brother, and there are now two pages in two books in this town that are about the two of them and neither of them has ever been shown to the other.**

And on the same afternoon a sheet went into the bag for the provincial town, two hundred miles off, and it is in this county's records and in nobody else's, and it is the third sheet that man has received from a Crown office in four and a half years and the first one he has sent back, and there is no returnable date on it and no claim number on it and no copy of it in this county, and none of those three facts is an accident.

It is a quarter of a page. It says that a warden's rank is a command and not a custody, and that a man appointed by a community may refuse and a man appointed by a person may not, and that this is not an argument against the offer but a statement of what the office is, and that a sealed record of a person is a record a person cannot contradict, and that this office has spent four years and a half learning that difference in a county that does not have the office and in a woman who keeps its records.

Then, in the middle of the page, two sentences that neither accept nor refuse anything:

*I am not able to say that I will not take it, and I am not able to say that I will. Both of those would be a promise made by a man who does not know what the eastern paths are and cannot find out from anybody in this county, and a promise made by a man who does not know what it is for is a claim and not a record, and I have had four years of learning what the difference is between those two words from a woman who is better at it than I am.*

*If the offer is held open, it will be held open. I am going out of this county on the twenty-second and I do not know for how long, and a man who is out of a county cannot be asked a question by anybody in it, and I would rather that were on your file in my own words than arrive at later in somebody else's.*

And under that, in the same hand, the sentence he took the best part of the whole two years to write, and it took about eleven seconds, and he wrote it the way he writes a load path: because he had got to the end of it and there was nowhere left to stand.

*A word on what the office is buying. The form of that series carries at its foot, in a smaller type, the office's own worked example of how a county is to fill a column headed Keeper where no office of keeper is found in the county schedule, and the example is a real name in a real guild, and I wrote in it, four years ago, on a bench in a yard, *and no other office*, and I meant it, and it went up, and a clerk four hundred miles off read the foot of a form on a wet day with a quill and made a man answerable for a road he cannot refuse, and a delegation came four hundred miles about it, and a man of sixty gave away six hundred and forty-eight pence in a gateway for a reason about a promise.*

*I am not going to say to you that the office is wicked, because the office is an office and it has done the ordinary thing its own desk did and the desk has never once had to check anything. I am going to say that the thing the office wants from me is not the thing I can do, and that the thing I can do is a bridge, and that a person who has been made out of a word by a printed example is going to be extremely good at the thing that is in his own hands and is going to be asked about a thing that is not.*

*That is not a refusal and it is not an acceptance. It is a description of a hand.*

And it is in the bag, and the bag goes on the Tuesday, and nobody in this county knows it was written, and it is the fourth thing in a notebook in a coat that he has told nobody about, and the list of six in that notebook has a seventh on it now and the seventh is a woman two hundred miles off who wrote to him first, and a list of people you have told nothing to is not the same thing as a list of people you have not told, and he has known that since the ninth of Longlight and has not entered it, and it is the one item on that page he is not going to enter.

**And at about the seventh hour that evening he went down Weir Street with the book in his coat, and the book is eleven inches by eight and it has four columns and no column for a name, and a road out of this county is a line and two hands and a date and a witness at each end, and he can make seven of the nine, and the two he cannot are a day and a book in a chamber over a tithe barn four hundred miles off in a county that has no office in it at all, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will let him lay a sheet of paper on that bench, and the sixth line says he may not open it on a day that county has not named, and the county named a day for four men of a yard and it is the fifteenth, and he goes on the twenty-second.**
