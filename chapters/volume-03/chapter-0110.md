# Chapter 0110

The thirtieth of Longlight, YR 313, was a Thursday and a market day, and the thirtieth of the month before it had been a Tuesday, and a man who counts things for a living can get from one of those to the other in about four seconds and will tell anybody who asks that this county keeps a thirty-day month and that it is the only thing in the calendar he has never once had to check, and that he has been out of this county a hundred and fourteen days, and that the printed figure at the foot of the terms in an office's hand has not moved in a year.

The market ran in one street with the water on the left of it. The Sedge Reach had been open to carts for a month and the pointing on the third bay was finished and hard and the Sedge Reach was a bridge that was holding, and there was no business in Merefen on that Thursday that anybody could name as urgent, which is the condition in which a county makes a decision and does not notice it has made one.

---

**The yard on Weir Street had a sheet of the Hearth Marches' paper on its bench-board, and the sheet had been accepted on the twenty-seventh, and on the thirtieth it was still lying face up on the bench because it was going out on the Saturday.**

Tovan Kest had signed it and the boy at fifteen had copied the fifth line, the one with no figure on it, without being told to, because that is what the boy does, and the four lines with figures on them had been standing on that board since the twenty-third.

"It is not signed by them yet," said Tovan Kest, to nobody in particular, and the man at the drawknife said:

"It is signed by us and it is in a bag on Saturday and it is a hundred and eleven days since I have read a word of a contract and this is the first one I have ever been frightened of, and I could not tell you one thing about that county, and I have not asked."

"You have asked me three things this week," said Tovan Kest.

"I have asked you three things and you have answered two," said the man at the drawknife, "and the one you have not answered is the one I am frightened of, and it is the boy."

**And the boy was not going, and that had been settled in that yard since before the contract was read and had never once been argued, and on the thirtieth Tovan Kest said it out loud for the first time, in a yard, in front of four men, and it is in the week-book with no figure against it.**

"He is fifteen. He is the best copier I have ever had and I have had two. He does not go. He stays and he does the stone and he copies."

"A charter of their own," said Aren Kest from the doorway. "You said the word in that hall on the ninth and I have been turning it over for three weeks."

"That is what I mean," said Tovan Kest. "Four hundred miles there and four hundred back and a fortnight of the road, and I have not been able to work out whether I have sent four men and a boy or four men. I have sent four men and it is on the sheet in four hands and I am not going to have his name under it, and that is not a figure and there is no line for it and I am not going to make one."

---

**And at about the third hour a road warden of the Merefen bridge guild went down Weir Street with a tool bag and turned in at a gate and did not go in, and then went on to the wharf and stood at the rail for a while.**

He had a thing in his notebook that was not a decision and he had been carrying it since the ninth of Longlight and it had got heavier rather than lighter in three weeks, and what it was was this:

*Whether I can open a road to a county four hundred miles off that has never been on a map in this kingdom.*

*And it cannot be done from this room, and the reason it cannot be done from this room is on a board four feet by two and a half that came in a wagon, and the board says, in the second line, that no name is to be read out on the far side of the line by anybody from the north and no name is to be written in the line's book at the north end, and in the fifth line that the road is kept by a hand at each end and a witness at each end and the witness at the Marches' end is chosen by the Marches.*

**A road is a line and two hands and a date and a witness at each end, and if the witness at an end is not there on the day, the road does not open and it does not fail, and nobody afterwards is to say that it failed.**

And the delegation went home on the eighteenth on a calendar of their own, and eleven days in a waggon is eleven days on their road and God knows how many on mine, and a witness at the Marches' end is chosen by the Marches, and I am four hundred miles off and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will let me name anybody to anybody, and I am not allowed to name anybody in that line's book at this end anyway.

**So I have a list of things that would have to be true, and the first of them is that a man at that end holds a road, and I do not know of one, and the only person I have ever met who holds a road at an end I cannot see is a tile-cutter of forty-one whom I have spoken to three times and asked nothing, and she is four hundred miles the other way and there is no line between her county and this one.**

He wrote that down, and then he wrote the honest second half, which is the half that a notebook is for:

*And this is not a decision and I am not going to let it become one in a book, because a decision written down in a book by a man with a notebook and a tool bag is a thing that other men find and act on, and I have spent two years watching other men find things I wrote down.*

*What it is: a list of six questions, of which I can answer two.*

*One. Is there a man at that end who holds a length of water. There is: a guild of eighteen men, and its first hand, and I have spoken with him, and he is a hand and not a keeper and he says his terms first.*
*Two. Will he hold the road. I do not know and I did not ask him, and the reason I did not ask him is that a man who has just lost an argument about a bushel of seed is a bad man to ask whether he will stand at the end of a line for ever, and I lost that argument, in my brother's yard, in a gateway, and he won it, and he won it in front of four men and he did it politely.*
*Three. Who is the witness at that end. Unknown, and not mine to name.*
*Four. What is a day to them. **Unknown. I cannot convert a rolling of theirs into a day of this calendar and there is not one of those in the other, and I have tried it twice on paper and both times the answer came out as a number with no meaning and I threw the paper away both times.***
*Five. What does the office that made me a keeper in a form call this road, and where did it get the word. Unknown, and asked in a letter sealed on the thirteenth and not answered.*
*Six. Whether a man from the north is permitted to stand at the end of a line at all. **The board answers that, and the answer is in the third line, and the third line is that the north is not to send anybody who can be asked to come and look, and I have read that line eleven times in three weeks and I do not know whether I am the man it is about, and I am not going to ask anybody in this county whether I am, because a man who asks that is a man who already knows.***

---

**And the last thing he put in that book, and it is the page that the whole of two years is standing behind, and he wrote it in the evening at the room over the cooper with a lamp and no light in the window.**

*Six people, and I have told none of them, and here is the list, and I am writing it down because a man who writes a list of people he has not told is a man who knows exactly what he has done and a man who does not is a man who has a relief.*

*Not the keeper of records, who has a copy of the fourth column of a return in her press and does not know that an office has relied on it, and who wrote a rule this month about an authority nobody can name the source of, and who has not been told that the authority is a sentence printed at the foot of a form with my name in it as the example. If I tell her, she will enter it, and the entering of it puts my name in a document in this county that a Crown's man may read in about nine years, and she would do it because she is not a woman who can be told a thing and leave it out of a book. **I am asking a woman with a filing system to keep a thing out of a book. That is the sentence and it took me three weeks to be able to write it down.***
*The magistrate, who has spent a fortnight being frightened of a sentence she made herself on the ninth of Greenmonth, and who has a paper on her bench about a boundary held by a body, and who would ask one question and get one answer and use both of them.*
*The reeve, who voted for the office on the ninth of Greenmonth and who would want it minuted that she voted for it, and who is right to.*
*The bench, which has been asked four times and has said yes four times and is not a bench, and which would have to be told that a county four hundred miles off has read a form and relied on a column, and which has two marks in a chest.*
*My brother, who has a fifth line in his own book with an hour against it and no figure on it, about a date I gave him in the wrong calendar, and who does not know I know what the date is a date of, and who does not know that I have a page of my own, and who is about to be four hundred miles off with four men for ninety days and a bag of salt.*
*The woman in the third press, who has sent the keeper of records a whole leaf of a return and told her what it cost her to send it and did not send a price.*

**Six. And the reason I have told none of them is not a reason I have chosen and it is the best reason anybody in this case has ever had, and it came out of a fair copy of four lines in a minute of a bench in a county four hundred miles the other way, and I have never met the woman who wrote it and I have read it perhaps thirty times:**

*we did not lose our register, we lost our refusal, a register is what you have instead.*

**A county that was cut off has no office. A line written in a county with no office cannot be served, because there is nobody in it to serve it on, and cannot be answered, because an answer has to arrive somewhere, and cannot travel, because there is no road at that end that anybody can put a date on. And that is why a promise kept a hundred and forty times in a field is the only durable thing anybody in that countryside has: it is the only record in the world that does not need an office to be true, because the men who swore it are still alive and can be asked.**

**And I have had that answer in my own book since the first of Embermonth of the year before, and it has never once told me why the second route does not answer, and I have stopped asking it, and that is the part of the habit that a rule about authority cannot reach.**

**And the reason nobody in this county knows that is the same sentence. And the reason it has been working for nineteen years and four and a half years and has never once been found out is the fourth half of it, which took me about a minute and which I am putting in this book in capitals because it is the whole of what I have learned this year and it is not a thing anybody can do anything about: AN INSTRUMENT THAT DOES NOT WORK IS NEVER HEARD FROM.**

*There is no failure anywhere in this. There is a county that cannot be served and a file that cannot be produced and a form that cannot be got at and a road that does not open on anybody's day. Every one of those is working exactly as printed. Nothing has gone wrong. **Nothing has gone wrong is the condition a thing is in when it has been doing harm for a long time, and the only way anybody ever finds out about it is when one person stands up in a room and refuses, and a refusal has to arrive somewhere, and there is nowhere in that county for it to arrive.***

*So I have told nobody, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will make one of them ask me, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will make me a person who asks.*

*And the one thing I can do is a list. I have got six questions and I can answer two of them and I have got a tool bag and a notebook and a bench of nine that is not a bench and a brother who is going to be four hundred miles off on Saturday with four men and no coin.*

---

*A road held, three weeks. — printed on the reverse of a form in an office's hand, in a smaller type, as a standard of measurement for the return, and the office cannot say whose figure it is, and the figure is out of my own notebook, and I have never seen it in a book anybody else will read, and there is nothing under the foot of the sheet.*

*Thirtieth of Longlight, YR 313, the fifth hour, a Thursday, a market day. The thirtieth of a month, and a month ago the thirtieth was a Tuesday, and I have been out of this county one hundred and fourteen days, and the printed figure has not moved.*

*Six questions. Two answers. A bag of salt going four hundred miles on Saturday with four men in it and a boy who is not going and a yard that has a contract and not an acceptance, because the other end has not said yes yet, and a contract accepted by one end of a line is not a contract.*

*It is a thing that is not a decision. I have not written it as one. I have written it as a list, and a list can only be checked, and I cannot check any of it from here, and on the twenty-second of a month I do not have a name for I am going to find out whether a road opens at all.*

---

**And at about the fourth hour and a half on the thirtieth of Longlight, YR 313, the bell went on the Old Toll bridge four hundred yards up that road, and it should not have, and everybody in this county knows on which day it goes.**

It is a bell with a band of forty-one rivets round it and a dull red seam in the metal where the band was closed, and it is rung by a boy of sixteen with two years of an apprenticeship who is still not good at it, holding one end of a line, and beside him a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper and has a bar he is not using.

The arrangement is a Monday. It has been a boy and a ferrywright since the twenty-fourth of Fallowmonth and a boy and a carpenter since the ninth of Hearthmonth, and it is a Monday because that is the day the far end of the line keeps, and the whole of the arrangement is in a page at the head of the yard's book for the week with the hour against it in a hand somebody would know, and nobody wrote a word in it about who rang the bell and nobody has written a word in it about who arranged it.

**The arrangement is not Aren's.** And on the thirtieth of Longlight the carpenter said the day was wrong, and the boy said he knew it was wrong, and the carpenter asked him what he was doing it for, and the boy said the wharf was loud and he wanted to see whether the far end heard it on the wrong day, and the carpenter said that was not a reason and then stood there and held the line anyway, because a man who has been a hand for nineteen years holds a line that a boy is on.

They rang it because that is the arrangement, and also because a boy of sixteen decided to find something out, and the two of those things happened on the same morning in the same four minutes and neither of them is in the page at the head of the yard's book, and it is not going to be written in it.

**And at the far end of the line, four hundred miles off, on a coping on a river this county does not have, nothing was struck, because that is a Monday arrangement and this was not a Monday.** Two people stood on a coping and waited and then went in, and one of them is a tile-cutter of forty-one and one of them keeps the road, and neither of them has ever been asked a question about it by anybody, and what the clerk of that assizes copied out about it is the same sentence they copied out in Fallowmonth and will copy out again, and it is not a bell and it is not nothing.

Aren Kest stood at the rail on the wharf with his tool bag at his feet and listened to the whole of it, and did not go up the road, and did not ring it, and nothing in the sound was his, and for the first time in three months nothing at the far end of it was anybody's either.
