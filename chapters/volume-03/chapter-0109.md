# Chapter 0109

The twenty-seventh of Longlight, YR 313, was a Monday and it was not a market day, and Captain Rell sent for him at about the third hour, which is the hour a watch does nothing in a town this size and everybody in the town knows it.

The watch house had a bench, a stove, a table with a lamp on it that was not lit, a board with four nails in it and three on the bench, and a commendation on the wall in a frame with a glass front and a dust line across the middle of it where somebody had taken it down and put it up again.

"Sit," said Rell. "There is a sheet come to this house and I want a man to read it to me who will not sell me anything."

He read it. It was four lines of a form of the Office of Provincial Registration, and the fourth line asked this watch to confirm, from its own knowledge, the name of the person entered in the fourth column of a return held in a provincial file, on the ground that a person having the care of a road in a county is a person whose movements the watch of any county may follow.

"There is no power in that," said Aren Kest, "and there is no officer in this kingdom who can put a watch to any such thing, and if you answer it you will be writing a name into a Crown's hand in a county that has not had a name written in a Crown's hand since the twenty-seventh of Fallowmonth, and you will be doing it for a form that a woman in a filing room four hundred yards off beat in nine minutes on the ninth of Greenmonth with the form's own construction."

"I know there is no power in it," said Captain Rell. "That is not why I sent for you."

"Then say the reason."

Rell looked at him for a while. He was fifty and had a commendation on his wall and nothing in this county that anybody could be asked about, and he had been in this room for eleven years in a town where everybody knew what he had done on the eleventh of Mudmonth eleven years ago, and he had never once asked anybody anything.

"I have been a captain twenty-six years," he said, "and in twenty-six years I have never been able to answer a form. Not one. There is nothing in this kingdom you can put in front of me that I can put a name to, because I have never had anything to do with a name." He put his thumb on the sheet. "And this one says there is a man four hundred miles off keeping a road, and if I write *confirmed* I do not know one thing about him, and if I write *not confirmed* I am saying he does not keep it, and I do not know that either. **I want it out of my house.**"

Aren Kest did not enjoy it and neither did Rell, and the whole of the rest of it took about four minutes.

"Write this," he said. "Write: *This watch has no knowledge of the matter and no power to obtain any, and returns the sheet unaltered.*"

"That is a form answer. I have had four hundred of those from that office and I could write it in my sleep."

"Yes. And it is the correct answer, and it is the answer your clerk of the ninth of Greenmonth gave to a count of persons, and the reason it is correct is that a thing against which nobody is in default is not refused and is not enforced and is therefore spent the moment it is answered." He stood up. "And the reason I am telling you this for nothing is that the man whose name is in that column has been asking for two years for a thing nobody will give him, and he is a seed-carter and he is not in this county, and you are the only office in this kingdom that could put a name in a hand and take one out, and you are going to do the correct thing instead, and I would like you to know that I know what that is worth."

Rell wrote it. It took eleven seconds.

"That is the last of it," he said, when the ink was dry, and he did not say thank you, and Aren Kest did not stay, and neither of them mentioned the eleventh of Mudmonth or a bell or a stair, and the two of them had been in a room together three times in eleven years and this was the third, and it was the last of them, and both of them knew it while it was happening.

**The leaf had come up the hill on the fourth, twenty-three days ago, in a bag with two other things in it, and it was sent on the first of the month, and the request Mara Vey sent back the other way went out on the second, and the two of them crossed in the bag, and neither of the two women will ever know which of them was in a hurry.**

*I am sending the leaf. I did not wait for your request, which went out on the second, and my leaf went on the first, and there is no post in this kingdom that will tell either of us what that means, and I have decided that I do not want to know.*

It is a whole leaf. Not a page, not a copy of the page, and not a leaf with the ruled cells at the foot left off. A copyist who leaves the blanks off is a copyist who has made an argument, and the woman who copied this one has been copying for thirty-one years and her grandmother before her for forty, and a whole leaf is a whole leaf.

*Object: the Water, and the ditch system of the four lower villages, and the standing water in the low ground at Sallow Marsh.*
*Date: in the second week of the rolling that begins with the low water, in a year the county keeps from the first sowing.*
*Person having knowledge: **Aren Kest, road warden, in the roll of the Merefen bridge guild, and no other office.***
*Keeper: **Oswin Rill, seed-carter, of Nether Dray in this county.***
*How often: twice a day, and the same man has not read this line before he wrote it.*

And in the margin, in a second hand, and recent, and the hand is an office's hand:

*Fourth column relied upon as the office of record for the object. Return forwarded. No further returnable date.*

And **on the back of the leaf, which is the ordinary paper of a village and not the office's own, in a third hand, in a small careful hand, and dated two years before the return:**

*I do not keep this. I have not kept this. I did not put my name in the fourth column. I told the man it was wrong and he said it had to be full or the office would fill it. Put it back and tell them I said so. — O. Rill.*

---

Mara Vey read the whole of it at about the sixth hour of the twenty-seventh of Longlight with the door on the lane open and the market noise not there, because it was a Monday and there was no market, and she read the front and then she read the back, and then she read the back again, and then she sat with her hands flat on the table for the length of a psalm.

**There are two hands on that leaf about one man and one of them is his.**

The one in the fourth column is a claim. It is in a hand that is not his, it was put there by a stranger with a printed sheet and a bag of string, and it says he keeps a road, and it is a sentence about a person that the person cannot contradict, because the person was never asked and the office has never heard of the objection and will never hear of it, and the county's minute cannot be served and there is no Crown's hand in that county at all.

The one on the back is a record. It is in his own hand, it is dated, it is signed, it is addressed to nobody, it says what he is not, and it says what the office was told and by whom, and it is the only thing in the whole of this business that a man has ever written about himself in a form designed to prevent it.

**And a record a person can contradict is a record of that person. And a record that cannot be contradicted is a claim about that person. And that sentence has been arrived at four times in four years and a half by four people in two countries, and the first time it was arrived at it was about a fired clay tile, and it has never once been printed on a form, and the office that printed this leaf has a column headed Keeper and a line in a margin in a third hand that nobody has ever read.**

And a third press in a city two hundred miles off has had that leaf on a shelf and has sent it, entire, to a keeper of records in a fen county, because the woman who keeps the third press has been reading a press register every working day since she was thirty-one and she said, in the fourth part of a letter on the first of this month, the only sentence anybody has said in four years that this office could not improve on: *a register answers what was entered on the shelf and nothing else.*

The whole leaf came out of the shelf because a woman's grandmother was a copyist. That is the entire mechanism of this case and there is nothing in it but a habit and a shelf and a grandmother, and no instrument in two countries can be pointed at any of the three.

---

**And she entered it, and the entry is the ordinary entry with the date and the hour and the claim number against nothing, and there is a name on it, and there has not been a name in that book for four months.**

*Twenty-seventh of Longlight, YR 313, seventh hour. Fair copy of a whole leaf, being a return of the R.C. series of a border county, made by a copyist of thirty-one years in the third press, provincial town, sent unasked and held in this room twenty-three days, entered entire including two ruled cells at the foot with nothing in them. Fourth column contains a name in a hand that is not the hand of the man, and the back of the leaf contains the same man's hand, dated two years earlier, saying he did not put it there. Entered against a person, with the hour, and entered with the claim number against nothing, because the office is not a party in this county and the county is not a party in this office and a village is not a person.*

Then she came to the part of the entry that is the entry.

*And entered, against no person, and entered separately because it is a different kind of fact: that this office has had this leaf for five days and has not sent it to anybody, and that the reason is four words long and is on the page, and is this: **there is nobody to serve.***

*Not to the office, which cannot be sued and cannot be refused and cannot be injured, and which would put a name in a hand. Not to the man, who is four hundred miles off in a county that is not on any map and whose road is not kept on a day anybody here can name. Not to the delegation, which was in this county eighteen days ago and went home on the eighteenth on a calendar of its own, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that will tell me whether it got there.*

*And entered: that a keeper of records in this county has one instrument and it is a press that cannot be served, and that this is the first time in four years and a half that the office has had something it could actually keep, and that keeping it will not help Oswin Rill, and that the reason it is being kept is that a leaf with a man's own hand on the back of it is the only kind of writing that has ever come into this room that a person could contradict, and this office exists to hold things a person could contradict, and it is four hundred years too late to be a press and it is the best instrument in the kingdom.*

She entered it, and she put the leaf in the press with a claim number against nothing on the wrapper, in her own hand, with the date and the hour, and she did not copy it out, because copying it out would make a second hand on it and the whole of the value of that leaf is that there is one man's hand on the back of it and no other hand anywhere.

And then she wrote, on the wrapper, under the claim number, four words, because a keeper of records is allowed one line on a wrapper and it is the only line in this office that is not a fact:

***There is nobody to serve.***

And having written them she did the other thing, which is the thing she is going to have to live with, and which took about a minute and a half and which she entered on the same page afterwards because she had promised herself on the ninth of Greenmonth that the not writing was the part that was a decision.

*Entered: that this office has two instruments and no third. It can put a leaf in a press, and a press cannot be served and cannot be refused and cannot be got at, and that is a holding and it is the only holding this office has. And it can write a letter, and a letter goes to a person, and a letter is the only thing this office has ever sent that a person could answer.*

*And that I did not write the letter, and that the letter would be one sentence, and that the sentence would be: the fourth column of a return held in a provincial file is contradicted by the back of the same leaf in a hand dated two years before it, and here is a true copy of both, and I am a keeper of records and I have no power over your file and I am not asking you for it and I am telling you what is in front of me.*

*And that sending it would put the name of Oswin Rill into a Crown's hand in a provincial office, in a file, against a county, where a figure could be kept about it, and that no name has been written down in any Crown's hand in this county since the twenty-seventh of Fallowmonth, and that I did not write the letter, and that the reason I did not write it is that I have spent this year finding out that a name in a Crown's hand is the only thing in the world that a person cannot get out of, and that the one protection I have ever been able to give anybody in this office is a book nobody can serve, and that I have it in my hand, and that I am not going to trade it for a sentence I would like to have said.*

*And entered, against no person, and entered last, and entered because a keeper of records who stops at the reason stops short of the fact: **that Oswin Rill said, in a hall in this county eighteen days ago, that the price of what he wants is that his county will get worse and his own village will blame him, and that I have read the price on the back of his own leaf in his own hand, and that the price is the same, and that there is no instrument in this kingdom that will pay a price on behalf of a man four hundred miles off, and that I have looked.***

---

**And at about the ninth hour that evening a road warden of the Merefen bridge guild came up the two flights of a stair over a weigh-house with a wet sleeve and put a block of dressed stone on the floor by the door because he was carrying it four hundred yards from a yard and his hands were full, and she made him put it down on the two trestles and not on the boards.**

"You are working late," he said.

"I have entered a thing this morning that I have not sent to anybody," said Mara Vey, "and I am going to tell you what it is and I am not going to ask you anything about it, and if you have come up here to be asked something you have come on a bad morning."

"I have come about a dressed block," said Aren Kest, "and about the fact that a bench has been asked four times and has said yes four times and is not a bench, and about a man of sixty who gave up six hundred and forty-eight pence for a reason about a promise in a gateway, and I have brought no numbers for you tonight because there are none in any of it."

"Then sit down," said Mara Vey, "and if you have brought no numbers then I will read you the one line on a wrapper in my press that is not a fact, and you may take it out of your head or you may not, and I will not ask you afterwards which you did."
