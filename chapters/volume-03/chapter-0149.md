# Chapter 0149

The twentieth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, was a Monday and it was not a market day, and the fair copy came down the line on the twelfth of the month and had been waiting in the room over a weigh-house for eight days, and the reason it had waited was that it was the first thing that had ever come into that office out of a place that had agreed to be checked and had not been asked to, and neither of the two people in the room could work out who should open it.

Alish Renn opened it, because she is thirty and second copyist and on a warrant of Mara Vey's own that is not in the press, and she said the thing that had to be said before it was opened, which is that a document with an address on it is a different animal from a document without one, and that this one is addressed to a shelf.

“Addressed to a shelf,” she said, “and the shelf is this room, and the wrapper says so at the top in the same hand as the account, and the hand is not a clerk’s, and it is a hand that has sent four lines to a room four hundred miles off and one copy of a column and has never once signed anything.”

“Then the shelf opens it,” said Mara Vey, “and I would like the wrapper kept whole and I would like the hour on the wrapper and not on the account, and I would like Sarn to be in the room and to write down that I said the room is full.”

The room was not full. It had four people in it. It was the only room in this county where a thing had come up out of a county that is not on any map, and there were two years of it, and the room knew.

The fair copy is four hundred and eight words. It is in the minute-man's own hand, which is not a clerk's hand and is the hand that has been keeping the only book in that county for twenty-two years, and this one is a list of what a county is and of what it has spent, and there is a second sheet folded with it.

It reads, in full, and then the second sheet reads in full, and the figures are as they stand:

*Entered in the last week of the year, and sent on a day this county named, and addressed to a shelf. It is the first thing this county has ever sent north that is not a refusal.*

**Nine hundred and forty-one.**

*And that is the head of it. The figure is hearthstones. The four villages of the white ground have nine hundred and forty-one of them standing in them, and I counted them in the eleventh rolling with two men off the north side of the bank, and it is entered against nothing.*

*And a line with nothing in a money column, and it is the only one of the six. Four men of a yard from a fen county four hundred miles off, on the council's account, at the guild's rate, with none. The last twenty-seven days of them were paid in ninety-six bushels of salt at nine pence the bushel and it lands clean. The rest of them is at none, and it is in this council's book and in the guild's book, and neither of those books can be made to answer to anybody in any other county.*

*And the three figures of this county. Eight thousand and thirty-one, which is readings of a gauge taken twice a day for eleven years in a book in a hut at a weir, and it is the only account of the water of this county that exists and nobody has ever asked to see it. Sixty, which is households on a roll, fifty-eight certain and two doubtful, and the roll says in its own second line that it is not a return. And nine, which is the stone and the nine jars, and it is the same nine twice and it is not eighteen.*

*And the question, which is the first question this county has put to another county: we keep nine sealed jars of a wheat that has not been grown here in forty years, and it will keep about four more years, and a third of our wheat is a variety nobody here can sow twice. We ask any county that has kept a seed of a thing it does not grow to send us a jar. We will pay the carriage and we will pay for the jar. We will put the name of whoever keeps it in a book that cannot be served and not in a book that can.*

And the second sheet is a working, and it is in pence, and it is what makes the first sheet an account instead of a claim.

*The work, at the guild's rate of eight pence the day. The four hundred and forty yards, three days, the fifth rolling: a hundred and twenty-eight, a hundred and twenty-eight, a hundred and twenty-eight, and ninety-six of iron and elm, is four hundred and eighty. The causeway, four days, the seventh rolling: a hundred and fifty-two four times, and ninety-six of timber, is seven hundred and four. The trench above the town, two men, thirty-one days: sixteen thirty-one times, and ninety-six of elm, is five hundred and ninety-two. The plan of the twelfth rolling, six worked days of nine, and the last three of the nine not worked: a hundred and eighty-four six times, is one thousand one hundred and four. The re-cutting of the boundary, nine days, and ninety-six of elm: a hundred and eighty-four nine times, is one thousand seven hundred and fifty-two.*

*And the sum of them. Four hundred and eighty, and seven hundred and four, is one thousand one hundred and eighty-four. One thousand one hundred and eighty-four, and five hundred and ninety-two, is one thousand seven hundred and seventy-six. One thousand seven hundred and seventy-six, and one thousand one hundred and four, is two thousand eight hundred and eighty. Two thousand eight hundred and eighty, and one thousand seven hundred and fifty-two, is four thousand six hundred and thirty-two pence, which is ninety-six marks and two shillings, and the county has none of it, and did not expect to, and the council took the debt onto its own account in the fourth week of the ninth rolling and has never once been asked to pay it and cannot be.*

*And on the same sheet, under the addition, the water, and the salt. The water came back over the re-cut boundary in the last night of the year and the plan held, and a footbridge of two bays and five yards took the whole night river for nine hours because it was a lid, and one man stood on it, and the near bay hangs four inches and the far bay is sound, and no name is entered and the reason is that this county has a roll. The salt is not lifted. It is a foot and a half down under four villages now and it was a foot and a half down a year and a rolling ago, and the ground comes down at the rate it went up and nothing makes it come down in any year. The first crop off two of the four villages came in this month and it is a failure, and it is on a board in a keeper's hand with the hour on it, and the board is not a book.*

That is all of it. Four hundred and eight words and a working, and the last hundred of the fair copy are the first time in the history of this case that a path-side county has asked the main world for anything.

She read it three times and the third time with a rule.

“Nine hundred and forty-one,” she said. “That is the wrong number and I can tell you it is the wrong number without going out of the room, and I cannot tell you what the right one is, and I am going to say both of those sentences in that order because the second one is the part nobody in a filing room ever says.

“The county's own figures are three and not one of them is a hearth. Eight thousand and thirty-one readings is a count of readings. Sixty households on a roll is a count of households and the roll itself says in its own second line that it is not a return. And nine is a stone and nine jars and the account has not doubled it, which is more than most accounts do. Three figures and not one of them is a number of people, and a figure at the head of an account is the one figure in a document that everybody reads and nobody checks.”

She put the rule down across the head of it.

“Strike it,” she said, “and strike the word under it as well, because if the figure goes the word has to go or the word becomes a caption on nothing, and the reason the figure is wrong is in the word. Nine hundred and forty-one hearthstones is a real count of real stones in four villages and it is not a count of anything a person lives in, because a house in that county is a hearth and a hearth is a household and a household is about sixty, and there are about sixty, and the county has never once counted itself and could not have told you where to begin.”

“Who counted the stones,” said Sarn Oyelaran.

“Two men from the north side of the bank and the man who keeps the minute, and none of them had a reason to be wrong and every one of them had a reason not to know better,” said Mara Vey. “That is the ordinary way a figure gets into the world. Nobody lied and nobody was deceived and there are about sixty households in four villages and there are nine hundred and forty-one stones, and a stone is easier to count than a household because a stone does not move while you are counting it.”

She wrote in the margin, in her own hand, and read it back.

*Entered, on the twentieth of this month, against no person: that the figure at the head of the fair copy of the account of a county that is not on any map is struck, and that the county's own account carries three figures and that none of them is a hearth, and that a figure at the head of an account is the only figure in a document that is read by everybody and checked by nobody, and that this office has struck a figure on the twentieth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, in a document that came out of a place which had agreed to be checked and had not been asked to, and that striking it is everything this office has done about it, and is more than the office which made the figure would have done.*

Then she stopped with the pen down, because there was a second thing on the sheet, and the second thing was in her own office's file, and it had no business being on a page from a county that is not on any map and was on it because she had looked for it.

“Four hundred yards up that road,” she said, “and four hundred miles off, there is a county of clay with nine hundred and forty-one hearths in it, and this office has held that figure since a list came down a line in the summer, and it is the only figure this office has ever held about that county and it is the figure a salt merchant's brother bought a roll of persons for at a farthing of a bushel without knowing what he had bought.”

Alish Renn said, “That is the same figure.”

“It is the same figure,” said Mara Vey, “and I have sat here for about a minute deciding what to write about it, and I am going to write down that it is a coincidence, and I am going to ask every person in this room to hear the word and not to improve on it, and I am not going to do one single thing with it.

“One county counted its hearthstones. One office counted a county's hearths. They are both nine hundred and forty-one and they are about four hundred miles apart and they have nothing whatever to do with each other, and if I were to write one word in that margin about the two of them together then in about forty years somebody would read it as a reason, and it is not a reason, it is two numbers that happen to be the same, and a thing you do arithmetic on is not a thing you have looked at.”

She wrote: *and it is a coincidence, and it is entered as a coincidence, and no person in this room is to do anything with it.*

The four hundred and thirty-two pence is the other half of it, and she went down it with a rule in her own hand and in the county's own order, and the county's order is that the addition is on the page and the total is at the foot, and not one figure was altered.

“Four hundred and eighty, and seven hundred and four, and five hundred and ninety-two, and one thousand one hundred and four, and one thousand seven hundred and fifty-two. Four thousand six hundred and thirty-two pence, which is ninety-six marks and two shillings, and the addition is right and I have checked it twice and it lands on the figure at the foot, which is the only test there is.”

“And the county has none of it,” said Alish Renn.

“The county has none of it and this is the first document in four years and a half that has come into this office with a sum in it that a person in this office can check, and every figure on it has a trade or a date against it, and not one of them is a name, and the fifth line is four men and the word *none*, and I have entered a line in this office's own day-book in the same run of the page and not on a separate leaf, and the separate leaf is a thing a person can take away.”

She read the question at the foot of it out loud twice, and the second time she read it slower, and the room heard it, and it is the thing that is going to be the last thing this county is for a long time.

“Seed,” she said. “Nine jars, four years of keeping in them, a third of their wheat that cannot be sown twice, and a request to any county that has kept a seed of a thing it does not grow, with the carriage paid and the jar paid for and the name of whoever keeps it entered in a book that cannot be served and not in a book that can.”

“Write to the county of clay,” said Sarn Oyelaran. “We have their figure. Nine hundred and forty-one hearths, and it is on a page in this office, and the letter goes in a day.”

“We have their figure and we have the fact that the road to them was closed in four lines of writing in the middle of the year by a bench that asked for it, and the second of those lines says the county's hand at that end is released from it and is not to be sought, and the third says the book at that end is shut and is kept shut and is not to be asked for, and the fourth says the line at that end is everything that county has to say to him.” Mara Vey did not raise her voice and did not need to. “And nobody in this office is going to write a letter past the third of those four lines on a Monday because the post is quicker on a Monday.”

Then the two of them, in the same room, twice.

Lysa Fenn came up the stair at about the third hour with a wet coat and did not want a chair, and she did not want the account either, and she had read about it in a filing room in the county town four days earlier, which is what a county of nine thousand does with a document that cannot be served, which is that it gets a copy of it from the man who copied it.

“If a body corporate enters that company,” she said, “then that company has a party, and the party is this office, and this office can be sued and can be shut and can be in default, and in about four years a man in a county of clay is going to write to somebody in this county and say that a company of seed and bridge in a county that is not on any map has asked this office for a favour. And the favour will be a letter. And the answer to that letter will determine whether a charter is a thing that can be relied on or a thing that can be sued.”

“Then do not enter it,” said Mara Vey.

“Do not enter it and it is a claim, and a claim is what I have been beating instruments with for nine years, and you know it, and you have a line in a day-book about it that I have not been shown and would not want to see.”

“I have the line,” said Mara Vey, “and I have the other line as well, which is that a claim cannot be sued and that is all of why I can answer a thing like this one, and all of why a company of eleven people in a county with no office can send me a question and get a real answer out of it in nine days. If I enter the company and it can be sued then in four years this office can be made to answer for a county that has no writ, no bell and no schedule, and I will be able to say so on the ninth of Greenmonth and somebody will write the next letter the same afternoon.”

They went back and forth on it for about a quarter of an hour and neither of them gave anything, and Alish Renn wrote down that neither of them gave anything, which is her office's way of entering a thing that has no figure against it.

Marda Oakes came up the same stair at about the fifth hour with a resolution of the county council in a folder and a card with a name and a county and a room and no seal and no office, which is the only kind of card Mara Vey has ever entered against a person, and which is now the second one in four years and a half.

The county council wants the Archive to be a body corporate that can be sued, and it is, and Marda Oakes came about something else and got to it in about four minutes.

“Enter it and do not enter it, those are the county's two positions and you are going to have one,” she said. “The county council will back the entry of the company because a company is a person a county can serve and this county has spent four years being unable to serve a single thing across a line, and it will oppose the letter, because the letter puts this county in the middle of a matter between two counties it has no standing in, and if the letter is answered badly this county will be the county that wrote it, and a county that has been cut off for four years and has never once been able to answer for itself does not want a matter with a name on it.”

“I have not written the letter,” said Mara Vey.

“You have the company,” said Marda Oakes, “and a company that has not written its first letter yet is a company that can be entered and can be heard and can be asked questions about, and a company that has written its first letter is a company with a correspondence, and a correspondence is a hand a county can be got at.”

And she said the other half, in her own words, and did not finish it, exactly as she had not finished it in the long room over a wharf a year before, and she said why, and the reason was the same reason.

“You are not an office yet,” she said. “You have a door and a number and a form with a printed space on it, and when that space is filled in the way it ought to be filled in, everybody who helped you is a person who helped you, and the company you are about to enter will be the second thing you have put your name to in four years and a half and it is going to be the one that gets you a letter from a county of clay in about four years that you cannot answer and cannot refuse and cannot be shut, and I am not going to say the second half of that out loud in a room where the second copyist writes it down.”

Nobody improved on it and Alish Renn entered it and it is the only entry in the day-book in four years and a half that is a sentence a person stopped in the middle of.

Between Marda Oakes going down the stair and the resolution, which is the order they happened in, the company got entered, and nobody in the room had expected to be in the room for it and all of them were.

It is in the Marches' last week of the year and it is on the same board as the list and in the same hand as the list, and the name is in Ferrow's hand and the rest is in the minute-man's, and it went down the line with the account because the account would be a bill without it.

The name is *the nine jars and the ditch.*

Nobody improved on it. The keeper of the seed room is sixty-six and has kept a stone room behind a chapel for forty years and has nine sealed jars of a wheat that has not been grown in this county for forty years in it, and the name of the company she has just joined is nine jars and a ditch, and the person who proposed that name has never met her and has said in a room in this county that a name with a number in it is a thing that cannot be sued by anybody for anything and that is the reason for it and the only reason.

“Enter it,” said Mara Vey, “with a date and a name and a witness at each end, and put my name against the north end and put their keeper's name against the other, and the keeper's name is on their own book and their own book cannot be served, and this office has never in four years and a half entered a witness it could not produce and is not going to start in a document about seed.”

It is entered. It is the fourth thing this office has entered that names a body, and the first three were all a form, and this one is nine people and about forty households and a guild and a council with no writ, and the entry is in the ordinary form with the claim number against the office and nothing against any person, and the claim number is the number of the account, which is a thing a clerk may do twice and which a keeper may not do once.

The witness at the south end is the keeper of records of a body corporate with a door and a number, in Merefen, in a county four hundred miles off, and she is answerable to a room over a weigh-house and to nobody else, and the witness at the north end is the keeper of a minute in a room under a tithe barn in a county that is not on any map, and he is answerable to about eleven people and to nobody else, and the two of them have never met and cannot be produced to each other and both of those facts are in the entry because a witness nobody can produce is worth exactly as much as it says and no more.

And its first act is a letter, and the letter is four lines long and it is not in this room and it is not going out of it.

*To any county that has kept a seed of a thing it does not grow. We keep nine sealed jars of a wheat that has not been grown in this county for forty years, and it will keep for about four more years, and a third of the wheat of this county is a variety nobody here can sow twice. We ask for a jar. We will pay the carriage and we will pay for the jar. We will put the name of whoever keeps it in a book that cannot be served and not in a book that can, and we will not read a name out of any book to anybody from this county, and we will not send anybody who can be asked to come and look.*

*And we are a county that is not on any map in this kingdom, and we have not got a road, and the address of this company is a shelf in a room over a weigh-house in a fen town and the shelf has a number on it, and the number is a door and a number and nothing else.*

It went into a bag on the twenty-first and it was not answered. It is not answered inside the year, and it is not going to be answered, and the reason is in the fourth line of the third line of a notice that came down the same road in the autumn and said that the book at that end is shut and is kept shut and is not to be asked for, and that the hand at that end is released and is not to be sought, and that the line at that end is everything that county has to say.

A county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths bought a roll of persons for a farthing of salt and does not know what it bought, and the man who bought it is on a coast and has wanted a canal wharf for nine years, and he has been told twice in writing and once in a market that a wharf costs money, and he has a file about nine hundred and forty-one households and no address on it and no person at the end of it, and the person who used to hold the other end of anything in that county was struck and the county did not replace them in time, and the road is not there.

Nobody in this room is told the figure of the markets. Nobody in this county has ever been told anything about a thing they have lost, because a thing you have lost is not a thing anybody can enter in a book, and the two books in the room over a weigh-house are the only two in this case that anybody can serve, and the road to that county is closed in four lines of writing and the four lines are correct.

Then the Archive resolved, and a body corporate with a door and a number does not resolve by wanting to.

It resolved because the fair copy said the county had asked to be heard on its own terms, and the county's own terms were six lines on a board in a room in a county that is not on any map, and one of the six lines is that the road is not to be made to carry a person, and there is therefore no way on earth to get anybody from there to here, and a hearing entered for a date that nobody can travel to is a hearing entered for a date on which the other party does not come, and that is a thing this office has done to two other offices in four years and a half and it has been entered every time and it is on the page.

Mara Vey wrote the resolution in the ordinary form, with the claim number against the office and nothing at all against any person, and the pen was put down when it was done, and then she read it back and it reads:

*Resolved, on the twentieth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, that this office will hear the delegation of the Hearth Marches on the terms the Marches have set, and that the hearing is entered for the ninth of Rainmonth of the year following, at the second hour, in this room, and that the terms are six lines on a board and are held in this office, and that no person is to be sent to ask anybody in that county anything, and that the delegation is not to be sent for, and that if the delegation does not come the hearing is not adjourned and is not refused, and it stands.*

It is the first resolution this office has ever entered for a date eighteen months away, and it is entered against no person, and the date is the earliest date the county could travel and the room was small and four of them would be standing, and the six terms and the entire of the arrangement are in a fair copy on the desk in a good hand, and nobody in this room is going to be able to tell anybody in four years whether anybody came.
