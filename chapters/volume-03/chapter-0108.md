# Chapter 0108

The twenty-third of Longlight, YR 313, was a Thursday and a market day, and the yard on Weir Street had had a man from the Hearth Marches in it since the ninth, and by the twenty-third he had been in this county two weeks and had said on the fourth day that a rate cannot be agreed by a man who is in the county for four days, and had gone on saying it for nine more days, and the four men had stopped arguing with him and started feeding him, which is what a yard does.

He was sixty, with a spliced line across the back of his right hand, and he had a sheet of his own in an oilcloth and he had read it out four times in a gateway in two weeks and he did not have it out on the twenty-third.

That was the mistake he made, and he made it because he was tired, and it is the reason the yard is where it is.

---

**The first thing that morning was a yard doing its own figures, and the figures are the yard's and not the Marches' and not the road warden's, and they go in pence.**

*Fourth of the sheet, being the rate. The yard's rate is eight pence a day a hand and the yard has held it nineteen years and it is in this book on four pages and I am not going to discuss it with anybody from a county four hundred miles off.*

*Four men. Thirty-two pence a day.*
*The boy. Four pence a day, being half, and he is fifteen and he copies the empty space under a number as well as the number and that is why he is on this and not on the stone.*
*Thirty-six pence a day.*
*Ninety working days, being the number the paper gives, and I have asked for the paper to be read out again twice and it is ninety both times and no man there can tell me what a rolling is in days and I have stopped asking.*

"Two thousand eight hundred and eighty," said the boy, who does the copying, "and three hundred and sixty."

"Two thousand eight hundred and eighty and three hundred and sixty," said the man at the drawknife, who was twenty-nine and had been in the yard since he was eleven and who does all of the yard's arithmetic, and did it in the yard, because Tovan Kest does not add in his head on a wet morning. He wrote nothing down and then he wrote it down.

"**Two thousand eight hundred and eighty and three hundred and sixty is three thousand two hundred and forty pence.** And that is three shillings a day for four men and a boy, and three shillings a day is a good day's work in a market town, and I have never in my life had a day like it, and I would like the figure standing on that board before anybody in this yard starts being clever about it."

"It is in this book," said Tovan Kest.

---

**And the fourth man of that crew was in the yard and had been in the yard for eleven years and had been called a symbol for eleven of those, and on the twenty-third of Longlight he said the only thing in that yard all day that he had been wanting to say for four years.**

Pei Tarrow was fifty-two and had a cart of his own on the Old Toll channel, and he had been in and out of this yard since the year of the Sundering, and in that year he had stood on the wharf with a rope in his hand and let eleven names be read out of a list that somebody had brought, and there are eleven people in this county who have told him in eleven different years that he is a survivor, and he has never once asked any of them for anything.

He had wanted freight for four years. He had said so in this yard twice, in front of four men, and both times it had been taken as a complaint about a trade that had gone quiet, and both times it had been a complaint about something else.

"Ninety days is not a season," he said, on the twenty-third, to nobody. "A season is when the work comes. Ninety days is a gap between two things and there is nothing in it, and a man who takes a gap is a man who is out of work in the middle of it and in the middle of it he is a man who took a contract he cannot keep, and you will not find me at the far end of that, and that is not a threat, it is a calendar."

"Then what do you want," said the man at the drawknife, who had asked him the same thing twice before and been answered about the water.

"I want it written that I go for the ninety days and I am back before the sowing," said Pei Tarrow, "and I want the number in the book with the hour, and I do not want my name in the book in the same line as the word survivor, and there is no word survivor in your book and I have read it and I am not accusing you of anything, I am telling you where to keep it."

The man at the drawknife looked at Tovan Kest.

"It goes in with the hour," said Tovan Kest.

"It goes in with the hour," said Pei Tarrow, "and I will tell you what else, and then I will go back to the cart. **Eleven years. Eleven of you have said a thing to me in a market or a yard or a doorway and every single one of you has said it as though it were a thing you were giving me, and not one of you has ever asked me a question, and I have stopped waiting, and a man who has stopped waiting is a man who is dangerous to a yard and I would rather say it here than have it said in a gateway.**"

Nobody improved it, and it is on the page, and the page is the one at the head of the week-book for the twenty-third with the hour against it, and the fifth line on that page is the one with no number on it, and Pei Tarrow read the fifth line when it was written and did not say anything about it, and did not know, and would not have cared, because there is nothing in that line that is about him.

---



The Marches' pay was not coin. The Marches' pay was salt and seed, and the man from the Marches had the figures in the oilcloth sheet and had to unfold it on a tying-up bench in a gateway, and the wind took the corner of it twice.

"Two hundred and eighty-eight bushels of salt at nine pence the bushel. Two hundred and sixteen of seed at three. Three thousand two hundred and forty pence, the whole of your sum, and not a penny of it to be carried and none of it to be argued with after."

"Nine pence," said the man at the drawknife. "**A bushel of salt at the Old Toll is three shillings. Three shillings is thirty-six pence. That is four times your nine.**"

"Four times," said the man from the Marches, "in your county, at your crossing, on your road. In mine a bushel of salt is nine pence and I have had it argued down from eleven by two men in a market who wanted it and one man who did not, and that is the price and it is the only price and I did not set it."

"Then take your four times and take the lot of it in salt," said the man at the drawknife, "and I will tell you what this yard does with two hundred and eighty-eight bushels of salt, and I will tell you in front of these four, and the answer is nothing. We burn about nine stone of it a year. The Old Toll will take some and the fishing town will take some and the coopers will take some and there is a man on the coast who deals in it and he comes twice a year. **That is two seasons' work and a bad one and a yard is not a merchant.**"

"Then what is a yard to do with it," said the man from the Marches.

"A yard is to do the work," said the man at the drawknife, "and be paid, and the day we start buying salt to sell salt we are a different trade and we will be a different trade by the second winter and you will have ruined this yard for four hundred miles of river and called it a contract."

Nobody in the gateway argued with that and the yard's man at the drawknife knew he had won it and knew within about a second that he had won the wrong half, and everybody in that gateway knew it, which is what a yard is.

And Pei Tarrow, from the cart end, without raising his voice: "**He is right and it does not matter, and I want it entered that I said so before it mattered, because a man who is always right about a thing that does not matter is a man you stop listening to.**"

"Then the seed," said the man from the Marches, and put a finger on the second line. "Two hundred and sixteen bushels, at three."

"**No.**"

"It is at the price of the same sheet and it is the same sheet and the sum is the same sum and I have not come four hundred miles to carry two numbers."

"Then I have not signed a sheet with two numbers in it," said Tovan Kest, and he had not said anything for nine minutes, and the four men looked round at him, because in nineteen years the yard has not refused a piece of work and the man who refuses it is the master and the master has never once done it.

They waited. A cart went past.

"Seed is three pence in your county," said Tovan Kest. "Oats is four pence at the Old Toll and we are in a year when a man who has oats before the sowing is a man with a roof. Two hundred and sixteen bushels of your wheat at three pence is six hundred and forty-eight pence and there is no price at all for it in this county and I will not write one, and that is not a complaint about your wheat, it is that a thing with no price is a thing the yard has to carry and a yard has one cart and a fortnight in it and a cutting contract and eleven marks and fourpence of crew pay owed to us by a trust that has no owner. **I cannot carry six hundred and forty-eight pence of seed.** Take it out and give me the salt and I will take the salt and I will say so out loud in front of these four that the salt is worth more to us than your sum and that we are taking it because a yard has to eat and not because it has been beaten."

"You have not been beaten," said the man from the Marches.

"No," said Tovan Kest. "**I have been outargued by a man with a sheet and a yard is not a court and this is not a court.**"

---

**And the man from the Marches took the seed off, and then he did the one thing in the whole of those two weeks that made the four men in that yard trust him, and it cost him a clause he wanted.**

"The last fifth," he said. "That is mine, not yours. Six hundred and forty-eight pence is not payable until the return, and the return is on a day of ours. I have wanted that since your clerk read your paper out in a hall and I have been keeping it and I have just given it up, and the reason is that I read your clause this morning before I came out and I have read it every day for a fortnight and I do not think a man should take a yard's money on a promise from a county he cannot check."

"You are giving up six hundred and forty-eight pence for a reason about a promise," said the man at the drawknife.

"I am giving up six hundred and forty-eight pence for a reason about a promise," said the man from the Marches, "and I would like the four of you to notice what it is, and I would like it noticed in a room, and I am not going to say it again."

He said it in a gateway in Weir Street, to four men of a yard and one of their master, in front of a cart, and nobody wrote it down.

---

**And at about the fourth hour Tovan Kest did his own arithmetic, and it is in the week-book in his own hand with the hour against it, and it is the only page in that book in ten years that is a page and not an entry.**

*Twenty-third of Longlight, YR 313, a Thursday, the fourth hour, market day. Terms of a contract with the yard of a county called the Hearth Marches, being four hundred miles off this one, being river work, being ninety working days.*

*One. The sum, at this yard's rate: four hands at eight pence, a boy at four. Two thousand eight hundred and eighty and three hundred and sixty. Three thousand two hundred and forty pence.*
*Two. Salt, two hundred and eighty-eight bushels, at nine pence by their own figure: two thousand five hundred and ninety-two pence. By the figure on this page for salt delivered to the Old Toll it is four times that, and I am not going to write the four times down, because if I write it down somebody will believe I believe it, and I do not believe it, and neither does the man at the drawknife, and the truth is that the salt is worth what it is worth over two seasons and neither of those seasons is this one.*
*Three. Seed: two hundred and sixteen bushels. **There is no price for it in this county and I will not put one in this book.** That is a line with nothing in it and I have left the space.*
*Four. The last fifth is payable on the return and the return is on a day they name. That is six hundred and forty-eight pence of this yard's money in a county four hundred miles off, and I have taken it, and a yard's book holds things set, and that is not set, and I have entered it anyway with the hour against it because the alternative is not entering it and then not knowing that I took it.*
*Five.* — and this is the fifth line and there is no number on it and there is no number ever going to be on it — *My brother went out of this county at the fourth hour of a market morning in the ninth month of last year and was gone eighty-eight days and came back with grey clay in the seam of his bag. The date of it is in his own account and it is not this county's date, and I have read the date and I have known for about a month what it is a date of, and I have entered it in this book with the hour against it, and he does not know I have entered it, and I am not going to tell him, and nobody in this county is ever going to ask to see this page, and that is the last thing in this book that I am going to write about my brother and I have written four entries about him in the last year and this is the fifth.*

He shut the book and the boy at the bench-board copied the four lines with figures on them and the hour, and copied the empty space under the third line as well, because that is what the boy does.

---

**And the paper itself is not in that book and never was, and a yard's book holds the fact of a thing and not the thing.**

The contract is a sheet of the Marches' own paper in the Marches' own hand and it is in a minute book in a chamber over a tithe barn four hundred miles off, and what is in Merefen is four lines and an hour and an empty space, and if anybody in this county ever wanted to see the contract itself they would have to go and get it, and the going would take eleven days in a waggon, and a man who has never been to that county would be told to come back.

That is the yard's rule and it is nineteen years old and it is painted on the wall in the hand of a man who is dead: **nothing that belongs to a man goes in this yard's book, and only a thing set.**

And a yard that writes a set thing down in a book in its own town has done the only thing a yard can do about a contract with a county four hundred miles off, and it has done it in pence, and it has left a space empty on purpose, and the space is on the line about the seed, and the seed is the part of it that is not worth anything anywhere yet, and a man in four hundred miles is at this moment sowing it.
