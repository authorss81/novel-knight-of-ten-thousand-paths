# Chapter 0139

It came into the market town in the third week of the ninth rolling, with a carter's boy and a basket of onions, and it was a sheet of paper and nothing else, and there was no name at the top of it.

Aren was in the room under the tithe barn with the roll chest against the wall and the door open, because the ninth rolling had come in dry and the guild's six days were done and the bank above the pond had come down on the fourth day of the ninth, as Ferrow had said it would, on the fourth day and not the third, and had buried about forty yards of the old bank and one of the forty ditchmen's barrows and nothing else.

The minute-man was in the room, because the minute-man was always in the room in the last hour of the second week, and the man who keeps the minute heard a stranger say a woman's name in a yard on purpose and has not been asked about it and will not be.

The boy put the sheet on the table, which is what a carter's boy does with a thing that has a person's name on it, and did not put a name on the table, because there was not one, and said, “For the man who came in the spring. The woman said the man who came in the spring.”

“From the woman at the far end,” said Aren, before the boy was out of the door.

“Yes. She said to give it to the man who came in the spring and she said if there is more than one of those then there is more than one of everything, and she said that about the fish.”

The boy went, and the door stayed open, and the minute-man put his pen down on the table, flat, which he had never done at a table with nothing in it on it.

“Four lines,” he said.

“Four lines.”

The sheet was folded once and it was a fair copy, and the hand was a small upright hand that was not a clerk's and was the same hand as a two-line leaf that had come down the same line in the fourth week of the fourth rolling and was folded into a notebook in a coat in a room in this county. He knew the hand. He knew where to go and stand and look, and the county did not know he knew, and that had been true for eight rollings and had not once cost him anything, and it cost him the second day of the third week of the ninth rolling.

He did not open it on the table. He read it standing at the slot of the window, and then he read it a second time with his thumb on the first line, which is a thing a man does when he is about to argue with a document and wants to be in the right place on it when he loses.

*Entered at the fourth hour on a day this county keeps, in the county's own book, and sent because the second of the county's terms says that no name is to be read out on the far side of the line by anybody from the north, and no name is to be written in the line's book at the north end, and this contains no name.*

*That the line is not kept, and is not to be kept again, and the county's hand at that end is released from it, and the hand at that end is not to be sought.*

*That the book at that end is shut, and is kept shut, and is not to be asked for, and the county asks that nothing sent north be treated by anybody as a return.*

*That the second of the county’s terms is given back to the man it was written for, whole, in the county’s own words, and that the county has kept it for nineteen years, and that the county does not know what the man is called and does not intend to be told, and that the line at this end is the whole of what this county has to say to him.*

Four lines. He read the fourth of them three times, and the fourth of them is the second of the county’s five terms, and it is forty-one words long, and he has had those forty-one words in his head in three sizes of writing since a market morning four hundred miles off, when a clerk read them out of a fair copy in a gateway because a boy of fifteen asked for them twice, and he has never in his life seen them in that county’s own hand, and the county has set them out again in its own hand and added to them, and it is a refusal of him.

He did not write them out in front of the room. He read the first three lines, which are the county’s own sentences, and then he said that the fourth line was the second of the county’s terms, that it was forty-one words, that he had read them out loud in a market town four hundred miles off in front of four men, and that he was not going to say them again in a room where nine people would hear them, because a thing said twice in two rooms becomes a thing a county has adopted, and this county had adopted it once already in a gateway and had never put it in a book, and he was not going to be the man who put it in one.

It is a refusal of him because he came four hundred miles to hold a road to a person, and a road is a line and two hands and a date and a witness at each end, and a person is what the second of the terms is for, and the terms said no, and the county has kept the terms for nineteen years and the north has not kept them, because there is a file four hundred miles off with a road warden's name in a column headed *Keeper*, written by a clerk off a printed example at the foot of a form, in an office that did not send anybody to write it, and it says *and no other office*, and it is the only name in this whole arrangement that neither end of the line ever put there.

The county is giving the term back. The county is saying: here is the reason there was no person at this end for nineteen years, and it is a reason you helped write, and it is correct, and you have spent nineteen years knowing it and not once putting it in a room.

He stood at the window with the sheet in his hand and could not for about a minute make the room wait, and the minute-man let him not, which is all that man has ever done for him and has never once said he was doing.

Then he carried the sheet to the table and put it down flat and said, “I am going to read this out in the county’s own copy so that nobody in this room has to trust my mouth for it, and I am going to read three lines and say what the fourth one is, and I am going to tell you before I start that I am not going to say the fourth one, and you may hold me to that or not as you please.”

He read it out. He read the three lines at the speed of a man reading a thing he is going to be held to, and then he said what the fourth one was, and the room heard four lines and not one of them got the fourth.

Nobody in the room knew what a line was, or that there had been a line, or that the sheet in his hand was a thing that closed one, except the woman of sixty on three hearths, who had heard a boy of eleven in a gateway in the second rolling ask whether being asked would put his household on a list, and who had stood outside a window in the third week of the seventh rolling and listened to a woman of sixty-six say nine words to a man about a man in the water, and who had understood this particular kind of document before anybody in the room had finished the second line.

“Who is it from,” said the minute-man.

“It is from the woman who keeps the road at that end, at the asking of a bench, and she is not the author of it. The author of it is a bench of nine in a long room in a county of clay, and they asked her for four lines out of their minute, and she copied them, and the copy says so at the top, which is the first line. It is checkable. Anybody who doubts it can go and stand in the room and look, and the room is named on it, and the hour is on it, and I have been in it.”

“You have been in it.”

“In the first week of the month before last of the year before this one, with a sheet of clay in my hand, and I read all five terms out loud in a gateway in a market town, and this is one of them, and I read it in three sizes of writing because it is longer than the rest.”

The minute-man wrote for a while, and then he said the thing that is the cost of the ninth rolling, and he said it in the plain voice.

“Entered, in the third week of the ninth rolling, against no person: a four-line notice was brought into the room by a carter's boy and there is no name at the top of it and I cannot enter a document that has no name at the top of it, because a document is filed against something, and this is filed against nothing, and it is therefore not a record of anything, it is a piece of paper in a room.

“And I am going to enter that too, and the two of those are in the same entry and they contradict each other, and I have known for two years that a book can hold a contradiction if the hand is honest, and this is the first one in twenty-two years and it is the one I am proudest of and I am not proud of anything else in this book.

“And the third thing, and it is against nobody either: that there is no column in this county for a notice with no addressee, and that four lines of it have now been read in a room with about nine persons in it, three of them out loud and one of them named and not said, and that in nine years there will be nine people alive who can say they heard it, and that is not a record and it cannot be produced and it cannot be served, and it is the only kind of thing this county has ever produced that an office four hundred miles off cannot get at, and I have spent twenty-two years wishing this room were harder to find.”

They read it again in the council chamber that evening, with the door open, because the door is open in the eighth rolling and the ninth.

The minute-man entered what was entered as a fact and not as a defeat, and entered the hour, and entered that the notice contains no name and that the absence of the name is in the notice itself, at the top, in the first line, and is the reason the notice was sent. And Munn asked whether the council could answer it, and the minute-man said the council could not answer it and could not decline it, because there was nobody in it to answer, and the council entered the fact that it had been shown four lines and had no standing to do anything with them.

And then a thing happened in that room that had not happened in it in nineteen years, and it happened because of a gateway and a boy of eleven and a true answer that nobody wrote down, and Ferrow said it and then said that he had said it before and had been ignored.

“In the second rolling, in a gateway, four of us told a boy there was no list, and I said in this room that in five rollings, when a list does come along, half of four villages will sign whatever is read out to them, and they will do it because we were right and not because they were deceived. It has taken seven rollings instead of five, and it has not been a list of households to be helped, which is what I thought I was warning them about. It has been four lines with no name on it, and there is nothing at the top of it to sign, and it has been read out in a room and in a yard and to about nine people, and every one of the nine is now a person who has read out a document they cannot prove and cannot show and cannot be shown.”

He put his hand on the table.

“So I have been right about the wrong thing for seven rollings, and I would like the room to know that the gateway is still standing, because the answer in it has not been contradicted by anything, and a county that answers a child in a gateway and then spends eight years being proved right about lists has not become harder to find. It has become the only room in this kingdom where a thing can be said and be true and be in no book, and that is a country I have never wanted to live in and I am living in it.”

The reading of that sentence in the next hundred years is not the county's problem. What happened that evening is that nobody in the room argued, and the minute-man entered Ferrow's sentence as a fact about the room, and the room sat with it, and the woman of sixty on three hearths, who had been outside that window twice in eight rollings and had heard everything through boards both times, said the only thing she said, and she said it to the minute-man and not to the bench.

“Enter it as a fact about the room and not as a thing anybody said to you,” she said, “because I have been outside that window twice and I have never once been in this room and I am not going to start by being asked a question I cannot answer, and a woman on three hearths who is not on the oath is not a person this bench asks things of, and that has been true for forty-one years and nobody has ever put it in a book, and I am asking for it now, once, and not for me, because the next woman on three hearths will be on three hearths for forty-one years and she will be standing in the rain outside this window and she will not know to come in.”

The minute-man wrote it. He read it back. It is the first entry in twenty-two years with a thing in it that a person asked to have put there about a class of persons and not about herself, and it is the only entry in that book that names nobody and is not about the council.

He called the second route once.

He did it properly, because there is a way of doing it and he is a man who has made one up in a gateway in about nine minutes twice, and the way is a hand at each end and a date and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping the road, and the second route had been made up properly in the first week of a month four hundred miles off in a shed with a witness, and had not been answered in nine days or in five rollings or at any time since.

So he made it up again in the last hour of the third week of the ninth rolling, in a room over a tithe barn in a county that is not on any map in this kingdom, with a woman of sixty-six ringing a bell over a stone room and about forty men on a bank putting sandbags on a hole, and he said the two hands and the date and the hour and the witness who was not the man who keeps the road, and the witness was the man at the drawknife, because he is not the man who keeps that road and he is a hand and not a keeper and he is in this county on a contract that is not paid.

And the line went out at the fourth hour, and the answer was the notice.

Not nothing. The notice. Four lines, a hand in a fair copy, a bench that asked for it, a woman who copied it out of a minute because a minute can be copied and a road cannot, and all of it standing at the far end of a line where a hand is supposed to be, and no hand, and no witness, and no date, and the thing the notice says is that there is not to be a hand, and it is correct, and it is the answer, and it is not a refusal either, and it is not nothing.

He knew it would be the notice before he called it, because he has known since the first week of a month in the first year of this case that the second route does not answer when it is called, and because a county that has never refused anything in its life takes its time, and because there has been a two-line leaf folded into his notebook for eight rollings with the words *we did not lose our register, we lost our refusal, and a register is what you have instead*, and the woman who wrote that sentence is a fourth column that says *wife* and is not a trade, and the copyist who carries her words four hundred miles is a different person and has never once claimed to be her.

Nobody said the word about a road not opening. Nobody in that room had the vocabulary for it, and a county that cannot refuse anything has no word for a road that is not there, and a thing that is not there cannot be looked for, and a thing that cannot be looked for does not get an entry, and the minute-man did not enter it and was asked by nobody.

He wrote it in his own book, and the book is his, and the entry is a line, and the line has the hour, and it is the only entry in that book in eight rollings that is not a figure and not a sentence about a person.

*Called the second route once, at the fourth hour, with a hand at this end and a witness at this end who is not the man who keeps that road, and the date, and the hour. The answer was the four lines. I was not surprised and I want that in the book in those words, because a man who is not surprised has already counted the cost, and the cost is a county four hundred miles off with a roll in three hands and a rule with no office behind it, and it does not need me, and it never did, and I have been four hundred miles from it for eight rollings telling a bench with no office in it what a road is.*

Then, on the same page and above that line, because it came to him while the ink was wet and because he is a man who has a notebook and not a day-book and has never once pretended otherwise, in a smaller hand and with no hour against it, in words he did not write and cannot account for and has never been able to account for, four hundred miles away and in two volumes:

*A road needs a living hand.*

He looked at it for a while. It was true and it was in his own book and it was not his, and there was nobody four hundred miles off to tell him where it had come from, and the road it was about was four hundred miles off in a county of clay and had just been shut in four lines by a bench that asked for it, in a document containing no name, on a day that county keeps, at the fourth hour, sent by a woman who keeps that end and who was never asked a question about it in about two years and has not asked one either.

He closed the book on it and went up to the staging, because it was after the fourth hour and the four men of a fen yard were on the north approach in the last of the light and there was a mile of new bank above the pond with sandbags on it, and the eighth rolling was over and the ninth was three weeks old and there were four rollings left in the county's year, and he was thirty-two years old and he was a man with a bar who was not to have a bar, and the only thing anybody in that county had ever asked him for in eight rollings was a number, and he had given her nine of them in her own hand and he had asked for none of the eight thousand and thirty-one.
