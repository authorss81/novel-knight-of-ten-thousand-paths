# Chapter 0681

**Wolfmonth the eighth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street with the hearing standing in the street and about four hundred people in it, nine hundred words under a cloth in a room over a saddler's, and a woman of about twenty-four who keeps a copy-book asking out loud what a thing is called.**

The nine hundred words were still on the table and the cloth was still on them and nobody had been able to establish who put it there.

A Saturday is not a first of a month, and the hearing does not sit on a Saturday, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head was standing at the low end of the boards anyway with his bar against his leg, which is what he does about four times a month and has done since the middle of Longlight.

About four hundred people were in that market street by the second hour and about two hundred of them stopped what they were doing, and the rest of them did not, and that has been the proportion on every market morning since the first of Embermonth.

The woman of about twenty-four who keeps a copy-book and the front of her shop had been in that street since the market came in at about the seventh hour and had been copying for about an hour, and what she was copying was the nine hundred words, out of a book of her own, in a hand that was not the hand they were written in.

“**I am not copying it for anybody,**” she said, to a table in her shop, at about the second hour, to nobody, and nobody thanked her for saying so because nobody heard her. “**I have copied nine hundred words three times since the twenty-ninth of Fallowmonth and I have not been paid for one of them and I have not been asked for one of them, and a copy-book is not a plan and I have known that since I was about nineteen.**”

Then she carried the book up the street in both hands and put it on the boards, and four people at the boards noticed the book and about five noticed the woman, and two hundred people in that market street watched a woman of about twenty-four put a book down on a set of boards and nobody thanked her for the walk.

“**I have copied nine hundred words and I cannot write a word at the top of them,**” she said. “**I have got a name for everything a person can buy in nine counties. I have got one for a barrel of lime and one for a sheet of foolscap and one for a boy that will read a number out loud, and I have not got one for this, and I would like one before the day is out.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard the whole of that and about five had heard a woman ask a market street for a word.

The copyist of about twenty-six was at the high end of the boards with a leaf in her hand, and she had heard all of it, and she gave the three numbers out loud in the flat way she asks things, and she gave them to about nine people and not to the four hundred.

“**Three things and then I am going back up thirteen stairs. About four people in nine counties have been asked things this year and about four of them are the only people in nine counties who have been asked anything. About nine people have come to this street ready to say no. And about four of those nine are the only ones of the nine who could carry anything at all, and by carry anything I mean walk four days up a made road and stand in front of a person and say one question and come back.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards had heard three numbers and about five had heard a woman count what nine counties has.

The rest of that market street was doing what it does on a Saturday. Two men were unloading boards off a cart at the low end and one of them was arguing the price of the carting. A woman of about thirty-three who keeps a chandler's shop had put a bench out of her shop and had not put a cloth over it, and she sells nothing at a market that anybody can tell, and she has done that for eleven years. A man of about thirty-eight who reads four clauses and points at one stood at the high end of the boards with his hands behind him and had said nothing since the cloth went on the table.

Three of the things this county has went past the boards that morning on carts. A chain went up the lane to the ford on a handcart with a boy of about nine walking beside it, and it has been on two posts there since the thirteenth of Fallowmonth. Nine tons of cut stone went past in the other direction on a wain, and the yard that sent it out stopped nine weeks ago, and the stone has not been collected, and four of the nine households at that crossing have stopped going there altogether. A sheet of printed paper with counties in order on it went up to the room over a saddler's in a handbasket and nobody read it, because it was a list of counties and not a name.

“**Say the other half of it,**” a man of about thirty-three said, who sells cloth three doors up and who had been at the boards since the market came in.

“**The other half is that the four who could carry are the four who said no in this street on the nineteenth of Fallowmonth,**” the copyist said. “**All four. In about nine seconds each, in the rain, in front of about nine people. I wrote all four down and I have read all four out at the boards on two firsts of a month and I am not going to read them out again.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard the whole of the shape of it and about five had heard a woman refuse to read four sentences.

Then the man with the badge said the thing that about nine people had been waiting about four months for, and he said it in about nine words, and then he said the other half of it, which nobody had asked him for.

“**A thing with no head is not a smaller thing. It is a harder thing to get at.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at the boards had heard the whole of it and about five had heard a man say nine words and stop.

“**I have been standing in this street on the first of every month for about four and a half years and I have had one thing to say on those days and it is that there is nothing to stand in, and about nine people have come and stood in it anyway, and about nine counties has got nine people who will say no at a gate and not one head to call them, and that is the whole of the arrangement.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and about four people at the boards looked at the ground and about five looked at the boards.

Then somebody at the high end of the boards said the question that about nine people in that market street had come to ask, and it was asked by a man of about fifty-one who owns a field on the low side of a cut a mile and a half from this street, and who had walked four miles to ask it and had said out loud in Fallowmonth that he wanted it said in a market street and not in a lane.

“**Then say what it is called,**” the man of about fifty-one said. “**Nine counties has got a thing and the thing has got no name. A thing with a name can be asked for. A thing with a name is a thing a person can refuse to give. I have been at these boards four times this month and every one of the four times somebody has said the word front, and not one of the four times has anybody said what it is a front of.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at the boards had heard a man ask a question and about five had heard a word.

Then the badge-man answered it, and he answered it in the wrong order on purpose, and about four people at the boards noticed that he did.

“**No,**” he said. “**I am not going to call it anything and neither is anybody else in this street. You do not name a thing to get at it. You name a thing so that it can be got at, by the people in it and by the people who want it, and about nine counties has spent four and a half years working out how to be a thing that cannot be got at, and I am not going to undo that in a market street on a Saturday because a woman of about twenty-four wants a word for her book.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at that market street had heard a man refuse a woman a thing she had walked up the street for, and about five had heard a man be careful.

“**And I am not going to say anything else about it in this street today,**” he said. “**I have stood at the first of every month for four and a half years and on those days anybody can stand in the street and nobody is asked for a reason. The first of Frostmonth is twenty-three days off. It is a Monday. It has never once been a market morning in four and a half years and I have never said so out loud, because saying so out loud is asking nine counties to turn up on a day when nothing is happening, and nine counties would turn up, and then it would be a custom, and a custom is a head.**”

Nobody said anything to that and about four people in that market street had heard a man explain why he had never once said a true thing he was sure of, and about five had heard a man protect a habit.

Then a woman of about thirty-one who keeps the smith's house at the junction said the question out loud, and she said it to nobody in particular and she had come four miles for it.

“**Is any of this for me?**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard the question and about five had heard a woman ask to be of use.

“**Because I will tell you what I have been doing since the middle of Rainmonth,**” she said, and nobody stopped her and nobody argued with her. “**The smith has been asked twice for a fire iron and has gone up the made road both times and has come back with the fire iron and has not been asked for anything else, and I have wanted to know what a person is for, and I have asked four people in this market town and not one of them has given me an answer, and I am not going to be told today that I am one of the nine.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people at the boards had heard a woman say that she is not going to be one of the nine, and about five had heard a woman ask to be of use and be refused by silence.

The badge-man did not answer her and about nine people in that market street noticed that he did not answer her, and about five did not, and nobody has been able to establish since which of those two groups was right.

Then the copyist asked her what she would do with the answer, and the woman of about thirty-one gave it in four words, and nobody thanked her for it.

“**I would saw it.**”

“**That is a real answer and I am not going to say so in this street,**” the copyist said, “**because the last time somebody in this county gave me a real answer I wrote it in a book, and the book is the problem and not the answer. That is the whole of what I have got to say to it.**”

Nobody thanked either of them and about four people at the boards had heard a keeper of records say that a book is the problem and about five had heard a woman say that she would saw it.

Then a man of about forty-seven who sells wool two streets off asked the question he had asked in a room over a saddler's in Fallowmonth, in the same words, and about nine people at the boards had heard him ask it once before.

“**Then can a person refuse a thing that has no name?**”

“**No,**” the copyist said. “**You can decline it. Those are two different things and this county has spent four and a half years learning the difference out loud at a set of boards. A named thing can be refused, and a refusal is a thing that can be carried up a made road and put in front of a man in a livery. An unnamed thing can only be stepped around, and four hundred people in this street will step around anything they cannot put a name to, including me, and including him.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people at that market street had understood all of that and about five had heard the word refuse twice and had not understood either of them.

Then the copyist said the other half of it, which was that the day-book on the table behind her had ten entries in it and that not one of the ten was a thing anybody had said in this street.

“**Nine hundred sheets a month going out. Four hundred and nine parish rolls a month coming in. A band on a fixed day that I do not alter whatever is in the county. Those three are the best thing nine counties has built and there is not one line in them about a woman of about twenty-four with a copy-book, or about a man of about forty-four with a wain, or about four people who could walk four days up a made road and have said no in the open this morning.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people at the boards had heard a keeper of a room say out loud that her own book does not hold what she was doing in this street, and about five heard the three figures and thought about the money.

Aren Kest was not in that market street on the eighth of Wolfmonth. He was four hundred paces off at the weigh-house with a stick in his right hand, and the copyist of about twenty-six said so out loud at the boards in the flat way, and nobody thanked her for saying it, and four people in that market street had counted the people at the boards and found nine without him and about five had not counted.

“**I am not going to say why he is not here,**” she said, “**and I am not going to say whether he knows what is being said in this street, and I will not be asked, because if I am asked then I am the person who is asked, and I have been the person who is asked for about four and a half years and I have not got a head over me and I want to keep it that way for about four more months.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards had heard a keeper of a room refuse a question before it was asked and about five had heard a woman explain what she is.

Then the four were asked, and they were asked in the open, one at a time, and nine people watched it happen and five of those nine could have told you before it started which way each of the four would go.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned was asked first and she said no in about four seconds.

“**I said no on the nineteenth of Fallowmonth in this street and I have had about a month to sit with it and I am still no. I am not a road and I am not a crossing and I am not a head and a person who carries a thing for nine counties is a head.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards had heard the number and about five had heard a woman say a thing twice.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat said no in about four seconds and gave no reason at all, and about nine people at the boards noticed that a man who had a reason for everything in Fallowmonth had not got one for this.

The woman of about thirty-eight who keeps the tally at a post came up from the coast on the Thursday and was asked and said no in about four seconds, and nobody argued with her, and she went back up the made road on the Friday with nine days in front of her and a cart and one carter.

The fourth of the four was a man of about forty-four who has kept a wain and a team on this made road for about thirty-one years and who had come down four hundred yards from a yard that was loading timber off a waggon, and he said no in about four seconds, and then he said the thing that about four people in that market street understood and about five did not.

“**I have been asked things four times this year,**” the man of about forty-four said, “**by two men in a livery and by a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a horse and by somebody I have never met, and I have said no three times and yes once, and the yes was about a horse. I would like nine people in this street to know that the one I said yes to was a horse and not a person.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at the boards had heard a man of forty-four put a horse in the middle of a thing and about five had heard a man say no three times.

Then the badge-man said the last of it and he said it to about nine people and not to the four hundred, and it was the true thing and he did not add to it.

“**So there are four people in nine counties who could carry anything and all four have said no in the open this morning, and that cannot be put right by talking about it, and I am not going to try, and I would like about nine people to notice that this morning is what being harder to get at looks like from the inside of it.**”

Nobody thanked him and two hundred people in that market street had been standing still for about a minute and then about half of them went back to what they were doing, and nine people stayed, and the market went on and the boards were still there and nothing had been decided.

What happened at about the third hour was not decided either. The woman of about twenty-four who keeps a copy-book picked the book up off the boards with both hands, the way she had put it down, and carried it back down the street to her shop front, and shut it, and put it on a shelf on its side against the wall where the dust had been gathering on about nine shelves in about four years, and said out loud, to about four people who were passing and about five who were not, that she was not going to keep a thing that nobody had named and that she would take it back up when somebody had a word for it and not before.

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street heard a woman of about twenty-four say that she would take a book back and about five did not, and about four people in nine counties have said since that she was wrong and about five have said since that she was right, and neither of those sets has ever spoken to the other.

**And the cost of that Saturday morning is nine hundred words under a cloth that nobody will withdraw and nobody will sign, four people who could carry anything and have said no in the open, and a copy-book on a shelf on its side in a shop front, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a quill pen at 4, a cake of ink at 5, a sheet of blotting paper at 19, a purse string at 22, a stick of red chalk at 23, a rushlight holder at 29, a bundle of pegs at 30, a hoop for a pail at 32, a sieve at 34, and a mail bag at 39.** Four, five, nineteen, twenty-two, twenty-three, twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-two, thirty-four and thirty-nine is two hundred and thirty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and thirty-seven pence is four marks and forty-five over.** The woman of about twenty-four put her hand flat on the book before she turned it on its side, which is not a thing anybody in nine counties has ever done to a copy-book, and then she went out to the front of her shop and set out a board of nine hundred words in her own hand and did not put a word at the top of it, and about nine people going to market that morning walked past a shop front with nine hundred words on a board and no name on them, and about four of them stopped and about five did not.
