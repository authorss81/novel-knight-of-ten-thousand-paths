# Chapter 0657

**Longlight the twentieth, YR 321, a Monday and not a market morning, a man of thirty-two having walked four miles in the dark to look at four marks on somebody else's water and four miles back, the second hour at the boards with the room over the weigh-house shut, and one paragraph off the ferry city that has taken three weeks to come and has cost somebody a job.**

The four marks were on the woman of about fifty's road and Aren Kest had not seen them, and he had been told about them by two people who had seen them and had believed both of them, and he had made a career out of not believing two people who had seen a thing, and about a fortnight ago he had made a different kind of mistake about something else entirely and had not yet worked out which of the two it was.

So he walked down. Four miles, out at about the third hour, with his stick in his left hand, past about four houses and a mile and a half of made road and a mile and a half of lane that stops being made, and he was at the ferry stone at about the fifth hour when there was light to see by and he was back at the market town's gate at about the tenth hour.

The marks were four pieces of lime the size of a fist, gone hard, put into the joint of a stone at the height of a man's thumb, one at the ferry stone and one at each of three places up the run.

He knelt down and put his hand on each of them and stood up and looked up the run and then walked back down it at about half the speed and looked at it again, which is the thing a man does who is good at loads and is not good at politics.

The palm of his left hand was warm. It had been warm at about the fourth hour on the mile and a half where the made road stops being made, and it was warm now at the seventh hour with his hand in about a foot of cold water, and it had been warm on about nine days out of the last thirty, and he had mentioned it to nobody on any of the nine and had not thought about mentioning it.

He took his hand out of the water and looked at it and put it back in.

The woman of about fifty had made this ford passable on about nine days in the last four years and had made it passable on about forty more without meaning to, by going out with a bar in spate, twice for a delegation and about nine times for her own road. He had been down about four hundred paces of it in a fortnight. Nothing about this crossing was his, and nobody in this county had asked him to stand in it, and the woman who kept it would have said so on about nine days' notice and had not been asked, and he said out loud, to a stone in a foot of water, that he was sorry for that.

Then the fourth mark answered, and that is the whole of what it was, and it is set down here because it happened in a foot of water on the twentieth of Longlight with about nobody watching.

It was not a voice and it was not a light and nobody in nine counties could have written it down afterwards without being laughed at. The water in his hand stopped being cold, and the whole four hundred yards of a ford that a woman keeps came up warm from the bottom to the sill in the way a wet stone comes up warm when a hand has been on it. The six men who had been in that water on the eighth of Longlight were not there. The man who had marked it was not there. Nobody was there, and it was over in a second and a half, and it cost nothing that anybody could put on a slate.

He stood in the water and did not move.

He said out loud, to himself, in about nine words, what it was, and then he said out loud, to himself again, in about nine words, what it had cost, and nobody heard either and nobody has since, and he did not put it in a notebook and there is no notebook in this county that has it in it and he did not look at the separate leaf in the two rooms over a saddler's that night and he has not looked at it yet.

What he said the first time was that a road could be open and he had not opened it.

What he said the second time was that he could go further now and there was nobody to ask him whether he wanted to.

Then he got out of the water and his palm was not warm and his left boot was full to the top and he walked back up four miles and he was at the market town's gate at about the tenth hour.

A mark at the height of a thumb is not a survey mark. A survey mark is on a post and it is above the water and you can see it from a distance and it tells you a height. A mark at the height of a thumb, four times, along a run, on the stones that are already there, is somebody writing down where the water stops. It is a tide line. It is what a man lays down so that the next man, in about four years, can look at a wall and know how high it came.

There were four of them and the highest was on the third stone past the ferry stone and it was a foot and about nine inches above the bed, and the lowest was up at the third marker and was three inches below the crown of the sill there.

He got his slate out of the side of his coat, which he had not carried a slate in about eleven years and had bought it in the market on the Thursday for a farthing and had a piece of it on because he had nothing else to write on, and he wrote four figures on the back of a handbill that had been off the boards and was in the dirt, and then he put the slate away and did not look at it again.

That is what the four marks were for and that is all they were for, and they were not for the road and they were not for a crossing and they were not for a column of about nine hundred men on a road about four days off, and they were put in by a clerk of about thirty-one who had been told to take the level and had taken it.

Aren Kest stood in a foot of water at the third mark at about the seventh hour on the twentieth of Longlight and understood that a Crown officer who wants to shut a ford on a particular day needs to know how high the water comes, and that the way to know that is not a survey and not a map and not a road office.

It is four marks on four stones, put there by one man with a stick of chalk in a morning, and then read in four years by another.

About four people at the market town's gate saw him come in off the lane in the state a man is in after eight miles with a limp, and about five did not, and he went and sat down for twenty minutes and did not tell anybody what he had gone to look at.

The paragraph came at about the second hour on the Monday.

The contact had been opened on the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth and it is the longest contact anybody in this county has run since the hearing started and it has no month and no weekday in it in anybody's mouth, because the woman of about sixty at the coast keeps the count in that manner and had said so out loud before anybody asked her to, and the copyist wrote the count on a leaf with no head over it: an open-crossing count, then a number of weeks, then an hour. Three weeks, and it is made into days exactly once, by a man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat, on a Tuesday in that week, out loud, at the low end of the boards, and nobody argued with him about it, and it is not made again anywhere, and nobody in the market street who heard him asked him to do it again, because about nine people in that street had by that week stopped believing that a thing which can be said in one manner can be said in another.

It came into the room over the weigh-house on the Monday in a folded leaf with a mark on the corner and no seal on it and nothing on the back, and the copyist of about twenty-six carried it down thirteen stairs and read it out loud at the boards in nine minutes with about nine people standing there and about four carts and a bar going about a Monday, which is nothing, and a hundred people who slowed down.

The woman of about sixty from the delegation was standing at about four paces off with her hands in her sleeves and did not sit down for any of it.

It was from the ferry city. It was from a woman who has kept a crossing there for about nine years and who has been in two rooms in this county in her life and who was in one of them for about nine days.

“**You will have the sheet by now,**” it said. “**I am not going to describe it. I am going to answer the question your woman asked, which was whether we were to be an army, and the answer is no, and I am not going to soften it, and I am not going to give you the reasons, and if you have nine counties that need reasons then you have nine counties that have not asked properly.**”

“**We will not come. We will not be moved. Do not ask us again this year.**”

“**And I am going to say one more thing and then this letter is finished and I am going back to a crossing, and you will not get another one of these off me in a hurry.**”

“**A city that was never severed does not take instruction from a paper a city that was. When that paper was made there were four thousand people in a city and about seven hundred and twenty in seven villages and there was a road across a river and about nine men in an office who decided that the road was the problem. I have been on that road. It is a good road and it is four hundred yards long and there is a bell on the end of it that was cracked before any of the people I work with were born and that nobody has rung.**”

“**Your paper says the peace and quiet of the realm. I have heard that phrase nine times in eleven years and it has been about nine different things and every one of them was somebody else being quiet.**”

“**You have nine counties and about nine people in a market street. Do not send us a paper. If you ever want to know whether we would help you, come and stand at a crossing and ask, and be prepared to be told no, and do not write the no down and do not come back with it.**”

Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her anything and the copyist folded the leaf and put it in the drawer about eleven inches deep, which is not far enough for the drawer and never has been.

Then the copyist said the only thing in that leaf that anybody in the market street wanted, and she said it before anybody could ask for it.

“**The woman of about sixty on the coast keeps the count of crossings open and weeks shut and she did that for nothing,**” the copyist said, “**and she is not the one who wrote that letter and the person who carried it is not a person any of us has met and I do not have a name for them, and I have written a person in the front of this leaf and I am reading it out because it is in the room and the room is shut on a Monday like every other day that is not a market morning, and about nine of you are standing at the boards because of what is on it.**”

“**I have written the woman who wrote it in the front of this leaf and nobody else and she is not in this county. The person who carried it has been doing a job on the far side of the count for about nine weeks and the job is holding a line of boats in weather,**” the copyist said, “**and that line of boats is how about nine hundred people on that coast get bread and salt in a month when the water is up. It has been held by two men in rotation for about two years. It cannot be held by one man for three weeks and I am not going to pretend to anybody in this street that it can, and I have said so out loud, and I am not the person who decided.**”

“**Who did?**” Salla Dunnock said.

“**Nobody decided,**” the copyist said. “**It is the way a thing is done when nobody is in a position to decide and everybody would do it. That is a sentence I have wanted to say in a street for about four and a half years and I have never had anything to say it about.**”

About nine people in that market street were standing at the boards and about four of them had a woman of about thirty-two leaning against a gate about nine paces off who had walked eight miles that morning to look at four marks and had come back and had not told anybody what the marks were for.

The woman of about thirty-eight took the leaf back off the copyist and read the middle of it again, out loud, to about nine people, and she read it in the words it was written in and she stopped at the part about the bell.

“**She has said no,**” Salla Dunnock said. “**And she has said no in a way I can put in my own mouth in about nine hundred mouths, and I did not expect that and I came here ready for something else and I have been in this town twenty-four days and there has been about nine things I did not expect.**”

Then she said the thing that she had come to say and had not been able to say for twenty-four days, and she said it to Aren Kest and not to the nine.

“**You still have not told me which of the two I should be ready for,**” she said, “**and you told me on the twenty-sixth of Greenmonth, at the low side of a gate, that you would not, and I have thought about it every day and I have got to a different place and I am going to tell you where it is and then you can say nothing at all about it, which I notice is a thing you are good at.**”

“**Do not say it here.**”

“**I am going to go back along a road in the morning,**” she said, “**and I am going to go back along a road that a man with a stick of chalk has been measuring for about four days and has not shut and is not going to, and that is the answer, and it is not the answer to my question and it is the answer to the one I am actually going home with, and I am not going to pretend to you that it is the same, and you can put that in whatever you keep.**”

She said it out loud in a street with about nine people in it and she said it to him, which meant that about nine people knew that the two of them had had a conversation at a gate twenty-five days ago, and about four of them went back to about four houses in the market street and told somebody in four days, and about five of them did not.

Aren Kest stood at the high end of the boards with his hand on his stick and did not answer her, and about nine people did not look at him at all.

**And the cost of that Monday is a column and it is nobody's, and four marks of lime at the height of a thumb and about nine weeks of two men in rotation holding a line of boats in weather are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a straw for a bed at 4, a pot for a fire at 10, a sheet of vellum at 18, a barrow of loam at 25, a three-legged stool at 30, a tin lamp at 34, a frayed rug at 37, a rush basket at 39, a clean cloth at 40, and a board of deal at 41.** Four, ten, eighteen, twenty-five, thirty, thirty-four, thirty-seven, thirty-nine, forty, and forty-one is two hundred and seventy-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and seventy-eight pence is five marks and thirty-eight over.** The leaf went into the drawer about eleven inches deep at about the third hour and the drawer was full to about where it is always full to and the copyist pushed it in with the heel of her hand, and about four hundred returns and one leaf and one question on a leaf and one standing of four and a half years on a leaf and one leaf with a page described on it went in at about the third hour of the twentieth of Longlight, and nobody in nine counties has ever taken that drawer out and counted what was in it and nobody in nine counties is going to.

Then the badge-man came down the lane at about the fourth hour, which he had not done in about nine days, and stood at the low end of the boards and read the copyist's reading of the standing of the hearing off the top of her head about nine seconds before she got to it, and got two of the nine words wrong, and she corrected him, and he said she was right and that he had said it wrong on purpose about four times in a year to see whether anybody in the room was actually listening, and that in about a year of standing in this street about four people had ever corrected him and about nine had stopped listening about four months ago, and that he would take the four.

Nobody thanked him for being corrected and the copyist said that she had not corrected him out loud and had corrected him in her hand on the leaf and had said it out loud only because she had a leaf in her hand at the time, and that if he made a habit of it she would stop.
