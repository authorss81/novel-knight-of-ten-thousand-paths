# Chapter 0656

**Longlight the sixteenth, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street with the room over the weigh-house open behind it, a road stood down for two days without being shut, and a man with a badge counting out loud how wrong he has been.**

The sheet had been on the boards four days and it had gone to eight other places in nine counties in the meantime, and it was on eight gates, because the district had said put it up and had not said where and the copyist's instruction had been given to one clerk in one town about one set of boards and had gone about nine counties like everything else a room says.

That is in the column. Nobody in nine counties is going to blame a man for putting up a notice where a notice goes.

The man of about forty-four who keeps a road in the waterworks country came into the market on the Thursday at about the second hour and stood at the boards for a minute and a half and then went and sat down on the wall where the woman of about sixty sits, and did not say anything for half an hour.

Then he said it.

“**I stood my road down on Monday,**” he said, “**and I want nine people to know that I did not shut it and that it is not shut, and that I stood my crew down because a sheet says the persons and labour of the district are required to give such aid as the officers shall set out, and I read that and I thought, well, they may set some out, and I have no way of knowing what they are setting out and it is Longlight and there is a ford on it, and I sent my men home on Monday morning and I have had nobody on that road since.**”

Nobody argued with him and about four people at the boards said nothing and about five said something, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have read a sheet that says the persons and labour are required to give aid and have read it as a thing that would one day say which aid.

“**How long,**” the badge-man said, and he said it the way he asks everything.

“**Two days. It is Thursday. It was Monday. I have four men and about nine households at the low end and the water is off four miles of made road and there is a week left of the dry and then it will not be a road and I do not know whether anybody is coming.**”

“**Is the road shut?**”

“**No,**” the man of about forty-four said, “**and that is the whole of it. If I had shut it I would have done it properly and your man would have been stood in front of it with a witness named by the people who work it, and I do not believe any of you would have let it through, and I did not want to find out. So I did not shut it.**”

About nine people in that market street heard a road-keeper explain why he had chosen the one thing that could not be argued with, and about four of them understood that he had chosen it on purpose, and about five understood that he had chosen it because it was easier, and neither of those was the whole of it and he did not correct either.

The badge-man said the thing that the whole morning turned on and he said it in about as many words as it took.

“**Then that is the answer to the question I have been asking for four days,**” he said, “**and I want the nine of you to hear where it comes from, because it does not come from the sheet. It comes from the fact that I built a rule about roads being shut, and about nine people agreed it out loud in this street, and every clause in it is a man doing one thing to a road, and I did not build any of it for a man who does nothing to a road and stands his men down on Tuesday and puts them back on Thursday and does not shut a single thing in between.**”

“**That is what it is for,**” the woman of about thirty-four who came from a third party in a market four days late said, from about nine paces off. “**Do not stand there and look at me like a man with a badge who has no head. I said it. It covers roads that can be written to and it finds people. It did not cover this and he has just told nine people that he knew it did not cover it and did it anyway, and you are all waiting for him to be caught, and he was not caught, because there is nothing in the sheet that says he cannot.**”

Nobody thanked her and the man of about forty-four said that she was right and that he had known it in about nine seconds on Monday morning and had sent his men home in about four minutes, and that he would do it again, and that he would like somebody to tell him what else there was to do with a piece of paper in his hand.

Mara Vey said it.

“**There is nothing you can bring it to,**” she said.

She had come down from the room and was standing at about four paces from the boards with the register under her arm, which she had brought down on the Thursday because it was a market morning and she brings it down on market mornings and had done so about four and a half years.

“**Bring it to us,**” she said, “**and I will read it into a book, and the book has nine questions in it and no finding under any of them, and I will not put a finding under this one, and there is no head in nine counties with which to call a meeting and the man who says so is standing about four paces from you.**” She turned the register round in her arms. “**Bring it to him and he will say out loud that it is not false because he thinks it is false, and he will be right, and he said it about nine months ago and he was right then and it has taken him a year and a half to be proved right in the worst possible way.**”

“**I did not say that this morning,**” the badge-man said. “**I said something else this morning and I said it at about the third hour and there were about nine people there.**”

“**You said that it shuts nothing,**” Mara Vey said, “**and I am not disputing it and nobody in this street is disputing it. I am saying that a room that cannot say a sheet is false was the reason a sheet with a name at the top of it was obeyed in this county for nine days a year ago, and that you told me in a lane in about nine counties that the only difference between that sheet and a false one is that a false one can be shown to be false, and that nobody had ever shown it, and that this one is not false and still cannot be got at, because there is nobody in nine counties whose business it is to say that a Crown instrument has no business here.**”

“**Then it is the same sheet,**” the badge-man said.

“**It is the same hole with a different paper in it,**” Mara Vey said, “**and the hole is not the paper and it is not the name at the top of it. The hole is that nine counties have nobody to ask.**”

About nine people in the market street heard that and about four of them had heard something like it before and about five had not.

The badge-man said that he was not going to argue with it and that she was better at this than he was and had been for about four and a half years, and that he would say the one thing he had to say and then he would go and do something else.

“**You are right and I have got no head to give you,**” he said, “**and I am not going to make one out of the fact that a woman has said something clever in a street on a market morning, and I have been asked about nine times this month whether I would make one and I have said no nine times and I am saying it a tenth time in front of about nine people.**”

Nobody asked him a tenth time.

Aren Kest was at the high end of the boards for all of it and about nine people in that market street did not know he was there, which had been true for about a year and which on the sixteenth of Longlight he had begun to find expensive.

He had the answer to the thing the woman of about thirty-four had just said. It was in his hands at about the eighth hour on the second of Longlight and it was three sentences long, and one of the three was the only sentence in it that anybody in nine counties could have argued with, and he had not said it, and a woman of about twenty-nine had looked at him across four feet of market at about the fourth hour on the second of Longlight and had not helped him and had said so.

He thought about the two rooms over a saddler's and the spare blanket on the sill and the half leaf lying face down on the table with nobody in nine counties who had turned it over or been going to, and he thought that if he said the sentence out loud on this market morning then nine counties would have it by Thursday and the badge-man would be the one who had not asked him to say it, and that in about a year there would be a version in which the badge-man had thought of it first, because that is what happens to a thing said in a street on a market morning.

And he thought, about the fourth hour on a Thursday in Thawmonth, that he had said in a lane to about four people who could hear that he wanted it, and that nobody had asked him, and that the only thing that had come of that in about a month had been nine people looking at him at a board, and that being wanted and being asked are not the same thing and that about four years of this county had turned out to be the difference between them.

He said nothing, and it cost him, and about nine people did not look at him at all, and nobody thanked him for saying nothing because nobody knew there was anything to be thanked for.

Then the badge-man did the thing that the last page of Greenmonth had promised anybody was coming, and he did it standing at the low end of the counter boards at about the fourth hour of a Thursday in Longlight in front of about nine people, and he did it in about nine seconds, and then he stood there while about four of them worked it out.

“**I said something out loud in this street on the twenty-fifth of Greenmonth,**” he said, “**and I have said it about four times since and I am going to say it now and then I am going to stop saying it for the rest of the year. I said that the meeting was not finished and that there is no rule about who calls the next one and that there is no head to call it with, and that I am not going to make one, and that in about four months somebody is going to want a thing out of this and there will be nobody to ask.**”

“**Well,**” he said, “**it is the sixteenth of Longlight. I would not like anybody in this street to do the sum for me out loud, so I am going to do it out loud and I am going to be wrong out loud, because I have been wrong out loud before and it costs less than being wrong quietly.**”

“**Greenmonth the twenty-fifth to Longlight the sixteenth is twenty-one days,**” the badge-man said, “**and I said four months, and I was wrong by about three months and about nine days, and I am going to say the true thing about that and then I am going to stop. Being right early about a thing you were right about is the same as being wrong about how long you had. I knew that the thing would come. I did not know when it would come and I gave a figure because a man who says soon is a man who has not thought, and a man who gives a figure has thought and got it wrong, and I would rather be the second. I have been the first twice and both times it cost somebody a year.**”

Nobody said anything about that.

“**And I am not moving the figure,**” he said. “**I am not going to say it was four months from March. It is twenty-one days and it was twenty-one days and about nine people in this street can count.**”

A man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat took it out and did it and put it back and did not make a tally of it, on the stated ground that a count of days is not a measurement of anything anybody had done, and nobody asked him to and nobody thanked him for it, and it was the first time anybody in that market had seen that satchel in about four months and about nine people noticed the coat and about five noticed the sheet.

Then the badge-man said that he was going to do the other thing he had come into the market to do, which was nothing, and went and sat on the wall.

Salla Dunnock came to the boards at about the fifth hour and put her hand flat on the back of them and said the sentence she had come eleven days to be able to say.

“**I have been in this town twenty days,**” she said, “**and I asked whether we were to be an army and I have got my answer and it is the answer I did not expect, which is that there is a paper and nobody has asked you anything and the paper does not ask you anything either. I can go back and say that. I want to say it out loud here first and I want about nine people to hear me say it, and then I am going to go back along a road that I have been told is not shut, and I would like about nine people to notice that I have said the words not shut and that I have not been asked to.**”

Nobody noticed it except about four people.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is nobody's, and about four men stood down for two days on a road that was not shut and about nine households at the low end lost a week of the dry in the doing are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a broom at 3, a cake of soap at 9, a jug at 16, a sheet of clean linen at 24, a footstool at 28, a hand shovel at 33, an oil lamp at 35, a table at 38, a candlestick at 40, and a wax taper at 41.** Three, nine, sixteen, twenty-four, twenty-eight, thirty-three, thirty-five, thirty-eight, forty, and forty-one is two hundred and sixty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and sixty-seven pence is five marks and twenty-seven over.** The man of about forty-four put his four men back on the road at about the sixth hour on the Thursday, the same Thursday, four days after he had taken them off, and he said out loud at the gate at the low end that he had stood them down and was putting them back and did not know whether he was right and would be told in a week, and about nine people at the gate heard him and about five of them repeated it at the gate at the high end in the evening, and by the Saturday the second half of it had got about nine miles further down the road and by the following week it had become two sentences and by Harvestmonth it had become a story a man who shut a road for two days, which was not what he had done and which he did not hear for a month.

Then the copyist of about twenty-six went up the thirteen stairs with the register and put it on the table in the room with a door on a lane and did not open it, and the press stood open with no date anybody would give, and the outgoing sheet had four ruled lines on it and the fourth was empty and was not going to be filled, and about eleven inches of drawer held about four hundred returns, and none of it was any use at all to anybody in nine counties, and that was the finding of the sixteenth of Longlight and nobody thanked her for it.
