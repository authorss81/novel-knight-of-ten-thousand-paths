# Chapter 0670

**Fallowmonth the thirteenth, YR 321, a Sunday and not a market morning, the second hour at the boards in a market street that is not a market, the seventh hour at the low end of a woman's four miles, a chain across a ford at about the eighth hour, and about nine people standing in four inches of water while one of them answers a question out loud.**

What happened on the Saturday was this, and it is set down here before anything else because everything after it is a consequence, and nine people in this county have already described the week before it as the week things started.

The captain came up the lane at about the eleventh hour with four men and a serjeant and a small book, and he came up to the boards at the low end of the market town's gate, and he asked the question in the open in front of about four hundred people on a market morning, and he said it in about nine words.

“**Which place or road is meant?**”

Nobody answered. Not the badge-man, not the copyist, not the man of about thirty-four with a sheet, not the woman of about thirty-six, not the man of about fifty-eight's own deputy, because there is no deputy, and not four hundred other people who were in that street. It took as long as it takes a carter to get a load round, and four hundred people found nine things to do.

Then the serjeant wrote a line in the small book, and the captain read it back, and about four people in that market street heard what he had written.

“**Asked at the boards, the twelfth of Fallowmonth, and no place given.**”

Nobody argued with it. Nobody said the man had no right. Nobody said that a road with a name could be argued with and a road without one could only be taken, and four people in that market street have said that sentence in nine different places since, and the first of them was the woman of about fifty at the low end of her own four miles on the Sunday morning, and she said it after she had already said the other thing.

The room over the weigh-house was shut on the Sunday, which it is on every day that is not a market morning, and nine people were at the boards at about the second hour, and the street had four carts in it and no market and a man with a bar who had been and gone.

The badge-man said the standing notice in about nine words and nobody stopped him, and he put the thing about the first of every month into it out of thirty-one years of habit, and then stopped in the middle of the sentence, and said that the first of a month was eighteen days off and that he would not say the rest of it on a Sunday, and four people at the boards understood why and five did not.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came down the lane at about the second hour with the register under her arm, on a Sunday, which she does not do, and she stood at about four paces from the high end of the boards and she did not come further and she did not go back.

Aren Kest was at the high end of the boards and she was at four paces off it and neither of them moved for the whole of the hour, and in that hour they said one thing to each other, and the thing was about the register.

“**The fourth line is ruled and it is empty,**” she said, to nobody, in the flat way.

“**I know it is,**” Aren Kest said.

Nobody said anything else to anybody and about four people at the boards understood that two people who keep separate things had said one sentence to each other in the open about a book, and about five understood that something had happened, and nobody thanked anybody for either of it, and about nine people in this county could have told you that those two have not stood in the same room in about a year, and about four of them would have said that it is the fourth of a month on a Sunday and it means nothing.

Then about nine people went down the lane, and about four of them went because they had been asked to and about five went because a man with a stick was going and that has been the whole of his function in this county for about a year.

They were at the ford at the sixth hour.

The ford is at the low end of a woman's four miles and it is four miles from the market town and the made road stops being made a mile and a half before it gets there. There is a ferry stone about four feet high at the edge of the water that has been there longer than anybody and is not a ferry. There are four marks of lime at the height of a thumb in four stones, put in on the eighth of Longlight by a clerk who was told to take the level, and they are where the water stops, and the highest of them is a foot and nine inches above the bed and the lowest is three inches below the crown of the sill at the third marker.

There was a chain across it.

It is a new chain, about thirty feet of it, and it is on two posts that have been driven either side of the approach, and about four inches of water is coming over the sill, and the four marks are nine feet above the water at the low side and two feet below it at the high side, which is what a month of rain does, and nobody in nine counties has a figure for the water and nobody is going to be given one.

There were four men and a serjeant on the far bank, and they were not working and they were not idling, they were standing at about the four places where a man stands when he is holding a place he has been told to hold, and nine people who keep that mile and a half came down in forty minutes to look at a chain, and four of them had come for a different reason.

The captain was there at about the seventh hour and he came down the bank on foot and did not send anybody, and he stood about nine paces off and did not come closer, and about nine people looked at a man of about forty-four in a coat that has been worn nine hundred miles and has been brushed since.

“**I want to say four things and then I am going to stop talking and let the county do what it is going to do,**” he said, and he said them in four minutes, and nine people heard all four.

“**One. I have not shut that ford. I have taken it. Those are not the same and I have thought about the difference for nine days and I am going to say it in a lane because the difference is the whole of my difficulty and nobody in nine counties can see it from where they stand.**”

“**Say the difference,**” the woman of about fifty said. She had a slate in her apron and the last column of it was begun, which is what she does in the first week of a month, and she had not written anything on it.

“**A thing that is shut has an end to it,**” the captain said. “**Somebody closes it and everybody can see the closure and everybody can come and stand at it and say it is shut, and then it is a fact that about nine people agreed on, and the Crown has to argue with about nine people instead of with a hole in a county. A thing that is taken has no end to it and no moment. It is in force from the hour of the sealing of it, which is about eleven weeks ago, and it has been in force the whole time, and there is nothing to stand at.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at that ford understood the whole of that and five understood that a captain had said something about a chain.

“**Two.**” He turned the small book over in his hand and did not open it. “**My paper says that any person being asked which place or road is meant shall answer. I asked, on Saturday, at your boards, in a market, in front of four hundred people, and nobody answered, and my serjeant wrote down that nobody answered, and that line is going to be read out in a room with a table in it in four years, and it is going to be read as a county that has been asked and has not answered, and it is going to be read that way by a man who is not a knave.**”

“**It is not true,**” the woman of about thirty-six said. “**It is a county that has been asked and has nobody in it who has standing to answer. Those are the same on your page and they are not the same in this lane.**”

“**I know they are not,**” the captain said. “**And I have four men at a ford with about nine days of forage and no written order to hold it, and I am holding it with men I cannot spare for about nine counties of work, and every day I hold it the more it looks like a closure, and I have no way of telling from where I am standing whether a thing I am doing is a thing I am doing or a thing I have become. And I have not got anybody in nine counties to ask, and I have not got a head to ask, and I have been told there is no head and I believe it.**”

Nobody said anything to that and four people at that ford had understood a thing about a man and five had understood a thing about a county.

“**Three, and this is the part I have not said to anybody and I am saying it in a lane because there is nobody else to say it to,**” the captain said. “**I have read your three clauses. A copy of them came down the road with a letter about a drain and I have read them, and I have read them twice, and I have had nine men read them, and not one of my men can find anything in them that stops me, and I have looked for something for nine days, and I am telling you that I did not look and that I looked.**”

The woman of about thirty-six said the thing that is the finding of that morning, and she said it out loud in a lane at a ford in front of nine people, and four of them understood all of it and five understood half of it.

“**Then they are not broken,**” she said. “**That is the whole of it and I want nine people to hear it in this order. The notice wants a man who proposes to shut a road to go to the end of it and say it out loud. Nobody in nine counties has proposed to shut this ford. The witness wants somebody named by the people who work this road. A man of about twenty-six could have named a man of about seventeen and it would have been perfectly done and the road would be shut exactly the same. The burden wants a man who said a road may be shut and who can be found afterwards, and there is no such man, and so the burden falls on nobody.**”

“**Then none of the three is broken,**” the badge-man said, from the bank, out loud, in a lane, with a chain in the water behind him and about nine people in front of him and a captain of about forty-four listening.

“**None of the three is broken,**” the woman of about thirty-six said, “**and the ford is shut, and that is not a hole in the rule. That is the rule working exactly as nine people made it about four and a half years ago, because the rule is about a man who proposes, and no man has proposed anything, and a man who is carrying out a paper is not a man who proposes.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people at that ford had understood the whole of that, and four of them had said nothing at all and five had understood half of it.

“**Four,**” the captain said, and he said it to the woman of about fifty, and he said it in the ordinary way, and he said it in front of nine people. “**Which place or road is meant?**”

That was the moment. Nine people at a ford at the eighth hour of a Sunday in Fallowmonth have said since that it was the moment, and four of them have been able to tell you what she was doing with her hands, and five have not been able to tell you anything about it.

The woman of about fifty had a slate in her apron and the last column of it begun, and she took it out, and she looked at it, and she put it back in the apron, and she did not write anything on it, and four people at that ford understood that she had decided not to write and five did not.

Then she answered, and she answered out loud, in a lane, in the rain, in front of a Crown captain and a serjeant and four men in a livery and nine people who keep a mile and a half.

“**This road,**” she said. “**This one. It goes from a stone in the mud at the low end to a milestone at the top and it is four miles and it is mine and it has been for nineteen years and my name is not on any paper in this county and it has not been on any paper in this county since before the hearing started, and that is why you could not find it, and I have just given it to you in a lane, and I would like nine people to notice that I was not asked whether I wanted to give it, and that nobody thought to ask me, and that I would have given it.**”

Nobody said anything at all.

“**I want to say the other half of it out loud,**” she said, “**and I am going to say it to him and not to you, because he has been honest in this lane for about four minutes and about four of you have noticed and about five have not. A road with a name on it can be argued with. A road without one can only be taken. I have been standing in market streets for nineteen years saying that to about nine people who keep roads, and about four of them believed me and about five of them wrote it down and neither of those two things is the same as it being useful.**”

“**And I know what I have done,**” she said. “**I have given a Crown officer the name of four miles of road in a lane, in the rain, in front of about nine people, and he has got a book, and it is going in it, and in about four years a man is going to read it out in a room with a table in it. And the day after that happens this ford is the only ford anybody in nine counties has ever heard of, and it will be shut because it has a name and not because anybody had anything to do with a ford. And I have thought about that in about four seconds and I am going to give it anyway, because the alternative is that it is shut with no name on it and nobody can ever argue with it, and there are four houses at the low end of this lane and in the worst week four in ten of the households at the two ends of my four miles cannot go round, and I said that figure out loud in a market street in Longlight and a man with a badge told me not to make it into a rule, and he was right, and it is not a rule, and it is about nine people who live at the low end of a lane that is under about a foot of water right now.**”

Nobody thanked her and the badge-man did not say that the man of about fifty had been right, and four people at that ford noticed that he did not say it and five noticed that he had not said it at all.

Then the captain wrote it down, and he wrote it in the small book, and he wrote it at once, and four hundred people had watched a man of about forty-four do that on a board in the rain on the nineteenth of Rainmonth, and nine people at a ford watched him do it on a Sunday, and four of them understood what it was and five understood that a name was going into a book.

Then he read it back to her, all of it, in the way he read the board back to a woman at a gate in a county two days off, and he did not soften a word of it and he did not read it faster than he had to.

Then the woman of about fifty said that it was right, and the captain said that it was very good of her, and the chain stayed where it was, and nobody thanked anybody.

**And the cost of that Sunday is a column, and it is nobody's, and about thirty feet of new chain and four inches of water over a sill and a four-mile road that has a name in a book now are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a chain of thirty feet at 29, a bollard at 30, a hand spike at 31, a bag of gravel at 32, a pick at 34, a second lantern at 35, a coil of rope at 36, a creel at 37, a mattock at 39, and a padlock at 40.** Twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six, thirty-seven, thirty-nine and forty is three hundred and forty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and three hundred and forty-three pence is seven marks and seven over.** The captain took his four men and his serjeant up the made road at about the ninth hour and left the chain on its two posts, and nine hundred paces up that road a yard was loading a sill, and nobody in that yard had heard about a name.

Then the woman of about fifty went up four miles of wet road with a lamp in her hand, and four people at the ford watched her go and five watched the water, and she did not stop at her own gate.

About half a mile up that road there is a gate on the low side with a house behind it, and the water off four miles of made road comes over the crown at that gate and goes across the lane, and the woman of about fifty stopped at it, and stood in it, and put the lamp down on the dry side so that it would not go out, and stood in about four inches of water in a lane at the ninth hour on the thirteenth of Fallowmonth and said out loud, to nobody, that she had been doing that for nineteen years and that there had never been a name on the road, and that there was one now, in a book, in a coat, and that the difference was going to be that in about four years a man would be able to say she had been told, and she would not be able to say she had not.

She picked the lamp up and went on up the road, and the water came over the crown at that gate for the rest of the night and went across the lane into the ditch, and about nine people in a market town four miles off did not know any of it, and about four of them would find out in about four years, from a board in a room with a table in it, and one of them was the woman of about fifty and she would be sixty-eight.
