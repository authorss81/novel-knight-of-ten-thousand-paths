**Hearthmonth the first, YR 321, a Wednesday and not a market morning and the first of a month, with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting because a first of a month is not a market morning nine times out of twelve, about four people at the boards out of habit, a bar of wood in the grass at the foot of a lane that nobody has lifted, a fourth course of stone that nobody has laid, and a man of about sixty-two with a wain keeping one horse out of a team of four.**

A first of a month comes whether or not anybody is in the street for it, and the first of Hearthmonth is a Wednesday, and there is no market on a Wednesday, and the room over the weigh-house is shut behind its own door from noon on Saturday until the seventh hour on Monday, and the boards in that market street stood in an empty street from about the eighth hour until about the eleventh.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that market street had come to the boards out of habit and five had not come, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the hearing has stood on the first of every month for about four and a half years and that nine times out of twelve there is no market, and that nobody has ever printed that anywhere, and five have said since that a hearing that only works on a market morning is a market and not a hearing.

Nobody thanked him and the badge-man said the true thing about it out loud, at about the ninth hour, in an empty market street, to about four people, and it was the last time anybody said anything in that street for a fortnight.

“**We have spent four and a half years building a thing that stands in a street on the first of a month and there is no street on the first of a month nine times in twelve. I have said that out loud once, on the eighth of Wolfmonth, and four people heard me and five did not, and here is the ninth of those twelves.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that empty market street had heard a man of fifty-eight say the whole of his own arrangement's shape in one sentence and five had heard nothing at all, and the weigh-house under the room had begun weighing at the seventh hour and nine people in that building could hear the town through the floor.

Then the four people at the boards did the only thing there was to do, which was to stand there.

Aren Kest came down the lane at about the ninth hour with his stick in his right hand and stood at the low end of the boards with the other three of them, and he did not say anything at all that morning, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a man of thirty-two who has been wrong in a room in front of about nine people stood in an empty market street on a first of a month and said nothing, and about five have said since that this was the correct arrangement and that they would rather have had the other one.

Nobody thanked him and the woman of about twenty-four who keeps a copy-book did not look at him and he did not look at her, and the man of about thirty-three who sells cloth three doors up did not look at either of them, and about four people in that empty market street had worked out what all three of them were not saying and about five had not.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people stood at the boards for about an hour and a half and five did not come, and one of the four was a man of about thirty-three who sells cloth three doors up and who had not spoken at those boards since the sixth of Frostmonth, and one of the four was the woman of about twenty-four who keeps a copy-book, and one was a man of about fifty-one who owns a field on the low side of a cut a mile and a half off, and one was a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything and nine people in nine counties have said since that the first of a month in Frostmonth was the day nine people worked a cut in the rain and that the first of a month in Hearthmonth was the day four people stood in an empty street, and that neither of those days is the same as the other one, and four people have said that the second of them is the whole of what this county has and five have said that the first of them is.

The other things had not moved either, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a thing in a county in a march is a thing that is standing still while a great deal goes past it.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that empty market street had a standing knowledge of all of that and five had not, and a man of about forty-three who keeps a yard four streets up the market street has been going over that stone in about nine minutes a time for about seventeen weeks and has not stopped and has not said no to anything, because there has never been a thing to say no to.

Nobody thanked him and four people in nine counties have said since that the man who goes over the stone is the reason the winter has a cost in it, and five have said that he is the reason it has a person in it, and neither of those sets has ever spoken to the other.

And a party takes one road in one county at a time off a list of about thirty counties in order, and has been doing it for nine weeks now, and a serjeant of about thirty-eight has been somewhere every three days since the middle of Fallowmonth with a sheet and four men, and nine people in nine counties have said that a three-day interval is a thing that can be counted backwards from today and five have said that nobody is counting it and that is the difference.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that empty market street had a standing knowledge of that and five had not, and the two roads that sent nothing in Wolfmonth are still not sending anything, and they are still the two the column is standing on, and nine people in nine counties have said that a road's silence is worth more in Frostmonth than its writing was in the summer.

The four things that were still where they were on the first of Hearthmonth are the four things.

The nine hundred words are on a table in a room over a saddler's under a cloth, and the cloth has not been moved since the first of Wolfmonth, and nobody has withdrawn them and nobody has signed them, and a woman of about thirty-four who is a ferrywright came nine days into a room in Embermonth and said four sentences about a gate and went back up a made road without a copy, and nobody in nine counties has an answer to what she said and nobody has gone after her, and the sixth clause has not been changed and is not going to be.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that empty market street had a standing knowledge of all of that and five had not.

The register in the room over the weigh-house has ten questions in it and no finding under any of them, and there is a fourth ruled head over a place where a finding goes, and it is empty, and it has been empty for about eleven months, and the copyist of about twenty-six said in that room on a Sunday in Wolfmonth that she was not going to rule a fifth head and that neither was anybody else, and nobody has.

Nobody thanked her and four people in nine counties know that a room is the only place in this county where a thing can be found, and five have never been in it.

The bar of wood is in the grass at the foot of the lane by the Low Gate, and it has been there since before the middle of Longlight, and nobody in nine counties has lifted it, and nobody has asked about it since the middle of the summer, and nobody has written anything down about who put it there, and no day has been named on which anybody will lift it, and the four questions on the board beside it are not going to be asked by anybody in this county.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that empty market street knew where that bar of wood was without being told and five could not have found it, and it is a bar of wood and not a gate and not a closure and not a chain, and nine people in nine counties have said that the one object in this county that nobody has touched is the only one of them that nobody could be blamed for touching.

And the stone for a fourth course is lying in a line on the bank at the high side of the cut at the market town's own gate, with the mallet on top of it, and it has been there since the middle of Rainmonth, and it was not laid on the first of Frostmonth by a mason of about fifty who stood in the rain for a whole day and said out loud that he was not going to lay it, and the mallet has not been picked up since and nobody is scheduled to pick it up.

Nobody thanked him and four people in that market town had walked past that line of stone in the last nine days and five had not noticed that the mason had said out loud that he was not going to lay it, and the water still goes under the road at that gate and the middle course of the sill is still gone and is still getting wider.

Nobody thanked anybody and the first of Hearthmonth is the first of a month and there is no market and nothing in this county has been decided in a month, and that is the finding, and it is not resolved.

Then a man of about thirty-four with a satchel said the only thing anybody said in that street about the finding, and he said it out loud, and nobody thanked him, and four people heard the whole of it.

“**Nine counties has got about nine people and no document, and the two words for that are either coalition or nothing, and I am not going to say either of them in a market street, and I am not going to say the second one in this county at all. A thing with no document cannot be signed and cannot be given away and cannot be taken off anybody, and that is not a thing wrong with the arrangement. I have been looking for something wrong with it for about five weeks and there is not one thing, and I would like about nine people to notice that the worst thing about the arrangement is that it cannot be given away, and that I spent two months in Fallowmonth writing nine hundred words trying to give it away.**”

Nobody thanked him and two hundred people were not in that street and four of the four who were had heard all of that and one of them had understood it.

Then the woman of about thirty-one who keeps the smith's house at the junction asked her question again, in that street, on a first of a month, to about four people, and nobody stopped her.

“**Is any of this for me.**”

Nobody said anything and the badge-man said the true thing about it and did not add to it and nobody thanked him.

“**No, and I have said no once before in this street and I am saying it again on a first of a month because the first time I said it I was busy with a cloth.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that empty market street had heard a man of fifty-eight apologise for being busy, and one understood that he was not joking and three did not.

“**But I am going to say the other half, because you asked me in public twice and a man of about fifty-eight does not get asked in public twice without answering it properly,**” he said, “**and the other half is that if you are on the thing then you are on it, and I do not have to find you, and you do not have to be asked, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is not nothing. It is not nothing and it is not a place, and a person who wants a place is not going to get one out of me, and I would rather say that in a market street on a first of a month than in a lane in March.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that street had heard a man of fifty-eight offer a woman of thirty-one the only thing he has, which is that she would not have to be found, and five have said since that it is the best offer anybody in this county has made to anybody in four and a half years, and four have said since that it is not an offer, and neither of those sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody said anything and the woman of about thirty-one said no out loud, and she said it kindly, and nobody thanked her and nobody argued with her.

“**I do not want to be on a thing I cannot find,**” she said, “**I want to be on a thing with a name on it that a man can read out at a gate, and there is not one, and I have wanted one since the middle of Rainmonth and I am not going to pretend that being easy to find is the same as being wanted.**”

Nobody thanked her and the four people at the boards said nothing and about four of them had heard a woman of thirty-one say that a thing she cannot be found on is not a thing she wants, and about five heard nothing at all because there were only four people in that street.

Nobody thanked him and the man of about thirty-three who sells cloth three doors up asked the last question of that morning, and he asked it out loud to about four people, and nobody stopped him.

“**Then is the first of the month the day this county finds out a thing, or the day it finds out that it has not got a day to find things out on?**”

Nobody said anything and nobody answered him and about four people in that empty market street heard the question and about one of them thought of an answer and did not say it, and about three had not thought of one, and he said out loud that the correct arrangement was that nobody answered it, and he went inside his shop.

Nobody thanked him and about four people in nine counties have said since that a first of a month in Hearthmonth produced the only question of that day and that the only person in nine counties who could have answered it is a woman of about fifty-one who keeps a lodging house four streets up, and that she was not there, and about five have said since that it is right that she was not there.

Nobody thanked anybody and the market street emptied at about the eleventh hour, and the boards stood in it, and the weigh-house went on weighing underneath the room, and about nine people in nine counties have said that the first of a month in Hearthmonth is a day nothing on it was decided and that the boards were still standing in the street with nothing on them but a list of counties and a cloth on nine hundred words four streets away.

What happened at about the sixth hour was at the top of the made road and nobody in that market street knew about it.

Then the man of about sixty-two did the second half of the thing, and the second half is the whole of it, and he said it out loud to about four people at the gate of that field and he said it once.

“**A man in a livery came up this made road on the Friday before last and asked me for a horse,**” he said. “**And I have said yes to that man four times in about a month, because I am paid by the day and there are nine men in a yard that I cannot say no to from a field. I have said yes four times. I am not going to say yes a fifth time, and I am not going to say no either, and I am not going to be the man who says no, because I have watched four people say no this year and every one of them has had to explain it to somebody afterwards.**”
