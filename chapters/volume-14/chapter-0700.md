# Chapter 0700

**Goatmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 321, a Monday and not a market morning, with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, a man four days off on this made road with a stick and on his own.**

Twenty-four days after the first of a month on which nothing happened, and a Monday, and no market, and the boards in that market street stood in an empty street with nothing on them, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit until the fourth hour, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the twenty-fifth of Goatmonth was the first day since the first of Hearthmonth on which nobody in nine counties said anything anybody wrote down.

Nobody thanked anybody and this is where the man of thirty-two was, and he was four days off that market town on foot, on his own account, with his stick in his right hand, and he had been going that road every morning since the first of Longlight and had about nine days left in him.

Nobody thanked him and the four days of that made road are hard at the two ends and soft in the middle, and the hard part at this end is the causeway and the soft part is the two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of the hill, and he has walked both of them about nine times since the middle of Harvestmonth and has never once gone down an orchard lane, and about four people in nine counties have said that a man who goes up a road every day of his life for four and a half years will walk nine different roads out of it and that about nine of them are orchard lanes with gates on them.

Nobody thanked him and the made road at four days comes off the shoulder of the hill onto a level stretch with the water on the low side, and the water at four days is not a river with banks. It is a broad channel about nine hundred yards wide at the top of a tide and about four hundred at the bottom, and the made road crosses it on a stone causeway about nine hundred paces long and about nine feet wide at the crown, and the causeway has a low parapet on the seaward side that a cart cannot get over and a man can lean on.

Nobody thanked him and about four people in nine counties know that the man who comes that road has been to the top of that causeway about nine times since the middle of Harvestmonth and has stopped there every time, and about five know that he stops there because there is nowhere else to stop, and that both of those things are true and that nobody has ever asked him which one he stops for.

Nobody thanked anybody and the day had been raining since the fourth hour and had stopped at about the second hour and started again at the fourth, and the wind was off the water and coming up the causeway at him, and his coat was wet through at the front and dry at the back, and he was not cold in the way that stops a man and had been walking since the sixth hour.

Then there was the ninth mile stone, which was behind him, and which he had walked past on the way out at about the eighth hour that morning, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who walks a road every day for four and a half years goes past nine mile stones the way a hand goes past a rail on a stair, and about five have said that since the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth there are people who stop at that stone and look at a wall on the high side of that road, and that he is one of them and has been every day since, and that he has not gone through the gate and is not going to, and that a gate which can be looked at can be looked at every day for about four years and that this is what four hundred people in a market street did to this county in one morning.

Nobody thanked him and he did not think about that any more than he thought about the weather, and the reason he did not is that he has thought about it every day for about eleven days and there is nothing left in it.

Then there was the lane with the weigh-house in it, four hundred paces from a door on that lane, which was behind him and had been behind him since the first of Longlight, and about four people in nine counties have said that a man has been going past four hundred paces of his own life for four and a half years and about five have said that four hundred paces is about as far as anything in this county is from anything else and that somebody worked that out on purpose a long time ago.

Nobody thanked anybody and he did not go up the lane on the twenty-fifth of Goatmonth and nobody has ever asked him to and nobody in nine counties has said to him that he was not going to.

Then there were two leaves of paper in the inside of his coat, and they had been in the inside of his coat since the twenty-second of Hearthmonth, and one of them had been read out in a market street to about nine people on a Wednesday and the other one had not been read out anywhere at all, and nobody in nine counties has ever asked anybody where the second one came from, and about four people know and about five do not.

Nobody thanked him and the one that was read out in that market street has been out of his coat twice since, both times to be shown to a man who asked about it on his own account, and both times about nine people have said that a man who carries a paper and shows it twice is not giving it away and is not hiding it and is doing a third thing that has no word in nine counties, and about five have said that the third thing is keeping it.

Nobody thanked him and he did not take them out, and about four people in nine counties have said that a man carrying two written refusals from two path communities does not take them out of his coat because taking them out of his coat is offering them to somebody, and about five have said that he does not take them out because he has not got anybody to offer them to, and that in about four years one of those two will be the reason and the other will be the reason it was believed.

Then there was the drove, and the byre, and a man shutting a gate at the head of the road, and this is the whole of that part.

Nobody thanked anybody and the gate at the head of that lane is a five-bar gate with a bar in it and the bar goes in on the lane side and not on the field side, and a man who shuts a gate on the lane side is shutting it against the road and not against himself, and about four people in nine counties have said that there are about nine gates on that made road that go that way and about five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked any of the nine why.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man was about forty-one and he keeps a drove and a byre and about nine hundred yards of wall at the head of this made road and nobody in nine counties buys anything off him except about four households that buy a beast in the autumn, and he was shutting a gate across a lane that comes off the made road and goes up over the shoulder of the hill, and there is no made road up there and has never been and about nine people in nine counties know where that lane goes and about five have been up it.

Nobody thanked him and the man had seen him coming every morning for about eleven days and had not said anything to him and had not been asked for anything, and on this afternoon the man shut his gate and put the bar in and turned round and saw that a man of thirty-two had stopped at the mouth of the lane instead of going on.

Nobody thanked either of them and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the only time in about four and a half years that the man who goes has been stopped by anybody at all, and that it was stopped by a gate, and about five have said that he stopped himself because a gate was coming down and a man with a stick is not going to be behind a gate when it lands.

Then the man of thirty-two asked the question, out loud, in the open, on a wet Monday afternoon at the head of a lane, to about one person, and nobody answered it.

“**There is a lane that goes up over that shoulder and comes down the other side somewhere I have not been. How old is it?**”

Nobody said anything and the man of about forty-one who keeps a drove put his hand on the bar of his gate and looked at the lane and looked at the man and did not answer for about as long as it takes to turn a cart.

Nobody thanked anybody and then he said ten words, and they are the only thing anybody has ever said to a person on that road about where a road goes.

“**It was a road before your county had a name.**”

Nobody said anything and nobody thanked him and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man of forty-one at a gate has said out loud that he knows the age of a lane he does not keep, and that he has known it for about eleven years, and that about nine people in nine counties knew that he knew it before he said it, and about five had never once thought of asking him.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of thirty-two did not ask a second question, and nobody in nine counties has ever been able to establish whether that was because he had the answer already or because he had worked out in about four seconds that a man who has said ten words at a gate has said all ten of them on purpose, and four people in nine counties have said that he has the answer already and five have said that nobody has ever asked him, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked him and the man of about forty-one shut his gate, and the bar went in, and he went up the lane with the drove behind him, and the man of thirty-two stood at the mouth of that lane for about as long as it takes to turn a cart and then went on up the made road on his own.

Nobody thanked anybody and he walked the rest of that day and got to the top of the causeway at about the fourth hour and put his stick down against the parapet because a man with a bad leg does not stand for nine minutes without something to hold, and he stood on the seaward side of that causeway looking at the water for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and the wind dropped at about the fourth hour and the water went flat on the far side and there was nothing moving on it at all.

Nobody thanked him and about four people in nine counties have said that a man went up that road on the twenty-fifth of Goatmonth and stopped at a wall on the high side of it and stopped at a gate in a lane that has been a lane since before his county had a name and then went on up a made road to a stone causeway and stood at the top of it, and that about nine steps of that is four and a half years and one step is about eleven days and that nobody in nine counties has ever been able to work out which of the two was the one that happened.

Nobody thanked anybody and nothing about any of it is in a document, and there is no page in any room in nine counties with any of it on, and nothing between two of the people in that county is in any document, and that has been the shape of it for four and a half years and is the shape of it on the twenty-fifth of Goatmonth.

Nobody thanked anybody and a lamp went on at the gate at the head of the causeway at about the fourth hour, and a man who keeps that gate puts a hand lamp on the coping at the low side of it every Monday afternoon in the winter, and it is a hand lamp with about four inches of candle in it and the candle had about nine minutes left in it, and it is the only lamp on this made road and it is lit whether anybody is there or not.

Nobody thanked anybody and on the gate post above that lamp there is a bell, and it has been on that gate since before the middle of Longlight, and it is a hand bell about the size of a fist, and a man who keeps that gate rings it when the water is over the causeway so that anybody on the far bank knows to stop, and about nine people in nine counties know that a bell is on that gate and about five know when it is rung and that it has not been rung in this weather at all.

**And the cost of that Monday is a lane that has been a lane since before this county had a name, two written refusals in a coat that have not been read out, and a gate at four days that is lit on a Monday afternoon whether anybody is there or not, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a mile of cord at 27, a hand lantern at 29, a shear at 30, a hurdle at 31, a gate shoe at 32, a wicket at 34, a barrow wheel at 35, a hod at 36, a barrow at 37, and a hand bell at 40.** Twenty-seven, twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six, thirty-seven and forty is three hundred and thirty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and three hundred and thirty-one pence is six marks and forty-three over.** The lamp was on the coping at the low side of that causeway with four inches of candle in it and about nine minutes to go on it, and the water was flat on the far side with nothing moving on it, and a man of thirty-two was standing at the top of a stone causeway four days off a market town with his stick against a parapet and his hands in a wet coat, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the lamp at that gate is the only thing on this made road that is kept for somebody who has not arrived, and then a bell answered across the water, and he had not rung it.
