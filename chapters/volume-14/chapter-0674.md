# Chapter 0674

**Embermonth the first, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning and the first of a month, the second hour in the market street at the boards with the hearing in the street and about four hundred people in the street and about two hundred of them stopped, nine clauses being read out loud from a man of thirty-two, and one clause objected to by a person who agrees with the other eight.**

The first of a month came whether or not it was a market day, and this one was a Thursday and it was a market morning, and about four hundred people were in that street by the second hour and about two hundred of them stopped what they were doing when a man of thirty-two with a ferrier's stick put nine hundred words on the low end of the boards and read them out in the street at a market where nobody could get at the inside of them.

About eleven people were at the boards. Four of those eleven could see the shape of it and the other five could only hear the words, and the five were not stupid and were not idle, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that they were the reason the whole of it was read out twice.

He read all nine clauses out loud. He did not stop at any of them and he did not hurry at any of them and he did not look up more than four times, and two hundred people in that market street heard nine hundred words on a Saturday morning and about four hundred heard about nine hundred words because they were too far off.

Then he folded them and put them inside his coat and nine people at the boards waited for something to be said about them.

The copyist of about twenty-six was at the boards with a leaf in her hand and everybody at the boards had been waiting about an hour for her to do the thing she had said in a market on the twenty-third of Rainmonth she would do.

She did not do it.

“**I am not going to read the letter this month,**” she said, out loud, in the street, in the ordinary flat way. “**I have read it twice and nine people have heard it twice and there is a horse in it and the horse has not changed. I said I would read it on the first of every month for as long as there was a horse in it, and I am not breaking that, and I am going to say the other half out loud so that nobody has to work out whether I have. A letter gets read out on a first of a month because it is the first of a month, and about nine days after the horse was taken that was a reason. It is not a reason now.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards understood and about five heard a woman decline to do a thing she had promised to do, and the badge-man said that it was her letter and her first of a month and that anybody who wanted to argue with it could come and stand where he was standing, which is a thing he says about four times a year.

Then the woman of about thirty-six stood up at the high end of the boards, and she had been up since about the second hour and she had not come to the reading and had listened to it with her head on one side, and nine people at the boards had noticed that and about five had not.

She said that she agreed with eight of the nine, and she gave the number of the one she did not agree with.

“**Sixth,**” she said. “**For the length of the emergency, each of the seven roads accepts the authority of the Crown in the keeping of its own crossing, in writing.**”

“**The rest of it is that the writing is held by the person named on the road's side, and by nobody in this county,**” she said, “**and I have read it twice on the road and I am not going to read it again.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard the whole of the clause and about five had heard the number.

“**Say what is wrong with it,**” the badge-man said.

“**It cannot be complied with,**” the woman of about thirty-six said. “**I am not objecting to it because I want a different plan. I want to be able to point at eight clauses and say those are right and there is the one that is not, and I cannot point at it as a thing that is wrong, because it is not wrong. It is a thing that cannot be done. A road that does not take orders cannot accept authority, because to accept an order a road has to have somebody in it who gives orders, and the whole of what seven roads have in common is that there is nobody at the end of any of them who gives orders. That is not a fault. That is what a road is. And you have written a clause that wants it the other way and you have numbered it.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had understood all of that and about five had heard a woman say that a clause could not be done.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat asked the question that the whole of that morning turned on, and he asked it in nine words and about four people at the boards had been waiting about nine days for somebody to ask it.

“**Then who signs it.**”

Nobody thanked him and nine people at the boards looked at the low end of the boards where the nine hundred words were not any more, and about five looked at the boards themselves.

Aren Kest said it, and he said it in about nine seconds, and he said it as a man reading out a clause he wrote himself four days ago.

“**The person named on the road's side. That is what the clause says and that is what it means. The road is not asked to give an order and the road is not asked to obey an order. A person is asked for a name, and the name is not an order, and the paper goes to the person and not to any house, and there is nobody in the chain between the person and anybody else. That is why it is worded that way. It was worded that way on purpose.**”

Four people at the boards heard the whole of that and about five heard the words.

The woman of about thirty-six said the next thing, and she said it flatly, and it was the thing that made nine people in that market street understand that the morning had turned.

“**So it is not an order and it is not a request. It is a name with a piece of paper attached to it.**”

Nobody said anything to that.

“**And I have been in a county where there was a list for about a year before the burning, and the list had nine men going round with it and about four pence a day and a basket, and every one of those nine men had a name at the top of a piece of paper, and none of the nine of them ever gave an order to anybody, and every one of them went into four houses a day and wrote down what was in them.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards looked at the ground and about five looked at her.

Then the badge-man said the true thing about it, and he said it in nine words, and he did not add to it, and that is the whole of what he did that morning.

“**A person who can be found afterwards is what the burden has been waiting four and a half years for. This county has written nine hundred words handing it over.**”

Four people in that market street understood all of it and about five heard a man with a badge say nine words and put his hand inside his coat.

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes a carter to get a load round, and nine people at the boards said nothing, and two hundred people in that market street went back to what they were doing and went home, and nobody thanked anybody for any of it.

Then a man of about twenty-nine who keeps the inn two doors up said the thing that about nine people had been working out since the sixth hour and about five had not.

“**Then the plan is eight clauses and a sixth one, and it is in his coat, and nobody has been asked to sign it and about two hundred people in this street have heard it. What happens to it now?**”

Nobody answered him and four people at the boards looked at the man of about thirty-four with the sheet and about five looked at the street.

“**Nothing,**” the badge-man said. “**That is the correct answer and I am going to say why in nine words and then I am going to stand about nine paces off and let two hundred people go home. A man who takes a plan back in public in a month like this one teaches nine counties that a plan can be taken back, and after that every person who writes anything down in this county will do it in a barn.**”

Nobody thanked him and the innkeeper of about twenty-nine said that he had not asked twice and that he had asked once on purpose, and about four people at the boards noticed that and about five did not, and it was the second time in four days that a man in this market town had said it.

Then the copyist of about twenty-six said one thing into a market street that about two hundred people were leaving, and she said it in the flat way and she said it to nobody.

“**For about four and a half years I have been the person a thing in this county goes to and be refused by,**” she said. “**A letter on the twenty-third of Rainmonth. A question from a woman on the first of Longlight. Nine hundred words that nobody has asked me to hold. That is three and I have said out loud in a street about nine times that a thing roads write to is a head at the top of a thing, and I said it about a drawer and about nine days ago I said it to a man with a stick about a series, and neither of those was the thing.**”

She put the leaf in her coat.

“**The thing is that nobody in nine counties has a place to put nine hundred words that are not about anybody, and there is no head for that either, and I am not going to rule a fifth line for it, and I am not going to pretend that is a defence of anybody including the man with the stick.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street who had been on their way out stopped and five had not, and two hundred people went home out of a market street with eight clauses and a sixth one in it.

It was read twice more that day and neither reading was in that street.

The hired room over a saddler's is where the hearing sits on the four or five days in a year that it sits, and it has a bench nobody uses and a table with a candle on it and a smell of saddle soap, and about nine people went into it at about the fourth hour because the badge-man had said the thing out loud in the market and about four of the nine heard him and about five came anyway.

The copyist of about twenty-six read it there, all nine clauses, at a table, with a candle, to nine people who had all read it in the street that morning or had not.

Nobody thanked her for reading it and she did not say anything about the letter and nobody asked her about it.

Five of those nine could see the shape of the thing by the sixth clause. The other four could only hear the words, and one of those four was a man of about forty-seven who sells wool two streets off and who said so out loud at the seventh clause and was not argued with.

“**I have understood every word of that and I have not got any of it,**” he said, “**and I have been in nine counties for about nine years and I have never once been able to get a thing I understood and a thing I had into the same place, and I am not complaining, I am putting it in the room so that about nine people know it is happening to me.**”

Nobody said anything to that and five people in that room gave him a cup of something and four did not.

A man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat said one thing in that room and it was the thing about the room that the copyist had said in the street, and he said it to the candle.

“**There are nine questions in a book two streets off and no finding under any of them and a fourth line ruled and empty, and a person has written nine hundred words in about a room like this one, and there is nowhere in nine counties to put nine hundred words that are not about anybody. I have got a satchel and I could carry it and I am not going to, and if I carried it then in about four years it would be in a drawer and I would be a man who carried a plan and a man who carries a plan is a head. That is the whole of it.**”

Four people in that room had understood that and five heard a man say that he was not going to.

Then it was read the third time at about the sixth hour in a lane, because that is what lanes are for and because nine people in nine counties have said that a thing said in a lane in the rain is the only version of it that anybody at the bottom of that lane ever hears.

The man of about fifty-one who owns the field on the low side of the cut stood in his own gateway and heard about four clauses of it and stopped the man at the third.

“**There is no such thing as a gateway lane in that,**” he said. “**You have got the ferry city and the county where the list was and the waterworks country and the coast and the fruit and the glass and the small one, and where in that is me. I am not a road anybody has ever heard of and I keep four miles of it because there is nobody else, and I have not got anybody to give your authority to, and I would not know what to do with a person in charge of me.**”

“**You are not one of the seven,**” the woman of about thirty-six said. She had walked down with him and not to argue. “**I know you are not one of the seven and I have said in four markets that there are nine people in nine counties and about four of them are not in any list anybody has written. I walked you down here so that you could hear a thing read out in a lane instead of in a street, and that is the whole of why I did it and I am not going to explain it again.**”

Nobody thanked her and the man of about fifty-one said that the sixth clause was the only one he had understood and that he did not want it.

“**I have understood one clause out of nine in a lane in the wet at about the sixth hour,**” he said, “**and the one I understood is the one that wants a person in charge of me, and I have known since about the fourth hour of the afternoon that that is what the whole thing was, and I would like whoever wrote it to know that I knew before I read it and that I have said nothing about it, and that is all I am going to say about it.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that lane understood what a man of about fifty-one had said and about five heard a man turn a thing down, and the man of thirty-two stood at the low side of that gateway for about nine seconds and said nothing at all, and four people in that lane had been waiting about a year for him to say something and five had not been waiting for anything.

**And the cost of that Thursday is one clause, numbered, and nine words said by a man with a badge about a person who can be found afterwards, and about two hundred people who heard nine hundred words on a market morning and nine of them understood the shape of it, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a dozen of tacks at 13, a cake of soap at 15, a purse string at 21, a rushlight holder at 25, a rule of boxwood at 27, a side of leather at 30, a sack of sand at 32, a lamp of tallow at 36, a water butt at 37, and a lock for a gate at 39.** Thirteen, fifteen, twenty-one, twenty-five, twenty-seven, thirty, thirty-two, thirty-six, thirty-seven and thirty-nine is two hundred and seventy-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and seventy-five pence is five marks and thirty-five over.** At about the fifth hour the copyist of about twenty-six carried the register up the thirteen stairs and put it on the table with the fourth ruled line of the outgoing sheet empty and not filled, and the woman of about thirty-six went down the lane at about the fourth hour on her own account to a gate that is not hers and put her hand on the post of it for about as long as it takes to say a thing out loud, and did not lift anything and did not shut anything and said nothing, and four people in four houses on that lane saw her do it and about five did not, and nobody in nine counties has been able to establish since whether she was thinking about clause six or about the horse.
