# Chapter 0675

**Embermonth the eighth, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the third hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, a captain of about forty-four reading out loud a printed list of roads with a name at the top of it, and about nine people hearing a question asked that nobody in this county has ever been asked.**

The party had been doing it for nine days and nobody in this county had heard about it until the Thursday.

What they were doing was this. In every county the captain comes to, they take one road. One at a time, in order, off a list, with a chain on two posts and about four men and no written order to hold anything, and they go on to the next county and take one road there. It is not a closure. Nothing is closed, nobody proposes anything, and there is no moment at which it begins.

It began in a county two days off on the Sunday, on a road that had a name on it, and the four men put the chain on before lunch and were gone by the fourth hour.

Nobody in that county had standing to say the road was theirs. Not the road-keeper, who had kept it about nineteen years. Not the households at the two ends of it. Not the county.

What the four men actually did on the Sunday was this, and nine people in nine counties have described it since in the same words without ever having spoken to each other. They came up the road at the ninth hour with a chain and two posts and a maul. They looked for about nine minutes. They drove the posts about four feet apart in the two places where the road was narrowest, which was not at a gate and was not at a bridge and was about two hundred yards below the high end where the ground came up. They put the chain on. They wrote the day and the county and their own serjeant's name on a board and nailed it to the post on the low side, on the outside, where a person coming up the road reads it before they are in the lane.

Nobody in nine counties has worked out why they chose the narrow place. Four people have said it was because it was narrow and five have said it was because it was where a cart would have to stop and put a wheel round, and one of those nine has said out loud that it was neither and that it was chosen by somebody who has looked at a map with two marks on it, and nobody has been able to establish since which of the nine is right.

Then they went on to the next county and did it again, and again on the Tuesday, and again on the Friday, and each time it was one road and each time it was off the same list, and each time there was no moment and each time nobody in nine counties could say that the road had been shut.

That is the part that has made nine people in this county say hard true things in market streets in a fortnight, and the hard true thing is always the same shape and one of them is a woman of about thirty-six who does not come to this county.

Then the captain came up to the boards at about the third hour on the Thursday with the serjeant and four men behind him and a folded sheet in his coat, and he did not send anybody, and he put the sheet on the boards himself, which about nine people at the boards noticed and about five did not.

“**I am going to read this out and then I am going to stop talking,**” he said, “**and I want it read out in this street and not in a room, because if it is read in a room then nine people in this county will hear about it in a fortnight and about four of them will have heard a wrong version first.**”

He unfolded it. It was a sheet about two foot by one and a half, printed on both sides, folded in four, with three breaks in the same three places, and it was a list.

It was a list of counties in order, going down the sheet, and against each county there was one road written out in full with its ends and its length and what it crossed, and against the fourth county down there was a road four miles long with a stone in the mud at the low end and a milestone at the top.

At the top of it, in a hand that is not the printed hand of the body, there was a name.

“**Read it all,**” the badge-man said. “**And then read the top again.**”

The captain read the whole of it out loud in that market street, thirty counties and thirty roads, and it took him four minutes, and four hundred people in that street heard it and two hundred had stopped what they were doing, and nine people at the boards heard the fourth county.

Then he read the top again.

“**Edric Hunn.**”

Nobody said anything and four hundred people in that market street heard a name read out twice at a set of boards and two hundred of them had no idea what to do with it and nine at the boards had.

Then the badge-man said one thing about it, and it was nine words, and then he stopped.

“**That name at the top. I have not said it in this street in about eleven years.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at the boards had understood the whole of that and about five had heard a man say that he had not said a name in a street in about eleven years.

Then the captain said the thing he had come to say, and he said it the way he said everything, which is that he gave the reason before he gave the answer and did not soften the answer and did not read it faster than he had to.

“**Three things. One. My warrant names a place. A warrant that names a place can be refused at a gate and I have been refused at a gate and I did not take anybody's neck off for it, because that is what a gate is for. Two. This list does not name a place. It names one road in one county at a time, taken out of a county by a list, and there is no road on this sheet that anybody in nine counties can say is theirs, because there is no clause in about nine hands that lets a man say a road is his, and there is not one at any gate in this county.**”

“**Say the third,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat said.

“**Three, and it is the only one that matters to anybody standing here.**” He turned his hand over and did not open the small book. “**I do not want to take thirty roads. I want to be able to argue about one. A thing I have to argue about every day of my life is a thing I have to be wrong about, and I have been wrong about a ford already this month and a man of about fifty told me so in front of about nine people, and he was right. A list does not let me be wrong about anything. It lets me be right thirty times without being asked once.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a captain of about forty-four explain why a piece of paper was worse for him and about five heard a man talk about a ford.

Then the woman of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat asked the one question that the sheet had not answered and that nine people at the boards had not thought of until the fourth county was read out.

“**What happens to the thirty-first road on that list,**” she said. “**Is there a county for it?**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards looked at the sheet on the boards and five looked at the man of about thirty-four.

“**I do not know,**” the captain said. “**I have got four counties on that sheet after mine and I have been in about nine of them. There are about thirty on it. It came out of a room about nine days ago with the day on the top of it and a name, and it was given to me at a table by a man who read out two counties and the day and then handed it across, and he did not read the rest of it and neither did I.**”

“**So you have taken nine roads off a list you have not read,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**I have taken about nine roads off a list of thirty and I have not read the last twenty-one and I am going to tell you in this street what I think that means, because I have been thinking about it for nine days and I have nobody to ask.**” He did not put the sheet away and he did not fold it. “**It means that in about three weeks a serjeant of mine is going to arrive somewhere with about four men and a sheet, and read a road off the page, and there will be nothing about that road I can say out loud. Not the road. Not the county. Not the day. I will have four men and a chain and about thirty names on a piece of paper and not one of them will be mine.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that market street had understood every word of that and about five heard a captain explain that he could not stop what he was carrying.

“**That is the third thing I have said and it is the third thing that is worse for me than it is for you,**” he said, “**and I am not going to say the first two again and about four of you heard them and about five did not.**”

Then the woman of about thirty-six said the thing that nine people at the boards had been carrying since the thirteenth of Fallowmonth and had not said out loud.

“**It is the same shape as the ford,**” she said. “**It is the same shape and everybody in this street worked it out on the Sunday and four of us have said it in four places since and five have not. On the Sunday a man came down a bank with four men and a chain and nobody had proposed anything and there was no moment, and this morning a man read a list off a board and there is still no moment, and in both cases the three clauses did their job exactly as nine people made them because they are about a man who proposes and there has not been one.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards had heard the whole of that and about five heard a woman say it twice.

Then the badge-man said the other thing, and he said it out loud in that market street in front of about four hundred people, and about two hundred of them heard all of it.

“**I said something out loud in this street on the twenty-fifth of Greenmonth and I counted out loud in this street on the sixteenth of Longlight that the interval between the two was twenty-one days and that I had said a longer figure and been wrong by about three months and about nine days. I am not moving the figure. I have not said it in this street since and I am not going to say it now.**”

Nobody said anything. Nine people at the boards had heard a man not say a figure he had been asked about four times.

“**And I said in this street, in Longlight, that being right early about a thing you were right about is the same as being wrong about how long you had,**” he said. “**And I have had two months to sit with what I said and I have arrived at the true thing about it and I am going to say it now, once, and then about four of you are going to say that I have been talking for thirty-one years and about five are going to have heard a man of fifty-eight say that being early was a kindness.**”

Nobody said anything.

“**It was a kindness,**” the badge-man said.

Then a man of about thirty-three who sells cloth three doors up asked the question out loud, in that market street, in the ordinary way, and nine people at the boards heard it and two hundred heard a man ask a question that nobody had known was a question.

“**Then was being right early a kindness.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody stopped him and the badge-man did not answer it, and four people at the boards noticed that the badge-man had not answered it and five noticed that a man had asked it.

The woman of about fifty answered it.

She was at the low end of the boards and she had come down the lane at about the fourth hour with a slate in her apron and the last column of it begun, which is what she does in the first week of a month, and she had been at that end of the boards for the whole of the reading and had not said anything and about four people at the boards had noticed that and about five had not.

“**No,**” she said. “**It was a kindness and it was to you.**”

Nobody said anything at that and two hundred people in that market street stopped what they were doing.

“**You were right on the day and you were wrong about how long, and being wrong about how long is the only part of that has cost anybody, and here is what nine counties did with the twenty-one days you gave us. We built a rule about a road being shut. It is a good rule. It is the best thing nine of us have ever made. And it has taken a horse off a mile and a half and a barrow off a lane and not one man with a paper, and I have stood at the boards on the first of every month for about a year and read things out in front of about nine people and I am the one who has read that out and I know it to be true and so do you.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard all of it and about five heard a woman say a man had given away time.

“**And I am not saying you were wrong,**” she said. “**I am saying that a kindness is a thing you do to a person, and this was a thing you did to yourself, and nine counties spent twenty-one days on a rule because of it, and if you had given us the longer figure we should have spent the longer figure on the same rule, and you will never know that, and I am saying it in a market street on a Thursday so that about nine people have it in a month instead of about four years.**”

Then she said the last of it and she said it to nine people and not to two hundred.

“**Nobody in this street is going to thank you for the twenty-one days and I am the one saying that out loud, and it is not a complaint about you, it is about a ford with a chain on it and four miles of road with a name in a coat and about nine people in this county who have been asked nothing at all for about two months.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at the boards looked at the ground and about five looked at the boards, and the captain read the fourth county down again on the sheet and said that it stood for the fourteenth of Fallowmonth and that the road on it was the road she had named in a lane in the rain on the Sunday, and he said it without any weight on it and he did not apologise for it and about four people at the boards heard the whole of that and about five did not.

Then the man of about fifty-one who owns the field on the low side of the cut came up the lane in the rain at about the fifth hour and stood at the low end of the boards, and he had walked four miles to do it, and four people at the boards understood why he had come and five did not.

He had nothing to say about the list. He said so in nine words, and then he said the other thing, and nine people heard it.

“**I keep four miles of that road and I have kept it about nineteen years and it had no name on it anywhere until the thirteenth of Fallowmonth, and it has now got one in a book in a coat, and this morning that book has a list in front of it with my four miles written out on it as though somebody had gone and got it from a map.**”

“**Nobody got it from a map,**” the captain said. “**It is in the small book and it is in the entry and the entry is dated the twelfth and read back to four hundred people in your market street. It came out of a lane in the rain on the Sunday and not out of a map, and if you would rather it had come out of a map I would understand the preferring of it.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard that and about five had heard a man apologise to a road.

The man of about fifty-one said that he would rather it had been the lane, and then he said the thing that four people in that market street understood and five did not.

“**I gave the name in a lane because a road with a name can be argued with and a road without one can only be taken, and I said that out loud in that lane and nine people heard it, and I have had two weeks to sit with what I said and I am telling you in a market street that I have got it wrong.**”

Nobody said anything at that and about two hundred people in that market street had stopped what they were doing again.

“**A road with a name can be argued with and a road with a name on a list can only be read out,**” she said. “**And I did that to my own four miles in the rain and I would do it again and I am not going to stand in this street and pretend to a person that I did not know.**”

Nobody thanked her and the market went on at about the sixth hour, and two hundred people went back to what they had been doing, and nine people at the boards stayed, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs with nothing in her hands at all and went back up again without opening the door, which is a thing she has done about nine times in about four years.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a list with counties on it in order and a chain on one of them, and a woman of about fifty saying out loud in a market street that a kindness is a thing you do to a person, and twenty-one days that nine counties spent on the only rule they had, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sheet of foolscap at 16, a billhook at 23, a horse collar at 24, a bundle of pegs at 27, a wicket latch at 29, a mattock at 30, a hand lantern at 31, a coil of rope at 32, a mail bag at 35, and a tally board at 37.** Sixteen, twenty-three, twenty-four, twenty-seven, twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-five and thirty-seven is two hundred and eighty-four pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and eighty-four pence is five marks and forty-four over.** The captain put the sheet back into the fold it came in and went down the lane at about the fourth hour with the serjeant and about four men and the chain was not with them because the chain was on a ford four miles down the lane and had been since the Sunday, and about nine hundred paces up that made road a yard was loading timber off a waggon and none of the nine men in it had heard a name read out twice at a set of boards.
