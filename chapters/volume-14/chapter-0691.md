# Chapter 0691

**Hearthmonth the second, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, the first of nine days of held crossings, and four gates in nine counties that nobody has agreed anything about.**

A Thursday is a market morning and the boards were standing in that market street by the seventh hour, and about four hundred people were in it by the second hour and about two hundred of them stopped what they were doing, which is the proportion on every market morning since the first of Embermonth and has not moved since the first of Embermonth.

The list of thirty counties in order was not on the boards. The man of about thirty-one in a livery had taken it down at about the fourth hour on the Saturday and had put it into a handbasket and the handbasket had gone up to the hired room over a saddler's, and nobody had read it going up.

The cold that morning came off the land and not off any water, and it had been in the ground since the middle of the night, and the ruts at the low end of that market street had a skin of white in them that broke under a heel and went to powder. Two men were unloading boards off a cart and breathing out of their mouths and not talking, because talking at that hour on a Thursday costs a man about nine seconds of work he does not get back. A woman of about thirty-three had put a bench out of a shop and had not put a cloth over it. Light came up the side of the weigh-house and found the upper edge of the boards, and the boards had been repainted twice since the middle of Longlight and were the colour of a wet slate, and about nine people in that street could have told you which of the two coats was on them last.

The room over the weigh-house had been shut behind its own door since noon on the Saturday, and a first of a month is a Wednesday and this was a Thursday, so on this morning it was open, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs at the seventh hour and stood in the lane while the room let the cold off it, and she went back up and shut the door again and came down a second time with a leaf in her hand and nothing on it, and about four people in that lane saw her come down twice and about five saw her come down once.

What came up the made road at about the seventh hour and stood at the low end of the boards was a man of about thirty-eight who puts boards on the inside of gates, and he had walked in and had not brought anything with him.

Nobody thanked him and he was not thanked for the four miles and he did not ask to be, and about four people in that market street had seen him come in and about five had not.

Then he said the thing that had happened, and he said it once, and he gave the hour and he gave the place and he gave it in the order it had happened in, and nobody stopped him.

“**Yesterday, at about the fourth hour, at the crossing four miles down the lane, a man in a livery came up to my gate with a small book and asked me the ordinary question, which is whether there was anybody at my gate who wanted to be spoken to. And I said there was not, because there was not, and he wrote it down and read it back to me and went away down the lane. That is the whole of what happened at my gate in about nine minutes.**”

Nobody thanked him and about two hundred people in that market street had heard a man account for a gate, and about four of them had understood that the whole of what happened was nothing happening, and about five had understood that a man had walked four miles to stand in a market street and say so.

Then he was asked what he had done with the rest of the morning, and he answered it without being asked twice, and about nine people in that street noticed that he answered the second question faster than the first.

“**I shut the gate and I put the board on the inside and I went back to the rails and I cut a rail that wanted cutting. That is the whole of my day and there is no order in it. If you want to know whether there is an order, then there is no order, and if you want one then you have to ask somebody for it, and the moment you ask somebody for it that somebody is the man in the coat and not the man at the gate.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in that market street had heard a man at a gate put a boundary round himself on his own account, and about five had heard a man say that he had no order.

Then the woman of about thirty-nine who keeps the second gate from the top of this made road said her piece, and she had come in on foot and had stood at the high end of the boards the whole time and had said nothing until then, and she said it in fifteen words.

“**Mine has been shut since the third hour yesterday and nobody has been near it.**”

Nobody thanked her and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a woman of about thirty-nine at a gate two days off said fifteen words in a market street and that the count of them has never been agreed, and about four people in that market street had heard one of the four gates named by the person who keeps it, and about five had not understood that that was what had happened.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned said the thing she has said before in a different street, and she said it standing up again, which she has now done four times since Fallowmonth.

“**I have a road and I have not sent anything, and I have not been asked for anything, and I want it written down in one place that a road which has sent nothing this month is not a road that has been beaten. It is a road that has been stood on.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people in that market street had heard a woman of thirty-six correct a word, and about five had heard a woman talk about a road she had not sent anything on.

“**And after he had gone I took a board off the inside of that gate and I shut it behind me and I put the board back on the inside,**” he said. “**And nobody asked me to. And there is nobody in nine counties who has asked me to, and I would like about nine people in this street to notice that I was not asked and that I did it anyway, and that a man who waits to be asked is a line on a page.**”

Nobody said anything and two hundred people in that market street had stopped what they were doing for the first time that morning, and about nine of them had understood the whole of what the man of about thirty-eight had said and about five had heard a gate.

Then the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head said the whole of the procedure out loud, and he said it in the middle of that market street in the open, to about four hundred people and to about nine of them, and it was nine words, and he did not say it again that day.

“**Nobody says anything to anybody, and nobody is asked.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that market street had heard the whole of what nine counties has said nine words, and about five had heard nine words.

Then he said what it was not, because about nine people had been going to ask him and he had thought about it on the way down the lane, and he said it in the flat way and he did not soften it.

“**It is not a muster. It is not a list. It is not a watch. It is not a rota. And there is not a name at the top of it and there is not going to be one.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at those boards had heard a man of fifty-eight refuse five separate things in one street in one morning, and five had heard a man being careful.

“**And the reason is the reason it has always been and I am not going to give it a new one because it is about four months old and it works. A man who says come to the gate on Tuesday is a head. A room that says it is a head. A book that says it is a head. If I say nine days, then in about four years a man in a room opens a book and reads nine days at the top of a page, and about nine people in nine counties have given themselves to a line of ink, and that is the end of a thing and not the beginning of one.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a man explain in one breath why he was not going to count, and five had heard a man not counting.

Then he gave the count out loud, and nobody had asked him for it, and he gave it to about nine people and not to the four hundred, and he gave two figures and gave them separately.

“**Nine days, from this morning to the tenth of Hearthmonth, inclusive. And four gates are standing this morning, and I did not ask them to, and none of the four has been told that there are three others, and if that is a fault then it is the fault I have spent four and a half years building and I have thought about it for about eleven weeks and I am not going to mend it.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that market street had heard a man put a fault of his own arrangement out loud in a market street on a Thursday, and about five had heard nine days.

Then the cost of it was said out loud, and it was said by a person who is not anybody's clerk and is not anybody's friend, and she said it in the middle of that market street and she has said this kind of thing about nine times in four and a half years and about five of the nine turned out to be exactly right and nobody has ever been able to tell which five before the fact.

The woman of about thirty-three who keeps a chandler's shop at the low end of the boards put a hand on the front of her own counter and looked at about two hundred people and said it, and nobody thanked her and nobody interrupted her.

“**Then say what it costs, because I have stood behind this counter on about four hundred market mornings and I have not heard a price,**” she said. “**It costs this. About four hundred people are going to walk out of this market street this afternoon and about nine of them are going to know that four gates in nine counties are standing shut on somebody's account, and about three hundred and ninety of the rest are going to walk past a thing they cannot name. That is the arrangement working. That is exactly what four and a half years of it is for.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people in that market street had heard a woman say that the county's best arrangement was made out of four hundred people not being able to say a word, and about five had heard a chandler price a habit.

“**And I would like about nine people to notice that I have said the true thing and that I have not said a single one of you is brave,**” she said. “**Nobody in this street did anything this morning. Four people in nine counties did something yesterday and nine of you heard about it over a table of nobody's. That is the whole of the difference between this county and a place with an army in it, and it is a difference you can only see on a bad morning, and this is a bad morning.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street had heard the cost of nine days of held crossings given in the only currency this county has, and five had heard a woman say that nobody in that street had done anything.

Then the man of about thirty-three who sells cloth three doors up asked the question that about nine people in that market street had been carrying since the middle of Harvestmonth, and he asked it out loud, and nobody stopped him.

“**Then how does anybody find out that it is working?**”

Nobody said anything and two hundred people in that market street had heard a man ask a question with no answer in it, and about four of them had thought of an answer and did not say it, and about five had not thought of one.

The badge-man said the true thing about it and did not add to it, and nobody thanked him.

“**They do not. On the tenth of Hearthmonth there will be nine gates or there will be four, and about nine people in this market street will be able to tell you which, and that is the whole of the reporting and there is no more of it and there is not going to be.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that market street had heard a man of fifty-eight say that his own arrangement could not tell whether it had worked, and five had heard a man decline to know.

Then the man of about thirty-three asked a second question, and he asked it because the chandler had said a thing at the low end of the boards that he had not stopped thinking about, and nobody thanked him for asking it.

“**Where does anybody write down that four gates were standing?**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had understood that this was the whole of the argument and five had not.

The woman of about thirty-three who keeps the chandler's shop answered it in her own trade and out loud and about nine people heard her.

“**Nowhere. That is what a chandler is for. I have got a book and I have got a slate and I have got a shelf, and if I write nine gates in any of them then in about four years it is a thing with nine gates in it, and a man comes to my shop and asks which gate and I have to say, and then he has got a gate. I sell nails and lamp oil and rope and I do not sell gates and I am not going to start.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street had heard a woman refuse a book, and five had heard a shopkeeper describe the difference between a thing you sell and a thing you know.

Then the market went on, and the two hundred went back to about nine things, and about nine people stayed at the boards.

What was not in that market street that morning was worth putting down in order, because about nine people in nine counties have said since that a county cannot see the shape of a thing that is not there.

There was no paper. There was nothing to sign and nobody offered to sign anything and nobody asked whether there was anything to sign. There was no book on those boards that morning, and the small book in the coat of the man of about thirty-one in a livery was inside the coat, and it had a page in it with a line on it and a place for the county and the gate and the day and his own name, and he was four miles down the lane and not in that street.

Nobody thanked anybody and about four people in nine counties have said that the first of nine days of held crossings cost this county nothing at all, and about five have said that it cost it a thing it had not had before, which is that about four hundred people in a market street now know that there is nothing to be told, and about four hundred people in nine counties do not know it, and nobody has agreed which of those two numbers matters.

Between the second hour and the sixth hour that street did the ordinary half of a Thursday, and the ordinary half is nine tenths of every day anybody in nine counties has ever had, and it went on with a Crown party's cart on the made road above it and four men on a lane four miles off and none of it connected to any of it.

The weigh-house weighed two loads of lime and a load of wool, and the man who keeps it said out loud, to four people who were in and five who were not, that the parish rolls came past his floor at the rate of four hundred and nine a month in a good month, and that nobody had ever asked him to make a mark on one of them. Nobody thanked him, and nine people in that market town know why he said it, which is that a man who can weigh everything is the only man in a county who can prove that a thing happened, and he has never once used it.

The man who owns the field on the low side of a cut a mile and a half off came in on foot at about the fifth hour and did not go to the boards. He stood at the low end of that street with his hands behind him for as long as it takes to turn a cart, and then he walked back out of it, and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him what he had come for, and four people in that market street have said since that he came to hear nine words, and five have said that he came to see whether four hundred people would stop, and nobody in nine counties has ever been able to establish which.

The light went out of that street before the light went out of the sky, because the bank to the west of that market town is higher than the street and the street runs east and west, and the lamp at the high end of the boards was lit at the fourth hour and stood lit until the market was gone. Nobody thanked anybody and two hundred people in that market street had been in daylight for six hours and five had noticed the lamp.

And then, at about the seventh hour, the first crossing was held, and it was held at the market town's own gate, and about nine people in that market street saw it happen from about two hundred yards off and about five did not.

A Crown cart came up the made road with two men on it and a man of about thirty-one in a livery walking at the near wheel, and it came to the gate at the market town's own gate and stopped, and the man in the livery got his small book out of his coat and opened it and asked the ordinary question, which is which road goes up to the works.

Nobody thanked him and the people at that gate did the ordinary work of that gate, which is to open it, and to let a cart through, and to say nothing to anybody, and they did all three of those things in about four seconds and one of them did not look up.

“**Then which road,**” the man in the livery said, and he said it out loud at about the seventh hour on a market morning with about nine people standing about eleven feet off.

Nobody said anything to anybody, and the gate's ordinary work went on, and the man in the livery read back what he had written and shut the book and put it inside his coat and got back on the near wheel, and the cart went on up the made road.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a cart went up a made road four miles off and then four days and came to a coast and never once was told what that made road was the road to, and about five have said that the made road is not the road to the works, it is the road everybody in nine counties uses, and that about four hundred people in this market street have used it twice a day for four and a half years and have never once called it anything at all.
