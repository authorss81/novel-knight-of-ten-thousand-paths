# Chapter 0694

**Hearthmonth the fourteenth, YR 321, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting, a sheet of printed paper read out in the open at a gate four miles and about nine hundred paces up the made road, and a figure of eleven with a day on it.**

The room over the weigh-house has been shut since noon on Saturday and is shut behind its own door on every day that is not a market morning, and the Tuesday is not a market morning, and about four people in nine counties have said that the room is shut about five days in seven and that the hearing has never once been told so in writing, and about five have said that the hearing is not a thing that can be told anything.

The road from that market town runs two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand with eleven houses on it and about nine hundred people and then goes on, made, past the last house and past the last gate, and the crossing is four miles and about nine hundred paces up it, and on the fourteenth of Hearthmonth the whole of it was in a wet that had been coming since the Sunday and that had turned the two ruts at the half mile into a single channel of standing water a foot across. A man walking it up with a barrow has to go in that channel and come out on the crown and put the barrow down on the crown to get his breath, and then go on, and four people in nine counties have said that this is the road nine counties has for the shape of the thing it is trying to do and about five have said that a road you can wade is a road anybody can walk and that is not an accident.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in nine counties have said that four and a half years of this arrangement has never once had to be carried up that road in the wet, and that the whole of the arrangement is about nine people who will get up and go to a gate and say no, and that a man with a barrow is not one of the nine and has never been one, and about five have said that is the arrangement working.

Nobody thanked anybody and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down the thirteen stairs, and the boards in that market town stood in an empty street with nothing on them, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the fourteenth of Hearthmonth was the day a thing was read out about four miles off and that nobody in that market town heard it read.

What was read out was this, and its shape is worth setting out in full, because a person at a gate can see all of it and nine counties has never seen any of it.

It was one sheet of a printed form with the county at the top and a district under it and a date, and then a paragraph, and then a schedule. The paragraph was the ordinary paragraph and every word of it was correct. It required the district to give up, at a crossing named on it, a stated figure of persons, by a stated day, for such aid as the officers of the Crown should require, and it took the keeping of them in the ordinary way and it was sealed in the ordinary way.

The figure was eleven. The day was the eighteenth of Hearthmonth. The crossing was named as the crossing where the lane off this made road takes the channel by the bridge. There was nothing in it about a punishment. There was nothing in it about an arrest. There was nothing in it about a man being taken. It said persons, and it said a number, and it said a day, and a person at a gate can do that in his head at the fourth hour in the wet.

The arithmetic came from the ordinary place arithmetic comes from, which is the roll. The two parishes that use that crossing had between them nine hundred and ninety-five persons on the last return. The district's standing assessment for a levy of aid is one in ninety. Nine hundred and ninety-five divided by ninety is eleven and five over ninety, and the form says take the whole number and do not carry the remainder forward, and nobody in nine counties has ever argued with the form about the five, because the form has been the form since before the middle of Longlight and the five has been thrown away every quarter for about four and a half years.

That is the whole of it. Nine hundred and ninety-five on a roll, and one in ninety, and eleven written in a place a man can reach with his hand.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a number is the only thing in nine counties that nobody has ever been able to refuse, and about five have said that a number is the only thing a person at a gate can be asked for without needing anybody to stand next to him.

The ordinary way a thing like that is carried out at a gate in this county is worth putting down, because it is the way every figure that has ever come up that road has been carried out, and nothing about it is special and all of it is a practice.

The sheet goes to the gate. The man or woman who keeps the gate reads it, standing up, in the open, with whoever is there, and does not fold it and does not put it away first. Then it is read back, out loud, to the same people, and the person keeping the gate says whether it is true. Then the figure and the day and the crossing go on the gate in chalk if the gate has a place to chalk, or on the back of a sheet nailed inside the house if it does not, and the date goes beside them. Then the man in a livery writes down what was said and not what was meant and reads that back too, and both readings go up the made road in opposite directions, and neither one is ever contradicted in writing by anybody.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in nine counties have said that this is the fourth part of this county that has never once been written down, after the three clauses, after the register, and after the day-book, and about five have said that it is not a part of anything and that it is a gate.

Then it was read out, in the open, at the gate, at about the fourth hour, to about nine people who were there on their own business, and the man who read it was the man of about thirty-one in a livery with the small book, and he read it standing up and he read it all of it.

Nobody thanked him and nine people at that gate heard a Crown officer read a figure of eleven persons out of a legal sheet at the fourth hour on a Tuesday in the wet, and about four of the nine heard every word and about five heard the figure and nothing else.

Then he turned the sheet over and read the hand at the top of it, which is what a man does when he reads a paper in the open, and he read it the way he reads everything, which is that he read what was on the paper.

“**And by the Marshal's hand. Edric Hunn.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people at that gate had heard a name that nobody in nine counties had heard in about eleven years except off a piece of paper, and about five had heard two words at the end of a sentence and had not known that they were a name.

Then he read back what he had written, and what he had written was that the district was given a figure and a day and a crossing, and that nobody at that gate had said anything.

Nobody thanked him and about four people at that gate had heard a Crown officer say the true thing about a county that has not answered a paper, and about five had heard a man read back his own page.

Then the man of about thirty-eight who puts boards on the inside of gates came out of the house he keeps behind the gate and stood at the low side of it with his hands behind his back, and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him anything, and about nine people at that gate had seen him come out and about five had not known there was a man in the house.

Then he said the thing that actually stopped that gate, and he said it in his own trade and not to the sheet, and nobody thanked him and nobody stopped him.

“**You have got a form that asks five things and it says the persons, and the horses, and the carts, and the goods, and the labour. You have not read the fifth one out. There is no person here for you. There are eleven of us on this gate and I am one and my mother is one and there is nobody else, and if you take eleven off this gate then the gate is not a gate, it is a place where men shout at each other about a chain.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at that gate had heard a gatekeeper refuse a figure by naming a fifth thing on the same form, and about five had heard a man say there was nobody to give.

Then he did the arithmetic, out loud, in the open, in about nine seconds, and nobody had asked him to and nobody thanked him for it.

“**The form's own rate is nine days a person, and that rate has stood since before the middle of Longlight and nobody in nine counties has ever argued with it. Eleven persons at nine days is ninety-nine days of labour. Ninety-nine days on nine men is eleven days. I have nine men and they come on the seventeenth and they work till the twenty-eighth and I will put it on this gate in chalk and any man in a coat that comes up here can read it, and that is the figure, and I am not refusing the figure, I am giving it you in the other one your own paper asks me for.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at that gate had heard a man at a gate obey a paper by turning it into the fifth thing on it, and about five had heard a man be arithmetic.

Then the woman of about forty-nine who keeps a kitchen and four beds on this made road came up on foot from the low end because she had a barrow of stuff for the lane, and she was not asked anything and nobody thanked her, and she said the one true thing about a figure with a day on it and she said it in the open to about nine people.

“**Then here it is, and I am the only person on this road who can count, and I have said that before. A name you can say no to. A road you can shut, if you ever wanted to shut one, and not one of you has wanted to in four and a half years. And a figure with a day on it, nobody in this county has ever once been able to say no to, and I know why, and it is because a figure is not a person, and a person at a gate can add.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people at that gate had heard a woman who keeps four beds put the price of nine hundred and ninety-five on a roll, and about five had heard her say a county cannot refuse a number.

“**We have spent four and a half years making three things we could refuse and the office has sent us a fourth one, and the fourth one is the only one of the four that cannot be refused, and it can be answered on Friday.**”

Nobody thanked her and nobody argued with her and about nine people at that gate had heard a woman give the whole of nine counties' position away in the plainest words anybody has used at a gate in about four and a half years, and nobody had thanked her for it and nobody has since.

Then the man of about thirty-eight asked the man in the livery a question in the open, and it was asked out loud and nobody stopped him and about nine people heard it.

“**What do you write at the eleven, if I do not give you eleven men?**”

And the man of about thirty-one in a livery said it out loud in the open at about the ninth hour at that gate and did not look at the sheet while he said it, and nobody thanked him.

“**I write eleven.**”

“**Then write it,**” the man of about thirty-eight said, “**and chalk it under mine, and let a man in about four years find the two figures on the same gate and work out which of us was the district.**”

Nobody thanked either of them and about four people at that gate had heard two men agree to leave a contradiction on a piece of chalk on purpose, and about five had heard a gatekeeper say write it.

Then nothing happened at that gate for about nine minutes, and nobody was hurt and nobody was killed and nobody was told anything, and the man of about thirty-one in a livery put the sheet inside his coat and said one thing at the gate on his way out and he said it to the gate and not to the nine people.

“**I am going to write that the district has answered in labour and that the answer was given by the person who keeps the gate and not by anybody in a room, and I am going to read it back, and I have never had a page like this one and I do not have a column for it.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at that gate had heard a Crown officer say that his own form had nowhere to put a county that converted a figure, and about five had heard a man say he had no column for it.

Then the nine men came up that gate one at a time over the following two days on their own errands, and none of them had been told by anybody that there was a figure, and none of them asked, and each of them read the chalk on the gate on the way past and went on, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine men reading eleven words of chalk and going on is the whole of what nine counties has become and about five have said that nine men reading eleven words of chalk and going on is nine men who will be somewhere else on the seventeenth.

Nobody thanked him and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the ninth of those nine men is a man of about fifty-three who keeps no trade anybody can name and who came up that made road from a village two days off and who put his hand flat on the chalk and read it and said nothing, and about four people have said that a man who reads a figure and says nothing is a man who has been counted before and does not want to be counted again, and about five have said that he said nothing because there was nothing to say to a gate.

Then the answer came back down the made road on foot, because it always comes back on foot, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head was in the lane outside the shut room at about the sixth hour and the man of about thirty-eight walked past him and told him the whole of it in about forty words standing in the wet with his hands behind his back.

Nobody thanked either of them and the badge-man said one sentence about it and it was twenty-four words and he did not say who had read the paper or where the figure came from.

“**They have sent us a number. I have got three clauses about people and roads and not one of them is about a number.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that lane had heard a man of fifty-eight say that a figure is outside four and a half years of work, and about five had heard a man find a hole in his own arrangement on a Tuesday night in the wet, and about four people in nine counties have said that the hole is the third one and that a thing with two holes in it is not the same object as a thing with three, and that nobody in nine counties has ever been told which of those two is worse.

Then the light went out of that lane at about the fourth hour, earlier than it should have, because the bank to the west is higher than the lane and the lane runs east and west, and the lamp outside the shut room of the weigh-house was lit at the fourth hour and stood lit until the seventh, and about four people in that lane had noticed a lamp burning outside a shut room on a Tuesday and about five had not.

Nobody thanked anybody and the answer was in chalk on a gate four miles and about nine hundred paces up the made road in about eleven words, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the first refusal of a Crown figure in four and a half years was written in chalk by a man who puts boards on the inside of gates, and about five have said that it was not a refusal and that the man said so himself twice and that it is the only refusal in four and a half years that its own author called something else.

Nobody thanked anybody and the sheet went back up the made road in a coat inside a made road that is four miles and about nine hundred paces long, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a figure of eleven persons went back up that road in a coat on the fourteenth of Hearthmonth and had gone four miles and about nine hundred paces by the seventh hour and that nothing about it had been signed by anybody, and about five have said that the fifth thing on the form was the whole of what this county did with it and that the fifth thing was the only one anybody was ever going to ask for again.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a figure of eleven that can be answered on Friday and a form that asks five things and has one column for each of four of them, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sheet of foolscap at 1, a sheet of writing paper at 2, a stick of red chalk at 18, a kettle at 30, a bench at 31, a pair of bellows at 32, a hurdle at 34, a saw blade at 35, a crate at 36, and a tally board at 39.** One, two, eighteen, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six and thirty-nine is two hundred and fifty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and fifty-eight pence is five marks and eighteen over.** Nine hundred and ninety-five has been on that roll since the last return and will be on the next one, and the five that gets thrown away every quarter is the fifth thing anybody in nine counties has found to give for free, and about four people in nine counties have said that this county has been giving that five away since before the middle of Longlight and about five have said that the five is the only figure in nine counties that nobody has ever once tried to keep, and that it will be missed when somebody notices it, and that nobody has noticed it yet.
