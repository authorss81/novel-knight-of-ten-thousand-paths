# Chapter 0677

**Embermonth the thirteenth, YR 321, a Tuesday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door, the third hour at the boards in a market street that is not a market with about nine people in it, a woman of about forty-four who keeps a crossing nine miles off saying no to a paper in front of about nine people, and a man of thirty-two who is not thanked for asking her.**

She came in on foot from the west at about the second hour and she was nine miles off her crossing and she had been walking since before it was light, and Nine people were at the boards because it was a Tuesday and there was nothing else to be in that street for.

About four of the nine had seen her come up the lane and about five had not, and the badge-man was at the low end of the boards with his bar and had not asked her anything and said afterwards that he knew who she was from the way she was walking and had made his decision about it in about four seconds.

He had said out loud at the second hour, to nine people, the whole of what he was not going to do, and four of the nine had understood it and about five had heard a man being careful.

“**A woman of about thirty-six told me on the sixth of this month at a board in a market with four hundred people in it that there was a sixth clause and that one person had objected to it and that the objection had been right. She did not say the woman's name and she did not say the county and she said that nobody was going to come and ask her. I have had seven days to decide what I do about that and I have decided that I do nothing at all about it, and I want about nine people to notice that a man of fifty-eight with a badge has spent a week doing nothing on purpose.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at the boards said nothing and about five said something at once and then stopped.

“**And I am going to say the rest of it now so that nobody has to guess at it,**” he said. “**There is no head in nine counties with which to send anybody to ask a woman of about forty-four whether she will sign a thing nine miles off. I am not going to make one. The man who wrote it is not going to send anybody because he has not sent anybody in about four years and because the last time something was sent to a person in this county it was not sent by anybody. And the only arrangement that would put a question in front of her this morning is an arrangement, and nine people have spent a year making sure that there is not one.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in a street that was not a market had heard a man explain that the correct procedure was to do nothing at all, and about four of them had worked out that he was not being careful and five thought he was.

She was about forty-four and she kept a crossing where the made road goes over a channel nine miles off in this county, and she had kept it about nineteen years with the man who keeps it with her, and there are about nine households at the two ends of that crossing and it is the only way any of them can get a cart out.

About nine tons of stone was standing in the low approach to it.

That is the whole of what had happened and it is worth setting down before anything else because everything after it is a consequence. On the fourth of Fallowmonth a Crown crew working forward out of a yard four hundred yards up the made road put about nine tons of cut stone in the low approach to that crossing to stand a sill on, because the ground at the high end was soft and the soft ground is a mile off and the stone was already broken.

They put it there at about the ninth hour and they were gone by the fourth hour the next morning and nobody came back for it. It is nine tons across a crossing about eleven feet wide and it is not a bar and it is not a chain and there is nothing to lift. A cart cannot get over it. A barrow cannot get over it. A person can get over it by going up the bank and down the other side.

Nine households at that crossing have been going round by the lane since the fourth of Fallowmonth, which adds about half an hour each way for a loaded cart, and about four of them have stopped going round and have stopped going at all.

That last part is the part that gets a road closed in nine counties and it has happened without anybody proposing it.

A household of about five people on the high side of that crossing sends a loaded cart to the market town four mornings a week and has been sending it round by the lane since the fourth of Fallowmonth, which adds about fifty minutes each way on a bad road, and about nine weeks ago the man of that household lost a standing contract over milk because the buyer said he could not get the load in before the market opened. He did not lose the contract because of the stone. He lost it because of fifty minutes, twice a week, for nine weeks.

Nobody proposed that either. Nobody shut anything. The stone is nine tons of cut stone in a low approach and there is nothing to lift and there is no moment at which it began.

“**I have been going round since the fourth,**” a man of about forty-three who keeps a yard four streets up the market street said at the boards on the Tuesday afternoon, out loud, to about nine people, “**and I have got four horses and one cart and I can take my load over that stone by going up the bank and down, and it takes me about nine minutes and I lose about nine minutes every time, and I have been doing it since the fourth of Fallowmonth and nobody has told me to and nobody has asked me and there is nobody in nine counties that I could tell.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at the boards had heard the whole of that and five had heard a man say that he could get a cart over some stone.

“**And I am not saying I cannot,**” he said. “**I am saying I have been doing it nine weeks and my knees have started, and there are about four of us at that crossing doing it and none of us has said no to anything, because there has never been a thing to say no to.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in a street that was not a market had heard the cost of nine tons of stone described by a man who goes over it.

“**Say what you have come for,**” the badge-man said, and he said it in nine words and stood back about nine paces.

She said what she had come for, and she said it to nine people in a street that is not a market, and she did not raise her voice once and about four people in that street have said since that the only time in about nine days anybody's voice went up in that street was the first of the month and it was not hers.

“**I have come to be asked whether I will put my name to the sixth clause,**” she said, “**and I have not been asked, and I have come nine miles on the word that there was a thing with a number on it, and I want to say no before anybody asks me, and I want to say it in a street because I have been told that is where things in this county are said.**”

Nobody said anything and four of the nine people at the boards understood the whole of that and five understood that a woman had come nine miles to say no to something.

“**Who told you there was a sixth clause,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat said.

“**A woman of about thirty-six told me on the sixth day of this month at the boards in a market where there were about four hundred people in it, and she told me the number and she told me that one person had objected to that number and that the objection had been right,**” the woman of about forty-four said. “**And she told me that nobody was going to come and ask me. That was the sentence. She said that a woman of about thirty-four was at the high end of the boards and that she was not going to come.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at the boards understood that a woman had been told in advance that she would not be asked, and about five understood that a woman had been told a true thing.

Then she said the reason, and it took her about nine seconds, and nine people in that street heard all of it and it is the reason the whole of this volume is built the way it is.

“**On the fourth of Fallowmonth a crew put nine tons of my crossing's approach and did not come back. On the sixth I was given a paper by a man in a livery about thirty-one with a small book, and he asked me to sign that I had seen the works put there, and I said no, and he wrote down that I said no, and he read it back to me and he said that it was very good of me, and that is the only paper I have ever said no to and I have had about nine days to work out that I will say no to a second one.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards looked at the ground and five looked at the lane.

“**And then on the Tuesday after that, the man who keeps that crossing with me went down there on foot at about the fourth hour in the rain and asked the officer with the book whether the district had it in front of them and would require the stone to be taken away, and the officer said no, and he said it politely, and he said that the works of the Crown's officers in the keeping of the works are not removable on the asking of a person, and he said that it was very good of him, and he wrote it down and read it back, and my man came home nine miles in the wet and has not been able to put it down.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at that street had heard the whole of that and about five had heard a woman say that a man had been told no politely and had written it down.

“**So here is the whole of what I have to say,**” the woman of about forty-four said, “**and I have said it in about nine seconds and I am not going to say it again. You have written nine hundred words and about nine of the nine clauses are right and I have read them twice on the road and I want that noticed. And the sixth clause wants a person to say yes on behalf of a road. There is no person. I am the person at that crossing and I cannot say yes on behalf of a road, and if I say yes on behalf of a road then I have stopped being a person who keeps a crossing and become a person who speaks for a road, and that is the one thing I have not done in about nineteen years, and I am not going to start it in a month like this one.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in a street that was not a market had heard the whole of what a month like this one is.

Then the badge-man said the true thing about it, and he said it in nine words, and about four people at the boards understood all of it and about five heard a man say a sentence.

“**A thing that cannot take orders cannot sign anything. That is not a hole. That is what it is.**”

Nobody said anything to that and the woman of about forty-four said one more thing and it was the last of it.

“**And I will say the other half of it so that nobody in this street can go about thinking that I came nine miles to be difficult.**” She put her hand flat on the boards, which nobody at the boards had seen a person do on a Tuesday. “**A man in a livery came to my crossing and asked for a thing and the man with me said no and was polite about saying it and it went in a book, and I have wanted for about nine days for that book to be something I could hold. And you are asking me to put my name on a paper that a man will keep, and I have had nine days of a man I have kept a crossing with for nineteen years not being able to put one thing down, and I am not going to add my name to a piece of paper and take that off him.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at the boards looked at the woman of about forty-four and about five looked at the lane, and nine people in that street have said since that nobody asked her what her name was, and about four of them have said since that that was the correct arrangement and five have not been able to say why.

Aren Kest asked her one question. He asked it out loud, in the street, in the ordinary way, and about nine people heard it.

“**Then what would you say yes to?**”

She thought about it for as long as it takes a carter to get a load round, and four people at the boards watched her think about it and about five watched the boards.

“**I would say yes to a thing that has nobody's name on it,**” she said, “**and I have not got one, and I am not going to make one up on a Tuesday to be able to say that I have been useful.**”

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat worked out the whole of it out loud, in the street, in about nine words, and about nine people in that street heard it and nobody thanked him for it.

“**Nine counties has written a plan that cannot be signed,**” he said. “**Not because the words are wrong. Because every signature it wants is a person who has agreed to speak for a road, and there is not one of those in nine counties and there is not going to be one, and a thing that wants a signature from a person who does not exist is not a plan that anybody has spoiled. It is a plan that was never possible, and it is the best one anybody in this county has written, and about nine people in nine counties are going to spend the next month finding that out and four of them are going to enjoy it.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard all of that and five had heard a man say that a plan was never possible.

“**And there is no clause about that,**” he said. “**I have been looking for about seven days. There is no clause about a thing that cannot be signed and no clause about a person who will not speak for a road and no clause about a coalition that is very hard to defeat because nobody in it will take an order. There is nothing. And there has not been one for about four and a half years, and I have not written one, and I am not going to, because I do not know what it would say.**”

Nobody thanked him and the badge-man said one thing, and it was nine words, and about four people heard it.

“**Then that is the first true thing anybody has said in this street in about a month and it is no good.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in a street that was not a market had heard a man with a badge say that a true thing was no good, and four of them had understood why and about five had not.

Then she picked her hand up off the boards and went up the lane, and nobody in nine counties said anything to her between the boards and the top of the rise, and the man of about thirty-four stood at the boards for nine minutes afterwards without taking the sheet out of his coat, and about four people in that street noticed that and about five did not.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is about nine tons of cut stone in the low approach to a crossing nine miles off that nobody has come back for, and a woman of about forty-four who walked nine miles to say no to a paper before she was asked it, and a man of thirty-two who did not ask her and has not been thanked for not asking her, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a cart hood at 19, a cushion for a cart seat at 21, a bundle cord at 22, a quire of paper at 27, a week's bread at 31, a window catch at 32, a whetstone at 34, a grinding stone at 35, a pound of nails at 36, and a firelock at 40.** Nineteen, twenty-one, twenty-two, twenty-seven, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six and forty is two hundred and ninety-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and ninety-seven pence is six marks and nine over.** She went nine miles back down the west road in the dark and about nine people at the boards watched the top of the lane until she was a shape on it and about five of them went home, and about nine households at that crossing went round by the lane that night with a load on because there were about nine tons of stone in the low approach and nobody in nine counties has standing to say a stone is anybody's.
