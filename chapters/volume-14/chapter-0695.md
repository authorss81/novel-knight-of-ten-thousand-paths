# Chapter 0695

**Hearthmonth the eighteenth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour at the crossing where the lane off this made road takes the channel, about nine men of that gate working the eleven days they put in chalk, a Crown cart on the deck, sleet driving off the water, and nine minutes.**

The bridge at that crossing is a cart bridge and has been for about ninety years, and it is one cart wide, and it has a plank deck about nine feet long on two beams, and there is a sill beam under the low end which was put in cracked in the spring and which has been propped since the middle of Longlight with a length of oak about four inches through, wedged at the high side and standing in the water at the low side, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that that prop is the only thing anybody in this county has built in four and a half years that is holding up a Crown order.

The lane at that crossing runs east and west and is one cart wide for about nine yards at the head of the bridge, and on the high side of it there is a bank that carries the hedge of a field, and on the low side there is a drop of about four feet to the channel margin with a stone wall at the bottom of it that goes under at the winter level. The bank is grass in summer and mud in a wet winter, and on the eighteenth of Hearthmonth it was mud to about the depth of a boot sole and it had a rut in it from a cart that had gone up in the summer and had not come down.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the whole of the eighteenth of Hearthmonth was decided on that bank and that nobody who was on the bank at the time had any idea of it, and about five have said that a thing decided on a bank is a thing that anybody in nine counties could have decided and that about nine of them were standing on it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the sleet that morning was coming off the channel on the north and it was coming sideways, and the water in the channel was making a noise that took the top off a voice at four feet, so that anybody at that bridge on that morning had to lean in to be heard or had to wait for a lull, and about nine people have said since that a thing done on a bridge in that weather in that noise is a thing done where nobody can hear it said afterwards.

About nine men were on the bridge at about the second hour, because the gate had written ninety-nine days of labour on the low side of that gate in chalk on the fourteenth and the eighteenth was the first day of the eleven, and they were moving sand off the high side of the approach where it had come down in the wet, and one of them had a barrow on the deck and one of them was on the low side of the deck with a shovel.

Then a Crown cart came up the lane from the made road with a man of about thirty-one in a livery walking at the near wheel, and it came onto the deck and stopped, because a cart bridge is one cart wide and there was a barrow on it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the driver of that cart stood on the seat and looked at the nine men and looked at the lane and did not get down, and the man in the livery got his small book out and said the thing he had come to say, which is that the sheet said persons and that the fifth thing on a form is not a substitute where the first thing on it is wanted.

Then he said the day, which was that day.

Nobody said anything to that and about nine men on a bridge in sleet had heard a Crown officer name a day that was the day it was, and about four of the nine had understood that the chalk on the gate had not been accepted, and about five had not understood anything except that they were standing on a bridge.

And Aren Kest came up the lane at about the second hour on foot with his stick in his right hand and got onto the deck and put his hand on the high handrail and looked at the water going under the low side, and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him what he was doing and nobody in nine counties had standing to ask him.

Nobody said anything to that and about four of the nine men on that bridge had worked out who he was in about nine seconds and about five had not, and none of the nine had been told to expect him and none of them had been sent for him.

Then he took his stick in his left hand and went down on his knees on a wet plank deck with a bad leg, and he put his right hand under the sill beam at the low side where it came down to the water, and he did not say anything at all.

Nobody said anything and about four men on that bridge had watched a man get down on a wet deck in the sleet and about five had watched a man with a stick stand still.

Then the nine minutes began, and nobody has been able to give a shorter account of nine minutes than this one and nobody has been able to give a longer one.

What he was doing was this. The sill beam was carrying the low end of the deck, and the deck was carrying the cart, and the oak prop was carrying the sill beam, and the water was under all of it and moving. You cannot lift that from under a loaded beam. You can work it back and forth about a quarter of an inch at a time and you can do that with one man's weight on the end of it and the beam will come about half an inch in about four minutes and the whole of it comes in about nine. He got his shoulder under the end of the sill beam and he worked it, and it moved, and he stopped and got his breath and worked it again.

Nobody said anything and four men on that bridge had heard the beam move twice and five had heard the water.

The cold that morning was in the sleet and not in the ground, and it went through a wet sleeve in about four minutes, and the hands that were working on that beam had been working since the seventh hour and had gone from stiff to numb in that order. A man whose hands have gone numb cannot feel whether a thing is moving and has to watch a line, and he watched the line of the beam against the line of the prop, and about nine people in nine counties have said that the whole of what a bridge going is two lines not agreeing with each other any more.

Nobody said anything and the man of about thirty-eight who puts boards on the inside of gates came to the low side of the deck at the sixth minute and stood with his hand on the frame and did not get down and did not go away, and nobody thanked him for standing there and nobody has ever thanked anybody for standing there.

Then one of the nine men asked him a question out loud in the sleet, and he was a man of about thirty-seven who has kept that gate for eleven years, and he asked it with his mouth about a foot from the other man's ear because of the noise off the water.

“**Is it going to hold up when you are not on it?**”

And the man of about thirty-eight said nine words back into his ear and turned and went up the lane to get a bar, and did not stop.

“**Nothing holds up when you are not on it.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody thanked either of them and nine people in nine counties have said since that the second of those two sentences is the whole of what happened at that crossing and that four people have understood it since and five have repeated it without understanding it, and that the two sets have never spoken to the other.

The man of about thirty-one in the livery said one thing at about the fourth minute and it was not a courtesy and it was not said to be pleasant, and nobody thanked him for it.

“**Will you stand up, please, and let us take the cart over.**”

Nobody said anything and Aren Kest did not stand up, and the man in the livery did not say it again, and about four men on that bridge had understood that the man in the livery had asked once and would not ask twice, and about five had heard a man ask a question in a storm.

Then two of the nine men on that bridge came to help him, because two men will come to help whether or not anybody asks, and he stopped and looked at them and said five words, and the five words were in his own trade and not in anybody's.

“**Go and stand over there.**”

Nobody thanked him and the two men went and stood over there, and neither of them argued, and neither of them has ever said out loud why they went, and about four people in nine counties have said since that those two men went because a man who puts boards on the inside of gates has never once in four and a half years asked a man in a coat to stand over there and has just done it in the sleet, and about five have said that the two men went because it was easier than the beam.

Nobody said anything and about nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of the second clause and that it works in both directions at once, which is that a burden only falls on a man who proposed, and that a man who proposes nothing cannot lay the burden anywhere, and that in about four and a half years nobody has ever needed to know which of those two it is going to be.

Then, in the middle of it, at about the fifth minute, the thing happened that actually stopped the cart, and it was not the bridge and it was not the man on the deck.

A man of about forty-four who keeps a wain and a team of four on this made road had been stopped on the lane behind that Crown cart for about nine minutes with nine sacks of wool on his wain going to a market two days off, and he could not get round the cart because the lane is one cart wide at that point and the verge on the high side is a bank with a drop on it.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody had asked him anything and nobody had told him the cart would be there and he had come up that lane at the ordinary pace of a man with a load.

Then he turned his team off the lane on to the high bank at the head of the bridge, which a wain can do and a cart cannot, because a wain's wheels are short and a cart's are not, and he got the wain up on to about nine feet of wet grass bank at about the sixth minute and turned it there and backed it down across the lane at the head of the bridge so that its tail stood square across the lane with its shafts out over the channel.

Nobody thanked him and he did not say a word and he got up onto the bank with the shafts and sat on the shafts with his feet on the wain's tongue and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a man of about forty-four who keeps a wain and a team of four decided the whole of the eighteenth of Hearthmonth in about four seconds by sitting down on his own shafts.

Then he put his hand on the near hindquarter of the near horse and left it there for the length of the job, which is a thing a carter does so that a horse knows a man is behind it, and the four horses stood in the sleet on nine feet of bank with a nine-sack load behind them and did not step off, and about four people in nine counties have said that the stopping of that Crown cart was done by four horses and a carter's hand and not by a man of thirty-two on a wet deck, and about five have said that the deck had to go first or there would have been nothing to stop.

Nobody thanked him and he got his wain down off that bank at about the eighth minute with a rope and one wheel at a time, and it took longer than getting it up and four men helped him with the rope and five did not and nobody thanked any of them, and the wain came down onto the lane with its tail about nine feet from the near end of the bridge and its shafts pointing back up the lane, which is the one way a wain can stand that a cart cannot be got past on either side.

Then the driver of the Crown cart tried to back up, which is what a cart does, and the wain was there, and the bank was behind him, and the man of about thirty-one in the livery looked at a wain across a lane for about as long as it takes to turn a cart.

Nobody said anything and nobody thanked anybody and about four of the nine men on that bridge had understood that a cart on a one-cart lane with a wain across it at the head is a cart that is not going anywhere, and about five had understood that a man who stops a cart does not need a bridge.

Then the beam came.

Nobody thanked him and nobody said anything and the low end of that deck went down about a foot in about three seconds and the plank at the corner split about a foot from the nail and the cart stood on a deck that was not level and the driver got his horses quiet in about four seconds and nobody was hurt and nobody was under it.

Nobody said anything and about nine people in nine counties have said since that there were four people on that bridge at about the seventh minute who were not on a job and that nothing happened to any of them and that nobody has ever asked them what they felt and that one of them has said he felt nothing at all and the other one has not said.

Then the nine minutes were over, and it was the second hour and about nine minutes later than it had been, and the sleet had not stopped and the water had not stopped.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything and the man of about thirty-one in a livery wrote for about four seconds and read it back to nine people on a bridge in the sleet in thirteen words and did not look at the deck while he said it.

“**The crossing is not passable. The deck is down. I will write that.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people on that bridge had heard a Crown officer record a bridge going down as a fact about a deck, and about five had heard a man say he would write it.

Then he got off the cart onto the bank, and the cart went back down the lane behind the wain, and the man of about forty-four came off his shafts and got his team down off the bank one at a time with a rope, and nobody thanked him, and about nine people in nine counties have said that the wain came down off that bank without a scratch on it in a way that a Crown cart could not have come down off it at all, and that this is a thing about a wain and not about nine counties.

Then the nine men of that gate went back to their sand, and nobody thanked them and nobody said anything to them, and by about the seventh hour they had got through the first of their eleven days in a sleet with a deck that would not carry a barrow, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the nine men of that gate went on and worked out all eleven days of it at a crossing that was not passable, and that a barrow could not go over that deck for the length of the eleven and that four of the nine carried on the barrow end over the low side on a rope, and that this is a figure nobody asked them for and cannot be handed back to anybody, and about five have said that it is the first time in about four and a half years that nine men in nine counties have done a thing nobody asked them for and got nothing for it and been told so.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head came up the lane at about the fourth hour and stood at the low end of that bridge in the wet and looked at the deck for about nine seconds, and he did not go on it.

Nobody thanked him and he said one thing, and it was not a defence and it was not a rule and nobody thanked him for it.

“**I have no clause for that. I have had no clause for that since the middle of Longlight and I am not going to write one at a quarter past eleven on a wet Saturday at a gate.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people at that gate had heard a man of fifty-eight say that he had no clause for the first time in four and a half years in a place where the whole of his standing is that he knows which of his three clauses a thing touches, and four people in nine counties have said since that it is the only time anybody has heard him say that he does not know, and five have said that he has known all along and had said so in two rooms in Fallowmonth and that nobody asked him what the difference was.

Nobody thanked him and he did not say one word about who did it and he did not say one word about whether it was right and nobody asked him either of those two things, and about nine people at that gate had wanted to ask him both of them and about four of them had the standing to and did not and about five had neither and asked anyway and were not answered, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the badge-man stood at the low end of that bridge for about a quarter of an hour and said the thirty-eight words that are printed above him and that every one of the thirty-eight was about himself and not about the man on the deck, and about five have said that a man who will not speak for a person he has known for four and a half years is telling that person something and that about nine people in nine counties have worked out what it is and about five have not.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in nine counties have said since that what happened on the eighteenth of Hearthmonth at the crossing where the lane off the made road takes the channel was that a man of thirty-two with a bad leg went down on his knees on a wet deck in a sleet and worked a beam back and forth for nine minutes and that he has never in four and a half years done anything of the kind and cannot afterwards stand in a market street and say that a rule about roads being shut was the whole of the arrangement, because a rule about roads being shut is a rule about the man who proposes the shutting, and nobody proposed this and there is no clause about a man who does not propose, and about four people in nine counties have said that he has opened the fourth hole in the arrangement by hand and on a Saturday, and about five have said that the fourth hole was already open in Longlight and that this is only the first time a person has fallen into it.

**And the cost of that nine minutes is a crossing that is not passable, a deck split about a foot from the nail, a wain that came down a bank sideways, and a rule about roads being shut that can no longer be said to be the whole of anything, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
