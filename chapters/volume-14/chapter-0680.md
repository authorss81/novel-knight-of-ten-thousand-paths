# Chapter 0680

**Wolfmonth the first, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning and the first of a month, the second hour in the market street with the hearing in the street and about four hundred people in the street, nine hundred words standing on a table in a room over a saddler's with nobody's name on them, and a woman of about fifty-nine who keeps the top of a lane deciding at about the sixth hour that she is going to go and ask somebody a question.**

The nine hundred words were still on the table.

They had been put back on the table on the Saturday by a copyist of about twenty-six and they had been covered with a cloth, which is not a thing anybody in nine counties has ever done to a paper, and on the Monday a woman of about thirty-six had gone up the stairs and looked at them and had not touched them, and on the Thursday a man of about forty-three who sells nails had come in and had asked whether he might read them and had been told he might, and had read about four clauses and had put them back and had said that the seventh clause was the wrong one and had gone out.

Nobody said out loud who had put the cloth there. Nine people in nine counties have said it was the copyist and about five have said it was the man with the stick and nobody has been able to establish it and nobody has asked.

Two people had tried to take a copy of it in a fortnight and both had been refused and neither had been argued with.

The first was a man of about forty-four who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned and who had come nine miles on the Thursday and had stood at the low end of the boards and had said that a thing nine hundred words long in a room nine miles from his road was not much of a return, and the copyist had said that she was aware of that and that there was nowhere else for it to go, and he had not asked again.

The second was a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a ferry and a crossing four hundred miles off and who had come back down the made road on the Tuesday with about nine days on her and no money, and who had asked for the nine hundred words and had been told no, and who had said that a no was correct and that she had a no of her own at home and that the two of them together were the only reason she was going back, and who had gone back up the made road on the Wednesday at the fourth hour and had not been at the boards on the first of a month.

Nobody thanked the copyist for either refusal and four people in this market street have said since that she was right both times and about five have said since that it was not that simple, and neither of those sets has ever spoken to the other.

They were on the table on the first of Wolfmonth and they were not signed and they had not been withdrawn, and four hundred people in that market street had heard nine clauses three times in about three weeks and about two hundred of them had stopped what they were doing and about nine people in nine counties could still tell you the number of the clause that a woman of about thirty-six had objected to on a Thursday.

The copyist of about twenty-six did not read the letter. She had said on the first of Embermonth that she had read it twice and was not going to read it a third time, and on the first of Wolfmonth she said nine words about it out loud in the street and went no further.

“**That stands. It is a decision and not a promise broken.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards had understood that and about five had heard a woman say that a decision was not a broken promise.

Then somebody asked the question that nine people in that street had come to ask, which was whether nine hundred words with nobody's name on them were any use at all, and it was asked by a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a horse at the top of the market street and who had not said anything at the boards for about a year before the sixth of Embermonth.

“**I have asked a horse's worth of people,**” she said. “**I asked on the sixth of this month and nobody answered and nine people have said since that two of the four roads I did not ask are the two nobody has a chain on, and that is the whole of what anybody in this county knows about why.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards had heard her ask it and five heard a woman who keeps a horse ask a question about a horse's worth of people.

The badge-man said the standing notice in nine words and then said the other thing, which is that it was a first of a month and that nine people would stand there and that anybody could stand there and that there was nothing to stand in and that nobody was going to ask anybody for a reason.

Then he said the third thing, and he said it in about nine words, and it was the standing defect and nine people in that market street had been waiting two months for him to say it in a street.

“**It came. It came in a form that none of us can refuse at a gate, and I was right on the day and wrong about how long, and the figure stands where it stands and I am not moving it, and nobody in this street is going to thank me for any part of that and nobody ever has.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at the boards had heard the whole of it and about five had heard a man say that he had been wrong about a figure, and the answer to whether being right early was a kindness was given in a market street on the eighth of Embermonth by a woman of about fifty and about nine people had heard it and nobody in that market street on the first of Wolfmonth had heard it from anybody else.

Aren Kest was at the high end of the boards with his stick in his right hand and four people at the boards had been waiting two months for him to say something about the ninth hundred words and about five had given up waiting somewhere in Fallowmonth, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room over the weigh-house was on the thirteen stairs with the register shut under her arm and did not come down, and neither of them spoke to the other and nine people in that market street noticed that and about five did not.

Then the man of thirty-two said the thing that four people in that street had expected and about five had not.

“**I am not taking it back,**” he said, and he said it to nine people and not to four hundred, and nine people in nine counties have said since that he had four hundred people in front of him and chose nine and that that was the whole of what he did.

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had understood the whole of it.

“**Not because eight of the nine clauses are right,**” he said. “**They are not all right and one of them is wrong and a woman of about thirty-four has told me why and I have had ten days and I have not got an argument. I am not taking it back because the fifth, the sixth and the seventh are the same three clauses about what goes into a book and about what a person is called and about when a person can end it, and about four of the nine people in this street know that those three are the best three anybody in this county has written. A county that takes back the three because the sixth is wrong has learned to take back anything, and nine people in nine counties would use that within a year and none of them would be in this street to be asked.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard the whole of it and about five had heard a man say that he was leaving nine hundred words on a table because three of them were good.

“**And I am not going to change the sixth,**” he said, “**because I have been wrong in front of nine people in a room over a saddler's and I did not know I was wrong and a woman walked nine days to tell me, and if I change it now then the only person in nine counties who has ever checked those nine hundred words is a woman from four hundred miles off who does not live here and will not be here in a month.**”

Nobody thanked him and the copyist of about twenty-six said one thing out loud from the thirteen stairs, which is that the sixth clause was the only one of the nine that could not be checked by anybody in this county because the person who would have to check it did not live here and had gone back up a made road on the Wednesday.

Nobody thanked her for saying that and four people at the boards had heard a keeper of a room say out loud that there was a thing in nine counties that could not be checked, and five heard a woman say that a person four hundred miles off was the only one who could do a thing that nine people at the boards were paid nine pence a week to do.

Then the badge-man said one sentence and it was the true one.

“**Then somebody local has to check it and there is not one of those either.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard a man of fifty-eight say the true thing about a gap in a thing they had just agreed was not a hole, and about five heard a man be honest about the county again.

Then the market went on, and two hundred people went back to what they had been doing, and nine people at the boards stayed, and the copyist went up the thirteen stairs with the register shut, and four people at the boards saw her go and about five did not.

What happened at about the sixth hour was on a lane a mile and a half from the market town and nobody in that market street knew about it.

There is a gate at the top of that lane where the made road stops being made and comes out onto a rise, and about four hundred yards below it a lane comes up off the low ground and joins it, and there are nine houses between that junction and the rise and about nine of them are held by the households at the two ends of the mile and a half.

The woman of about fifty-nine keeps the second of those nine from the top. She had given a bay gelding of about nine hands to four men in a livery on the Monday of the twenty-second of Rainmonth because a man had asked her for it and had said that it was very good of her before she had got the word out, and she had said afterwards, and to about nine people, that she would have said no and did not get as far as it, and that she had kept that lane for about thirty-four years and had never in her life been asked for anything by anybody, and that she did not know what to do with a person who asks.

That last sentence is on the page in a market street four miles away and it is the reason of everything that happened on the Friday of the sixteenth of Embermonth.

About nine households on that mile and a half can be found in about nine minutes if you know which nine, and the eight below the woman of about fifty-nine are a house at the junction that keeps a smith, a house above it that has been empty since Thawmonth, five houses along the lane, and a house at the bottom where the lane meets the made road and where a boy of about eleven has kept a stick about the length of his own arm for about two years and has never shown it to anybody.

Five of the eight were at their gates or their doors that Friday and nine people on that lane could see the woman of about fifty-nine go up and down it twice, and four of the five at their doors said nothing to each other about it and one did.

The one who said something was a woman of about thirty-one who keeps the smith's house and who has a want of her own that has nothing to do with anybody, which is that the smith has been asked twice in a month for a fire iron and has gone to the made road both times and has come back with the fire iron and has not been asked for anything else, and that she would like to know what a person is for.

“**You have been up and down this lane twice,**” the woman of about thirty-one said, from her own door, in the rain.

“**I have,**” the woman of about fifty-nine said.

“**Then I will tell you what I would tell you if you had not?**” the woman of about thirty-one said, “**and I am saying it because you have been up and down twice and if you go up a third time you will have come here to be watched, and everybody on this lane will have worked that out before you got to the top.**”

Nobody thanked her and the woman of about fifty-nine said that she was not going to go up a third time and that she was going now, and four people on that lane heard a woman turn down a thing she had decided and four did not.

Her Friday had been a Friday. She got up at the fourth hour and fed a horse that is not a horse for the market town's four hundred people and mucked out behind a wall, and she put nine pounds of lime into a hole at the top of the lane where the water gets under the made road and stands in it for about four days at a time in a wet month, which is a thing she has done every Friday since about Harvestmonth and which nobody in nine counties knows about and which is not in any series and which nobody has thanked her for.

Then she had stood at her own gate with a bar in her hand and looked down the mile and a half as long as it takes a carter to get a load round and had not gone down it.

“**I am fifty-nine,**” she said, out loud, at about the fourth hour, to the gate. “**And I have been on this lane thirty-four years and I have never in my life been asked for anything by anybody and I have been asked for a horse and I have given it and I did not get as far as saying no, and that is the only thing that has ever happened to me on this lane that was done to me instead of by me.**”

Nobody on that lane heard that and four people four hundred yards away heard a woman talking to a gate and four did not, and the woman of about thirty-one at the smith's house has said since that she heard every word of it and has not told anybody what she heard and will not be asked.

She had come up from the second of those nine gates at about the fourth hour on the first of Wolfmonth and had gone down the lane to the top of the rise and had stood at her own gate in the rain and had done nothing at all for about an hour, and about four of the eight households on that lane had seen her do it and about four had not and nobody had asked her.

Then she said one thing out loud at about the fifth hour, to nobody, standing at her own gate in the rain, and four people in four houses on that lane heard it.

“**Then somebody has to go and ask one of them whether they will say no to a man with a book if one comes, and I am the only person on this lane who has been asked for anything by anybody in about thirty-four years, and I am about fifty-nine, and I have not got nine hundred words and I have not got a book, and what I have got is that I know what it is to be asked and not get as far as saying anything.**”

Nobody said anything to that and four people in four houses on that lane heard a woman say what she was going to do and four did not, and nobody thanked her because nobody was in the lane.

She went down the lane at about the sixth hour in the rain to the top house, which is held by a household of about six people who have been on that lane nine years and who have never in that time been asked anything by anybody in nine counties, and about nine households on that mile and a half can say that about themselves and about four of them can say that they have said it out loud.

She did not knock for about as long as it takes a carter to get a load round, and about four people inside that house heard a woman on the step and about four did not, and the woman of about fifty-nine said out loud that she was about fifty-nine and that she kept the second gate from the top and that she had not come about a horse and that there was not going to be anything else said on the step.

Nobody on that lane thanked her for saying that and four of the eight households heard every word of it through four inches of rain on a mile and a half of lane that stops being made at the top.

Then she knocked.

**And the cost of that first of a month is nine hundred words standing on a table under a cloth in a room over a saddler's with nobody's name on them and nobody's name taken off them, and a woman of about fifty-nine walking down a mile and a half of lane in the rain to ask one person one question that she has no standing to ask, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a cake of size at 9, a hand brush at 25, a linen sheet at 28, a paddle at 31, a chain for a gate at 32, a foot scraper at 35, a coil of hemp at 36, a footstool at 37, a staple and hasp at 39, and a boat's fender at 40.** Nine, twenty-five, twenty-eight, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-five, thirty-six, thirty-seven, thirty-nine and forty is three hundred and twelve pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and three hundred and twelve pence is six marks and twenty-four over.** The door opened about a hand's breadth and stopped, and a person inside asked who it was., and a woman of about fifty-nine gave her name and said that she kept the second gate from the top and that she had come to ask one question and would not come in, and the door stayed where it was, and it is not in this chapter what the person inside the door said, and about nine people on that mile and a half have said since that the woman of about fifty-nine was still standing at the top house in the rain at about the ninth hour with her hand on the latch and had not gone home.
