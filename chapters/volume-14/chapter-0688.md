# Chapter 0688

**Frostmonth the eleventh, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street and then the third hour at the door on a lane of the room over the weigh-house, which is shut behind its own door on every day that is not a market morning and is not shut today, nine people in it, a press standing open with no date, a drawer about eleven inches deep, ten questions in a register and no finding under any of them, and a woman of about fifty-one who keeps a lodging house saying what a book is for.**

The room was open because it was a market morning, and nine people had come in about a door on a lane between the third hour and the fifth hour, and every one of them had come about a return.

That is the whole of what the room is for on a market morning. A return is a piece of paper that a district asks a room for and gets, and four hundred of them come into this room in a good month, and nine people came in about a door on a lane on the morning of the eleventh of Frostmonth and five of them came more than once a week and four did not.

Nobody thanked the copyist of about twenty-six for any of it and nobody has ever thanked her for any of it and four people in nine counties have said that a room that is not thanked is a room that cannot be got at, and five have said that it is a room, and neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

The room is about eleven paces by eight and it has one window, which is on the lane side and which has not been opened since the middle of Longlight because the copyist says the draught comes off the lane and puts about nine leaves out of order, and she has been saying that for four and a half years and nobody in nine counties has ever opened it or argued with her about it. There is a counter along one wall that is about four feet high and has been scrubbed so often in nine years that the grain of it has gone. There is a chair for a person who comes about a return and there is no second chair, and four people a week stand, and five sit, and the copyist has never once offered the second chair and has never once had a person refuse it.

Underneath the floor the weigh-house works, and on a market morning the beam under the boards goes down about twice an hour when a wain comes in over the scale, and nine people in that room have noticed that they can hear the whole town through the floor and five have said that it is the only reason they trust the room.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in nine counties have said that a room people trust because they can hear a wain through the floor is doing the same work as a room with a head over it, and five have said it is a floor.

What the people in that room had come about that morning was four returns and one question. One return had a man entered in it twice in two districts and neither of the two entries was wrong. One return had gone to a district that has not existed since the middle of the summer. One was a parish roll that had come back with a page torn out of the middle of it and no hole where the page had been, which is not a thing anybody in nine counties can explain. And one was a sheet that had gone out in the ordinary way and had come back in the ordinary way and the woman who brought it wanted to know why it had taken nine days, and the copyist said that it had taken nine days because it had gone by cart, and the woman said that it had not gone by cart, and the copyist said that it had, and four people in that room heard a keeper of records be beaten on a fact she is right about and five did not.

Nobody thanked either of them and the woman took her return and did not ask again, which she has not done in about four years of coming about returns.

There is a press in that room that stands open. It has stood open for four and a half years and nobody has ever dated it, and four people have said the date is the first of Longlight and five have said the date is the day the room was taken, and the copyist has refused both in the same four words on about nine occasions, and the four words are that it is not dated.

Nobody thanked her and nobody argued with her and nine people in that room had heard those four words and five were about to hear them.

There is a register on the table under the window. It has ten questions in it and not one finding under any of the ten, and there has not been a finding under anything in that book since the middle of Harvestmonth. Ruled over the place where a finding goes is a fourth head, and nothing has been written under it for about ten months, and the copyist said in a shut room on a Sunday in Wolfmonth that she was not going to rule a fifth head and that neither was anybody else.

Nobody thanked her and four people in that room had heard it said on a Sunday and five were hearing it for the first time.

There is a day-book on the table beside the register with eleven entries in it, and a drawer about eleven inches deep under the counter which is shut, and which has about nine leaves in it that have no head over them, and which is not counted, and which nobody has ever counted, and the copyist said out loud on the Sunday in Wolfmonth that she had not counted it since Harvestmonth and was not going to count it in Frostmonth.

Nobody thanked her and nobody in that room took a paper out of that drawer to count what was in it, and four people in nine counties know that it is not counted and five think that it is counted in the winter.

Nobody thanked her and nine people in that room had watched a woman of fifty-one say that she was not a clerk and had never been in a room like that one in her life, and four of them had understood that the sentence was the reason she was going to be believed and five had not.

“**Then what is the drawer for?**”

Nobody thanked her and the copyist of about twenty-six said no, and she said it flatly, and four people in that room had expected her to know and five had not.

“**I do not know. There are about nine leaves in it and they have no head over them and I put them in there myself over about four years and I have not counted them since Harvestmonth and I am not going to count them this month. If somebody in about four years opens that drawer and counts them there will be a number against them and no head over the number, and that is the same position as the fourth head in the register, and I have not got an argument against it because I have not got an argument for it either.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that room had heard a keeper of records say that she does not know what a drawer is for and five had heard a woman describe a hole in her own work without trying to fill it.

“**That is the first time I have said that out loud,**” she said, “**and I am saying it because a woman of about fifty-one has just said the truest thing anybody has said in this room and I would like about nine people to know that the drawer is not better than the rest of it.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that room had heard a woman of twenty-six say that the best thing anybody had said in four and a half years was true and that her own work was the same as the rest of it, and five had heard a keeper of records be honest on a market morning.

Then the copyist was asked, in that room, by nine people over two hours, what the room is for, and she answered it, and she has answered it about nine times in four and a half years and she said more this time than she has said any other of the nine.

“**A room like this can hold a decision somebody can be brought to,**” she said. “**It can refuse a thing and keep the refusal for four and a half years, and nine people in nine counties know that a thing roads write to is a head at the top of a thing, and I have said that out loud in four markets and in a lane and to a man with a stick and I have never once been argued out of it. It can count. It can put a day against a thing. And that is the whole of what it can do and I have been doing it since the middle of Harvestmonth and before that for about four years.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people in that room had heard a keeper of records list a room's powers and about five had heard a woman describe a parish office.

“**And here is what it cannot do and I am going to say all of it out loud in one go because about four of you have come in here this month to ask me to do one of these four things and I have said no four times and nobody has asked me why and I am telling you why in a room with the door open.**”

Nobody said anything and about nine people in that room put down whatever they were holding.

“**It cannot name a road. It cannot call a meeting, because a room that calls a meeting is a head and I have said that in four markets. It cannot hold nine hundred words that are not about anybody, and two people have asked me to hold them and I have said no twice and I was right twice. And it cannot be a person at the end of a road, which is the one that matters, and I have known that since the middle of Longlight and I have not said it out loud in a room before today.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people in that room had heard a woman of twenty-six say the fourth of the four and about five had heard a room say what it is not.

Then the woman of about fifty-one who keeps a lodging house four streets up the market street said the other half, and she had come in at about the third hour with a return that was not hers, and about nine people in that room heard her and about four of them understood why she had come in and about five thought she had come in about her own paper.

“**I have had about nine men of a forward party under my roof since the middle of Fallowmonth and two of them are there tonight,**” she said. “**They have not asked me for anything. They pay on a Saturday and they are quiet and one of them has been ill for nine days and has not asked me for anything either. So I will tell you what a book is for, and I am not a clerk and I have never been in a room like this one in my life.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people in that room had heard a woman say she was not a clerk before she said the thing, and about five had not.

“**A book is for the person who is in it and not for the person who keeps it. Every one of the four books I have read in this county in about four years is a book about about nine people, and not one of the four of them knew it was being written, and I have fed about four of them while they were in it and I did not know which of them were in it until I read the paper.**”

Nobody thanked her and about two hundred people had heard a thing in that room that had not been said in that room before, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that it is the truest thing anybody has said about a book in this county in about four years and about five have said that it is a lodging-keeper's complaint and not a finding.

“**And here is the whole of what your three series are for,**” she said, “**they are for the person who is in them. Four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, nine hundred sheets a month, a band on a fixed day. That is a county being able to say out loud that a person exists and can be produced. And that is worth nine counties four and a half years of work and nobody in this room should be ashamed of it, and I have watched a room do that for four years from four streets away.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four people in that room had heard a woman of fifty-one say that a parish office is worth four and a half years and about five had heard a lodging-house keeper say something kind about a book, and the copyist of about twenty-six said no word at all for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, which four people in that room noticed and about five did not.

Then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room said her own thing, and she said it standing up, and it is the third time she has said it in four and a half years and it is the same three sentences each time.

“**I keep the room and not the book. I have said since the middle of Harvestmonth that the fourth head ought not to be there, and I still say it, and I am right about it, and it is not going to be unruled, and I am not going to stop saying it.**”

Nobody thanked her and the copyist said no in about four seconds and gave the reason in four words and was not argued with.

“**It is not mine to rule.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in that room had heard the same twelve words for the third time in three months and about five had heard them and understood that the woman who keeps the room had asked to be thanked for asking and had not been.

Then a man of about thirty-four with a satchel came in about the fourth hour and asked to see the register, and the copyist gave him the register, which nobody does, and about four people in that room noticed that she gave it to him and about five did not.

He read the ten questions. He did not read the empty head. He put the register down flat on the table and squared it against the edge the way she does, which he has never done in his life, and about four people in that room saw a man of thirty-four do a thing he has watched a woman do four hundred times.

“**I expected a finding under the fourth,**” he said, “**and there is a head ruled for one and it has been empty for ten months, and that is the best thing in this room, and I am not going to say why, because if I say why then the next person through that door works it out and writes it down, and then the empty head is a thing somebody chose.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that room had heard a man of thirty-four refuse to say the reason for the best thing in a room and about five had heard a man protect a silence he had no standing in.

Then a man of about thirty-eight who puts boards on the inside of gates came in about the fifth hour with nine boards in a handbasket and asked whether he could leave them in that room over the winter, and the copyist said no in about four seconds and gave the reason and was not argued with.

“**A board on a gate is a notice and a notice in a room is a record of a notice, and a record of a notice in a county with no head in it is a thing that can be read by anybody who comes through that door in about four years, and about nine of them come through that door in a month and not one of them is the person the board was for.**”

Nobody thanked her and the man of about thirty-eight said out loud that this was the fourth thing he had been refused in about four years and that he had come anyway each time and that the door was worth about four miles of walking, and four people in that room heard a man say that a refusal was worth the walk and about five heard a man value a door he had not been thanked for.

Nobody thanked him and he took his nine boards back up the lane and the copyist shut the door at about the fifth hour and did not bolt it, because it is a market morning and about nine people come in about a door on a lane and about four of them come more than once a week.

Nobody thanked her and Aren Kest was in the lane outside that door at about the fourth hour with his stick in his right hand and the woman of about twenty-four who keeps a copy-book was on the step beside him, and neither of them said anything to the other, and about four people going in and out of that door saw two people standing on a step not saying anything and about five did not.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a room that can hold a decision and cannot be a person at the end of a road, a lodging house that has fed about four people it did not know were in a book, and nine boards taken back up a lane, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a quire of paper at 12, a press handle at 28, a rule of boxwood at 29, a cake of ink at 31, a day-book leaf at 32, a shelf board at 34, a stamp pad at 35, a pound of twine at 36, a binding board at 37, and a lock for a drawer at 39.** Twelve, twenty-eight, twenty-nine, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six, thirty-seven and thirty-nine is three hundred and thirteen pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and three hundred and thirteen pence is six marks and twenty-five over.** A woman of about sixty came in about a door on that lane at about the sixth hour with a return that had come back wrong and wanted to know what to do about it, and the copyist looked at it and said that it had come back wrong twice, once in the middle of the summer and once on the first of Frostmonth, and that neither of the two was entered in anything, and that she was not going to enter the second one either, and then she handed the paper back across the counter and said out loud that that was what the room was for, and about four people in that lane heard a keeper of records say a true thing in a market morning and about five did not.
