# Chapter 0679

**Embermonth the seventeenth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, one household's money counted at the boards for four roads that have to be walked to, and then the fourth hour at a cut on a mile and a half of lane where nine people turn out for work nobody ordered.**

Four of the seven had been asked. That had been done by a man of about thirty-four with a satchel on his own account and on his own time, and he had gone out on the Monday and come back on the Thursday, and nine people in that market street had worked out in nine seconds that nobody had sent him and about five had not thought about it.

He had asked four roads the sixth clause and the fourth of the seven had written back and two had not.

Four hundred people in that market street on a Saturday morning do not know what the sixth clause is, and two hundred of them had stopped what they were doing because a woman of about thirty-three who keeps a chandler's shop at the low end of the boards was counting money at the high end and counting it out loud.

Nine households were being counted. That is the whole of it and it took twenty minutes and the money was for four roads walked to and not for anything else.

The items were a sheet of foolscap and a ream of it, a cake of ink, a quill pen, a bundle of tapes, about four pounds of twine, a leather strap, a tin lamp and a candle, and about nine days of a man's legs, and the nine days of the man's legs was the whole of the cost and the paper was not.

“**I will do it at four pence a day and I will not have it written that I did it for nothing,**” the man of about thirty-four said, at about the third hour, in a market street, to about nine people.

Nobody thanked him and four people in that street understood that a man had priced himself in front of four hundred people and about five had heard a figure.

Then the count came out in the open, house by house, and it was counted by the chandler of about thirty-three because she counts for a living and nobody else in that street would do it out loud.

The house at the top of the market street gave eighteenpence. The two houses either side of the inn gave eleven each. The house that keeps the weigh-house key gave seven. The four that keep two yards at the low end gave four each. The house of a woman of about sixty who has been at the boards four times in a year gave nine.

Then the man of about thirty-four with the satchel said his own house gave nothing, and he said it before anybody had read it out, and nine people in that market street heard a man say a figure of nothing.

“**I have got four horses that a crew asked for twice in about a month and I paid for a barrow both times and I paid the man who brought it and not the man who owns it, and that is the way it is done and I am not going to pretend it is not a cost, and I am not going to argue about it and neither is anybody else at these boards.**”

Nobody argued with him and four people at the boards said nothing and about five said something, and nine people watched a man of about thirty-four put nothing into a thing he had walked four roads for, and nobody thanked him for saying so and nobody thanked him for the four days.

There was an argument about the yard of the man of about thirty-eight, which kept four horses two streets down and which the chandler had not counted because nobody had put anything down for it.

“**That yard can afford ninepence,**” a man of about forty-four said, who sells nails at the high end of the boards and who had been standing there for about twenty minutes.

Nobody argued with the man of about thirty-eight about the horses and four people at the boards agreed with the man of about forty-four and five did not.

“**It can afford ninepence,**” the man of about forty-four said. “**It has had four horses asked for twice in about a month and it paid a man for a barrow both times and it went out on the road it does not keep. That yard has been in nine counties' arguments since the middle of Rainmonth and it has not been in one penny.**”

Nobody thanked him and the man of about thirty-four said one sentence about it in the ordinary way and nine people at the boards heard it.

“**It has taken four horses and not one of them was taken from me, and if you want to count the county that is a good thing to do and you are not going to get a penny out of me this morning and I would rather say so than have nine people work out that I am hard up.**”

Nobody argued with him after that and four people at the boards said nothing and about five said something at once and then stopped, and the man of about forty-four said that he had only meant to be fair and was not thanked for it and was not thanked for saying anything at all this morning.

Then the chandler of about thirty-three counted it out loud and it came to seventy pence, and she wrote it on the back of a sheet and not in anything else, and said out loud that it was the back of a sheet because there was nowhere else for it and that this was the second time in about a year that a thing had been counted in that street and put on the back of a piece of paper.

Then the badge-man said the true thing about what seventy pence was, and he said it in nine words, and about four people at the boards understood all of it.

“**That is one man and half a man. That is four roads and two gates.**”

Nobody thanked him and the man of about thirty-four said that the two roads that had written had both asked for it to come back with an answer by the fourteenth of Rainmonth and that he was not going to be the man who told a road there was no answer, and that he had nine days to find one.

Then he said the other thing, and it is the second cost of this volume said out loud in a different mouth from the mouth it was said in before, and nine people at the boards heard it and four understood it.

“**The copyist keeps three series and they are correct and they have been correct for about four and a half years and I have said in four streets that they are correct.**” He had the sheet out of the satchel and he did not look at it. “**About nine hundred sheets a month going out and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month coming in and a band on a fixed day. I have watched her refuse a letter into a drawer and I agreed with her about it in a lane, and I have said since that she is right, and she is right.**”

“**Then say the other half,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said, from the boards, in the flat way.

“**Two of the four roads I asked have written back and two have sent nothing,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and neither of those two things is a return. A return is a thing a district asks for and gets. A road that has written to us has written to us because four people went and stood in front of it and would not go away, and nobody has asked the fruit valley for anything since the Sundering and it has answered inside nine days, and there is no series in nine counties into which the fact that it answered goes. It is not a roll. It is not a sheet going out to a district. It is a thing about four that four of the nine of us know and about five do not.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard the whole of that and about five had heard a man explain that the county's records could not hold the only thing that had happened.

“**And I have been thinking about it for four days on the road and I have got one thing to say about it and it is not a complaint,**” he said, “**and it is that the three series in this county are the best thing anybody in nine counties has built and there is not one line in them about a road that has said yes or a road that has sent nothing, and that is because a series is for counting people and a road is not people, and the county has been counting people for four and a half years and the roads are the other half and they are not in any of it.**”

Nobody thanked him and the copyist said out loud, in a market street, in the flat way, that a road that has decided something is not a return and is not a roll and is not a sheet going out to a district, and that she had said that to a man with a stick in a room over a saddler's a week before and had said it in four markets before that, and that a man of about thirty-four had just said it better than she had and that she wanted that noticed.

Nobody thanked her for saying it.

Then somebody said the other thing, out loud, in that market street, and it was the difference that matters and nine people heard it and four understood why it had been said.

“**Then the two that sent nothing are not the two he is standing on?**”

That came from a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a horse at the top of the market street and who has not said anything at the boards for about a year, and nine people in that market street looked at her.

“**He has got a chain on the ford four miles down the lane and about four men on the coast road and he has had them on both since the eighth of this month,**” she said, “**and the waterworks country and the glass country have sent nothing and he has not set foot in either of them and there is not a chain in either of them and nobody has asked anybody in either of them to stop anything.**”

“**Then what has happened there,**” a man of about thirty-three said.

“**Nothing,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**And I want nine people to notice the shape of that and I am not going to explain the shape of it because I am a woman who keeps a horse and I have not got nine hundred words about it. Nothing has happened in two counties where nothing was asked of anybody, and something has happened in nine counties where about nine people have been asked things all year.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard all of that and about five heard a woman say that nothing had happened.

Then the market went on and two hundred people went back to what they had been doing and nine people at the boards stayed.

What happened at about the fourth hour was ordinary and it is the ordinary half of this month and it went on for five hours.

The water off four miles of made road comes over the crown at a gate a mile and a half up a lane and goes across the lane into the ditch, and it has been coming over the crown since the middle of Rainmonth, and the barrel drain under the lane where the water is taken off the made road had been choked in Fallowmonth with about four inches of a lime crust gone hard and nine people had got it clear on the seventh of Harvestmonth and the frost had lifted two hours after it had begun.

It had been coming over the crown again since the eighth of Embermonth.

Nine people turned out at about the fourth hour and nobody ordered it and it was not a contract and it was not the market town's business and nobody thanked anybody. There was a man of about fifty-one who owns the field on the low side of the cut and who was at the top of it with a board and a bar because he has been there every Friday since the middle of Rainmonth, and there was a man of about fifty who lays stone at the market town's own gate and who had not been asked and came because there was a boy of about seventeen on the bucket end.

There was a woman of about thirty-eight with a bar, a boy of about thirteen, and a boy of about seventeen on the bucket end, and nine feet of a thing that was not a ford and not a crossing and was a length of approach, and about nine hundred yards up the made road a yard was loading timber off a waggon and none of the nine men in it knew that anybody was in a cut.

The work was the barrel. It is a barrel of lime-washed brick about four feet long under a stone lintel, and the water that goes through it is the water off about four miles of made road, and when it is choked it does not run, and when it does not run it comes over the top of the cut at the low side, and that is where nine feet of channel nine inches deep and nine feet long has been cut in the lane.

There were about three inches of it, and about two inches of that was the lime crust gone hard in a night, and it had to come off in pieces, and the water was about a foot in the channel and came over the crown at about every fourth or fifth pull of the chain.

A chain on a bar went through the mouth of the cut and nine people took it in turns, and nobody arranged the turns and the turns arranged themselves in about nine minutes the way they do. Four of them could not lift it at the second pull and five could, and the man of about fifty said out loud that that was the correct proportion and that anybody who could lift it twice running was doing it wrong.

The barrow went up the bank nine paces and tipped, and the boy of about seventeen had been on the bucket end since the fourth hour and he counted the trips out loud because counting is what a boy does when he has nothing else to do with a morning, and he got to a hundred and forty and said it out loud, and the man of about fifty said that a hundred and forty was a good afternoon and that he had done ninety in his twentieth year and had been proud of it.

Nobody thanked the boy of about seventeen.

At about the sixth hour the man of about fifty-one who owns the field on the low side came down off the bank and asked the woman of about thirty-eight with the bar a question she had been waiting nine weeks to be asked.

“**Is anybody going to come and do this in Fallowmonth.**”

“**No,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said.

“**Then I have been coming here on Fridays for nothing since the middle of Rainmonth and I would like somebody to know that, and I would like it said in a market street and not in a lane, because a lane is where the two of us are and I want nine people who do not keep this road to know that a man of fifty-one has been standing in nine inches of water on Fridays for nine weeks.**”

Nobody thanked him and the woman of about thirty-eight said that he would not be thanked for it and would not be argued with, and that both of those were the arrangement, and four people at the cut understood why and five thought she had been unkind.

“**I have said it to be unkind,**” she said. “**A thing nobody has been thanked for and nobody has been argued with is a thing that cannot be given up, and a thing that cannot be given up is the only kind of road work there is.**”

Then the chain came out with the last of it at about the seventh hour and nine people at that cut worked out what they had done without anybody saying so.

**And the cost of that Saturday is seventy pence on the back of a sheet and four roads walked by a man on his own time and two of them that sent nothing and four households at the boards counting what they could and one man counting out that he had nothing, and about four inches of silt in a barrel drain on a lane that nobody ordered, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a bundle of tapes at 18, a seal box at 24, a quill pen at 25, a ream of foolscap at 30, a leather strap at 31, a tallow candle at 32, a tin lamp at 34, a string of chalk at 35, a frosted pane at 37, and a parcel of twine at 40.** Eighteen, twenty-four, twenty-five, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-seven and forty is three hundred and six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and three hundred and six pence is six marks and eighteen over.** The barrel came clear at about the seventh hour. The water found it inside a minute and a half of the last of the silt coming out and went off down the ditch, and by the time the boy of about seventeen had done his hundredth trip the channel at the low side was dry for the first time since the middle of Rainmonth. Then the man of about fifty-one went out into the field on the low side with a lamp and came back after about nine minutes and said that there was a new watercourse about four feet long in a corner of that field and that there had not been one in it in nine years and that he was not going to say a word about it to anybody who had not come and stood in it.
