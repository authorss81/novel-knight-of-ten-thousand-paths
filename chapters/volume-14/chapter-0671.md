# Chapter 0671

**Fallowmonth the nineteenth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with four hundred people in the street and about two hundred of them stopped, and a woman of about thirty-eight off the coast of returning roads deciding in the open to ask a city four hundred miles off a question nobody in nine counties has standing to ask.**

The room over the weigh-house was open behind its own door and had been since the second hour, and there were four hundred people in that market street because it was a Saturday, and about two hundred of them stopped what they were doing when a woman of about thirty-eight came to the boards and said she had come up the made road on the Wednesday with a carter and had slept one night in a barn at the low end of the market street and had not been to any of the four houses she had passed.

Nobody had sent for her. That was the first thing she said and she said it before anybody asked her anything, in the way of a person who has had about nine days to decide which order to say things in.

The hearing was in the street in the sense that the hearing is a room and two clerks and a man with a badge and about nine people who come, and on the nineteenth of a month it is a man with a bar and a street and about nine people, and about two hundred others who have stopped.

“**Say what you have come about,**” the badge-man said, and he said it in about nine words and then stood back about nine paces, which he does about four times a year.

“**I am going to say a thing about four people,**” she said, “**and then I am going to say something else, and I want the second thing said before anybody answers the first one, because the first one is only about who can get there and the second one is the whole of why I have come up four days of made road with a carter I paid out of my own money.**”

About four hundred people in that market street had heard that and about two hundred of them had stopped, and nine people at the boards understood what she had just done and about five understood that she had done it.

Then she named them, and she named them by age and by trade and she did it in the order the street already knew them, and about two hundred people in that street knew three of the four.

“**A man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head. A man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat. A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned. And me, and my name is Salla Dunnock, and I am thirty-eight, and I keep the tally at a post on the coast of returning roads and I am not the woman who holds the post.**”

Nobody said anything to that and four people at the boards had heard her give the whole of her own standing in nine seconds and about five had heard a woman introduce herself.

“**I came eleven days on a cart in Greenmonth,**” she said, “**with a woman of about sixty and a boy of about nine, and I asked in this street whether we were to be an army, and four hundred people heard me ask it, and I went home on the twenty-fifth of Longlight and I have not been answered it, and it is the thirty-first day since that morning, and I am telling you the number because I have noticed that nobody in this county counts out loud unless it is about money.**”

Then the badge-man put her the question he had put to about four people in a month, and he put it in the street and not in a room, and about two hundred people heard it.

“**Then what do you want?**”

She said what she wanted in about nine seconds and she said it as a person who has wanted a thing since the twenty-fifth of Longlight and has had about nine days of a barn and a cart to say it properly in.

“**I want the seven roads asked a question. One question. The question a city four hundred miles off has already said in advance it will answer, and will not refuse to answer, and nobody asked it and it did not ask. I want one of the four of us to go up there and say it out loud to a person who keeps a crossing, and I want it said out loud and not written, because a thing said in a room gets taken out of a room.**”

About two hundred people in that street had stopped what they were doing and nine at the boards had understood the whole of it and about five had understood that somebody was going to go somewhere.

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes a carter to get a load round.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat took his hand out of his coat and then put it back, which four people noticed and about five did not, and he said the thing that is the answer everybody in nine counties had already given without being asked.

“**No.**”

“**You have not been asked,**” the badge-man said.

“**I have said no three times this year and I am not going to be asked a fourth,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**I said it in Thawmonth and I said it in Longlight and I said it in Fallowmonth, and every one of the three was to a different person and every one of the three was in a street, and I have said no to four pence a day and a basket and I am not going to be asked now to do nine days of made road on my own account.**”

“**I did not ask you,**” the badge-man said.

“**No, and that is the problem and I have been standing at this end of it for about a month,**” the man of about thirty-four said, and four people at the boards looked at the ground and about five looked at him.

Then the woman of about thirty-six said it, and she said it flatly and without any weight on it, which is how she says everything.

“**No, and I will give the reason in nine words and then I will not give it again this year. The last time I was asked to go round with anything it was a list, and it was four pence a day, and I said no three times, and in the county I keep a road in they had a list for about a year before the burning and the list took nine men going round with it and nobody burned anything at all.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards had heard the reason and about five had heard a woman say no.

That left two, and four hundred people in a market street on a Saturday morning do not know what to do with a number, and the badge-man said the number out loud in nine words because he had been doing that for thirty-one years and it was the only thing he had ever been any good at.

“**Two. There are two of the four left, and I am one of them, and I have had a month of not asking anybody anything and about nine days since that ended.**”

About four people in that market street understood what he had just done and about five understood that a man with a badge had said a number.

Then he said the other half of it, and he said it to nine people and not to two hundred, and it was the reason nobody in that street could give a name to.

“**And I am not going. I am going to tell you why in nine words and then I am not going to say it again. I have said nine times that there is no head in nine counties with which to call a meeting, and I am not going to make one, and a thing that sends a person is a head, and if I send one of you up four hundred miles to ask a question then I have made one out of a pair of boots and a woman, and nine people in this county will work that out inside a week and will never ask me anything again.**”

Nobody said anything to that and four people at the boards looked at the boards and about five looked up the lane.

“**And that is not a reason for not asking the question,**” he said. “**That is a reason for not being the one who asks it, and there is a difference between the two, and nine of you have just heard me make it and about five have not, and the five of you are going to say in about four months that I stopped a thing that should have gone on.**”

That was the sentence that made nine people in that street understand the shape of it, and it was said at about the third hour with the rain coming on, and four hundred people had been in that street and about two hundred had stopped and nine were still at the boards.

Then the badge-man asked her one question, and it was the only question he asked that morning, and nine people at the boards had been waiting about a month for somebody to ask it.

“**Say the question. Word for word. Say it as you would say it at a crossing.**”

She said it, and she said it slowly, and it came to nine words.

“**Will you tell us which place or road the sheet you sent us is meant.**”

Nobody said anything to that and four people at the boards repeated it out loud under their breath and about five of them did not, and the badge-man watched four of them do it and did not stop them.

“**Say it again,**” the woman of about thirty-six said, from the high end of the boards.

The woman of about thirty-eight said it again in the same words and nine people at the boards had it by the second time and four hundred people in the street had heard a sentence of nine words that they could have said themselves.

“**That is what I am going to say,**” she said. “**And here is what I want the nine of you to notice. It is a question and not an answer. There is no name in it and no road in it and no county in it, and it does not say we have agreed to anything and it does not say we have been asked anything before. It is the first thing anybody in nine counties has been able to put in a sentence since the hearing started, and four of you noticed that and about five of you have not and I am not going to wait for the five.**”

“**You have worked out how to say a thing that asks for nothing,**” the badge-man said.

“**I have worked out how to say a thing that a man in a livery cannot write down as consent,**” she said, “**because a man in a livery writes down what was said and not what was meant, and nine men in nine counties have been taught that about nine times, and it is the one thing in nine counties that is working.**”

About two hundred people in that market street had stopped what they were doing and nine of them at the boards understood that a woman of about thirty-eight had worked out in nine days a thing that a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and thirty-one years of standing in streets had not got.

Then the chandler of about thirty-three said the last of it, out loud, and nine people heard it.

“**Then somebody in this county will have to be the one who asks it, and it is going to be somebody who has not been asked, and nine people in this street came here ready to say no. Is that the whole of what this morning was for?**”

Then somebody else said the thing that had been underneath all of it since the fifth of Fallowmonth, and it was said by a woman of about thirty-three who keeps a chandler's shop at the low end of the boards and who had said nothing for about half an hour.

“**Then this thing cannot be asked. That is all of it. You cannot ask it, because asking it means somebody has to be sent, and sending is making a head, and the man with the badge will not make one and nine people in this county have spent a year making sure that nobody ever makes one. So the thing cannot be asked and there is nobody to be disappointed about it.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards looked at the ground.

Then a man of about twenty-nine who keeps the inn two doors up said the other half of it, out loud, in the ordinary way, and nine people heard all of it.

“**That is the first time anybody has said the hearing is the wrong size for a thing.**”

Nobody argued with him and four people at the boards looked up and about five did not, and the badge-man said the true thing about it in nine words.

“**It is. It has never been the wrong size for anything until this morning, and I have stood at these boards for thirty-one years and asked this street to be told a thing once a month, and the whole of what it could do was put a sentence into nine mouths. A hearing with no bench in it cannot ask a question, because a question has to be put to somebody with standing to answer it, and there is nobody in nine counties with standing over a city four hundred miles off, and there is nobody in that city with standing over us, and both of those are true at once and nine of you can see it.**”

Then the woman of about thirty-eight said the last of it, and she said it to two hundred people in a market street on a Saturday morning, and it was the decision, and nobody in that street had agreed with it before she said it and nobody agreed with it afterwards.

“**Then I will go,**” she said.

Nobody said anything.

“**And I want nine people to notice what I have just said and what I have not said. I have not said we will send anybody, because nobody is sending anybody and there is nothing to send anybody with. I have not said I will be back in a month, because I do not know what I will be back with. I have said that a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a tally at a post on the coast of returning roads will get up on Monday morning and walk out of this market town up the made road with nine days of road in front of her, and that she will ask one question out loud to a person who keeps a crossing, and that nobody in nine counties will have sent her.**”

“**You have not been asked to do that,**” the badge-man said, and he said it in the voice he uses about four times a year.

“**No,**” she said, “**and that is the arrangement, and I would like the nine of you to notice that a man with a badge has said the true thing about it in a market street and has not tried to stop me, and I am not going to say that anybody was thanked for anything today, because nobody was.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that market street had understood that a decision had been made in the open and about five had understood that a woman was going to walk nine days.

Then the woman of about thirty-six said the thing she had come to that market to say, and she said it once and did not say it again, and nine people heard it.

“**She is going the wrong way. I want that said in this street by somebody who is not her and nine of you are going to remember it. There is a chain across a ford four miles down this lane and a man with a book who wrote four miles of road into it on the Sunday, and nine people in this county have given that man an answer in a lane in the rain, and every one of us is standing in a market this morning instead of standing there. She is going the wrong way and she is going because there is nobody else, and both of those are true and I am not going to pretend one of them cancels the other.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards looked down the lane and about five looked at the woman of about thirty-eight, and the woman of about thirty-eight looked at neither.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a decision that four people did not agree with and about two hundred people heard, and nine days of made road in front of a woman of about thirty-eight and a chain across a ford four miles down a lane that nobody walked down that morning are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a pot of tallow at 7, a hand bell at 11, a quire of blotting paper at 12, a bundle of waxed string at 19, a padlock at 24, a shoe last at 29, a gallon of oil at 31, a hank of yarn at 32, a hand spike at 34, and a shutter hinge at 40.** Seven, eleven, twelve, nineteen, twenty-four, twenty-nine, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four and forty is two hundred and thirty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and thirty-nine pence is four marks and forty-seven over.** The market went on at about the fourth hour and about two hundred people went back to what they had been doing and nine people at the boards stayed, and the woman of about thirty-eight went and stood at the low end of the boards for about as long as it takes a carter to get a load round and then went up the lane without looking back at the weigh-house, and nobody in nine counties said anything to her between the boards and the inn, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs with the register under her arm and shut it again at the door and went back up, which is a thing she has done about nine times in about four years.
