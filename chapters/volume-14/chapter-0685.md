# Chapter 0685

**Wolfmonth the twenty-seventh, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it, a woman of about forty-nine who keeps a kitchen and four beds on the made road come down off the made road to say in about nine minutes what two men in livery did at her door, and a boy of about fourteen who is not at the end of her long table.**

The boy went on the Wednesday.

That is the whole of it and it took about four minutes at her door and it was not a set piece and nobody was told anything and nobody was hurt, and nine people in that market street had worked out by the second hour on the Thursday that the woman of about forty-nine who keeps the kitchen at the fourth house on the made road had something to say, and five had not.

She came down off the made road on foot at about the second hour, which is four hundred yards of hard sand out of the market town, and she came down it with her sleeves rolled and there was a boy of about eleven holding her basket at the bottom of the rise who is not the boy of about fourteen and is her own.

Nobody thanked her for the walk and four people at the boards had seen her come up the market street and five had not.

She kept a kitchen and four beds on the made road and she has kept it about nine years, and the crews that have been working forward on that road since the middle of Rainmonth have eaten in it most nights since, and there are about nine men in a yard four hundred yards up the road and four of them have paid every night and five have paid in coin on a Saturday, and that is a kitchen with a trade in it for about a month.

Nobody thanked her for the trade and four people in nine counties have said that a kitchen that feeds a Crown crew is a kitchen that has been asked something, and five have said that a kitchen that feeds anybody is a kitchen, and neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Then she said it, and she said it in about nine minutes, and she said it once, and she did not raise her voice once, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the only time anybody's voice went up in that street this month was on a first of a month and it was not hers.

“**A man of about thirty-one in a livery came to my door on the Friday before last and asked me for a witness,**” the woman of about forty-nine said. “**He said the second clause wants somebody named by the people who work the road, and that a kitchen is not a road, and he asked me anyway, because he had a page for my door and a page wants an answer. And I said no, and he wrote down that I said no, and he read it back to me, and he said that it was very good of me. That is the first one.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at the boards had heard the whole of that and five had heard a woman describe a man being polite.

“**And he was right to ask,**” she said, before anybody could say anything. “**I want that said. He had a page and my door was on it and nobody in nine counties has ever given a man the right to ask a person at a gate which place or road a paper is meant, and he did not have it that day either, and he asked me in my own doorway with his hat in his hand and I have had nine days to work out that I would have said the same about a horse.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a woman defend a man who had taken something off her and five had heard a woman be fair.

“**Then on the Tuesday he came back,**” she said. “**And he did not ask me a question. He said he wanted a person who could read a number out loud, because there is a post at the quarry end with a figure on it and a figure that is read wrong is a figure a man gets hurt on, and I said the boy of about fourteen could not read, and he said he would find out. And he found out at about the fourth hour of the same afternoon, because the boy was in my yard and the boy read a figure off the corner of my own oven to me out loud when I asked him what it was, and the man of about thirty-one was standing in the yard at the time and heard it, and wrote it down, and read it back, and said it was very good of the boy.**”

Nobody said anything and two hundred people in that market street had stopped what they were doing, and nine of them understood the whole of it and five understood the man of about thirty-one and nothing else.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at the boards had heard a woman say that a man of about thirty-one had been right twice at one door in four days, and five had heard a woman say that the second one cost her a boy.

“**I did not say no to the second one,**” the woman of about forty-nine said. “**I want that said in a market street and I want it said by me and not worked out in a lane. I have had a fortnight of a hired boy running since the middle of Rainmonth and I have had nine years of a kitchen before that, and a boy of about fourteen costs fourpence a day and I pay him out of the trade, and the trade is nine men in a yard and four of them pay nightly. On the Wednesday morning the boy was gone and the yard had gone with him and I have two beds of trade left out of four and nine people a day in a kitchen built for about twenty.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street had heard the price of a boy in a kitchen put into a market street and five had heard a woman add up four and a half weeks of Rainmonth.

“**Nobody has been hurt and nobody has been killed and nobody has been told anything,**” she said, “**and the boy of about fourteen can read a figure and write his own name and his mother lives in a village two days off, and he is not in any danger and I am not going to stand in this street and make him into something. He is a boy of about fourteen that a man in a livery asked for at my door and I gave him.**”

Nobody thanked anybody and two hundred people in that market street had heard it and nine of them had understood that a kitchen in this county had just paid for a rule about roads with a person, and five had not.

The rest of that market street was doing what it does on a Thursday and nobody had stood still for more than about four minutes at a time. A load of lime had come in at the low end at the seventh hour and had not gone up the rise and the man who brought it was arguing with a man of about twenty-eight who has a yard four streets up, and the two of them had been arguing since before the market came in and were still arguing at noon. A woman of about thirty-three who keeps a chandler's shop had a bench out of her shop and no cloth over it and sells nothing that anybody can tell. A man of about thirty-three who sells cloth three doors up had said nothing at all that morning, which four people at the boards had noticed and five had not, and he had not said anything on any market morning since the first of Embermonth.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that market street had watched a load of lime go nowhere and five had not looked up.

The boy of about fourteen had come to that kitchen in the second week of Rainmonth out of a village two days off, and his mother had agreed to it for a fortnight, and the fortnight had been renewed twice without anybody putting anything in writing, and the wage had been fourpence a day and his bed was a board at the end of the kitchen under the slope of the roof where the slates come in about four inches lower than they were meant to.

Nobody thanked her and four people in nine counties have said that a bed under a roof at fourpence a day is the ordinary arrangement in this county and five have said that it is a bed under a roof at fourpence a day and that the two are not the same.

Then the woman of about thirty-one who keeps the smith's house at the junction asked a question out loud, and she asked it of the man of about thirty-four with the satchel and not of the woman of about forty-nine, and nine people at the boards heard it.

“**Then is it the second visit, or the second page?**”

Nobody thanked her and the man of about thirty-four said that he had been asking himself the same thing for two days and had got nowhere with it, and that a page for a door is a thing a man in a livery makes on his first day in a county and carries for the rest of the year and fills in whatever he gets, and that nobody in nine counties has ever asked whether a man is allowed to have a second page about a door that has already got one.

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard that said out loud and five had not, and the badge-man said nothing at all about it, which four people noticed.

Then the copyist of about twenty-six asked the question that the copyist asks, and she asked it once, in the flat way, at about the fourth hour of that Thursday, and nine people at the boards heard it.

“**Did he ask you twice for the same thing.**”

Nobody thanked her and the woman of about forty-nine said no in about four seconds, and it is the true answer and it is the whole of what happened.

“**That is the part I cannot get past,**” she said. “**He did not ask me twice for a thing. He asked me once for a witness, which I said no to, and then four days later he asked me once for a boy, which I said yes to, and both of those were the first time, and nine people in this street are going to hear the word twice used about that and I am telling you now that it is the wrong word and that I have not got a better one.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards had heard a keeper of records ask a question she knew the answer to and five had heard a woman say that the county's word for it is the wrong word.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at that market street had heard a woman of about forty-nine be fair to a man who had cost her a fortnight, and five had heard a woman be fair and not like it.

Then the man of about fifty-eight with a badge did a thing nobody at the boards expected, and nine people in that market street saw him do it and five did not, and what he did was write nothing down, because he has not carried a book in that street since Longlight, and instead he said the whole of the arithmetic out loud in the open, and he said it wrongly on purpose.

“**A man of about thirty-one came to a door on a Friday and on a Tuesday. That is two visits. The rule this county built has been in place since the middle of Harvestmonth and it has taken a horse and a barrow and not one man with a paper, and here is what two visits at a kitchen door have taken, and it is a boy of about fourteen and a fortnight at fourpence a day and four beds of trade out of four. I am not saying the two are the same. I am saying that nobody in nine counties has put them next to each other and I am putting them next to each other now, in a market street, on a Thursday, and nine people have heard it and five have not.**”

Nobody thanked him and two hundred people in that market street had heard a man of fifty-eight do arithmetic on a thing that is not a number, and four of them at the boards had heard the true thing and five had heard a man keep a book he does not carry.

Nobody said anything and the woman of about forty-nine said one thing about that and it was the only time her voice went up on that Thursday and it did not go up, and nine people heard it.

“**Put them next to each other again,**” she said, “**on a first of a month, in this street, where a man of about fifty-eight can be asked about it by anybody. A kitchen is not a hearing and a Thursday is not a first of a month, and I have been in nine hearings in this county and four of them changed nothing, and the one thing I have noticed about all nine of them is that the only people who ever say anything useful are the ones who came on their own account.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street had heard a woman of forty-nine say that a hearing is a bad instrument and that the only useful speakers in nine of nine hearings came on their own account, and five had heard a woman who keeps a kitchen make an argument about the county's arrangements that nobody at those boards had made.

Then the badge-man said the true thing about it, and he said it in nine words, and he did not add to it, and that is the whole of what he did about it.

“**Then the rule is not what is taking them. The asking is.**”

Nobody thanked him and two hundred people in that market street had stopped what they were doing, and four people at the boards had heard a man of fifty-eight say that the thing this county built is not the difficulty and five had heard a man say that being asked is.

Nobody said anything and the man of about thirty-four with a satchel said the other half of it, and he said it to about nine people and not to the four hundred, and he did not put a sheet out of the satchel.

“**About four people in nine counties have been asked things this year,**” he said, “**and I have said the number out loud in this street three times and I am going to say it a fourth time because a woman of about forty-nine has just done the arithmetic on a fortnight of it. Nobody in nine counties has been asked anything and got a second visit for it. About four of us have been asked twice and every one of the second visits was for a different thing, and every one of the second visits has been refused, and being refused has not cost anybody anything that anybody can put a price on.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at the boards had heard a man say that being refused was free and about five had heard a man who has watched a kitchen lose a boy say that being refused was free.

“**And a man of about thirty-one in a livery has been at a kitchen door twice in four days, on a page, for two different things, and he was polite about both,**” he said, “**and that is the whole of what I can say and I am not going to say the rest of it in a market street because about four of the people who would be glad to hear the rest of it are standing in it.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody said anything and about nine people in that market street understood the whole of that and about five did not, and the copyist of about twenty-six said out loud that she was not going to write any of it down and that about four people had heard that and about five had not.

Then Aren Kest said one thing at the low end of the boards, and he said it to the woman of about forty-nine and not to the market street, and she heard it and about nine people four feet away heard it and about five did not.

“**I have walked up that road nine times since the middle of Rainmonth,**” he said, “**and I have eaten in your kitchen twice and you have not charged me for either and I did not ask whether I was to be charged, which is the whole of what is wrong with me and not one word of what is wrong with you.**”

Nobody thanked either of them and the woman of about forty-nine said one thing back and it was not soft and it was not hard.

“**I did not charge you because you looked like a man who would not have paid and I have nine days a week and about nine people a day and about four pence is about four pence,**” she said. “**You can pay me for the two meals any day you like and I would rather you did.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at those boards had heard a woman of about forty-nine tell a man of thirty-two to pay her and about five had heard the correct arrangement of it, and the man of thirty-two paid her for the two meals that morning in coins at the boards in front of nine people and did not make a thing of it and was not thanked.

What happened at about the ninth hour was at the fourth house on the made road and nobody in that market street knew about it.

She had nine in the kitchen and about four beds of trade and a boy of about eleven of her own who had been holding her basket that morning, and there was no boy of about fourteen at the end of the long table, and the long table is about eighteen feet and there is a bench down each side of it, and about nine people eat in that kitchen most nights and about twenty can.

Nobody thanked her and the kitchen did what it does at the ninth hour, which is that everybody is talking and nobody is listening, and a man of about thirty-three who eats there four nights a week and who has a trade two streets off the market said one thing out loud, at about the ninth hour, to the table and not to anybody.

“**The boy is not to be asked for twice in a month.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four men at that table heard a man say a thing about a boy they had all met twice and about five did not, and the woman of about forty-nine said out loud that it was the first thing anybody in that kitchen had said in about nine years that was about somebody who was not in the room, and then she said that she was going to bed, and nobody thanked her for the nine years or for the fortnight.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a fortnight of a hired boy at fourpence a day, two beds of trade out of four in a kitchen nine years old, and a boy of about fourteen who can read a figure and is in no danger, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a fire iron at 9, a linen sheet at 22, a cake of size at 25, a hanglock at 28, a trivet at 30, a bundle of straw at 31, a pail at 32, a side of bacon at 34, a bag of flour at 35, and a kitchen board at 40.** Nine, twenty-two, twenty-five, twenty-eight, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five and forty is two hundred and eighty-six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and eighty-six pence is five marks and forty-six over.** A man of about thirty-four with a satchel was in that market street until about the sixth hour and then went up the made road on foot to the fourth house and stood at the low side of it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and did not knock, and the woman of about forty-nine saw him through the kitchen door and shut the door, and about four people inside that kitchen heard a door shut and about five did not.
