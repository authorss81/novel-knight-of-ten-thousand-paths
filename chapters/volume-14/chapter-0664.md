# Chapter 0664

**Harvestmonth the first, YR 321, a Sunday and not a market morning and the first of a month, the second hour in a market street with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door, about eleven people standing in the wet, and a copyist of about twenty-six reading a letter out loud on the boards because she said in a market on the Saturday that she would.**

The first of a month is the only day anybody comes, and it comes whether or not it is a market day, and this one was a Sunday and the room over the weigh-house was shut behind its own door and the copyist of about twenty-six had brought the register down thirteen stairs in the rain and had put it on the boards and had not opened it.

There were eleven people in the street and four of them had not come to a first of a month before and were standing at the edges of it in the manner of people who have arrived somewhere and are not sure whether they are allowed to be there, and the badge-man said in about nine words that anybody could stand there and that there was nothing to stand in and that nobody was going to ask anybody for a reason, which he says every first of a month and which he has got slightly shorter each time over about a year.

The copyist of about twenty-six read the letter out loud, and she read all of it including the count, and she read the count in the words the boy of about eleven had used and not in days, and eleven people in a wet market street on a Sunday heard a road four days off give its own figure for the first time.

“**Nine crossings open and four weeks shut, in two years, kept on a stick about the length of your arm,**” she read, “**and the boy says that is his count and that he has a copy of it in his own hand in case, and that his mother has the other one at the back of a dresser, and that neither of them has ever shown it to anybody before this morning.**”

Then she read the last two lines and four of the eleven understood them and the rest understood that something had been asked for.

“**We are not writing to ask you for the horse. We are writing because in about four years there will be a man who says that no horse was taken off that lane, and there will be nine of us who say there was, and he will have a book and we will not. That is the whole of what we want. We want somebody in this county to write down that there was a horse.**”

Nobody thanked her and the register stood shut on the boards with nine questions in it and no finding under any of them, and the copyist had said out loud in a market on the Saturday that she would do this on the first of every month for as long as there was a horse in it, and nobody had asked her to and nobody had thanked her and here it was on a Sunday in the rain with about eleven people.

The woman of about thirty-six, who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned, was at the high end of the boards and had been there since about the second hour and had not come to the reading and had listened to it with her head on one side.

She had been away about three weeks. She had gone four days down a made road and four days back it, to a county where the thing that had happened was a horse, and she came into that market street on the Friday evening in a cart with somebody else's basket in her lap, and four people in the market town had seen her go and nine had heard about it and nobody had asked her why she had gone.

The badge-man asked her the question he had been getting round to for about a month, and he asked it in the open at the boards in about nine words, and eleven people heard it, and it was not a question anybody in nine counties had ever asked anybody.

“**Have you ever been offered a job?**”

The woman of about thirty-six did not answer it at once, and the silence was not the silence of a person who has not understood. It was the silence of a person who has understood and has to find out how to say a true thing about herself in a market street in front of about eleven people who keep roads, and it took her as long as it takes a carter to get a load round.

“**No,**” she said.

Nobody said anything.

“**And I am going to say the rest of it out loud because I have had about three weeks of a cart to get to the place where I can,**” she said, “**and I am not saying it so that anybody here will offer me one. I am saying it because you are all going to find out in about a month anyway and I would rather it came from me in the rain on a Sunday than from somebody who counted.**”

“**Say the rest of it,**” the badge-man said, and he said it gently, which he did about four times a year.

“**I have been offered work three times since I came to this county and I have said no three times, and I want it understood that the three were not the same and were not better than what you are all standing here doing, and that they were offers to be paid to go round with a list and get names on it.**”

Four of the eleven people in that street understood all of that and seven understood half of it.

“**That is what my county did,**” she said. “**Not a burning. A list. For about a year before the burning, and the burning was about four people and a sheet and it took one morning, and the list took a year and about nine men going round with it and nobody burning anything at all. And I have said no three times to a person who was offering me four pence a day and a basket, and I would like it noticed that I said no and not that I refused to help anybody. I said no because I did not want to be the person who goes round with it, and I have not once in about four years wanted to be that person, and I have not once in about four years wanted anything else either, and I have not been asked what I want, and you have just asked me the wrong question, because the wrong question has an answer that is no and the right one has not got asked.**”

Then the badge-man said the thing that had been sitting in him for about a month, and he said it out loud in a market street in the rain, which is where he says everything, and about eleven people heard it and about four of them understood why it was a confession.

“**I am going to say what I have been doing for the last month and I am going to say it because a woman of about thirty-six has just stood in front of about eleven people and told me I asked the wrong question, and she is right, and I would like the nine of you to have it in a street in my own words rather than hear about it later.**”

Nobody said anything and eleven people in that market street stood in the rain and waited, which they did not know they were allowed to do.

“**On the twelfth of Longlight I asked a man in this street to be the name a warrant goes in a place for, and he said no, and he gave me a reason that was better than anything I have said in this street in about thirty-one years, and I said out loud that I was not going to ask twice. And I have not asked him twice, and I have not asked anybody anything since, and that is the whole of what I have done with a month. I have thought about that street every day and I have not been able to think of one person in it who could be asked for something and would say yes, and I have gone round that list four times and it is the same list, and four of the nine of them keep a road and about five of them keep a room or a shop or a child.**”

“**And then I asked her,**” the badge-man said, “**because she is the one person in nine counties who has said a hard true thing in a market street about nine times and has never once been asked for anything, and I asked her the question I had and not the question she has, and I have a badge and no head and no wage except about nine pence a week for standing in a street, and I have not got anything to offer a person of about thirty-six, and I am not going to pretend that I have. I am not going to make a post. I have said nine times that I will not make a head and I am not going to make a post either and I would like the eleven of you to notice that those are different promises and that I have made both of them in the same street in about nine days.**”

About four of the eleven people in that street looked at the ground and about seven looked at the woman of about thirty-six, and she did not look at anybody.

“**And I am not going to ask anybody anything for about a month,**” the badge-man said, “**and if the twenty-two notches on a piece of driftwood on a coast four days off are in my head at about the fourth hour every morning of my life then that is a thing I have got to carry on my own and not hand to a woman of about thirty-six on a Sunday.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked about the driftwood and about four of the eleven people in that market street understood what he had said and about seven heard a man say that he was not going to ask anybody anything for a month, which is the only sentence in it that anybody in that street could have put in a book.

Then a man of about forty-nine who keeps a road four days below this one asked the hearing, in the open and in the ordinary way, whether it could say out loud that a horse had been taken off a lane in another county.

“**No,**” the badge-man said. “**It has no bench in it and it cannot say that a sheet is false and it cannot say that a horse is stolen, and there is nothing at the top of it, and if it said it then it would be a thing said by nine people in a street on the first of a month with no head, and about four men in a livery would come here in a fortnight and ask us all to say it again and would write down every one of us who would.**”

“**Then what is it for,**” the man of about forty-nine said, which is a question he has asked twice in about a year.

“**Ask that on a day when somebody wants an answer,**” the badge-man said, “**and I have not got one and I am not going to give you a good one in the rain.**”

Then he asked the question he had actually come to ask, and he asked it because he had heard what she had said and he was not a man who waits for the second thing to arrive on its own.

“**What would you have to be asked to say yes to?**”

“**A wage and a name that is not at the top of it,**” the woman of about thirty-six said, and she said it in about nine words and did not stop, and about four of the eleven people in that market street put their heads on one side at once, because that is not an answer anybody in nine counties had said out loud. “**And I have thought about that for three weeks in a cart and I want to say the rest of it before anybody starts putting a good face on. I have not been asked whether I want to do the reading. I have been the one who reads. It happened because I would not stop and it has been four and a half years and I have said the first clause and the second and the third in a market street about nine times, and nobody has ever asked me whether I would rather somebody else did it, and if I had said no I would have been a woman who reads the first clause, which is a different animal and not a better one.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people at the boards looked at the ground and seven looked at her.

Then she named the second hole, and she named it out loud in a market street in the rain on a Sunday, and she was not the protagonist and she was not a person who has been in a room, and she said it to about eleven people in the worst possible place to say it, which is the only place anybody in this county has asked her to say things.

“**I said in a market street in Thawmonth of last year that the rule covers roads that can be written to, and I have said it in about nine streets since, and it is still true and I am not going to say it twice for the sake of a morning.**”

“**Then say the other one,**” the badge-man said, “**because the woman of about twenty-nine said one in a market street on the sixteenth of Longlight and I have been carrying it about for about a fortnight and I have not put it anywhere, and you are the only person in this county who has been in a county where a thing like that was done and come out of it.**”

“**The other one is this,**” the woman of about thirty-six said. “**Our three clauses are all about a man doing a thing to a road. A notice wants a man at the end of a road. A witness wants somebody named by the people who work it. A burden wants a man who can be found afterwards. A paper does not do a thing to a road. The paper does not want a witness. The paper is not a burden. And in nine counties there is nobody whose business it is to say that a paper has no business here, and that is the hole, and it has been the hole since Longlight and everybody in this street has heard about it, and it is not a small thing and I will not have anybody stand in this street on a Sunday and say it is.**”

“**Say what has changed,**” the copyist said, from the boards, and she said it in the flat way.

“**What has changed is that the paper has turned into a man,**” the woman of about thirty-six said, “**and I want that in nine mouths because in about three weeks every person in nine counties is going to understand the hole and about nine people are going to think that the hole is shut. It is not shut. It is worse than the paper. A man at a gate can be answered. A man at a gate is asking, and a person at a gate will answer him, and the answer goes in a small book, and the book is a thing we have all got an opinion about and none of us has ever had to hold. And there is not one of my three clauses about a book and there is not one of them about a person who has been asked and has answered without anybody having offered them the chance to say no first, and that is not a small thing either, and I have been three weeks in a cart thinking about it and that is the whole of what I have come back with.**”

Nobody said anything at that and about four people in that market street had stopped breathing in the ordinary way and seven were working it out.

“**And I am not going to tell you what to do about it,**” she said, “**because I have been asked by nine people in the last year what a road should do about a Crown paper and I have answered four of them and I have been wrong in two and I am not going to be asked a fifth time until somebody has told me what the question is.**”

Nobody thanked her.

Then Aren Kest, who had been at the high end of the boards for the whole of it, asked her one question, and it was a question he had not asked her before and had asked about four other people in a year, and eleven people in that wet street heard it and four understood why he was asking it.

“**The horse,**” he said. “**Did the woman whose gate it was ever hear that it was written down?**”

“**No,**” the woman of about thirty-six said. “**And she will not, because there is no road between here and there that keeps a post and the man who carried it has gone back down the made road on the Monday, and if the copyist reads it on the first of every month then that woman will hear about a horse in about four months, in a market street, from a stranger, if anybody at all thinks to tell her, and nobody will, because nobody here has ever had to think about getting a thing back to a house four days off and that is the actual cost of the whole of this and I have been in a cart for three weeks finding it out.**”

**And the cost of that Sunday is a column and it is nobody's, and three offers of four pence a day and a basket that were said no to and about a year of a list in a county where a list was burned are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a pot of tallow at 6, a broom at 8, a hurdle at 22, a barrow wheel at 24, a mallet at 25, a sack of lime at 28, a hand adze at 29, a rubbing stone at 32, a quire of paper at 36, and a door case at 40.** Six, eight, twenty-two, twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-eight, twenty-nine, thirty-two, thirty-six and forty is two hundred and fifty pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and fifty pence is five marks and ten over.** The woman of about thirty-six walked up the lane at about the fourth hour to a gate that is not hers and does not keep a road, and put a hand on the post of it for about as long as it takes to say a thing out loud, and did not lift the bar and did not shut it and did not say anything, and four people in four houses on that lane saw her do it and seven did not, and nine people in this county know it happened and four of them know why.

Then the register went back up the thirteen stairs in the rain, shut, with nine questions in it and no finding under any of them, and the room over the weigh-house was shut behind its own door for the rest of that Sunday, and the fourth ruled line of the outgoing sheet was empty and was not going to be filled, and eleven people went out into a wet street in nine counties and four of them had been asked a question and seven had not.
