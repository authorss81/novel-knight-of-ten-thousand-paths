# Chapter 0676

**Embermonth the tenth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the fourth hour in the hired room over a saddler's where the hearing sits on the four days a year it sits, about nine people in it, a woman of about thirty-four who is a ferrywright and keeps a crossing and the approaches to it standing up out of a chair and saying four sentences, and a man of thirty-two sitting down and not knowing he is wrong.**

She came in at about the fourth hour with about nine days on her and nobody had sent for her and she was not with anybody.

The nine days were like this and four people in that room got it out of her in four minutes and five did not try.

She had left a crossing at the fourth hour on the first of Embermonth with about nine days of road in front of her and no money for any of it beyond what she had in her coat. She had come up a made road that a bridge crew had been working on for about a month, which meant that four days of it had men and timber on it and that she had slept twice in a barn with about nine men in it and that nobody had asked her anything on any of the nine days.

Not one person in nine days had asked her anything. That was the thing she kept coming back to and four people in that room heard the shape of it and about five heard a woman talk about a road.

“**I have been on a road for nine days and everybody on it has fed me and not one person has asked me who I am,**” she said. “**And I know what that is. That is the whole of what I came to tell you and I have not said a word of it yet and I would like it noticed that I have been holding it for four sentences' worth of room because I have not got a room.**”

Nobody thanked her and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat said that nine people in that room had understood that and about five had not.

Nobody in that room knew her. The copyist of about twenty-six had the nine hundred words in her hand because it was a Saturday and nine people had come, and about four of them could see the shape of it and five could only hear the words, which is the same proportion as on the first of the month and which the copyist had stopped remarking on.

The woman of about thirty-six was at the high end. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat was at the door because he always was. The badge-man was not there and had not been asked to come and had said out loud that he was not coming to a room in an inn over a saddler's and that four of them would understand why and five would not.

Aren Kest was at the low end with his stick in his left hand and the nine hundred words in his coat.

The woman stood up when she came in, which nobody in that room expected, and four of the nine noticed it and five did not, and she said her name before anybody asked her anything, in the way of a person who has been in about nine hundred rooms.

“**Sera Quen,**” she said. “**I am about thirty-four. I am a ferrywright and I keep a crossing and the approaches to it, and my city is four hundred miles off and it was never severed, and I have not been in this room in about eleven years and I was last in a hall in a city that had not been told anything either.**”

Four people in that room had understood the whole of that and about five had heard a woman give her name.

Nobody thanked her for saying it and the man of about thirty-four with the sheet said the first question anybody in nine counties had ever asked her, which was the wrong one, and he asked it in the ordinary way.

“**Are you the crossing that keeps its count in crossings open and weeks shut.**”

“**Yes,**” Sera Quen said. “**And I keep it because nobody writes it down anywhere and because a road that keeps its own count is a road that knows what it has done. I have read what your woman read out in a market street on the first of Longlight about four times on the road and I have not got a copy and I am not going to ask for one.**”

Then she looked at the table, and the nine hundred words were not on the table because the copyist had them in her hand and was holding them against her chest, and about nine people in that room watched a woman of about thirty-four look at a copyist of about twenty-six holding nine hundred words against her chest.

“**Is that the thing,**” Sera Quen said.

“**That is the thing,**” the copyist said, “**and it is nine hundred words in nine clauses and about four of the nine of you can see the shape of it and about five can only hear the words, and I am going to read it out because there is no head for it in this county and there has not been one for about four and a half years.**”

Nobody said anything and Sera Quen said the thing that made about nine people in that room understand what the rest of the afternoon was for.

“**Do not read it,**” she said. “**I have read it. I have had thirteen days and I have read it on the road and I have read it in a barn and I have had a copyist of about twenty-six in a place four hundred miles off who read it to me in about nine minutes and would not give it to me.**”

Nobody thanked her and the copyist of about twenty-six said, in the flat way, that the hand was an established refusal and that nobody in nine counties had ever got a copy of anything and that she had not known until about four days ago whether she wanted one.

Then the copyist said the other thing, and it was about her three series, and she said it to a woman of about thirty-four who had said the first sentence out loud in a room.

“**I keep three series and they are good,**” she said. “**About nine hundred sheets a month out and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month in and a band on a fixed day I do not alter. I have kept them about four and a half years and there is nothing about what has happened in nine counties since the middle of Longlight that goes into any of them, and I have said that out loud about nine times, and this morning I said it in a market street to a man with a stick and I am saying it to you because you have walked nine days and you have not asked me for anything and that is twice in nine days.**”

Nobody thanked her and Sera Quen said the thing about the series that is the second cost of this volume.

“**I have got a crossing that counts its own crossings open and its own weeks shut and it has counted in a child's hand in my city for about two years, and there is nowhere for it to go,**” she said. “**A roll is for people who are on a roll. My count is not about people. It is about whether water is under the sill, and a county that keeps that kind of count has to be allowed to keep it and not to send it anywhere, and the whole of what your second clause does is arrive at a gate and ask a person to write it down in a book he carries in his coat.**”

Four people in that room had heard both halves of that and about five had heard a woman ask a woman for nothing.

Then the woman of about thirty-four said the second thing, which was to a man of thirty-two and not to the room.

“**You are the man who wrote it.**”

“**I am,**” Aren Kest said.

“**Then I am going to ask you one question and you are going to answer it, and then I am going to say four sentences, and I have walked nine days to do those two things in that order and not the other way round, because a person who answers first and then hears is a person who has already agreed to about half of it.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that room had understood the arrangement and five had heard a woman say that she had a plan.

“**What is it for?**” Sera Quen said. “**Nine hundred words and nine clauses. What is it for?**”

And Aren Kest told her.

He told her well. He told her it was for the thing nobody in nine counties could do alone, which was to ask, and that it asked one question out loud of a person who keeps a crossing, and that it named no road and put nothing into any return, and that it had four clauses about not doing things and about not filling a space in a book, and that the sixth clause was the only one anybody had ever objected to and that the objection had been right and that it had been answered and that the whole of the thing was that a road which is not on a paper cannot be found and a road which is on a paper can be argued with.

He said that last part with his hands still on the table and he said it because he believed it, and about nine people in that room had heard a man explain the whole of a thing in about four minutes and about four of them could tell afterwards that it had been the whole of it and about five could not.

Nobody thanked him and Sera Quen stood there through all four minutes without sitting down and without moving her hands and four people in that room noticed that and about five did not.

Then she said the four sentences, and she said them without stopping and without taking breath and she said them to about nine people in a room over a saddler's, and nine people in nine counties have said since that it was the plainest thing anybody had heard in about a year.

“**Your sixth clause wants seven roads to accept the authority of the Crown in the keeping of their own crossings for the length of the emergency, in writing, with a named person on each side. I have kept a crossing and the approaches to it for about nineteen years and I have never once been able to make a road do a thing, and the reason is that there is nobody at the end of a road to be told anything, so a road cannot be asked whether it has been told and cannot answer. Which means that the day one of your seven roads puts a name on your paper, a man will have a name to say at a gate for the rest of his life and the road itself will not be able to open its mouth, and that is not a new thing in this world, that is what a Sundering is. You have built four and a half years on a road not having a name on it and you have written nine hundred words putting one on every road in nine counties, and I am not going to be one of the nine people in this room who agrees to it, and I have walked nine days to say that in a room and not in a street.**”

Nobody said anything in that room for about nine seconds and nine people in nine counties have given the length of that silence as nine seconds and about four of them have given it as longer and five have given it as shorter.

Then the woman of about thirty-six said the thing that four people in that room had worked out and about five had not.

“**Say that again and say it slower,**” she said, “**because you have just said out loud in a room the thing I have been saying in four markets since the first of the month and about four people have understood it and about five have written it down, and neither of those two things has ever made anybody do anything, and you have said it nine days' walk from a crossing that has never been severed.**”

“**No,**” Sera Quen said.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat did the thing that nobody in that room had thought of doing and that four people in nine counties have said since was the only useful thing anybody did in that room all afternoon.

He did not read the whole of it.

He took the nine hundred words out of the copyist's hand, which nobody had asked him to and which the copyist let him do, and he read the sixth clause out loud and nothing else, and he read it in the flat way she asks things.

“**For the length of the emergency, each of the seven roads accepts the authority of the Crown in the keeping of its own crossing, in writing.**” He looked up. “**And the writing is held by the person named on the road's side, and by nobody in this county, and there is no order anywhere in it, which is what the whole of it turns on.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in that room had heard one clause of nine hundred words read out and four of them understood the whole of the nine hundred words from the one clause and about five had not.

“**Now read the ninth,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**I am not going to read anything,**” Sera Quen said.

“**Then I am going to read it and you are going to let me, and I am going to do it because a thing nobody will read out loud in a room does not exist, and I have been the man a thing arrives for for four years in a different trade and I am tired of it.**”

Nobody thanked him and the copyist of about twenty-six said that a room was a room and that she was not going to be argued with about a room, and about four people in that room heard a man be argued with and about five did not.

Then Aren Kest did the thing that the rest of that afternoon turned on, and he did it out loud, and he did it in about four seconds, and nine people in that room heard it.

“**I am not going to ask you to say that again,**” he said. “**And I am not going to ask you to put it another way, and I am not going to ask you what a Sundering is, because you have just told nine people in a room and about four of them have understood it and I am one of the four. I have been wrong and I have been wrong for about nine seconds and I do not yet know how I have been wrong and I am not going to find out in this room in front of about nine people who came here on a market morning.**”

Nobody thanked him and Sera Quen said the one other thing she said in that room, and it was one sentence and it was not softening.

“**You did not write it to be wrong,**” she said. “**You wrote it in about nine hundred words on a Tuesday in a room nobody had heard of and you have been wrong in about nine hundred words and it took me nine days and four sentences and that is the difference between us and I am not going to flatter you about it.**”

Nobody said anything and the copyist put the nine hundred words down on the table face up and did not fold them and did not put them in her coat, and four people in that room saw her do it and about five did not, and nobody thanked her and nobody thanked the woman of about thirty-four and nobody thanked anybody for anything at all on that Saturday.

Then the two months and the rest of it came out of the woman of about thirty-six, and nine people in that room heard it and about four of them had not known any of it.

“**There is a thing underneath the four sentences and I am going to say it once,**” she said, “**because I have been carrying it for about nine days and I have not said it in a street and I am not going to say it in a street now.**”

Nobody said anything.

“**On the nineteenth of Fallowmonth a woman of about thirty-eight stood in a market street and said that the question was whether to ask a city four hundred miles off a thing it has already said it will answer. And every person in this room has known since that morning that somebody was going to go. And none of us asked her to go and none of us sent her and about four of us have understood since that we were not waiting for an answer. We were waiting to be asked.**”

Four people in that room had heard the whole of that and about five had heard a woman say that a county was waiting to be asked.

“**And this morning the answer came up a made road on foot and it was not an answer, it was a person,**” the woman of about thirty-six said, “**and nine people in this county have spent a fortnight working out a way of being asked, and she has told us in four sentences that the way we worked out is the way that has been tried.**”

Nobody thanked her and Sera Quen said one thing about that and it was the only thing she said about the county of returned roads or about any person in it.

“**A county that has worked out a way of being asked is further along than anybody in this world,**” she said, “**and I have not got time to say the rest of it and I am not going to say the rest of it in a room. You have had a woman walk nine days and about four of you have asked her nothing and about five have asked her what she eats.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in that room looked at the table and about four of them looked at the door and five looked at the nine hundred words.

Then the man of thirty-two got up off his chair in about four seconds and then took about four seconds longer than that getting to the door, and nine people in that room watched a man of thirty-two leave a room he had written nine hundred words in, and nobody said anything to him about it and nobody thanked him for anything and four people in that room had worked out that he was not going to take the nine hundred words with him and about five had not.

He left them on the table. That is a fact about that Saturday and it is not in any book and it has not been put in any book and nine people in nine counties know it and four of them know why.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a man of thirty-two sitting in a chair while a woman of about thirty-four who walked nine days says out loud that his ninth clause is a Sundering with a different word at the front of it, and nine hundred words that are on a table face up in a room over a saddler's and are not withdrawn and are not signed, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a quill knife at 17, a cake of ink at 18, a sealing wax at 21, a bundle of laths at 29, a pick at 30, a shovel at 31, a froe at 34, a hand adze at 35, a hod at 36, and a press handle at 39.** Seventeen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six and thirty-nine is two hundred and ninety pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and ninety pence is six marks and two over.** Sera Quen went out of the room at about the fifth hour and down the stairs and out into a market street where about four hundred people were still in it and about two hundred of them were still stopped, and she stood at the low end of the boards for about as long as it takes a carter to get a load round and looked up and down the street as though she were measuring it, and then she went up the lane on foot toward the made road and nine people in that market street watched a woman of about thirty-four go out of a room and up a lane and not one of them had heard a word of the four sentences.
