# Chapter 0697

**Hearthmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in that market street with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, a Crown cart that came back down a made road on the Friday with a man in a livery who had stood against a wall, and nine people who put four conversations together and got to the same figure.**

The Crown cart came back down that made road on the Friday at about the seventh hour in the dark with one man in a livery on the near wheel and nobody spoke in the cart, and it went into a barn at the low end of that market street and the horse was put in and the cart was not unloaded and both of those things were ordinary.

Nobody thanked anybody and by the second hour that street had four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them had stopped what they were doing, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth was the largest crowd this market town has had since the first of Embermonth, and that about four hundred of the people in it did not know they were in the same street as each other.

The cold that Saturday had been in the ground since the middle of the night and had not got out of it, because the light in that street comes up the east end and goes off the west end by two in the afternoon and the bank to the west is higher than the street, and a market that has four hundred people in it in that kind of cold is a market of people moving. Two men had boards out of a cart at the low end by the seventh hour and had their breath going and had not said anything to each other since the sixth. The lamp at the high end of the boards was not lit and would not be lit until the fourth hour. The weigh-house weighed two loads of lime and a load of wool and went on weighing underneath a room that was shut, because that room shuts at noon on a Saturday and this was the second hour, which nobody in nine counties has ever been able to explain and nobody has asked about.

Nobody thanked anybody and four conversations were going in it at about the second hour and they were four feet apart and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is how a market street works and that four conversations about the same thing four feet apart for a morning is a thing that happens about four times in nine counties in four and a half years, and that on this morning nobody put them together on purpose.

The first conversation was at the low end of the boards and it was the man of about thirty-three who sells cloth three doors up, who had a load of cloth going up that made road on the Friday and had stopped at the ninth mile stone because a man in a livery put a hand up.

“**He got off the cart and walked about nine paces up to a wall on the high side and stood against it with his back to the road for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and then he got back on and gave me the nod to go on, and I went on, and I did not look at the wall because I have a load and I did not think about it until about four miles further on.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at those boards had heard a cloth seller say that a Crown officer stood against a wall, and about five had heard a man say he did not think about it until four miles on.

The second conversation was at the high end of the boards and it was a man of about forty-four who keeps a wain and a team of four on that made road, and what he said was the plainest fact anybody said in that street that morning and he said it to nobody in particular.

“**There is a gate in that wall. It has been shut as long as I have been driving that road and I have been driving it since I was about nine. It is a gate in a wall and there is a house on the wall and I have never once in my life seen it open and I have never once been through it and I would not go through it if it was open.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that market street had heard a wain driver say that a gate he had never gone through had been shut all his life, and about five had heard a man talk about a wall.

The third conversation was at the boards themselves and it was a man of about nineteen who keeps nine hives and takes his honey up that made road on Thursdays, and what he said was about a chimney.

“**There is a chimney behind that wall and there is smoke off it about four days in seven. I have seen it from the road since I was about eleven and I have never once thought of anything about it. It is a chimney and it is four days in seven and it is behind a wall and there is a gate in the wall and I have never asked what is behind the wall and I have never asked anybody who does.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that market street had heard a nineteen-year-old say that he had never asked what was behind a wall, and about five had heard a young man be honest.

The fourth conversation was about two hundred yards down that market street at a cartage yard, and it was the man of about forty-three who keeps that yard four streets up, and he said his piece to two men and nine people standing there and did not come up to the boards at all.

“**Carts go in that gate. They do not come out. And carts come out of it loaded. I have had a cart of my own go in there about nine years ago and come out loaded and I asked the carter where it had been and he said not to ask. I did not ask. I have thought about not asking about once a week for about nine years and I have never once asked anybody.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that market street had heard a yard keeper say that he has not asked a question once a week for nine years, and about five had heard a man talk about his own yard.

Then a question was asked in that market street, and it was asked out loud at the low end of the boards by the woman of about thirty-three who keeps a chandler's shop, and nobody stopped her, and two hundred people heard it.

“**Then should not somebody go up that road and stand at it?**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a question and about five had heard a woman ask whether a person should be sent, and then four people answered it in four different ways in about nine seconds and none of the four answers was a yes.

The cloth seller said that he went up that road on a Friday with a load and not on any other day, and did not go up it on a day he was not going up it. The wain driver said that he went up it when he had a load and that a gate that is shut is a gate that a man does not stand at, and that he had been asked twice this year where the works were and had told both of them the same thing both times. The woman of about forty-nine said that a person who goes up a made road and stands at a wall is a person who is on a made road, and that she had a fortnight of a hired boy once and was not going to describe to two hundred people what asking twice costs, and that nobody thanked her for not describing it. And the badge-man said eleven words and did not say anybody's name in them.

Nobody thanked him and nine people in that market street had heard a man say that he was not going to ask anybody to go and look, and about five had heard a man say that he was not going to send anybody, and nobody in nine counties has ever been able to establish what else he said on that morning, and he has not repeated it.

Then somebody in that market street said the word, and it was said by the man of about forty-three who keeps that yard, and he had come up from his own yard at about the third hour because four people had told him what he had said downstairs, and he said it in his own trade and nobody stopped him.

“**There is a gate in a wall and there is a house over it. That is what a building like that is called. I have pulled carts past nine of them in nine counties and there is one on every road worth the name and nobody ever looks at one and that is the whole of what a gatehouse is.**”

Nobody said anything and four hundred people in that market street had heard the word said out loud in a market street for the first time in nine counties, and about two hundred of them had stopped what they were doing for the fourth time that morning, and nine of those two hundred understood what the word was for and about five did not.

Nobody thanked him and the crowd was told nothing by anybody who was in it, and that is the whole of it, because nine people in nine counties have said since that the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth was the day four hundred people stood in a market street and were told nothing at all by anybody, and that the only man in that street with a sheet on him was a man of about thirty-three who sells cloth and the sheet he had was a bill of lading.

Then the putting together happened, and it happened in the open, and it happened among nine people who were not sitting together and who did not know they were nine.

The first of the nine was the man of about thirty-three who sells cloth, who had the ninth mile stone and the nine paces. The second was the wain driver, who had the gate. The third was the man of about nineteen, who had the chimney and the four days in seven. The fourth was the yard keeper, who had the carts. The fifth was a woman of about forty-nine who keeps a kitchen and four beds on that made road, who had the beds and had said in a market street in Frostmonth that a man who can count what comes past his door is the only man who can prove that a thing happened. The sixth was the badge-man, who had the four and a half years. The seventh was a man of about fifty-one who owns a field on the low side of a cut a mile and a half off, who had the mile and a half. The eighth was the copyist of about twenty-six, who had the three series and no line in any of them about a wall. The ninth was the woman of about thirty-three who keeps a chandler's shop at the low end of the boards, whose name is Nell Drury, and who had been in this market town for about eleven years and had sold nothing at a market that anybody can tell for nine of them.

Nobody thanked anybody and the ninth of the nine put it together out loud in the middle of that market street at about the third hour, and nobody stopped her and nobody helped her, and two hundred people heard it and nine of them understood all of it and about five understood the half of it that is about a gate.

“**I am going to add four things up and I am not going to tell anybody what they add up to, because I do not know what they add up to and I am not going to guess at a thing in a market street with two hundred people in it. Here is the four. A man stopped a cart at a gate nine miles up a made road and stood against the wall for about as long as it takes to turn a cart. There is a gate in a wall with a house over it. There is a chimney behind it going four days in seven. And carts go in it and carts come out of it loaded.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street had heard a woman say four things and refuse to add them, and about five had heard a shopkeeper be careful.

“**And here is the fifth thing and it is not a thing anybody said to me. I have kept this shop eleven years and I have sold out of this street four days a week in eleven years, and a man in a coat has come up this street on about nine hundred days, and on every single one of those nine hundred days he went past that made road at the top of the street or he did not go past it, and if he went past it then a wall with a house on it was nine hundred paces off his shoulder. Four and a half years is about nine hundred days. Nobody ever stopped. Nobody in this street ever stopped anybody for looking at a wall.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in that market street had heard the figure, and two hundred people had heard a woman say that about nine hundred days is four and a half years, and nine people understood that a thing had been visible from a made road the whole time nine counties had been building rules about roads, and about five understood that a chimney goes four days in seven.

Then the crowd did what four hundred people do when there is nothing left to be told them, which is that it went back to about nine things, and the nine people at the boards stayed, and the four conversations that had been four feet apart at the second hour were one conversation by the fourth hour with nine people in it who had not arranged it.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that market street had worked out that they were the nine and about five had not, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth is the only day in about four and a half years on which nine people in nine counties put a thing together in the open in a market street and four hundred people heard every word of it and understood nine words of it.

Then the copyist of about twenty-six said the true thing about her own half of it and nobody thanked her.

“**Nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room. Four hundred and nine parish rolls a month come in. A band on a fixed day that I do not alter whatever is in the county. There is not one line in any of them about a wall on a made road at nine miles, and there is not going to be one, and I am not going to rule a head for it, and that is the fourth time in about four and a half years that the thing this county is about has been a thing I cannot put down.**”

Nobody thanked her and four people in that market street had heard a keeper of records say that her own work cannot hold the shape of the thing she lives in, and about five had heard her say the fourth time.

Aren Kest stood at the high end of those boards for the whole of that morning and said nothing at all, and nobody thanked him and nobody looked at him, and four people in that market street worked out that the ninth of the nine was putting a figure on a thing that four hundred people already had, and five had not counted the nine.

Then the badge-man said one thing, and it was eleven words, and he did not say it to the two hundred.

“**It has stood there the whole four and a half years.**”

Nobody thanked him and nine people in that market street had heard a man of fifty-eight say the whole of what had happened in eleven words, and about five had heard eleven words.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody said anything for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and then the market went on, because a market goes on, and the two hundred went back to about nine things and nine people stayed at the boards.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in nine counties have said since that nobody in that market street said a word to anybody about what they ought to do about it, and that nobody named anybody, and that nobody wrote anything down, and that four hundred people walked out of that market street that afternoon with the location of a gatehouse in nine counties in their heads, and that five people in nine counties have said that this is the most dangerous thing that has happened in this county since the middle of Longlight, and that four people have said it is the most useful, and that the two sets have not spoken to each other and that one of them is going to be wrong.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that market street said one more thing out loud that afternoon, and it was said by the man of about fifty-one who owns a field on the low side of a cut a mile and a half off, and what he said was a thing about distance and nothing else, and five people heard it as a complaint about the Crown.

“**It is nine miles up a made road from this street and there are about nine hundred people between here and it, and it has been there four and a half years. That is all I have got. I am not going to tell two hundred people what to do about a wall at nine miles, because I do not know and because if I did know then I would be the man who told this county what to do about a wall at nine miles, and in about four years that would be a head.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that market street had heard a man refuse to be useful for the fourth time in a month, and about five had heard a farmer complain about the Crown, and that the two readings of that sentence are the two readings of this county and that neither of them is chosen between anywhere.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody in that market street said which of the two is right, and the market town got on with its Saturday, and a wain went up the lane to the ford with a chain on a handcart and a boy of about nine walking beside it, and nobody stopped that boy and nobody asked him anything, and nine people in nine counties have said that the only person in that market street on the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth who was not told anything was nine years old and walking beside a handcart.

**Four and a half years, and you have been walking past the front of it twice a day, and you never had to decide anything about it, and that is the whole reason nobody ever named it. None of it can be put back.**

**And the cost of that market morning is a wall on a made road at nine miles that four hundred people can now point at, and a word said out loud in a market street that had not been said out loud in nine counties, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sheet of foolscap at 1, a crowbar at 28, a hurdle at 29, a gate stop at 30, a barrow wheel at 31, a hod at 32, a chain link at 34, a lantern at 35, a wicket at 36, and a barrow at 37.** One, twenty-eight, twenty-nine, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six and thirty-seven is two hundred and ninety-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and ninety-three pence is six marks and five over.** That wall has a gate in it that has been shut since before the middle of Longlight and nobody in nine counties has been through it and one man in a livery stood against it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart on the twenty-fourth of Hearthmonth, and four people in nine counties have said that a wall with a house on it is the cheapest thing in this county to look at and about five have said that it is the only thing in this county that cannot be looked at twice.
