# Chapter 0692

**Hearthmonth the fifth, YR 321, a Sunday and not a market morning, with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting, the fifth of nine days of held crossings, four gates and no market, and a question that came round again in Fallowmonth and has not been answered.**

A Sunday in a market town of about two thousand people is a day with the shutters up and the weigh-house shut from noon on the Saturday until the seventh hour on the Monday, and the room over that weigh-house is shut behind its own door for the same hours and for the same reason, and nobody in nine counties has ever been able to establish by whom that shutness is decided and nobody has asked.

On that Sunday there was no market, and there were about nine people in nine counties who had wanted to be in a market street that morning and were not, and the boards stood in an empty street from about the ninth hour until about the eleventh with nothing on them, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because a lamp on a Sunday is a lamp somebody has paid for out of a shop.

Nobody thanked anybody and about four people in nine counties have said since that the fifth day of nine days of held crossings was the day this county told the whole of its own business out loud to about nine people and not to the four hundred, and about five have said that the fifth day was the day nothing happened.

The weather that Sunday came down out of the east and did not stop, and it was not the cold of the Thursday, which had been in the ground, but a wet that got into the hem of a coat and stayed there, and the lane outside the weigh-house was the worst of it because it held water against the wall on the low side and had done since the previous night. Four people in nine counties have said that the lane is where this county does its business and about five have said that the lane is where this county does nothing and would do it in a room if there were a room, and that there is a room and that the room has a door on this lane.

The smith's house at the junction was shut and cold at the back of it, and the woman of about thirty-one who keeps it came out once at about the tenth hour to put a bar across her own door and went in again, and nobody thanked her, and about four people in that lane had noticed that a woman put a bar across a door on a morning with nobody in the street that wanted one and about five had not.

Then the report of the fifth day went round the lane, and it went round because about nine people in nine counties tell each other things on a Sunday and there is no market and no room and no series to put it in, and the report is the whole of the reporting there is.

Nobody thanked anybody and the report was that a gate four miles up the lane had been shut at about the fourth hour yesterday by a woman of about fifty-two who keeps a cow and eleven hundred yards of a wall and who had never in her life been asked a question by anybody in a coat, and that a man in a livery had come to it and had asked for a person who could read a figure out loud, and that she had said there was nobody, and that he had read back what he wrote and gone away.

Nobody thanked her and about four people in that lane had heard a cow-keeper become a gate on a Sunday with no instruction, and about five had heard that a man asked for a reader and did not get one.

Then the report said that a gate on the low side of the ford had been standing since the third hour yesterday and that a Crown party had come to it with a sheet at about the ninth hour and had gone away at about the tenth, and that at that gate the people had gone on doing the ordinary work of that gate, which is to open it and to let a cart through and to say nothing to anybody.

Nobody thanked anybody and about four people in that lane had understood that this was the whole of what a held crossing is, and about five had heard that a cart went through a gate.

What happened that day was that two of the four gates that were standing on the second of Hearthmonth were still standing, and that a fifth gate had come up on its own between the high side of the waterworks country and the made road, and that nobody in nine counties knows the name of the person who shut it, and that the woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a tally at a post has said in two counties since that a gate that stands is a gate somebody stood at, and that the number of gates has nothing to do with the number of people, and that both numbers have been two and five on the same days.

Nobody thanked her and four people in that lane had heard a keeper of a tally say that a county's count and a county's head are two different numbers, and five had heard a woman say that two and five are the same.

Then the question came round again, and it came round because on the nineteenth of Fallowmonth a woman of about thirty-eight had stood in a market street in front of about four hundred people and asked whether nine counties was going to ask a city four hundred miles off a thing that city has already said in writing that it will answer, and nobody in nine counties has ever been able to establish who asked her to ask it, and she has never said, and about four people in nine counties think she was put up to it and about five think she was not.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that lane had heard the oldest question in nine counties come round a second time, and five had heard a question.

Then four people said no to being the one who decides it, one at a time, and they said it fast, and nobody argued with any of them.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head said it first, and he said it in fourteen words, and he said it standing outside the shut door of a room he had not knocked on.

“**It is not mine to decide and I have said why four months ago.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a satchel said it second, and he gave a reason in nine words, and nobody thanked him.

“**I write to them, I do not ask them.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six said it third, through the door, without opening it, and she said it from the other side of nine inches of oak, and about four people in that lane heard a keeper of records refuse a question through a shut door.

“**I have three series and there is not one of them for a decision nobody has made.**”

And the man of about thirty-two with a stick said it fourth, and he said it on the step, and he said it once, and he did not give a reason and nobody asked him for one.

“**Not me. Somebody with standing has to do it and it is not going to be me.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people in that lane had heard four people decline a thing that has to be decided by somebody, and about five had not known until that morning that it had to be decided by anybody at all.

Then the man who decided it came up the lane on foot at about the tenth hour, and he was a man of about fifty-two who keeps four miles of made road under contract for about nineteen years, and he hires about nine men to that road in Frostmonth, and he has said in a market street that he will not give a name to the second of the three clauses, and nobody in nine counties has argued with him about it since.

Nobody thanked him and nobody stopped him and he had come a mile and a half in the wrong boots for it and he said so before he said anything else.

“**Then it is me, and I am going to say why in the only trade I have got, and nobody in this lane is going to thank me for it and I am not going to ask.**”

Nobody thanked him and about nine people in that lane heard a man agree to decide a thing and about four had expected somebody else to.

Then he gave the reason, and he gave it the way he gives a figure on a contract, which is from the bottom up, and he said it once and nobody stopped him.

“**A city four hundred miles off has said in a letter carried on an ordinary post that it will name a place if anybody in nine counties asks it. Read that again and hear where the weight is. It has not named a place. It has put nothing to its name. It has said that if a thing is asked of it then it will do a thing it has never done. Every word of that letter is a thing it has not done.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that lane had heard a contractor parse a document for the part that has been done, and about five had heard a man read a letter.

“**And then the day one of us asks it, it is in the taking. Not that it takes anything. That it is in the taking. A city that has been asked a question is a city that has been drawn into it, and a city that is in a thing cannot get out of it afterwards, and every word that city says for the next nine years will be a word it says inside the thing and not outside it. My father kept a road for the district for thirty-one years and he was asked for a boat in the flood year, and he gave the boat, and after that every argument we had about the road was an argument about the boat.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that lane had heard a man describe how one yes takes nine years, and about five had heard a story about a flood.

“**And then the last of it, which is the whole of it. Nine counties cannot refuse a city. Not cannot as in it is hard. Cannot as in nine counties has never once had to. There is not a man in nine counties who has stood in front of a thing a city said and said no to it, and there is no practice at it, and there is no clause, and there is not one person in nine counties who could do it twice. And the only way to keep on not having to is not to ask.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that lane had heard the true thing said by a man who had no standing to say it and who had standing in his own trade, and about five had heard a man refuse to let nine counties take a thing it had never refused.

Then the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head said the one thing he had come down that lane to say, and he said it in one go, and he said it to the man of about fifty-two and not to the lane, and he did not thank him and did not defend him.

“**I have no clause for what you have just said and I have never had one, and I have spent four months telling a market street that this thing has no head, and a thing with no head has one advantage over a thing with a head, which is that nobody can tell you that you were out of order.**”

Nobody thanked either of them and about four people in that lane had heard a man of fifty-eight give another man the only thing he had, which was that there was nothing to be out of, and about five had heard a man say that he had no clause, which he has said at four of these boards and in two rooms and in one market street in Fallowmonth and had never said it to another man before.

Then the man of about fifty-two said the other half of it, and he said it looking at the badge-man, and nobody stopped him.

“**And do not put it on the arrangement. I am not part of your arrangement. I have never been asked what a road is and I have said no to a man in a livery twice this year and both times I gave him a reason and both times he wrote it down. I have got no standing here and I know that better than the nine of you, and I said the thing because I keep four miles of it under a contract for nineteen years and not because I have got a view on nine counties.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that lane had heard a man take his own words off a thing and put them back on his trade, and about five had heard a man refuse to be useful.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a satchel asked one question, and he asked it out loud and in the open and in the lane, and he asked it because the man of about fifty-two had said a thing that nobody in nine counties could carry four days up a made road.

“**Is the city the only thing four hundred miles off that has said it will do a thing if it is asked.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in that lane had heard the question, and about five had heard a man ask a question on a Sunday in the rain, and nobody answered it then and nobody answered it on the Tuesday and nobody has answered it since, and about four people in nine counties have said that it is the only question anybody in this county has asked this year that nobody has answered and that nobody is going to, and about five have said that it is not a question at all and that a man with a satchel asks things so that they are on the record that there was no answer.

Then a question was asked out loud, in the lane, by a person who is nobody's clerk and nobody's man and who is not any of the nine, and nobody stopped her and nobody answered her.

“**Then is a rule that has never been used the best thing this county has made?**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in that lane had heard the question that has been standing in nine counties since the twentieth of Wolfmonth, and about five had heard a woman ask it a second time and out loud and in the open, and it stood there in the lane for as long as it takes to turn a cart and then the lane was a lane again.

Then the question that had been asked in Fallowmonth came out one last time, and it was not the question about the city, and nobody had asked it, and nobody answered it.

“**Then who sent anybody?**”

Nobody said anything to that and four people in that lane understood that the question was about a paragraph that had come off the coast in the middle of the summer and about a woman who had gone up a made road and come back down it, and about five understood that it was about nothing at all.

Then the man of about fifty-two picked up his boots in one hand and said the last thing he said in that lane, and he said it to the ground, and it was sixteen words.

“**I am not going to say who sent anybody and neither is anybody in this lane.**”

Nobody thanked him and he went back up the lane to his four miles with the boots in his hand and got there at about the second hour and found the rail he had started on the Saturday cut through and finished, and cut another one, and nobody thanked him for either.

Then the rain stopped at about the fourth hour and the lane went from being a bad place to stand in to being a place where about nine people were standing, and the four of them who had been there when the question was asked were still there and the five who had come later had not heard the question, and about four people in nine counties have said since that this is how a question dies in nine counties, and about five have said that this is how anything dies in nine counties, and neither of those sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked anybody and the fifth gate was still standing on the Monday morning and the sixth came up on the Tuesday and about four people in nine counties have said since that the number went up every day without anybody deciding that it should, and about five have said that the number going up is not a decision and is not a count and is not a series, and that in about four years somebody in a room is going to want to know what the number was on each of nine days and that nobody will be able to tell him, and that he will be right to want to know.

Nobody thanked anybody and the city four hundred miles off was not named in that lane that day, and nobody was sent, and nothing was written down anywhere, and the room over the weigh-house stayed shut behind its own door until the seventh hour on the Monday, and on the Monday the copyist came down the thirteen stairs and unlocked the door and opened it and did not write the fifth day in the day-book, and the day-book stood at eleven entries where it had stood on the first of Hearthmonth, and nobody asked her why.

Nobody thanked her and four people in nine counties have said that the day-book standing still is the whole of the finding of the fifth day and that five have said that the day-book standing still is the most frightening thing that has happened in nine counties in about four and a half years, and that neither of those sets has ever spoken to the other.

**And the cost of that Sunday in Hearthmonth is a city four hundred miles off that has not been asked, a question about who sent anybody that has not been answered, and a day-book that stands at eleven entries, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sheet of foolscap at 1, a wedge at 2, a hobnail at 3, a lath at 4, a hank of yarn at 13, a kettle at 30, a bench at 35, a hand lantern at 36, a besom at 37, and a hand bell at 39.** One, two, three, four, thirteen, thirty, thirty-five, thirty-six, thirty-seven and thirty-nine is two hundred pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred pence is four marks and eight over.** A hand bell hangs in nine county buildings and has hung in the smith's house at the junction since before the middle of Longlight and is rung by whoever is behind the counter when a debt is called in, and on the fifth of Hearthmonth nobody rang it, and about nine people in that market town have said since that the reason nobody rang it is that there was nothing to call in and that a bell that is only rung for money is the only bell in a market town that anybody can stand behind a shut door and hear and not go out to.
