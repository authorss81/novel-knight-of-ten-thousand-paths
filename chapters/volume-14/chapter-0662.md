# Chapter 0662

**Rainmonth the twenty-third, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour at the boards in a market street with about four hundred people in it, and a letter with a hand on it that the copyist of about twenty-six has seen before and will not say where.**

The letter came in at about the second hour on the Saturday and it came with two people, and about four hundred people in the market street watched it come in, because a man of about thirty-seven with a road bag and a boy of about eleven standing at his shoulder is a thing that a market street looks at.

Nobody in that street knew the man. He had come four days off the made road with the bag on his shoulder and he had slept in a barn on the Wednesday and had a road bag and about nine days of other people's boots in it, and he said, when the copyist asked him at the boards, that he had been hired at a posting house at the top of a lane four days off, and that the person who hired him had put the letter in his hand at a gate and had not told him where it came from and had told him it was wanted at the market town and that he would know the market town by the weigh-house.

“**He said the road stops being made a mile and a half before you get there,**” the man of about thirty-seven said, “**and he said that the lane he came off is not on any board in any town I have been in for nine years and that is how I found it.**”

Then he gave the letter over and went and stood at the low end of the boards with his bag between his feet, and the copyist of about twenty-six turned it about twice and did not open it, and four hundred people in that market street waited.

She opened it.

It was a leaf, and it was folded in two, and it was written on both sides in a hand that is not the hand of a Crown clerk and is not the hand of a copyist, and the hand was a good hand, and the hand had nine words to a line and the lines were even and the page was full and there was nothing on the back of the fold.

The copyist read it out loud at the boards in about two minutes to about four hundred people, which she said afterwards she should not have done and did.

“**To the room with the door on the lane, and to anybody else who will stand still long enough,**” she read. “**We are writing because a horse is gone. On the Monday last, at about the ninth hour, four men in a livery this county has not seen came up the mile and a half where the made road stops being made, and they asked at four houses for a horse, and a horse went with them off the top of the lane, and it was a bay gelding of about nine hands and it belonged to nobody in particular, which is not the same as belonging to the road, and we are going to be exact about that because it is the only part of this that anybody will try to get wrong.**”

About four hundred people in that market street had found out in about a minute that there was a lane four days off which had lost a horse, and about nine of them had found out at the same time that a man had come to four houses on a Monday morning and asked for a horse, and about four hundred had not found out anything else that morning.

“**A bay gelding of about nine hands does not belong to nobody,**” the copyist read, “**and we are not writing to get it back and we are not writing to complain about it. It is on the made road four days of here and it will be in a county we have never been to, and there is nobody we know who could go and get it, and we are not going to send anybody. We are writing because a horse off that mile and a half is a horse off about nine households, and about nine households is the whole of us, and four of those households have no other horse and one of them has a man who cannot walk a mile.**”

Then the copyist stopped, and four hundred people in a market street in the rain waited, and she said out loud that the rest of it was four lines and that she was going to read it.

“**The count is nine crossings open and four weeks shut, in two years, and it is kept by a boy of about eleven who will tell it to anybody who asks him and who has not been asked by anybody before this morning. Do not turn it into days. A week that is shut is not seven of anything. We have been asked nothing and we are counting it anyway and we are telling you the count because there is a thing happening on the top of this lane and the count is the only thing any of us has that somebody else has not got.**”

Then she read the last four lines, and four people in that market street understood all four and four hundred understood one.

“**We are not writing to ask you for the horse. We are writing because in about four years there will be a man who says that no horse was taken off that lane, and there will be nine of us who say there was, and he will have a book and we will not. That is the whole of what we want. We want somebody in this county to write down that there was a horse.**”

There was not one thank you in that market street and the letter was not the reason.

The boy of about eleven was standing at the low end of the boards with the man of about thirty-seven and he had not moved during any of it, and the copyist turned round and asked him, in the flat way she asks things, whether the count in the letter was his count, and he said it was, and she asked him whether nine crossings open and four weeks shut was what he had, and he said it was, and she asked him how he kept it, and he said out loud, in a market street, in front of about four hundred people, that it was on a stick.

“**A notch for a crossing open and a notch the other way for a week shut, and it is a stick about the length of your arm and my mother has it at the back of the dresser and I have a copy of it in my own hand in case,**” the boy said. “**I have never shown it to anybody. My mother said I was not to.**”

Four hundred people in that market street had heard about a stick, and four of them had understood that a road in a county four days off had been counting its own openings and shut weeks in a child's hand for two years because nobody had ever come and asked.

Nobody said anything about the days. A man of about thirty-four with a sheet in his coat was at the boards and he did not do the sum, and four people at the boards looked at him not doing it, and the badge-man said out loud that he was glad and gave the reason in nine words, which was that nine crossings open and four weeks shut was a count of a thing and a number of days would be a different thing wearing the same coat.

Nobody thanked him and the copyist went on with what she had come down to say, and the boy of about eleven stood at the low end of the boards with the man of about thirty-seven and looked at the stones.

Then somebody at the high end of the boards said the obvious thing, which is that the copyist had turned the leaf about twice before she opened it, and that a woman who keeps a room for four and a half years does not turn a thing about twice for the shape of it.

“**You know the hand,**” the badge-man said, and he said it as a fact and not as a question.

“**I have seen it,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said. “**I am not going to say where I have seen it and I would like about nine people in this market to notice that I have said that and am not going to say, and I would like the rest of you to notice what I have just done, which is that I have refused to answer a question about a hand in the middle of a market street, which is the whole of what this county is for, and I have never done it before and I have not decided to start.**”

About four hundred people in that market street heard a woman refuse to answer a question about a hand, and about four of them understood that the refusal was not about the hand, and about four hundred went on to the part about the horse.

“**Say one thing that is not where,**” the badge-man said, because he had been doing this for thirty-one years and he knew that a refusal with nothing standing behind it is only sulking.

“**That it is a good hand,**” the copyist said. “**And that it was a good hand when I saw it, and that it is a good hand now, and that a person does not get better at a thing by writing about a horse.**”

Nobody thanked her and the badge-man said that he had got what he was entitled to and that he would not ask again, which is the thing he says in this street about everything.

Then the badge-man asked the one question the morning turned on, and he asked it of the copyist and not of the four hundred people, and he asked it in nine words.

“**Was the horse asked for?**”

The copyist said that she did not know and could not ask, and the man of about thirty-seven with the bag at the low end of the boards said that he had been in four houses on a mile and a half and had heard it from a woman of about sixty, and that it was asked for, and that the woman of about sixty had said that a man in a livery had come to her door and asked her for a horse in a voice, and that she had given him one.

“**She said she would have said no,**” the man said. “**And she said that she did not get as far as saying anything at all, and that the man said that it was very good of her before she had got the word out, and that she has thought about it every day since, and that the thing she cannot get past is not the horse.**”

“**What is it?**” the badge-man said.

“**That he asked her,**” the man of about thirty-seven said. “**She said that if he had taken it she would have stood in the gateway and put the bar across and that she would have been right to, and that he asked her, and that she is fifty-nine and has kept a lane for thirty-four years and has never in her life been asked for anything by anybody, and that she does not know what to do with a person who asks.**”

Then the badge-man said the thing that was the finding of that morning, and he said it into a market street with about four hundred people in it, and he said it slowly, and four people heard all of it and four hundred heard one line of it.

“**Then it is the second clause,**” he said, “**and it is the second clause of a sheet that does not shut a single road, and it has been on eight gates in nine counties for about five weeks, and I have been standing in this street saying out loud that it shuts nothing, and I was right, and I have been wrong about the whole of what I have been saying, and I would like about four people in this market to understand that those two sentences are both true and that I have been the man saying both of them.**”

“**Say the second clause out loud,**” the copyist said.

“**All and every the persons, horses, carts, goods and labour of the district wherein those works stand are required to give such aid to the officers of the Crown as those officers shall require, at such times and in such manner as the officers shall set out.**”

He shut the badge up inside his coat.

“**That is it, and that is the only one of the three things anybody in this street has ever been moved by, and it has moved a horse off a mile and a half four days from here in about nine seconds, and not one of my three clauses touches it, and I have not got a clause about a man who says that it was very good of her before you have finished saying no, and there is not one clause in about nine hands in nine counties about a person being asked.**”

Nobody said anything and four people at the boards looked at four hundred people at the boards, and four hundred people went home to nine counties with a horse on a made road they had never walked.

Then the copyist did the thing that the rest of that morning turned on, and she did it in the open, and four hundred people watched a woman of about twenty-six decide where a piece of paper goes.

She said that the standing of the letter was the best standing of anything that had ever come into that room, because it was a road, and a real one, and inside the nine counties, and one of the seven, and that she had thought about the register for about a minute and a half and had nearly put it in.

“**And I am going to say out loud why I am not going to,**” she said, “**and I am going to say it to about four hundred people and not to about nine. Nine questions stand in that book and no finding under any of them, and every one of those questions was asked in this county by a person who lives in it. If a road writes to me then that book is a thing roads write to. And a thing that roads write to is a head at the top of a thing, and a woman of about twenty-nine told that room in Mudmonth of last year that a name in a room is a head at the top of a thing, and she was right, and I have not got an argument against her and I have had about four and a half years to find one.**”

Nobody thanked her and four hundred people in that market street did not agree and four of them agreed entirely.

“**So it goes on a leaf with no head over it, in my hand, and I am going to read it out at the boards on the first of every month for as long as there is a horse in it, and I am going to say the number of crossings and the number of weeks in the words the boy used and not in days, and that is all I am going to do with it, and I would like nine people to notice that the first letter any of the seven has ever sent anybody in this county has been refused by a drawer.**”

And then the copyist of about twenty-six said the true thing about herself, out loud, in a market street, to about four hundred people, and it was the only thing she said that morning that was not a figure.

“**I have wanted a letter for about four years, and I have said so out loud about four times to about nine people and nobody has ever sent me one, and I have just found out that I do not want one, and I would have found that out in about four days if one had come, and that is worth more than the letter and I am not going to pretend it is not.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six was not thanked for that either and did not appear to want to be.

Then Aren Kest came down off the boards to where the boy of about eleven was standing at the low end of them and asked him one question, and it was the only thing he had said at that end of the boards all morning, and about four hundred people heard it and about four understood it.

“**Did the man on the lane say which county the horse was going to?**”

The boy said no, and said that his mother had asked the same thing and that the man had said that it was a matter of where the works were, and that he had not asked again.

Aren Kest went back up to the high end of the boards after that and said nothing else for the rest of the morning, which nobody in this street has ever called a habit, because calling it a habit would be a thing said about a man instead of to him.

What he was doing was not a sum. A woman of about fifty-nine who had kept a lane for thirty-four years had said she would have said no and had not got as far as it, and the reason she had not got as far as it was that a man had said that it was very good of her first. The whole of the difference between a road that is kept open and a road that is taken is the half second in which a person decides whether they have been asked or told.

He had been the man a thing arrives for for four years and had spent a year of it inside the second clause without once putting those two words next to each other, and the reason was not that he had been slow. The second clause is written to be read as an instruction to an officer, and nine people in nine counties had read it that way, and he had read it that way, and a woman in a county four days off had read it as the opposite and had been right.

He thought about four marks of lime at the height of a thumb and a false baulm with a wedge driven under it and a book with a column in it that a clerk had opened because nobody had asked him to, and he thought that the Crown had not beaten nine counties with a paper. It had beaten them with politeness, one house at a time, four days of road away, in a livery, and had said that it was very good of her each time, and everything it had done was in the second clause, and was therefore not a closure, and was therefore outside everything nine counties had built.

He had the rest of it still in his hands and did not say it and nobody asked him for it, and four hundred people in that market street had gone home knowing a horse had gone off a mile and a half, and four of them had understood that a county had been asked for a thing and had said yes.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is nobody's, and a bay gelding of about nine hands and nine crossings open and four weeks shut on a stick about the length of a boy's arm are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sheet of newspaper at 1, a pot for the paste at 4, a dozen of tacks at 11, a rule of boxwood at 12, a cushion for a cart seat at 13, a grinding stone at 27, a ream of foolscap at 32, a cart hood at 34, a chain for a gate at 36, and a trunk at 40.** One, four, eleven, twelve, thirteen, twenty-seven, thirty-two, thirty-four, thirty-six and forty is two hundred and ten pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and ten pence is four marks and eighteen over.** The man of about thirty-one slept in the barn on the Thursday night and was given a bed in a cart shed on the Friday night and went back down the made road on the Monday with four days of wages and about nine days of other people's boots still in his bag, and the boy of about eleven went up the mile and a half on foot with a stick about the length of his arm in his hand and a copy of it in his own hand in his pocket, and four people at the boards watched him go and four of them thought about the stick and four of them thought about the horse.

Then the copyist went up the thirteen stairs and put the leaf in the drawer about eleven inches deep with about four hundred returns in it, and did not put it in the register and did not rule a head over it, and the drawer shut about halfway on about nine other leaves and on about four hundred returns and on one letter from a road, and the room over the weigh-house stood open with no date anybody would give, and the fourth ruled line of the outgoing sheet was empty, and about four hundred people in a market street had gone home with a lane four days off in their heads and not one of them had been asked to do anything about it.
