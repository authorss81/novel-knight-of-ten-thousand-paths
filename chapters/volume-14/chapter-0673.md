# Chapter 0673

**Fallowmonth the twenty-ninth, YR 321, a Tuesday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the inn full from the third hour, a man of thirty-two with a permanent limp and a ferrier's stick writing out about nine hundred words at a settle by a fire, in nine clauses, and nobody asking him to sign it.**

He started at the fourth hour because the inn fills from the third and a full room is the only kind of room in this county where a man can sit for six hours and not be asked a question.

That is not true and he knew it was not true. About nine people in that room knew who he was and about nine of them did not, and two of the nine who knew had a reason to want something off him and one of them had a reason to want to tell him something, and neither of them came to the settle, and he was aware of all of that for about four seconds at the fourth hour and then stopped being aware of it, which is a thing that happens to him about twice a year and which he does not examine.

He wrote it in nine clauses. He numbered them, because the alternative is that a thing of nine hundred words cannot be argued with by anybody, and he had watched a woman of about twenty-six refuse a letter out of a drawer on the grounds that a book roads write to is a head at the top of a thing, and he had agreed with her out loud and had not liked it.

He wrote the heading first, and then the first clause, and he wrote them in the order a person would say them, and he wrote the whole thing out in about nine hundred words over five hours without stopping except to get more wood, and the innkeeper of about twenty-nine brought him a cup of something hot at about the sixth hour and did not ask what he was writing and was not thanked for the cup because nobody thanked anybody in that room.

What he was not doing with it was the part he had worked out before the third hour.

He was not giving it to the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room over the weigh-house, and the reason was not manners and was not the four hundred paces between two doors and was that a room with a press in it and a drawer about eleven inches deep is the only place in nine counties where a thing of nine hundred words could be kept, and the moment it is in that room it is in a county, and a county is a thing a paper is about.

He was not giving it to the copyist of about twenty-six, and he had thought about that one longer, because she was the person in nine counties who would know within a week that the third series could not carry it and would say so before anybody else did.

He was not giving it to the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head, and that one had a reason that took about nine seconds to work out and then stayed for the rest of the afternoon.

“**A man with a badge who carries nine hundred words is a head,**” he said, out loud, to a table, at about the fifth hour. “**He has said nine times that he will not make one and if he walks up a lane with this inside his coat then about nine people in nine counties have got a thing that arrives, and about four of them will come to him for it and about five will not, and in about four months there will be a version of this in which he wrote it.**”

About four people in that room heard a man of thirty-two say that about a man of fifty-eight and about five heard a man talking to a table, and nobody in that room told him that the badge-man would have said the same thing and nobody told him that he was wrong about the badge-man, and both of those were the correct arrangement on a Tuesday.

Here is the whole of it.

“**A FRONT, AND WHAT IT IS NOT. 1. A front is not an army and it will not be one, and the first thing it is not is a list, and the second thing it is not is a column, and anybody who joins one has agreed to those two things and to nothing else. 2. Nine counties and the seven roads have no standing whatever over one another and this front does not propose to give them any. It proposes to do one thing that none of them can do alone. 3. The sheet that is on nine sets of boards requires that any person asked which place or road is meant shall answer, and nine counties cannot answer it, and about nine people in nine counties have worked out in about a year that the reason is not that nobody will answer. It is that nobody has standing to answer, and standing is the whole of the difficulty.**”

He put the pen down at that point and read the first three clauses over again and did not change them, and four people in that room were looking at the table and about five were not, and about nine people in nine counties could have found a better second clause.

“**4. So the front asks. The seven roads are the ferry city, the county where a list of names was burned, the waterworks country, the coast of returning roads, the fruit valley, the glass country, and the small one. Each of them is asked by a person who keeps a crossing or keeps a road, and the question is one question and it is these nine words: which place or road is the sheet meant. It is asked out loud to a person who keeps a crossing. It is not written. 5. The front names no road. It gives no figure of men, horses or households. It puts nothing into any return, roll or sheet, and any man who wants to know what the front has decided will have to ask one of the nine people who was in a room.**”

He wrote the sixth clause and it took him the longest, and he got it right the first time, and it is the clause that is wrong.

“**6. For the length of the emergency, each of the seven roads accepts the authority of the Crown in the keeping of its own crossing, in writing, and the writing is held by the person named on the road's side and by nobody in this county. 7. And the acceptance may be ended by the person named on the road's side at any hour, without notice and without a reason given, and no front, hearing or county may ask for it to be continued. 8. What the front does if a road is asked which place is meant and cannot answer is nothing. It does not name. It does not guess. It does not fill a space in a book.**”

Then the ninth clause, which is the reason, and he wrote it last and he wrote it at about the ninth hour with his hand going and he did not change it afterwards.

“**9. And this is the only shape a thing like this can take. A road that has not put its name to a paper cannot be found. A person who is not on a paper cannot be compelled to answer, because compulsion in this county runs one way and it runs through the papers, and a road that is not on a paper is not in the taking at all, and a thing that is not in the taking cannot be argued with by anybody including the people in it. Nine counties has spent a year and a half finding out that it cannot be argued with. This is the other way round. Put a name on the road while the taking is running and the road is in it, and a road that is in it has to be argued with, and arguing is what a road is for.**”

He read the whole of it through once and it took about nine minutes and he did not change one word of it, and four people in that room could have told you that it was the best thing anybody in nine counties had written in about a year, and about five of them could not have told you anything about it.

It is competent. That is the whole of what can be said for it in its own favour and it is not nothing, because in nine counties there are about nine sentences a year that are competent and every one of them has a head over it.

Then he said the part that made it the plan anybody would actually carry, and he said it out loud to a room of about nine people at about the ninth hour, and he said it to nobody in particular.

“**Sixth and seventh. There is a name on each side of that and a signature on each side of that and no clause in about nine hands in nine counties has ever wanted a name on both sides of a thing, and the reason it does is that a person who can be found afterwards is the whole of what four and a half years of this county has been about.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that room had heard the shape of it and about five had heard the words.

Then the innkeeper of about twenty-nine, who had been standing at the far end of the room for about an hour with a tally-stick he was not using, said the question.

“**You are asking seven roads to put their names to a Crown paper, and you wrote that on a Tuesday in the only room in this town where nobody can hear you. Do you want it read out in a street?**”

Nobody thanked anybody and about four people in that room had understood what a man with a tally-stick had just asked and about five had heard a question about a Tuesday.

Then the innkeeper said his own want, which he had not said in about four years, and he said it to a table.

“**I keep a room where a person can sit for six hours and not be asked for anything. That is the whole of what this room is and it is why people come to it and not to the boards. I have had a Crown man drink in it twice in about four years and both times he was on somebody's business and both times about nine people in this room worked out in about nine seconds that a man had come in here to find somebody, and both times nobody gave him a name, and that is twice that a room full of about nine people has done a thing that a hearing with no bench in it could not do.**”

He put the tally-stick on the settle.

“**So if you are going to read nine hundred words out in that street on Saturday then about nine people will come in here afterwards and ask me what it was about, and I will not tell them, and about four of them will work out that I am the man who had it in my room first and about five will not, and either way I have been put in the middle of it. I am not refusing to keep my room. I am telling you what keeping my room costs.**”

Nobody thanked him for the cup and nobody thanked him for that and about four people in that room understood what an innkeeper of about twenty-nine had just said and about five heard a man talk about a room.

Aren Kest said that he had not thought of it and that the objection was better than anything he had put on the paper, and the innkeeper said that it was not an objection to the paper and did not want it treated as one.

“**I keep a room,**” he said. “**That is all I have said and I am not a road and I am not a crossing and I am not one of your seven and I would like it noticed that I am not.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people in that room had heard a man of about twenty-nine put himself outside nine hundred words and about five had heard him say he was not a road.

Aren Kest said that he did not, and he gave the reason in about nine words.

“**A thing read out in a street in this county gets carried to nine counties inside a week and about four people will understand it and about five will tell it wrong, and about four of the people who will tell it wrong are the same four every time, and one of them keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned and she is the best listener in nine counties and I am not going to hand her four hundred words on a Tuesday.**”

The innkeeper put the tally-stick in his pocket and said that he had not asked twice, and he said that out loud in the ordinary way, and about nine people in that room heard a man of about twenty-nine say out loud that he had asked once on purpose, and nobody in that room thanked him and nobody has said since that the inn was the right place for it.

Then the copyist of about twenty-six came in at about the ninth hour and stood at the end of the settle and did not sit down.

The room over the weigh-house was shut behind its own door, which it is on every day that is not a market morning, and she had come down the lane in the rain with nothing in her hands, and about four people in that room noticed that she had come down for nothing and about five did not.

“**I am not here about a book,**” she said. “**I am here because a woman of about thirty-eight left this market town on Monday morning up the made road and I went up the lane to the top of the rise to see whether anybody followed her, which nobody did, and I want to say one thing and then I am going to go back up the thirteen stairs.**”

“**Say it,**” Aren Kest said.

“**I keep three series and I have kept them for about four and a half years and they are good.**” She said it flatly, in the way she asks things. “**About nine hundred sheets a month going out, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month coming in, and a band on a fixed day that I do not alter whatever is in the county. A man in a livery can put a board on a gate in nine counties and not one line of it comes into my room, because he is not sending me a sheet, he is writing on a gate, and a return is a thing a room can be held to and a gate is not a return.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that room had understood all of it.

“**That is the thing you have just written nine hundred words about and you have not once put it in a series and you cannot,**” she said, “**because what has happened in nine counties since the middle of Longlight is not a record. It is a man asking a person a question at a gate and the person answering and the answer going into a book he carries in his coat. There is no head for that. I have had about four and a half years and nine questions in a book and no head over the fourth, and the fourth is the one for this, and I am not going to rule it and neither is anybody else.**”

“**I know,**” Aren Kest said.

“**Then say it out loud so that four people in this room have heard a man who has written nine hundred words admit that the room he writes in cannot hold them,**” she said.

He said it out loud, and about four people in that room heard it and about five did not, and nobody thanked her for coming down thirteen stairs in the rain to say it and nobody thanked him for saying it.

Then she looked at the nine hundred words on the table for about four seconds and said one more thing, and it was the only thing she said about the plan itself in that room or in a street or in a lane.

“**I do not know whether it is any good,**” she said. “**I have read it and I have counted the clauses and I have not got an argument against it and I have had about four and a half years to get an argument against a thing and I have never once had one that held. That is not the same as it being good and I am not going to say it in a street on Saturday to four hundred people, and if one of you nine does say it in a street then I want it noticed that I did not.**”

Then she went back up the thirteen stairs in the rain and nobody said anything to her on the lane, and about four people in that room watched her go and about five went back to what they were doing.

The nine hundred words lay on the settle until about the tenth hour, and a woman of about forty-one who kept a chandler's shop three doors down came in out of the wet and read about nine lines of it standing up and put a finger on the sixth clause and did not say anything and went out again into the wet.

Nobody thanked her and nobody stopped her and about nine people in nine counties know that a woman read nine lines of it and pointed at the sixth and said nothing, and about four of them know that she was in the street on the Saturday saying that the thing could not be asked.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is about nine hundred words that are the best thing anybody in nine counties has written in about a year, and about nine hundred words that ask seven roads to put a name on a paper, and a woman of about thirty-nine who came two days down a made road on foot in Rainmonth to ask the copyist one question and was told she did not know, and nobody in nine counties has been able to answer her since, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a tally stick at 9, a horn button at 18, a skin of glue at 19, a board of deal at 27, a cooper's hoop at 28, a bushel of lime at 30, a barrow wheel at 31, a hurdle at 32, a sheet of oiled paper at 35, and a bucket at 36.** Nine, eighteen, nineteen, twenty-seven, twenty-eight, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-five and thirty-six is two hundred and sixty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and sixty-five pence is five marks and twenty-five over.** He folded the nine hundred words in four along the folds he had made when it was still nine hundred words and put them inside his coat, and the innkeeper of about twenty-nine put the tally-stick on the settle where the paper had been and did not say anything about it, and nine people in that room had watched a man of thirty-two get up off a settle in about four seconds and then take about four seconds longer than that getting out of the door, which is the whole of what a permanent limp is and which nobody in that room mentioned.
