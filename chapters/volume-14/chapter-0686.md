# Chapter 0686

**Frostmonth the first, YR 321, a Monday and not a market morning and the first of a month, with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting because there is no market, the fourth hour in the rain at the market town's own gate where the water goes under the road into the channel, and nine buckets, a chain on a bar, a lamp, and a mason of about fifty who does not lay the fourth course.**

The first of a month that is not a market morning is a first of a month with nobody in the street, and nobody in nine counties has ever printed that anywhere, and the badge-man said it out loud on the eighth of Wolfmonth in a market street and four people heard him and five did not.

Nobody thanked him for saying it. On the first of Frostmonth the weigh-house began weighing at the seventh hour, which is the only business in that street, and a market is not a thing you can have without a day, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the whole arrangement of this county rests on a market and five have said since that it does not.

Nobody thanked him and nobody in that street stood in it, and there is nobody to have thanked, and four people who came down to the boards at the ninth hour out of habit stood in an empty market street and five did not come.

The water comes over the crown of the made road at the market town's own gate and goes under the road into the channel, and it has been coming over the crown since the eighth of Embermonth, which is the same week a printed list of about thirty counties went onto the boards four streets away, and nine people in nine counties have said that those two things have nothing to do with each other and five have said that they have everything to do with each other.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody has been able to establish which of those two is right and nobody has been able to establish whether anybody said it.

Under the road at the gate there is a culvert. It is a barrel of cut stone about four feet long and three feet wide, and it is the oldest thing in this market town that anybody knows the date of, and there are three courses of sill under the silt in it, and the middle course of the three is gone.

That is the whole of what is wrong. The middle course went out at some point in about four years and nobody can say when and nobody has looked, and when a middle course goes out of a sill the water finds the gap and goes round the rest of it and under the road in a hole it has made for itself, and a hole under a made road is not a hole in the road, it is a hole under about nine feet of made road, and there is no hole to put a hand in.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in that market town could have told you that from the gate without being told and five could not, and a mason of about fifty who lays stone at that gate could tell you in about nine seconds.

Then there is the stone for a fourth course. It is cut and it is dressed and it is the right shape, and it is lying in a line on the bank at the high side of the cut where anybody coming in at the gate can see it, and the mallet is on top of it, and it has been on top of it since the middle of Rainmonth, and four hundred people have gone past it and two hundred of them have not seen it and nine of them have.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody laid it and nobody picked the mallet up and nobody in nine counties has scheduled picking it up, and that is the state of it this first of a month and it is going to be the state of it for the rest of this month, and there is no day on which anybody is going to say the word.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people in this county know the arrangement and five have worked out the arrangement on their own, which is that a thing that is visible and not done is a different thing from a thing that is not done, and four people in nine counties have said that the mallet is the worst object in this market town and five have said that it is a mallet.

The rain came in from the west at about the ninth hour and it did not stop until about the fourth hour of the afternoon, and it was the kind that gets into a coat at the shoulder and stays there.

Nine people turned out at the cut and nobody ordered it. It is not a contract, it is not the market town's business, and there is nobody whose job it is.

There was a man of about fifty who lays stone at the market town's own gate, and he had not been asked, and he came because there was a boy of about seventeen on the bucket end and because the water was coming over the crown of a made road at the town's own gate and a road that runs under water at the gate is a road that has stopped being a road.

Nobody thanked him and four people at the cut had seen him come and five had not, and he has laid stone at that gate for about twenty-six years and nobody in nine counties pays him for any of it and he has never asked.

There was a crew of nine men off a yard four hundred yards up the made road, working forward under a contract taken out of an advance party, and the yard owns them, and the yard was loading timber off a waggon at nine hundred yards and nine men in it knew there was a cut at the gate.

Nobody thanked the yard and four people in nine counties have said that a yard that takes its contract out of an advance party is a yard that is inside the thing it is building a road for, and five have said that a contract is a contract and the men were paid on Friday.

There was a man of about sixty-two with a wain and a team of four who came at about the third hour because a wain is the only thing in nine counties that can take nine buckets of wet stone off a made road and put it somewhere, and he came with three of his four horses and did not say why.

Nobody thanked him and four people at that cut had counted his horses and five had counted them and not thought about the fourth, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a team of four is a team of four and four have said that a man with a wain who brings three is a man who is keeping one somewhere.

There was a woman of about thirty-eight with a bar and nine buckets, and a man of about fifty-one who owns the field on the low side of a cut a mile and a half off, and a man of about thirty with a bar, and a boy of about thirteen, and nine households from the street who turned out because the water was going over the crown at their own gate and nobody had asked them to.

Nobody thanked any of them and four people at the cut had worked out that the nine households were the nine households whose gates are inside the nine hundred yards of made road that drains into that cut, and five had not.

And there was a boy of about seventeen on the bucket end, and he was there because his father works in a yard two streets up the market street and had told him to go and see whether there was anything to be done, and the boy had gone and there was.

Nobody thanked him and nobody thanked his father and four people in that market town have said that a boy of about seventeen is the only person in nine counties who gets sent to find out whether there is work, and five have said that he was the only one who went and looked.

The work was the silt and the middle course of the sill.

There were about nine feet of it and it had come out of the channel in the summer and gone hard, and two inches on top of it was a lime crust gone hard in a night, and it had to come off in pieces and not in a mass, because a mass of nine feet of wet stone at the wrong end of a bar will put a man in the channel and the channel is about four feet deep and it is fed from above.

Nobody thanked anybody and four people at that cut knew that and five had been told it by the mason once and never twice.

A chain on a bar went through the mouth of the cut, and the bar was about five feet of ash and about four inches through, and the chain was about thirty feet and it had been on the barrow drain a mile and a half off about four years ago and had been kept for that.

Nobody thanked anybody and about four people at the cut took the chain in turns and about five did not, and nobody arranged the turns and the turns arranged themselves in about nine minutes the way they do, and two of the nine could not get both hands on the bar at the first pull and one of those two was a man of about fifty who lays stone and neither of them said anything about it.

The mason said one thing about it at about the fourth hour, in the ordinary way, and about nine people at the cut heard it and about four of them agreed with it and about five thought he was showing off.

“**You get your arms in from the side and not from the top,**” he said, “**and when the bar comes back it comes back to your hip and not to your chest, and a man who pulls it to his chest is a man who will be in the channel by the fourth hour and he will not be able to say how.**”

Nobody thanked him and the man of about fifty tried it twice and got it right on the second time and said nothing, and about four people at that cut watched a man of fifty learn something from a man of fifty in front of about nine people and about five watched a man pull a bar.

The barrow went up the bank nine paces and tipped, and the boy of about seventeen was on the bucket end, and he counted the trips out loud because counting is what a boy does when he has nothing else to do with an afternoon, and he counted in fives, and he got to about seventy and lost his place twice and started again from nothing both times, and about four people at that cut noticed that he had started again and about five did not care.

Nobody thanked the boy of about seventeen and nobody thanked the man of about sixty-two with the wain, who took the load down the made road about four hundred yards and tipped it and came back, and did it about nine times an hour in the rain, and about four people at the cut counted his turns and about five stopped counting after the fourth hour.

Aren Kest came down to the cut at about the fifth hour on his own account and carried buckets from the high side to the low side for about two hours, and the boy of about seventeen counted him as one of the nine and did not count him separately, and nobody thanked him for the two hours and the man of about fifty with the wain did not offer him a place on the wain.

Nobody thanked him and four people at that cut had worked out in four seconds that the man with the stick was the man who wrote nine hundred words, and about five had not, and the mason of about fifty said one thing to him at about the sixth hour, in the ordinary way, and nine people at the cut heard it.

“**You will go over that bank wrong on the way up and wrong on the way down and there are nine paces of it,**” the mason said. “**Put your left hand on the rope and go up backwards. It is slower and it is the only way that a man with a bad leg does not put a foot into a wet cut in front of about nine people.**”

Nobody thanked him and Aren Kest went up the bank backwards with his left hand on the rope for about an hour afterwards, and four people at that cut watched a man of thirty-two take a mason's advice about a bank and about five watched a man go up a bank slowly.

Then the lamp was lit at about the fourth hour because it was the first of a month and the light goes at about half past three in Frostmonth, and the lamp was a tin lamp with a candle in it and about four inches of candle left, and a man of about thirty with a bar held it above the cut with his arm up for about an hour and a half and nobody relieved him and nobody thanked him, and four people at that cut watched a man hold a lamp over a hole in a made road in the rain and about five watched the light.

Nobody thanked him and the wain went down with the ninth load at about the fifth hour and about four people at the cut knew that the ninth load was the last of the nine feet and about five knew it because the boy of about seventeen said it out loud.

Nobody thanked anybody and the mason got his arm down into the cut at about the fifth hour and put a lamp in there with him on a stick, and looked at the sill for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and then came out of the water and said the true thing about his own trade, out loud, to about nine people, in the rain.

“**The middle course is gone. Not cracked. Gone. There is a hole in the middle of three courses the size of my two hands and the water goes through it and it has been going through it since about the middle of Harvestmonth and every week it has gone through it the hole has got a little wider and nobody has been under this road since the spring.**”

Nobody thanked him and about four people at that cut had heard a mason say that a hole under a made road has been getting wider for about eleven weeks and five had heard a man describe his own work in the rain.

“**And I am not laying the fourth course,**” he said. “**I want that said out loud today in front of nine people, because in about nine days somebody at these boards is going to say that a mason of about fifty has been at this cut for a whole day in the rain and has not laid a stone, and I am not going to let that be said without the other half of it. The fourth course wants the middle one back first. There is nothing to lay a fourth course on. That is not a reason and it is not a law, it is a mason telling nine people what is under his hand, and it is the only thing I have ever been able to say that anybody has ever written down.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at that cut had heard a man refuse to be praised for a thing he had not done and five had heard a mason say that he is not a law.

Then a man of about thirty-three who lives in a street off the market street asked the question that nine people at that cut had been asking all afternoon, and he asked it out loud, in the rain, and it is the question nobody in nine counties has an answer to.

“**Then when?**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody answered him and the mason of about fifty said one word, and it was not a date, and four people at that cut heard it and five did not.

“**When somebody pays for it.**”

Nobody thanked him and two hundred people have not gone past that gate in the rain and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man of fifty-one who owns a field is the only person in this county who could pay for a fourth course of stone and has not offered, and five have said that a man who owns a field is not a person the market town asks, and neither of those sets has ever spoken to the other.

The water found the clear part of the cut inside about a minute and a half of the last of the lime crust coming out, and it went under the road into the channel on its own, and four people at that cut saw it go and five had their eyes shut against the rain.

Nobody thanked anybody and the water went under the road and the road stayed where it was and four people in that market town would say afterwards that the gate had not moved and five would say that the water had gone under it, and both of those are true and one of them is the whole of what a road is.

The lamp went out at about the sixth hour and the crew knocked off at about the seventh and the rain stopped at about the seventh, and nine people stood at the cut in the wet and did not go home for about as long as it takes to turn a cart.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of about sixty-two with the wain took his three horses down the made road toward the ferry stone, and four people at that cut watched a wain go off the high side with three of a team and five watched it go.

Then the boy of about seventeen said the thing that this block of the county's year was for, and he said it out loud, in the wet, to the mason and not to anybody else, and nine people at that cut heard it and four of them understood why he said it and five thought he was a boy.

“**I am not going to be a mason.**”

Nobody thanked him and the mason of about fifty said that a boy of about seventeen is not going to be a mason, and he said why, in his own trade, and it took him about four seconds.

“**A mason wants about nine feet of arm and you have got about four and a half, and it will not grow. I could not do this at seventeen and I did not want to at seventeen and I have wanted to every day since about the age you are now. That is the whole of it and it is the only advice I am able to give anybody.**”

Nobody thanked him and four people at that cut had heard a man of fifty say that a trade is a thing a person wants and not a thing a person is given, and five had heard a mason being kind, and the boy of about seventeen said out loud that his father kept a yard two streets up and that a carter came out of the market town on Tuesdays for the sand, and the mason said that was a different thing and that the boy would want to be in it by Thursday, and the boy of about seventeen said he would be at the sand yard on Thursday, and nobody thanked him and nobody said he would not be.

Nobody thanked anybody and the nine buckets were stacked on the high side of the cut and the chain was coiled on the bar and the bar was in the bank, and the stone for a fourth course was lying in a line on the bank where anybody coming in at that gate can see it and the mallet was on top of it where it has been since the middle of Rainmonth, and nobody picked the mallet up and nobody said when and nobody in nine counties is going to say.

**And the cost of that first of a month is nine feet of silt out of a cut at the market town's own gate, a hole in the middle course of a sill that has been getting wider for about eleven weeks, and a boy of about seventeen who has decided to be a carter instead of a mason, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a chain link at 15, a lamp of tallow at 24, a bushel of sand at 25, a barrow wheel at 27, a coil of rope at 29, a hand adze at 31, a hod at 34, a scythe stone at 35, a pick at 36, and a barrow at 39.** Fifteen, twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-seven, twenty-nine, thirty-one, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-six and thirty-nine is two hundred and ninety-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and ninety-five pence is six marks and seven over.** The boy of about seventeen went up the lane at about the seventh hour to the sand yard and stood at the gate of it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart and did not knock and then knocked, and the man who keeps that yard said out loud that he was short of a man for Thursdays and not for anything else, and the boy of about seventeen said Thursday, and four people in that market town heard about that exchange afterwards and five did not.
