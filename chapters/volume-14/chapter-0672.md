# Chapter 0672

**Fallowmonth the twenty-fourth, YR 321, a Thursday, the second hour at the top of the made road four miles out of the market town where four bridge crews are working forward, a woman of about thirty-eight on her fourth day of carting on a road that is somebody else's working ground, and about nine seconds at a gate that is lawful.**

She came out of the market town on the Monday morning with a cart and one carter and about nine people watching her go, and four of those nine understood where she was going and about five did not.

The five were not stupid and they were not wrong. She had told all nine of them the same thing, which was that she was going four days up the made road to ask a question. About four people in that market town know where the waterworks country is. About five know that there is a four-day made road going down to a coast and that the woman had come up it in Greenmonth on a cart with a boy of about nine and a woman of about sixty, and that going back up it was the thing anybody would have expected, and going the other way was not a thing anybody had a shape for.

Nobody thanked her and nobody said she would manage, and the badge-man had said the true thing about it, which was that he was not going to ask her anything and was not going to stop her, and about four people at the boards on the Saturday had understood that and about five had heard a man being careful.

The carter was a man of about forty-one and he took the job for two reasons and one of them was money and the other one was that nobody else in that market town would take it. He had a cart with a good off-side wheel on the Monday. He has wanted nine days of paid work with no cart at the end of it since Rainmonth, because a cart left four miles up a made road is a fortnight of looking for it, and he said so at the boards in about nine words and about four people heard the sense of it and about five heard a carter asking for more money.

“**I am not saying I will not go,**” he said. “**I am saying I want the cart back and I am saying it now instead of on the fourth day in a lane four hundred miles from here with a man I have never seen.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody said he had been sensible, and the woman of about thirty-eight said that she had heard him and that the cart would come back, and about four people in that market street understood that she had made a promise she had no standing to make and about five understood a carter.

The made road is two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand out of the market town and then it is a made road, and it goes up through about eleven houses and past nine hundred people and on toward the ferry stone and the coast road. Four bridge crews are working forward on it. They are not the column. They are a yard's men on a contract taken out of the advance party, and the yard owns the crews and is four hundred yards up the road with timber off a waggon, and about nine men in this county have been putting sills into culverts since the middle of Rainmonth and every one of those nine men is working ahead of everybody else, which is the whole of what a bridge crew is.

By the Thursday she was four miles out and the crew had gone on past her in the night, and what was in front of her on the made road at about the second hour was nine feet of cut stone lying across the crown of it with a chain round it and about four men standing at the four corners of it in the manner of men who have been told to wait for a cart.

They had a cart. The problem was that the cart was in a gateway nine hundred yards back and the cart was hers.

The man who came out to meet her was thirty and in a livery this county had not seen since the middle of Rainmonth, and he came up the high side because the low side had the stone on it, and he stopped nine paces off and did not come closer, and about four people who were within seeing understood the whole of what was about to happen and about five did not.

“**Are you the cart.**”

“**I am the cart,**” she said.

“**I am going to tell you why I need it and I am going to tell you the whole of why, and then I am going to ask you, and I would rather you heard all of it before you answered than heard it afterwards.**”

Nobody said anything and about four men of that crew looked at her and about five did not.

“**That stone came out of a culvert head on the fourteenth and it is going into a culvert head about a mile on, and it is nine feet and it weighs about two tons and it is on the crown of a made road, and a made road with two tons on the crown of it is not a made road, it is a place where a cart puts a wheel through, and if a cart puts a wheel through here on Friday then somebody who is not me will be standing in this lane with a thing he cannot get out of.**”

“**Say the part about the sheet,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said.

The man of thirty said the part about the sheet, and he said it in about two hundred words, and he said it well, and he was not a knave and nobody in that lane has ever said he was.

“**The sheet that is on the boards in your town says that all and every the persons, horses, carts, goods and labour of the district are required to give such aid to the officers of the Crown as those officers shall require, at such times and in such manner as the officers shall set out. That is one of three things on it and it is the one I am working from and it is the only one of the three that has ever moved anything in this county, because it does not want anybody to propose anything and it does not want anybody afterwards. It wants a person to be asked. That is me, at your gate, asking.**”

“**You have not asked yet,**” she said.

“**I have not asked yet, and I am going to, and here is the whole of what I have, so that you know what you are saying no to.**” He turned the small book over in his hand and did not open it. “**I have got a page for this gate and the page says the county and the long field on the high side and the two thorn trees and the day, and it does not say your name because I have not got your name. It says that I asked which place or road is meant and that the person at the gate said she had not been told. I write down what was said and not what was meant, because I was taught that about nine times and I have got it right about nine times out of nine.**”

“**And after I say no,**” she said, “**what goes in the book?**”

“**What you say goes in the book, and I will read it back to you before I shut it, and I will ask you whether you want to hear it, which I ask everybody, and it is not a form.**”

Nobody said anything at that and four people in that lane understood it and about five did not.

“**And then I say yes,**” he said, “**and I move your cart out of your own gateway with my own four men, and the stone goes into that cart and goes a mile and comes out of it, and by the fourth hour your gateway is where your gateway was and your horse can go out of it, and no man in this county has shut anything and no road has been shut and there is nothing on any board anywhere about any of this.**”

“**Say the part about it being from the hour of the sealing,**” she said.

He said that part too, in nine words, and he said it without looking at the book.

“**From the hour of the sealing of it, which is eleven weeks ago, so you have been in it since before the leaves, and nobody asked you and I am not asking you now because I am asking you now, and those are two different things and both of them are true.**”

Then he asked her, and he asked her in the ordinary way, and nine seconds went by, and four people in that lane counted them and about five did not.

“**Will you take the stone.**”

“**I will take the stone,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said.

She said it in about nine seconds and she did not hesitate and she was not thinking about it, and the reason she did not hesitate is the whole of that morning and about four people in that lane worked it out inside four seconds and about five of them worked it out about an hour later when they were told.

Nobody thanked anybody and four men of that crew got the cart and the other five stood at the four corners of the stone, and the work took two hours and it was done properly and nobody gave the man of thirty any trouble at all.

That is the whole of what happened at that gate, and it took about nine seconds, and it was lawful, and nothing was shut.

The Tuesday had been the hard day and it had nothing to do with anybody.

There is a gate on that made road about nine hundred yards past the ferry stone where the lane comes up off the low ground, and it is kept by a house that has kept it for about nine years, and on the Tuesday morning there was a board pegged on the inside of it. The board is about a foot and a half long and it is under the drip line and out of the way of a bar, and it is held on by two pegs, and on it in chalk in an upright hand that is the same size in every letter is the county, the gate described by the long field on the high side and the lower pasture and the two thorn trees, the day, a man's own name in full, and one line saying that he asked which place or road was meant and that the person at the gate said she had not been told.

The woman of about thirty-eight stood at the low side of it for about as long as it takes a carter to get a load round and did not touch it and did not go up to the house.

“**How many of these are there,**” she said.

“**Forty-one on this made road,**” the carter of about forty-one said. He had been counting them since the Monday because he had nothing else to do with his eyes for nine days. “**He told a man in a livery in the second county that he would have them all by the middle of Frostmonth and that he writes about forty words to a gate and that another man in another county writes about eleven, and he said that out loud to nine people in a lane in the ordinary way, as though he were talking about a load he had carried.**”

Nobody said anything to that and the woman of about thirty-eight looked at about nine feet of chalk writing on a board about a foot and a half long and four people who were within seeing understood all of it and about five understood that a man had written something down at a gate.

“**There is one of these at every gate on this road between here and the waterworks country,**” the carter said. “**I have been past nine of them since Monday and I have seen nine houses open their gates faster for a man in a livery than they have for me, and I did not work that out until the third one.**”

She got the cart up the lane and did not say anything about it, and four people in that lane have said since that a woman stood at a board on the inside of a gate and did not go up to the house, and about five have not been able to say why.

On the Wednesday night they slept in a barn with a stone floor about a mile on, and there were about nine other people in it, and none of them asked her anything and she asked nobody anything, which is the ordinary arrangement on a made road. One of the nine was a man of about fifty-seven who had a basket and a stick and who said out loud, in the ordinary way, to nobody, that he had come down from the waterworks country and was going on to a market and had nine days of road in front of him and had done it forty times.

“**What do you carry,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said.

“**Nothing that is anybody's,**” the man said. “**And I would advise you to ask that question in that order more often than you do.**”

About four people in that barn laughed and the other five did not, and the carter of about forty-one said afterwards that he had been the one who worked it out, on the Thursday morning, about four miles down the road, when he watched a woman hand her own cart to a man in a livery in about nine seconds without stopping to think.

The crew got the stone into the cart and got the cart round on the made road and went a mile with it and put it into a culvert head, and then the man of thirty came back up the road on foot with his book shut in his coat and no money, because nobody had mentioned money and he was not the sort of man who mentions it when there is a sheet behind him.

The woman of about thirty-eight went up the made road after that with her cart and about nine days of road in front of her and nine words in her mouth that she had said to nine words of nine people in a market street on the Saturday, and she got about four hundred yards up the road and then stopped and stood in the middle of the made road for about as long as it takes a carter to get a load round.

Nobody saw her stop. There was nobody there.

What she was doing was not turning a thing over. She had turned a thing over for nine days on a cart and nine days in a barn before that and she had worked out nine words that a man in a livery could not write down as consent, and she had said the nine words out loud on the Saturday in front of about two hundred people, and about four of them had noticed and about five had not.

And then she had gone and got into her own gateway and been asked for her own cart by a man with a small book who had said in about two hundred words that the whole of what he had was a paper, and she had said yes in about nine seconds without stopping, and she had said yes to a man doing a thing she had said out loud in a market street four days before, and she had said yes lawfully, and nothing had been shut, and she had said yes in nine seconds.

The whole of what she had wanted was nine words that asked for nothing.

She said the nine words out loud in the middle of a made road at about the fourth hour on the Thursday, to nobody, once, and then she got back up on the cart and went on up the road, and about four hundred yards up the road a yard was loading timber off a waggon and nine men in it had not heard about any of it.

Nobody in nine counties had sent her and nine people had watched her go and about five of them had not known where she was going, and the reason she gave them was that she had asked a question eleven days on a cart and had not been answered it, and that was the whole of the reason and it was not enough and she went anyway.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a gateway with about nine feet of cut stone standing in it for two hours in the morning of a day in Fallowmonth, and a woman of about thirty-eight who said yes in about nine seconds to a thing she had said out loud four days before in a market street, and nine days of made road in front of her and no head in nine counties to send her, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a cake of soap at 2, a cake of ink at 7, a boot brush at 25, a scythe stone at 27, a shoe sole at 28, a pound of nails at 30, a pail at 31, a creel at 32, a pound of wool at 35, and a trunk at 40.** Two, seven, twenty-five, twenty-seven, twenty-eight, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-five and forty is two hundred and fifty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and fifty-seven pence is five marks and seventeen over.** She got the cart back up the made road at about the ninth hour on the Thursday and put it on the high side at the top of a rise above the ferry stone, and when she looked at it the off-side wheel had a crack in it about the length of her hand that had not been there on the Monday, and she stood and looked at it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and about nine hundred yards on the road a crew was loading timber off a waggon and none of the nine men in it had been near her cart.
