**Frostmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it, a Crown's cart in the ordinary post with a printed sheet in it that the man driving it does not know what it is, and the first day of this, and the practice not running on it because the room is open.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts along both sides and the boards at the low end standing with a printed notice on them under three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on the frame of them. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house came off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of the last market morning before Hearthmonth, and the ordinary part is that about nine hundred people buy what they meant to buy and about nine hundred buy a second thing they had not meant to and about nine hundred go home the way they came.

A drove went up that lane four days off on that same Thursday, the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up, and four have said since that ninety is one more than the figure this county said out loud six days ago, and that about a year of a figure going up by one every Thursday is a thing nobody in nine counties has ever counted before.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for six hundred and twenty-four days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and has not been picked up, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The practice did not run on that morning and the number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is two hundred and fifty-eight mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 323 and two hundred and fifty-two of them were due on a day this room is shut, and six went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and he is one of the nine and has been one of the nine for about a year. Eight of the ten things have been held on every morning but two since the first of Wolfmonth, and the two that have not are the ninth of the ten and the tenth. One of them is the head of a cart track with nine hurdles across it that nine people at the end of it put there and would like to stay, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth YR 322, which is three hundred and forty-three days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is three hundred and twenty-four days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is three hundred and ninety-one days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that three hundred and ninety-one days is a year and about a month and that a year and about a month is not going to close over anything, and five have said that about four and a half acres of standing grass is a quantity and not a wound, and that about nine feet of it across is the width of the made road and the width of the broom and the length of the gap in that wall, and that none of the three has been closed.

On those boards there is now a printed notice in two sentences and three copies of a roll nailed one over the other, and the notice asks for the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is two hundred and sixty-five days past and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that two hundred and sixty-five days is very nearly nine months, and five had not, and four have said that about three months of asking is the most that is left in it, and that a notice that long past its date has shut nothing and has asked nothing.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four have said since that a sheet with a date on it about nine weeks off is the first paper out of that office anybody in this county has been glad to see, and five have said that being glad of it is not the same as being asked.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it was a foot wide and half an inch deep and had filled with grit. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and about four have said that about nine hundred names cut by people who never met is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep and that a hundred and forty-four days of a roll with nothing on it has still not made that stone into an answerable thing.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times and does not touch a frame has said no three times without speaking, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and four have said that five frames under about nine skirts going into the first market morning of Hearthmonth is a trade and not a finding.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is not in any document this county keeps.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four people in nine counties had seen the thumb-drag across the end of the top line and read it for a moment as the first stroke of a letter, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it, and about four hundred people went past those four lines that morning without looking at them.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households. Four people in nine counties have said to each other that nine households on that mile are a set of people with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that a form has one line for a county and nine households have nine names between them.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is three hundred and ninety-one days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and forty-four days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down. Inside the covering paper folded over the back of the second copy there is a sheet of paper folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and it says that nobody in this county is paid to keep a road, and that a form a county cannot fill is not to be filled by writing somebody else's name on it, and that a county which will not govern ought to say so in writing to the people it is not governing, which is about four lines on one folded sheet and about nine months old. Nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked and nobody has offered to say.

Then at about the ninth hour of that Thursday a Crown's cart came up that made road in the ordinary post and stopped at the boards at the low end, and the man who drove it got down and had a folded sheet out of the seat box and did not know what was in it and neither did the man on the box with him. About four people in that street had seen that cart go up about five times in about a year and five had not, and about four have said that a cart in the ordinary post is the only kind of thing in this county that arrives twice.

The sheet is one page and it is not the roll and it is not a copy of the roll and it does not go over anything. It asks for the name of one person to stand against the roads of nine counties, and it says under the Marshal's hand in two sentences that the place nine days ride off is being held so that what is called the Sundering is not done again in this world, and at the foot of it there is a date that is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth, which is about nine weeks off the day it went up. The man who drove it nailed it to the frame of those boards beside the three copies of the roll and not over them, with four nails, and did not take anything down.

Four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that it is the best offer anybody here has ever been made, and five had not.

“**It is the best offer anybody here has ever been made.**”

About nine people were standing close enough to hear that and about four hundred people in that street were not close enough to hear anything, and nobody argued with the four people about any part of it, and one of them said it out loud once in his own trade to about nine people and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road did not hear a syllable of it. Here means this county and not the nine of them, and the reason is that a person standing in a market street in this county is the only kind of person who can honestly have heard every offer this county has ever been made, and five people in nine counties have said that about nine hundred people along that made road have not heard this one either.

The count of things on that board is five, and it was four yesterday. The count of questions on it is three, and it was two. The first is the notice's own, and it wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties, and it is three hundred and six days past its date. The second is the roll's own, and it has nine lines and three copies of them and the fourth of the nine is this county's and every one of the nine is empty. The third is the sheet's, and it wants the name of one person and it has a date about nine weeks off it. Four people in nine counties have said since that five things on one board is not a board with one question on it and that three questions that do not agree with one another is not a decision and is not a refusal, and five have said that a board is a board.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour and had stood at the edge of about nine people and had read the sheet twice and had not touched any of the three copies of the roll and had not touched the sheet either. That was the whole of what he came down for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to see what a year of refusing two papers had produced on a board, and a third thing had gone up on that board in the ordinary post while he was standing at it.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and two hours standing at the edge of about nine people and had a hot band across the knee by about the eleventh hour. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And in fifty-one days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document and has never been said out loud in a room, and that was on his hand all through the two hours he stood at those boards.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Frostmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over that weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who has run two hundred and fifty-two mornings of a number said twice in the dark has not run one on the day a sheet went up beside his roll, and that about the not running one is the finding and the sheet is only paper.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who stands at a board and does not write on it is a man nobody thanks. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road had heard nothing about a sheet that had gone up at the low end of his market street that morning.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one is gone to powder, and the ninth thing was not dead and was not open and had no name against it, and there were five things on that board and the fourth line of the roll was still empty on all three of its copies.
