# Chapter 0801

**Frostmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with four hundred-odd people in that street, a Crown's cart in the ordinary post with a printed sheet in it that the man driving it does not know what it is, and the practice not running because the room is open.**

The street was full by the ninth hour. Carts stood along both sides with their shafts up and the shafts knocking in the frost, and the noise of it was the ordinary noise of a Thursday, which is to say no noise at all that anybody has chosen. The boards at the low end stood with a printed notice on them under three copies of a roll, nailed one over the other on the frame.

The bolt came off the room over the weigh-house at first light. The copyist of twenty-six went up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

Nine doors down, the cloth was still over the bench. Four people in that street had stopped seeing it. Five had never seen it. A woman carrying a skep of apples went past it without her step changing, and he noticed that she did not, and he noticed himself noticing, and he thought that a man who has started noticing which nine doors he is not looking at has nothing else left to do with a morning.

Then the cart came up.

He heard it before he saw it, at the far end of the made street, the ordinary post going by at the pace the post goes by, no faster than a loaded cart with one driver and a boy on the box. It stopped at the boards. The man got down. He had a folded sheet out of the seat box and he did not know what was in it, and neither did the boy on the box, and neither did the woman at the third door who came out to ask what he was doing.

The man read the address on it, found the boards, and nailed it to the frame. Beside the three copies of the roll. Not over them. Four nails, one in each corner, and he did not take anything down.

Four people had seen the cart turn off the made road. Five had not.

The sheet was one page. It was not the roll and it was not a copy of the roll and it did not cover anything. It asked for the name of one person to stand against the roads of nine counties. Under the Marshal's hand, in two sentences, it said that the place nine days ride off was being held so that what is called the Sundering would not be done again in this world. At the foot of it there was a date.

The twenty-ninth of Goatmonth. Nine weeks off, less a day.

A wright put down his brace and read it. He read it again with his mouth slightly open and then said it out loud, once, in his own trade, to the four people nearest him.

“**It is the best offer anybody here has ever been made.**”

Nobody argued with him. A woman selling nothing in particular from a trestle asked him what he meant by that and he said he meant what he said, and she turned away, and a carter's mate said his father got the offer of a job at the causeway once and turned it down, and the wright said that was not the same thing, and the mate said it was not, and they left it there.

It was the eleventh hour before the man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane.

He came down it slowly and he came down it on the left leg, the one that has been worse every year since he was twenty-six, and he carried a stick in his right hand and put a great deal of weight on it and none at all on the rail. Four hundred paces of a lane and two hours standing at the edge of nine people. There is a hot band across the knee by the eleventh hour that does not go away until he has been off it a full night, and on a morning like this one it is there by the tenth.

He had come down to see what a year of refusing two papers had put on a board.

A third thing had gone up on that board in the ordinary post while he was standing at it, and the driver had taken nothing down.

He read the sheet twice. He did not touch it. He did not touch any of the three copies of the roll either. The notice above them wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and the first of Mudmonth was two hundred and sixty-five days ago, and no road has been shut anywhere in this county since.

So the frame carried five things. A notice past its date, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them, and this. And the questions on it did not agree with one another. The notice wanted one name for all nine counties. The roll wanted a name against each of nine lines and had three copies of it for no reason anybody could give him, and the fourth of those nine lines is this county's. The sheet wanted one name, different from the first, and wanted it inside nine weeks.

He stood there and he worked out that there were three questions on that frame and not one answer, and that he knew the answer to none of the three.

He had a name available. That was the thing he understood while he was standing at it, and he understood it the way you understand a step you have already taken in the dark. There were people in that street who would give him their name if he asked them for it in the right trade, because they had been giving him things all morning without being asked. He could have had four names in an hour. He could have had a name for the sheet before the frost came off the shafts.

He did not ask. There was no moment at which he decided not to ask. He simply found, when he turned round at the tenth hour, that his mouth had stayed shut the whole time, and that the reason was not modesty and not the leg, and that the reason was that a name taken off a person standing in a market street and written on a line is a thing that person cannot get back. He had spent four and a half years in this county learning where a figure comes from, and he had learned the other half of it too, which is that a figure given to you by somebody else is not yours to hand on.

The ear on the side the stick is on is gone. It went in the water with everything else. It is the side she spoke from and it will always be the side she spoke from. On a market morning with four hundred people in that street he is a man you can stand beside and say a whole sentence to and never know whether he got it, and he has made a trade out of that, and the trade is a bad one.

There was also, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed that had been going fifty-one days. It had not faded by anything he had done to it, including the two things he had tried. It was in no document this county keeps. He had never said it out loud in a room in front of one other person, and there was no panel for it and no explanation and no page of its own, and he had a rule about pages of his own and he was keeping it.

A carter came up to him while he was still at the boards and put the question that everybody in that street had been carrying since Friday.

“Will you put a name to it?”

He looked at the man. The man was forty or so and had a horse's head on his hands and he was asking it the way you ask for a light.

“No.”

“You have not thought about it.”

“I have thought about it since the cart turned off the made road and I have been at this frame an hour and a half and I have not written anything on it, and if I had an answer I would not have asked you what your name was.”

The carter took that better than it could have gone. He said that his cousin had a name and would have put it up on Friday, and that the cousin was in the fen county and had never seen this street, and that this was the trouble with nine counties and one line between them.

“Your cousin is not here,” said the man of thirty-two. “That is the whole of it. If he were here I would ask him. A line with a name on it is a name somebody put there on a morning, and if that morning was a hundred miles away then it is not this county's name and it is not this county's road.”

“And if the morning is this morning?”

“Then it is this county's name, and I will not write it, and both of those are mine to decide and neither of them is yours.”

The carter went off up the street with his hands still full. Nothing about the sheet had changed and nobody had been thanked and nobody was going to be.

The rest of that Thursday went on without him and would have gone on if he had never come down the lane at all.

The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of the street, and the carter got it up again with a bar in the time it takes to turn a cart twice over, and no one stopped on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day. Nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road bought what they meant to buy and went home the way they came.

He had said nothing out loud about the sheet and he had answered nobody. He was not thanked for that, and nobody would have thanked him for it, and the not being thanked was the ordinary condition of the job and not a cost.

Four days out along that made road, a drove went up the lane on that same Thursday at the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety drove since the second of Mudmonth. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in. The gap is nine feet long and four feet high and the stone comes from a quarry two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for six hundred and twenty-four days. The ninth yard has not been started. The mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and has not been picked up, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will, and he has not said.

At the head of a cart track four hundred yards off there are nine hurdles across it that nine people put there and would like to keep there. The last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth, three hundred and forty-three days ago. Nobody has been up it since the second of Goatmonth, three hundred and twenty-four days. He did not go up it.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones at the end of that track, on a flat where nine households graze beasts on a rota they have kept for nine years, there are nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width since the twenty-second of Mudmonth of two years back. It is three hundred and ninety-one days old. It is dead inside and standing outside and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed. Nobody has ever been past the ninth of the nine stones. The far side of them has never been shown to anyone.

He did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone either. The ninth mile stone is the height of a knee and somebody had cleaned round the foot of it with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, and the ring that cleaning leaves is a foot wide and half an inch deep, and it had already taken grit into it again.

Four hundred yards past it, the eleventh mile stone has nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back two hundred years. Nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them. That is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep.

Nine doors down the cloth was still over the bench.

On the sill of the weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth. Four people in nine counties have seen the thumb-drag across the end of the top line and read it for a moment as the first stroke of a letter. Nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody. The stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and he has never asked her for it. Four hundred people went past those four lines that morning without looking down at them.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of the boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Frostmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of the people at the boards went out into five trades. Four stayed.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway. The hand bell on the gate post is the size of a fist and was not rung. Four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

He went out of that market street at the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and the stick in his right hand. Five things were on that board. The fourth line of the roll was still empty on all three of its copies, and it was not filled in, and it was not taken down, and it was not torn up.

**And the cost of that Thursday is five things on one frame, three questions on it that do not agree with one another, a date nine weeks off it, one man saying eleven words out loud to four people and nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road not hearing a syllable of it, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them, fifty-one days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that nobody in nine counties knows about, a practice run for two hundred and fifty-two mornings and not run at all on this one and written down nowhere, one thing gone quiet three hundred and forty-three days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
