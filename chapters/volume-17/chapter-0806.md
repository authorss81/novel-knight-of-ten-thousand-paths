# Chapter 0806

**Hearthmonth the twenty-second, YR 323, a Tuesday and not a market morning with the room over that weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, twenty-six days after a sheet went up on those boards, and the second of three things said out loud in one street in one week by two people who are not him.**

A Tuesday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the twenty-second of Hearthmonth with about nine people in it and five things on the boards at the low end and a man planing hurdle rails against the wall of the third door. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house had gone on from noon on the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Tuesday, and the ordinary part is that a Tuesday on a made road carries about a ninth of what it carries on a Saturday and that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that road have not heard what went up on those boards twenty-six days ago.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the seventeenth of Hearthmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety-three drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the same ninety-three went up five days ago, and four people in nine counties have said since that about five days is now the interval at which the figure of a drove changes and that a figure nobody hears change is a figure nobody in nine counties is counting.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for six hundred and fifty days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and has not been picked up. Four people in nine counties have said since that six hundred and fifty days is about a year and eleven months and that a man who has carried the same stone for a year and eleven months has done about nine hundred journeys and that nobody in nine counties has asked him for the figure.

The practice has run two hundred and seventy-one mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the twenty-second of Hearthmonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is two hundred and seventy-seven mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the twenty-second of Hearthmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and seventy-one mornings is about a year of a number said twice in the dark and that about three weeks of a sheet on a board is a different kind of time again.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and he is one of the nine and has been one of the nine for about a year. Eight of the ten things have been held on every morning but two since the first of Wolfmonth. One of the two is the head of a cart track with nine hurdles across it that nine people at the end of it put there and would like to stay, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth YR 322, which is three hundred and sixty-nine days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is three hundred and fifty days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is four hundred and seventeen days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that four hundred and seventeen days is a year and fifty-two days, and five have said that nine feet of it across is the width of the made road and of the broom and the length of the gap in that wall, and four have said that a hurdle is a thing a person holds up themselves and that about nine hundred yards of grass that nobody will hold up is the same kind of thing in a different trade.

The count of things on that board is five and the count of questions on it is three, and it has been five and three for twenty-six days. The first question is the notice's own and wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred and twenty-one days past its date, and the second is the roll's own and has nine empty lines on each of three copies, and the third is the sheet's and wants one name and has about thirty-seven days left in it, and about four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that thirty-seven days is five weeks and two days, and five had not.

The sheet that went up on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth asks for the name of one person to stand against the roads of nine counties and says under the Marshal's hand in two sentences that the place nine days ride off is being held so that what is called the Sundering is not done again in this world. It has been on that frame twenty-six days and nobody in nine counties has put a name against it and nobody in nine counties has been asked to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a sheet nobody has answered in about four weeks is not a threat and is not a question and is a third thing, and five have said that about a third thing on a board with two other things on it is a board nobody in this county is reading.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and seventy days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down. Inside the covering paper folded over the back of the second copy there is a sheet of paper folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked. Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to be.

Then at about the tenth hour of that Tuesday the man planing those rails at the third door was asked one question about his own trade, kindly, by a person who wanted to know what a hurdle was made of and what it cost to have one made. He was about forty-four and he answered in thirty-seven words and about nine people in that market street heard all of it and about five did not.

“**I have made four hundred hurdles in my life and every one of them went up where the person who wanted it put it, and a line with a name on it is the end of that.**”

About nobody in nine counties argued with him about any part of it, and nobody in that market street said anything back to him for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about nine people went on what they were doing. About four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that the second of three things said out loud in that street in that week was also not a refusal of the sheet, and five had not, and four have said since that a woman who washes for nine households will not speak for nine households and a man who has made four hundred hurdles will not put a name on a line, and that neither of those is the sheet and that about the two of them do not agree with each other and cannot both be carried by a county of about two thousand people.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour and had stood in that street and had heard the second of those three things said and had said nothing about it and had not answered it and had not said that it was wrong. That was the whole of what he came down for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to hear three things in one week out of three people who are not him and he had heard two of them, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have heard none of either.

The cost of that Tuesday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and four hundred yards of a made road and had a hot band across the knee by about the seventh hour. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And in seventy-seven days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document and has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that was on his hand all through the two hours he stood in that street.

The ordinary part of that Tuesday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped, and the man planing the rails got his plane back off the third door and set it down on his knee and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Tuesday in Hearthmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the twenty-second of Hearthmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-second of Hearthmonth is a Tuesday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred hurdles is the number of hurdles in one man's life and about four hundred people are on a far bank and that neither of the two fours is on a line.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs on a Tuesday and has not come down them on a Tuesday in about four and a half years. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it, and about nine people went past that door that morning and about four of them looked down at it and about five did not.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee, and the clean ring at the foot of it, which is a foot wide and half an inch deep, has filled with grit for about the fourth time since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four people in nine counties have said that a ring that fills is a ring somebody does about once a fortnight. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and about five have said that about two hundred years is twenty generations of people who cut a name into a thing and that about none of them ever came back to read one.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times and does not touch a frame has said no three times without speaking, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and four have said that nothing in that yard is on any board in nine counties and that nothing in it is on any line anybody has written this month.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile are a set of people with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that a man who has made four hundred hurdles in his life would be the only person in this county who can say what a hurdle is for and that nobody has asked him.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is four hundred and seventeen days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Tuesday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by two people who were not him. That is the two hundred and seventy-first morning. The day-book in the room over that weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about the second of three things said out loud in one street in one week is written down anywhere either.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who hears two things that cannot both be true and does not say so is a man nobody thanks, and five have said that about that is correct and is not a defence. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and he had heard two of three things said out loud in that street in that week by two people who are not him, and he had not been one of the two on any morning since the first of Wolfmonth a year ago, and about four hundred people have never once heard him say anything.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one is gone to powder, and there were five things on that board and about thirty-seven days left on the sheet, and the fourth line of the roll was still empty on all three of its copies.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a man of about forty-four saying thirty-seven words out loud once in one street about four hundred hurdles in his life and a line with a name on it, and about nine hundred people not hearing it and nobody arguing and nobody agreeing, a sheet twenty-six days on a board and about thirty-seven days left in it, three questions on one board that do not agree with one another, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and a fourth line empty on all three, seventy-seven days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that nobody in nine counties knows about, a practice run for two hundred and seventy-one mornings and written down nowhere, one thing that went quiet three hundred and sixty-nine days ago and is alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, one thing that has never been held by anybody at all, about nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
