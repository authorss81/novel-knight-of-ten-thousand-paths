**Hearthmonth the twelfth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it, sixteen days after a sheet went up on those boards beside three copies of a roll and not over them, and a question asked at the low end of that street about a trade and then a second question about what to call the woman who answered it.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the twelfth of Hearthmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts along both sides and five things on the boards at the low end. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house came off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Saturday in the eleventh day of Hearthmonth, and the ordinary part is that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have still not heard what went up on those boards sixteen days ago.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the tenth of Hearthmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety-two drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and two more than the figure this county said out loud ten days ago, and four people in nine counties have said since that a drove is the only thing in this county that goes up a hill by a number nobody can check.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for six hundred and forty days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and has not been picked up, and about four people in nine counties have said since that six hundred and forty days is a year and nine months and that the ninth yard has gone not started through a whole winter and a whole summer.

The practice did not run on that morning and the number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is two hundred and sixty-nine mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the twelfth of Hearthmonth and two hundred and sixty-three of them were due on a day this room is shut, and six went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and he is one of the nine and has been one of the nine for about a year, and eight of the ten things have been held on every morning but two since the first of Wolfmonth. One of the two is the head of a cart track with nine hurdles across it that nine people at the end of it put there and would like to stay, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth YR 322, which is three hundred and fifty-nine days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is three hundred and forty days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is four hundred and seven days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that four hundred and seven days is a year and about six weeks and that about nine feet of it across is the width of the made road and the width of the broom and the length of the gap in that wall, and five have said that it will not close over in this winter either.

About four people in nine counties have said out loud that a sheet which asks for the name of one person and says in two sentences under the Marshal's hand that the place nine days ride off is being held so that what is called the Sundering is not done again in this world is the best offer anybody in this county has ever been made, and about four of the four said it out loud once, in their own trades, to about nine people each, and nobody in nine counties argued with any of the four about any part of it. Five people in nine counties had not worked it out, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road had not heard it at all.

Then about the tenth hour of that Saturday, at the low end of that market street, outside the door of the office at the low end of it, the man of about fifty-four who keeps that weigh-house floor asked a woman of about sixty-one one question about her own trade, kindly, in his own trade, the way a person asks a question he has already thought about the answer to. He asked her what the office did with the paper that came into it, and she answered him in twenty-nine words, and about four people in that market street heard all of it and about five did not.

“**I keep the weights and the ribbon and the county's one seal and I keep them in a drawer, and I have not weighed a thing in nine years.**”

About four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that a woman who has kept a set of standard weights for about nine years and has weighed nothing with them in about nine years is a woman whose trade has not changed and whose hands have, and five had not. Four people in nine counties have said since that a drawer eleven inches deep is not a collection and that the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights is the whole of what nine counties have to put on a paper.

He asked her then what to call her, because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and she told him, and it was sixteen words, and four people in that market street heard it.

“**Nell Thrush, and I would like the paper this county is copying to be worth something.**”

About four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that a woman who has kept the county's one seal for about nine years and has not had it asked for twice in about nine years would rather be the woman who kept it than the woman who gave it, and five had not, and four have said since that wanting a paper to be worth something is not the same as being asked what a seal is for, and that nobody in nine counties has asked her that and nobody is going to.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door all morning and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer eleven inches deep in it was not opened, and the seal is still in it and its ribbon is still round it and the standard weights are still beside it. The man behind the counter at the front of that office was born in that street and was behind that counter and nobody went in and nobody asked him what a gate is for, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a counter and a door and a man who was born in the street is a thing this county has had for about a year and about nobody has put a name against it.

The count of things on that board is five and the count of questions on it is three, and it has been five and three for sixteen days, and four people in nine counties have said since that a board with five things on it and three questions on it is not a board anybody can answer and that sixteen days is not long enough to tell whether it ever will be. The first question is the notice's own and wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred and eleven days past its date, and the second is the roll's own and has nine empty lines on each of three copies, and the third is the sheet's and wants one name and has about forty-seven days left in it.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and sixty days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down. Inside the covering paper folded over the back of the second copy there is a sheet of paper folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and it says that nobody in this county is paid to keep a road, and that a form a county cannot fill is not to be filled by writing somebody else's name on it, and that a county which will not govern ought to say so in writing to the people it is not governing. Nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked and nobody has offered to say.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to be, and about four have said since that a woman who keeps a seal nobody has asked for and a woman who has said one thing out loud in a lane about about fourpence a year are not the same kind of person and that about four hundred places writing a paper by hand is what both of them have in common.

The bolt on that room came off at first light and the thirteen stairs were up and about nine people at the low end of that street could hear the press working at about the ninth hour. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and nothing about the four lines on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in sixteen days, and no fifth head is ruled, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in sixteen days, and the fourth ruled head is empty, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four people in nine counties had seen the thumb-drag across the end of the top line and read it for a moment as the first stroke of a letter, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it, and he asked a different person a different question about a different drawer at about the tenth hour that morning.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it was a foot wide and half an inch deep and had filled with grit. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and about four have said that nine hundred names cut by people who never met is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep, and five have said that a stone with nine hundred names on it is bigger than four hundred places writing a paper by hand and is not worth more than one.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times and does not touch a frame has said no three times without speaking, and five have said that he has said twice out loud he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and four have said that five frames under about nine skirts is a trade and not a finding and that about nobody in nine counties has asked to put it on a line.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile are a set of people with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is four hundred and seven days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour and had stood at those boards and had read the sheet again and had not touched it, and that was the whole of what he came down for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to see whether the four people who had said it was the best offer anybody in this county has ever been made would say it a second time, and one of them said it a second time, in a different trade, to a different nine people, and nobody argued with that one either.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and two hours standing at a board with about four hundred people round it and had a hot band across the knee by about the eleventh hour. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And in sixty-seven days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document and has never been said out loud in a room, and that was on his hand all through the two hours he stood at those boards.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. A barrow went over on its axle at the low end of that street and about nine people stepped round it without stopping and one of them righted it before the carter came back. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and one of them said a price out loud that about four people heard and about five had not, and they came out of the shop with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day, and about four hundred people went along that street and about nine of them stopped at a frame.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Hearthmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the twelfth of Hearthmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twelfth of Hearthmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard what is nailed up on a frame at the low end of this market street.

Then the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over that weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who has run two hundred and sixty-three mornings of a number said twice in the dark has not run one on any of the eleven market mornings since the year turned.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who listens to a woman tell him her name is a man nobody thanks, because what he did was not go away first. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and on the twelfth of Hearthmonth a woman of about sixty-one told him a name because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and that name is not on any paper in this county.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one is gone to powder, and there were five things on that board and about forty-seven days left on the sheet, and the drawer eleven inches deep in the low room at the back of that office was still shut and the seal is still in it.
