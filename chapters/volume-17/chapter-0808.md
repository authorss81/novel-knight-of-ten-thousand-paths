# Chapter 0808

**Goatmonth the first, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over that weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with four hundred-odd people in that street, thirty-five days after a sheet went up on those boards, the twenty-fourth first of a month in a row arriving at five things, and a sheet of foolscap ruled into nine lines at the low end of that street with a trade on each and no name on any.**

The street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the boards at the low end of it and the bolt had come off the room over that weigh-house at first light and the copyist of twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs.

The cloth was over the bench nine doors down, and the first of Goatmonth went past it without raining, which is the only thing anybody in that street said about it.

The first of Goatmonth came and went in that street and nine people saw it come and nine saw it go, and four hundred people along that made road did not know it had arrived. It is the twenty-fourth first of a month in a row to come to those boards and find nothing standing at them. Twenty-four is more than two dozen, and a person can hold two dozen in his head, and holding a figure is not doing anything with it.

Four days out, ninety-five drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, ninety-five on that same Thursday at the sixth hour and back down at the fifth hour with two men. Ninety-five is one more than ninety-four and a hundred is five more and five drove is about five weeks of a figure nobody in this county has said out loud.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the ninth has not been started. One man has been carrying the stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and fifty-nine days.

Behind the ninth of the nine hurdles at the head of that cart track there are nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and twenty-six days. The last cart of lime came up that track three hundred and seventy-eight days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and fifty-nine days, which is a year and about six days short of a full one, and six days is not going to change anything at the top of a cart track.

The woman of twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at the sixth. The register holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none. The day-book stands at eleven entries. The fourth ruled head of that page is blank and there is no fifth one cut. The press stands open and there is no date on anything in that room.

There are four lines of chalk on that door sill, still, under a hood the rain has never got behind. They have gone six weeks without a hand near them. Nobody has read them out loud to anybody, and there is a stick of chalk in a press drawer upstairs that he has never once asked her for.

The copyist of twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm and did not look at it, and she has been told about none of it.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the flap of a stall that sells nothing but a board, a person nobody in nine counties can name laid a sheet of foolscap down and ruled it into nine lines with a straight edge and a knife.

They wrote a trade on each of the nine lines and no name on any of them. A wheelwright, a saddler, a tanner, a slater, a cooper, a miller, a smith, a drover, and a woman who keeps a dairy. One hand. Nine trades and not nine names. Nothing was sealed. It was not nailed up and it was not sent anywhere.

Nine people looked at it. Five did not.

“Who put that there,” said a man at the second door.

“He has gone up the street,” said the stallholder, who sells nothing but a board and who has not moved his own board.

“Whose board is it?”

“That one.”

“He did not tell you.”

“He did not tell me anything.”

So nobody in nine counties has asked that person where they got the paper, and nobody has asked them who else has one, and four people have said since that the person who laid it on that flap does not know who else has one either, and that is the whole of what is known about it.

Four hundred places are writing a paper by hand because nobody has a seal to give them, and this is one of them. Nine lines with a trade on each is the ninth ruled thing in this county in four and a half years, and a ruled line is not a head, and nobody has put a head to it.

The count on that frame has been five things and three questions for thirty-five days. The notice wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred days past its date. The roll wants a name against each of nine lines and has three copies. The sheet wants one name and has four weeks left in it.

The sheet is true in what it says and false in what it assumes, and both of those are on the page of it, and about five people have worked out neither.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and seventy-nine days. Nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's. It is not filled in, and it is not torn up, and it has not been taken down.

He asked the stallholder two questions before he looked at the lines at all.

“Who ruled them.”

“Not me.”

“Did you see it done?”

“No.”

“Then somebody stood where you are standing and put nine trades on a piece of paper this morning and went away again, and you did not see it.”

“I have been selling nothing but a board since Candlemas,” the stallholder said, “and I am not going to stand in the cold watching a man I do not know write on paper. That is what I do instead.”

“And if one of those nine trades is yours?”

“It is not.”

“And if one of them is the trade of a man who does not exist in this street?”

“Then somebody has written down a thing they wanted written down, which is what people do, and I do not say that to be unkind. I say it because you are asking me as though the paper had asked to be true.”

The man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour, stood at the boards, and then went down to the low end of that street and looked at nine lines on the flap of a stall for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

He did not ask who wrote it. He did not ask where it came from. He had come down to see what a county does with a paper that nobody can seal, and the answer was standing in front of him: a county had written nine lines on it, and put a trade on each of them, and no name on any of them.

He stood there and thought about the nine trades and about the fact that he could have told you within nine seconds whether six of those nine trades were the right trades for this street, and about the fact that he could not have told you the name on a single one of them. Nine lines with a trade on each is a list that can be argued with, and there is not one line on that paper that anybody in nine counties can check.

The rest of that Thursday went on. Two boys moved nine sacks of meal off a cart at the high end of that street and nine people watched and four offered and one was taken up. The two women at the third door spent four minutes by the clock on a bolt of cloth and came out of the shop with it wrapped and a second parcel neither of them had gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day, and four hundred people went along that street and nine of them stopped at a flap of a stall.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of the boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Goatmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of them went out into five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the first of Goatmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post is the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years, and neither nine lines nor four hundred people has been asked what it thinks.

The five frames of brood in the walled yard are under nine straw skirts and the board is still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of nineteen has been in and out of that yard nine times with a honey pail and has not touched a frame. His trade is now on nine lines of somebody else's paper and nobody has asked him whether it is.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are two miles of wall and orchard owned by nine households, which is the third set of nine in this county with a reason of its own about a roll, and a form with one line for a county is about eight lines too short for them, and nine lines with a trade on each is not nine households either.

He went out of that market street at the eleventh hour and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The nine hurdles were across the top of that cart track where they have been for a year, and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one had gone to powder. Five things on that board and four weeks left on the sheet, and the fourth line of the roll still empty on all three of its copies.

**And the cost of that Thursday is this county's own copy of the hand paper begun, nine lines, nine trades, no names, no seal, not nailed up and not sent anywhere, and nobody in nine counties knowing who else has one, a twenty-fourth first of a month in a row arriving at five things on a board, a sheet thirty-five days on that board and four weeks left in it, three questions on one frame that do not agree with one another, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three, eighty-six days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet three hundred and seventy-eight days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
