# Chapter 0844

**Longlight the twenty-sixth, YR 324, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and eighty days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and seventeen days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and eleven days between two sheets of which nine were practice mornings.**

Seven people stood up on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold and said a number before the light, and about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over later the same morning the woman of about twenty-nine came down those thirteen stairs with the second lamp still warm in her hand and put it on the sill by that weigh-house door and went back up.

It was the three hundred and eighty-first morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and seventy-seven mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not. The practice ran on nine of the eleven days between the first sheet and the second one and the number was four on all nine of them, and nobody wrote any of the nine down and nobody was told about them.

A county that has been asked a question once does not have a way of being asked a second one. Two of the four hundred places writing a paper out by hand in nine counties have now had a sheet come up their road twice, and about four people in this county have said that this is the first time in about four and a half years that anything has happened to this county twice, and about five have said that a thing which happens twice needs a form and this county does not have one.

A carter of about forty-four who comes up that made road twice a week said one thing about it in his own trade, at the low end of that street, at about the tenth hour, to about nine people and to nobody in particular.

“**A Crown's cart comes up this road four times in a good month and I have been on it about nine times in four and a half years and I have never once known what was in it, and there has been a sheet in one of them and the second one is not here yet, and if it comes I will not open it and I will not be asked what is in it.**”

It was seventy-five words. About four people near enough heard all of them and about five heard the end of them, and nobody stopped what they were doing, and the second sheet had not come up that road by the time he went to bed that night.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and twenty-four days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. Nothing has been written on those lines and nothing has been taken off that frame and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and forty-five days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and seventeen days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and the five things on it have not changed in a hundred and eighty days and neither have the three questions.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and forty-five days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. Nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand, and about four hundred copies have never met one another and never will unless somebody asks two of them to.

A line with a trade on it can be argued with and a paper nobody can seal binds nobody, which is safe. Binding nobody is not the same as owing nobody and it is not a plan, and about four hundred places writing one out by hand is the largest single act of mutual aid in nine counties in about a year and it has no place to go and nobody to take it to.

At the front of that office there is a counter about nine feet from that frame and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and about nine things were done one after another on that counter on that Tuesday and nothing was written on any of them. The man behind the counter has the key to that drawer in his pocket and has not used it in about nine years, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a seal is for or what a gate is for.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Tuesday, as it has been on every morning since the seventh of Longlight, and about nine people went past that door during the morning and about four of them looked at it. The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it was not opened once in a hundred and eighty days, and the county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and a door being shut is not a drawer being shut.

Four days out, a hundred and fifteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on Longlight the twenty-first, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up, and about nine people along that made road have walked behind one on a working day and told nobody, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing a paper out by hand for a place nine days off that none of them has ever stood on.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for eight hundred and four days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start. The mallet lies out on that bank with the line of stone and has not been picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

Nine hurdles are across the head of that cart track foot to foot, and the top one carries a hurdle shoe whose thumb of lime has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and twenty-three days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut five hundred and four days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth.

The bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has not been lifted and has not been scheduled for lifting. About nine houses about four hundred yards back behind those nine hurdles would like them to stay and have not been asked one thing in five months and are not going to be, and about nine people in nine counties have said that a community nobody has asked is not a community anybody in this county can speak for.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-sixth of Longlight is a Tuesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off that causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth and one crossing is not a road.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and seventy-one days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and a place that is still held nine days off is not cured by anything anybody in this county has done.

Under about nine straw skirts in that walled yard there are five frames of brood and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four. The young man of about nineteen has carried a pail in and out of that yard nine times this week and has not opened a skirt. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide with the mat of it thicker than it was a month ago.

The rest of that Tuesday went on the way a Tuesday goes on in a market town of about two thousand people where nothing has happened for nine days.

The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and the woman who keeps it was the only person in that county who had been asked to hire a room twice in about four and a half years and had not been asked why either time. About nine people were in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road did the ordinary part of a Tuesday.

There is a particular thing about a county that has been asked a question once, and he had a reason for noticing it, being that in about four and a half years he has been the person in this county who is asked the same thing twice and has never once been asked anything at all.

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a dairy at the third door was carrying two pails up the step at about the eleventh hour and put one down to shift her grip, and a boy of about nine offered to take the other one and was told which one to take. A wain stood empty at the high end of that street where it stood on the Saturday and had stood there since, and about nine people had walked round it four days running.

A clerk in nine counties has not been sent to ask that road office four hundred miles off for a second sheet, and about nine people in this county have worked out that nobody is going to be. He had a reason for not offering, being that he had been asked nothing in four and a half years and did not know what an offer of that kind is called.

About four people have said that a county which will not go and get a thing is a county that has decided the thing is not coming, and about five have said the same thing about four and a half years. Two women at the second door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Tuesday in Longlight is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Of that about nine, five went out into about five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

A cloth lay over a bench nine doors down from those boards and nobody in that street touched it or asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on any of the eleven days between two sheets. None of the three series kept in that room is worked out from either of the other two, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Tuesday was the leg, which took eleven miles of a made road before light and about nine of them again afterward, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and thirty-one days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. Eleven days and nine mornings of a number said twice by two people who are not him did not reach it, and there is nobody in nine counties he could tell.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it, and neither is a page in a woman's hand at the back of a day-book, and neither is the sentence on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. A page that has been authenticated has not been agreed to. A place that is still held has not been won. A damaged engine has not been mended. And a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and thirty-one days is not a cure.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone and did not go in at the low end of that street. Five things were on that board and one hundred and seventeen days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and a second sheet was not in this county yet, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is eleven days between two sheets of which nine were practice mornings and the number was four on all nine, and a county of about two thousand people with no way of being told a thing twice and about four hundred places writing a paper out by hand with no form to put the paper in. The drawer eleven inches deep not opened once in a hundred and eighty days and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for. Nine trades on nine lines of a paper nobody can seal and no names on any of them and no place it may go. Two hundred and thirty-one days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet five hundred and twenty-three days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
