# Chapter 0841

**Longlight the fourteenth, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and sixty-eight days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and five days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a Crown's cart coming up that made road in the ordinary post with two satchels under the seat.**

That market street fills from about the eighth hour and by nine there were about four hundred people in it, and the five things were up on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over that weigh-house before the light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill.

About four hundred people did about four hundred things in that street that morning that have nothing to do with a sheet on a frame or with anything else. About four of them stopped at the frame and read the roll and about five did not stop at all, and the rest went past it at the speed of people going past a thing they have read before.

There is one thing about a Crown's cart in the ordinary post that nobody in this county has ever had to think about, being that it stops where it stops and what comes out of it is not a thing anybody in nine counties chose to be told. The cart came up that made road at about the seventh hour with two satchels under the seat and a driver who did not know what was in them, and he was not asked and did not ask.

The cart went on up the hill in the dark after the market and the two satchels went into the office at the low end of that street with the rest of the post. The man behind the counter wrote nothing on either of them and did not open either of them and put them on a shelf under the window with about nine other things, and nobody in that market street asked what was in them, because nobody in that market street had any reason to think there was anything.

He had thought about it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and what he had thought about was that a post is a post and that a county which opens what comes in the post has become a different kind of place, and he could not have said that out loud without it sounding like an argument, and about nine people in that street would have heard an argument and about four of them would have repeated it.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and twelve days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. It is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up, and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper.

The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and thirty-three days past its own date and has shut nothing. The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and five days ago, and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. Nothing has been nailed over anything and nothing has been taken down, and about four hundred people in that street have walked past those five things this month and about nine of them have stopped.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and thirty-three days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. It has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere.

About four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand, and no two of about four hundred copies agree with one another because nobody has asked them to and nobody has shown one to another.

A line with a trade on it can be argued with, and a line with a name on it cannot, and about four hundred places writing one out by hand in about four months is the largest single act of mutual aid anybody in nine counties has heard of and is not a document. Not one of the four hundred has ever met another of them and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines of this county's copy.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that market morning, as it has been on every morning since the seventh of Longlight. It had stood open on its hook on four market mornings before that, being the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth, the two Saturdays after it and the seventh of Longlight, and it does not stand open again in this six weeks and nobody stands in that doorway.

The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of that room was not opened once in a hundred and sixty-eight days. The county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody has asked what a gate is for, and a door that is open is not a drawer that has been opened and a room that has been looked at is not a thing that has been taken out of it.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot with a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on the shoe of that one gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and eleven days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and ninety-two days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth.

The bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has not been lifted and has not been scheduled for lifting, and not one of those nine hurdles has been lifted and no bar has been tried anywhere in this county. About nine houses about four hundred yards back behind those hurdles put them there themselves and would like them to stay, and they said so once through a shut door in twenty-nine words in Goatmonth of the year before last, and nobody has asked them one thing since and nobody is going to.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and ninety-two days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high. The ninth yard has not been started and it does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

Four days out, a hundred and fourteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the hundred and fourteenth went up on Longlight the fourteenth at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up, and a figure that has gone past a hundred is a figure nobody in this county argues about.

The second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs was unlit that morning, because the room it belongs to was open and it is lit on the five mornings a week that the market does not come. The practice does not run on a market morning and nobody in this county has said a number at the fourth hour on any morning about four hundred people were in this street, and there is no fourth hour in that room today because the room is a room people buy paper in.

The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and sixty-eight days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning and about four of them looked down. The stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it, and four lines on a sill are not the beginning of a cure.

The press in that room stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down. A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody. Nothing on those four pages says where anything is shut, nobody in nine counties has asked to see them, and about nine people in this county know that they exist.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fourteenth of Longlight is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off that causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung.

About nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth and about four hundred yards of it is the stretch that begins at the bank and runs inland past a shut door. About four hundred people on that bank have never once been across in about nine years, and one crossing is not a road, and nobody on this side has asked a second question about it.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and fifty-nine days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard stand under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four. The young man of about nineteen has carried a pail in and out of that yard nine times this week and has not opened a skirt and nobody in nine counties has asked him for an account of the three things he has said out loud in that street in about four and a half years.

About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide with the mat of it thicker than it was a fortnight ago, and a thicker mat is a mat that has stopped moving, and nobody has asked anybody about it. Four things in this county are about nine feet across and none of the four has been asked about.

The rest of that market morning went on the way a market morning goes on. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. A man of about forty-five set a trestle against the front of the second stall at about the tenth hour and put nine weights on it in a row and took them off again and put them back four inches along.

The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and a man of about thirty-eight who runs a weekly wain between that inn and the causeway four days south stood at his own board for about a quarter of an hour and said nothing to anybody. About nine hundred yards of that made road above the causeway is the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of it have never known it was smooth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Longlight is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked after it. The half leaf in that press is face down and was not turned over on a market morning. Three series stand in that room and none of the three is worked out from another, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Thursday was the leg, which took about nine miles of a made road and about four hundred yards of a lane either side of it and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile. It was the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and nineteen days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. No clerk has ever entered it in anything and no box and no shelf in this county has ever been offered for it.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence in an unclaimed hand on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Nor is a page that has been authenticated, which is not a thing anybody has agreed to, and not a place that is still held, which is not a victory, and not a damaged engine, which is not a rescue. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and nineteen days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone and did not go in at the low end of that street. The two satchels were on a shelf under that window with about nine other things and were not opened. Five things were on that board and one hundred and five days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a cart in the ordinary post that stops where it stops, and two satchels on a shelf under a window that nobody opened on a market morning because about four hundred people were in that street buying string, and the drawer eleven inches deep not opened once in a hundred and sixty-eight days and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for. Nine trades on nine lines of a paper nobody can seal and no names on any of them and about four hundred copies of it in nine counties that have never met one another. Two hundred and nineteen days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet five hundred and eleven days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
