# Chapter 0823

**Thawmonth the twenty-seventh, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, ninety-one days after a sheet went up on those boards, twenty-eight days past the date at the foot of that sheet, four copied pages authenticated in the room over the weigh-house in her own trade, and a man of thirty-two the fourth day off a made road with nothing to do with any of it.**

By the ninth hour that street had about four hundred people in it and the five things were up on the frame at the low end, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and thirty-five days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and the notice above them is three hundred and fifty-six days past its own date, and the date at the foot of the sheet went past twenty-eight days ago. Nothing in nine counties is shut on it. About nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road never knew there was a date on it at all.

The four copied pages were on the board under that sill where they had been since the Thursday before, and they were the only four documents in this county with nine roads on each of them and nobody's name against any of the nine, and they were not in any room that anybody could walk into for nothing.

At about the tenth hour the woman of about twenty-nine shut the door of that room at the top of those thirteen stairs, which she does not do on a market morning, and about nine people were on that floor, and she put the four sheets square on the board and turned them so that all four of them faced the same way.

Then she did the thing she does in her own trade, and what it is, is that a person who keeps records compares what is on a paper against what was put on the paper, and says which of the two she can vouch for, and stops there.

She said it out loud, once, in her own trade, to about nine people, and the market was going on four hundred strong in the street below.

“**I have compared the four of them against one another and against what was put on them, and I can say that what is on these four pages is what somebody put on these four pages. That is the whole of what I can say. It is not a saying that the roads are the roads, and it is not anybody consenting to anything.**”

It was sixty-four words. About four people on that floor heard the whole of it and about five heard the middle of it and went on looking at the boards, and nobody said anything back to her for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

Then she said one more sentence in her own trade, and it was nine words, and about nine people on that floor heard it.

“**A page that is authenticated is not consented to.**”

As to consent there was none in that room that morning, and nobody was asked for any and nobody in nine counties was asked for any, and about four people in nine counties have said since that it is the first sentence anybody in this county has said out loud about a piece of paper that is true, and about five have said that it is a sentence and not a finding, and about four have said that a sentence in a room of about nine people is about as far as nine counties takes anything.

The copyist of about twenty-six was in that room for the whole of it with the day-book under her arm. She was not told about any of it and she was not asked about any of it, and she ruled no head. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none that morning and has gained none in ninety-one days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none, and there is nothing in it about four pages. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Then the four of them went into the press on the top shelf, above the half leaf, which is face down where it was and was not turned over and is not going to be turned over by anybody in nine counties. About nine people saw four sheets of foolscap go into a press that has no date on it, and about four of them have thought about that since and about five have not, and the thing about a press with no date on it is that anything put into it has stopped being about a day.

Then she turned the fourth sheet over on the board and read the last line of it out loud, because it is on the fourth sheet and she had read the whole of the fourth sheet, and what is written on that last line is this.

“Written to be given to Orla, and she is not at this address, and the place it was written in is not named here.”

That is the only name on any of the four pages, and it is the only name anybody in this county has read out loud in a room in about four and a half years. About four people in that room had never heard it said and about five had heard it once and did not connect the two things, and nobody asked about it and nobody asked who had written it and nobody asked how old the four of them are.

The man of thirty-two was the fourth day off a made road and about nine miles short of the head of it, at a wall of eight yards that is back in and nine that is not, with the line of stone on the bank and the mallet on top of it. He was not in that room and was not on that street and was not in this county. He has not been called by that name since he was carried out of a place these four pages do not name.

It was not said to him and he was not told it that week, and nothing about it went into a book, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard of four pages or of a name.

Four lines of chalk are on the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the tenth hour of that Thursday with about nine things done one after another and nothing written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office is a low room with a door in front of it, a counter and a clerk's table, a press with no date on it, and a drawer eleven inches deep. It was shut behind its own door on that Thursday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties has gone into it in about nine years, and the drawer in it was not opened once in ninety-one days. The county's one seal is in there still with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Four days out, a hundred and three drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and three went up that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and fifteen days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank, which is the width of the made road and the width of the standing broom four days off.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-seventh of Thawmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and thirty-four days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and fifteen days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to this year.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and eighty-two days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate is about four, and no sixth has gone in, and the board is still on the skirt stones at that gate, and nobody has asked the young man of about nineteen for an account of the three things he has said out loud in that street in about four and a half years.

The rest of that Thursday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and the woman who keeps it marked the four on her tally-stick by the door and put no board out on the made street after noon.

A Crown's cart came up that made road at about the seventh hour going the other way and did not stop, and its driver did not know what was in the two satchels under his seat, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that road have never once been told what goes up and down it in the ordinary post.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Thawmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of the nine people at the end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

On a market morning the practice does not run, and there was no fourth hour in that room over the weigh-house that morning, and there has not been one on any market morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. The second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs is lit at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week when the market does not come and it goes out at about the sixth. The number last said out loud on that floor is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

Nine doors down from those boards there is a bench with a cloth over it, and it has been there about four and a half months, and nobody lifted that cloth that day and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Thursday was in four days of a made road in a leg that takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and forty-two days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that no person in nine counties has ever been shown it. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and forty-two days.

**And the cost of these four pages is four copied pages authenticated in a room over a weigh-house by a woman in her own trade on a morning when the man they bear on was the fourth day off a made road and was told afterwards, sixty-four words and then nine, and the nine being that a page authenticated is not consented to. One name is on the last line of the fourth of them and nobody in that room had heard it and nobody has asked about it since and he has not said it out loud and it went into no book. A page can be found to be what was put on it and be found to be nobody's business, and the same woman said both of those on the same morning, and neither of the two answers to the other. One hundred and forty-two days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and thirty-four days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
