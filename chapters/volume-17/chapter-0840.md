# Chapter 0840

**Longlight the eleventh, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and sixty-five days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and two days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and nobody in this county a delegate to anything.**

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and seventieth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and sixty-six mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are gone and a month and ten days have not brought them back.

There is no delegate to anything in this county and nobody here has been asked to be one. Four people in nine counties have said that a county which cannot call a hearing and has not called one in four and a half years has no business sending anybody anywhere, and five have said that nobody in nine counties would accept one if it offered.

About nine hundred yards of a stair nine days off from any town in this county is being argued about in about four hundred places at one time by people who have never met one another, and this county has put nine trades on nine lines of a paper with no seal on it, and no names on any of them, and no representative of this county has been at any part of that argument, and nobody in this county has been asked for one.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and nine days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up, and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and thirty days past its own date and has shut nothing, and the date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and two days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nothing has been nailed over anything and nothing has been taken down.

The door of the low room at the back of the Crown's road office stood open on its hook on four market mornings in three weeks and about nine people stood inside it on each of those mornings and looked at a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it. The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of that room was not opened once in a hundred and sixty-five days. The county's one seal is in it with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody has asked what a gate is for, and a door being open is not a drawer being opened and a room being looked at is not a thing being taken out of it.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot with a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on the shoe of that one gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and eight days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and eighty-nine days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle was lifted and no bar was tried and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has not been lifted or scheduled for lifting. The ninth thing is alive and unnamed and unheld and shut, and about nine houses about four hundred yards back behind those hurdles put them there themselves and would like them to stay and have not been asked about anything in five months.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and eighty-nine days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and it does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

Four days out, a hundred and thirteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on the Thursday before, which was the seventh of Longlight, at about the sixth hour, and two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up and a figure that has gone past a hundred is a figure nobody argues about.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and thirty days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and it has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand and no two of about four hundred copies agree with one another because nobody has asked them to and nobody has shown one to another. Not one of the four hundred has ever met another of them.

A line with a trade on it can be argued with. A paper nobody can seal binds nobody, which is safe, and binding nobody is not the same as owing nobody and is not a plan, and about four hundred places writing one is the largest single act of mutual aid in nine counties in about a year and is not a document, and nobody in nine counties has met anybody else who did any part of it.

At the front of that office, which is about nine feet from that frame with the five things on it, there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and about four hundred people in nine counties have said since that a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has, and about five have said that the cheapest instrument is the one nobody in nine counties has looked at, and neither of those two is on any paper in this county and neither of them has been said out loud in that street.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on that sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and sixty-five days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about nine people went past that door on the eleventh of Longlight and about four of them looked down, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it, and four lines on a sill are not the beginning of a cure.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and fifty-six days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard stand under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four, and the board lies on the skirt stones at that gate. Nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide with the mat of it thicker than it was a month ago, and a thicker mat is a mat that has stopped moving, and nobody has asked anybody about it.

The rest of that Monday went on and about nine people were in that market street and no more than about nine, and four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and five did not. A hound belonging to nobody in that street lay in the one place on the paving the sun came down on and stayed in it for about two hours while nine people went round it, and a boy of about nine tried to move it twice and did not try a third time.

Then he was four days out at the causeway at about the third hour in the afternoon, and the winter hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway every Monday afternoon and the eleventh of Longlight is a Monday, and the lamp was put there about nine feet from the water, and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung because the water was off the causeway.

About nine people were on that coping and about four of them had come out to see the lamp go on, and the wind came off the water and went through the leg.

From the low side of that causeway you can see about four hundred yards of new kerb lying in a line along the made road on that far bank, and about nine hundred yards of it has gone down there since the first of Mudmonth, and a person has been paid to lay it and laid about four hundred yards of it herself at about nine feet a week, and she comes across this water about twice in a fortnight for stone and about four hundred people on that far bank have never once been across in about nine years.

This county has walked on the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties for about six months and has not asked, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth, and about nine hundred yards of a stair nine days off is being argued about in about four hundred places by people who have never met, and this county's contribution to all of it is nine trades and no names and no seal and nobody asked.

Nine doors down from those boards the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Longlight is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

The cost of that Monday was the leg, which did eleven miles of a made road and about four hundred yards of a lane either side of it and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and sixteen days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that four lines of chalk and four hundred yards of somebody else's kerb do not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a paper nobody can seal. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and sixteen days.

He came back up that made road after dark and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and one hundred and two days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and the nine hurdles were across that cart track, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is that about nine people stood four times in a room nobody in nine counties had gone into in about nine years and looked at a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it, and the drawer eleven inches deep was not opened once in a hundred and sixty-five days and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights are still in it, and nobody asked what a seal is for. A woman of about thirty-four who lays about nine feet of kerb a week on the far bank of a causeway has been paid to lay about nine hundred yards of it since Mudmonth and about four hundred people over there have never once been across, and a road kept by somebody else's decision has been walked on for about six months and about nine hundred people never knew the road was smooth. Nobody in this county is a delegate and nobody here has been asked to be one, and this county's contribution to about four hundred papers is nine trades and no names.
