# Chapter 0819

**Thawmonth the seventeenth, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, eighty-one days after a sheet went up on those boards, eighteen days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a hand lamp going on a coping four days south that afternoon.**

There were nine people in that street at the ninth hour and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since noon on the Saturday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and twenty-five days.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and tenth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four, and the man with the bad hip wrote nothing down, because nothing goes down anywhere in this county. The six mornings gone in the water in Mudmonth are six.

“He came down those stairs with nothing on Saturday,” the woman at the door said.

“He did.”

“And none of us asked him about it.”

“That is the correct number of things to ask him about and you have got it right.”

The number has been four for three hundred and six mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in eighty-one days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in eighty-one days. The fourth ruled head of that page has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

The four lines of chalk on the sill of that weigh-house door mean four and stand under a hood that has kept the rain off them since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth. Nine people went past that door on the seventeenth of Thawmonth and about four of them looked down and about five did not. About four people in nine counties have seen the thumb-drag across the end of the top line and read it for a moment as the first stroke of a letter, and about five have never seen it at all.

Then, at about the ninth hour, at the low end of that market street, a man of about sixty who puts that hand lamp on that coping was asked one question about his own trade, kindly, because the man of thirty-two wanted to know what a person does on a Monday afternoon four days south.

About four people heard him say all of that and about five were doing something else at the time and would have heard it if they had turned round.

“**I put it out about two hours before the light goes and I take it in about as long after it, and about four hundred people on the far bank have never once seen it lit, and about nine have told me they have seen it from their own door.**”

It was fifty words. Nobody in that street argued with him about any part of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a lamp somebody has to walk out with in the afternoon and walk back in with at night is the most expensive kind of safety there is and costs nothing to anybody, and about five have said that about nine people have seen it from their own door and about four hundred have not and that the nine are on the near bank.

He asked him then how long he had done it, and the man said every Monday afternoon in the winter for about nine years, and that in Frostmonth and in Hearthmonth he had done it twice a week because the water came over the causeway twice in that season and the hand bell on that gate post is the size of a fist and has to be rung on the days the water is up, and that about nine people on this side heard that bell the last time it was rung and nobody on the far bank is known to have answered it.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a hand bell rung at a gate post with nobody known to have answered is a bell nobody rings twice in a row on purpose, and about five have said that a Monday lamp and a Monday bell are the same arrangement with the same man carrying both.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and twenty-five days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not looked at it in about four and a half years and about nine have stopped at it in the whole of that time.

This county's own copy of the hand paper was ruled on that flap of a stall forty-six days ago and it has nine lines on it with a trade on each and no name on any, and it has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and nobody in nine counties knows who else has one and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of its nine lines.

The notice above those three copies wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and the first of Mudmonth was three hundred and forty-six days ago, and nothing has been shut anywhere in this county since and nothing has been opened anywhere in this county since either.

The practice does not run on a market morning and does run on this one. That is the whole of the arrangement and about four people in nine counties have said that a county which keeps its one practice on the five mornings its room is shut has worked out that a practice is a thing you do when nobody is looking, and about five have said that about nine hundred people looked at the frame at the low end of that street on the other two mornings of the week and that a practice nobody sees is not a practice a county has.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the ninth hour with about nine things done one after another and nothing written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Monday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. Nobody in nine counties went into it. The drawer eleven inches deep in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Then the man of thirty-two stood at those boards with nine people in that street and no market in it and touched nothing on that frame, and he kept it to about the eleventh hour.

He had meant to see what a refusal in one person's own trade does to a county in a week. What it had done was that a man of fifty-one had come down thirteen stairs on Saturday morning and had not come back up them on Saturday morning, and that about four hundred people in that street had not seen any of it happen, and that the fourth ruled head of that page was still empty on the Monday morning after it and no fifth head was ruled in this county.

He stood there and worked out the shape of it, being that about nine hundred names in a book four miles off and about nine trades on a flap of a stall are enough to answer a paper with one line on it, and that the one thing they cannot do is be written down anywhere in this county, and that about four and a half years is how long it has taken this county to arrive at having everything it needs and nothing it may use.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a man with everything he needs and nothing he may use is not a fool and is not a coward and is not somebody who has not thought about it, and about five have said that he is a man who has worked out what a county can refuse and has not worked out what a county can do, and that those are different questions and that this county has only ever asked the first of them.

He said nothing about that to anybody and he was not the author of anything said out loud in that street that week and nobody thanked anybody in that street that week.

The cost of that Monday was in the leg, which takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise and is worse than it was at the close of the last year, and which had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour of that day, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and thirty-two days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own.

A hearing with no bench in it stands in that street on the first of every month and since the first of Goatmonth YR 321 it cannot be called and does not sit, and the twenty-sixth first of a month in a row is a fortnight away, and about four people in nine counties have said that an institution which cannot be called cannot be asked for a ruling and about five have said that about four and a half years of a thing that cannot be called is a habit and not a refusal.

About four hundred yards of laid kerb go north on the far bank of that causeway from the far side of the water, and then grass, and a shut door, and about four hundred people in this market town who have never been across. About four people in nine counties have said since that about nine hundred yards of kerb on the wrong side of a causeway is a road somebody has been paid to lay since the fifth of Wolfmonth, and about five have said that nobody on this side has asked by whom and about four have said that a road which is somebody else's decision is still a road and still gets walked on.

The rest of that Monday went on. There were nine people in that street and no more than nine. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and marked the two occupied ones on its tally-stick by the door and put no board out on the made street, because there was nothing left to fill.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of those boards and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because there is nothing off the wet slate of that frame on a Monday in Thawmonth to need it. Five of the about nine people in that street went out into five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp went on the coping at the low side that afternoon, and the water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years, and about four hundred yards of laid kerb on the far side of that water go north into grass and about nobody on this side has asked who lays it.

Four days out the ninth mile stone is the height of a knee and the ring somebody cleaned round the foot of it on the Saturday before is a foot wide and half an inch deep and has grit in it again, and about four hundred people go past that stone every day of the week and about four of them clean round it and about five do not. Four hundred yards past it the eleventh mile stone carries about nine hundred names going back about two hundred years.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and five days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it lies in a line on the bank.

There are five frames of brood in that walled yard and they are under about nine straw skirts and the sixth has not gone in. The young man of about nineteen has been in and out of that yard about nine times this week and has touched no frame, and the board has been off the skirt stones and put back on, and nobody in nine counties has asked him which of the two he would rather.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and twenty-four days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and five days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to this year.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and seventy-two days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The cloth was over the bench nine doors down and nobody asked what is underneath it. The half leaf in that press lies face down. The three series in the room above that weigh-house are three series, none of them derived from another, and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and he did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and eighteen days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.
