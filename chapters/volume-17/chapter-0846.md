**Rainmonth the first, YR 324, a Sunday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and eighty-five days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and twenty-two days past the date at the foot of that sheet, the twenty-eighth first of a month in a row coming to that frame and finding five things on it and nothing standing at any of them, and a second sheet read out once at the high end of that street.**

Rainmonth is carried at thirty days and it began on that Sunday, and Longlight ran thirty days and ended the day before. Seven people stood up on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold and said a number before the light, and about nine people in this county had worked out inside about nine seconds what a month is and about five had not.

It was the three hundred and eighty-fourth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and eighty mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not. It has been four on every morning since the water and on no market morning at all, and this Sunday was the first of a month and was not a market morning.

The second sheet came up that made road on the twenty-ninth of Longlight, which was eleven days after the first one came up on the eighteenth. The practice ran on nine of those eleven days and the number was four on all nine of them, and nobody wrote any of the nine down and nobody was told about them.

There is one thing he has never said out loud about those two sheets, and it is that he wanted to have been in that room for the second one as much as for the first, and about nine people in that market street have worked out that a man who goes and stands at the back of a hired room about a thing nine days off is doing the same thing nine days running. He had thought about it on the Friday and had decided that wanting to be in a room is about the room and not about the stair.

It came up on a Crown's cart with a driver of about forty-four who did not know what was in it and was not asked and did not ask, and he was still in that market town two days later because his load was not coming. He read the whole of it out loud once, standing up, in the same room at the high end of that street, and about nine people heard all of it and about five did not, and nobody asked him to read it again.

The sheet says that the upper terraces are out of the hands of the men who held them. It says that the place is still held from below. It says that the central engine is damaged, and that the damage is not a victory and is not anybody's doing that anybody in this county can name, and it says in one line near the foot of that page that the Warden of that place came down off it on the twenty-second of Longlight and gave herself up, and that she is to be tried.

The name of that Warden is written out at the foot of that line in a clerk's hand, because a line about a person in a document carries a name and a line about a person in a mouth does not, and about nine people in this county know what a document is for and about four have said that this is the whole of what one is for. Nobody in this county had heard that name in about eleven volumes of anything, and about nine people who heard all of that line did not write it down and about five of them did not hear it at all.

There is a woman standing in one line on a sheet a long way off, and he could not have said her name out loud in that room and had a reason for it, being that this county learned four and a half years ago that asking a person to be the one who keeps something is the last thing anybody should do to her.

About four people in nine counties have worked out that a person who gives herself up in a week when a place nine days off has been lost by somebody is not doing it for the place, and about five have said that what a person does in a week like that is a thing about the person and not about the door. Nobody in nine counties has asked how the trial goes and nobody in this county has been asked anything about it.

A man has gone down into the lower archive of that place and has not come out. That is one line and it is on the sheet and nobody in nine counties has seen the archive and nobody in this county has asked about the man, and about four people in nine counties have said that a lost man in a lower room is a thing nine counties read about and cannot do one single thing about, and about five have said the same about about four hundred places.

There is no place in this county that that sheet is now in. It is not on the frame at the low end of that street and it is not in the register in the room over that weigh-house and it is not in the day-book and it is not under a fifth head, because there is no fifth head and the copyist of about twenty-six was not in that room and has been told about none of it.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and twenty-nine days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. Nothing has been written on any of those nine lines on any of those three copies and nothing has been taken off that frame. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and fifty days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and twenty-two days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and neither sheet read out at the high end of that street is one of the five.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and fifty days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. About four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has ever met another of them, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines of this county's copy.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Sunday, as it has been on every morning since the seventh of Longlight. It has not stood open on a market morning since the seventh of Longlight and nobody stands in that doorway. The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it was not opened once in a hundred and eighty-five days, and the county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for.

Four days out, a hundred and sixteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on Longlight the twenty-eighth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever stood at the top of that lane and watched one go up.

Across the head of that cart track there are still nine hurdles set foot to foot, a hurdle shoe on the top one and its thumb of lime gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and twenty-eight days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut five hundred and nine days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle has been lifted and no bar has been tried.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the first of Rainmonth is a Sunday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off that causeway and the hand bell on that gate post is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and seventy-six days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them, and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

Five frames of brood stand under about nine straw skirts in that walled yard and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide with the mat of it thicker than it was a month ago, and a thicker mat is a mat that has stopped moving.

Four lines of chalk are on the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood that keeps the rain off it. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about nine people went past that door on the first of a month and about four of them looked down, and nobody has read them out loud in about four and a half years.

That is the twenty-eighth first of a month in a row to come to that frame and find nothing standing at it, and the count carries forward from twenty-seventh at the first of Longlight and is not begun again. The next one is the first of Harvestmonth, which is six days beyond the end of Rainmonth and six days past the end of what this county has been doing for seven months, and nobody in this county has written a number against it.

The rest of that Sunday went on the way a Sunday goes on in a market town of about two thousand people where about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard either of two things that were read out in a room. About nine people were in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road did the ordinary part of a Sunday.

A man of about forty-four who keeps a smithy at the high end of that street put a barrow against the front of his own door and stood in the road about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice and then went back inside.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Sunday in Rainmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

The half leaf in that press is face down and was not turned over on a first of a month. The three series in that room stay three series, none of them derived from another, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Sunday was the leg, which took four days of a made road in nine days and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and thirty-six days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. Two sheets about a place nine days off do not reach it and a name written at the foot of a line in a clerk's hand does not reach it either.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it, and neither is the sentence on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll, and neither is a name given up on the far side of a stair about nine hundred yards long. A page that has been authenticated has not been agreed to. A place that is still held has not been won and a damaged engine has not been mended. And a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and thirty-six days is not a cure.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone and did not go in at the low end of that street. Five things were on that board and one hundred and twenty-two days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.
