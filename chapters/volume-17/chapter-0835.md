# Chapter 0835

**Mudmonth the forty-eighth, YR 324, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and forty-two days after a sheet went up on those boards, seventy-nine days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of about forty-three with a stone merchant's yard asking one question at the low end of that street in his own trade and getting no answer to it.**

About four hundred people were in that street by the ninth hour and the five things were up on the frame at the low end of it, and the wet had not come off the slate, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

The door of the low room at the back of the Crown's road office stood open on its hook for the second market morning running, and about nine people were inside it before the tenth hour, and about four hundred people went in and out of that office in front of it that morning and about nine went further in than the counter.

The doorway itself stayed empty again. The two people who stood in it four months ago and did not go in are not the sort of people who come back, and nine people in that street had worked that out inside nine seconds and five had not.

The drawer eleven inches deep in that room was not opened. The county's one seal is in it with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and the drawer has not been opened once in a hundred and forty-two days, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a thing that is looked at twice and not opened twice is a thing somebody is being careful with, and about five have said that it is a drawer and a person is not careful with a drawer on an ordinary morning.

About nine people looked at a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it over about the length of two cups of something, and nobody asked anything about it. One of them asked whether the window was ever washed and the man behind the counter said that it was not, and nobody asked him anything else, and about four people in that street have said since that the first question anybody asked in that room in about nine years was about the glass.

Then, at about the eleventh hour, a man of about forty-three with a stone merchant's yard eleven miles up that made road to the north said one thing out loud about his own trade, at the low end of that market street, kindly, to about nine people, and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**I sell setts and kerb and nine feet of anything a person wants laid in a good week and four feet in a bad one, and I am told there is new kerb going down on the far bank of the causeway four days south, and I would like to know who lays it, and whether it is out of my stone.**”

It was sixty-two words. Nobody argued with him about any part of it and nobody answered him about any part of it.

About nine people were standing near enough and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it, and the answer he got was that about four people in that street did not know and about five knew a person who had not said, and one man said that the far bank was a shut door and nine hundred yards of kerb and nothing else, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a shut door is what a person calls a place they have never been, and about five have said that it is also what a place that has been a shut door to them for nine years is.

He sells about nine feet of setts a week in a good week and four feet in a bad one, and about four hundred people in nine counties buy off that yard and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not been inside it, and he has been up the Wethergill cart track twice with a cart, once in Goatmonth of the year before last and once in Harvestmonth of last year, both times to see what was laid at the end of it, and he has been told in a lane in this county that what is laid at the end of that track is not the road in four copied pages.

That was a cost and it is not an oversight, and he has not gone up there a third time, and nobody has asked him to.

The four miles of made road above that causeway four days south have been the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth, and he has never once been to that causeway with a cart, and nobody in nine counties has asked him why a man who sells stone four miles from the best four miles of made road in nine counties has not been to see them.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and eighty-six days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up, and the notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and seven days past its own date and has shut nothing. The date at the foot of the sheet nailed up beside them went past seventy-nine days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and seven days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine, and about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of them has ever met another.

Four days out, a hundred and ten drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and ten went up on the Thursday before, which was the forty-sixth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and sixty-six days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk, under the hood over that door, about nine feet above the sill, and the rain does not reach them. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and nobody has read them out loud to anybody in about four and a half years.

The register in the room over that weigh-house holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and forty-two days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none, and the fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and was told about none of it and was not told about a door or a question either. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the forty-eighth of Mudmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and eighty-five days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and sixty-six days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and thirty-three days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The weighing floor in that weigh-house is nine feet by eleven and the boards on it have been worn into a dip in the middle two inches deep by four and a half years of feet standing still on it, and a dip in a floor takes the weight off a person standing on it. Nobody has ever had it repaired, and nobody in nine counties has ever asked what a sheet weighs.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over. Four things in this county are about nine feet across.

The woman who keeps the inn at the high end of that street had four beds full and kept her board out on the made street from first light and marked four on a tally-stick by the door at noon and put the board out again after noon, which is what she does on a Saturday. The stall flap with this county's copy of the hand paper on it is the flap of a stall that sells nothing else, being a beer stall that shut in Hearthmonth, and the man who owns it takes the paper off to wash the flap on a wet morning and puts it back on the same morning, and has done so nine times.

The rest of that Saturday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Mudmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day a man asked a question and got no answer. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach that frame and stand at it for two hours, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and ninety-three days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that sixty-two words about kerb does not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a paper with a trade on it and no name. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and ninety-three days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stood open on its hook behind him with the drawer in it shut. Five things were on that board and seventy-nine days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a door open on two market mornings now with about nine people inside it and the drawer not opened and a man of about forty-four who asked about a hinge nobody has checked, and sixty-two words said out loud once in his own trade by a man who sells about nine feet of stone in a good week about a piece of kerb four days off on the other side of about nine hundred paces of causeway, and about nine people near enough and no answer. Being told the thing you went and looked at twice is not the thing you went for was a cost in this county in Mudmonth and a man has not gone back. About nine hundred yards of a stair nine days off being argued about in about four hundred places, and this county contributing nine trades and no names. One hundred and ninety-three days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and eighty-five days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
