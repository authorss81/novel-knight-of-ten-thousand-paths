# Chapter 0847

**Rainmonth the ninth, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and ninety-three days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and thirty days past the date at the foot of that sheet, eight days after a second sheet was read out once, and nothing done about either of them in this county.**

Seven people stood up on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold and said a number before the light, and the man of thirty-two was one of them on that morning and not one of the two, and about nine people on that floor gave him room the way they have given him room every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth.

It was the three hundred and ninetieth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and eighty-six mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not. The practice does not run on a market morning and there is no fourth hour in that room on a market morning and there has not been one since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth.

A place nine days ride from any town in this county is still held, and it is held from below, and its central engine is damaged. A man has gone down into the lower archive of it and has not come out. The upper terraces are out of the hands of the men who held them and that is not the same as the place being out of anybody's hands, and about nine days off from here a set of stairs about nine hundred yards long is still a set of stairs about nine hundred yards long.

A victory cannot be preserved by one person. It was said nowhere in this county that morning and it is the finding of the thing, and about four people in nine counties have said out loud that whatever happened on that stair belongs to about nine people and about nine hundred yards and not to one man, and about five have said that the finding is about nine hundred yards of stair and not about anybody at all.

He had worked out in about four and a half years that he is the only person in this county who could be named for anything, and he has never once done the naming. About four people in nine counties have said that a man who has never done it is not the same man as one who has refused to.

There was no reason on that floor why he should have, and about four people in this county have said that a man who says a thing out loud on a cold floor has made it a thing the floor knows about. About five have said that there is a list of things in this county that must not go on a cold floor and that this would be one of them.

About four hundred people in nine counties have been reading about both of those sheets for about four months. About nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard either of them. The two numbers are the same size by about nine and they are not about the same people at all, and about four people have said that a county which is written about and not told is in the position of a road everybody walks on and nobody maintains, and about five have said that is not the same as a road nobody walks on.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and thirty-seven days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. Nothing has been written on any of those nine lines on any of those three copies and nothing has been taken off that frame and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and fifty-eight days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and thirty days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and about four people in this county have said that five things on one frame is not one question and that three questions which do not agree are not three questions.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and fifty-eight days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. It has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand, and not one of the four hundred has ever met another of the four hundred and not one of the four hundred knows whether any other of them has a seal.

A paper nobody can seal binds nobody, which is safe, and binding nobody is not owing nobody and is not a freedom and is not a plan. A county which can only refuse cannot hold, which is not, and a county which cannot hold is offered an occupation and told the offer is protection, and about four people in nine counties have said that the second half of that sentence is the one that has done the damage, and about five have said nobody in this county ever wrote the first half.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Monday, as it has been on every morning since the seventh of Longlight, and it did not stand open on any market morning after the seventh of Longlight and nobody stands in that doorway. The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it was not opened once in a hundred and ninety-three days, and the county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for.

At the front of that office, about nine feet from that frame, there is a counter and behind it a man who was born in that street, and about nine things were done one after another on that counter that Monday and nothing was written on any of them. About four hundred people in nine counties have said since that a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has, and about five have said the cheapest instrument is the one nobody has looked at.

Four days out, a hundred and seventeen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on Rainmonth the fifth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road do not know that it goes.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for eight hundred and seventeen days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start. The mallet lies out on that bank with the line of stone and has not been picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

Nine hurdles are across the head of that cart track foot to foot, and the top one carries a hurdle shoe whose thumb of lime has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and thirty-six days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut five hundred and seventeen days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle has been lifted and no bar has been tried.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the ninth of Rainmonth is a Monday, and the afternoon is the lamp's. The lamp was put about nine feet from the water on a coping about nine feet wide with the hand bell on the gate post above it the size of a fist, and the water was off that causeway and the bell was not rung.

About nine people were on that coping and about four of them had come out to see the lamp go on, and the wind came off the water and went through the leg. About nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth and about four hundred yards of it is the stretch that begins at the bank, and about four hundred people over there have never once been across in about nine years.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and eighty-four days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and a place nine days off that is still held is not a cure for it either.

Five frames of brood stand under about nine straw skirts in that walled yard and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide there and it will not close over, and four things in this county are about nine feet across and none of the four has been asked about.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about nine people went past that door that Monday and about four of them looked down, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and ninety-three days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it and was told about neither sheet.

In that room a press stands open with no date on it, and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which stays face down. A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody, and nothing about either of the two sheets has gone into any of it.

There is one thing about the eight days between the reading of that sheet and that Monday, and he had wanted to spend them, being that about four people in nine counties have said that eight days is not long enough to do anything about a thing nine days off and about five have said that eight days is long enough to read about it four times.

The rest of that Monday went on the way a Monday goes on in a market town of about two thousand people. About nine people were in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not.

A carter stopped at the low end of that street to shift a load and was under it about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Rainmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of that about nine went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

A cloth lay over a bench nine doors down from those boards and stayed where it lay, and nobody in that street touched it or asked what was under it. The half leaf in that press is face down and was not turned over on the morning of the finding. The three series in that room stay three separate series and none of the three is worked out from another, and the band figure is in no document in this county.

The cost of that Monday was the leg, which took eleven miles of a made road before light and about nine of them again afterward, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and forty-four days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. A victory nine days off that cannot be preserved by one person does not reach it, and about nine people on that floor four days off did not know it was there.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it, and neither is a page in a woman's hand at the back of a day-book, and neither is the sentence on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. A page that has been authenticated has not been agreed to. A door that has been opened is not a drawer that has been opened. A place that is still held is not a place that has been won, and a damaged engine is not a thing that has been mended. And a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and forty-four days is not a cure.

He came back up that made road after dark and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and one hundred and thirty days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and a place nine days off is still held and a man in a lower archive has not come out, and nobody was thanked for any of it.
