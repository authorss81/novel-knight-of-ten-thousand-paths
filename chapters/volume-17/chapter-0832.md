# Chapter 0832

**Mudmonth the thirty-sixth, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and thirty days after a sheet went up on those boards, sixty-seven days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a hand lamp going onto a coping at a causeway four days south with four hundred yards of new kerb visible from it.**

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and forty-fifth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and forty-one mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are six lost mornings and are gone.

“A month and four days off that first morning,” the woman at the door said.

“You count them by the door and I count them by the floor and they come out the same.”

“They come out the same because there is one door and one floor.”

There is a thing about that exchange that he has never said out loud to any of the seven of them, and it is that he is the only one of them who has ever needed the number. The woman at the door counts the mornings because a room that is shut behind its own door on all but two mornings of a week gives a person very little else to hold on to, and the others count because she counts. He counts because the number is the only sentence this county has ever let him finish.

Then he was on the made road four days out at about the third hour in the afternoon, with the leg going and the stick in his right hand, and the winter hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway every Monday afternoon and the thirty-sixth of Mudmonth is a Monday, and about nine people on this side of that water have seen that lamp go on about nine hundred times and about four hundred people on the far bank of it have never seen it lit.

The lamp is a tin with a wick in it and a bail to carry it by and about four inches of tallow in it, and it is put down on the coping at the low side about nine feet from the water because that is where a person crossing on foot can see it from the far side, and a hand bell the size of a fist is on the gate post above that coping and is rung when the water is over the causeway, and the water was off it that day and the bell was not rung.

The causeway is about nine hundred paces long and about nine feet wide at the crown. About four hundred people live in a market town on the far bank of that water and about four hundred people in this county have said out loud that not one of them has ever been across it in about nine years, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never been to the far bank at all and are not going.

About four hundred yards of kerb has been laid in a line on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth. It is visible from the low side of that causeway on a day when the water is off it, and it is new enough that the joints are still pale and the ground at the back of it has not gone to grass, and the line of it runs inland from the bank past a shut door and stops, and about nine hundred yards of it has gone down since the first of Mudmonth and about nine people have been at it.

The four miles of made road above that causeway are the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties. They have been the smoothest since the fifth of Wolfmonth, and about nine people in nine counties know that they are not a county road and that somebody with a warrant came up them, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known that the road they walk on is different from the road they walk on anywhere else.

A road kept by a warrant is a road whose keeping is somebody else's decision. Nobody on this side has ever asked about that and nobody on this side has ever been told who decides it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a road being smooth is a thing you can see from a causeway four miles off and about five have said that what you cannot see from four miles off is who put it there.

He stood on that coping for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and looked at about four hundred yards of new kerb on a bank he had not been to and did not cross, and the wind came off the water and went through the leg, and about nine people were on the coping that afternoon because the lamp is a thing about nine of them come out for.

There is a road on the other side of that water that a man could walk in about an hour, and he has done the sum often enough to know it, and about nine hundred yards of new kerb is the first thing anybody has laid on that bank since before this county had a hearing anybody could call. He wanted to cross. He had wanted to cross for about four days and he had about as much idea now of what he would say on the far side as he had of what the far side is, and that was the whole of the argument, and it held.

A woman of about fifty-one who keeps the inn at the high end of that street kept her board out on the made street from first light and had four beds full and marked four on a tally-stick by the door before noon, and she did not put the board out after noon, which is what she does on a Monday in this county. A boy of about eleven went along that made street with a stick and turned over nine pieces of shingle off it in about an hour and put them on a wall, and did it again on the Tuesday. The ditch at the low side of that road has been cut about four feet deep in one place by two winters of water and about nine people have fallen into it in nine years and about four of them told anybody.

The rest of that Monday went on. About nine people were in that market street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not. A carter's wheel came off at the low end of that street and he had it up and on again inside about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and came down those boards and found them, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and seventy-four days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and the notice nailed above those three copies is three hundred and ninety-five days past its own date and has shut nothing, and the date at the foot of the sheet beside them is sixty-seven days past and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on that sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and thirty days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for ninety-five days and it has nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has ever met another, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines of this county's copy, and nobody in nine counties knows who else in nine counties has one.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for. At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep, and that room was shut behind its own door on that Monday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and thirty days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for.

Four days out, a hundred and eight drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on the Thursday before, which was the thirty-second of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and fifty-four days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

The rope on the hand lamp at that causeway is twelve inches long and is spliced at the bail, and the lamp has been carried out to that coping about nine hundred times in about nine winters and has been dropped in the water twice and fished out both times by a man who was going across for another reason. The coping is nine feet wide and is set with a fall of about four inches in every nine, and a person who walks it at night without the lamp does not fall, because the crown of it is higher than the edges, and about nine people on that bank know that and about four hundred people have never been told it.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody near enough to lift it and nobody near enough to ask. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day the lamp was put on a coping. Three series stand in that room, none of them derived from another, and the band figure is written down nowhere in this county.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and seventy-three days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and fifty-four days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and twenty-one days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and four things in this county are about nine feet across and about nine people in nine counties have asked why and about four have answered.

The cost of that Monday was the leg, which did eleven miles of a made road and about four hundred yards of a lane either side of it and had a hot band across the knee by about the sixth hour, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and eighty-one days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that looking at four hundred yards of somebody else's work from a coping does not touch. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence in an unclaimed hand on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and eighty-one days.

He came back up that made road that night and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door, and five things were on that board and sixty-seven days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these four days off is a lamp put on a coping at the low side of a causeway about four days south on the one afternoon of the week it goes there, and about four hundred yards of new kerb on a bank nobody on this side has asked about, and about nine hundred yards of it since the first of Mudmonth by people nobody on this side has asked what they are paid for or by whom. The four miles above that lamp are the smoothest four miles in nine counties because somebody with a warrant came up them, and this county has walked on one for about six months and has not asked, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth. One hundred and eighty-one days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and seventy-three days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
