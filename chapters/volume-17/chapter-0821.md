# Chapter 0821

**Thawmonth the twentieth, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, eighty-four days after a sheet went up on those boards, twenty-one days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and four copied pages coming up that made road in the ordinary post with the man who had copied them.**

The street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm. Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and its corner had lifted where the damp came up that wall, and nobody had put it back down and nobody in nine counties had ever seen it.

The five things were one printed notice gone soft at the edges with the wet, three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on top of it, and the sheet a carter had nailed up beside the roll and not over it on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. Eighty-four days of it, and the count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the day the fifth of them went up.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and twenty-eight days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. The notice above them is three hundred and forty-nine days past its own date and has shut nothing. The date at the foot of the sheet went past twenty-one days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. About nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road never knew there was a date on it at all.

The cart came up at about the eighth hour in the ordinary post, being the fourth of them to come up that road since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and the driver did not know what was in the two satchels under his seat and was not asked and did not ask. More paper has come into this county in about four and a half years than has ever gone out of it, and about four hundred places are writing it out by hand besides, and about nine hundred people along that road have not heard of any of it.

On the seat beside him was a man of about forty-four with a bundle on his knees about nine inches deep and about eleven inches wide, wrapped twice, once in brown paper and once in oiled cloth, and tied with a cord that had been tied and untied and tied again. He set it down at the low end of that market street and stood holding it the way a man stands holding a thing he has carried a long way and is not going to put down in the wet.

He asked at the frame whether the woman of about twenty-nine was in. The man of thirty-two with the stick, who was standing about four feet off with the market going round him, said that she was, and said nothing else, and the man of about forty-four said that he had four sheets for her and that he had copied them and had not written them.

Then the man of thirty-two asked him one question about his own trade, kindly, at the low end of that market street, because he wanted to know what a person in this county does with a document that no clerk will touch.

About nine people were near enough for the answer and the market went on round them.

“**I copy what people in this county cannot pay a clerk to copy, and I have copied about nine hundred of them in about nine years, and the copy is the whole of what I do, and the paper it is on is theirs and not mine.**”

It was forty-seven words. He said them the way a person says a thing he has said to himself in about nine years and has never once said out loud in a street. About four people heard all of it and about five heard the end of it, and nobody argued with him about any part of it.

Then the man of thirty-two asked him what to call him, because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and the man told him, and it was Tosh Sennen, and about nine people in that street had watched him bring a bundle up eleven miles of made road and had not known until that morning what to call him.

About nine people in that street did not ask him whether the four sheets were right, and he had been waiting about nine years for one of them to. He said that he has read back about nine of the about nine hundred things he has copied and that he had begun to think of reading back as a favour he does people, and that it is not a favour, being that it is the other half of the trade and that about nine hundred people who cannot pay a clerk have never once been asked it by anybody in nine counties.

The woman of about twenty-nine came down those thirteen stairs at about that hour with the day-book under her arm and she looked at the outside of the bundle without touching it, and she had known what was in it before it came up the road, and about four people in that street had worked that out inside nine seconds and about five had not.

She said one sentence to him, in her own trade, asked to a person who copies documents for people who cannot pay a clerk, and the market was going on round the two of them while she said it.

“**Take them up and set them on the board by the sill, and I will tell you by the second hour tomorrow whether what is on them is what somebody put on them, and that is the whole of it.**”

It was forty words. Neither of them wrote it down. About four people heard the whole of it and about five heard the end of it, and the man of about forty-four said that a fortnight was what he had allowed himself and that a fortnight was longer than he had ever waited for anybody in this county.

The bundle went up those thirteen stairs at about the tenth hour and came back down in the hands of the copyist of about twenty-six with the cord off and the brown paper off, and there were four sheets of foolscap on the board under that sill in four different hands. About nine people saw them laid out and about four of them looked at them twice.

The four sheets went up those stairs and not one thing went in with them. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none that morning. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six was in the room for the whole of it and was told about none of it and ruled no head. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On a market morning the practice does not run at all, and that is the whole of what happened in that room this morning, as it has been on every market morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. There is no fourth hour in that room this morning and there has not been one on any market morning since that day, and the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs is lit at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week when the market does not come, and it goes out at about the sixth. The number last said out loud on that floor is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and has not been asked for.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the tenth hour with a sheet of foolscap in front of him and a pen in his hand and nothing to write on it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Thursday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Four days out, a hundred and two drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and a hundred and two went up that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up, and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up, and a figure that has gone past a hundred is a figure nobody argues about.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and eight days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it lies in a line on that bank, about nine feet of it, which is the width of the made road.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the coping is about nine feet wide, and the twentieth of Thawmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it, foot to foot, with a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on that shoe gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and twenty-seven days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and eight days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to this year. Nine houses about four hundred yards back put those hurdles there themselves and would like them to stay.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and seventy-five days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

In a walled yard about a mile off there are five frames of brood under about nine straw skirts, and a board lies on the skirt stones at that gate and has been put back on every time it has been taken off, and the young man of about nineteen has gone in among them about nine times this week with a pail and has not lifted a skirt and has not added a sixth.

The rest of that Thursday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Thawmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

Then the man of thirty-two stood at that frame for the last half hour with the leg going and the stick in his right hand and touched nothing on it, and kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had come down that lane to see what eighty-four days of a sheet had produced, and what it had produced by the eleventh hour of that Thursday was four sheets of foolscap in four hands and a man of about forty-four carrying them up eleven miles of made road in the ordinary post.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise and is worse than it was at the close of the last year, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and thirty-five days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that nobody in this county could enter it in anything. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and thirty-five days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door.

**And the cost of these four sheets coming up a made road in the ordinary post is a man of forty-four who has copied about nine hundred documents for people in this county who cannot pay a clerk and who has read back about nine of them and who had begun to think that reading back is a favour he does people, four sheets of foolscap in four hands on a board under a sill with a register that holds ten questions and gained no entry and a day-book that stands at eleven entries and gained none and a fourth ruled head still empty and no fifth head ruled anywhere in this county, twenty-one days past a date that has shut nothing in nine counties, two hundred and twenty-eight days of a roll on a frame with the fourth line empty on all three of its copies, three hundred and forty-nine days past a printed notice that has shut nothing, a hundred and two drove going up a lane nobody in nine counties has ever watched go up, a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street and nobody in nine counties having asked him what a gate is for, a low room shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, one hundred and thirty-five days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and twenty-seven days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
