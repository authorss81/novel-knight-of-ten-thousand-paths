# Chapter 0830

**Mudmonth the twenty-eighth, YR 324, a Sunday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, a hundred and twenty-two days after a sheet went up on those boards, fifty-nine days past the date at the foot of that sheet, three days of these six weeks after this one and the fourth day a Tuesday.**

There were about nine people in that street at the ninth hour and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since noon on the Friday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and sixty-six days.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and thirty-ninth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and thirty-five mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are six lost mornings and are gone.

“A page that is authenticated is not consented to.”

“That was the sixth of Mudmonth and it is the ninth word I have heard said out loud about a piece of paper in about four and a half years.”

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and twenty-two days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on the top shelf of it above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about four hundred people a week go past that door and about nine of them look down, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in about four and a half years, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

At about the seventh hour the woman of about twenty-nine said one thing out loud, in her own trade, asked to about nine people in a room over a weigh-house on a Sunday, and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**A room over a weigh-house is for keeping what people cannot keep, and that is all it has ever been for, and four pages and ten questions and eleven entries are three things in one room and none of them is a decision.**”

It was forty-three words. Nobody argued with her about any part of it and nobody was thanked for it, and about four people in nine counties have said that a woman who keeps records for fourpence a year has now described her own room accurately and about five have said that accuracy about a room is not the same as holding it.

Then, at about the ninth hour, at the low end of that market street, the man of thirty-two with the stick stood at the frame with about nine people in that street and no market in it, and the frame is about four feet off the wall and about five feet high, and he touched nothing on it.

The five things on that frame are one printed notice, three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on top of it, and a sheet with a date on it nailed up beside the roll and not over it. The count of things is five and the count of questions is three and the three questions do not agree with one another, and that has been the standing of that frame for a hundred and twenty-two days.

The date at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth YR 323 and it is fifty-nine days past. Nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The notice above the roll is three hundred and eighty-seven days past its own date and has shut nothing. The roll is two hundred and sixty-six days on that frame with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper.

This county's copy of the hand paper is still on the flap of that stall at the low end of that street. It has nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and it has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand and no two of about four hundred copies agree with one another because nobody has asked them to, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of its nine lines.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street, which is about nine feet from that frame, there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the ninth hour with a sheet of foolscap in front of him and a pen in his hand and nothing to write on it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Sunday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it in this six weeks, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and twenty-two days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Two people have stood in the doorway of that room in about four months and neither of them has gone in. About four people in nine counties have said that a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has and about five have said that it is the one nobody in nine counties has looked at, and neither of those is on any paper in this county and neither of them has been said out loud in that street.

Four days out, a hundred and seven drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on the Thursday before, which was the twenty-fifth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up. A hundred and seven is about a hundred and ten and about four hundred people in nine counties have never known what any of them weighs.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and forty-six days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and it does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years and nobody lifted it and nobody tried it.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth is a Sunday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot with a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on that shoe gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and sixty-five days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and forty-six days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle was lifted and no bar was tried in these six weeks. The ninth thing is alive and unnamed and unheld and shut, and about nine houses about four hundred yards back behind those hurdles put them there themselves and would like them to stay and have not been asked about anything in four months.

A man of about forty-three with a stone merchant's yard has been told in a lane in this county that the road behind those nine hurdles is not the road in four copied pages, and that nobody in nine counties has ever known what the road in those four copied pages is, and about four people heard all of it and about five did not, and being told the thing you went and looked at twice is not the thing you went for.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and thirteen days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and it is not an oversight.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and four things in one county are about nine feet across and not one of the four has been asked about.

The rest of that Sunday went on. There were about nine people in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not, and about four hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not been inside that office in about four and a half years. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Sunday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Sunday was in the leg and the ear and a hundred and seventy-three days. The leg takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise and is worse than it was at the close of the last year, and the ear on the side the stick is on is gone, and it is the side she spoke from, and it always will be.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and seventy-three days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that has had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and seventy-three days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and fifty-nine days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and the four copied pages were in the room over the weigh-house and not in his keeping, and the re-anchoring had been refused on the sixth of Mudmonth and nobody had argued with him about it and nobody had been thanked for it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is four copied pages authenticated in her own trade and left in the room that keeps them, nine words said out loud that a page authenticated is not consented to, a refusal honoured on a Saturday and not argued with and not asked about, and a name read in a room by one man and said out loud by nobody and put in no book. A man of forty-three went up a shut cart track twice with a cart and was told in a lane in this county that the thing he went and looked at twice is not the thing he went for, and that is a cost and not an oversight and it is not resolved. Nine houses about four hundred yards back behind nine hurdles they put there themselves have not been asked about anything in four months and have said nothing and are not going to. Nothing on that frame was cured. One hundred and seventy-three days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and sixty-five days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
