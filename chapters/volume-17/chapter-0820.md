**Thawmonth the nineteenth, YR 324, a Wednesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, eighty-three days after a sheet went up on those boards, twenty days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and about four hundred places writing a paper that nobody in nine counties can seal.**

There were about nine people in that street at the ninth hour and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since noon on the Saturday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and twenty-seven days and about four hundred people have walked past it in about seven and a half months.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and twelfth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The man with the bad hip put nothing on a board or a page, because there is nowhere in this county to put it. The six mornings this county lost to the nine days of water in Mudmonth are six lost mornings and nobody recovers them.

“Six weeks,” the woman at the door said.

“Six weeks and a day.”

“And it has been four for the whole of it.”

“It has been four for three hundred and eight mornings and it was eight before the water and nobody has worked out why it was eight and nobody is going to ask, and about four people in nine counties have said that a number that was eight for a year and four for the year after is not a number anybody has to explain.”

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in eighty-three days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in eighty-three days. The fourth ruled head of that page has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Four lines of chalk are on that sill and they mean four and nobody has added to them since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nobody has read them out loud to anybody in about four and a half years. Nine people went past that door on the nineteenth of Thawmonth and about four of them looked down. The stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and has not been asked for.

Then, at about the ninth hour, at the low end of that market street, the man of thirty-two with the stick stood at the frame with nine people in that street and no market in it and touched nothing on it.

The five things on that frame are one printed notice, three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on top of it, and a sheet with a date on it nailed up beside the roll and not over it. The count of things is five and the count of questions is three and the three questions do not agree with one another, and that has been the standing of that frame for eighty-three days.

The date at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth YR 323 and it is twenty days past. Nothing in nine counties is shut on it. About nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road never knew there was a date on it at all. The notice above the roll is three hundred and forty-eight days past its own date and has shut nothing. The roll is two hundred and twenty-seven days on that frame with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, on a Wednesday morning with nine people in it and nobody saying anything, the thing this county has spent four and a half years arriving at arrived.

About four hundred places are writing a paper by hand because nobody has a seal to give them, and about four hundred places is a figure nobody in nine counties has arrived at by counting, and about four people in nine counties have said that about four hundred is what is left after about nine counties of offices have found that they cannot do it, and about five have said that about four hundred is a county's worth of people who have heard about this somewhere and about nine hundred along eleven miles of made road here have not heard anything.

This county has joined them. Its copy has nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and it has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of its nine lines, and nobody in nine counties knows who else has a copy.

About four hundred copies of that sheet are in about four hundred places and no two of them agree with one another, because nobody has asked them to. That is the whole reason they do not agree. Two copies of the same printed sheet written out by hand by two people who have never met and were never told there was another one will differ in about nine lines out of about nine, and the difference is not carelessness, and it is not anybody keeping something back.

About four people in nine counties have said since that no two of about four hundred copies agree because nobody has asked them to, and about five have said that about four hundred places copying a sheet by hand is the largest single act of mutual aid in nine counties in about a year and is not a document, and about four have said that a thing which is not a document and which about four hundred places have each got a copy of is either the largest thing anybody has done or nothing at all, and nobody in nine counties can tell which and nobody is going to be asked.

This county's copy is one of the about four hundred and it is the only one anybody in this county knows about. It is on the flap of a stall at the low end of that market street and it has been there about six weeks, and about four people in nine counties know of it and about five do not, and it was ruled with a straight edge and a knife on the flap of a stall that sells nothing but a board, and the nine trades on it are a wheelwright, a saddler, a tanner, a slater, a cooper, a miller, a smith, a drover and a woman who keeps a dairy, written in one hand by a person nobody in nine counties can name.

A paper nobody can seal binds nobody.

That is safe. It is the safest thing anybody in this county has ever had on a frame, being that there is nothing on it that anybody in nine counties has to obey and there is no name on it that anybody in nine counties can be blamed for having written, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road could walk up to that frame tomorrow morning and take it down and nobody could say they had stolen anything.

A county of about two thousand people which can only refuse cannot hold.

That is not safe and it is not a finding and it is not anybody's fault. It has been said out loud in this county about four and a half times in about four and a half years, once in a lane by a person who refused to govern in writing and once in an inn by a person who had a copy of a roll in a drawer under a stair and once on a market morning in front of about four hundred people about one ear and one leg, and about four people in nine counties have said that this county can refuse a paper, a form, a date, a name, a road and a room, and that it cannot hold a paper, a form, a date, a name, a road or a room.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a county which has about nine hundred names against their trades in a book four miles off and about nine trades against no names on a flap of a stall has everything it would need to hold and no place to put any of it, and about five have said that a place to put it is a ruled head in a register that already has ten questions and no finding under any of them, and that nobody in nine counties has been asked for that head in any trade but one, and about four have said that the one is a man who keeps a book of about nine hundred names and that he asked for it in his own trade and to his own advantage and was refused in his own trade nine days ago.

About four people in nine counties have said that neither half of that is a rescue and about five have said that neither half is a plan. A paper nobody can seal is not a freedom and is not the same as a paper nobody has to obey, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road could walk up to that frame tomorrow morning and take all five things down, and about four of them might, and about five would not, and nobody in nine counties could say they had stolen anything. A county which can only refuse cannot hold, and a county which cannot hold is offered an occupation and told the offer is protection, and both of those are true and neither of them is anybody's fault and neither of them is resolved.

He stood at that frame for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and let it be that, because he had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and a county which can say no very well and cannot say yes to anything at all is a county that has spent four and a half years being extremely careful with about nine words, and about four people in nine counties have said that about nine words in four and a half years is not a mistake and about five have said that it is not a method either.

At the front of that office there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and at about the tenth hour he had a sheet of foolscap in front of him and a pen in his hand and nothing to write on it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Wednesday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. Nobody in nine counties went into it, and the man of thirty-two was not in it and was not at its door and was four hundred paces away at that frame. The drawer eleven inches deep in it was not opened once in six weeks. The county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights are still in it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has, being that it needs nobody to travel and nobody to be paid and nobody to agree, and about five have said that the cheapest instrument an occupation has is also the one nobody in nine counties has looked at, and about four have said that the cheapest instrument in this county is a form with nine empty lines on it which nobody has torn up.

Then the man of thirty-two stood at that frame for the last half hour of that morning with the leg going and the stick in his right hand and nothing in his mouth, and he kept it to about the eleventh hour.

The cost of that Wednesday was in the leg, which takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise and is worse than it was at the close of the last year, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and thirty-four days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and thirty-four days.

The scar behind the ninth of the nine stones is four hundred and seventy-four days old and it is not cured and it is not going to close over, and the dead grass in it is about nine hundred yards long and nine feet wide and it stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road on this side of them have never known they were there.

The rest of that Wednesday went on and would have gone on if nobody had ever come down that lane. There were nine people in that street and no more than nine, and about four of them went into that office during the morning for a halfpenny copy of something and about five did not. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and marked the two occupied ones on its tally-stick by the door and put no board out on the made street, because there was nothing left to fill.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Wednesday in Thawmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the nineteenth of Thawmonth is a Wednesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

Four days out, a hundred and one drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the hundredth of them went up on the sixth of Thawmonth into a field with a stone wall round it with two men, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the hundredth of anything is a figure somebody writes down and about five have said that nobody in nine counties has watched one go up. Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and seven days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up.

The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it lies in a line on the bank. The bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years and nobody lifted it and nobody tried it.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in. At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder, and the last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and twenty-six days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and seven days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to this year.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county. Nobody has been past the ninth of the nine stones and the far side of them is not shown and the omission is a decision.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and he did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door.

Five things were on that board and three questions on it that do not agree with one another, the fourth line of the roll was empty on all three of its copies and was still empty, and the roll was not filled in and was not taken down and was not torn up, and the new sheet stayed nailed up beside the roll and not over it.
