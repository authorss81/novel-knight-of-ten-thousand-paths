# Chapter 0833

**Mudmonth the thirty-ninth, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and thirty-three days after a sheet went up on those boards, seventy days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and the door of the low room at the back of that office standing open on its hook for the first time in about nine years.**

That market filled the street by the ninth hour and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

The door of the low room at the back of the Crown's road office stood open on its hook. It is nine inches narrower than the front door of that office and it is behind the counter, and it opens into one room with a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep in the wall on the left as you come in.

About nine people were inside it at about the tenth hour. Two of them were not in there for anything in particular and one of them was there because the man behind the counter had said the room was open and had said it to about nine people in that street and about four of them had heard him.

The doorway itself stayed empty. Two people have stood in that doorway in about four months and neither of them went in, being a man of about thirty-four who came up eleven miles of made road with an empty cart and a man of about forty-two who keeps a smithy at the high end of that street, and about nine people in that market street on that Thursday did not stand in it and went in instead.

He watched the doorway from about thirty feet off it and thought about the two men who had stood in it, and about why a third person should have gone in while those two did not, and could not get an answer that was not about the weather or about the hour. A door that has been shut behind its own door for nine years is shut on the outside too. He was about nine feet from it at the time and he had not gone in either, and there was a sort of honesty in that which he distrusted.

The room is about nine feet by eleven and it is colder in it than in the office because the window is nine inches by four and high and has not been washed since before anybody now in that office was born. There is a counter along one side of it with nothing on the counter, and a clerk's table with a stool, and a press against the far wall with no date on it and about four inches of daylight above the door of it, and a drawer eleven inches deep set into the wall at the height of a person's hip.

About nine people looked at all of that over about the length of two cups of something. Nobody touched anything. One of them put a hand on the edge of the clerk's table and took it off again, and about four people in that street have said since that a person who puts a hand on a table and takes it off again has been in a room, and about five have said that a room with a table in it and nothing on the table is a room about a thing that is kept somewhere else.

The drawer was not opened. It was not opened by anybody in that room on that Thursday and it was not opened by anybody else that day, and it is eleven inches deep and the county's one seal is in it with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody asked what a gate is for and nobody asked what a set of standard weights is for either.

The man behind the counter said one thing out loud about it, in his own trade, to about nine people, and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**That door is open because the hinge has been stiff in this wet since the first of Mudmonth and I have not had anybody in to look at it, and it will be shut again on Friday and it will be open on Saturday if the wet keeps up.**”

It was forty-nine words. Nobody argued with him about any part of it and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and nobody in nine counties has asked him whether that is why it is open.

About four people in that street have said since that a man telling nine people a door is open because a hinge has been stiff is a man saying something true, and about five have said that he would have said the same about a door opened for any other reason and nobody asked him which, and about four have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked what is in that room and about five have said that a room being open is not a thing being taken out of it.

A door that is open is not a drawer that is opened. About nine people went into that room and came out of it and the drawer is where it was, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine people in a room is a number and a drawer eleven inches deep with the county's one seal in it is a different kind of thing, and neither of those two is on any paper in this county.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and seventy-seven days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it has not been filled in and has not been taken down and has not been torn up. The notice nailed above those three copies is three hundred and ninety-eight days past its own date and has shut nothing, and the date at the foot of the sheet nailed up beside them went past seventy days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road do not know there is a date on anything.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for ninety-eight days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any of the nine and no seal on it, and about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand and no two of about four hundred copies agree, because nobody has asked them to and nobody has shown one to another, and not one of the four hundred has ever met another.

At the front of that office, which is about nine feet from that frame with the five things on it, there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and about nine things were done one after another on that counter that morning and nothing was written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

The register in the room over that weigh-house holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and thirty-three days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and was told about none of it and was not told about a door being open either. The press in that room stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Four days out, a hundred and nine drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and nine went up that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and fifty-seven days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years and nobody lifted it and nobody tried it.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and the standing in this county is that not one of them has ever been across in about nine years.

The four miles of made road above that causeway have been the smoothest four miles in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth and nobody in nine counties has asked anybody about it, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in about four and a half years.

Nine hurdles are still set across the head of that cart track foot to foot, the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and seventy-six days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and fifty-seven days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and twenty-four days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the board is still on the skirt stones at that gate, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and four things in this county are about nine feet across.

The rest of that Thursday went on the way a market morning goes on. A wain came round the low end of that street with a load on it that was not tied properly and two men had nine seconds to get it before it went, and nobody stopped on account of that either. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped, and the inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Mudmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and one of the four stood at the frame for about as long as it takes to read the sheet at the low end of that street twice and touched nothing on it.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody within reach of it or asking after it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day a door was open. There are three series in that room and none of them is worked out from another, and the band figure is in no book in this county.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach that frame and had a hot band across the knee by about the eleventh hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and eighty-four days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that a door standing open in a room about nine feet by eleven does not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is whatever is on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll in a hand nobody has claimed. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and eighty-four days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office was shut on its own hook behind him the way it is shut. Five things were on that board and seventy days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of an open door is about nine people standing in a room about nine feet by eleven with a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep, and the drawer not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it, and nobody asking what a seal is for. A man said forty-nine words about a stiff hinge and nobody asked him whether that was why it was open. Two people stood in that doorway in about four months and neither of them went in and about nine people went in on this morning and came out again. A door that is open is not a drawer that is opened and a room that has been looked at is not a thing that has been taken out of it, and the drawer was not opened once in a hundred and thirty-three days and is not going to be. One hundred and eighty-four days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and seventy-six days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
