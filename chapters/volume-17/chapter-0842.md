# Chapter 0842

**Longlight the eighteenth, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it and the man of thirty-two four days off a made road and not on it, one hundred and seventy-two days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and nine days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and the two satchels opened at about the eighth hour.**

Four days out, seven people stood up on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold and said a number before the light, and the man of thirty-two was not one of them and was about nine miles short of the head of that made road. It was the three hundred and seventy-fifth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and seventy-one mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are gone and a month and four days have not brought them back.

About nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part of a Monday is that a made road carries about four ninths of what it carries on a Saturday and that about nine hundred of those people did not know that anything had come past them in it.

Four days out, a hundred and fourteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on Longlight the fourteenth, which was the Thursday, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up, and about nine people along that made road had walked behind one on a Tuesday in Harvestmonth and had told nobody.

Then the two satchels came off the shelf under that window at about the eighth hour and were opened on the counter of the office at the low end of that market street, because about nine people were in that street and no more than about nine on a Monday, and the man behind the counter did not open them in a room and did not take them up the four hundred pace lane to the woman of about twenty-nine.

There was one sheet in the first satchel and it was not for that office and it was not a third copy of anything. A carter of about thirty was in the room at the high end of that street with the load his cart was waiting on still to come, and he read the whole of it out loud once, standing up, in about nine minutes.

About nine people in that room heard all of it and about five did not. Nobody asked him to read it again, and nobody asked him to read it a second time after about a quarter of an hour had gone by, and about four of the about nine had understood every word of it and about five had not understood the middle of it at all.

The sheet says that at about the sixth hour on the fourth of Longlight, which was fourteen days before that Monday, people with a Crown's warrant and about nine others, among them people who used to sit in an office four hundred miles off from here, went up about nine hundred yards of a stair at a place nine days ride from any town in this county. The gate at the top of that stair is held, and the people on it are not leaving.

It says also that the seat which gave the warrant has not sat for about four months and that about four hundred places are writing a paper by hand because nobody has a seal to give them, and it carries no seal and it is not the roll and it is not the notice and it is not the sheet that has been on that frame since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. It says nothing whatever about the roads of this county and it does not ask this county for anything.

He was four days off on a made road when that sheet came up that road and he was not in that market street when it was read out in it. About nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road heard it about as long after it happened as about four hundred people in nine counties heard about the thing it was reporting, and neither figure is about him and neither figure has anything to do with whether he was standing there.

A man who carries other people's paper up four miles of made road does not open it, and there is a counted thing about that which is not a finding and is not a fault. About four people in nine counties have said that an ordinary post is the one instrument in this business that nobody has found a way to use, and about five have said that nobody has found a way to use it yet.

“**I carried two satchels up four miles of made road off a cart that came up on Thursday and I did not open either of them and nobody asked me what was in them and I did not ask, and I read the one out on Monday because I am here until Tuesday and the man who takes my load does not come till Wednesday.**”

It was sixty-five words. He said them standing at the near end of a table in a room he had paid for with his own dinner, and about four people heard all of them and about five heard the end of them, and nobody wrote any of it down.

He had wanted to ask her one thing and did not, and what he had wanted to ask was not a good question, being that the only thing he could have asked was whether that sheet was going to change anything on that frame. He had thought about it on the fourth of the four days and had decided it would not, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road had decided nothing at all.

There is no place in this county that the sheet is now in. It is not on the frame at the low end of that street and it is not in the register in the room over that weigh-house and it is not in the day-book and it is not under a fifth head, because there is no fifth head and nobody has ruled one and the copyist of about twenty-six was not in that room and has been told about none of it.

There is a road on that lane and he had worked out something about it on the fourth of the four days, being that an ordinary post is the only thing that has ever carried news into this county and that the same post carries out more paper than it carries in. About four hundred hand copies went the other way in four months and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard a word of any of it.

About four people in nine counties have said that the two figures are the same size by about nine and are not about the same people at all, and about five have said that a road which carries paper both ways and news neither way is a road nobody in nine counties is on.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and sixteen days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. Nothing has been written on any of those nine lines and nothing has been written over any of them and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and thirty-seven days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and nine days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and a sheet read out in a room at the high end of that street changes none of the three.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and thirty-seven days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. Nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine, and about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand, and not one of the four hundred has ever met another of the four hundred.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Monday, as it has been on every morning since the seventh of Longlight, and it did not stand open on that Monday because a Monday is not a market morning and nobody was in it.

The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it was not opened once in a hundred and seventy-two days. The county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody has asked what a gate is for.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot with a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on the shoe of that one gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and fifteen days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and ninety-six days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth.

The bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has not been lifted and has not been scheduled for lifting. About nine houses about four hundred yards back behind those nine hurdles put them there themselves and would like them to stay, and nobody has asked them one thing in five months and nobody is going to.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and ninety-six days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start. The mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up, and the third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the eighteenth of Longlight is a Monday, and the afternoon is the lamp's. About nine feet from the water, on a coping about nine feet wide, with the hand bell on the gate post above it the size of a fist, and the water was off that causeway and the bell was not rung.

About nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth and about four hundred yards of it is the stretch that begins at the bank. About four hundred people on that bank have never once been across in about nine years, and nobody on this side has put a lamp on anything for them and nobody on this side has been asked to.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and sixty-three days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them, and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard stand under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four. The young man of about nineteen has said out loud three things in that street in about four and a half years and nobody has asked him for an account of any of them, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide with the mat of it thicker than it was a fortnight ago.

The rest of that Monday went on the way a Monday goes on in a market town of about two thousand people where nothing has happened. About nine people were in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not.

Two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. A hound belonging to nobody in that street lay in the one place on the paving the sun came down on and stayed in it for about two hours while about nine people went round it.

The light left the west bank in about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and what it found first was the upper edge of those boards. The lamp at the high end of them stayed dark, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Longlight is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them tonight that wants a light put under it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades, and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards a cloth lay over a bench and stayed where it lay, and nobody in that street lifted it or asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over on the morning a sheet was read out at the other end of that street.

The three series in that room go on being three series and none of the three is worked out from another. There is a figure that this county has never written down and that appears in this county in no document of any kind.

The cost of that Monday was the leg, which took four days of a made road and about nine miles of it in one day and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and twenty-three days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. Nobody in nine counties knows it is there, and a sheet read out in a room four days of made road away does not come near it.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence in an unclaimed hand on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Nor is a page that has been authenticated, which is not consent, and not a place that is still held, which is not a victory, and not a damaged engine, which is not a rescue. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and twenty-three days.

He came back up that made road on the twenty-first and did not go in at the low end of that street, and he was told about the sheet on the twenty-third, after it had been read out, and neither of those two facts has any bearing on either of the others. Five things were on that board and one hundred and nine days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a sheet that came up four miles of made road in the ordinary post and was read out once in a room at the high end of that market street and was not read out again, and about nine people who heard all of it and about five who did not, and the man of thirty-two four days off on a made road when it came and told about it five days after it was read. The drawer eleven inches deep not opened once in a hundred and seventy-two days and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for. Nine trades on nine lines of a paper nobody can seal and no names on any of them. Two hundred and twenty-three days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet five hundred and fifteen days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
