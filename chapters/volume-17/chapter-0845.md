**Longlight the twenty-eighth, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and eighty-two days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and nineteen days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and the last market morning of a month carried at thirty days, and a second sheet not yet in this county.**

About four hundred people were in that street and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over that weigh-house before the light. Rainmonth begins on Sunday and Longlight has been carried at thirty days and ends on Saturday, and about nine people in that street knew what a month is and about five did not.

A Crown's cart came up that made road at about the seventh hour on the ordinary run and it carried nothing this county wanted, and about four hundred people in that street had no reason to think it had. The driver did not know that and was not asked and did not ask, and about nine people at the low end of that street saw it come past and about four of them looked at the satchels under the seat and about five did not, and nobody has asked that driver anything since.

The first sheet came up this road on the eighteenth of Longlight and was read out once in a room at the high end of that street and was not read out a second time and is not in this county now. The second one had not come up this road by the ninth hour of that Thursday, and nobody in nine counties had asked whether it was coming and nobody in nine counties had been sent to find out.

He had a reason for wanting that carter to have been in that street on that Thursday, and the reason was about nine days old and had nothing to do with the sheet. He could not have said the reason out loud in a market street with about four hundred people in it and about four of them looking at him, and about nine people in that street have worked out since that he wanted to be somewhere on a day and about five have said that is a thing everybody wants about nine days out of ten.

He had wanted to ask that carter one thing and had not, and about nine people in that street have worked out since that he wanted somebody to be in that room and not him. About four people in nine counties have said that a man who reads a thing out once in a room and then goes back to his load is not making a decision about the thing, and about five have said that he is not the kind of person who makes decisions about anything at all.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and twenty-six days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. Nothing has been written on any of those nine lines on any of those three copies and nothing has been taken off that frame, and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and forty-seven days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and nineteen days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and the first sheet read out at the high end of that street was not nailed up on it and never will be.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and forty-seven days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. About four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines of this county's copy and nobody knows who else in nine counties has one.

A line with a trade on it can be argued with and a line with a name on it cannot. A paper nobody can seal binds nobody, which is safe, and binding nobody is not owing nobody and is not a freedom and is not a plan, and about four hundred places are doing it in about four months and not one of them has met another.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Thursday and it has been shut behind its own door on every morning since the seventh of Longlight. Nobody stands in that doorway and it does not stand open. The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it was not opened once in a hundred and eighty-two days, and the county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for.

Four days out, a hundred and sixteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the hundred and sixteenth went up on Longlight the twenty-eighth at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up, and about nine hundred yards of that stair nine days off is being argued about in about four hundred places by people who have never met.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for eight hundred and six days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start. The mallet is out on that bank with the line of stone and nobody has picked it up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

Nine hurdles are across the head of that cart track foot to foot, and the top one carries a hurdle shoe whose thumb of lime has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and twenty-five days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut five hundred and six days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle has been lifted and no bar has been tried.

The bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has not been lifted and has not been scheduled for lifting, and about nine houses about four hundred yards back behind those nine hurdles would like them to stay. Nobody has asked them one thing in five months and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said that a refusal nobody has asked about is a refusal this county cannot put on a line.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-eighth of Longlight is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off that causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and seventy-three days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them, and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and a place nine days off that is still held does not cure anything four days off either.

Under about nine straw skirts in that walled yard there are five frames of brood and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four. The young man of about nineteen has carried a pail in and out of that yard about nine times this week and has not opened a skirt. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning and about four of them looked down, and nobody has read them out loud in about four and a half years.

The second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs was not lit that morning, because the room it belongs to was open and it is lit on the five mornings a week the market does not come. The register holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and eighty-two days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none, and the fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county.

The press in that room stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down. A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody, and nothing about a sheet read out in a room at the other end of that street went into any of it, and the copyist of about twenty-six was told about none of it.

The rest of that market morning went on the way a market morning goes on, and about four hundred people in that street had about four hundred reasons to be in it and about nine of them had one about a stair. A man of about forty-five set a trestle against the front of the second stall at about the tenth hour and put nine weights on it in a row and took them off again and put them back four inches along, and he has done that on every market morning for about nine years and nobody in nine counties has asked him why.

The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. A boy of about nine tried to move a hound belonging to nobody in that street and did not try a second time, and about nine people went round the hound the way they go round a thing they have agreed about without agreeing.

The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and the same room at the high end of that street was empty at about the fourth hour and had been empty at about the fourth hour for nine days, and nobody had booked it for anything, and about nine people had noticed the sign on it, and about four hundred along eleven miles of made road had not.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Longlight is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Of the about nine people at the low end of that street, five went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

A cloth lay over a bench nine doors down from those boards and stayed where it lay, and nobody in that street touched it or asked what was under it. The half leaf in that press is face down and was not turned over on the last market morning of a month. The three series in that room stay three separate series and none of the three is worked out from another, and the band figure is in no document in this county.

What that Thursday cost him was the leg, which carried him about nine miles of made road and about four hundred yards of the lane that runs beside it, and which had gone from an ache at the near end of it to a needle somewhere in the last mile of it. And there was the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and thirty-three days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. Nothing about a place nine days off, read or unread, comes near it.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence in an unclaimed hand on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Nor is a page that has been authenticated, which is not consent. Nor is a place that is still held, which is not a victory, and a damaged engine is not a rescue. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and thirty-three days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the second sheet was not in this county. Five things were on that board and one hundred and nineteen days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.
