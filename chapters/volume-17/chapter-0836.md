# Chapter 0836

**Mudmonth the fifty-fifth, YR 324, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and forty-nine days after a sheet went up on those boards, eighty-six days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and the door of the low room at the back of that office open on its hook for the third market morning running with about nine people inside it.**

That market filled the street from about the eighth hour and by nine there were about four hundred people in it, and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

The door of the low room at the back of the Crown's road office stood open on its hook for the third market morning running. About nine people were inside it by the tenth hour and about nine more were inside it by the eleventh, and the man behind the counter had not said one word about it to anybody and had been asked about it by nobody.

About four hundred people went in and out of that office in front of that door that morning, and about four hundred of them did not turn their heads at it, because a door standing open on a hook behind a counter is not a thing about four hundred people can be got to notice on a market morning.

About nine people in that room looked at a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a window about nine inches by four that has not been washed. One of them stood in front of the drawer for about as long as it takes to read a sheet at the low end of that street twice, and put a hand out about halfway, and did not touch it, and took the hand back and went out.

The drawer is eleven inches deep and it is set into the wall at the height of a person's hip. The county's one seal is in it with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and the drawer has not been opened once in a hundred and forty-nine days, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody has asked what a set of standard weights is for and nobody has asked what the ribbon is for.

A door that is open is not a drawer that is opened. A room that has been looked at is not a thing that has been taken out of it. About four people in nine counties have said since that nine people have now been inside that room three times and the drawer is still in the wall where the carpenter left it, and about five have said that a thing nobody in nine counties has asked for is the only kind of thing that stays where it is.

The man of thirty-two with the stick stood about four feet off that counter at about the eleventh hour with the market going round him and did not go in, and nobody asked him why not, and about nine people in that street saw him not go in and about four of them noticed that he had looked at the door from the outside and about five did not notice him at all.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and ninety-three days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up, and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and fourteen days past its own date and has shut nothing, and the date at the foot of the sheet nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past eighty-six days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and fourteen days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand, and not one of the four hundred has ever met another of them, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines of this county's copy.

About four hundred places writing the same paper out by hand is the largest single act of mutual aid in nine counties in about a year, and it is not a document, and about four people in nine counties have said that the two of those go together and about five have said that the second of them makes the first of them arguable.

Four days out, a hundred and eleven drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and eleven went up on the Thursday before, which was the fifty-third of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and seventy-three days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank, and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years.

The hinges of the door of that low room have been on that frame since before the office was an office and there are three of them, and the top one has about an inch of play in it and has had it since before anybody now in that street was born, and a person who opens that door does it slowly and the door is not heavy. The sill of that room is about nine feet from the front door of that office and about nine inches below it, and a person who has been in that room has come back out and up.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in about four and a half years, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fifty-fifth of Mudmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

About nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth, and four hundred yards of it is the stretch that begins at the bank and runs inland. The four miles above that causeway have been the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and ninety-two days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and seventy-three days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and forty days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the board is still on the skirt stones at that gate, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over. Four things in this county are about nine feet across and not one of the four has been asked about.

A carter came up that made road in the ordinary post at about the eighth hour with a load of lime in sacks and did not stop, and his boy of about nine got down off the tail and went into that market street and came back with a paper twist of something and gave it to him in the shaft, and neither of them stopped. A woman of about twenty-two who sells nothing but a tray of needles and string has had her pitch outside that office for nine years and has moved it back four feet from that counter twice in that time because of what goes on in front of it, and about four people in nine counties have worked out which it was the second time.

The rest of that Saturday went on the way a market morning goes on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Mudmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

The register in the room over that weigh-house holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and forty-nine days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none, and the fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and was told about none of it and was not told about a door, a drawer or a hand reaching out and stopping short of a wall. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Nine doors down from those boards the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day a hand stopped about four inches short of a drawer. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg, which takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise and is worse than it was at the close of the year before, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that a hand about four inches short of a drawer in a cold room does not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a door that is open. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stood open on its hook behind him with the drawer in it shut. Five things were on that board and eighty-six days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a door open on three market mornings with about nine people inside it each time and a drawer eleven inches deep not opened once in a hundred and forty-nine days and a hand about four inches short of it once, and nobody asking what a seal is for. About four hundred places are writing a paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has met another, and this county's contribution is nine trades and no names, and a road kept by somebody else's decision has been walked on for about six months and not asked about. One hundred and forty-nine days of a drawer that has not been opened, fifty-four days of new kerb on a bank nobody here has asked about, four hundred and seventy-three days of a shut cart track with nine hurdles across it that the people four hundred yards back put there themselves.
