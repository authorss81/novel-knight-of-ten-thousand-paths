# Chapter 0837

**Longlight the first, YR 324, a Friday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and fifty-five days after a sheet went up on those boards, ninety-two days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and the twenty-seventh first of a month in a row coming to those boards and finding five things on them and nothing standing at any of them.**

Longlight is carried at thirty days and it began on that Friday, and Mudmonth ran sixty days and ended the day before, and there is a change of month in this county twice in the time it takes a person to say a number twice before the light.

Four days out, seven people stood up on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and sixty-third morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and fifty-nine mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are six lost mornings and a month boundary does not bring them back.

“A month changes today,” the woman at the door said.

“It changes on the first, and it has done that since before I came into this county.”

“And we are three hundred and sixty-three mornings into a thing.”

“We are.”

There is one thing he has never said to the seven of them, and it is that a month coming is the only announcement any of them get. Nothing else in this county keeps time at the person. The board at the low end of that street has a date on it that went past ninety-two days ago and the register has ten questions under none of which anything is written, and about nine people in nine counties have read the hand paper on the flap of that stall and about four hundred places are writing one out by hand, and the one thing that has come to about seven people standing on a cold floor is that the month has changed and that they were still here when it did, which took about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

Longlight the first came to those boards and found a printed notice gone soft at the edges with the wet, three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on top of it with nine empty lines on each of them and the fourth of the nine this county's, and a sheet with a date on it nailed up beside the roll and not over it whose date went past ninety-two days ago.

That is the twenty-seventh first of a month in a row to come to that frame and find nothing standing at it, and the count carries forward from twenty-sixth at the first of Mudmonth and is not begun again here, and the twenty-eighth will come at the first of Rainmonth.

About nine people were in that market street at the ninth hour and no more than about nine, and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since about noon on the Thursday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and ninety-nine days, and the notice nailed above them is four hundred and twenty days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth YR 323 and it is ninety-two days past, and nothing in nine counties is shut on it, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road do not know there is a date on anything.

Four days out at about the tenth hour, the water was off that causeway four days south and the causeway is about nine hundred paces long and about nine feet wide at the crown, and from the made road where it comes off the shoulder of the hill onto the level stretch you can see about four hundred yards of new kerb on the far bank lying in a line along the made road there, and about nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth.

About nine people on this side have looked at that line of kerb and about nine hundred along eleven miles of that made road never knew the road was smooth at all, and the four miles above that causeway have been the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth. Nobody on this side has asked who laid the nine hundred yards of it or what for, and about four hundred yards of it is the stretch that begins at the bank, and about five people have said out loud that they did not know it was new.

A man of about thirty-eight who runs a weekly wain between that causeway and this market town said one true thing about those four miles out loud once, in twenty-seven words, in his own trade, on the seventeenth of Goatmonth, and about nine people heard all of it and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not heard a word of it since.

A road kept by a warrant is a road whose keeping is somebody else's decision. This county has walked on one for about six months and has not asked, and about four people in nine counties have said that a road is the easiest thing in the world to be given away by people who are not on it and about five have said that nobody in nine counties has been on that road in a cart since the fifth of Wolfmonth except about nine of them.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on that sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and fifty-five days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about nine people went past that door on the first of Longlight and about four of them looked down, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

Four days out, a hundred and twelve drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and twelve went up on the Thursday before, which was the sixtieth of Mudmonth and the last day of a month carried at sixty, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and seventy-nine days, and eight of the nine yards of that wall are back in, and the gap in it is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and it does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and twenty days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has ever met another, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street, which is about nine feet from that frame with the five things on it, there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and about nine things were done one after another on that counter that day and nothing was written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Friday, as it is on every morning the market does not come, and it had stood open on its hook on three market mornings, being the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth and the two Saturdays after it, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and fifty-five days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and ninety-eight days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and seventy-nine days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has not moved in about eleven years.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and forty-six days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over. Four things in this county are about nine feet across and none of the four has been asked about.

A Crown's cart came up that made road in the ordinary post about four times in a good month and went on up the hill in the dark that night with two sacks under the seat, and the driver did not know what was in them and was not asked and did not ask, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that road have never once been told what goes up and down it in the ordinary post. The barrow at the low end of that street had been standing against that wall since the middle of the week with its handles up, and about nine people went round it every morning of that week and about four of them stopped once to look at a handle that has been re-cut once on the wrong side.

The rest of that Friday went on. About nine people were in that market street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not. A carter stopped at the low end of that street to shift a load and was under it about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Friday in Longlight is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Of those nine, five went out into about five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked after it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on a first of a month, and a first of a month has come to those boards twenty-seven times in a row and nothing has ever been turned over on one of them in about four and a half years. Three series stand in that room and none of the three is worked out from another, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Friday was the leg, which had four days of a made road in it and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and six days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that a first of a month does not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence in an unclaimed hand on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Nor is a paper with a trade on it. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and six days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street. Five things were on that board and ninety-two days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.
