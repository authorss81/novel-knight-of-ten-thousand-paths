# Chapter 0850

**Rainmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 324, a Wednesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, two hundred and nine days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and forty-six days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and six days before the first of Harvestmonth, which is outside what this county has been doing for seven months.**

Seven months is seven months on a carried thirty-day month with Mudmonth at sixty days and every other month at thirty, and it is the second hundred and ninth day since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth of the year before, counted as a span and not as an inclusive number. Rainmonth is carried at thirty days and the twenty-fifth is inside it, and the first of Harvestmonth is six days off and belongs to whatever comes after this.

Seven people stood up on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold and said a number before the light, and the man of thirty-two was one of them that morning and not one of the two.

It was the four hundred and second morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and ninety-eight mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not. There is no fourth hour in that room on a market morning and there has not been one since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and the number has gone round every morning of seven months and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road do not know a number is said twice.

The place nine days ride off is still held, and it is held from below, and its central engine is damaged and a man is in a lower archive who has not come out, and the Warden of it came down off it on the twenty-second of Longlight and gave herself up and is to be tried. About four hundred people in nine counties have been reading about all of that for about four months and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard one word of it.

He has never once been asked what he thinks should be done about any of it and about nine people in nine counties have said that not being asked is the only part of the arrangement he would not change. About four have said that he is the only person in nine counties who could go and look at any of it and about five have said that a man who goes and looks is doing a job and not being given an honour.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and fifty-three days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. Nothing has been written on any of those nine lines on any of those three copies and nothing has been taken off that frame and nothing has been torn up, and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and seventy-four days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and forty-six days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. That sheet is a fifth thing on that frame beside three copies of a roll and it was never nailed over any of them, and the count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth.

The low room at the back of that office was open on four market mornings of these seven months, being the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth, the two Saturdays after it and the seventh of Longlight, and about nine people were inside it on each of those mornings and looked at a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it. It was shut behind its own door on every morning after the seventh of Longlight and nobody has stood in that doorway since.

The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of that room was not opened once in two hundred and nine days. The county's one seal is in it with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody has asked what a gate is for or what a set of standard weights is for, and a door that is open is not a drawer that is opened.

Across the head of that cart track there are nine hurdles set foot to foot, a hurdle shoe on the top one and its thumb of lime gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and fifty-two days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut five hundred and thirty-three days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle has been lifted and no bar has been tried.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for eight hundred and thirty-three days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start. The mallet is out on that bank with the line of stone and nobody has picked it up. A third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is still unlaid.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for six hundred days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them, and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over. Four things in this county are about nine feet across and none of the four has been asked about.

About nine people in nine counties have said that a county which notices what is about nine feet across is a county that has run out of larger things to count, and that is the only thing in seven months that anybody in this county has run out of. There are five frames of brood in that walled yard under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four.

Four days out, a hundred and nineteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on Rainmonth the nineteenth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and seventy-four days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. It has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has ever met another of the four hundred.

Nine trades on nine lines and no names on any of them and no seal on the paper, which is this county's whole contribution to about four hundred papers. About four people in nine counties have said that a line with a trade on it can be argued with and about five have said that nobody in nine counties has yet argued with one, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard that there are any lines at all.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about nine people went past that door that Wednesday and about four of them looked down, and nobody has read them out loud in about four and a half years.

The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in two hundred and nine days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it.

The press in that room stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down. A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody, and nothing about the practice, or the ten things, or the four lines of chalk, or either sheet, or the offer, or the bleed is in any document this county keeps.

The rest of that Wednesday went on the way a Wednesday goes on in a market town of about two thousand people at the twenty-fifth of a month. About nine people were in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not.

About nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road did the ordinary part of a Wednesday and about nine hundred of them have not heard about either sheet and about four hundred people in nine counties have read about both. There is a particular thing about a county that can put those two numbers side by side and not be able to do one single thing about either of them, and he had a reason for turning the page over on it, being that he had been putting the two numbers side by side since the eighteenth of Longlight and had run out of ways of saying them.

A woman of about thirty-one was at the third stall from those boards at about the eighth hour with about nine letters on a board on two trestles, and she did not read any of them out loud that morning because it was not a market morning, and about four people in that street have worked out that about nine hundred mornings a year is the whole of what she does and about five have said that nobody has ever asked her whether she would rather be doing something else.

A carter stopped at the low end of that street to shift a load and was under it about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, and about four people in that street waited for him to come out of it and about five went on. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped, and the inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-fifth of Rainmonth is a Wednesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off that causeway, and the hand bell above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people over there have never once been across in about nine years.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Wednesday in Rainmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

A cloth lay over a bench nine doors down from those boards and stayed where it lay on the last day of the seventh month of it. The half leaf in that press is face down and was not turned over on the last page of anything. None of the three series in that room is worked out from either of the other two, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Wednesday was the leg, which took eleven miles of a made road before light and about nine of them again afterward, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and sixty days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. Two hundred and sixty days is what this county has on him and nobody in nine counties has ever been told the figure, and about nine people on a floor four days off have stood next to a man with it for seven months and not one of them has known.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it, and a page in a woman's hand at the back of a day-book is not either, and neither is the sentence on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. A page that has been authenticated has not been agreed to. A door that has been opened is not a drawer that has been opened. A place that is still held has not been won and a damaged engine has not been mended. And a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and sixty days is not a cure.

He came back up that made road after dark and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door with the county's one seal in the drawer in the wall of it, and there is no first of a month in this county for another six days and that one is outside this.

On a floor above a byre four days off, in the dark, at the fourth hour, the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs four miles from here is lit, and seven people are standing on that floor, and a number is said twice in the dark by two people who are not him. On the flap of a stall at the low end of a market street in this county there is a sheet of paper with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine, and nobody was thanked for any of it.
