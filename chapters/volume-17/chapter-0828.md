# Chapter 0828

**Mudmonth the twenty-second, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, a hundred and sixteen days after a sheet went up on those boards, fifty-three days past the date at the foot of that sheet, a hand lamp going on a coping four days south that afternoon, and a refusal that has not been cured.**

There were about nine people in that street at the ninth hour and no more than about nine, and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since noon on the Friday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and sixty days, and the notice nailed above them is three hundred and eighty-one days past its own date and has shut nothing.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and thirty-fifth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and thirty-one mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water are six and are not coming back and nobody in nine counties is going to recover them.

“They have been at the boards a hundred and sixteen days,” the woman at the door said.

“They have.”

“And not one of them has a name on it.”

“Not one of the three of them has a name on any of the nine lines.”

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and sixteen days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on the top shelf of it above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. Nobody has added to them since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nobody has read them out loud to anybody in about four and a half years, and about nine people went past that door on the twenty-second of Mudmonth and about four of them looked down.

Then, at about the ninth hour, the man of about fifty-four who keeps that weigh-house floor said one thing out loud, in his own trade, to about nine people, and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**I have kept this floor about four and a half years and I have said a number out loud on it before light on the five mornings a week, and nobody in nine counties has ever asked me what a sheet weighs, and a page is not a weight, and I have not been asked about the four of them either.**”

It was sixty-one words. Nobody argued with him about any part of it, and nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not heard it, and about four hundred people in that market street have not heard it and about nine have.

At about the eleventh hour the man of thirty-two with the stick went up that made road as far as the head of it, which is about four days of walking off and about nine miles of it, and the leg took the first hour badly and the second hour worse and the third hour at about the speed of a man with a barrow.

The drove went up that lane on the Thursday before, which was the eighteenth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and a hundred and six have gone up it since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and that lane is about nine feet wide where it is made and the wall at the head of it is about nine feet high with about four feet of the top of it open where nine yards are not back in yet.

He stood at that wall for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, which is nine minutes, and the gap in it is about nine feet long and about four feet high and one man has been carrying the stone for it about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and forty days and has not started the ninth yard, and could not be near it without somebody standing with him, which is a fact about four and a half years of a bad leg and about nine people who know it, and about five who do not, and nobody has asked him to be near it and nobody is going to. The mallet is on that bank with the line of stone and it was not picked up and the ninth yard has not been started and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and forty days.

As to the people at the end of that shut road, nobody has asked them about the four copied pages and they have said nothing about them, and about nine people in nine counties know that they have not been asked and five of them have not thought about it. About four people in nine counties have said since that this month is the fourth month of a silence that nobody has broken in either direction, and about five have said that a road which can be shut in a lane by one person saying a thing out loud about his own trade is a road that somebody else has to be asked about, and about four have said that nobody in nine counties has been asked about it and that is the working.

The bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years and nobody lifted it and nobody tried it, and the third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it lies in a line on that bank about nine feet of it, which is the width of the made road and of the standing broom and the length of that dead line of grass.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-second of Mudmonth is a Monday, and the lamp was put there that afternoon about the third hour. The coping is about nine feet wide and the water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and fifty-nine days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and forty days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and seven days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the young man of about nineteen has been in and out of that yard about nine times this week with a pail and has not opened a skirt.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter, and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street and has stood behind it for thirty-one years, and about nine things were done on that counter one after another during that Monday and not one of them was written on, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Monday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and sixteen days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for.

The weigh-house floor four days off is four paces long and about nine feet wide with a sill at the door and a hood over it about nine feet above the sill, and about seven people stand on it before light on the five mornings a week, and four hundred people in this county have never been on it and nine have. A man of thirty-two started it about four and a half years ago and has not been one of the two who say the number on any morning since about the first of Hearthmonth, and nine people in this county know that and five do not.

The rest of that Monday went on. There were about nine people in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something and about five did not. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped as long after the light had gone as the light had taken to go.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades, four stayed on at the boards, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road did not know any of it had happened.

The ninth mile stone on that made road four days off stands at the height of a knee, and the eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it carries about nine hundred names cut into its side going back about two hundred years, of which nine can be read and four people in nine counties have tried to read more. He cleans the ninth one on a Tuesday and a Saturday and not on a Monday, and grit is in the ring round its foot again, and a man with a bad leg and a stick does that in about nine minutes on a Tuesday and about eleven on a Saturday because Saturday is market and the ground is harder by the ninth hour.

The three series in that room over the weigh-house are kept apart on purpose and none of them is taken from another, and a figure that appears in two of them is a figure somebody has written down twice, and four people in nine counties have said that this county's whole defence is that it has never once let a figure out of one book into another.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over on the day after a refusal. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Monday was in the leg and the ear and a hundred and sixty-seven days. The leg took nine miles of a made road out and nine miles of it back, being eighteen miles in about two days on a bad hip, and it had a hot band across the knee by the fourth hour of the way back, and the ear on the side the stick is on is gone, and it is the side she spoke from, and it always will be.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and sixty-seven days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that has never once been said about in any other way at all. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and sixty-seven days.

He went out of that market street at about the ninth hour of that Monday and went up the made road and came back down it, and he did not go up it past the ninth mile stone on the way out because it was not a Tuesday or a Saturday, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of this is sixty-one words said out loud once in his own trade by a man who has kept that weigh-house floor four and a half years, nine people in the room and four of them hearing all of it, and the whole of it being that nobody in nine counties has ever asked him what a sheet weighs. Four days off, the man of thirty-two stood at the head of his own wall for nine minutes in front of a gap about nine feet long and about four feet high that he cannot be near without somebody standing with him, and the ninth yard has not been started and the mallet was on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and was not picked up. Four hundred people in that market street have not heard what was said on that floor and nine have. One hundred and sixty-seven days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and fifty-nine days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
