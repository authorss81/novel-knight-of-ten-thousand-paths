# Chapter 0812

**Goatmonth the thirteenth, YR 323, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, forty-seven days after a sheet went up on those boards, sixteen days still to run on the date at the foot of it, and a man of thirty-two standing at the low end of a street at the ninth hour looking at a door he did not go through.**

There were nine people in that street at the ninth hour and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since noon on the Saturday before, and the roll has been on those boards one hundred and ninety-one days.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the two hundred and eighty-sixth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The man with the bad hip wrote nothing, because nothing is written, and the six mornings lost in the nine days of water in Mudmonth are gone and are not being recovered and a successor inherits them as gone.

“They will ask somebody,” the woman at the door said. “They always ask the man who is easiest to find and not the man who knows.”

“Then we are seven men who are easy to find and not one man who knows.”

“That is a worse answer and it is the true one and it is the same answer it was six weeks ago.”

The number has been four for two hundred and eighty-two mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in forty-seven days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in forty-seven days. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

The stone sill of that weigh-house door carries four lines of chalk under the hood the rain has never got behind. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nine people went past that door that morning and about four of them looked down and about five walked over it, and about four and a half years is the longest anybody in nine counties can say they have not heard those four lines read out loud.

Then, at about the ninth hour, at the low end of that market street, the man of thirty-two with the stick stood opposite the Crown's road office and looked at it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

There is a counter at the front of that office. Behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street and has been at that counter since he was twenty-one. Behind him there is a door, and behind that door there is a low room with a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep, and in that drawer there is the county's one seal with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it.

He had come down that lane at the ninth hour to see whether a man behind a counter would be asked anything in six weeks, and the answer was that he had not been, and that about four people in nine counties had worked out in the same six weeks what that meant.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a counter and a door and a man who was born in the street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has, being that it needs nobody to travel and nobody to be paid and nobody to agree, and about five have said that the cheapest instrument an occupation has is also the one nobody in nine counties has looked at.

About four have said since that what it costs to sit behind that counter is a counter and a room behind it and a key in a pocket, and that nobody has to have agreed to anything and nobody has to be told what a gate is for, and about five have said that the cheapest instrument is cheap because there is no price on it and a thing with no price on it is a thing nobody has to defend.

Every one of those things is in no paper in this county. The sheet on the frame at the low end of that street asks for one name and has sixteen days left in it. The roll has nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's and has been empty for one hundred and ninety-one days. The notice above them wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred and twelve days past its date and has shut nothing in this county or in any of the other eight.

There are three questions on that frame and none of them is a question about the man behind the counter.

He stood in the street and worked out that this was the whole of it, being that the cheapest instrument an occupation has is a man who has been asked nothing, and that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have never noticed the counter at all, and that the man behind it was born four hundred yards from where he was standing and had been asked nothing in six weeks and had noticed that he had not been asked, which is a different thing and not a better one.

What that counter does on an ordinary morning is worth putting down, being that about four people in nine counties have said that a counter which charges a penny for a copy of a notice and a halfpence for a copy of anything else is not an instrument anybody has to be frightened of, and about five have said that the ordinary work of that counter in a week of about two thousand people is about nine things, and that about nine things a week is why nobody has ever thought to ask the man behind it anything. He found, when he turned round at about the tenth hour, that he had gone on standing in the street, and the reason was not the leg and not the ear and not caution, and the reason was that a counter asks nothing of anybody, and he had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and he had found in six weeks that the other side of that is a counter that asks nothing of anybody either.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a door is the only part of an occupation that has to be paid for twice, being once to build and once every morning to shut, and about five have said that the shutting is the cheap half and the building was somebody else's problem nine counties off.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Tuesday and it had been shut behind its own door on every one of the seven weeks since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. Nobody in nine counties went into it. The drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

A badge-man of about fifty-eight came into that office at about the tenth hour and went out of it again without stopping at the counter, which is three times now since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and about four people in that street have said that a badge-man who comes and goes out of an office is not an occupier and is not a clerk, and about five have said that a man who comes and goes out of an office three times in seven weeks and stops at nothing is a man who is waiting for something, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what.

About four hundred people in this county of about two thousand buy something on a Thursday and about four hundred buy something on a Saturday, and nobody has ever worked out whether that is because there are about two thousand people or because there are about nine hundred who come in, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a county is the length of its market street and not the length of its parish, and about five have said that eleven miles of made road is nine counties and that this street is one of them and the smallest.

Then the man of thirty-two went down the street to the flap of the stall where the nine lines were, and they were seven days older than they had been on the last Thursday, and the trades had not changed and the names had not come, and about nine people said what their own trade was when he asked them in a lane, and about nine of them answered inside nine seconds.

About nine people in nine counties have said since that not one of the nine trades on those nine lines has been asked about by anybody, and about five have said that not one of the nine people whose trade it is has been asked whether it is there. The man who keeps the trades' second book of this county has about nine hundred names in it against a trade and he was not asked about the stall flap either, and about four people in nine counties have said that a man with a book of nine hundred names and a woman with a book of nine questions are the two people in this county best placed to say what a form is for, and about five have said that neither of them has been asked.

The cost of that Tuesday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a door and stand opposite it for about two hours and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed ninety-eight days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own.

The rest of that Tuesday went on and would have gone on if he had never come down the lane at all. There were nine people in that street and no more than nine, and about four of them had come within four feet of that counter in about seven weeks and about five had not, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road had not been near it at all. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and marked the two occupied ones on its tally-stick by the door and put no board out on the made street, because there was nothing left to fill.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Tuesday in Goatmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirteenth of Goatmonth is a Tuesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

Four days out along that made road the ninth mile stone is the height of a knee, and somebody had cleaned round the foot of it with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, and the clean ring a bit of stick leaves is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had taken grit into itself again before the middle of the week. Four hundred yards past it the eleventh mile stone carries about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and about nine of them can be read by anybody alive in this county.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and the board is still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of about nineteen has been in and out of that yard about nine times this week with a honey pail and has not touched a frame and has not added a sixth, and nobody has asked him for an account of the three things he has said out loud in that street in about four and a half years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder in the wet. The last cart of lime came up that track three hundred and ninety days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and seventy-one days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and thirty-eight days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are two miles of wall and orchard owned by nine households, and those nine households are nine households that voted and nine people that said one thing each, and they are not a body and they have not become one.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it.

There are four holes in the arrangement this county has made for itself and they are four and not five. About four people in nine counties have said that a form nobody in this county can fill is the ordinary condition of a form and not a fault in it, and about five have said that a county which has found four things it cannot do has not yet found a fifth, and nobody in nine counties has been asked which four and nobody is going to be asked this year.

The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down where it has been since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and it was not turned over on that Tuesday and it is not going to be turned over this year.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and seventy-one days, and the ninth yard has not been started through a whole winter and a whole summer, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and it has not been picked up, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will.

The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid, and the stone for it is lying in a line on the bank with the mallet on top of it, and about four people in nine counties have said that a course of stone is a thing about nine feet long and that about nine feet is the width of the made road and of the standing broom and of that dead line of grass in the scar, and about five have said that four things in one county are about nine feet across and that none of the four has been asked about by anybody.

The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and about four people in nine counties have said that three sets of figures kept by three people who never compare them are the only arrangement in this county that has never once been wrong, and about five have said that three series that are never compared are also three series that can never catch anything.

The leg has taken eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise since before the first of Mudmonth and it is worse than it was at the close of the last year, and about four people in nine counties have said that a leg which is worse every year and is never once described as worse is a leg that is being paid for in silence, and about five have said that a man who says nothing about it is not being careful and is being careful.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and he did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and sixteen days were left on the sheet and the fourth line of the roll was empty on all three of its copies and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street, which about four people in nine counties have called the cheapest instrument an occupation has and which nobody in nine counties has looked at, a low room shut behind its own door on every one of seven weeks with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, one man standing opposite that door for about two hours and not going in, a man of fifty-one with about nine hundred names against their trades in a book about four miles off who was not asked about a flap of a stall nine feet away from him, seven people on a floor four days off saying four twice for the two hundred and eighty-sixth morning with the loss of six mornings still six, a sheet forty-seven days on a board and sixteen days left in it, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three, ninety-eight days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet three hundred and ninety days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
