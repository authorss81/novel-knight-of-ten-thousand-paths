# Chapter 0829

**Mudmonth the twenty-fourth, YR 324, a Wednesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, a hundred and eighteen days after a sheet went up on those boards, fifty-five days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of thirty-two going up thirteen stairs on a morning the room is shut.**

There were about nine people in that street at the ninth hour and no more than about nine, and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since about noon on the Monday before, and the roll has been on those boards about two hundred and sixty-two days and has not been filled in on any of them, and the notice nailed above them is three hundred and eighty-three days past its own date and has shut nothing.

Four days off, on a floor about nine feet by eleven above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and thirty-seventh morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and thirty-three mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are six lost mornings and are not going to be got back by anybody.

“The copyist came down on the Monday,” the woman at the door said.

“She came down at the fifth hour and the lamp was still in.”

“Then it is out.”

“It is out at the sixth and she came down at the fifth and it was out of the lamp and not of the room.”

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and eighteen days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on the top shelf of it, about nine inches down, above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Then, at about the seventh hour, the man of thirty-two with the stick went up those thirteen stairs and the leg took the last four on the rail, and about nine people were in that street and not one of them was on that stair, and about four of them noticed the door at the top of it and about five did not.

She had said on the Monday that he might look at them. She said so in her own trade and it was forty-four words and nobody in nine counties has asked her what looking is for.

“**You may look at them and you may not have them, and that is not me being careful with you, it is that a thing kept by a person who keeps records is still hers on the morning after it has been read out.**”

He read the first of them and the second and the third, and the four hands are as they were, and each of the four sheets is about eleven inches by nine and is folded once across the middle and has been folded once for about nine years, and nobody in nine counties can say who wrote any of the four or when or for whom. He did not ask. Nobody offered. The nine roads on each of them are the nine roads and the tenth is the tenth, and about four people in this county could name ten of them and about five could not, and a column of nine fixed points beside nine roads is a set of instructions and not a question, and about four people in nine counties have said that a set of instructions is the easiest kind of paper to be handed to a person who cannot refuse one without giving a reason.

He turned the fourth sheet over himself and he read the last line of it, which is a line he has not read out loud and has not put in a book and has not said to anybody in nine counties, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not heard it and are not going to.

The first of the four sheets has a road at the top of it and about nine roads under that, and the fixed points beside them are a season and a year and in two cases a month, and the second sheet has a road at the top of it and about nine roads under that with the same fixed points in a different order, and the third has the same nine again with one of them gone and a blank about half an inch deep where it was, and the blank is not a refusal because nobody has asked anybody anything at all and nobody in nine counties has asked why a line is empty on a sheet that nobody can seal.

A man who reads a name in a room by himself does not have to do anything about it. That is the whole of what a name in an unclaimed hand is and it is not nothing, being that he read it, and it is not a cure, being that a name is not a thing anybody has been asked for.

The pages went back onto the top shelf of that press, which is about nine inches deep at the mouth and about a foot and a half inside, above the half leaf and the half leaf stayed where it is, face down, and they are in that room and not in his keeping, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a person who looks at four documents and leaves them where he found them has done the only thing there is to do with them, and about five have said that it is also the cheapest thing anybody in nine counties has done this year, and about four have said that it is not a decision and about five have said that it is the decision.

Going and looking at a thing twice and being told that the thing is not the thing you went for is a cost, and it is not an oversight.

A man of forty-three with a stone merchant's yard said that in a lane on the thirteenth of Mudmonth, about a cart track at the end of eleven miles of made road and about a mile and four hundred yards beyond it, and it has not been resolved since and it is not going to be resolved in this county this year, and about four people in nine counties have said that a county which finds out on a Saturday that it went and looked at the wrong thing twice has spent about nine miles of cart road twice and about four and a half months of not knowing, and about five have said that not knowing is where this county started, and about four have said that it is a cost and not an oversight and that saying so is not the same as doing anything about it.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been there about four and a half years and the hood over that door is about nine feet above the sill. Nobody has read them out loud to anybody in about four and a half years and about nine people went past that door on the twenty-fourth of Mudmonth and about four of them looked down.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street, which is about nine feet from the frame with the five things on it, there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and about nine things were done one after another on that counter that morning and nothing was written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Wednesday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and eighteen days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for.

A room over a weigh-house is fourpence a year and has a deed of thirteen words and a press that stands open with no date on it and a register at ten questions and a day-book at eleven entries and a fourth ruled head that has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road do not know that any of that is on the other side of thirteen stairs.

The rest of that Wednesday went on. There were about nine people in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Wednesday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-fourth of Mudmonth is a Wednesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

Four days out, a hundred and six drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on the eighteenth of Mudmonth, which was the Thursday before, and nobody in that street has ever watched one go up. One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and forty-two days, and the ninth yard of that wall has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and sixty-one days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and forty-two days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to this year, and about nine households that put them there would like them to stay.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and nine days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and four things in one county are about nine feet across.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day a man read a name. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Wednesday was in the leg and the ear and a hundred and sixty-nine days. The leg took thirteen stairs up and thirteen stairs down and about four hundred yards of a lane either side of them, and it had a hot band across the knee by the ninth hour, and the ear on the side the stick is on is gone, and it is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and sixty-nine days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that a name on the last line of a fourth page does not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and sixty-nine days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and about four hundred people along eleven miles of that road went on about four hundred trades that day and about nine of them passed the ninth mile stone and about four of those nine looked down at it, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and fifty-five days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of this is a name read in a room by one man and said out loud by nobody and put in no book, forty-four words said once in her own trade by a woman who keeps records and who has been asked nothing about a seal in about nine years, four sheets looked at and put back on the top shelf of a press that has no date on it above a half leaf that stays face down. He read the fourth of them himself and turned it over and did not ask and nobody offered. A man who reads a name in a room by himself does not have to do anything about it, and it is not nothing, being that he read it, and it is not a cure. One hundred and sixty-nine days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and sixty-one days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
