# Chapter 0849

**Rainmonth the twenty-first, YR 324, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and five days after a sheet went up on those boards that was not answered and one hundred and forty-two days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and about four hundred places writing a paper by hand and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road who have heard neither thing.**

The offer on that frame at the low end of that street asked for the name of one person to stand against the roads of nine counties and it went past its date about four months ago and it has shut nothing. This county has not answered it and has not refused it out loud and has not written a word about it, and about four people in nine counties have said that not answering is the same as refusing when a date is on a thing, and about five have said that this county has not noticed a date in four and a half years and cannot be expected to start now.

The paper that a refusal would have gone on is the paper this county is copying by hand. That is the whole of it, and it is not a coincidence anybody arranged, being that about four hundred places in nine counties found out the same way at about the same time, being that there was a sentence to agree with and no seal to agree it with.

He has never told anybody in nine counties that he is the person a refusal would have gone to. About nine people in nine counties know it and about four of them have worked out that the reason it has not gone is not modesty, being that a county which cannot call a hearing does not get one by having somebody in it who would answer, and about five have said that this is the fourth time in about four and a half years that the same answer has come out of the same mouth in a lane, and about four hundred people in this county have not heard any of them.

There is a road on that made road and he has never once worked out what it is for. About nine hundred people along eleven miles of that road have not heard that the offer was made and have not heard that it went past its date.

About four hundred people in nine counties have been reading about the two things that came up that road in the ordinary post for about four months. About four people in nine counties have said that two numbers which are both about nine hundred and are not about the same people are the only honest description of this county anybody has ever produced, and about five have said it is not a description anybody can print.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and forty-nine days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. Nothing has been written on any of those nine lines on any of those three copies and nothing has been taken off that frame and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and seventy days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and forty-two days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and a refusal that is not published is not one of the five things.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and seventy days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. It has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand, and no two of about four hundred copies agree with one another because nobody has asked them to.

Nine trades, being a wheelwright, a saddler, a tanner, a slater, a cooper, a miller, a smith, a drover and a woman who keeps a dairy, and no name on any of them. Nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of those nine and nobody in this county knows who else in nine counties has a copy, and about four people have said that a line with a trade on it can be argued with and a line with a name on it cannot, and about five have said that nobody in nine counties has yet argued with any of the nine.

There is no delegate to anything in this county and nobody here has been asked to be one. He is the only person in this county who could be put against nine roads and about nine people in nine counties have said so since the twenty-third of Longlight, and not one of them has said it to him, and about four hundred people in that street went on buying string.

About four of those nine people have said that the not being asked is the only thing in this county that is working, and about five have said that a thing which works because nobody is doing it is not a thing anybody in nine counties can be given.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Saturday and it has been shut behind its own door on every morning since the seventh of Longlight. It did not stand open on any market morning after the seventh of Longlight and nobody stands in that doorway. The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it was not opened once in two hundred and five days, and the county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for.

Four days out, a hundred and nineteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on Rainmonth the nineteenth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up and come back down.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for eight hundred and twenty-nine days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start. The mallet is out on that bank with the line of stone and nobody has picked it up, and about nine feet of a fourth course of sill lies in a line beside it. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

Nine hurdles are set across the head of that cart track foot to foot, a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on that shoe gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and forty-eight days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut five hundred and twenty-nine days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle has been lifted and no bar has been tried.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-first of Rainmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off that causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and ninety-six days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of those stones, nobody has ever been past the ninth of them, the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

There are five frames of brood in that walled yard and they stand under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the gate about four. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and four things in this county are about nine feet across and none of the four has been asked about.

Under the hood where the rain does not reach there are four lines of chalk on the stone sill of that weigh-house door. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning and about four of them looked down, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

The second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs was not lit that morning. The room it belongs to was open, and it is lit on the five mornings a week that the market does not come. The register holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in two hundred and five days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none, and the fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county.

That press stands open and undated in the room over the weigh-house, with the four copied pages on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down. A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody, and nothing about the refusal this county did not publish went into any of it, and nothing about either sheet read out at the high end of that street went into any of it.

At the front of that office, about nine feet from that frame, there is a counter and behind it a man who was born in that street, and about four hundred people went in and out of that office that Saturday for a halfpenny copy of something. About nine went further in than the counter and about four of those nine were in there for the ordinary reason and about five were in there because it was warm.

The rest of that market morning went on the way a market morning goes on, and about four hundred people in that street had about four hundred reasons to be in it. A man of about forty-five set a trestle against the front of the second stall at about the tenth hour and put nine weights on it in a row and took them off again and put them back four inches along.

The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. A hound belonging to nobody in that street lay in the one place on the paving the sun came down on and stayed in it for about two hours while about nine people went round it.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Rainmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

A cloth lay over a bench nine doors down from those boards and stayed where it lay, and nobody in that street touched it or asked what was under it that day. The half leaf in that press is face down and was not turned over on the day of an unpublished refusal. None of the three series in that room is worked out from either of the other two, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Saturday was the leg, which took about nine miles of a made road and about four hundred yards of a lane either side of it and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and fifty-six days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. A refusal that is not published reaches nobody at all and does not reach it.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll, which is in an unclaimed hand. Nor is a page that has been authenticated, which is not consent. Nor is a place that is still held, which is not a victory, and nor is a damaged engine a rescue. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and fifty-six days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone and did not go in at the low end of that street. Five things were on that board and one hundred and forty-two days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

Nine trades on nine lines of a paper nobody can seal and no names on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine.
