# Chapter 0824

**Mudmonth the first, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, the first of a month and a month carried at sixty days, ninety-five days after a sheet went up on those boards, thirty-two days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of thirty-two back down a made road and told a thing.**

The first of Mudmonth came to those boards and found five things on them and nothing standing at any of them. A printed notice gone soft at the edges with the wet. Three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on top of it, nine empty lines on each of them and the fourth of the nine this county's. And a sheet with a date on it nailed up beside the roll and not over it, whose date went past thirty-two days ago.

That is the twenty-sixth first of a month in a row to come to that frame and find nothing standing at it, and the count carries forward from twenty-fifth at the first of Thawmonth and is not begun again here.

There were about nine people in that street at the ninth hour and no more than about nine, and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since about noon on the Friday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and thirty-nine days, and the notice nailed above them is three hundred and sixty days past its own date and has shut nothing.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and twentieth morning, and Mudmonth is carried at sixty days and about half of them were still to come.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and sixteen mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water are six lost mornings.

“A month changes today,” the woman at the door said.

“It changes on the first and it has done that since before either of us was in this county.”

“And we are three hundred and twenty mornings into a thing.”

“We are.”

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in ninety-five days, being a month and about ten weeks. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on the top shelf of it above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Then, at about the seventh hour, the man of thirty-two with the stick came up those thirteen stairs, and his leg took the last four of them on the rail, and about nine people were in that street below and not one of them was on that stair.

She told him. She did not ask him to sit down and she did not make it into a thing and she said it in her own trade, asked to a person who keeps records and not to a man of thirty-two, and about nine people were in that street below and every one of them was out of that room.

“**Four copied pages came up the made road in the ordinary post on the Thursday before last, and I compared them against one another and against what was put on them, and what is on them is what somebody put on them, and I said that out loud in this room on that Thursday and nine words went with it. Nine of your ten roads would take their dates from the tenth one, so that all ten of them answer to one history. They do not say where anything is shut. Nobody has been asked to consent to anything. And they do not say one word about the woman who wrote them, and nobody in nine counties knows who she is.**”

It was one hundred and twenty-one words and she did not raise her voice once and the whole of it was over in about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a woman who keeps records has told this man the method and not the answer, and about five have said that she could not have told him the answer in her own trade because in her own trade nobody has one, and about four have said that the last nine words of it are the whole of why a set of four sheets of foolscap is not a thing anybody can be argued out of.

He asked her nothing. He did not ask who wrote the four of them and he did not ask how old they were and he did not ask her what she thought the tenth road was, and he did not ask whether she had put a word of it in the book, and about nine people in that street were not in that room and about four of them have said since that a man who asks nothing about four documents is a man who has already worked out what asking would cost, and about five have said that he is not a person anybody in nine counties asks.

A man of thirty-two who has spent four and a half years learning that a question asked about a person's own trade is the only kind anybody answers asked nothing at all on the first of a month, and about four people in that county have noticed that and about five have not, and about nine people along eleven miles of that made road have not heard of it.

Then he said that he would come back on the Saturday, and he came back on the Saturday, and about four people in that street saw him go into that room on the Monday and about five did not look up.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the ninth hour of the first of Mudmonth with a sheet of foolscap in front of him and a pen in his hand and nothing to write on it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Monday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. Nobody has read them out loud to anybody in about four and a half years, and about nine people went past that door on the first of Mudmonth and about four of them looked down, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and has not been asked for.

Four days out, a hundred and three drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on the Thursday before that, which was the twenty-seventh of Thawmonth, at about the sixth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that street has ever watched one go up.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap in it is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and nineteen days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it lies in a line on that bank, about nine feet of it.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the first of Mudmonth is a Monday, and the lamp was put there that afternoon about the third hour. The coping is about nine feet wide and the water was off the causeway, and the hand bell on the gate post above it is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and thirty-eight days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and nineteen days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and eighty-six days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody. That omission is a decision and it is not an oversight.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and the yard wall is about nine feet high, and no sixth has gone in, and the board is still on the skirt stones at that gate. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide with the mat of it thicker than it was a month ago, and a thicker mat is a mat that has stopped moving, and nobody has asked anybody about it, and about four people in nine counties have said that four things in one county are about nine feet across.

The ninth mile stone on that made road four days off stands at the height of a knee, and a foot wide and half an inch deep of clean ground is round the foot of it where somebody went round it with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, and it had taken grit into it again by noon on the Monday. The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past that one has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and about nine of those names can still be read and about four people in nine counties have tried to read more.

The rest of that Monday went on. There were about nine people in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over on a first of a month. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Monday was the leg, which had four days of a made road in it and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and forty-six days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that has no page of its own anywhere in this county. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and forty-six days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding, and stood at the low end of it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over before he went, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and three questions on it that do not agree with one another, and nobody in nine counties was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of a first of Mudmonth is a month carried at sixty days beginning on a Monday that is not a market morning, and one hundred and twenty-one words said out loud in a room over a weigh-house in her own trade on a morning when the man it was about was four days down a made road with the leg worse. She told him the method and not the answer, because in her own trade she has no answer, and nine of his ten roads would have taken their dates from the tenth, and one history cannot be wrong the way nine separate ones can. He asked her nothing at all, which is the only answer anybody in nine counties knows he gave, and about four people in that street noticed that and about five did not. One hundred and forty-six days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and thirty-eight days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
