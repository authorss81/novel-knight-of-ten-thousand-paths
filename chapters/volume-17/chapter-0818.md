# Chapter 0818

**Thawmonth the fifteenth, YR 324, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, seventy-nine days after a sheet went up on those boards, sixteen days past the date at the foot of that sheet, a man of fifty-one going up thirteen stairs for the first time in about nine years, and a woman of about twenty-nine saying no.**

The street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill.

The date at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth and it is sixteen days past. Nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The roll has been on that frame two hundred and twenty-three days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and the notice above them is three hundred and forty-four days past its own date.

There is no fourth hour in that room over the weigh-house this morning because the practice does not run on a market morning, and there has not been one on any market morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. It is lit at the fourth hour at the foot of those thirteen stairs on the five mornings a week when the market does not come, and it goes out at about the sixth. The number last said out loud on that floor is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

Then, at about the tenth hour, a man of about fifty-one went up the thirteen stairs at the back of that weigh-house for the first time in about nine years.

He had asked for it in the only way anybody in this county asks anybody for anything, being that he had asked about his own trade and had been answered about his own trade and had then said out loud what he wanted in his own trade, in a street, once, to about nine people, on a Tuesday a fortnight ago.

The room over a weigh-house is fourpence a year. There is a press standing open with no date on it. There is a register at ten questions and no findings. There is a fourth ruled head on the page and it has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last. There is a day-book at eleven entries. There is a drawer about eleven inches deep. There is a deed of thirteen words and about four and a half years of a lamp at the foot of those stairs at the fourth hour.

He said what he wanted and it was in the trade of a person who keeps records, asked to a person who keeps records, on a Saturday morning with about four hundred people in the street below and none of them on those stairs.

He wanted a fifth head ruled on that page, under the fourth, so that a trade could be written against a name in one book instead of two, so that about nine hundred names and about nine trades would stand together in one place in this county instead of standing together in a room over a bakehouse four miles off and nine trades standing together on a flap of a stall at the low end of a market street.

About four people on that floor heard all of it and about five did not, being that nine people are in that room on a market morning.

She said no.

It was fifty-three words, and it was said without heat, in the way a person says a thing they said once about four and a half years ago and have never had to say twice.

“**I do not rule a head for a book that keeps nine hundred names, because a ruled head is a head somebody has to be wrong about, and I have not been wrong about anybody in this room since the first of Hearthmonth and I am not going to start on a Saturday.**”

About four people on that floor heard the whole of the answer and five heard only the no, and nobody said anything back to him for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

He asked her, not for an account of it, because he had not come up those stairs for one, whether she would take a fortnight.

She said that a fortnight was a longer time than four and a half years and that it would be the same answer on the thirtieth of Goatmonth and on the first of Thawmonth and on the first of a month in the year after that, and about nine people on that floor heard all of it.

Then the copyist of about twenty-six came to the top of those stairs with the day-book under her arm and was in the room for the last of it, and she was not told about any of it, and she ruled no head, and nobody asked her to rule one.

The fourth ruled head of that page is still empty. No fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The register holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in seventy-nine days and gained none that morning. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody. The press stands open with no date on it and the deed of that room is thirteen words long and none of them is about a head.

He came down those thirteen stairs at about the eleventh hour and went out into about four hundred people and nobody in that street asked him anything, and about nine people in that street saw him come down into them and said nothing.

The man of thirty-two with the stick was four hundred paces down that lane at the boards at that hour and was not on those stairs and was not in that room, and he was not asked what a seal is for and he was not asked what a head is for, and nobody in nine counties has asked him about either.

What he had come down that lane for was to see what seventy-nine days of a sheet and nine lines on a flap of a stall and a person in a doorway had produced. What it had produced by the eleventh hour of that Saturday was a man coming down thirteen stairs with nothing, and about four hundred people in a street who had not seen any of it happen.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the tenth hour with about nine things done one after another and nothing written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office the low room with its own door in front of it has been shut behind that door on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and on that Saturday nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer eleven inches deep in it was not opened once, and the county's one seal is in there still with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

About nine hundred names and about nine trades are in this county and they are not in the same room, and they are not on the same paper. The woman who keeps one of the two has now been asked for a ruled head in her own trade and has said no, and that is the first time anybody in nine counties has asked a keeper of records for something that would have made one county out of two books.

Then the man of thirty-two stood at those boards with the market going round him and touched nothing on that frame, and he kept it to about the eleventh hour.

What that Saturday cost was the leg and the ear and a hundred and thirty days. The leg took about four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a frame and stand at it for about two hours, and it had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour. The ear on the side the stick is on is gone, and it is the side she spoke from, and it always will be.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and thirty days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that it has had no panel and no explanation and no page of its own, and that nobody in nine counties is going to give it one.

The deed of that room is thirteen words long and it was drawn by a man who is dead and it has been held up twice in about four and a half years, once by the woman of twenty-nine who keeps it and once by a woman of about thirty-four who asked to see what fourpence a year bought and went down the stairs without saying anything. Nobody in nine counties has asked either of them what thirteen words is worth.

The floor of that room is the floor of a weigh-house and it is four paces long, and there is a lamp at the foot of the stairs and a sill at the door and a press with no date on it and four lines of chalk that mean four. A man of about fifty-four keeps that floor and has kept it for four and a half years and has a number said out loud on it before light on the five mornings a week the room is shut, and has never asked for the stick of chalk that is in a press drawer upstairs.

A refusal given by a person who keeps records to a person who wants a record is the only kind of refusal this county has ever had. About nine refusals given in about four and a half years by about nine people is nine households that voted and is not a body, and a county which can only refuse is a county that cannot hold, and about four and a half years is now the whole of what this county has.

The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it lies in a line on the bank with the mallet on top of it, and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years and nobody lifted it and nobody tried it.

The rest of that Saturday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank and found the top edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Thawmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fifteenth of Thawmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

Four days out, ninety-nine drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth and a hundred went up on the sixth of Thawmonth, which was the Thursday before, and about a hundred and one is the figure now, and nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up. Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and three days, and the ninth yard has not been started.

Five frames of brood under about nine straw skirts is what that walled yard has held for about a month, and the young man of about nineteen went past the gate with an empty pail and came back with it full and did not go in, which is a thing he has done about nine times this month and which is about bees.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and twenty-two days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and three days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and seventy days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Nine doors down from those boards there is a bench with a cloth over it, and it has been there all winter, and nobody lifted that cloth that day and nobody asked what was under it. In the press in the room over that weigh-house the half leaf lies face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is taken from another.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and the stick in his right hand and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. Five things were on that board and three questions on it and sixteen days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a man of fifty-one going up thirteen stairs for the first time in about nine years and coming down them with nothing, a woman of about twenty-nine saying no in fifty-three words in her own trade to a request made in his own trade and to his own advantage, a fourth ruled head that has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth still empty and no fifth head ruled anywhere in this county and a register at ten questions with no finding under any of them and a day-book at eleven entries and a copyist of twenty-six who ruled no head and was told about none of it, about nine hundred names and about nine trades in this county and not in the same room, a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street and nobody in nine counties having asked him what a gate is for, a low room shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, a sheet seventy-nine days on a board and sixteen days past the date on it and nothing shut anywhere in nine counties, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three and two hundred and twenty-three days on that frame, one hundred and thirty days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and twenty-two days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
