# Chapter 0809

**Goatmonth the fifth, YR 323, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over that weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, thirty-nine days after a sheet went up on those boards and four days after nine lines were ruled on a flap of a stall at the low end of that street, and the finding that nobody can put a name on a line and everybody can put a trade on one.**

There were nine people in that street at the ninth hour and nine lines on a flap of a stall four days old and the bolt had been on the room over that weigh-house since noon on the Saturday before and had stood bolted every night of that week.

The cloth was over the bench nine doors down and has been for four and a half years, and on a Monday nobody in that street goes past nine doors of anything.

Four days out, ninety-five drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth and the same ninety-five went up four days ago. A drove that has not moved in four days is a drove somebody has stopped counting, and nobody in nine counties counts it except the man who counts it.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the ninth has not been started. One man has been carrying the stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and sixty-three days, which is a year and about three weeks, and three weeks is also what is left in the date at the foot of a sheet, and nobody in nine counties has put those two figures together.

Four days off, seven people stood up on a floor above a byre before the light and said the number out loud. It was the two hundred and eightieth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number had been four for two hundred and seventy-four mornings and had been eight before the water, and six mornings were lost in the nine days of water in Mudmonth and are gone.

“It is the only thing in this county that has not been put on a board,” said the man with the bad hip. “I have been waiting a year for somebody to nail it up.”

“If somebody nails it up we will find out what they would have used it for, and I do not want to know.”

“You do not want to know because you already know.”

The register in the room over that weigh-house holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in thirty-nine days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in thirty-nine days. The fourth head on that ruled page stands empty and no fifth one has ever been cut. The press stands open with no date on it anywhere, and the leaf at the back of the day-book carries nine figures and a date and no names at all.

Under the hood above that weigh-house door, out of every weather this county has, four lines of chalk are still on the sill. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nobody has read them out loud to anybody in four and a half years. Nine people went past that door that morning and four looked down at it and five walked on.

The count on that frame has been five things and three questions for thirty-nine days. The notice wants one name for every road in nine counties and is three hundred and four days past its date. The roll wants a name against each of nine lines and has three copies and nothing on any of them. The sheet wants one name and has twenty-four days left in it.

A sheet nobody has answered is not a sheet anybody has refused, and a refusal is the only thing in this county that has a date on it.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, nine people looked at nine lines on the flap of a stall, and the man of fifty-four who keeps that weigh-house floor was asked one question about them.

He was asked it kindly, by a person who wanted to know how a line of that kind could ever be settled.

“**Put a trade on it and I can tell you inside nine seconds whether the trade is right, and put a name on it and there is nobody I could ask.**”

Nine people heard all of it. Five did not.

Nobody argued with him about any part of it, and nobody said anything back to him for the length of time it takes to turn a cart twice over.

Then four people in that lane were asked what their own trade was, in their own words. Nine were asked and nine answered inside nine seconds. Nobody was asked whether his trade is on one of those nine lines, and not one of the nine knows.

A tanner said his trade was tanning and was told that was right, and asked what else was on the paper, and was not told. A cooper said his trade was coopering and was asked whether a cooper was on the list, and had to stand there while the person who asked him worked it out, and he was not on it, and the man who kept the weigh-house floor said so himself and said it was not because there was anything against the man, it was that whoever ruled the paper had not asked him.

Nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines.

The finding is this, and it was on the page before the week was out.

A line with a trade on it is a line somebody can be argued with. Nine people in a lane can be asked what a trade is, and can be told they have it wrong, and four of them were told so inside nine seconds.

A line with a name on it is a line nobody in nine counties can be argued with, because nobody has asked the person the name belongs to.

And a line with a trade on it is not a name, and a paper nobody can seal is not a freedom.

The man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour and stood at that flap and listened to thirty-one words and said nothing about them and did not ask the man of fifty-four anything else. He kept it to the eleventh hour.

He had meant to see whether a paper with nine trades on it and no names on it could be argued with, and nine people in a lane had argued with it inside nine seconds without knowing that they were doing it.

He had also come with a thing in his chest that he had carried down four hundred paces of that lane, which was that if the finding held then the roll on those boards was the wrong paper. Nine lines for nine counties, three copies, a name against each line, and the fourth line this county's. A form that wants a name and is kept in triplicate is a form built for a county that knows which person answers for a road. Nobody here knows that. There is nobody to put on any of the nine lines, and there has not been for a year, and the fourth line has been empty on all three copies for one hundred and eighty-three days.

He stood at the flap and worked out that he had known this since the roll went up and had not once allowed himself to finish the thought, and that finishing it would have meant going to the board and taking it down, and that a man of thirty-two with a bad leg does not take down a board in a market street on a Monday with nine people in it.

So the thought was finished and nothing was done with it, and he went home up the four hundred pace lane, and that was the whole of what he did with the best thing he had learned in six weeks.

The tanner came back to the flap of the stall a second time, on his own, and stood in front of it, and the man of fifty-four was still standing near it and the two of them worked it out between them in about nine seconds.

“You are a tanner.”

“I am a tanner.”

“And there is a tanner on that.”

“And I am not that tanner, because that tanner is on the far side of the water and has been for nine years, and I know that because I have paid his brother twice this year for the loan of a frame.”

“So you could tell them the trade is wrong.”

“I could tell them one of the nine trades belongs to somebody who is not in this town. That is not the same as telling them the trade is wrong, and it is much more than they have asked anybody.”

The man of thirty-two was close enough to hear all of it. He heard a thing he had not expected to hear, which was that the finding had a second half and the second half was the half that hurt. A line with a trade on it can be argued with, and it can only be argued with by somebody who knows the trade, and the people who know the trades are the trades.

“So who holds this paper?” the tanner said. “Nobody. That is the answer. Nobody holds it. It is nailed to a flap of a stall by a man who has gone up the street.”

“And who has another one?”

“Everybody who has ever copied a form by hand, which in this county is about four hundred people, and not one of us knows a single other one of them.”

The man of thirty-two wrote nothing down and looked at the ninth line for a long time, and the ninth line was a woman who keeps a dairy, and there were three dairies in a mile of this street and he knew that inside nine seconds and could not tell which of them it was.

The rest of that Monday went on. A load of turf came off the tailboard of a cart at the low end of that street and nine people carried about half of it back on again before the cart was moved. The two women at the third door had four minutes of argument about a bolt of cloth and came out with it wrapped under one arm, and the man with the wain put his tailboard up and took his wain off down that made road. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and kept its tally-stick by the door of it.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of the boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Goatmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five went out into five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fifth of Goatmonth is a Monday, and the lamp was put there that afternoon. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post is the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years, and about four hundred Mondays is about four and a half years of a lamp, and a lamp somebody lights on their own is not a thing anybody in nine counties has asked for.

The ninth mile stone is the height of a knee and the ring somebody had cleaned round the foot of it is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had filled with grit again, and the stick that cleans it is one of about nine sticks in this county that anybody carries for that purpose. Four hundred yards past it the eleventh mile stone carries nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back two hundred years, and a stone with nine hundred names cut into it is not a roll anybody can be added to.

The five frames of brood in the walled yard are under nine straw skirts and the board is still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of nineteen has been in and out nine times with a honey pail and has not touched a frame and has not added a sixth.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are two miles of wall and orchard owned by nine households, which is the third set of nine in this county with a reason of its own, and nine lines with a trade on each is that same problem ruled out on a stall flap, and nobody has asked either.

He went out of that market street at the eleventh hour and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The nine hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one had gone to powder, and the man with the wain had not gone up to look at them. Five things on that board and nine lines on a flap of a stall, and the fourth line of the roll still empty on all three of its copies.

**And the cost of that Monday is the finding that nobody can put a name on a line and everybody can put a trade on one, and nine people arguing with a line in a lane inside nine seconds without knowing it and nobody in nine counties asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines, thirty-one words said once out loud at the low end of that street, and one man working out that the roll is the wrong paper and not taking it down, a sheet thirty-nine days on a board and twenty-four days left in it, three questions on one frame that do not agree with one another, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three, ninety days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet three hundred and eighty-two days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
