**Goatmonth the fifth, YR 323, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, thirty-nine days after a sheet went up on those boards and four days after nine lines were ruled on a flap of a stall at the low end of that street, and the finding that nobody can put a name on a line and everybody can put a trade on one.**

A Monday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the fifth of Goatmonth with about nine people in it and five things on the boards at the low end and nine lines on a flap of a stall four days old. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house had gone on from noon on the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Monday, and the ordinary part is that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have thirty-nine days of not knowing behind them and about three weeks and three days in front of them and have not worked out that either of those figures is a date on a sheet.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the first of Goatmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety-five drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the same ninety-five went up four days ago, and four people in nine counties have said since that a drove which has not moved in four days is a drove somebody has stopped counting, and five have said that about nobody in nine counties counts it except the man who counts it.

It is two hundred and eighty-six mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the fifth of Goatmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and eighty mornings is about a year of a number said twice in the dark and five have said that about the number is the only thing in this county that has not been put on a board.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and he is one of the nine and has been one of the nine for about a year. Eight of the ten things have been held on every morning but two since the first of Wolfmonth. One of the two is the head of a cart track with nine hurdles across it that nine people at the end of it put there and would like to stay, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth YR 322, which is three hundred and eighty-two days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is three hundred and sixty-three days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is four hundred and thirty days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that four hundred and thirty days is a year and seventy days, and five have said that nine feet of it across is the width of the made road and of the broom and the length of the gap in that wall, and four have said that it will not close over and that nobody in nine counties has asked the man whose board it is on about any of it.

The count of things on that board is five and the count of questions on it is three, and it has been five and three for thirty-nine days. The first question is the notice's own and wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred and four days past its date, and the second is the roll's own and has nine empty lines on each of three copies, and the third is the sheet's and wants one name and has about three weeks and three days left in it. About four people in that street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred yards of that made road is the distance from those boards to the ninth mile stone, and five had not, and about nobody has said what a yard of road weighs.

The sheet that went up on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth asks for the name of one person to stand against the roads of nine counties and says under the Marshal's hand in two sentences that the place nine days ride off is being held so that what is called the Sundering is not done again in this world. It has been on that frame thirty-nine days and nobody in nine counties has put a name against it and nobody in nine counties has been asked to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a sheet nobody has answered is a sheet nobody has refused, and five have said that about a refusal is the only thing in this county that has a date on it.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and eighty-three days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down. Inside the covering paper folded over the back of the second copy there is a sheet of paper folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked. Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to be.

Then about the tenth hour of that Monday, at the low end of that market street, nine lines on a flap of a stall were looked at by about nine people, and the man of about fifty-four who keeps that weigh-house floor was asked one question about them, kindly, by a person who wanted to know how a line of that kind could ever be settled. He answered in thirty-one words and about nine people heard all of it and about five did not.

“**Put a trade on it and I can tell you inside nine seconds whether the trade is right, and put a name on it and there is nobody I could ask.**”

About nobody in nine counties argued with him about any part of it and nobody in that market street said anything back to him for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. About four people in that lane were then asked what their own trade was, in their own words, and about nine people were asked and about nine answered inside nine seconds, and not one of them was asked whether his trade is on one of those nine lines and not one of them knows.

About four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that nobody can put a name on a line and everybody can put a trade on one, and five had not. About four people in nine counties have said since that a line with a trade on it is a line somebody can be argued with, because about nine people in a lane can be asked what a trade is and can be told they have it wrong, and five have said that about a line with a name on it is a line nobody in nine counties can be argued with, because nobody has asked the person the name belongs to. And about four have said that a line with a trade on it is not a name and a paper nobody can seal is not a freedom, and that nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine lines.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour and had stood at that flap and had listened to thirty-one words and had said nothing about them and had not asked the man of about fifty-four anything else. That was the whole of what he came down for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to see whether a paper with nine trades on it and no names on it could be argued with, and about nine people in a lane had argued with it inside nine seconds without knowing that they were doing it.

The cost of that Monday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and four hundred yards of a made road and had a hot band across the knee by about the seventh hour. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And in ninety days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document and has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that was on his hand all through the two hours he was in that street.

The ordinary part of that Monday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. A load of turf came off the tailboard of a cart at the low end of that street and about nine people carried about half of it back on again before the cart was moved. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped, and the man with the wain put his tailboard up and took his wain off down that made road. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and kept its tally-stick by the door of it and put no board out on the made street, and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Goatmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the fifth of Goatmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fifth of Goatmonth is a Monday and the lamp was put there that afternoon. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about four hundred Mondays is about four and a half years of a lamp and that a lamp somebody lights on their own is not a thing anybody in nine counties has asked for.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before and had stood bolted every night of that week, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs on that Monday. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in thirty-nine days, and no fifth head is ruled, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in thirty-nine days, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in about four and a half years, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it and is not going to. About nine people went past that door that morning and about four of them looked down at it and about five walked on.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it was a foot wide and half an inch deep and had filled with grit again, and the stick that cleaned it is one of about nine sticks in this county that anybody carries for that purpose. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and about four have said that about nine hundred names is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep and that a stone with nine hundred names cut into it is not a roll anybody can be added to.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had gone in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times and does not touch a frame has said no three times without speaking, and five have said that he has said three things out loud now and that about three is the number of things a person of nineteen can carry out of a market street in one week.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. Then the fourth hour of that Monday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by two people who were not him.
