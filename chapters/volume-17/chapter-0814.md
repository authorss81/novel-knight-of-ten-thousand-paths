# Chapter 0814

**Thawmonth the first, YR 324, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, the first of a month and the first of a year, sixty-five days after a sheet went up on those boards, two days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and nothing shut anywhere in nine counties.**

The year turns at the first of Thawmonth. That is not this county's arrangement and it is not any county's arrangement that anybody in nine counties knows of, and it happens once, and it happened on this morning, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds what a year is and about five had not.

A year turns on a market morning for the first time in this manuscript's memory, and it turned on a morning when about four hundred people were in a street buying string and about nine were standing at a frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

The first of Thawmonth came to those boards and found five things on them and nothing standing at them. A printed notice gone soft at the edges with the wet. Three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on top of it, nine empty lines on each and the fourth of the nine this county's. And a sheet with a date on it nailed up beside the roll and not over it.

That is the twenty-fifth first of a month in a row to come to that frame and find nothing standing at it, and the count carries forward and is not begun again here.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a first of a month arrives whether anybody has put anything against it or not, and about five have said that twenty-five in a row is a habit and not an attendance, and about four have said that the only thing this county ever puts on that frame is a paper with a date on it, and that the date is two days past and that nothing in nine counties is shut.

The date at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth YR 323 and it is two days past. About nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road never knew there was a date on it at all, and about four hundred people in that market street walked past it at about the ninth hour of the morning it went past, and about nine of them looked at it.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a date which has gone by shuts nothing, because a date is the only part of a sheet that can be late and the rest of it has never been late about anything, and about five have said that the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth was a Thursday, and that a drove went up a lane four days off that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and that two men went with it.

That drove is the ninety-ninth to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up, and the ninety-ninth went up on the same day as the date at the foot of a sheet about the roads of nine counties.

A page that made anything of that would be making something of it, and this one does not.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and nine days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's. The notice above them wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and the first of Mudmonth was three hundred and thirty days ago, and no road has been shut in this county since and none has been shut anywhere that anybody in nine counties has heard about.

The practice does not run on a market morning and so for sixty-five days now there has been no fourth hour in that room over the weigh-house at all, and the number last said out loud on that floor is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in sixty-five days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in sixty-five days. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county and the copyist of about twenty-six has ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about four hundred people went past that door on the first of a year without looking down at it.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and eighty-nine days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a bakehouse four miles off was asked one question about her own trade, kindly, because the man of thirty-two wanted to know what a person does on a first of a month.

Her back room is where the trades' second book of this county has been kept for about nine years. About four people standing there heard all of it and about five did not.

“**My oven is lit on the first of every month as well as on the two market days, and there has been a first of a month come down to this street twenty-five times in a row, and about four people know that and about five do not, and I have never sold a loaf of bread on the strength of it.**”

It was sixty-two words. Nobody in that street argued with her about any part of it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a person who is asked what she does on a first of a month and answers about her oven has answered the only question anybody in that market street actually had, and about five have said that about four people knowing and about five not knowing is the standing arrangement of this county and that a year does not change it.

Then the man of thirty-two asked her what to call her, because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and she told him, and about four people in that street heard it and about five did not, and about four people in nine counties have said that a county with a bakehouse four miles off and a book in its back room has the one place in nine counties where a trade and a name have stood together in one line for about nine years, and about five have said that the woman who keeps that oven is not the woman who keeps the book and that nobody in nine counties has ever asked the two of them to meet.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the tenth hour on the first of a year with a sheet of foolscap in front of him and a pen in his hand and nothing to write on it, and nobody asked him what a gate is for.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Saturday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. Nobody in nine counties went into it. The drawer eleven inches deep in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a year is the longest ordinary thing a county has, being that it goes by without anybody deciding it and leaves everything exactly where it was, and about five have said that the longest ordinary thing a county has is the frame at the low end of its street, which is now two hundred and nine days into a roll that was meant to take a day.

Then the man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour and stood at those boards on the first of a year with the market going round him and touched nothing on that frame, and he kept it to about the eleventh hour.

He had come to see whether a year turning on a market morning would produce anything at a frame with five things on it. Nothing stood at it. About four hundred people in that street had gone on buying string and about nine had stopped and about five of those nine had worked out inside nine seconds what a year is and about four had not.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg and in the ear on the side the stick is on. The leg took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a frame and stand at it for about two hours and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and the ear is gone and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and sixteen days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own.

The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it is lying in a line on the bank with the mallet on top of it, and nobody has laid it and nobody has picked the mallet up, and about four people in nine counties have said that a course of sill is about nine feet long and that about nine feet is the width of the made road and the width of the standing broom and the length of that dead line of grass, and about five have said that four things in one county are about nine feet across and that none of the four has been asked about by anybody.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over on the first of a year. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere.

The rest of that Saturday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and the woman who keeps it marked the four on her tally-stick by the door and no board went out on the made street after noon.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Thawmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the first of Thawmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

That Tuesday the ninth mile stone four days off stood at the height of a knee with a ring of clean ground round its foot a foot wide and half an inch deep, cut with a bit of stick on the Saturday before and full of grit again by noon. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards on has about nine hundred names cut into it going back two hundred years, and about nine people in nine counties can read nine of those names and about four have tried to read more.

Behind a walled yard about a mile off there are five frames of brood under about nine straw skirts and a board on the skirt stones at the gate that has been put back on every time it has been taken off. The young man of about nineteen went in among them with a pail and lifted one skirt and put it back and stood looking at it, which is a thing about four people in nine counties have argued about and about five have not.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and eight days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and eighty-nine days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and fifty-six days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Nine households graze beasts on a rota they have kept for about nine years on the flat at the end of that track, and those same nine households put the nine hurdles across the head of it themselves and would like them to stay and said so once through a shut door in twenty-nine words, and they have not said it twice and are not going to, and about four people in nine counties have said that nine households that voted and nine people that said one thing each are the same nine and are not a body.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and the stick in his right hand and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. Five things were on that board and three questions on it and the date at the foot of the sheet was two days past and the fourth line of the roll was still empty on all three of its copies. A year turned and nothing in nine counties was shut and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of that first of Thawmonth is a year turning on a market morning for the first time in this county's memory with about four hundred people in a street buying string, twenty-five firsts of a month in a row arriving at five things on one frame and nothing standing at any of them, a date two days past and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road who never knew there was a date on it at all, sixty-two words said once out loud about an oven lit on the first of every month, a sheet sixty-five days on a board, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three and two hundred and nine days on that frame, a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street and nobody in nine counties having asked him what a gate is for, a low room at the back of that office shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, a ninth drove going up a lane on the same day as a date at the foot of a sheet about the roads of nine counties and nothing made of it, one hundred and sixteen days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and eight days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
