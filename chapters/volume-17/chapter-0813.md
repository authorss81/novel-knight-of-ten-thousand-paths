# Chapter 0813

**Goatmonth the twenty-fourth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, fifty-eight days after a sheet went up on those boards beside three copies of a roll and not over them, five days left in the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man who hauls stone saying one thing out loud about five days.**

The street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

The date at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth and there are five days left in it. About four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that five days is a working week, and about five had not, and nobody in nine counties has been asked what a county is supposed to do on the thirtieth of Goatmonth.

A date at the foot of a sheet is the only part of it that anybody in nine counties can read without a trade, and a date is a promise about a future made by people who are not here and will not be here to be asked what they meant by it. Five days is a working week, and a working week is how long it takes to get a thing done, and nothing in nine counties has been started that would take five days.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for six hundred and eighty-two days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up.

The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid, and the stone for it is lying in a line on the bank, and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate is where it has been for about eleven years and nobody lifted it and nobody tried it and nobody is going to.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and two days. Its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and about four hundred people have walked past it in about seven months and about nine have stopped, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down.

The notice above those three copies wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth, and the first of Mudmonth was three hundred and twenty-three days ago, and no road has been shut in this county since and none has been shut in any of the other eight that anybody in nine counties has heard about.

This county's own copy of the hand paper was ruled on that flap twenty-three days ago and it has nine lines on it with a trade on each and no name on any, and it has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and a paper nobody can seal is the only kind of paper this county has ever been able to produce.

Four days out, ninety-eight drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and ninety-eight went up on the Thursday before this one, which was the twenty-second of Goatmonth, and two men went with it into the field inside the stone wall, and nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up.

The practice does not run on a market morning, and so this morning is a morning with no fourth hour in the room over that weigh-house and no lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs, and neither has there been on any market morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. On the five mornings a week when the market does not come, the lamp is lit at the fourth hour and it goes out at about the sixth. The number last said out loud on that floor is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in fifty-eight days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in fifty-eight days. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and nobody has ruled a fifth head anywhere in this county. The copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour that morning with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill, and nobody in nine counties has asked her to rule a head and she has not ruled one.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in about four and a half years.

Then, at about the tenth hour, in front of that stall with the nine lines on its flap, a man who hauls stone for a quarry two miles off was asked one question about his own trade, kindly, because the man of thirty-two wanted to know what a working week is in a trade that is paid by the day.

About four people standing there heard all of it and about five did not.

“**Five days is a working week and I have been paid by the week for about nineteen years, and a week is how long it takes to get a thing done and not how long it takes to think about doing it.**”

It was forty-two words. About four people in that street had heard a working week defined as a thing to be done and about five had heard it defined as a thing to be thought about, and nobody argued with him about any part of it.

A wright at the second door said that five days was nothing, because a thing that takes five days is a thing that could be done in a morning if anybody wanted it done. The man who hauls stone said that was true and that it was also why nobody had asked him to do anything this month, since there was nothing anybody wanted done that came to five days. About nine people were near enough for that and none of them said anything.

Then the man of thirty-two asked him what to call him, because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and the man told him his name, and it is not on this page, and nobody in nine counties has asked to write it down.

That is not a way of keeping a secret. It is what happens in a market street of about two thousand people when a name is given to somebody who asked about a trade and the person giving it does not think that name will ever be wanted again, and this county has about nine hundred names in one book about four miles off and no names at all in nine lines on a flap of a stall, and the two of those facts are not in the same county.

A man of fifty-one who keeps about nine hundred names against their trades has been asked about his own trade twice in three weeks and has been asked for nothing both times. Being asked about is the only thing this county has ever offered anybody and it is not an office and it does not last.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter all morning with about nine things done one after another and nothing written, and nobody asked him what a gate is for. He is the fourth generation of it and about four hundred years of this county's road business has run through that one family, and a thing kept in a family for about four hundred years is not held by anybody and is not owned by anybody and is simply there, and about four hundred years is a great deal longer than the paper nailed up beside it has been on that frame.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Saturday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer eleven inches deep in it was not opened once, and the county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it.

A date which is five days off is the last thing on a sheet anybody in nine counties will talk about, and a date which has gone by is the first thing anybody in nine counties will forget, and both of those are the ordinary way a county hears anything at all.

Then the man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour and stood at those boards with the market going round him and read the date at the foot of that sheet once and touched nothing on that frame, and he kept it to about the eleventh hour.

He had meant to see whether a date would do in five days what a printed notice had not done in three hundred and twenty-three, and the answer he got was that five days is a working week and that about four hundred people had gone on buying string.

He stood there and let that be the finding, because a man who has spent four and a half years learning where a figure comes from has learned that the figures that work on a form are the ones somebody checked, and that five days was checked inside nine seconds by a man who hauls stone and by nobody else in nine counties.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a board and stand at it for about two hours and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and nine days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that has had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own.

The rest of that Saturday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and a carter from the coast was told the price twice and took a bed.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Goatmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-fourth of Goatmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard that there is a date on anything.

Four days out, the ninth mile stone is the height of a knee and the ring somebody had cleaned round the foot of it on the Thursday before is about a foot wide and half an inch deep and had taken grit into it again. Four hundred yards past it the eleventh mile stone has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them.

The five frames of brood in the walled yard at the low end of that lane are under about nine straw skirts this week as they have been for about a month, and the board is still lying on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of about nineteen has carried a honey pail into that yard about nine times since Monday and has not once put a hand on a frame and has not put in a sixth.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and one days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and eighty-two days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to this year.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and forty-nine days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are about two miles of wall and orchard owned by nine households, and nobody in nine counties has asked any of those nine households what they would put on a form with one line for a county, and a household is not a name, and nine households is not a body, and that is the whole of what nine households are and are not.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over on that Saturday. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another, and three sets of figures kept by three people who never compare them have never once been wrong and can never catch anything either, and neither of those has been put in any document.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and the stick in his right hand and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. Five things were on that board and three questions on it and five days left on the sheet and the fourth line of the roll still empty on all three of its copies. Nothing in nine counties was shut and nothing in nine counties was opened and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of that Saturday is five days left in the date at the foot of a sheet and about four people in a market street working out inside nine seconds that five days is a working week and about five not working it out, forty-two words said once out loud in a trade that is paid by the day, a sheet fifty-eight days on a board, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three and two hundred and two days on that frame, a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street doing about nine things one after another with nothing written, a low room at the back of that office shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, this county's copy of the hand paper twenty-three days old with nine lines and nine trades and no names and no seal, one hundred and nine days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and one days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
