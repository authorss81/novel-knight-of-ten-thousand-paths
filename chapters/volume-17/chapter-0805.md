**Hearthmonth the seventeenth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it, twenty-one days after a sheet went up on those boards, and the first of three things said out loud in one street in one week by three people who are not him.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the seventeenth of Hearthmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts along both sides and five things on the boards at the low end. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house came off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Thursday, and the ordinary part is that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard what went up on those boards twenty-one days ago and have nine weeks of winter in front of them to not hear it in.

A drove went up that lane four days off on that same Thursday, the seventeenth of Hearthmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety-three drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four people in nine counties have said since that ninety-three is one more than ninety-two and that one is a figure nobody in nine counties has an opinion about.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for six hundred and forty-five days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and has not been picked up, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The practice did not run on that morning and the number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is two hundred and seventy-three mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the seventeenth of Hearthmonth and two hundred and sixty-seven of them were due on a day this room is shut, and six of those went by in nine days of water with nobody standing on that floor, and nobody is going to recover the six.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and he is one of the nine and has been one of the nine for about a year. Eight of the ten things have been held on every morning but two since the first of Wolfmonth. One of the two is the head of a cart track with nine hurdles across it that nine people at the end of it put there and would like to stay, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth YR 322, which is three hundred and sixty-four days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is three hundred and forty-five days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is four hundred and twelve days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that four hundred and twelve days is a year and forty-seven days and that four and a half acres of standing grass is a quantity and not a wound, and that it will not close over, and that nine feet of it across is the width of the made road and of the broom and the length of the gap in that wall.

The count of things on that board is five and the count of questions on it is three, and it has been five and three for twenty-one days, and about four people in nine counties have said since that three weeks is long enough for a county to notice a fifth thing and that this county has not noticed it, and that not noticing is not the same as not minding and is not the same as agreeing. The first question is the notice's own and wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred and sixteen days past its date, and the second is the roll's own and has nine empty lines on each of three copies, and the third is the sheet's and wants one name and has about forty-two days left in it.

On those boards there is a printed notice in two sentences and three copies of a roll nailed one over the other and a sheet with a date on it nailed up beside them and not over them. The notice says a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth and it is under the Marshal's hand and there is no name at the foot of it. The first of Mudmonth is two hundred and eighty-six days past, and about four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that two hundred and eighty-six days is very nearly ten months, and five had not.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and sixty-five days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down. Inside the covering paper folded over the back of the second copy there is a sheet of paper folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked. Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to be.

Then about the tenth hour of that Thursday, at the low end of that market street, a woman of about thirty-nine who keeps a bindle and does other people's washing was asked one question about her own trade, kindly, by a person who had about nine household's worth of linen and no woman to take it to. She answered in forty-two words and about nine people in that market street heard all of it and about five did not, and nine people in nine counties had already said that she should not be asked anything at all, and she was asked anyway, in her own trade, and she answered.

“**I wash for nine households on this street and I am not going to stand at that board and say what nine households think of a sheet of paper, because when I say it they will all say that I said it.**”

About nobody in nine counties argued with her about any part of it, and nobody in that market street said anything back to her for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about nine people went on what they were doing. About four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that the first of three things said out loud in that street in that week was not a refusal of the sheet, and five had not, and four have said since that a woman who washes for nine households has about nine households' worth of opinion in her and is not going to spend it on a board, and five have said that not spending it is the same as saying none of it is hers, and neither of those is the sheet.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a person standing in a market street in this county is the only kind of person who can honestly have heard every offer this county has ever been made, and that four of them said so out loud three weeks ago and that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard that either, and five have said that in a county of two thousand people there are about four hundred of the kind of person who says a thing out loud and nine hundred of the kind who waits.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour and had been in that street at about the tenth and had heard that one thing said and had said nothing about it at all. That was the whole of what he came down for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to see whether anybody in that street would say anything out loud about that sheet, and one person had, and what she had said was not about the sheet at all, and about nine people had heard all of it and about nine hundred had not.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and two hours in a street with about four hundred people round it and had a hot band across the knee by about the eleventh hour. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And in seventy-two days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document and has never been said out loud in a room, and that was on his hand all through the two hours he stood in that street.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about the ninth hour with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is not the record.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it, and about four hundred people went past those four lines that morning without looking down at them once.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had taken grit into it again by about the ninth hour. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and about four have said that about nine hundred names cut by people who never met is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep, and five have said that about a stone is a thing nobody in nine counties can take down.

Five frames of brood went into that walled yard under about nine straw skirts before the first of Hearthmonth and there has been no sixth, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had carried a honey pail out of that yard and back about nine times that week without touching a frame. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times and does not touch a frame has said no three times without speaking, and five have said that three things said out loud in one street in one week is about three weeks of a man saying no without speaking, and four have said that about nine skirts is not a line anybody has drawn anywhere in nine counties.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile are a set of people with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that a woman who washes for nine households and nine households on a mile of stone are two sets of nine and that neither of them is a name.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is four hundred and twelve days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

The ordinary part of that Thursday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day, and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Hearthmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the seventeenth of Hearthmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the seventeenth of Hearthmonth is a Thursday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about nine households of washing is about nine households of people and that neither of the two nines has been asked about the sheet.

Then the fourth hour of that Thursday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over that weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a woman of about thirty-nine who washes for nine households was the first person in this county to say a whole thing out loud in that street in three weeks, and that nobody in nine counties has decided whether that is a beginning or a Tuesday.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who hears a woman say forty-two words and says nothing back is a man nobody thanks, because saying nothing back is the whole of what he did. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and he had now heard one of three things in that street in that week and he was not going to say one word out loud about any of them.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one is gone to powder, and there were five things on that board and about forty-two days left on the sheet, and the fourth line of the roll was still empty on all three of its copies.
