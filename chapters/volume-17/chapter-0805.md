# Chapter 0805

**Hearthmonth the seventeenth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with four hundred-odd people in that street, twenty-one days after a sheet went up on those boards, and the first of three things said out loud in one street in one week by three people who are not him.**

The street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the boards at the low end of it and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light and the copyist of twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs.

The cloth was over the bench nine doors down, and the woman who owns the bench has begun to hang her two best aprons over the edge of it, which nobody has commented on.

Four days out, ninety-three drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, ninety-three on that same Thursday at the sixth hour and back down at the fifth hour with two men going with it. Ninety-three is one more than ninety-two and one is a figure nobody in nine counties has an opinion about.

The ninth mile stone is the height of a knee and the clean ring round the foot of it is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had taken grit into it again by the ninth hour. Four hundred yards past it the eleventh mile stone carries nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and nobody in nine counties can take the stone down.

The woman of twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at the sixth. Ten questions stand ruled in that register and not one of them has a finding under it, and the count has not moved. The day-book stands at eleven entries. The fourth head on that ruled page is ruled and empty, and there is no fifth head ruled anywhere on it. The press stands open with nothing dated on it, and the last leaf of the day-book behind it carries nine figures, a date, and no names at all.

Under the hood over the weigh-house door, where the rain has never once got in, there are four lines of chalk on the stone sill. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nobody has read them out loud to anybody, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and he has never asked her for it. Four hundred people went past those four lines that morning without looking down at them once.

The room does not run the practice on a market morning, and so for twenty-one days there has been no fourth hour on that floor, and the number last said out loud there is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, a woman of thirty-nine who keeps a bindle and does other people's washing was asked one question about her own trade.

She was asked it kindly, and out loud, by a person standing in front of her who had nine households' worth of linen at home and no woman to take it to.

“**I wash for nine households on this street and I am not going to stand at that board and say what nine households think of a sheet of paper, because when I say it they will all say that I said it.**”

Nine people heard all of it. Five did not.

Nobody argued with her about any part of it, and nobody in that street said anything back to her for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and nine people went on what they were doing.

A man at the second door said that she had answered the question that was not asked. She said that it was the only one she had. He said, and she waited for it, and he did not finish it, and she went back to the second basket.

The man who had asked her said that it was a fair answer and that he was sorry for the trouble of asking. She said it was no trouble and that he should ask her again the next time he had a sheet he did not understand, and then she said, before he had gone, that she had not read it herself, only what the man with the handbar had read out of it, and that this was the same as not having read it at all.

That was the first of the three things said out loud in that street in that week by a person who was not him.

It was not a refusal of the sheet. Four people had said the sheet was the best offer anybody in this county had ever been made and about five had not worked it out and nine hundred along eleven miles of made road had not heard it at all. What she had refused was to be a mouthpiece for nine households, and that is a different thing and it is not on the sheet.

Four people in nine counties have said since that a person standing in a market street in this county is the only kind of person who can honestly have heard every offer this county has ever been made, and that four of them said so out loud three weeks ago, and nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard that either. Five have said that in a county of two thousand people there are four hundred of the kind of person who says a thing out loud and nine hundred of the kind who waits.

Five frames of brood went into that walled yard before the first of Hearthmonth under nine straw skirts and there has been no sixth, and the board is still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of nineteen has carried a honey pail out of that yard and back nine times this week without touching a frame. A man who takes honey nine times and does not touch a frame has said no three times without speaking.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are two miles, a mile of stone wall on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and nine households own it. A woman who washes for nine households and nine households on a mile of stone are two sets of nine and neither of them is a name.

The person who had asked her asked her, afterwards, whether she had read the sheet.

“I told you I had not.”

“You told me you had only heard the man with the handbar read it.”

“That is the same as not having read it and you knew it was when I said it.”

Somebody at a trestle two doors up laughed. The woman of thirty-nine picked up her second basket and said that she had been asked this once before, by a man who wanted to know what the nine households thought of the road rates, and that she had given him the same answer she had given today, and that the man had gone away satisfied, which was the part that had stayed with her.

“He wanted to write it down.”

“He wanted to know it so that he could write something himself and us underneath it. That is all writing is. Half the men in this street want a thing put in a book with their own name on the line above it and nine hundred other people underneath it and no.”

“Then who writes the names?”

“I do not know,” she said. “That is not my trade either.”

The man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour and was in that street at the tenth and heard that one thing said and said nothing about it at all. He kept it to the eleventh hour.

He had meant to see whether anybody in that street would say anything out loud about that sheet. One person had, and what she had said was not about the sheet at all, and nine people had heard all of it and nine hundred had not.

He thought about the bindle on her hip and about how a person carrying other people's washing has no time of her own, and about how that is the answer to nine hundred people as often as it is an answer to a printed sheet, and about how nobody in nine counties has ever once offered that to anybody as a reason for anything. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from. He has not in four and a half years been asked a question he could answer as well as the woman standing in front of him.

The cost of that Thursday was the leg, four hundred paces of a lane and two hours in a street with four hundred people round it, and the hot band across the knee by the eleventh hour. And the ear on the side the stick is on, which is not coming back, and which is the side she spoke from. And seventy-two days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document, and that has never been said out loud in a room.

The rest of the day went on. At the low end of the street a cart shed its near wheel in the frost, and the carter got it back on with a bar in the time it takes to turn a cart twice over, and the only remark anybody made was that the frost was a good thing for finding that out in. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had its four beds full again and put the board out on the made street, and the ostler slept on a bench in the taproom because there was nowhere else for him. Nine people went along that street at the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of the boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Hearthmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of the nine went out into five trades and four stayed at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the seventeenth of Hearthmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The causeway had the water off it and the hand bell on the gate post, which is the size of a fist and takes two hands to swing properly, was not rung, and the four hundred people on the far bank of that water have not crossed it in about nine years.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the ninth has not been started, and one man has been carrying the stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and forty-five days. Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are nine hundred yards dead inside its own width, four hundred and twelve days old, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them.

He went out of that market street at the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles across the top of that cart track have not been moved since the second of Goatmonth and the lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. Five things on that board and forty-two days left on the sheet and the fourth line of the roll still empty on all three of its copies.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a woman of thirty-nine who does other people's washing saying forty-two words out loud once in one street and nine hundred people not hearing it and nobody arguing with her and nobody thanking her, a sheet twenty-one days on a board and forty-two days left in it, three questions on one frame that do not agree with one another, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three, seventy-two days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet three hundred and sixty-four days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
