# Chapter 0811

**Goatmonth the eighth, YR 323, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, forty-two days after a Crown's cart nailed a printed sheet beside three copies of a roll and not over them, twenty-one days still to run on the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of about fifty-one answering one question about his own trade at the low end of that street.**

The street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm. Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench where it has been all winter, and the corner of it had lifted where the damp came up that wall and had not been put back, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it and about five had never seen it at all.

The five things were one printed notice gone soft at the edges with the wet, three copies of a roll nailed one over the other on top of it, and the sheet that a carter had brought up in the ordinary post on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth and had nailed beside the roll and not over it. The frame carried five things and three questions and the three questions did not agree with one another, and about four people in that street had worked out inside nine seconds that the sheet had twenty-one days left in it and about five had not.

The notice wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and says that a road with no name against it is to be shut before the first of Mudmonth. The first of Mudmonth was three hundred and seven days ago and no road has been shut anywhere in this county since, and about nine people in nine counties have said that out loud at some point in those three hundred and seven days and nobody has put it on anything.

The roll has been on that frame one hundred and eighty-six days. Its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's. About four hundred people have walked past it in six months and about nine have stopped, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down.

Seven days ago a person nobody in this county can name laid a sheet of foolscap on the flap of a stall at the low end of that street and ruled it into nine lines with a straight edge and a knife and wrote a trade on each of the nine and no name on any of them. The nine trades are a wheelwright, a saddler, a tanner, a slater, a cooper, a miller, a smith, a drover and a woman who keeps a dairy. That paper is sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and nobody in nine counties knows who else has one.

Four days out, ninety-six drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and ninety-six went up that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up, and about four people in nine counties have said that ninety-six is past ninety and that a figure which is past ninety does not get argued with, and about five have said that nobody in nine counties knows what a drove weighs either.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap in it is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and sixty-six days, and the ninth yard has not been started and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up.

The room over the weigh-house does not run the practice on a market morning, and so for forty-two days now there has been no fourth hour in that room at all. The number last said out loud on that floor is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and has gained none in six weeks. The day-book stands at eleven entries and has gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and nobody has ruled a fifth head anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and about four people looked down, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in about four and a half years. The stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has never asked her for it.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, in front of the stall with the nine lines on its flap, the man of thirty-two with the stick asked a man of about fifty-one one question about his own trade, kindly, because he wanted to know what a person does with the second book of a county.

He asked him who kept it and where it was kept and what went in it, and he asked it kindly, because the man of thirty-two has spent four and a half years learning that a question asked about a person's own trade is the only kind anybody answers. About four people standing there heard all of it and about five did not.

“**I keep the trades' second book of this county and I have kept it about nine years and every trade in this county is in it against the name of the man who keeps it, and it is the only place in nine counties where a person and a trade stand together.**”

It was fifty-two words. He said them the way a person says a thing he has said to himself a great many times and has never once said out loud. A wright at the second door said that was nothing, because every shop in that street knew whose trade it was, and the man of fifty-one said it was nothing until somebody wanted to know it in writing and could not have it, and the wright said he had never wanted it in writing in his life, and about four people heard that exchange and about five did not.

The man with the stick then asked him what to call him, because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and the man told him, and it was Corryn Falder, and about four people in that street had known the book and not known the name on it.

Then, half an hour later, standing at the low end of that same street with his hand flat on the frame of that stall, he said one more thing out loud, once, in his own trade, to about nine people.

“**I want a trade against a name in one book instead of two and I cannot have it because the woman who keeps the other one will not rule a head for it.**”

About four people in that street heard all of that and about five did not, and nobody argued with him about any part of it. About four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has kept a book for about nine years and has asked one thing of one person in about four and a half years asked it in his own trade and to his own advantage and knows the difference and asked anyway, and about five have said that knowing the difference is not the same as not having asked, and about four have said that a man who says out loud that he asked to his own advantage has already done the only part of it that costs anything.

That question was not put to anybody in that street. He did not ask her and nobody asked him on her behalf, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs at about that hour with the register under her arm and was told about none of it and ruled no head. The fourth ruled head of that page is still empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and about four people in nine counties have said since that an empty head has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last and that about four and a half years is a long time for a line to stay ruled and empty.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the tenth hour of that Thursday with a sheet of foolscap in front of him and a pen in his hand and nothing to write on it. A badge-man of about fifty-eight had come into that office and gone out of it again twice since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth without stopping at the counter, and nobody in nine counties has asked him which he is, being a clerk or an occupier, and about four people have said that a badge-man who comes and goes out of an office is not an occupier and is not a clerk, and about five have said that is two things to be instead of one.

About four people in nine counties have said since that a counter and a door and a man who was born in the street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has, being that it needs nobody to travel and nobody to be paid and nobody to agree, and about five have said that the cheapest instrument is also the one nobody in nine counties has looked at.

Those two things are in no paper in this county, and neither of them was said out loud in that market street, and about four hundred people went on buying string.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a counter and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it, and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on every one of the six weeks since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth and nobody in nine counties went into it. The drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Then the man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour and stood at those boards with the market going round him and read the sheet again and touched nothing on that frame, and he kept it to about the eleventh hour.

He had meant to see what six weeks of a sheet and seven days of nine lines had produced in this county. What it had produced was a second book with about nine trades in it against about nine hundred names, kept by a man of fifty-one at the low end of a market street, and a set of nine lines with nine trades on them and no names on any of them, and the two of those had never been in the same room.

He stood there and worked out what he had worked out before and worked out again, being that nobody can put a name on a line and everybody can put a trade on one, and that about nine hundred names were already in a book that nobody in nine counties had ever asked to see.

He said nothing about that to anybody, and he was not the author of anything said out loud in that street that day, and nobody thanked anybody in that street that day, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road had not heard any of it.

The hearing that cannot be called and does not sit had sent nobody to that market street on that Thursday, and it had not sent anybody on any of the six market mornings since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and about four people in nine counties have said that an institution which cannot be called does not have to answer a sheet, and about five have said that an institution which cannot be called also cannot be told what is in this county. which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a board and stand at it for about two hours and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed ninety-three days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that had never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and there was no panel for it and no explanation and no page of its own.

The trades' second book itself was not in that street that morning and has not been in that street any morning in about nine years. It is kept about four miles off in a room over a bakery and it is about nine inches thick and it goes back further than anybody in this county alive, and about nine hundred names are in it against a trade, and about four people in nine counties have said that the answer to a form with one line on it has been sitting in a book about four miles off since before the form was printed, and about five have said that a book nobody has asked to see is not an answer to anything.

The rest of that Thursday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and a carter from the coast was told the price twice and took a bed.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Goatmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the eighth of Goatmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

Four days out, the ninth mile stone is the height of a knee and the ring somebody had cleaned round the foot of it on the Saturday before is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had taken grit into it again. Four hundred yards past it the eleventh mile stone has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and the board is still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man of about nineteen has been in and out of that yard about nine times this week with a honey pail and has not touched a frame and has not added a sixth.

The standing broom on that east rise is about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide and the water laid nearly all of it flat, and about four people in nine counties have said that the mat of it is thicker than it was a fortnight ago and that a thicker mat is a mat that has stopped moving, and nobody has asked anybody about it.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track three hundred and eighty-five days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and sixty-six days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to this year.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and thirty-three days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are two miles of wall and orchard owned by nine households, and about four people in nine counties have said that nine households on that mile have not been asked about a roll and have not been asked about anything else, and five have said that nine households is nine names and a form has one line.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and the stick in his right hand and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. Five things were on that board and three questions on it and the fourth line of the roll still empty on all three of its copies. Twenty-one days were left on the sheet and nobody in nine counties had put a name on any of it.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a man of fifty-one who has kept a book with about nine hundred names in it against their trades for about nine years and who asked one thing of one person in about four and a half years and asked it in his own trade and to his own advantage, this county's own copy of the hand paper seven days old with nine lines and nine trades and no names and no seal and not nailed up and not sent anywhere, a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street and about four people in nine counties saying in the last six weeks that he is the cheapest instrument an occupation has and nobody in nine counties having looked at him, a low room at the back of that office shut behind its own door on every one of six weeks with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, a sheet forty-two days on a board and twenty-one days left in it and nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road who have not heard it, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three, ninety-three days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet three hundred and eighty-five days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
