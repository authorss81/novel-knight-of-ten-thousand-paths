# Chapter 0838

**Longlight the fifth, YR 324, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and fifty-nine days after a sheet went up on those boards, ninety-six days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of about forty-three with a stone merchant's yard finding out who lays kerb and not going anywhere to ask.**

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and sixty-sixth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and sixty-two mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are gone and are not coming back with the month.

“The stone man asked a question on the Saturday.”

“He asked it at the low end of the street and about nine people were near enough.”

“Then somebody stood near enough to hear that he asked it, which is not the same as somebody answering it.”

Then, at about the ninth hour, a man of about forty-three with a stone merchant's yard eleven miles up that made road to the north stood at the low end of that market street and said one thing out loud in his own trade, to about nine people, and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**A carter told me on Sunday that there is a woman at the far bank of that causeway who lays kerb and has been at it since Mudmonth, and that she comes across this water about twice in a fortnight for stone, and I am not going to the far bank, I am going to be here on the Saturday, and I am going to ask her one question and it is going to be about kerb.**”

It was seventy-seven words. Nobody argued with him about any part of it and nobody asked him why he had waited, and about four people in that street have said since that a man who has found out a thing and is going to wait for a person to arrive with it is a man who has learned something about asking, and about five have said that he asked the same question on the Saturday before and got no answer and has not asked anybody else in between.

Waiting is the whole of what he has learned and it is not a thing anybody in this county can be taught, because about nine people in nine counties have been asked a question this year and about nine of those people asked it on a day of the week they had chosen themselves, and the stone merchant of about forty-three chose a Saturday morning at the low end of a market street where about four hundred people go past, and put it about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and then went home up eleven miles of made road and left it.

There is nobody in nine counties who has asked him what he is paid for, and he is paid by the yard for what is already down and not for what ought to be, and about nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on the far bank of that causeway since the first of Mudmonth and about four hundred yards of it is the stretch that begins at the bank and runs inland past a shut door.

About four hundred people live on that far bank and the standing in this county is that not one of them has ever been across in about nine years, and about four people in nine counties have said that one person crossing twice in a fortnight for stone does not make it a road anybody walks and about five have said that a standing in a county about something that has stopped being true is a standing and not a road.

Four days out, a hundred and twelve drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and twelve went up on the Thursday before, which was the sixtieth of Mudmonth and the last day of a month carried at sixty, at about the sixth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and three days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up, and the notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and twenty-four days past its own date and has shut nothing, and the date at the foot of the sheet beside them went past ninety-six days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and twenty-four days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has ever met another.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on that sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and fifty-nine days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for. At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep, and it was shut behind its own door on that Tuesday, and it had stood open on its hook on three market mornings, being the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth and the two Saturdays after it, and shut on every other morning of the six weeks, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and fifty-nine days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about nine people went past that door on the fifth of Longlight and about four of them looked down, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fifth of Longlight is a Tuesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water.

The four miles of made road above that causeway have been the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth, and a road kept by a warrant is a road whose keeping is somebody else's decision, and this county has walked on one for about six months and has not asked, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and eighty-three days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and two days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and eighty-three days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and fifty days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and the yard wall is about nine feet high, and no sixth has gone in and the board is still on the skirt stones at that gate. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and four things in this county are about nine feet across.

The barrow at the low end of that street had a wheel that had been playing about a quarter of an inch and had been righted with a wedge on the Friday before by a man who said nothing about it, and the handle of it has been re-cut once and the re-cut is on the wrong side. The press in that weigh-house has a screw on the front of it that has been painted over four times and about nine people have scraped at it with a thumbnail over four and a half years and nobody has ever asked why.

Two men were setting nine feet of dressed stone at the second gate on that Tuesday and arguing about whether it should go on edge or on its face, and they went on arguing for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and then set it the way one of them had said the other would set it. The man of thirty-two with the stick stood about nine feet off that with his weight on his right foot and watched them and was not asked anything and asked nothing. About four hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known that four miles of it are smooth, and neither of those two men knows it either and neither has been told.

It is about nine feet of stone going into a gate that has nothing to do with anybody with a warrant, and it took about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and he has watched about nine hundred yards of kerb go down on a bank he cannot reach and about nine feet of stone go into a gate he can see, and there is no argument available between those two things that he has ever been able to make out.

The rest of that Tuesday went on. About nine people were in that market street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and about five did not. A carter's near wheel came off at the low end of that street and he had it up again with a bar in about nine minutes and nobody stopped on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Tuesday in Longlight is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked after it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day a man said he was going to wait for a person. Three series stand in that room and none of the three is worked out from another, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Tuesday was in the leg, which did four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to stand at a frame for about an hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and ten days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that a man waiting to ask a person a question does not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence somebody wrote on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll and never came back for. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and ten days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and ninety-six days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a question asked twice at the low end of a market street and no answer to either, and then a man saying out loud in seventy-seven words that he is going to be here on the Saturday and that the question is going to be about kerb, and a person on the far bank of that causeway who comes across this water about twice in a fortnight for stone and has laid about four hundred yards of a line about nine hundred yards long since Mudmonth. Nobody on this side has asked her what she is paid for and by whom. This county has walked on a smooth road for about six months and has not asked. One hundred and fifty-nine days of a drawer not opened, five hundred and two days since the last cart of lime, four hundred and eighty-three days of a shut road behind nine hurdles that the people four hundred yards back put there themselves. Two hundred and ten days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet five hundred and two days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
