# Chapter 0815

**Thawmonth the fourth, YR 324, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, three days after a year turned on a market morning, five days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of fifty-one in that street on a day he does not come on.**

There were nine people in that street at the ninth hour and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since noon on the Saturday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and twelve days.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and first morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The man with the bad hip wrote nothing, because nothing is written, and the six mornings lost in the nine days of water in Mudmonth are still six and are not being recovered.

The floor those seven stand on is about nine feet by eleven and the cow door stands open on the cold from before the light until the light is properly up, and they stand where the light off that door reaches and not one step further into the room. There is a pail under the leak in the far corner and it has been under that leak since before the water and it is not a third of full.

“Four,” the woman at the door said, and then she said it again, and then she waited to see whether the others would say it again, and about four of them did straight away and about three waited for somebody else.

“That is the whole of what nine days of water does to a thing,” she said. “It does not break it. It only teaches it to wait.”

The number has been four for two hundred and ninety-seven mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and one of the two that has not come back is about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide that the water laid flat four days off.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in sixty-eight days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in sixty-eight days. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Four lines of chalk are on the sill of that weigh-house door under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth. Nine people went past that door on the fourth of Thawmonth and about four of them looked down. The stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has never asked her for it.

Then, at about the ninth hour, at the low end of that market street, a man of about fifty-one was standing at the flap of a stall that sells nothing but a board, reading nine lines on it, on a Tuesday, in a street with no market in it.

He keeps the trades' second book of this county. He has kept it about nine years. It is in a room over a bakehouse four miles off and it is about nine inches thick and about nine hundred names are in it against a trade.

He does not come up that street on a Tuesday. About four people in that street had worked out inside nine seconds that a man who keeps a book does not come up a street without a market in it, and about five had not worked it out and did not look up from what they were doing.

A county is the only kind of place on this road where a person can be asked about a thing he keeps, because everybody in a market town is a person somebody has to buy something from, and that is the cheapest interrogation in nine counties and nobody has ever used it. The man who keeps that book has been in this street nine hundred times in about nine years and has been asked about his own trade once in about four and a half years, and it was on a Thursday in Hearthmonth and it was about a door.

The man of thirty-two with the stick came down the four hundred pace lane at about the tenth hour and found him there, and asked him one question about his own trade, kindly, because the man of thirty-two wanted to know what a person does with a book on a day there is no market.

About four of the nine people standing in that street heard all of it and about five of them heard the middle of it.

“**I have kept that book about nine years and about nine hundred names are in it against a trade, and there are nine trades on that flap with no name on any of them, and in about nine years nobody has ever put one of those two things beside the other and looked at them.**”

It was fifty-five words. About four people in that street heard both halves of that and about five heard one of them, and nobody argued with him about any part of it.

He said then that he had come up on a Tuesday for the first time in about nine years because a date at the foot of a sheet went five days ago and had shut nothing, and that a man who keeps a book about nine hundred names in it knows what a date does to a book, being that a book is the only kind of paper anybody in nine counties has ever taken seriously.

A book of about nine hundred names is a roll of roads with nine hundred lines on it, and nine hundred lines is not a form anybody can fill, and the county's problem was never that it had too few names.

Then the man of thirty-two asked him what he was going to do about it, because a man of thirty-two has asked about four things in about four and a half years and has been refused three of them and has not argued with any of the three.

“I am going to go to the woman who keeps the other book,” he said, “because that is the only place in this county where what I want is a ruled head and not an argument, and I have been carrying it about for three weeks and I would rather carry it than put it in a lane.”

He went down the street at about the eleventh hour and the two of them went in opposite directions and neither of them said anything else, and about nine people saw that and said nothing about it.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street. He was at that counter at about the ninth hour with about nine things done one after another and nothing written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for, and he has noticed that nobody has, and a man noticing is the first half of being asked and the second half has not arrived.

The low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door on that Tuesday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. Nobody in nine counties went into it. The drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Five things on one frame and three questions on it and about nine hundred names in a book four miles off and nine trades on a flap of a stall is more paper than anybody in nine counties expected to see in a year, and none of it is joined to any of the rest of it. A county is a set of unjoined papers, and about four and a half years is how long it has taken this one to get to that.

Then the man of thirty-two stood at those boards with nine people in that street and no market in it and touched nothing on that frame. The date at the foot of that sheet is five days past and nothing in nine counties is shut and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road never knew there was a date on it at all.

The cost of that Tuesday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a board and stand at it for about two hours and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and nineteen days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that no person in nine counties has ever been shown it or told about it or asked to account for it. It has no page of its own.

The rest of that Tuesday went on. There were about nine people in that street and no more than about nine. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and marked the two occupied ones on its tally-stick by the door and put no board out on the made street, because there was nothing left to fill. There had been wet in the night on the low ground and the four hundred pace lane down to that street was carrying about an inch of it at the low end, and every one of those nine people came through it in boots that were not the right boots for it.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Tuesday in Thawmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the fourth of Thawmonth is a Tuesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

The ninth mile stone four days off is the height of a knee and has been for as long as anybody can remember, and the ring a person leaves when they clean round its foot with a stick is a foot across and half an inch deep and takes grit into itself in about two days. Four hundred yards beyond it the eleventh mile stone has nine hundred names cut into its side going back two hundred years and about nine of them are still legible.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and ninety-two days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it lies in a line on the bank.

Five frames of brood stand under about nine straw skirts in a walled yard and no sixth has gone in. The young man of about nineteen has been at that gate about nine times this week and the board is still on the skirt stones, and nobody in nine counties has asked him for an account of the three things he has said out loud in that street in about four and a half years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and eleven days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and ninety-two days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and fifty-nine days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another, and three sets of figures kept by three people who never compare them have never once been wrong and can never catch anything either.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and he did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door and the drawer in it was not opened. Five things were on that board and five days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a man of fifty-one standing in a street with no market in it on a day he has not come up in about nine years, fifty-five words said once out loud about nine hundred names against their trades and nine trades against no names, a refusal that has not happened yet and is five days away and is in his own trade and to his own advantage, a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street doing about nine things one after another and noticing that nobody asks him anything, a low room shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, seven people on a floor four days off saying four twice for the three hundred and first morning with the loss of six mornings still six, a sheet sixty-eight days on a board and five days past the date on it and nothing shut anywhere in nine counties, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three and two hundred and twelve days on that frame, one hundred and nineteen days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and eleven days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
