# Chapter 0807

**Hearthmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over that weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with four hundred-odd people in that street, thirty days after a sheet went up on those boards and a month and a day before the date at the foot of it, and the third of three things said out loud in one street in one week by three people who are not him.**

The street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the boards at the low end of it and the bolt had come off the room over that weigh-house at first light.

The cloth was over the bench nine doors down and had been there before this county began copying the roll, and the woman of twenty-nine has not mentioned it since Wolfmonth.

Four days out, ninety-four drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, ninety-four on the twenty-fourth of Hearthmonth, which was the Thursday this week. Ninety-four is one more than ninety-three and a hundred will come on some Thursday in Mudmonth and nobody in nine counties has worked out which one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the ninth has not been started and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone. One man has been carrying the stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and fifty-four days.

The woman of twenty-nine lit the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs at the fourth hour herself and the copyist of twenty-six was up there the whole of that morning and did not come down. Ten questions are ruled in that register and none of them has a finding, and thirty days have not produced one. The day-book stands at eleven entries. The fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled. The press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of the day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is in no document this county keeps.

Four lines of chalk are on the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood that keeps the weather off them. They mean four. They have not been touched since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nobody has read them out loud to anybody and nobody has wiped one of them and nobody has added a fifth. The chalk itself is in a press drawer upstairs and he has not asked her for it once in four and a half years. Four hundred people went past them that morning and four looked down.

The room does not run the practice on a market morning, and so for thirty days there has been no fourth hour on that floor, and the number last said out loud there is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

The count on that frame has been five things and three questions for thirty days. The notice wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is two hundred and ninety-five days past its date. The roll wants a name against each of nine lines and has three copies of it. The sheet wants one name and has thirty-three days left in it. A month is long enough for a county to be asked a question three times over, and this county has been asked once and has written nothing down anywhere.

The sheet is true in what it says and false in what it assumes, and both of those are on the page of it. A paper that is true and false at once is a paper nobody in nine counties can throw away and nobody in nine counties can use.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and seventy-four days. Its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's. Nobody has filled it in and nobody has torn it up and it is still on that frame where it was nailed. Folded inside the covering paper at the back of the second copy there is a sheet folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it.

Two refusals stand in four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand. They were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth, and nobody has asked either of those two men anything since, and nobody is going to.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, a young man of nineteen with a honey pail on his hip was asked one question about his own trade.

He was asked it by a person who wanted to know what a man does with five frames of brood in the fourth month of winter.

“**That sheet wants one name and it has a date at the foot of it about five weeks off, and five weeks from now there will be five frames under nine skirts or there will not be five frames, and a name on a line does not change which of those two it is.**”

Nine people heard all of it. Five did not.

Nobody argued with him about any part of it, and nobody in that market street said anything back to him for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and nine people went on what they were doing.

A woman selling honey at the third door said that was no answer to anything, and he said it was not meant to be, and she said then what was it meant to be, and he said it was meant to be the true thing, and she said the true thing about what, and he said about the frames, and she said anybody can see the frames, and he said that was the trouble, and neither of them said anything else and both of them were right.

He has said twice out loud, before this, that he will make no more bees than the bees he has got. That was the third thing he has said out loud in that street in four and a half years. Nine people heard all of it and four hundred did not, and nobody in nine counties has asked him for an account of the other two.

The man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour and was in that street at the tenth and heard all three of the three things inside one week and said nothing out loud about any of them and did not answer any of them and did not say that any of them was wrong. He kept it to the eleventh hour.

He had meant to hear three things in one week out of three people who are not him, and he had heard them.

Standing there he found that for the first time in four and a half years the arithmetic in his head had nothing to add up, and he was obliged to sit with that. Three answers, three trades, one street, one week, and not one of them was a refusal of the sheet and not one of them would be carried by the same county. A woman who washes for nine households will not speak for them. A man who has made four hundred hurdles will not fix them in one place. A man of nineteen will not promise what five frames will do.

There is no fourth thing to weigh the three against, because he had not offered one, and there is no line on the sheet with his name against it that he could offer one on.

The woman who had been at the honey stall found him later at the boards and asked him, pointfully, whether he had an opinion.

“I have four.”

“That is not the question.”

“Then ask it properly and I will answer it.”

She looked at the four lines on the roll for a while and then said that a man who has four opinions and writes none of them down is a man who has decided that the four are worth more unrecorded than they would be recorded, and that she had known men like that and had married one.

“What happens to the four?”

“They sit where they are until somebody asks me the same question twice.”

“Who is going to ask you twice?”

He did not have an answer to that and he did not pretend to. He had been in this county four and a half years to stop being asked where a figure came from, and he had achieved that completely, and the cost of achieving it completely was that nobody asked him anything at all, twice or once, and he had never once put those two facts in the same sentence until the seventh of a month on a Saturday at the low end of a market street in Hearthmonth.

“I do not know,” he said. “That is the honest end of it.”

“Then it is four opinions in a market town of two thousand people and the ninth of the frames is still on that gate and no one has said anything about it.”

The rest of that Saturday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in the time it takes to turn a cart twice over, and four hundred people went along that street and nine of them stopped. The two women at the third door argued over a bolt of cloth for four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day.

The light came off the west bank and found the top edge of the boards, and somebody lit the lamp at the high end of them, because the ninth hour of a latewinter Saturday goes dark early and there is something off the hard slate that wants light on it. Five of them went out into five trades and four stayed on at those boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-sixth of Hearthmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post is the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

The ninth mile stone is the height of a knee and the ring somebody had cleaned round the foot of it is a foot wide and half an inch deep and had filled with grit. Four hundred yards past it the eleventh mile stone carries nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back two hundred years, and a name cut into a stone two hundred years ago is a name nobody ever came back to fill in.

The five frames of brood in the walled yard are under nine straw skirts and the board is still on the skirt stones at the gate, and the young man has been in and out of that yard nine times with a honey pail and has not touched a frame and has not added a sixth. Four things said out loud in a market street in four and a half years is a man who has stopped answering with his hands.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are two miles of wall and orchard owned by nine households, and nine lines with a trade on each is the same problem as nine households on a mile, and neither of them has been asked.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are nine hundred yards dead inside its own width, four hundred and twenty-one days old, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them.

He went out of that market street at the eleventh hour and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, which is a thing he has not done once in six weeks and did not do this week either. The hurdles are still across the top of that cart track and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder and nobody has gone up to see that. Five things on that board and thirty-three days left on the sheet and the fourth line of the roll still empty on all three of its copies.

**And the cost of that Saturday is three things said out loud in one street in one week by three people who are not him, being a woman of thirty-nine in forty-two words, a man of forty-four in thirty-seven words and a young man of nineteen in fifty-four words, and nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road who have heard none of them and nine hundred more who have not heard that it is the best offer anybody in this county has ever been made, a sheet thirty days on a board and thirty-three days left in it, three questions on one frame that do not agree with one another, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three, eighty-one days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet three hundred and seventy-three days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
