**Hearthmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 323, a Saturday and a market morning with the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and a market in that street, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in it, thirty days after a sheet went up on those boards and about five weeks before the date at the foot of it, and the third of three things said out loud in one street in one week by three people who are not him.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and the street was full by the ninth hour of the twenty-sixth of Hearthmonth with about four hundred people in it and carts along both sides and five things on the boards at the low end. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house came off at first light and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Saturday four days before the first of Goatmonth, and the ordinary part is that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have thirty days of not knowing left in them before a month turns and about five weeks after that.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the twenty-fourth of Hearthmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety-four drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four people in nine counties have said since that ninety-four is one more than ninety-three and that a hundred will come on some Thursday in Mudmonth and that nobody in nine counties has worked out which one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for six hundred and fifty-four days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and has not been picked up, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The practice did not run on that morning and the number last said out loud at the fourth hour is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is two hundred and seventy-nine mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the twenty-sixth of Hearthmonth and two hundred and seventy-three of them were due on a day this room is shut, and six of those went by in nine days of water with nobody standing on that floor, and nobody is going to recover the six.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and he is one of the nine and has been one of the nine for about a year. Eight of the ten things have been held on every morning but two since the first of Wolfmonth. One of the two is the head of a cart track with nine hurdles across it that nine people at the end of it put there and would like to stay, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth YR 322, which is three hundred and seventy-three days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is three hundred and fifty-four days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is four hundred and twenty-one days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that four hundred and twenty-one days is a year and fifty-six days, and five have said that nine feet of it across is the width of the made road and of the broom and the length of the gap in that wall, and four have said that it will not close over in this winter either and that nobody in nine counties has been asked about the straw under it.

The count of things on that board is five and the count of questions on it is three, and it has been five and three for thirty days. The first question is the notice's own and wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred and twenty-five days past its date, and the second is the roll's own and has nine empty lines on each of three copies, and the third is the sheet's and wants one name and has about five weeks left in it. About four people in nine counties have said since that a month is long enough for a county to be asked a question three times over and that this county has been asked once and has written nothing down anywhere.

The sheet that went up on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth asks for the name of one person to stand against the roads of nine counties and says under the Marshal's hand in two sentences that the place nine days ride off is being held so that what is called the Sundering is not done again in this world. It has been on that frame thirty days and nobody in nine counties has put a name against it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the sheet is true in what it says and false in what it assumes, and that both of those are on the page of it, and five have said that a paper which is true and false at once is a paper nobody in nine counties can throw away and nobody in nine counties can use.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and seventy-four days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down. Inside the covering paper folded over the back of the second copy there is a sheet of paper folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked. Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to be.

Then about the tenth hour of that Saturday, at the low end of that market street, a young man of about nineteen with a honey pail on his hip was asked one question about his own trade, kindly, by a person who wanted to know what a man does with five frames of brood in the fourth month of winter. He answered in fifty-four words and about nine people in that market street heard all of it and about five did not.

“**That sheet wants one name and it has a date at the foot of it about five weeks off, and five weeks from now there will be five frames under nine skirts or there will not be five frames, and a name on a line does not change which of those two it is.**”

About nobody in nine counties argued with him about any part of it, and nobody in that market street said anything back to him for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about nine people went on what they were doing. About four people in nine counties had worked out inside nine seconds that the third of three things said out loud in that street in that week was also not a refusal of the sheet, and five had not, and four have said since that a woman who washes for nine households will not speak for nine households, and a man who has made four hundred hurdles will not put a name on a line, and a man of nineteen will not promise what five frames will do, and that about none of the three is the sheet and that about no two of the three can be carried by the same county.

He has said twice out loud that he will make no more bees than the bees he has got, and this was the third thing he has said out loud in that street in about four and a half years and about nine people heard all of it and about four hundred did not, and nobody in nine counties has asked him for an account of the other two.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour and had been in that street at about the tenth and had heard all three of those things inside one week and had said nothing out loud about any of them and had not answered any of them and had not said that any of them was wrong. That was the whole of what he came down for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to hear three things in one week out of three people who are not him, and he had heard them, and the three of them do not add up to a position and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have heard none of them.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and two hours in a street with about four hundred people round it and had a hot band across the knee by about the eleventh hour. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And in eighty-one days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document and has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that was on his hand all through the two hours he was in that street.

The ordinary part of that Saturday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and about four hundred people went along that street and about nine of them stopped. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out of the shop with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street all day.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Hearthmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the twenty-sixth of Hearthmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-sixth of Hearthmonth is a Saturday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about five frames under about nine skirts is the size of a thing nobody in nine counties can be asked about and that about five weeks is the size of a thing everybody in nine counties can count.

The room over that weigh-house stood open from first light, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour herself, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up there the whole of that morning and did not come down them. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and the press stands open with no date on it, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is not in any document this county keeps.

There are four lines of chalk on the stone sill of that weigh-house door under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have not been touched since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read them out loud to anybody and nobody has wiped one of them and nobody has added a fifth, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it, and about four hundred people went past them that morning and about four looked down.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and had been cleaned with a bit of stick on the Saturday before at about the seventh hour, and the clean ring at the foot of it was a foot wide and half an inch deep and had filled with grit. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and nobody in nine counties can read more than nine of them, and about four have said that about nine hundred names cut by people who never met is the only roll in this county nobody had to be paid to keep, and five have said that a name cut into a stone two hundred years ago is a name nobody ever came back to fill in.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts, and the board on the skirt stones at the gate was still on them, and the young man of about nineteen had been in and out of that yard about nine times with a honey pail and had not touched a frame and had not added a sixth. Four people in nine counties have said since that a man who takes honey about nine times and does not touch a frame has said no three times without speaking, and five have said that he has now said a fourth thing out loud and that a fourth thing said out loud in a market street is a man who has stopped answering with his hands.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile are a set of people with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that about nine lines with a trade on each is the same problem as nine households on a mile and that neither of them has been asked.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is four hundred and twenty-one days old and has never been held by anybody at all.

Then the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, which is before the light, and no number was said on that floor, because the room over that weigh-house is open on a market morning and the practice does not run on one. That is the whole of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that three things said out loud in one street in one week by three people who are not him is the largest number of things said out loud in that street in about four and a half years, and five have said that about three is not a decision and is not a refusal and is not a movement.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who hears three things in a week and repeats none of them back is a man nobody thanks, because repeating is the only thing anybody in a market street can do with a thing they have heard. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and he had heard three things and written none of them down and answered none of them.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg bad and about nine miles of ache in it and the stick in his right hand, and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. The hurdles were still across the top of that cart track and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one is gone to powder, and there were five things on that board and about five weeks left on the sheet, and the fourth line of the roll was still empty on all three of its copies.
