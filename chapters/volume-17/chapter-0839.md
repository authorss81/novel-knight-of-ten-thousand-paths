# Chapter 0839

**Longlight the seventh, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and sixty-one days after a sheet went up on those boards, ninety-eight days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and one question asked at the low end of that street about kerb and answered, and a name given that was not asked for.**

By the ninth hour there were about four hundred people in that street and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill.

The door of the low room at the back of the Crown's road office stood open on its hook, and it was the fourth of the four market mornings it has stood open on, being the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth, the two Saturdays after it and Longlight the seventh, and about nine people were inside it by the tenth hour, and about four hundred people went in and out of that office in front of it that morning and about nine went further in than the counter.

The drawer eleven inches deep in that room was not opened. It was not opened on that Thursday and it has not been opened once in a hundred and sixty-one days, and the county's one seal is in it with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for.

About nine people went into that room for the fourth morning of the four and came out of it, and about nine people who did not go in did not go in, and nobody has asked either of those two groups why. A room with a table in it and a drawer in the wall is a thing nine people can be interested in on a market morning and four hundred cannot.

At the low end of that street there was a barrow-cart with four loads of dressed kerb on it and a carter standing beside it, and about nine people were round that cart, and a woman of about thirty-four was standing at the near end of it with a sett under her arm and a pair of gloves on and a hammer in the belt of her skirt.

She had come across that causeway for the stone. The kerb on that cart is bought on this side and carried over in four loads and unloaded at the gate post, and she has crossed that water about twice in a fortnight since Mudmonth and has laid four hundred yards of the nine hundred yards of kerb that have gone down on that far bank since Mudmonth. Her own four hundred yards is the stretch that begins at the bank and runs inland past a shut door, and that door has not been opened in nine years and has nothing to do with kerb.

The man of about forty-three with a stone merchant's yard eleven miles up that made road to the north asked her one question, kindly, at the low end of that market street, in his own trade, and about nine people were near enough and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**I lay kerb. I have laid about four hundred yards of it since Mudmonth and I lay nine feet of it myself in a week, and I am paid by the yard, and the stone comes across this water four loads at a time.**”

It was forty-four words. She said them into the space in front of that cart and about four people heard all of them and about five heard the end of them, and about four hundred people in that market street went on doing what they were doing.

Then the man of thirty-two with the stick asked her what to call her, because he wanted to know who to ask a second time, and she told him, and it was Dalla Quillan.

“**It is Dalla Quillan, and I am at the low end of this street about twice in a fortnight for stone and not for anything else.**”

It was twenty-six words. About nine people heard it and nobody repeated it and nobody wrote it down. About four people in that street have worked out since that a name on a person who comes twice in a fortnight for stone is the only kind of name that can be asked a second time, and about five have said that he asks it the same way he asks everything else, which is once, in her trade, kindly, and then he does not ask again.

She is about thirty-four and has been laying kerb since she was about twenty-two, and about four hundred yards of the line on that far bank is hers, and nine feet of it is a week, and nine people have been laying kerb on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth, and nine hundred yards of it has gone down.

There is nobody in this county who has asked her what she is paid for, because she has been paid by the yard and everybody who knows that knows it by looking, and nobody has asked her by whom. About four people in nine counties have said since that a person who is paid by the yard by somebody is the ordinary arrangement in nine counties, and about five have said that it is ordinary on this side of the water and not ordinary on the other side of it and nobody has worked out why.

About four people in nine counties have also worked out the arithmetic of it, being that a person who lays nine feet of kerb in a week does not lay four hundred yards of it in four months, and four people have said that nine people at nine feet a week for nine weeks is not nine hundred yards either, and about five have said that nobody has asked anybody to make those two numbers agree and nobody is going to.

A road kept by a warrant is a road whose keeping is somebody else's decision. The four miles above that causeway have been the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth. This county has walked on one for about six months and has not asked, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth.

About four hundred people in nine counties have said out loud that not one of the four hundred people on that far bank has ever been across that water in nine years, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road did not know on that Thursday that one of the four hundred had come over twice in a fortnight for stone. Four people in nine counties have said that one crossing is not a road and five have said that a standing in a county about a thing is a standing about a thing and not a thing itself.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and five days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up, and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and twenty-six days past its own date and has shut nothing, and the date at the foot of the sheet beside them went past ninety-eight days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and twenty-six days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it, and nobody in nine counties has been asked whose trade is on any of the nine, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has ever met another.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs unlit, because it is a market morning, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and sixty-one days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it and was told about no name either. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at that back carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

The four lines of chalk on the stone sill of that weigh-house door are under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it.

Four days out, a hundred and thirteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and thirteen went up that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and eighty-five days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the seventh of Longlight is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and four days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and eighty-five days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and nobody is going to.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and fifty-two days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over. Four things in this county are about nine feet across and a woman laying nine feet of kerb a week is a fifth, and none of the five has been asked about.

The rest of that Thursday went on, and the two women at the third door were still at their bolt of cloth in the doorway at noon, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and a man at the second stall measured nine feet of tape twice in a morning and got the same answer twice. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Longlight is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and the barrow-cart with four loads of dressed kerb on it went off up that made road at about the eleventh hour and did not go toward the causeway.

Nine doors down from those boards the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day a name was given in a market street. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and twelve days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that nobody in this county has seen and nobody on the far bank of a causeway four days south has heard of. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a name given in a market street. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and twelve days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stood open on its hook behind him with the drawer in it shut. Five things were on that board and ninety-eight days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a door open on four market mornings with about nine people inside it each time and the drawer not opened once in a hundred and sixty-one days, nine trades on nine lines of a paper nobody can seal and no names on any of them, and a woman of about thirty-four who lays about nine feet of kerb a week on the far bank of a causeway and who has laid about four hundred yards of the about nine hundred yards that have gone down there since Mudmonth and who crosses that water twice in a fortnight for stone and who was asked one question about kerb and answered it in forty-four words and gave her name in twenty-six. Nobody on this side has asked what she is paid for and by whom, and about four hundred people on that bank have never been across, and a road kept by somebody else's decision has been walked on for about six months. Two hundred and twelve days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet five hundred and four days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
