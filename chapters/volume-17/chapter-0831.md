# Chapter 0831

**Mudmonth the thirty-second, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and twenty-six days after a sheet went up on those boards, sixty-three days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and ninety-one days of a hand paper with a trade on each of its nine lines and no name on any of them.**

That market filled the street from about the eighth hour and by nine there were about four hundred people in it, and the five things were up on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

He came down that lane at the eighth hour with the stick in his right hand and turned his head away from the made road as he came, so that the noise of the morning came down the ear he still had. He had been doing that at every gate and every stile for about four and a half years, and it had never once occurred to him to describe it to anybody as an arrangement, because it was not an arrangement. It was only the way he walked, and he was about as sure of that as he was of anything in this county.

A market morning is a Thursday or a Saturday in this county, and there are twelve of them in the six weeks this stretch of days runs to and thirty mornings on which the room over that weigh-house is shut behind its own door instead. The practice happens on the thirty and not on the twelve, and that is the whole of why it is on a floor about nine feet by eleven and not in a room.

He had worked that out in his first winter here and had stopped finding it remarkable. A thing a man can only do on the days a county is shut behind its own door is not a practice. It is what is left of one, and there is nobody in nine counties he could have said that to.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and seventy days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it has not been filled in and has not been taken down and has not been torn up, and no name in nine counties has gone against any road on any paper. The notice nailed above those three copies is three hundred and ninety-one days past its own date and has shut nothing. The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past sixty-three days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road do not know there is a date on anything.

This county's own copy of the hand paper is on the flap of a stall at the low end of that street and it went onto that flap on the first of Goatmonth with a straight edge and a knife, and it has been there ninety-one days, and it has nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any of the nine and no seal on it, and it has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere.

The nine trades are a wheelwright, a saddler, a tanner, a slater, a cooper, a miller, a smith, a drover and a woman who keeps a dairy, and they were put there by nine different hands in nine sittings over about four and a half weeks. About nine people in nine counties have asked nobody in this county whose trade any of the nine is.

He stood about four feet off that flap and read the nine trades off it again the way he had read them off it every market morning since the first of Goatmonth. He knew the shape of what he was looking for and had never once found it, which had begun to feel like an answer rather than like a failure, and it was about as far as he had got with it in ninety-one days.

A line with a trade on it can be argued with. A line with a name on it cannot, because a trade can be looked up and a name cannot, and a person can be asked a question and a trade cannot be asked anything at all. That is the finding and it has not moved in ninety-one days and it is not going to move by anybody standing at a stall.

About four hundred places are now writing the same paper out by hand. No two of about four hundred copies agree with one another, because nobody has asked them to agree and nobody has shown one to another. Not one of the people writing one has ever met another of them, and about four hundred people in nine counties are each holding a piece of paper that binds nobody, seals nothing, asks nothing of anybody, and has nobody's name on it.

The man of thirty-two with the stick stood at the low end of that street with the market going round him and asked one question, kindly, in his own trade, of about nine people, and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**I want to know whether anybody in this county knows who else is writing one of these out, and I am not asking what is on anybody's, because it is not mine to ask.**”

It was thirty-four words. He said them into the noise of a market morning and nobody argued with him about any part of them, and the answer he got was that about four people in this market street did not know and about five knew a person who had not said.

He had worked out long ago what he was doing when he asked a question in another person's trade instead of his own. It was not kindness, exactly. It was that a question about your own trade is a question about a thing you already know the shape of, and a person can hear that in the first four words and stop listening, which takes about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over. Kindness was in it too, and he would have let that stand if anybody had put it to him.

Then he asked a second question, of nobody in particular, and it was seven words, and about nine people near enough heard it.

“**Then nobody in nine counties knows either.**”

About four people in that street have said since that a man who asks two questions in a market morning and neither of them is about a name has worked out something, and about five have said that what he has worked out is that a paper four hundred people are writing is not a document and has no keeper and cannot be got hold of, and neither of those two is on any paper and neither of them was said out loud in that street.

The register in the room over that weigh-house holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and twenty-six days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on the top shelf of it above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in about four and a half years, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

He had come down that lane that morning meaning to look at the sill, and had not looked at it, and knew that he would not go back up at the fourth hour tonight and look at it either. There was a particular kind of not-looking he had got very good at, and it was about the only skill in this county that had improved in four and a half years.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street, which is about nine feet from that frame, there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the ninth hour with about nine things done one after another and nothing written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Thursday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it in about nine years, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and twenty-six days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Four days out, a hundred and eight drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and a hundred and eight went up that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that market street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and fifty days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and it does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirty-second of Mudmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water, and the standing in this county is that not one of them has ever been across in about nine years.

The four miles of made road above that causeway have been the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth, and nobody in nine counties has asked anybody what a road that is smooth for a reason is.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot with a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on that shoe gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and sixty-nine days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and fifty days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle was lifted and no bar was tried and no bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has moved in about eleven years. The ninth thing is alive and unnamed and unheld and shut.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and seventeen days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the yard wall is about nine feet high and the board is still on the skirt stones at that gate. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over. Four things in this county are about nine feet across, being the gap in that wall, the width of that made road, the width of the broom and the width of the dead line of grass, and about nine feet of kerb a week is a fifth measure in this county that comes to nine, and nobody has asked about that either.

The rest of that Thursday went on the way a market morning goes on, being that about four hundred people in that street did about four hundred things that have nothing to do with a flap of a stall. A carter's near wheel came off at the low end of that street and he got it up again with a bar while the people round him kept talking, and he was under it for about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice and then drove off with the wheel on. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Mudmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody near enough to lift it and nobody near enough to ask what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house was face down and was not turned over on the morning a man asked two questions about a paper. Three series stand in that room and none of them is derived from another, and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county. A press with no date on it keeps whatever is put in it, and the four copied pages have been on its top shelf since the twenty-seventh of Thawmonth, and about nine people in nine counties know they exist.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which takes eleven miles in four hours before light and about nine otherwise and is worse than it was at the close of the year before, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and seventy-seven days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that nobody standing at a flap of a stall with a straight edge has ever seen. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence in an unclaimed hand on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Nor is the paper four hundred people are writing with no seal on any of them. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and seventy-seven days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and sixty-three days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a door at the back of an office that has been shut behind its own door for a hundred and twenty-six days with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that has not been opened once, and nine trades on nine lines of a paper nobody can seal and nobody in nine counties has met anybody else who has written one, and two questions said out loud in a market morning and neither of them about a name, and about four hundred places writing the same paper out by hand and no two of about four hundred copies agreeing because nobody asked them to. A line with a trade on it can be argued with and a paper nobody can seal binds nobody, which is safe, and about four hundred places writing one is the largest single act of mutual aid in nine counties in about a year and is not a document. One hundred and seventy-seven days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and sixty-nine days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
