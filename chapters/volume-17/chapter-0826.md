# Chapter 0826

**Mudmonth the thirteenth, YR 324, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, a hundred and seven days after a sheet went up on those boards, forty-four days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of about forty-three saying one thing out loud in a lane at the low end of that street about a road he has been up twice.**

That Saturday put about four hundred people into that street and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm and had been for an hour.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and fifty-one days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. The notice above them is three hundred and seventy-two days past its own date and has shut nothing. The date at the foot of that sheet went past forty-four days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have never known there was a date on it at all.

Four days out, a hundred and five drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the last of them went up on the Thursday before, which was the eleventh of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that street has ever watched one go up.

Then, at about the eleventh hour, at the low end of that market street, in the lane that runs back off it between the office and the second door and is about nine feet wide at the mouth and narrows to about six inside, a man of about forty-three said one thing out loud about his own trade, to about nine people, and about four of them heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

He keeps a stone merchant's yard about eleven miles up that made road to the north and has kept it about nine years, and he sells setts and kerb and about nine feet of anything a person wants laid, and about four hundred people in nine counties have bought stone off him and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not.

He has been up the Wethergill cart track twice. Once in Goatmonth of the year before last and once in Harvestmonth of last year, both times with a cart, both times to see what was laid at the end of it, because a man who sells stone is paid for what is already down and not for what ought to be, and nine miles of cart road is about four hours each way with a loaded cart and two with an empty one, and he went twice and paid for both himself out of nine feet of setts a week.

“**I have been up that track twice to see what is laid at the end of it, and what is laid at the end of it is not the road in those four copied pages, and nobody in nine counties has ever known what the road in those four copied pages is, and I have spent two trips and about nine miles of cart road to find out that I do not know either.**”

It was seventy-four words. He said them into a lane with a wall on one side of him and about nine people in it, and nobody argued with him about any part of it.

Being told the thing you went and looked at twice is not the thing you went for.

That is a cost and it is not an oversight, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who goes and looks twice and is told he went to look at the wrong thing is a man who has been paid for two trips of about nine miles each by nobody and who has learned one thing that nobody in nine counties had, and about five have said that he could have learned it by asking one person in a market street in about nine seconds, and nobody asked him why he went up there twice and nobody is going to.

About a mile of made road and then a cart track with a stone in the middle of it, and nine hurdles across the head of that track foot to foot with a hurdle shoe on the top one and a thumb of lime on that shoe gone to powder, and about nine houses about four hundred yards back behind those hurdles who put them there themselves and would like them to stay and said so once through a shut door in twenty-nine words.

There was nobody at the head of that track in this six weeks. No hurdle was lifted and no bar was tried and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years and nobody lifted it. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and fifty days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and thirty-one days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth. The ninth thing is alive and unnamed and unheld and shut, and a man of about forty-three with a stone merchant's yard has now been told in a lane in this county that it is not the road in four pages, and about nine people in nine counties have worked out that this county now knows one road it does not have and about five have worked out that a man who sells stone is the only person in this county who would have gone and looked twice.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. Nobody has added to them since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about nine people went past that door on the thirteenth of Mudmonth and about four of them looked down, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and he was at that counter at about the eleventh hour with a sheet of foolscap in front of him and a pen in his hand and nothing to write on it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Saturday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap in it is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and thirty-one days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the thirteenth of Mudmonth is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and ninety-eight days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Nine households graze beasts on a rota they have kept for about nine years on the flat at the end of that track, and the same nine households put the nine hurdles across the head of it, and about four people in this county have said that a rota kept for nine years and a hurdle put up once are two different things, and about five have said that the people at the end of it would like the hurdles to stay and have said so once and are not going to say it twice.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and about four people in nine counties have said that four things in one county are about nine feet across and that not one of the four has been asked about.

A stone merchant's yard eleven miles up that made road sells about nine feet of setts a week in a good week and four feet in a bad one, and about four hundred people in nine counties buy off it while about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not been inside it, and a man who sells what is already down has a standing reason to go and see whether anything new has been laid anywhere.

The rest of that Saturday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and one of them carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Mudmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed on the boards.

The man of thirty-two stood at that frame for the last half hour of that morning with the leg going and the stick in his right hand and heard what the man of about forty-three said in the lane behind him and said nothing about it, and about four people in that market street had worked out that he heard it and about five had not, and about nine people along eleven miles of that made road have heard neither the seventy-four words nor the refusal.

A man of thirty-two who has been in this county about four and a half years and who put nine of his own roads behind a refusal of the copied pages on the Saturday before has now been in a lane on the Saturday after behind a man who sells stone, and about four people in nine counties have said that a county which can only refuse has found out this month what it is refusing and about five have said that what it is refusing is a road nobody can name.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county. The four copied pages are on the top shelf of that press above the half leaf and are in that room and not in his keeping.

The cost of that Saturday was in the leg and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and fifty-eight days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that a man selling stone finding out in a lane about nine counties do not know a road does not touch. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and fifty-eight days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. Five things were on that board and forty-four days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of this is a man of forty-three with a stone merchant's yard who has been up a shut cart track twice with a cart to see what is laid at the end of it and who has been told in a lane in this county that the road behind the nine hurdles is not the road in four copied pages and that nobody in nine counties has ever known what the road in those four pages is, seventy-four words said out loud once in his own trade to about nine people with about four hearing all of it and about five hearing the end of it, the thing you went and looked at twice not being the thing you went for, and that being a cost and not an oversight, two trips of about nine miles of cart road paid for by nobody, nine houses about four hundred yards back behind nine hurdles that they put there themselves and would like to stay, no hurdle lifted and no bar tried and the bar of ash across the Low Gate where it has been for about eleven years, forty-four days past a date that has shut nothing, two hundred and fifty-one days of a roll on that frame with the fourth line empty on all three of its copies, three hundred and seventy-two days past a printed notice that has shut nothing, a low room shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, one hundred and fifty-eight days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and fifty days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
