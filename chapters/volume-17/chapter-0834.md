# Chapter 0834

**Mudmonth the forty-third, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, one hundred and thirty-seven days after a sheet went up on those boards, seventy-four days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a hundred and two days of a hand paper on the flap of a stall at the low end of a market street.**

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and fiftieth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The number has been four for three hundred and forty-six mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not, and the six mornings this county lost to that water in Mudmonth are six lost mornings.

“The stall man has had that paper up a hundred days.”

“A hundred and two.”

“He does not know it is there, does he.”

“He put it on the flap and he does not take it off and he does not know what is on it, and about four hundred people a week go past that flap and about nine of them stop.”

He has thought about that man more than he has thought about most people in this county, and what he has arrived at is that the stall man is doing something that nobody in nine counties has thought of doing, which is to leave a paper where about four hundred people a week will walk past it and take it off his flap to wash on a wet morning and put it back the same morning. A keeper is a thing people expect of a man like that. A man who washes round it is not a keeper of anything, and about nine people stop at that flap and none of them stops twice.

Then, at about the sixth hour, he was four days out at the ninth mile stone on that made road, and the stone stands at the height of a knee, and a foot wide and half an inch deep of clean ground is round the foot of it where somebody went round it with a bit of stick on the Saturday before, and it had taken grit into it again by the middle of the day.

The eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past that one has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years, and about nine of those names can still be read, and about four people in nine counties have tried to read more, and nobody in nine counties has cut a name into it in about a hundred and forty-four years because a person who cuts a name into a mile stone is doing a thing about the road and not a thing about a name, and about five people in nine counties have worked out that those are two different things and about four have not.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on that flap for a hundred and two days and it has nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any of the nine and no seal on it, and it has been sealed nothing and nailed nowhere and sent nowhere, and about four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand.

There is nobody in nine counties who knows who else in nine counties has one, and nobody in nine counties knows what is on the copy that is not on this one, and nobody has shown one to another, because there is no road in nine counties along which about four hundred people could go and show about four hundred papers to one another, and there is a Crown's cart in the ordinary post that goes up and down this made road four times in a good month, and its driver has never once been asked what is in it and does not know.

About four hundred places writing the same paper out by hand is the largest single act of mutual aid in nine counties in about a year. Nobody arranged it. Nobody has met anybody who did any part of it in any other part of it, and no two of about four hundred copies agree with one another, and about nine counties have about four hundred people each holding a piece of paper that binds nobody.

A line with a trade on it can be argued with, because a trade can be looked at and a trade can be asked about in a market street by anybody who has a question about it. A line with a name on it cannot be argued with, because a name on a paper is not a person in front of you and cannot be asked anything at all. That is the finding and it has not moved in a hundred and two days.

A paper nobody can seal binds nobody. That is safe, and about four people in nine counties have said that out loud and about five have said that binding nobody is not the same as owing nobody, and neither of those two is on any paper and neither of them has been said out loud in this county's market street.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and eighty-one days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not taken down and it is not torn up. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and two days past its own date and has shut nothing, and the date at the foot of the sheet beside them is seventy-four days past and nothing in nine counties is shut on it.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on that sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and thirty-seven days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it, and nothing about four hundred places went into that room. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Four days out, a hundred and nine drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the last of them went up on the Thursday before, which was the thirty-ninth of Mudmonth, at about the sixth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and sixty-one days, and eight of the nine yards of that wall are back in, and the gap in it is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and it does not start, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

A Crown's cart came up that made road in the ordinary post going the other way and did not stop, and its driver did not know what was in the two satchels under his seat. Nine hundred people along eleven miles of that road have never once been told what goes up and down it in the ordinary post, and more paper has come into this county in four and a half years than has ever gone out of it.

About nine hundred yards of a stair nine days off from any town in this county is being argued about in about four hundred places at one time, by people who have never met one another, and about nine hundred yards of a stair nine days off is a thing this county has read about in a room and has not been on and will not be on. This county has contributed nine trades to it and no names, and nobody in this county has been asked to contribute anything to it, and nobody in this county is a delegate to anything.

The trouble with that stair was that he could not raise it as a want of his own. Nobody in this county has been asked to contribute anything to it and he has not asked to contribute anything to it, and there is a difference between those two that nobody in nine counties has ever put a question to, and about four and a half years of standing at the low end of that market street had taught him that the difference was the only part of the whole arrangement anybody could actually call his.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the forty-third of Mudmonth is a Monday, and the lamp was put there about the third hour. He was about nine miles north of that coping with the line of stone on the bank beside him, and the water was off that causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and the standing in this county is that not one of them has ever been across in about nine years.

The four miles above that coping have been the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties since the fifth of Wolfmonth. About nine people in nine counties know why, and about nine hundred along eleven miles of that made road have never known the road was smooth, and nobody in nine counties has asked anybody about it.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot with a hurdle shoe on the top one and the thumb of lime on that shoe gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and eighty days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and sixty-one days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and twenty-eight days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The line of stone on that bank has been in the weather since the frost and two of the stones in it have moved four inches downhill, and the mallet is on the top of the line rather than beside it, and has been on the top of the line since the middle of Hearthmonth. He has shifted the two stones back twice with his foot and has not picked the mallet up. A man who puts his foot under a stone is still a man who is not picking the mallet up.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in, and the young man of about nineteen has carried a pail in and out of that yard about nine times this week and has not opened a skirt, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over. Four things in this county are about nine feet across and not one of the four has been asked about.

The byre he slept in that night has a cow door that does not shut and a hinge that has not been oiled since before he came to this county, and the cold comes in off the floor at the fifth hour and goes out at the ninth. He has slept in four byres in four and a half years and has never once slept in a room. The woman who keeps the inn at the high end of that market street kept her board out on the made street from first light and had four beds full and marked four on a tally-stick by the door.

The rest of that Monday went on and about nine people were in that market street and no more than about nine, and four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something during the morning and five did not. A woman of about forty put a pail out on the paving outside the second door at about the tenth hour and took it in again at about the fourth and neither time said anything to anybody. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped and nothing else.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and put out the last of the wet on the top row of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Mudmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody lifted it and nobody within about nine feet of it asked what was underneath. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over. Three series stand in that room and none of them is worked out from another, and the band figure is written down nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Monday was the leg, which did eleven miles of a made road and four hundred yards of a lane either side of it, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and eighty-eight days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that about four hundred people writing the same paper out by hand does not reach. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet in the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Nor is a paper with a trade on it and no name. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and eighty-eight days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone twice in one day, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and seventy-four days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of a hundred and two days of a paper is that about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of them has met another and no two of them agree, and a line with a trade on it can be argued with and a paper nobody can seal binds nobody, which is safe, and binding nobody is not the same as owing nobody and is not a plan. Nine trades on nine lines and no names on any of them and nobody in nine counties asked whose trades they are, and about nine hundred yards of a stair nine days off being argued about in about four hundred places by people who have never met, and this county's contribution to it being nine trades and nobody in this county a delegate to anything. One hundred and eighty-eight days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and eighty days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
