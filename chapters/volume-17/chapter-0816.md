# Chapter 0816

**Thawmonth the sixth, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, seventy days after a sheet went up on those boards, seven days past the date at the foot of that sheet, a hundredth drove going up a lane four days off at the sixth hour, and a person standing in the doorway of a low room at the low end of that market street at about the tenth hour and not going in.**

The hundredth drove went up that lane four days off on that Thursday, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour of the afternoon, and two men went with it.

About nine people in that market street have never watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up. A hundred is a figure people argue about and nobody has ever argued about this one. The hundredth went up on a Thursday morning with the weather doing nothing, and about four hundred people four days off were in a street buying string, and neither of those facts is going to be made into anything by anybody.

Then the street was full by the ninth hour and the five things were on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

The date at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of Goatmonth and it is seven days past. Nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The roll has been on that frame two hundred and fourteen days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's, and the notice above them is three hundred and thirty-five days past its own date and has shut nothing in this county or in any of the other eight.

On a market morning the practice does not run at all, and that is the whole of what happened in that room over the weigh-house this morning, as it has been on every market morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth. The second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs is lit at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week when the market does not come and it goes out at about the sixth. The number last said out loud on that floor is four, and it was eight before the water, and it has not gone back.

The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in seventy days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in seventy days. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it.

At about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, a man of about thirty-four stood in the doorway of the low room at the back of the Crown's road office and did not go in.

There were about nine people in that street at that hour and about four of them saw him stand there and about five did not.

He had come up eleven miles of made road that morning with an empty cart behind a horse that did not need to be there, which is the one thing about him that anybody in that street thought about afterwards, because a carrier does not bring an empty cart up eleven miles of made road on a market morning for nothing. He had put the cart at the low end by the second door and had walked back down the street to the office as though he had come to buy a halfpenny copy of something.

The doorway of that low room is nine inches narrower than the front door of that office and it is behind the counter and behind the man who was born in that street, and a person standing in it is standing in the office and not in the room. He stood in it for about as long as it takes to read the sheet at the low end of the street twice.

He did not go in. He did not touch the door and he did not knock and he did not sit down on the clerk's table that is four feet inside it, and the man behind the counter did not ask him for a halfpenny and did not ask him anything at all.

At about the eleventh hour he went away. The door of that low room was shut behind him and it had been shut behind its own door on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody followed him, and nobody asked him why he had come.

About nine people in that street were not buying anything at the time and not one of them thought about what he came for, and the whole of that street knows what he did not do, which was to go in. He wanted to know whether a paper that had come into that office was going to shut a road he runs loads on, and about nine hundred yards of made road between here and the coast is what he runs them on, and nobody in nine counties told him anything, and no road in this county has been shut at all. A man who drives eleven miles with an empty cart and is told nothing goes back down eleven miles with an empty cart, and that is the ordinary way a question is answered in nine counties.

The drawer eleven inches deep in that room was not opened. The county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights are still in it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

About four people in that street had worked out inside nine seconds what the cheapest instrument an occupation has is, being a counter and a door and a man who was born in the street, and about five had worked out that the cheapest instrument is also the one nobody in nine counties has looked at. A man who stands in a doorway for the length of a reading and then goes away is cheaper than any of the about nine men with a warrant nine days ride off, because he costs nothing and travels nothing and agrees to nothing, and the reason he costs nothing is that nobody has offered him anything.

Then the man behind that counter came out and stood at the boards at about the eleventh hour, beside the man of thirty-two with the stick, which is the second time he has done that since the sheet went up.

About four people in that street had worked out that the two of them were talking and about five had not looked up.

The man of thirty-two was not at that door and was not in that doorway and was four hundred paces away at the low end of that street at about the tenth hour, at a frame, with the market going round him. He found out about it afterwards and did not go and look at the doorway.

The man behind the counter said it once, out loud, in his own trade, and it was sixty-three words, and about four people heard it and about five did not.

“**He stood in that doorway about as long as it takes to read that sheet twice and he did not go in, and I did not ask him why he had come and he did not tell me, and I have been behind this counter thirty-one years and that is the first man ever to stand in that doorway and not come in.**”

About four people heard that and about five did not, and nobody said anything back to him for as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

Then he said that he had not gone in after him and had not touched the door and had not opened the drawer, and that the drawer has not been opened by anybody in about nine years and that he has the key to it in his pocket and has not used it.

He said that last part as though it were the ordinary end of a sentence about a door, and about nine people heard all of it. A man with a key in his pocket for nine years who says he has not used it has said the only sentence anybody in nine counties has said this year about the county's one seal, and nobody asked him for it.

A counter and a door is an instrument that asks nothing of anybody, and the man behind that counter has now been asked nothing for about seventy days and has noticed it twice and has said so twice to one person. Being noticed by one person is not being asked, and a county which has worked out that its cheapest instrument is a man who will not be told anything is a county that has found the shape of its own problem.

He went back inside the office and did not go past the counter. The low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door and the drawer in it was not opened once more.

Then the man of thirty-two stood at those boards for the last half hour of that morning. He had meant to see whether seventy days of a sheet, seven days of a date going and coming, and a person standing in a doorway would produce a name anywhere in this county, and the answer was that a man had come eleven miles with an empty cart and had gone back down the road with it, and that nobody had followed him.

That is not a finding. It is the shape of a finding, and the shape of a finding is not a finding, and it is the only kind this county has ever got.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a board and stand at it for about two hours and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from, and which will always be the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and twenty-one days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties had asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that nobody in nine counties has ever given it a page of its own, and that there is no box for it and no shelf for it and no register in this county it could go in.

The rest of that Thursday went on. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account, and the same empty cart went down the made road at about the eleventh hour with a horse behind it that did not need to be there, over about nine hundred yards of made road and down past the ninth mile stone. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Thawmonth is dark and there is something off the hard slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the sixth of Thawmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years, and the four miles above that lamp are the smoothest four miles of made road in nine counties and about nine hundred people have not been told why.

The ninth mile stone on that made road four days off is the height of a knee, and a foot wide and half an inch deep of clean ground is round the foot of it where somebody went round it with a stick on the Saturday before, and it has grit in it again. The eleventh mile stone four hundred yards past that one has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years and about nine of them can still be read.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for six hundred and ninety-four days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

The brood in the walled yard four days off is five frames under about nine straw skirts and the yard wall is about nine feet high. The board on the skirt stones at that gate has not been taken off this week, and the young man of about nineteen has carried a pail in there and out of it about nine times and has not opened a skirt and has not added a sixth frame.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and thirteen days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and ninety-four days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and sixty-one days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone. Five things were on that board and three questions on it and seven days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and a low room at the back of that office was shut behind its own door with the drawer in it unopened, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a man of about thirty-four who came up eleven miles of made road with an empty cart and stood in a doorway at about the tenth hour for as long as it takes to read a sheet twice and did not go in and went away and was not followed and was not asked, sixty-three words said once out loud by a man who was born in that street about the first man in thirty-one years to stand in that doorway and not come in, a counter and a door and a man who was born in that street, which is the cheapest instrument an occupation has and which nobody in nine counties has looked at, a low room at the back of that office shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, a hundredth drove going up a lane nobody in nine counties has ever watched go up, a sheet seventy days on a board and seven days past the date on it and nothing shut anywhere in nine counties, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three and two hundred and fourteen days on that frame, one hundred and twenty-one days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and thirteen days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
