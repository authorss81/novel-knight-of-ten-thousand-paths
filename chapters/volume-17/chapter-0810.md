# Chapter 0810

**Goatmonth the seventh, YR 323, a Wednesday and not a market morning, the room over that weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, forty-one days after a sheet went up on those boards and nine days after nine lines were ruled on a flap of a stall, and the last day of the first six weeks of this, and nothing on any of the boards with a name on it.**

There were nine people in that street at the ninth hour and nine lines on a flap of a stall nine days old and the bolt had been on the room over that weigh-house since noon on the Saturday before.

The cloth was over the bench nine doors down and had been for six weeks and for the four hundred and eighty-odd days before that, and it will be there on Monday.

Four days out, ninety-five drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth and the same ninety-five went up six days ago. Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the ninth has not been started, and one man has been carrying the stone two miles in his own hands for six hundred and sixty-five days, which is the second year, and the ninth yard has now gone not started through two winters.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre, seven people were on their feet in the dark before the light and saying a number. It was the two hundred and eighty-second morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four.

“Say it again for me,” said the man with the bad hip, “because it is the last morning of six weeks and I want to hear whether it has moved.”

“It has not moved.”

“It was eight before the water and it is four now and it has been four for two hundred and seventy-six mornings, and there are nine of us, and we are going to go on saying it for as long as we can stand on this floor, and one day there will be two of us and then one, and then it will be a thing that was said.”

“Then it will have been said for a long time.”

“A long time is not the same as being heard. I have worked that out over these six weeks and I do not like it and I have not found a better version.”

The day-book in the room over that weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and nobody has ruled a fifth one anywhere in this county. The register holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in six weeks. The copyist of twenty-six has not come down those thirteen stairs on a Wednesday and has not come down them on a Wednesday in four and a half years. The press stands open and nothing in that room carries a date.

Four lines of chalk remain on the sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood the weather has never got behind, and they have gone six weeks without anybody adding to them. They mean four. They have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nobody has read them out loud to anybody in six weeks. The stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and he has never asked her for it.

At the back of the office at the low end of that market street there is a low room with a door in front of it and a counter in it and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep.

That room was shut behind its own door on every one of the six weeks of this. Nobody in nine counties went into it. The drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

At the front of that office there is a counter and behind the counter there is a man who was born in that street. About a clerk not touching a thing is not the same as the thing not being there, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, nine people looked at those nine lines again.

Nine people said what their own trade was again, and nine of them answered inside nine seconds again. Not one of the nine trades on those nine lines has been asked about by anybody in nine counties, and not one of the nine people whose trade it is has been asked whether it is there.

A line with a trade on it is a line somebody can be argued with, and the argument can only be had by somebody who knows the trade, and that is the whole of the finding, and the whole of the finding is worth about nine seconds, and nine seconds is not a government.

The count on that frame has been five things and three questions for forty-one days and will be five things and three questions tomorrow. The notice wants one name for every road in nine counties and is three hundred and six days past its date and has shut nothing. The roll wants a name against each of nine lines, has three copies of itself, and the fourth of the nine is this county's. The sheet wants one name and has twenty-two days left in it and is under the Marshal's hand.

Three questions on one frame do not agree with one another, and a frame that agrees with nothing is not a decision and is not a refusal and is a board.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and eighty-five days and about four hundred people have walked past it in six months. Its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth line is empty on all three. It is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down.

The man of thirty-two came down the four hundred pace lane at the ninth hour and stood at those boards and read the sheet one more time and touched nothing on that frame, and he did not go down to the flap of the stall, and he kept it to the eleventh hour.

He had meant to see whether six weeks of a sheet and nine lines and three things said out loud had produced a name anywhere in this county.

It had not.

He stood there and let that be the finding, because he had come four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and a man who will not put a name on a line has no figure to account for, and for six weeks that has been the cleanest thing he has had and it is not a thing anybody can use.

The cost of that Wednesday was the leg, four hundred paces of a lane and four hundred yards of a made road and the hot band across the knee by the seventh hour. And the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And ninety-two days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that has had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own. Nine figures in one woman's hand on the back of a day-book is not a cure for it. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet. Neither is four lines of chalk on a sill.

The rest of that Wednesday went on. A cart lost its near wheel at the low end of that street, and the carter had it back up on with a bar before anybody had finished saying so, and went on up the made road. The two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for four minutes by the clock, and went in, and came out with it wrapped and paid for. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full, and the two empty ones were marked on the tally-stick by the door and no board went out on the made street.

The light went off the west bank and found the upper edge of the boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Wednesday in Goatmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of them went out into five trades and four stayed on at the boards.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the seventh of Goatmonth is a Wednesday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post is the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years, and nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard of a sheet or a roll or a sentence on a folded sheet or nine lines on a flap of a stall.

At the head of the cart track the nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last lime cart came up that track three hundred and eighty-four days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut three hundred and sixty-five days.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are nine hundred yards dead inside its own width, four hundred and thirty-two days old, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and nobody is going to be held by anybody this year.

Between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill there are two miles of wall and orchard owned by nine households. A trade on a line is not a name. A household is not a name. This county has three kinds of thing that are not a name and has never had one.

At the boards, at about the half hour, the man behind the counter at the front of the office came out and stood beside him, which he had not done in six weeks, and neither of them said anything for a while.

“You have been standing at that frame every market morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth.”

“Not every market morning. Four of them.”

“That is not what I would have said either.”

The man behind the counter was born in that street. He was the fourth generation of it and had been at that counter since he was twenty-one, and in six weeks nobody had asked him what a gate is for, and he had noticed that nobody had, and it had begun to sit on him in a way he had not expected.

“There is a room at the back of this office,” he said at last, “and a drawer in it, and a seal in the drawer, and I am the fourth man in this family to be the one who has the key to it and the first one who has never been asked what it is for.”

“You have the key.”

“I have the key. It has been in my pocket since I was twenty-one.”

“You have not been asked because nobody wants to be told what a seal is for.”

“That is what I worked out at about the second day and I have been carrying it about for six weeks.” He took a key out of his pocket, looked at it, and put it back. “You are the only person in this county who has come down that lane in six weeks who has not asked me that, and I want you to know that I noticed, and that noticing is not the same as being glad.”

He went back inside. The door of the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door, and the drawer in it was not opened once more, and the county's one seal went on being in it.

He went out of that market street at the eleventh hour and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and that was the last day of the first six weeks of it. Five things were on that board, the sheet was still nailed up beside three copies of a roll, the fourth line of that roll was empty on all three of its copies, and the roll was not filled in and was not taken down.

**And the cost of six weeks is a printed sheet on a frame beside three copies of a roll and twenty-two days still left in it and nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road who have not heard it, three things said out loud in one street in one week by three people who are not him, not refusals of the sheet and not answers to it and not carryable by one county, this county's own copy of the hand paper begun with nine lines and nine trades and no names and no seal and not nailed up and not sent anywhere, a woman of sixty-one keeping the county's one seal in a drawer nobody went into and wanting the paper to be worth something, a man of thirty-eight who has said one true thing about four miles of made road out loud once and nine hundred people have not heard it, three copies of a roll with nothing on any of them and the fourth line empty on all three and not taken down, four lines of chalk under a hood where the rain does not reach that are still four and are still not writing, nine figures on the back of a day-book that is not the record, ten questions with no finding under any of them and no fifth head ruled, ninety-two days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet three hundred and eighty-four days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
