**Goatmonth the seventh, YR 323, a Wednesday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, forty-one days after a sheet went up on those boards and nine days after nine lines were ruled on a flap of a stall, and the last day of the first six weeks of this, and nothing on any of the boards with a name on it.**

A Wednesday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and the street was quiet at the ninth hour of the seventh of Goatmonth with about nine people in it and five things on the boards at the low end and nine lines on a flap of a stall nine days old. The bolt on the room over that weigh-house had gone on from noon on the Saturday before and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it. The cloth nine doors down was still over the bench, and about four people in that street had stopped seeing it, and about five had never seen it at all.

Then four days off along that made road, and about nine hundred people along it did the ordinary part of a Wednesday in the seventh day of a month, and the ordinary part is that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have forty-one days of not knowing behind them and three weeks of not knowing in front of them and a date on a sheet at the other end of the road that nobody in nine counties has answered.

A drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the first of Goatmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. That is ninety-five drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the same ninety-five went up six days ago, and four people in nine counties have said since that a drove which has not moved in six days is a drove somebody has stopped counting, and five have said that about nobody in nine counties counts it except the man who counts it.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in, and the gap in the wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side. One man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for six hundred and sixty-five days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank two miles off with the line of stone and has not been picked up, and about four people in nine counties have said since that six hundred and sixty-five days is the second year and that the ninth yard has now gone not started through two winters.

The practice has run two hundred and eighty-two mornings and the number said out loud at the fourth hour of the seventh of Goatmonth is four, and it was eight before the water and it has not gone back. It is two hundred and eighty-eight mornings on the calendar from the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth YR 322 to the seventh of Goatmonth, and six of them went by with nobody standing on that floor in nine days of water, and nobody is going to recover the six. Four people in nine counties have said since that two hundred and eighty-two mornings is the first figure of that practice in this county's history to be two hundred and eighty-something and that about the something is not a milestone and is not on a board.

There are ten things and about nine people know the number at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that room is shut, and he is one of the nine and has been one of the nine for about a year. Eight of the ten things have been held on every morning but two since the first of Wolfmonth, and both of the two are four days off on a made road and about four hundred yards inside nine hurdles. One of them is the head of a cart track that nine people at the end of it put nine hurdles across and would like to stay, and the last cart of lime came up that track on the thirteenth of Hearthmonth YR 322, which is three hundred and eighty-four days ago, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, which is three hundred and sixty-five days.

That tenth thing is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is four hundred and thirty-two days old. It is dead inside its own width and standing outside it, and it is on the back of a roadkeeper's board in a cart shed four days off. Four people in nine counties have kept saying that four hundred and thirty-two days is a year and about nine weeks, and five have said that it will not close over and that nine feet of it across is the width of the made road and of the broom and the length of the gap in that wall, and four have said that none of those three has been closed by anybody at all.

The count of things on that board is five and the count of questions on it is three, and it has been five and three for forty-one days, and it will be five and three tomorrow. The first question is the notice's own and wants the name of the person who keeps every road in nine counties and is three hundred and six days past its date and has shut nothing. The second is the roll's own and has nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's. The third is the sheet's and wants one name and has about three weeks left in it and is under the Marshal's hand. About four people in nine counties have said since that three questions on one frame do not agree with one another and that about a frame that agrees with nothing is not a decision and is not a refusal and is a board.

The sheet that went up on the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth asks for the name of one person to stand against the roads of nine counties and says in two sentences that the place nine days ride off is being held so that what is called the Sundering is not done again in this world. It is true in what it says and it is false in what it assumes, and about four people in nine counties have said the best offer anybody in this county has ever been made out loud once in four trades to about nine people each, and about five had not worked it out at all, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have heard none of it.

The roll has been on those boards one hundred and eighty-five days and its nine lines are empty on all three copies and the fourth of the nine is this county's, and it is not filled in and it is not torn up and it has not been taken down and about four hundred people have walked past it in six months. Inside the covering paper folded over the back of the second copy there is a sheet of paper folded in four with one sentence on it in a hand that is not the hand of any clerk this county has ever had, and it says that nobody in this county is paid to keep a road, and that a form a county cannot fill is not to be filled by writing somebody else's name on it, and that a county which will not govern ought to say so in writing to the people it is not governing. Nobody in nine counties has said who wrote it and nobody is going to be asked and nobody has offered to say.

Then at about the tenth hour of that Wednesday, at the low end of that market street, the nine lines on that flap of a stall were looked at by about nine people again, and about nine people said what their own trade was again, and about nine of them answered inside nine seconds again, and not one of the nine trades on those nine lines has been asked about by anybody in nine counties and not one of the nine people whose trade it is has been asked whether it is there. About four people in nine counties have said since that a line with a trade on it is a line somebody can be argued with and about five have said that the argument can be had only by somebody who knows the trade, and about four have said that that is the whole of the finding and that the whole of the finding is worth about nine seconds and that about nine seconds is not a government.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the Saturday before, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs on a Wednesday and has not come down them on a Wednesday in about four and a half years. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room had the second lamp at the foot of them lit at the fourth hour, and it went out at about the sixth hour, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in six weeks, and no fifth head is ruled, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none in six weeks, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it, and the press stands open with no date on it.

On the sill of that weigh-house door there are four lines of chalk under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and nobody has read the four lines out loud to anybody in six weeks, and the stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked her for it. A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody and is not the record, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine figures in one woman's hand on the back of a book is a thing a clerk will not touch and that about a clerk not touching a thing is not the same as the thing not being there.

At the low end of that market street the office has a counter and a door and a man behind it who was born in that street, and at the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a counter in it and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on every one of the six weeks of this and nobody in nine counties went into it and the drawer in it was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights are still in it, and about nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and about nobody is going to be asked this year.

The man of thirty-two with a stick had come down the four hundred pace lane at about the ninth hour and had stood at those boards and had read the sheet one more time and had touched nothing on that frame and had not gone down to the flap of the stall, and that was the whole of what he came down for and he kept it to about the eleventh hour. He had meant to see whether six weeks of a sheet and nine lines and three things said out loud had produced a name anywhere in this county, and it had not, and about four hundred people in that market town had walked past a notice and three copies of a roll in six months and past five things on that frame in six weeks, and not one of the three questions on that frame has been answered by anybody.

The cost of that Wednesday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and four hundred yards of a made road and had a hot band across the knee by about the seventh hour. And in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is permanent and is the side she spoke from and will always be the side she spoke from. And in ninety-two days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that has had no panel said about it and no explanation and no page of its own, and about which a figure in one woman's hand on the back of a day-book is not a cure and neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet and neither is four lines of chalk on a sill.

The ordinary part of that Wednesday is that about nine people each put a thing down and pick it up again. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at about the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes by the clock and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped. The inn at the high end had two of its four beds full and kept its tally-stick by the door of it and put no board out on the made street, and about nine people went along that street at about the fourth hour who had not been in it at the ninth.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Wednesday in Goatmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards, and nobody thanked anybody on the seventh of Goatmonth either.

The hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway four days south every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the seventh of Goatmonth is a Wednesday and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and was not rung, and four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never been across in about nine years, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside nine seconds that about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard a sheet or a roll or a sentence on a folded sheet or nine lines on a flap of a stall.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners are nine households, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nine households on that mile are a set of people with a reason of their own about a roll and have not been asked, and five have said that a household is nine households and not a name, and about four have kept saying that a trade on a line is not a name and that a household is not a name and that about this county has three kinds of thing that are not a name and has never had one.

The ninth of the nine stones at the end of that nine-foot line in the grass is about the height of a man's knee and the tops of them are all at the same height. The dead grass in the scar stops at the ninth of them and nobody has been past them, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat beyond them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has looked over them and nobody will, and that nine-foot line of dead grass is four hundred and thirty-two days old and has never been held by anybody at all and is not going to be held by anybody this year.

Then the fourth hour of that Wednesday morning, which is before the light, and the number said out loud on that floor was four and it was said twice by two people who were not him. That is the two hundred and eighty-second morning. The day-book in the room over that weigh-house stands at eleven entries and did not gain one, and the fourth ruled head is still empty and no fifth head is ruled, and nothing about the practice is written down anywhere still, and nothing about nine lines with trades on them is written down anywhere either, and nothing about the sheet at those boards is in any document this county keeps.

He was not thanked for any of it, and four people in nine counties have been saying that a man who ends six weeks with a board he did not write on is a man nobody thanks, and five have said that about the not writing is the whole of what he did. He had come into this county four and a half years ago to stop being asked where a figure came from, and a Crown's cart had brought up the made road a printed sheet that was not the roll and did not go over it, and about four people in nine counties had said out loud in one street that it was the best offer anybody in this county has ever been made, and about nine hundred people had not heard it.
