# Chapter 0822

**Thawmonth the twenty-fourth, YR 324, a Monday and not a market morning, the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the fourth hour on a floor four days off with seven people on it, eighty-eight days after a sheet went up on those boards, twenty-five days past the date at the foot of that sheet, four sheets of foolscap on a board under a sill in four hands, and a man of thirty-two going up the made road at about the sixth hour for four days.**

There were about nine people in that street at the ninth hour and the bolt had been on the room over the weigh-house since noon on the Saturday before, and the roll has been on those boards two hundred and thirty-two days.

Four days off, on a floor above a byre with the cow door open on the cold, seven people stood up and said a number before the light. It was the three hundred and fifteenth morning.

“Twice.”

Four. Four. The man with the bad hip put nothing on a board or a page, because there is nowhere in this county to put it, and the six mornings this county lost to the nine days of water in Mudmonth are six lost mornings and nobody recovers them.

“Six days since the cart came,” the woman at the door said.

“It was a Thursday and the cart is a Thursday cart.”

“That is the same thing twice.”

“It is the same thing twice and neither of them is a reason.”

The number has been four for three hundred and eleven mornings and it was eight before the water, and two of the ten things came back after the water and two have not.

In the room over that weigh-house the woman of about twenty-nine had the second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs lit at the fourth hour and it went out at about the sixth, and nothing about the four lines of chalk on the sill went in with it. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in eighty-eight days. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county. The press stands open with no date on it and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Then, at about the sixth hour, the man of thirty-two with the stick went up that made road with his stick and a crust of bread in a fold of cloth, and the leg took the first four hundred yards badly and then took the rest of them the way it takes everything now. About four people in nine counties knew where he had gone and about five did not, and about four of the seven people on that floor four days off knew he would not be on it that morning and about three did not, and nobody asked him to be on it and nobody asked him where he had gone.

There were four sheets of foolscap on the board under that sill, and they had been on that board since the Thursday before, and about nine people had gone past that room in six days and about four of them had stopped at the door and about five had gone on up to the office for a halfpenny copy of something.

Four sheets is not a parcel and is not a book and is not about nine lines on the flap of a stall, and a woman who keeps records for fourpence a year has about four and a half years of what people bring her, and she knows the weight of a thing by how it is held rather than by what is on it. This one had been carried flat and had not been folded, and it had been carried by a man who knew not to fold it, and that told her more about the last hand to touch it than any of the four hands on it.

Each of the four was in a different hand. The first was a narrow upright hand that got its downstrokes thin. The second was the hand of a person who writes fast. The third had four words on a line and a good deal of white between them. The fourth was written low on the page, close to the edge, as though the writer had been holding it in the other hand.

Each of the four carried a column of nine roads down the left of it, and beside each of those nine, in the middle of the page, one fixed point out of that road's own past, being a season and a year, and in one case an hour, and in one case nothing at all.

That was all they said, on the first of them and on the second and on the third. On the fourth the nine roads were in a column and beside each of the nine there was one fixed point, and the fixed points on the fourth were not all in the same hand as the column, and about four people in this county would have noticed that and about five would not.

As to who wrote any of the four of them, or when, or for whom, or for what, nobody in nine counties knows and nobody in nine counties has asked, and about four people in this county have said that four documents nobody can place are a thing this county has got quite good at. Nothing on the four of them says where anything is shut. Nothing on the four of them asks anybody for anything. Nothing on the four of them carries a seal, and there is no seal in this county to put on one.

Tosh Sennen was in that room at about the ninth hour on the Monday with his hat in his hand, because a man who copies documents for people who cannot pay a clerk waits to be told what a set of them is, and she asked him one question about his own trade, in her own trade, asked to a person who does that for a living.

“**Nine.**”

He said it straight away and he did not put it any way at all. It was one word and it stood in that room for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about four people on that floor heard it and about five of them were looking at the four sheets on the board and not at him.

Then she told him, once, in her own trade, and this is what she said and it was twenty words and about nine people on that floor heard all of it.

“**Read back is the half of the trade that finds out whether the paper is what was put on it.**”

He said that he had known that, and that about nine hundred people had not known it and had not asked him, and that he had begun to think that a man who reads back is a man doing people a favour, and that it is not a favour and that it is the only part of it anybody can be held to.

The copyist of about twenty-six was in that room for the whole of it with the day-book under her arm and a piece of chalk in her hand that was not for the sill. She was not told about any of it and she was not asked about any of it. She ruled no head and nobody asked her to rule one, and the fourth ruled head of that page is still empty and it has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and the copyist of about twenty-six has been in this county about four and a half years and has ruled nothing on that page since the first of them.

Then the man of about forty-four put his hat on and went back down eleven miles of made road in the ordinary post with nothing in his hands, and he had allowed himself a fortnight and used six days of it. About nine people watched him go and about four of them thought that a man who gives a thing up and waits to be asked to come back is a man who has done that before, and about five thought nothing about it at all.

She said by the second hour of that Monday that she would not know what was on the four of them until she had them a day, and that a day is a day, and that about nine counties have never once been asked to wait a day for anything anybody wrote.

On the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, there are four lines of chalk. They mean four. Nobody has added to them since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nobody has read them out loud to anybody in about four and a half years, and about nine people went past that door on the twenty-fourth of Thawmonth and about four of them looked down.

At the front of the Crown's road office at the low end of that market street there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and about nine things were done one after another on that counter during that Monday and nothing was written on any of them, and nobody in nine counties has asked him what a gate is for.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Monday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer in it was not opened once. The county's one seal is in there still with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody has asked what a seal is for.

A counter and a door and a man who was born in that street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has, being that it needs nobody to travel and nobody to be paid and nobody to agree, and about four people in nine counties have said that this year and about five have said that the cheapest instrument is also the one nobody in nine counties has looked at, and neither of those is on any paper in this county and neither of them has been said out loud in that street.

Four days out, a hundred and two drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and the hundred and second went up on the Thursday before at about the sixth hour, which was the twentieth of Thawmonth, The man of thirty-two went up that same made road at about the sixth hour on the Monday and was four days off it and came back down it on the Saturday, and the drove and the man went up the same road within about two days of one another and neither of them knew the other was on it.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-fourth of Thawmonth is a Monday, and the lamp was put there that afternoon. The coping is about nine feet wide. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

The rest of that Monday went on. There were about nine people in that street and no more than about nine, and about four of them went into that office for a halfpenny copy of something and about five did not, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not been inside that office in about four and a half years. The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes without anybody stopping on his account. The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped about as long after the light had gone as the light had taken to go.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was not lit, because the ninth hour of a Monday in Thawmonth is dark and there is nothing off the wet slate of them to need it. Five of those nine people went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap in it is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and twelve days, and the ninth yard has not been started. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and the stone for it is lying in a line on the bank with the mallet on top of it, and the bar of ash across the gate at the Low Gate has been where it is for about eleven years and nobody lifted it and nobody tried it.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and the yard wall is about nine feet high, and no sixth has gone in, and the young man of about nineteen has said out loud three things in that street in about four and a half years and nobody has asked him for an account of any of them.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and thirty-one days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and twelve days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and seventy-nine days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a bench with a cloth over it, and the cloth has been there all winter and about four people in nine counties have stopped seeing it, and nobody lifted it that day and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room over the weigh-house is face down and was not turned over. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Monday was in the leg, which had four days of a made road in front of it and about eleven miles of it, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and thirty-nine days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that has never once been shown to anybody. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and thirty-nine days.

**And the cost of four sheets of foolscap lying on a board under a sill for six days is a man of forty-four who has read back nine of about nine hundred documents and has begun to think that reading back is a favour he does people, four hands and four columns of nine roads with one fixed point beside each and nobody in nine counties able to say who wrote any of them or when or for whom, a man of thirty-two gone up a made road for four days with nobody in this county asking him to stay or asking him where he had gone, three mornings of a practice run four days off with him not on that floor and the sixth morning still lost to the water in Mudmonth, twenty-five days past a date that has shut nothing, two hundred and thirty-two days of a roll on that frame with the fourth line empty on all three of its copies, three hundred and fifty-three days past a printed notice that has shut nothing, a low room shut behind its own door with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, one hundred and thirty-nine days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and thirty-one days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
