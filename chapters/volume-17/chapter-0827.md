# Chapter 0827

**Mudmonth the eighteenth, YR 324, a Thursday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting, the ninth hour with about four hundred people in that street, a hundred and twelve days after a sheet went up on those boards, forty-nine days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a second person standing in the doorway of the low room at the back of that office and not going in.**

About four hundred people were in that street by the ninth hour on that Thursday and the five things were up on the frame at the low end of it, and the bolt had come off the room over the weigh-house at first light, and the copyist of about twenty-six was up those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm.

The roll has been on that frame two hundred and fifty-six days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. The notice above them is three hundred and seventy-seven days past its own date and has shut nothing. The date at the foot of that sheet went past forty-nine days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it, and forty-nine days is seven weeks and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not heard of it.

Four days out, a hundred and six drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and a hundred and six went up that same Thursday at about the sixth hour and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour, and two men went with it. Nobody in that street has ever watched one go up and about four hundred people in nine counties have never watched one go up.

Then, at about the tenth hour, at the low end of that market street, a man of about forty-two stood in the doorway of the low room at the back of the Crown's road office and did not go in.

He keeps a smithy at the high end of that street and has kept it about nine years, and about four hundred people in nine counties have had a shoe mended at it and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road have not. He had bought a halfpenny copy of the printed notice off the man behind the counter twice since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth and had come in on that Thursday for the ordinary reason.

About nine people stood in that street at that hour and about four of them saw him stand in that doorway and about five were buying string.

The doorway of that low room is nine inches narrower than the front door of that office and it is behind the counter and behind the man who was born in that street, and a person standing in it is standing in the office and not in the room. He stood in it for about as long as it takes to read the sheet at the low end of that street twice.

He did not go in. He did not touch the door and did not knock and did not sit down on the clerk's table that is four feet inside it, and the man behind the counter did not ask him for a halfpenny and did not ask him anything at all.

At about the eleventh hour he went away. The door of that low room was shut behind him and it had been shut behind its own door on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and there was nobody following him, and nobody asked him why he had come.

About nine people in that street were not buying anything at that hour and not one of them thought about what he did, and the whole of that street knows what he did not do, which was to go in. A man who has been in a street about nine years and has bought a sheet off that counter twice knows there is a room behind that door, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a smithy keeper who stands in a doorway for the length of a reading and then goes away is the cheapest kind of person there is, being that he costs nothing and agrees to nothing and has been in that street since before the office was one.

The protagonist was not in that doorway and was not at that door and was about four hundred paces away at the frame at the low end of that street at that hour, with the market going round him, and he saw him stand there and did not go and look at the doorway afterwards and nobody asked him about it.

Then the man behind that counter said it once, out loud, in his own trade, and it was sixty words, and about four people in that street heard it and about five did not.

“**That door is nine inches narrower than this one, and she is the second person this year to stand in it and not come in, and I have not asked either of them why and neither of them has told me, and the key to the drawer in that room is in this pocket and I have not used it.**”

About four people heard that and about five did not, and nobody said anything back to him for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and about four people in that street have worked out that he said the wrong word and have not said anything about it.

At the back of that office there is a low room with a door in front of it and a clerk's table and a press with no date on it and a drawer eleven inches deep. That room was shut behind its own door on that Thursday as it has been on every morning since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nobody in nine counties went into it, and the drawer in it was not opened once in a hundred and twelve days. The county's one seal is still in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for and nobody is going to be asked this year.

A smithy at the high end of that market street has been in it about nine years and has been to that office twice at a halfpenny a copy, and a man who has been in a street that long has seen about four hundred people go into that office and about four hundred people come out of it and has counted about nine people who went further in than the counter, and about five of those nine went in the first year and about four have not been back.

A counter and a door and a man who was born in that street is the cheapest instrument an occupation has, being that it needs nobody to travel and nobody to be paid and nobody to agree. About four people in nine counties have said that again since and about five have said that the cheapest instrument is the one nobody in nine counties has looked at, and about four have said that two people have now stood in that doorway and not gone in, which is a finding and not a count.

Four lines of chalk lie on the stone sill of that weigh-house door, under the hood where the rain does not reach, and they mean four, and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning without looking down at it, and nobody has read them out loud to anybody in about four and a half years.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for seven hundred and thirty-six days, and the ninth yard has not been started, and the mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up. The third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid and about nine feet of it lies in a line on that bank.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the eighteenth of Mudmonth is a Thursday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off the causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about four hundred people live on the far bank of that water and have never once been across in about nine years.

At the head of that cart track nine hurdles are still across it foot to foot and the thumb of lime on the shoe of the top one has gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track four hundred and fifty-five days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut four hundred and thirty-six days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and nobody lifted a hurdle and nobody tried a bar.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for four hundred and three days, and the dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody.

The five frames of brood in that walled yard are under about nine straw skirts and the yard wall is about nine feet high and no sixth has gone in, and about nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide and will not close over, and about four things in one county are about nine feet across and about nine people in nine counties have asked why and about four have answered.

The badge-man of about fifty-eight had come into that office and gone out of it again twice since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth without stopping at the counter, and nobody in nine counties has asked him which he is, being a clerk or an occupier, and a badge-man who comes and goes out of an office is not an occupier and is not a clerk, and that is two things to be instead of one. About four people in that street have worked out what a door and a counter do without a badge, and about five have not.

The rest of that Thursday went on. A near wheel came off a cart at the low end of that street and the carter had it up again with a bar in about nine minutes and nobody stopped on his account, and the two women at the third door were still at their bolt of cloth in the doorway an hour later, counting the threads at the selvedge against each other, and the inn at the high end had four beds full and its board out on the made street from first light.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Thursday in Mudmonth is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people went out of that street into about five trades and about four stayed at the boards.

The four copied pages are on the top shelf of the press in the room over that weigh-house, above the half leaf, which is face down, and they are in that room and not in his keeping, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and twelve days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county.

Nine doors down the cloth was over the bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked what was under it. The half leaf in the press in that room is face down and was not turned over on the day a man stood in a doorway. The three series in that room are three series and none of them is derived from another and the band figure is not written down anywhere in this county.

The cost of that Thursday was in the leg, which took four hundred paces of a lane and about four hundred yards of a made road to reach a frame and stand at it for about two hours and had a hot band across the knee by about the tenth hour, and in the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone, and which is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed one hundred and sixty-three days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person, and that a man standing in a doorway does not see and does not touch. Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Neither is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Neither is a sentence in an unclaimed hand on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll. Neither is a bleed that has not faded in one hundred and sixty-three days.

He went out of that market street at about the eleventh hour with the leg grinding and did not go up the made road past the ninth mile stone, and did not go in at the low end of that street, and the low room at the back of that office stayed shut behind its own door. Five things were on that board and forty-nine days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of this is a man of forty-two with a smithy at the high end of that street standing in the doorway of a low room at the low end of it for as long as it takes to read a sheet twice and not going in and going away and not being followed and not being asked, sixty words said once out loud by a man who was born in that street about a door nine inches narrower than his own and a key in a pocket that has not been used, a low room shut behind its own door on every one of a hundred and twelve mornings with a drawer eleven inches deep in it that was not opened once and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for, forty-nine days past a date that has shut nothing, two hundred and fifty-six days of a roll on that frame with the fourth line empty on all three of its copies, three hundred and seventy-seven days past a printed notice that has shut nothing, a hundred and six drove going up a lane nobody in nine counties has ever watched go up, one hundred and sixty-three days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet four hundred and fifty-five days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
