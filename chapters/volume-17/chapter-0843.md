**Longlight the twenty-third, YR 324, a Saturday and a market morning, the room over the weigh-house open and the hearing not sitting and about four hundred people in that street, one hundred and seventy-seven days after a sheet went up on those boards, one hundred and fourteen days past the date at the foot of that sheet, and a man of thirty-two told about a sheet that had been read out in a room on the Monday, after it was read out and not before.**

He came back up that made road on the twenty-first of Longlight and had been two days in that market town with the leg grinding before anybody said one word to him about the room at the high end of the street. He was told at about the eighth hour by the copyist of about twenty-six, who came down those thirteen stairs with the register under her arm and said it in her own trade and then went back up them without waiting to be asked anything.

“**A sheet came up on the Thursday in a Crown's cart and two of us opened the satchels on the Monday morning on the counter, and a carter read it out once in a hired room at the high end of the street, and about nine of us were in that room and about four of us heard the whole of it and about five did not, and nobody asked him to read it again and nobody has written any of it down.**”

It was eighty-three words. About four people near the foot of those stairs heard all of them and about five heard the middle of them, and nobody in that market street stopped buying string, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of that made road did not know on that Saturday that a sheet had been read out about nine days earlier.

She did not tell him what was on it. He had not asked her, and about four people in that street worked that out inside about nine seconds, and about five have gone on working it out since, and the reason she gave nothing else is that she keeps records and not opinions and a record of what was read out in a hired room at the high end of a market street is not a record of that room.

A place nine days ride from any town in this county is still held, and the gate at the top of about nine hundred yards of a stair is held, and the people on it are not leaving. A place that is still held is not a victory and a gate that is still held is not a defeat. About four people in nine counties have said that both of those sentences are true at the same time about the same gate, and about five have said that a county which waits to find out which of the two it is has not begun.

The roll has been on that frame three hundred and twenty-one days with nine empty lines on each of three copies and the fourth of the nine this county's. There is nothing written on any of those nine lines on any of those three copies and there has been no name against any road on any paper in nine counties. The notice nailed above those three copies is four hundred and forty-two days past its own date and has shut nothing.

The date at the foot of the sheet that was nailed up beside the roll and not over it went past one hundred and fourteen days ago and nothing in nine counties is shut on it. The count of things on that frame is five and the count of questions on it is three, and the three questions have not agreed with one another since the twenty-sixth of Frostmonth, and nothing has been nailed over anything and nothing has been taken down.

This county's copy of the hand paper has been on the flap of that stall for a hundred and forty-two days with nine lines on it and a trade on each and no name on any and no seal on it. About four hundred places in nine counties are writing the same paper out by hand and not one of the four hundred has ever met another of them, and about four hundred places have been doing it for about four months.

He could not do the sum of those two figures and had given up trying to, which was new, and about four people in nine counties have said that a man who gives up a sum is a man who has understood that it is not his. Nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road have not heard either of the two things, and about four hundred people in nine counties have been reading about both of them for about four months, and the two numbers are the same size by about nine and they are not about the same people at all.

At the front of that office, which is about nine feet from that frame with the five things on it, there is a counter and behind it there is a man who was born in that street, and about four hundred people went in and out of that office on that Saturday for a halfpenny copy of something. About nine went further in than the counter and about five of those nine were in there for the ordinary reason.

At the back of that office the low room was shut behind its own door, as it has been on every morning since the seventh of Longlight, and nobody stands in that doorway and it does not stand open on that Saturday. The drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it was not opened once in a hundred and seventy-seven days. The county's one seal is in there with its ribbon round it and a set of standard weights beside it, and nobody in nine counties has asked what a seal is for.

Four days out, a hundred and fifteen drove have gone up that lane since the second of Mudmonth YR 322, and the hundred and fifteenth went up on Longlight the twenty-first, which was the Thursday, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field inside the stone wall and came back down at about the fifth hour. Two men went with it, and nobody in nine counties has ever watched one go up.

One man has been carrying that stone about two miles in his own hands for eight hundred and one days, and the gap in that wall is about nine feet long and about four feet high, and the ninth yard has not been started and does not start. The mallet is on the bank about two miles off with the line of stone and it was not picked up, and the third course of sill at the culvert at this town's own gate is unlaid.

Nine hurdles stand across the head of that cart track foot to foot, and a hurdle shoe is on the top one of them with the thumb of lime on that shoe gone to powder. The last cart of lime came up that track five hundred and twenty days ago and the road behind the hurdles has been shut five hundred and one days, and nobody has been up that track since the second of Goatmonth, and no hurdle has been lifted and no bar has been tried.

At the causeway four days south the hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter, and the twenty-third of Longlight is a Saturday, and the lamp was not put there. The water was off that causeway and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is the size of a fist and was not rung, and about nine hundred yards of kerb has gone down on that far bank since the first of Mudmonth and nobody on this side has asked why.

Behind the ninth of the nine stones there are about nine hundred yards of grass nine feet wide that has been dead inside its own width for five hundred and sixty-eight days. The dead grass stops at the ninth of them, and nobody has ever been past the ninth of them and the far side of them has never been shown to anybody, and that omission is a decision and not an oversight.

Five frames of brood stand in that walled yard under about nine straw skirts and no sixth has gone in. About nine hundred yards of a standing broom on that east rise lies about nine feet wide with the mat of it thicker than it was a month ago. Four things in this county are about nine feet across and none of the four has been asked about, and one of the four stops at the ninth of nine stones and is not looked over.

The second lamp at the foot of those thirteen stairs was unlit that morning, because the room it belongs to was open and it is lit on the five mornings a week that the market does not come. The register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gained none in a hundred and seventy-seven days, and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none.

The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in this county, and the copyist of about twenty-six ruled no head and has been told about none of it. The press stands open with no date on it and the four copied pages are on its top shelf above the half leaf, which is face down, and a leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody.

Under the hood where the rain does not reach there are four lines of chalk on the stone sill of that weigh-house door. They mean four and they have been dry and sharp there since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth, and about four hundred people went past that door that morning and about four of them looked down. The stick of chalk is in a press drawer upstairs and the man of about fifty-four has not asked for it.

The rest of that market morning went on the way a market morning goes on, and almost everything that was said in that street that Saturday had nothing to do with a stair about nine hundred yards long.

He had wanted to be in that room and had not been, and what he had wanted was not a thing he could have said out loud in a market street with about four hundred people in it, being that he wanted to have been one of the about nine who heard all of it.

He had thought about that on the Thursday and had worked out that the wanting was about himself and not about the stair. About four people in nine counties have said that the two are hard to tell apart in a person who spends his mornings saying a number twice. A man of about forty-five set a trestle against the front of the second stall at about the tenth hour and put nine weights on it in a row and took them off again and put them back four inches along, and he did that on every market morning and had done it for about nine years and nobody in nine counties has asked him why.

The two women at the third door took a bolt of cloth apart in the doorway over about four minutes by the clock and went in after it and came out with it wrapped, and one of them was carrying a second parcel she had not gone in for. The inn at the high end had four beds full and kept its board out on the made street from first light, and a man of about thirty-eight stood at that board for about a quarter of an hour and said nothing to anybody at all.

The light went off the west bank about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over and found the upper edge of those boards, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit, because the ninth hour of a Saturday in Longlight is dark and there is wet slate on that frame to need it. Five of about nine people at the low end of that street went out into about five trades and about four stayed on at the boards.

Nine doors down from those boards there was a cloth over a bench and nobody lifted it and nobody asked after it. The half leaf in that press is face down and was not turned over on the morning he was told. Three series stand in that room and none of the three is worked out from another, and the band figure appears nowhere in this county.

The cost of that Saturday was the leg, which took four days of a made road in five days and went from an ache to a needle on the last mile, and the ear on the side the stick is on, which is gone and is the side she spoke from.

And there was, on the inside of one wrist, a bleed two hundred and twenty-eight days old that had not faded by anything he had done to it, that nobody in nine counties has asked him about, that is in no document this county keeps, and that has never been said out loud in a room in front of one other person. A sheet about a place nine days off does not reach it, and nobody in nine counties knows that he was told about that sheet five days after it was read.

Four lines on a sill are not a cure for it. Nor is a page in a woman's hand on the back of a day-book. Nor is the sentence on the folded sheet inside the covering paper of the second copy of that roll, which is in a hand nobody has claimed. Nor is a page that has been authenticated, which is not consent, and not a place that is still held, which is not a victory, and not a damaged engine, which is not a rescue. Nor is a bleed that has not faded in two hundred and twenty-eight days.

He did not ask her one thing about that sheet and he has not asked her one thing about it since, and about four people in that market street have said that a man who does not ask is not the same as a man who does not care, and about five have said that there is no way at all to tell the two apart from outside. Five things were on that board and one hundred and fourteen days past the date and the fourth line of the roll empty on all three of its copies, and nobody was thanked for any of it.

**And the cost of these six weeks is a sheet about a stair nine days off coming up four miles of made road in the ordinary post and being read out once in a room at the high end of that market street by a carter who was waiting on a load, and a man of thirty-two learning about it five days afterward from a woman who keeps records and was not asked for an opinion, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of made road who have not heard any of it. The drawer eleven inches deep not opened once in a hundred and seventy-seven days and the county's one seal and its ribbon and a set of standard weights still in it and nobody asked what a seal is for. Nine trades on nine lines of a paper nobody can seal and no names on any of them. Two hundred and twenty-eight days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that has not faded by anything anybody has done to it and that nobody in nine counties knows about, one thing gone quiet five hundred and twenty days ago and alive and shut and unnamed and unheld, about nine hundred yards of grass four days out that will not come back, the use of one ear, the use of one leg, and a year, none of which is in any of it and none of which has a price.**
