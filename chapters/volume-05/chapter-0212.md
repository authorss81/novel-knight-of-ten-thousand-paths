# Chapter 0212

**The second day, and the market, and the hour the market opens, which is not an hour anybody in this city is keeping and is the same four days out of seven, and about four hundred people in a street of about two hundred yards with glass on one side of it and reed and salt fish on the other, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick standing in it at the third hour with a cup he has been given and has stopped listening in the middle of.**

There is no market day here. That was the first thing a man who had come up out of a valley that keeps no months and no hours found out about this place, and it took him two days, and on the second day he stood in the middle of a street with about four hundred people in it and worked out, standing up, in the time it took a man to look twice, that this street is busy four days out of seven and that he had come down the hill on one of the four, and that nobody in it thought the word market meant anything in particular.

**And the market is the trade of this place, and the trade is not selling. What is sold on that street is bread at the price of a market at home and glass at a price that has not moved in four hundred years and salt fish, reed, rope, oil, and a quantity of small things that a man who has been in eleven markets in four counties could not name. What is not sold and what the street is for is that about four hundred people know each other.**

A woman of about thirty at a stall with reed in it said the thing out loud, once, to him, in about eleven words, and a few of the people on that street have said since that she is the only person in that market who has ever said it to a stranger and that she said it because he had asked her what the market was for, which is a question nobody asks.

“**You are going to try to remember us,**” she said. “**And if you do you have got a place, and if you do not you have got a day, and I would like you to have the day.**”

**And the reason, which is not the same as the trade, is that a name in this city is a person somebody can be shown. A name in a book is a person somebody can be shown. A name in about four hundred heads is not, because four hundred heads are in this city and are going to be in this city next year. That is the difference and I am not going to say it again and I have said it to two men in four hundred years and one of them was you.**”

And she went back to her reed, and four people on that street have said since that a woman who gives a stranger that in eleven words and then says she will not say it again is doing the ordinary trade of that street, which is that everybody in it is extremely careful with a stranger and nobody in it is unkind to one.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in that street for an hour and got described four times, and none of the four was hostile and three of the four were wrong, and he did not correct one of them, and a few of the people on that street have said since that the not correcting is the only part of that day he has not gone over in four days.**

The first was at the fourth hour of the morning. A woman of about fifty with a pail said, to a woman of about thirty with a pail, and not to him, in the middle of a stall, that there was a man on the glass side with a stick and that he was off a road.

“Off a road,” said the second woman. “How do you know that.”

“**By the stick. Nobody lives here and carries a stick like that and I have been in this market thirty-one years and I have not seen one in the street twice.**”

The third was a boy of about fourteen selling nothing and standing beside a man who was selling something, and he said it out loud to nobody in particular, and four people heard him, which is a thing that happens on that street and is not rudeness and is not gossip and is a man of that age saying a thing he has just worked out. He said that the man with the stick was in a place where there were no wheels that made a noise, because he had watched him walk forty yards and had counted that his feet went down before his body did, and he said that a man walks like that when he has been carrying something heavy a long way, and a few people on that street have said since that a boy of fourteen who has watched a stranger walk forty yards and worked out a thing about his feet has told the truth, and that four of them have said the truth was not the interesting part.

**And the fourth was the one that was wrong, and it was wrong in one detail, and the detail was that the man with the stick was going somewhere, and the man of thirty-two with the limp stood in the middle of that street and let it stand, because it was the fourth of four and the first three had been about where he had come from and this one was where he was going, and a man who has been a question in a market for an hour does not open his mouth.**

He had been a question for an hour and a bit, and the hour and a bit had been the fourth hour and the fifth, and in that hour and a bit four people had worked out four things about him, and three of the four were the truth and the fourth was a mistake about what was going to happen to him next, and a man of thirty-two with a limp stood there and thought about the fourth one for a long time afterwards, and a few people in that city have said since that the mistake was the ordinary one, that a man standing in a market for an hour is assumed to be going somewhere, and that the man with the stick was in fact going to stand in that street for nine more days, and that four people there got that wrong on the second day and did not get it corrected until the eleventh, and that nobody corrected it on purpose.

Nobody wrote any of it down, and that is the thing about the second day. A few of the people on that street have said since that they expect a stranger to produce a paper every time one comes down that road, and that the man with the stick never asked anybody to write anything and never said his own name to anybody, and that those two things together are the reason nobody on that street can say where he is, and that a man who wants to be found in this city has to want it very much and say his name out loud in a market, and that this one did neither, and that four people there have been arguing about that for two days and have not settled it.

---

**And there is a man at the corner of that street with nothing in front of him, and he is the trade, and he has been the trade for eleven years, and his price is a penny a name and he will say anybody's name in that street to anybody who pays him and he has never once been asked to do it by anybody who is not from this city.**

He is a man of about thirty and he does not look up much and he has a way of standing with his hands behind him that puts a person who wants to talk to him in the position of a person who is wasting his morning. A man of about forty with a bad thumb came and stood next to him for two minutes and then went away, and the man of thirty did not say anything to him and did not look at him, and a few of the people on that street have said since that the man of thirty knew the man of forty and that the man of forty had come to find out about somebody and had worked out inside two minutes that the man of thirty was not going to be the one to find out for him.

“A penny,” he said, to a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick, when he was asked what it cost. “A penny a name and you may have as many as you like and I have never been asked for one by anybody who is not standing in this street on a working day. **You are not standing in this street on a working day. You are standing in it on a day you have brought with you, and I do not know where it came from and I do not want to, and there is a difference and it is the only one I know.**”

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp did not buy a name, and a few of the people on that street worked that out inside a minute and a half and have said since that it was the first thing a stranger had ever refused in that market, and that nine of them have said they would have bought one, and that the man of thirty did not appear to have heard any of it and has been asked since and says that it is not a thing he is paid to have heard.**

---

**And he bought two things and paid what he would have paid at home, and the two were a pound of bread and a hand's breadth of glass rod, and the glass rod was for no purpose at all and he has never said so to anybody, and a man of about forty at the glass stall asked him what he wanted it for and he said he did not know and the man of forty said that was the best answer he had had in four years and cut him a piece and did not weigh it against anything.**

A pound of bread, a penny. Four feet of glass rod, twopence. A cup of something hot and a piece of salt fish, a penny. One and two is three and three and one is four pence.

**And that is all he spent in the second day, and a man who has kept a waggon-book for most of his working life, four hundred miles off, in a county that keeps two market days a week, has said that four pence is a man being fed and put somewhere for a night in a country where things are dear, and that in this country it is a man being allowed to be there and the difference between the two prices is what a stranger is charged, and that the man of thirty did not charge him anything at all, and that the man of thirty's trade is that a name is a penny and a person is not for sale and those are two different markets on the same street.**

**And he had a cup in his hand from about the fourth hour, and a man of about thirty at a stall put it there and said his name and told him where he was sleeping and asked him how long he meant to stay, and a man of thirty-two with three open paths behind him and a limp and a stick in a city that has a count of days and no months did not lose the thread of that conversation for about two thirds of it, and then lost the rest of it, and about four people on that street have said since that a man who goes quiet in the middle of a thing he is in the middle of is a man who is a long way from somewhere, and that nobody in that market has ever said that to a man of thirty-two with a stick, and that four hundred people watched it happen and did not say a word.**

He did not say he had lost the thread. He has four things open behind him and a leg that is the same leg it was in the spring, and a hand that is warm at an hour that does not exist here, and he has never once said out loud to anybody what it is that he cannot hold, and four people in four counties have noticed and none of them has asked, and this is the first country he has been in where the not asking is a trade.

**And he finished the conversation by asking a question, and the question was a good one and it was the right question and he had it ready, and he had it ready because he had been working on it since the second hour of the previous day, and he got it out, and the man of thirty answered it in one breath, and he was a third of the way through working out what to ask next when he realised he had been holding the same cup for eleven minutes and had not taken a drink out of it.**

---

**And a man of about fifty came down that street at about the sixth hour with a waggon behind him and got described in the time it takes to set a basket down by three people, and the man of fifty did not mind, and a few of the people on that street have said since that the difference between the man with the stick and the man with the waggon was a handful of words of talking, and that the man with the waggon said his name twice in a quarter of a minute and where he had come from once, and that after that nobody on that street said anything about him again, and that both of those men stood in the same street within a quarter of an hour of each other and one of them is not a thing anybody can carry out of here and the other one is.**

He watched the waggon go. It went up the lane toward the works and it did not stop at the gate and nobody at the gate looked at it, and a boy of about ten was holding the mule and the boy was talking about nothing at all, and the man of fifty was gone in about four minutes.

---

**And then the man of fifty came back down the lane on foot at about the seventh hour, and stopped, and looked at the man with the stick, and asked him whether he could carry a hod, and a man of thirty-two who has carried a barrow up onto a bridge deck five months ago said yes without a word, and they went up the lane four hundred yards and into a yard and the man of fifty put a hod in his hands and he carried it for an hour and a half, and it was limestone, and the work was ordinary, and after nine minutes he was faster than the man of thirty-four on the other side of the yard, and the man of thirty-four said so, out loud, twice.**

Then the man of fifty stopped him in the yard and paid him, and the pay was a loaf and a piece of salt fish and nothing else, and a man of thirty-two with a limp looked at it in his hand and the man of fifty watched him look at it.

“**That is the trade,**” said the man of fifty. “**I have had a man off a road in this yard twice since you can remember and both of them I fed and neither of them I paid, and I would like you to have the first hour of knowing why, because you have got the face of a man about to be insulted and you are not going to be.**”

“Then I will not take the bread,” said the man of thirty-two.

“**You will take the bread,**” said the man of fifty, “**because refusing it makes you a story, and a story in this city is a man somebody can be shown, and you are the last man on that lane who wants to be one. A man who is fed is a man who was here. That is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement since before either of us.**”

He took the bread. He ate it sitting on an upturned pail in the yard of a works with a furnace in it that had not gone out in four hundred years, and about four of the men in that yard looked at him and then at the stick and then away, and one of them, a man of about twenty-six with lime on his hands, said the only thing anybody said to him in that yard.

“You are not from the works.”

“No,” said the man of thirty-two.

“**Then do not stand in the way of the second hod, we are short of men today and you are not one of them and you are not going to be,**” said the man of twenty-six, and he was right, and the man of thirty-two moved, and about four of the men in that yard have said since that a stranger who says nothing when he is told he is in the way is a stranger who has been in a yard before.

---

**And he started counting the days on the second night, in a notebook he had been carrying for four years and had not written in since a lane in a county four hundred miles off, and the count was his own and the city had no number for him and the three places the count is on a wall in were written in a hand he could not read from where he was standing, and so a man of thirty-two kept a count of days in a city that keeps a count of days, and it was the first thing he had written down in about eleven weeks that was not a figure of work, and a few people in that city have said since that the stranger with the stick keeps a book, and that a man who keeps a book in a city that does not is the only person in it doing anything out of the ordinary, and that nobody has asked him what is in it and that this is the single most correct thing anybody in that city did all week.**

**And the market went down over about an hour after that the way it had come up, and the reed and the salt fish went in under a cover and the glass rods went up in a handbarrow and the street was swept by two men who are on that street every day of the count, and about four hundred people went off up four lanes in about nine minutes, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick stood in the middle of an empty street holding a cup that was not his and a piece of glass rod he had no use for and knew before he had bought it that he would still have in a month.**
