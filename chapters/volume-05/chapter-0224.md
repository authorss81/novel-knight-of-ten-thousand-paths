# Chapter 0224

**The twenty-fourth day of a count of days that this city keeps, and the second hour and after, and a lane that goes out of the city at the west end and a barrier of three bars at the road end of it and a hut with a man of fifty-five in it who has been in that hut nineteen years, and a man of about forty-six with a satchel and a printed sheet in his hand walking up to it, and the sheet has four figures on it and a place name in the object column and nothing anywhere on it that is a person's name, and a few hundreds of people in this city do not know that any of that is true, and a few of them are two hundred yards off and one of the four is on a ladder and one of the four is in a bindle yard and neither of those two has been told.**

The man of fifty-five had turned back a few hundreds of people and had not once had to raise his voice, and a few of the people in that hut have said since that he did not raise his voice on the twenty-fourth day either and that the four words he said were the same four words he has said since he came into that hut, and that they are not the four words anybody expects at a gate, and that a few hundreds of people have been turned round at that gate in nineteen years and that not one of the four hundred has ever been told what the words are for.

“Then you turn round.”

**And the man of forty-six turned round, and he turned round as fast as a man turns round who has not been asked twice, and he had not been asked twice, and nobody in that hut has ever worked out why a man with a sheet of paper in his hand turned round at a gate in a city he had been in for four days, and nobody in that hut is going to ask him, because a gate does not ask, and a man of fifty-five who has been in a hut for nineteen years has never wanted to know and has never once been told what it is he is for.**

The man of forty-six went up four hundred yards of lane past the works and round by a bindle store and into a yard with a roof on two sides and no roof on the other two, and he came in with a satchel and a face on him, and the face was not anger and was arithmetic, and a man of forty-six who has left about three thousand forms at about three thousand doors in eight years and has been turned round at a gate once in this city and has come back up a lane to a yard with a bundle of cord on his back in about a quarter of an hour has worked out inside a quarter of an hour that the three bars outside are for bundles and not for men, and he is right, and the reason he is right is that nine people a day go through those bars with a bundle and a few hundreds of people have been turned round at them in nineteen years.

**And he did not ask the man of sixty-six anything, and a man of forty-six came into a yard and did not ask a man of sixty-six a single question, and the reason is that the man of forty-six had a sheet in his hand that was wrong for a gate, and the only way to make it right was to stop it being a sheet, and stopping it being a sheet was not a question and was not a favour and was nothing that anybody in that yard had to agree to.**

A bale of cord and reed is about four feet long and there are nine of them in a yard at any hour of a working day and they go out on a barrow and come in on a barrow and they are not numbered and they are not weighed and a few of the people in that yard have said since that a thing this city cannot name is a bundle, and a bundle is a bundle because nobody has ever worked out what is in it, and that is the whole of the trade of a bindle yard, and it has been all of it for four hundred years.

He put the sheet flat inside one and corded it over with about four turns and the cord went through the loops that are already in it for that, and a man who cords a thing over with four turns through the loops in it is a man who has watched somebody do it, and a man of forty-six who cords a printed sheet into a bale of cord and reed has done the only clever thing anybody has done in that yard in four hundred years, and it was done in a minute and a half, and nobody in that yard thought it clever.

The man of sixty-six watched him do it and did not stop him and did not say anything about it, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six watched a printed thing go into a bundle in his own yard and that he said one word while it happened and that the word was *cord*, and that a man of sixty-six who has made about four hundred pieces of paper in twenty years said *cord* and then went back to a strip of paper and that a woman of about thirty with a pail, who was at a door at the fifth hour, has said since that the man of sixty-six said *cord* in the tone of a man saying a price, and that she has heard him say a thing in that tone nine times in twenty days and that eight of the nine were about paper.

---

**And he went back down the lane at about the third hour and out through the road gate with a barrow and a bale on it, and the man of fifty-five in the hut said nothing, and a man of fifty-five has said the same four words to a few hundreds of people in nineteen years and has never once not said them, and a man of fifty-five who has a rule about four words does not have a second set for the case where a man is carrying a bundle, and this is not carelessness and is the answer the man of about forty at the winch gave once at the far end of the bridge in about eleven words, and a gate is a machine for letting liars through, and the cost of stopping one is everything and the cost of letting one through is nothing, and this city has been getting that trade right for four hundred years and has never once been thanked for it by anybody because nobody knows it is a trade.**

Then a child of nine years old came down the lane with a bundle on one shoulder and went through the barrier and up the far bank and away, and nine people went through behind it, and the man of fifty-five in the hut did not look at it, and the man in the hut has not counted the tenth and has not counted the tenth since he came into the hut, and a man of fifty-five who has turned back a few hundreds of people in nineteen years has let about nine thousand through and has not written one of them down because the hut has no book in it and there is nobody in this city who would want one.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the mouth of the lane at the third hour with a stick and a coat, and he was two hundred yards off and he could not see the barrier, and he had not come down to the gate, and a man of thirty-two who has been in this city twenty days has watched a few hundreds of people turned round at three gates and has not gone to any of the three of them, and the reason is that a gate does not stop a man for asking, and he was not going to be the man who asked a hut a question and then wrote down what it answered, and there is nobody four hundred miles off who could have used it and he does not know whether that is the reason or whether it is a reason he has made up for himself, and he has not decided.**

**And he went to the end of the lane instead, and the end of the lane is nine hundred feet of paving with a kerb on each side and a drain under the edge of it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp walked it and turned round at the end and walked it back, and he walked the four hundred yards and back four times between the second hour and the fifth hour, and he was not going anywhere and was not waiting for anybody, and a man of thirty-two who has been in this city twenty days has got into a habit of it, and about four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand have not noticed the habit and about four have, and the four have not said anything to each other because the four have not met.**

**And on the way back up the lane at about the fourth hour he stopped in a yard where a man of about thirty-four with lime on his hands was batching, and the man of thirty-four had seen him go past four times and did not stop him and did not look up, and a man of thirty-four who does not look up when a stranger walks his yard four times is a man who has decided that a man who walks is a man who is not asking anything, and he is right, and about nine thousand people in this city work that out in about half a second, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is the only one anybody here has met who takes twenty days to get to it.**

The man of thirty-four had a gather out of the furnace and about four of the people in that yard have said since that a gather in that yard has a count of seconds in it and that a man who loses the thread of a conversation loses the thread of a gather, and that on the twenty-fourth day a man of thirty-two with a limp watched a gather go from the colour it should be to the colour it should not be and did not move and did not speak, and that the man of thirty-four turned round and looked at him and then at the gather and then back at him, and said nothing and put the gather in the mould, and that a few of the men in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-four who has been doing that for nineteen years has never once had to shout, and that this is the first time in four hundred years anybody in that city has needed a man in a coat to do a thing in a glass yard and needed him to be nine hundred feet off and not in the room.

**And a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of the works with a bucket in one hand and his other hand on the top of it, at the second hour and again at the ninth, and came down it and went up it and did not look down at anybody, and a man of fifty-two has gone up and down that ladder four times a day for nineteen years, and nobody in this city has ever asked him what the bucket is for, and he has never said, and on the twenty-fourth day he came down it at about the fifth hour instead of the sixth and stood at the bottom of it for about two minutes, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was two hundred yards off and did not see that either, and the two of those facts are in the same city on the same day and have never been in the same conversation and are not going to be.**

---

**And the twenty-fourth day in this city was a day like the other twenty-three that a man of thirty-two with a limp had been here for, and the four things it did were that the oven was hot at the second hour and at the ninth, and nine hundred feet of quay worked four jobs at once and none of them was waiting for anybody, and a market on about two hundred yards of street was open and had about four hundred people in it and a man of thirty at a corner of it with nothing in front of him, and a man of fifty with a waggon has fed a stranger every day for twenty days and has not asked him one question, and the reason is a man of thirty who says anybody's name in that street for a penny worked it out in a second on the second day and has never said it out loud, and the man with the waggon has never asked him either, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has been fed by both of them in the same city and has worked out the arrangement in about half a minute on the eighth day and has not written it down.**

**And he stood at the top of it and looked down nine hundred feet of paving at a man with a barrow going out through three bars with a bale of cord and reed on it, and he watched him go through, and a man of fifty-five in a hut said nothing at all, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has now watched about four hundred people go through three bars in this city and has watched a man of forty-six go through twice and has been told by nobody anything at all about either.**

---

And the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days and where it will be standing on the thirtieth, and the man of forty-six went up the rise with a barrow and a bale and a satchel, and a man with a barrow going up that rise is a man of thirty with a barrow and a mule and small glass things in straw, and the two of them went up the same road four days apart and neither of them looked back, and one of them was going to a town two hundred miles off with a piece of paper in a bundle and the other one was going up a hill with a mule, and neither of them knew what the other one was doing, and this city is a place where that is ordinary and has been ordinary for four hundred years.

**And there is a sheet of printed paper in a bundle of cord and reed on a barrow going up a rise out of this city with four figures on it and a place name in the object column and nothing anywhere on it that is the name of a person, and the four figures are all the same figure and the figure is nine, and a book under a board in a yard at the bottom of a lane says eight, and nobody has put the two numbers side by side, and about four hundred people in this city did not know on the twenty-fourth day that they had done anything and will not know on the twenty-ninth and will not know on the thirtieth, and the light was standing where it was standing, and the man of forty-six with a bundle went up the rise, and the bundle went with him.**
