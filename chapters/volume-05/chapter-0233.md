# Chapter 0233

**The thirty-fifth day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the fourth hour, and the room off the lane that goes down to the quay, and a strip of paper about a foot long lying on the bench with a mark in the corner of it that nobody in that room put there, and four people looking at it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the door of it who came down four hundred yards of lane and has been at the door of it nine minutes, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that anything at all has come back into this city, and the two of those are the ordinary condition of a working city and are not a thing anybody has ever noticed.**

It came in a bundle. That is the whole of how it arrived, and a strip of paper about a foot long came back into a city of about nine thousand people inside a bale of cord and reed with about four turns of cord through the loops in it, and the cord had not been cut and had not been retied, and a man of about fifty-five in a hut had let it through three bars without saying the four words, and the four words are for bundles and this was a bundle and that is the correct operation of a gate.

The mark in the corner of it is a line and a bar and a figure.

It is the same shape. It is the same size. It is on the same corner of a strip of the same length ruled in the same five lines by the same rule nine inches long and the same strip of whalebone, and there is no ink on it and it is not a scar. Four people looked at it for about four minutes and not one of the four said anything for the whole of the four minutes, and a woman of twenty-eight put her hand flat on the bench two inches from it and did not touch it.

**Nobody in that room can say what it is, and that is not because anybody in that room is keeping it back. It is that four people looked at a mark in the corner of a piece of paper for about four minutes, and every one of the four has been asked that question by somebody in their working life and not one of the four has an answer, and a man of sixty-one has been asked the same question four times in nineteen years and has said no four times and has never said anything else.**

Then a woman of about thirty with a pail came down the lane at about the fourth hour and half, looked at it, and said nine words and went on to the door she had come to. Four people in that lane have repeated those nine words since and none of them has improved on them and none of the four was in the room.

*That is a mark and I have not put that one there and I am going to work.*

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp came into that room at about the fifth hour and was the fifth person in it, and the only one of the five who was a stranger.** He has not been in a room with a mark on the table in front of him for forty days, and he had spent nine minutes of it at the mouth of a yard. A man of thirty-two with a limp has not once been the first person through the door of anything in this city, and the three times he has come inside a thing in forty days a man of sixty-six, a woman of twenty-eight and a man of sixty-one have each told him that the same arrangement of paper, a bench and a rule is the whole of what this city has ever given anybody. Nobody has said the three of those out loud in one sentence, and he is the only person in nine thousand who could have.

The ordinary work went on while the four of them looked. A woman of twenty-eight folded. A man of fifty with a waggon counted cord into a ball and stopped counting twice and started again. A boy of fourteen came to the door with a bundle on his shoulder, looked at the four of them standing over a piece of paper, said nothing at all, and went out again. He has the names of eleven places in this city and gets them from nine people, and a room with nine bundles of cord on a shelf is not one of the nine.

Then the man of fifty with a waggon said the one thing anybody said in that room in four minutes.

“**I have been to four of them,**” he said, “**in nineteen years, and I have never been asked how and I am not going to say, and I have never once put two of them together in one sentence and I am not going to now, and that is not modesty, that is the only way a man keeps a thing he has not been asked about.**”

And nobody improved on it, and a woman of twenty-eight folded a strip of paper and did not turn round, and a man of fifty with a waggon went back to his cord and did not say one word about the four of them again that day.

He asked the one question. It is the sixth thing he has asked anybody in this city in thirty-five days and the only one he has asked twice.

“May I hold it.”

“No,” said the woman of twenty-eight, and did not look up.

He waited. He waited for about a minute and a half with his hand out and then he put it down.

“Then tell me what it is called.”

“**I do not know,**” said the woman of twenty-eight. “**And I have been asked four times in four years and I have given four answers and one of the four was right and I have never been able to work out which one, and if you ask me a fifth time you will get a fifth answer and it will be wrong, and I would like it noticed that I have told you that in advance and that you have now asked me twice in about a minute and a half.**”

“**The word for the light in this city is a plain one,**” said a man of thirty-four with lime, from the door, where he had been standing for about a minute without any of the four having noticed him. “**That is a thing a man of sixty-six told you on the sixth day of this count and I have heard him tell it once, and I am not going to improve on him, and the two of you have been talking about a mark and not about the light and I am going back to a batch.**”

And he went out. A man of thirty-four who does not improve on a man of sixty-six is a man who has been in a glass yard nineteen years and has heard a man of sixty-six say a true thing once.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp has now been told no by five people in this city in thirty-five days, and the five of them did not consult each other.** A man of sixty-one in a yard at the end of a lane past two gates has been asked what a mark is four times in nineteen years and has said no four times. About four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand people do not know that any of the five said anything.

---

**And a man of sixty-six came down the lane at about the sixth hour, and he had not been asked to come.** He came down four hundred yards of lane to a room he had not been in for about nineteen years, and he had been told about a strip of paper by a woman of twenty-eight at about the fourth hour and half, and he had not asked what it was on the way down, and a man of sixty-six who has not asked anybody anything in about six years did not ask.

He looked at the mark the way a man looks at a thing he has been asked to be right about.

Then he said the reason, and it is the finding of the whole of this, and it is about nineteen words, and it is the fourth thing he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper, and he said it to a room and not to the stranger.

“**You are the only person in this city that is in a book somewhere else. Build this with you in it and two places have got a join in them, and a join with a person in it is a column, and a column can be filled in without asking anybody.**”

Nobody improved on it. A woman of twenty-eight folded a strip of paper and did not turn round, and four hundred yards up that lane a man of thirty-four with lime put a gather into a mould and did not turn round either, and those two men are nine hundred feet apart and neither of them knew the other had heard the same shape of a thing and neither of them has been told.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp did not answer it, and the reason is that a man of fifty-two had told him on the twenty-eighth day of this count that he was the same as a piece of paper, and this was the second time in seven days that a man in this city had told him what he was, and a man of thirty-two with a limp could not use the first one and has not used it and is not going to.** He stood in the middle of that room for about two minutes and said nothing at all, and then he said the thing that is the rest of the thirty-fifth day, and it is about eleven words and it was not a question.

“**I am not asking to build it. I am asking whether a strip can be a road, and I have been in this city thirty-five days and I know the answer to that from a bench in a hall four hundred miles off, and I did not need this room to find it out.**”

“**Then you did not need to be in it,**” said Dermit Crane, and went back to the doorway.

A man of sixty-six has told a man of thirty-two with a limp that he did not need to be in a room, and it is the only time in twenty years a man of sixty-six has told a person to go away from a room he was in, and he did not raise his voice and did not say the four words of a gate, and the two of those are a different set of four words and nobody in this city has ever confused them.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp asked one more question and it is the seventh thing he has asked anybody in this city in thirty-five days and the only one he has asked three times, and it took him about a minute and a half to get to it and he asked it to a man of fifty with a waggon, and the answer was six words.**

“**Has one ever come to you.**”

“**Twice. Nine years apart. Both times a strip of paper with a mark in the corner of it and both times I put it in the wall of my own cart and both times I have got it out since and both times there was a different mark in the corner of it and I have never been told why and I have never asked and I would like it noticed that I have never asked, because a man who asks that in this city is a man who has started asking.**”

Nobody improved on it, and a woman of twenty-eight folded a strip of paper and did not turn round, and a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in a room off a lane and did not ask a seventh question.

**And then nobody would touch it for about an hour and a half.** A woman of twenty-eight wanted to put it with the blanks and was told by a man of fifty with a waggon that it was not a blank, and a man of thirty-four with lime came in at about the half hour and looked at it and said that a thing on a bench is a thing on a bench and he was not going to be the man who picked it up. The strip of paper lay on a bench in a room off a lane for about an hour and a half while three people wanted to move it and would not and a fourth person wanted to say so and said nothing. A man of thirty-four with lime has been in a glass yard nineteen years and has learned that the way to keep out of the end of a thing is to be in the room for the start of it and say nothing at the end.

**And nothing in that room changed hands for about an hour and a half and the ordinary work went on around a piece of paper, and about four hundred people in this city have been in rooms where a thing came back that nobody sent, and about nine of them have been in this one.** A woman of twenty-eight folded two hundred strips that day. A man of fifty with a waggon counted cord into three balls and put them on the shelf. A boy of fourteen came and went twice. None of it was arranged for a stranger and all of it was ordinary and a man of thirty-two with a limp has said since that the ordinary part was the part that told him what had happened, and that the mark in the corner of the strip was the smallest thing in the room.

**And the strip went on the shelf with the nine bundles at about the seventh hour, and it was not counted.** A man of sixty-one in a yard at the end of a lane past two gates keeps a column of about nine hundred marks in a book about two inches thick, and he was not asked about the nine hundred and first and has not been told that there is one.

A man of thirty-four with lime came back into the room at about the seventh hour and half, which is not a thing he does, and stood in the doorway for a minute and a half and then went out again without saying one word. A man of fifty-two in that same yard heard about it from nine feet off and did not comment, and the two of them have been going up and down the same ladder on the same wall for nineteen years and have never once discussed a room off a lane.

And the room shut at about the ninth hour, and a man of thirty-two with a limp walked nine hundred feet of paving and turned round at the end of it and walked it back, and the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days and where it will be standing on the fortieth, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works four times with a bucket in one hand and came down it four times.

**And about four hundred people in this city did not know on the thirty-fifth day that a mark had come back into their city out of a bundle and had gone onto a shelf without anybody doing anything about it, and about nine of them know and have not compared it to anything, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went up four hundred yards of lane at about the ninth hour and went into a glass yard and stood at the mouth of it for about twenty minutes and did not go in, and nine men were on a batch and one of them was fifty-two and one of them was thirty-four and neither of the two looked up, and a man of thirty-four with lime put a duckboard down on the stone where a man of thirty-two with a limp had been standing for about eleven minutes and did it without a word and without looking at him, and a duckboard has been under that stranger's feet in that yard for twelve days and four of the men in that yard have not worked out who put the first one there and are not going to.**
