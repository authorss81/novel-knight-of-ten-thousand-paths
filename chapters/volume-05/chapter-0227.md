# Chapter 0227

**The twenty-seventh day of a count of days that this city keeps, and the fourth hour, and a yard at the bottom of a lane past the works and round by a bindle store with a roof on two sides and no roof on the other two and about two hundred bundles of cord on racks, and a man of about forty-six with a satchel standing at a bench with a strip of paper a foot long in his hand and not cutting it, and a printed sheet clipped to another printed sheet that is the same series and is not the same sheet, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the top of the lane who came down it at about the fifth hour and is at the mouth of that yard and has not come in, and a few hundreds of people in this city do not know that a man of sixty-six is holding two things in his hands and cannot put either of them down, and a few of them do and are not in that yard.**

The second sheet is the same series. It is the same size and the same stock and the same six columns and the same six lines and it is printed from the same plate, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of forty-six held the two of them up side by side about nine inches apart for a second and a half and that the two of them are the same paper and that the only difference anybody in that yard could see was that one of them had been in a bundle of cord and reed for two days, and that a man of forty-six who has left about three thousand forms at about three thousand doors in eight years has now left four, and that two of the four are in the same yard.

“It came back clipped to the first one,” he said. “**Same series, same six questions, same six lines. And this one has nothing in the object column and I am telling you that as a man who has carried it four days and looked at it, and I am not telling you why, because I do not know, and I have never once known why anything in this satchel is printed the way it is printed, and there is a room in a town two hundred miles off with about nine hundred of these in it and about four presses and eleven men, and not one of the eleven has ever come up this road.**”

**And the object column on the first sheet had a place name in it, and the object column on the second sheet is empty, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that this is the only difference between two sheets of the same series that anybody in that yard can see, and that a man of sixty-six put his thumb on the empty column and left it there for a minute, and that a man of sixty-six has never in twenty years refused to fill in a space on a printed sheet because he did not know what went in it, and that there has never been a printed sheet in that yard until the twenty-second day.**

---

**And the man of forty-six said what the second sheet wanted, in a yard, in the plainest register anybody in that city has for a piece of printed paper, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he said it in nine seconds and that it is the plainest description of an office anybody has ever given in this city, and that he was not complaining and was not accusing anybody and was saying a thing that is true about a room he has never been in.**

“**This one is not asking your city a question about itself. This one is asking the district for a number of people in it who know about a thing. That is all it is. There is a case about somewhere and the case wants a number and it has got a district and it has got a date and it has not got the thing, and it is not going to write the thing down, and I have carried it four days and I have worked out that an office that will not name a thing is not hiding it. It is doing the same thing you are doing.**”

**And nobody in that yard improved on it, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of forty-six with a satchel has said in nine seconds the truest thing that has been said in that yard in four hundred years and that it is about a printed sheet and not about this city and that a man of sixty-six at the bench has not moved and has not written and that the nine seconds are the only time a man in that yard has said a thing and had it be right on the first go, and that the man of sixty-six has not said one word and the man of forty-six has not said a second one, and that neither of them is a man who misses a thing like that, and that they have both missed it and a few of the people in that yard have noticed that they have both missed it.**

**And then the man of forty-six asked for it, and he asked for it the way he asks at about three thousand doors, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of forty-six who has left about three thousand forms in eight years has never once asked a door to refuse and has only now asked one to refuse, and that he did it in eleven words and that the eleven words were the ordinary words and that the ordinary words are why they worked.**

“I would like you to make me one of your pieces of paper with the same six questions on it and the fifth one left off and the sixth one empty when it is finished, and I would like it to be a foot long and I would like it to cost nothing, and I would like to be able to carry it back in a satchel and say to a room in a town two hundred miles off that this city has declined.”

---

**And the man of sixty-six put the strip down, and he did not say the same eleven words, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has written about four hundred of those pieces of paper in twenty years and has not declined to make one in twenty years, and that this is the first refusal anybody in that yard has ever seen him make and that it is not a refusal of the man and that the man of forty-six understood that inside a second and did not argue and has not argued since.**

“**I will not make it,**” said the man of sixty-six.

Then he said the reason, and it is about twenty-two words, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he said it standing up, and that a man of sixty-six has not stood up at that bench in about nine years, and that nobody in that yard asked him to stand up and that he stood up because the reason is a thing a man says on his feet.

“**A form is a document. A document is a thing a court can do something with. I made one on the twenty-second and it has four figures on it and it went out in a bundle of cord and reed on a barrow on the twenty-fourth, and I am not going to put a second one about this city into the same room in the same book as the first one, and there is a man in that room who will not have asked me anything.**”

He sat down.

“**That is all of it and I am not going to say it a second way, and the sheet goes back in the satchel, and I am not going to charge you for not having it.**”

---

**And the man of forty-six took it and put it in the satchel and did not fold it, and then he said the thing that the rest of the twenty-seventh day is made of, and he said it in a yard, standing, in about nineteen words, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he said it about a sheet of paper and not about this city and that a man of thirty-two with a limp at the mouth of that yard heard all of it and that he is the only person in this city who knows what the man of forty-six was talking about, and that nobody in that yard has said one word to him about it and he has not asked.**

“**Then it is worth nothing, and I am not saying that to be clever, because it is what it is. The first one is gone. It went out on the twenty-fourth in a bundle and it is two hundred miles off and it is in a book. You not filling this one in does not take that one back and it does not put a hand on it, and I am going to walk down off this rise at the ninth hour with a satchel and a blank sheet in it and I am going to say in a room that a door declined, and the room will write that down, and the room will still have the other one, and I have been doing this eight years and I have never once known which of the two things in a satchel was the one that mattered until this week, and it was the one that was filled in.**”

Nobody improved on it. And a man of sixty-six at a bench picked up a strip of paper and did not look up, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that this city has said no to a form for four hundred years and has never once been told what the no bought, and that a man with a satchel has told it, in a yard, for nothing, and that nobody in that yard thought it was the best thing anybody had said there until nine seconds after he had said it and by which time he was up the lane.

**And a child of nine years old came down the lane at about the fifth hour with a bundle on one shoulder and went past the mouth of that yard and did not come in and did not stop, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a child of nine years old went past a yard on the twenty-seventh day where a man was refusing to make a document and that nobody in that yard told it anything and that it did not look in, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp at the mouth of that yard did not call out and did not follow it and did not put his hand behind him, and that a man of thirty-four with lime has been right about four men on a lane for twenty-seven days and this is the fifth one and none of the four has ever been the man of thirty-two, and that nine thousand people in this city do not ask, and that this is now the third time in twenty-seven days that a person in this city has been in front of him and has not been asked anything by anybody, and that the three of them have been a woman of about thirty with a pail, a man of about forty at a winch, and a child of nine years old, and that a few of the people in that lane have noticed the three and have not said so to each other and have not said so to him.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh day a man of thirty-two with a limp was in a lane about four hundred yards from a bindle yard and nine hundred feet from a glass works, and he put his left hand flat on a wall the way a man puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the wall was warm on the other side of his hand too, and he read what the refusal is made of, and a few of the people in four counties four hundred miles off have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp put his hand on a wall in this city twenty-seven days into a crossing that nobody in that city has ever been told the length of and that what came back was a list, and that nobody in this city or in that county has ever seen anything and that a man of thirty-four with lime has said that you can tell whether a bank is square and he cannot, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp has now been told that a refusal in this city is made of the same thing a bank is made of, which is somebody turning up, and that he is not going to say it that way in a book and has not said it that way out loud.**

**And he did not write it down, and a few of the people who have been in that lane have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp has had four of those in four years and has written one down, and that the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it and he cannot account for where it came from, and that there is nobody in this city he could tell it to, and that the reason he cannot account for where it came from is that it came from a hand on a wall and there is no wall in it.**

What he had, and he wrote it out in his own hand in a room with a bench too short for him in it at about the tenth hour and it is eleven lines and there are no words in it, is this. A list. Under the list there is a column, and in the column there are shifts, and against one of the shifts there is a man. **The man is fifty-two and he is on the third shift and he has been on the third shift for nineteen years and there is no other man's name anywhere in the eleven lines, and a few of the people in four counties have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in a room in a city of about nine thousand people and wrote down the fact that a man he has never spoken to is on a shift he has never seen and then went to sleep, and that the eleven lines are the only page he has ever filled in in four years and it is eleven lines of a thing he is not allowed to have.**

Underneath the eleven lines there is something older and there is no column for it and it is not a list. **It is a decision and it is four hundred years old and it is a thing eleven people did in a room once, and what they chose was to pay it every day instead of once, and they chose it because paying it once was a thing that could have been refused and paying it every day is a thing that cannot, and a few of the people in four counties have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp has written down that a city of about nine thousand people has been keeping a lamp the way a bridgekeeper keeps a wall, which is by somebody turning up, and that he has written it in his own notebook on the twenty-seventh day of a count of days that the city keeps and he does not, and that a city of about nine thousand people have not been told and are not going to be.**

And the eleventh line of the second column was in it as well, because it is under the same list, **and the only thing about it he could put on the page is that a route is a thing four hundred people kept a column for, and that a woman and a boy went out on one of them in a year this city counts in days and has not got a name for and that there is no column in four hundred years of anybody's book for what became of either of them, and that a man of sixty-one said so on the previous day before he was asked and that he did not ask and that he is not going to, and that the not asking took four seconds on the twenty-sixth day and nine minutes to write down on the twenty-seventh, and that the two of those figures are in the same notebook and have not been added together and are not going to be.**

**And he lay nine hours on a bench that was too short for him with the stick standing up in the corner where he could reach it, and the left hand was warm at the fourth hour and the fourth hour is an hour this city keeps by a furnace, and the lamp was standing where it had been standing on the first day, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of the works at the second hour and at the ninth and came down it and went up it and has never once been asked by anybody in this city in nineteen years what the bucket is for, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in this city twenty-seven days and has now written down one thing he cannot use and is not going to be able to use, and a few hundreds of people in this city did not know any of it and will not.**
