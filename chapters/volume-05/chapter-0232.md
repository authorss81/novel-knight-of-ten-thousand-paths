# Chapter 0232

**The thirty-third day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the fourth hour, and the room off the lane that goes down to the quay, and a child of nine years old at the bench with a strip of paper about a foot long in its left hand and the thinnest pot of glue in the city open beside it, and nine people in that room who carry, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the door of it who came down four hundred yards of lane on purpose and has not come in, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that any of it is happening and are not going to.**

The mark goes in the corner. It took about a second and a half and it is a line and a bar and a figure, and it is not ink and it is not a scar. A man of sixty-one at a bench at the end of a lane past two gates has about nine hundred of them in the second column of a book about two inches thick, and he said out loud in nineteen years that the marks in that column and the marks that go on strips of paper are the same shape and the same size and are not the same thing, and he did not say why and was not asked.

**Nobody in that room asked a child of nine years old what it was doing, and that is not because anybody in the room had decided not to. Nine people watched it and not one of them said a word, and not one of the nine has thought about it since, and two of the nine were not in the room at the time.**

A woman of about thirty with a pail was in the room. She has said the same thing four times in years and three of the four have been to men who came up a road with a stick.

“Nobody calls it anything,” she said, at the door, and went on to the door she had come to.

“**Nobody calls it anything,**” said the woman of about thirty with a pail, “**and I will tell you why short and then I am going to work. A name in a book in this city is a person somebody can be shown. That is the whole trade. And there are nine of us who carry and one of us who is nine years old, and if that one has a name then the name is a thing that can be carried out, and if it cannot be carried out then it cannot be a name.**”

She went in with the pail. Four people in that lane have said since that a woman of thirty told a stranger the whole of the post in one breath and then went to work, and that the four men she told it to are all still alive and three have gone and the fourth is standing at the door of a room forty feet off and did not go in.

---

**And a child of nine years old has a day like anybody else's, and there is no ceremony in any part of it and no assembly and nobody who comes out of a door to see it off.** It carried a hod in a glass yard four hundred yards up that lane eleven times between the second hour and the fourth, and a man of thirty-four with lime did not count them. It had a piece of bread off a sill at the third hour and did not say thank you and was not expected to. It walked four hundred yards of lane twice and stopped in a market on about two hundred yards of street where a few hundreds of people were and bought nothing, and then it came down the lane to a room off it and put a mark in the corner of a strip of paper about a foot long, in about a second and a half. That is the whole of what it did with its day.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at that door for about an hour and a half.** A woman of twenty-eight came in past him and went out again and came in again and did not speak to him once. He was at the mouth of that lane at the second hour and at the third and at the fourth and did not go down it to the glass works, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has been standing at the door of the same room two days running, and on the first of the two days he came in and sat down and on this one he has not come in at all.

The child came out of the room at about the fifth hour with its bundle on one shoulder and went down the lane and did not go out through a gate. It had not gone out on the thirty-first or the thirty-second. It had gone out on the thirtieth, which was a duty. A city of about nine thousand people has a child who does not go out on three days running about once in four years, and this is the first time any of the nine people in that room has been in the room for it.

Then it went out at about the seventh hour, and nine bundles went out behind it, and a man of fifty-five in a hut with three bars said nothing.

---

**Nobody in that room is told what is in a bale. That is not a rule and nobody in this city could make one.** Nine people go out of a gate four days in seven, not one of the nine is told what is in the bundle, and not one of the nine has ever asked, and a bundle is a bundle because nobody has ever worked out what is in it, and that has been the ordinary way a city's whole trade of the outside is done for four hundred years.

The nine of them are nine and half of them are women and they go out at the second hour and they are back inside by the ninth if they are not let in and by the tenth if they are. A woman of twenty-eight said the thing she has wanted to say for four years, in about nineteen words, in the ordinary register there is for work. It is the first time in four years she has said it in a room with more than two people in it. Nine people heard all of it and none of the nine has been able to answer it, and one of the nine was asked about it twice in four years and gave the same words both times.

“**I have carried for four years and I have been turned back at three gates four days in seven and I am not going to stop carrying, and the reason I am saying it in a room is that there are nine of us who go out and about four hundred of us in this city have been out, and not one of the two numbers knows anything about the other one, and I have worked that out about four times in four years and I have never once said it out loud and I have said it once.**”

Nobody improved on it. A man of thirty-two with a limp was at the door of that room when she said it and heard about a third of it and worked out the rest afterwards, and the part he worked out afterwards is the part he is not going to write down.

He waited until the middle of the afternoon and asked her the only question left in it, and he asked it in nine words and in the plainest register he has.

“**Is it four hundred out of nine thousand.**”

“**It is about four hundred and I have not counted and I am not going to, and the number that matters is nine and you have had that twice.**”

And a man of thirty-two with a limp did not ask her a third question about a number. He has now been given nine in the same conversation twice by a woman of twenty-eight who was folding a strip of paper at the time. A man of thirty who sells names for a penny in that city has said out loud that the arrangement of being fed and not paid for is the only protection the city has, and that nobody knows it is one, and that the man of thirty has not been asked and did not say it to a stranger. A man of thirty-two with a limp heard it from a woman of about thirty with a pail eight days ago and has written down that sentence and has not written down the rest of what she said.

---

**And the room shut at about the ninth hour and a woman of about fifty who keeps it was not in it, and the hook by the door had a coat on it in the morning and had not at night, and nobody in that lane has ever asked her where she goes between the second hour and the ninth, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in this city thirty-three days and has worked out that the woman who owns the room is the only person in it who is not on the four days in seven and has not asked her.**

**And a strip of paper was put into his hand at about the fifth hour and he put it in the inside pocket of a coat, and eleven minutes later he could not remember what the paper was for.** He had held it and looked at it and put it away. Four hundred yards up that lane a man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud to nine men in a working yard that the fourth time a man loses the thread of a thing is a habit and not a fault, and that a man who has lost the thread of it four times has not got it any more, and that he said it once and went back to a gather and has not said it since.

He took the strip out at the sixth hour. There were five lines on it and a mark in the corner, and the mark was a line and a bar and a figure and it was not ink. He looked at it and put it back and did not write it down. A man of thirty-two with a limp has now been in this city thirty-three days and has been in that room twice and has been given two strips of paper he cannot remember the purpose of. He has asked about four things in the first fortnight and got four straight answers in nine words each and has not once told any of the four that they were being difficult, and the fifth thing he asked anybody in this city was asked on the thirty-first day by a woman of twenty-eight who told him in advance that she did not know.

---

**And a man of sixty-six was in that room for nine minutes at about the seventh hour and did not sit down and did not touch anything.** He stood in a doorway of a room he had not been in for about nineteen years and looked at a shelf with nine bundles of cord on it for nine minutes. He did not look at the child once, and the reason is that he has looked at about nine hundred children come to that bench in nineteen years, and looking at one of them is a thing he does not do twice in a day.

“They are not for this room,” he said, to the room, in about eleven words. “**They are for the shelf and the shelf is for the four days in seven and the four days in seven have not changed and nothing about them is going to.**”

And then the other thing, about nineteen words, and it is the third thing he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper. A man of sixty-one has said two such things in nineteen years and a man of fifty-two said three in two days, and none of the three has ever been in the same room as the other two. There are three men in this city who say true things out loud to strangers and the three of them have not met.

“**And they go to people who are not told anything. That is the ordinary way. And if anybody in this room is ever asked what is in a bale then the answer is that he does not know, and it is not a lie, and a man who is asked that is a man who has started asking.**”

Nobody improved on it. A woman of twenty-eight folded a strip of paper and did not turn round. Not one of the nine who carried a bundle out of that room afterwards thought about it.

---

**And at about the ninth hour a child of nine years old came down that lane with a bundle on its shoulder and went out through the road gate and up a far bank and away, and nine people went through behind it, and a man of fifty-five in a hut said nothing.** A hut with a rule about four words and no rule about bundles is not a policy and is a machine, and a man of forty at a winch gave the whole of it in about eleven words years ago: the four words are for bundles, and a bundle is a bundle because nobody has ever worked out what is in it.

A man of thirty-two with a limp has never been to that gate. He has been two hundred yards from it on nine days out of forty and has not gone to it once, and a man who walks behind a thing for forty days and never once gets in front of it is not watching it and is being shown it, and nobody in that lane has worked out which of the two it is and neither has he.

He was at the end of the lane at the ninth hour and did not go with it and did not call out and did not ask it anything. This is now the seventh time in thirty-three days that a person in this city has been in front of him and has not been asked anything by anybody, and the seven have been a woman of about thirty with a pail, a man of about forty at a winch, a man of sixty-one, and a child of nine years old four times, and a child of nine years old accounts for four of the seven, and the arithmetic of that is a count of occasions and not a count of anything else, and nobody in that lane has added it to anything.

**And the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works with a bucket in one hand and came down it and went up it, and nobody in this city has ever asked him what the bucket is for. A man of thirty-two with a limp had in the inside pocket of a coat a page with a seventh line on it that says a person, and eleven lines in a notebook, and two strips of paper about a foot long with five lines ruled across them and nothing written on any of the five and a mark in the corner of each of them, and the four of those things were about four inches apart in one pocket on the thirty-third day of a count of days that this city keeps and that he does not, and none of them has met any of the others, and about four hundred people in this city did not know any of it and are not going to.**
