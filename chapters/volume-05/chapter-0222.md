# Chapter 0222

**The twenty-second day of a count of days that this city keeps, and the fourth hour, and a yard at the bottom of a lane past the works and round by a bindle store with a roof on two sides and no roof on the other two and about two hundred bundles of cord on racks, and a man of sixty-six at a bench under the roof with a rule nine inches long and a strip of whalebone and a pot of the thinnest glue, and a man of about forty-six with a satchel and a printed sheet in it who came down that lane four hundred yards on purpose because a boy on a step told him where the paper was made, and the man of thirty-two with the limp and the stick is at the top of that lane and has been for two hours and has not come down it, and a few of the people in two yards nine hundred yards apart have noticed both halves of that and have not said so.**

The man of sixty-six did not stop cutting. That is the first fact about the twenty-second day and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six who does not stop cutting a strip of paper for a stranger is a man who has decided the stranger is not going to be answered, and that in that yard it is nearly always the other one, and that on this occasion it was the first one and nobody in that yard could tell the difference and nobody asked.

The strip came off the whalebone in one piece and a foot long and he laid it on the bench and put the whalebone down beside it and only then looked at the man of forty-six, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that this is the only time in twenty years a man of sixty-six has put the whalebone down before he had been asked twice and that a man who puts a tool down is a man who has decided the conversation is going to last longer than a refusal.

Then the man of forty-six took a sheet out of the satchel and put it on the bench, and it lay about nine inches from the board and the book, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the top of that lane for two hours on the twenty-second day and could not see the bench and would not have been able to read what was on it standing where he was, and that the two objects on that bench that afternoon were a foot apart and neither of them could be shown to anybody and one of them was about to go two hundred miles off in a satchel.

---

**And the sheet is printed and not ruled and it is about the size of a broadside with a little less paper than a broadside, and the man of sixty-six turned it round about the width of a hand and put his thumb on the corner of it and held it there, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has never in twenty years touched a thing that was not made in his own yard with his own tool and that he touched that one with a thumb.**

It is ruled in six columns. Five of them have a head on them and the sixth has no head at all and is the same width as the other five, and the man of sixty-six read the five heads and then read the sixth one and then read the five heads again, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he read the five heads twice because the first time he was reading them and the second time he was counting them, and that a man who counts the columns on a piece of paper in his own yard is a man who has been given a piece of paper.

“**Who printed that,**” he said.

“A press in a town two hundred miles off,” said the man of forty-six. “**Four of them print it, and I have been to three of the towns, and I could not tell you the difference between the four sheets and I have never once been told what the series is for and I have never once asked, because it is not on the list and the list is what I am paid for.**”

“Then it is not yours.”

“**It is not mine and it is not yours and that is why I have brought it here, and I have brought it here because you make paper, and a man who makes paper is the only door on my round that a form has ever come back out of.**”

---

**And the man of forty-six said the thing that the rest of the twenty-second day is made of, and he said it in a yard in this city to a man of sixty-six who makes eleven-word refusals, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he said it once, in the tone of a man saying a price, and that nobody in that yard improved on it and that one of the four has been asked since whether he knows what he said and he does not.**

“**I am not asking you to fill it in. I have never asked anybody to fill one in and I am not going to start with the man who makes the paper, because a man who makes paper would have to tell me what is on it and neither of us wants that. I am leaving it on your bench the way I have left it on about three thousand benches, and I will come back for it in two days on my way round and if it is filled in I will take it and if it is not filled in I will take it and that is the whole of my trade in this city and it takes a minute and a half.**”

The man of sixty-six did not move the sheet.

“You are leaving it on my bench.”

“**I am leaving it on your bench.**”

“**And if I fill it in.**”

“**Then it goes back down the road and a man in a town two hundred miles off reads four figures off it and puts them in a book, and that is what happens to about one door in nine, and you are not one of the nine and I have not got anybody else in this city to try.**”

---

**And the man of sixty-six took his hand off the corner of it and folded the sheet once and did not fold it in four, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six folds a thing in four when he is going to keep it and folds it once when he has not decided, and that in twenty years a few hundreds of people have watched him do both and have not been told the difference, and that the difference is the whole of what a yard is.**

Then he put the rule down and put both hands flat on the bench on either side of the sheet, and he did not look at it while he said what he said, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that this is the only time a man of sixty-six has put two hands flat on a bench in twenty years and that he put one of them down on the sixth day in front of a man of thirty-two with a limp, and that the two of those are the same gesture and that nobody in that yard has worked out why a man puts his hands down when he is not going to pick something up.

“**I will tell you three things about that sheet and I will tell you all three short, and then I am going to go back to my bench, and you may leave it here and you may not, and if you may not then you may take it and I will not write anything on it.**”

“**One. It has four places in it for a figure and not one place in it for a name. That is not a thing I chose and it is not a thing I can refuse. I have made about four hundred pieces of paper in this yard in twenty years and not one of them has a figure on it and not one of them has a name on it, and the reason is that a piece of paper with a number on it cannot be refused, and you have brought me a piece of paper with four of them on it and nowhere to put a man's name, and I have not got a way of saying no to that piece of paper, and it has not occurred to me in twenty years that I would be asked to.**”

“**Two. The other side of it is in a hand I do not know and it is not printed and I am not going to talk about what is written on the other side of a piece of paper that has come into my yard.**”

“**Three. I do not know what the series is for. I have not known for about two days, which is the longest anybody in this city has gone without knowing something, and I have not asked, and I would like it noticed that I have not asked and that I have never in twenty years not asked, and that a man of forty-six with a satchel has been in my yard for about two minutes.**”

---

**And four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that the man of sixty-six said the three things and the middle one of the three was the one that was about the paper and that a man of sixty-six who will not talk about the other side of a piece of paper has told this city something about himself and did not know that he was doing it, and that the man of forty-six did not hear the middle one at all and asked about the first one again, and that the reason he asked about the first one again is that he has been leaving forms at about three thousand doors for eight years and has never once been at a door that could not refuse one.**

“The four places for the figure,” said the man of forty-six. “**That is four figures.**”

“That is four figures,” said the man of sixty-six. “**Or it is one figure written four times. I have not got anything to tell you about which of the two it is, and neither has the man who printed it, and both of us would have to go and look at about four other sheets in three other towns to find out, and neither of us is going to do that, and that is the ordinary way a printed thing works and it is the reason a thing that is printed is not the same as a thing that is written.**”

**And the yard was quiet in the way a yard is quiet and the light was standing over the lane and a few hundreds of people in this city went on doing what they had done at the second hour and at the ninth, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works with a bucket in one hand and came down it at the ninth hour and went up it again on the twenty-third, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the top of a lane two hundred yards off with a stick and a coat and a page in the coat, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that both of those men are in a book in a county four hundred miles off that this city has never been told about and that neither of them is in a book in this city, and that a man of thirty-two has been here twenty days and has not been asked to be put in anything, and a man of fifty-two has been somewhere for nineteen years and has never once been asked to be put in anything either, and that the two of them are the only people in this city who are in no book and one of them has been here twenty days.**

**And the city did the four things it does on every day of a count of days, and the four of them are the reason a man of thirty-two with a limp has not been able to think for twenty days and has not told anybody why, and he has not told anybody why because there is no why in it, and a few of the people in four places have said since that the twenty-second day in this city was a day the oven was hot at the second hour and at the ninth, and that nine hundred feet of quay worked four jobs at once and none of them was waiting for anybody, and that a lighter came up under the quay and went along the side in the standing water and did it twice and the second time was faster, and that a market on about two hundred yards of street was open and had a few hundreds of people in it and a man of thirty was at a corner of it with nothing in front of him who says anybody's name in that street for a penny and who has never been asked for one by anybody who is not standing there, and that none of the four of those things was a question and none of the four of them had an answer in it, and that is the ordinary condition of a working city that has asked nobody anything for four hundred years.**

**And a man of sixty-six said that about a printed sheet in his own yard, and a few of the people in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six who has made about four hundred pieces of paper in twenty years has never in twenty years had a printed thing put on his bench, and that the two kinds of paper in that yard on the twenty-second day were about nine inches apart and had been in the same city for four hundred years and had never been in the same yard, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp was two hundred yards up a lane at the time and has not been told any of it and is not going to be.**

---

**And the man of forty-six said the other thing and he said it standing, and he had not said it in the tone of a man saying a price, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that it is the only time anybody in that yard has heard a man of forty-six use any other tone and that a woman of about thirty with a pail came down the lane at about the fifth hour and heard the end of it and has repeated it since four times.**

“**I am going to have to come back for it in two days and put it in a satchel and take it down off this rise to a town two hundred miles off, and I have to be able to say that is a form and it is a form and it came from a door, and I have never in eight years been asked a question by a door and I am not going to start at the first one, and if it is filled in I will take it and if it is not filled in I will take it, and that is what I am going to say in a room, and I would like it noticed that I have just said the same thing twice and that the second one was for me and not for you.**”

**And the man of sixty-six asked him a question, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has not asked anybody anything in about six years and did it on the twenty-second day to a man of forty-six nine minutes after refusing to talk about the other side of a printed sheet, and that the two of those are the same afternoon and that nobody in that yard worked out at the time that they were.**

“**Has anybody ever come here.**”

“From that town.”

“**From anywhere.**”

“**No,**” said the man of forty-six. “**In eight years. Not once. A form goes out to about four hundred doors and it comes back to a room in a town and a man turns it over, and nobody has ever walked up that road to see what a form said, and I have never told anybody that and I have never been asked it, and you have asked me it in a second and a half, and I would like it noticed that it is the first question anybody in your city has asked me and it is the one thing I know the answer to, and I did not know until you asked it that it was the thing I would have said if anybody had asked me anything.**”

**And the man of sixty-six went on cutting for a minute and did not answer it, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that the silence was not a refusal and was not thought and was a man of sixty-six finding out that there is a second thing about a piece of paper that is worse than the first, and that the first thing is that four figures can leave this city and the second thing is that nobody is ever going to come up that road and find out what became of them, and that a man of sixty-six has kept a board over a book for twenty years for a reason that has just been joined by a second one, and that he did not say the joining out loud and nobody in that yard is going to make him.**

---

Nobody improved on it. The man of sixty-six picked the rule up and went back to the strip, and the sheet lay on the bench about nine inches from a book with a board over it, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six and a man of forty-six were in that yard for nine minutes on the twenty-second day and that neither of them said one word about the lamp, and that the lamp was standing where it had been standing, and that the man of forty-six looked at it once on his way out and did not ask what it was, and that four people in that yard have said since that a man who has been down a road since before the light and has been in a city for two days has not asked what the light is, and that this is now four strangers in four days who have been in this city and not asked that, and that nobody in that yard has begun to think this means anything, and that a few hundreds of people in this city have not either.
