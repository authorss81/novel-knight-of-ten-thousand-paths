# Chapter 0206

**Two hundred miles off, in a provincial town, on the fourth of Embermonth, YR 314, a Wednesday, and the second hour, and a man of sixty-one came into the room with the stove in it and put a stoppered jar on the bench at the end of it, and the jar is a hand's width and a half and it is not a thing anybody in that building had seen before, and he had been up a pass to get it and he had come back down, and he is not a fool and he is not a villain and he is a man who keeps a post he should not be keeping and has kept it for about two years by knowing what people in a building are short of.**

He got the post the way everybody gets a post in an office, which is by being the person who already knows where the thing is. He has been in this building since the middle of last year and he came to it out of a room over a weigh-house in a county two hundred miles from this town where a keeper of records used to keep his access and sold it, and he did not get that room and everybody in both places knows that he tried, and a handful of them in this building have said that he is the only man in it who can find a thing in about four minutes and that this is all his usefulness and it is not a small usefulness.

His name is Halven and he is sixty-one. He carries a satchel and a waxed cloth and a small hammer he has never used and has never explained, and he put the jar on the bench and took his hand off it and stood back from it, and the woman of thirty-three who fills in the *Keeper* column said, “That is not ours.”

“It is not,” he said. “That is why I have brought it to a room that is not mine instead of taking it out of the province in a cart, and I would like it noticed that I could have done the second thing and that I did not do it, and that I have thought about the first thing about four times on the road and I have not done it, and I am not a good man, so you may make of that whatever you find useful.”

**Nobody in that room believed him and nobody in that room disbelieved him and some of them in that building have said since that a man who says out loud in a room that he is not a good man and has thought about the other thing nine times is doing one of two things and that in nine years of watching that office nobody has been able to tell which.**

**And he wants a thing, and the thing is not in this building, and he has said so out loud twice in two years to two different people and neither of them has been able to do anything about it, and it is the whole of what is wrong with him and it is not a moral.**

“I have been trying to get into a room over a weigh-house in a county two hundred miles from here for about two years,” he said, unasked, in the middle of a conversation about a jar. “**A keeper of records there has a press in it and about four hundred documents in it that this office has never seen and never will see, and I have offered that woman four things in two years and she has taken none of them, and the last thing I offered her was a thing I am not going to describe in this room, and she said that a man who brings a thing he will not describe to a records office has told the office what he is.**”

“And you went on trying.”

“I went on trying for about four months after that and then I came here, because this building has presses in it too and nobody in this building has ever said no to me, and I have got a post in a building where nobody says no, and that is all my career and I am sixty-one and I have a satchel and a hammer I have never used.”

Nobody in that room said anything about that, and about four people in that building have said since that it was the truest thing anybody had said in that room in the whole time he has been in that building and that it had nothing to do with the jar, and that a man who is honest about what he wants is harder to turn out than a man who lies about what he is.

**And the office has a rule about an unclassified thing, and the rule is old, and it works like this, and a few in that building could describe it without being asked.**

A man who carries a thing into the province that the province has not seen is to bring it to a room, and a person is to write it down, and the thing is to be entered, and the entry is to go on to whoever it is for. It is not a law and it is in no book and it is a practice, and it is older than the building, and it is the only practice in that building that everybody in that building obeys without knowing that they are obeying it, and a man of about fifty who is a clerk of the press has said that in thirty-one years he has never known it broken and that he would not know what to do about it if it were, and that a man who brought in a thing and a man who took a thing out of the province without saying were two men doing the same job with different tempers.

**And a thing that goes on to whoever it is for goes out of the building in a bag, and the bag goes on the ordinary post, and the ordinary post is the only instrument in this kingdom that everybody trusts, and two or three of them in that room have said that this office is careful about what it prints and not careful about what it sends, and that those are two different dangers and the second one is nine hundred years older than the first.**

The jar has a cork in it and a wax over the cork and the wax has been broken and put back, and the glass is not glass that anybody in that building makes, and there is about a cup and a half in it, and the colour of it does not change, and a woman of about twenty-nine who is in the room because she is a clerk of the returns pressed put her hand on the jar on the bench and said that it was warm and that it had been on a bench in a room with a stove in it for a quarter of an hour and it should not have been, and took her hand off, and did not say anything else about it.

Nobody in that room knows what it is. That is the fact of the second hour of that Wednesday and it is the fact of all of that year in that office, and the office has four hundred forms a month and a rule about an unclassified thing brought into the province, and the rule is that a man brings it to a room, and a person writes it down, and the thing is entered and goes on to whoever it is for.

---

**And he said the name of a place out loud in that room, and the form has no column for a place, and nobody wrote the name down, and about four people in that building have said since that a man came into an office and said the name of a place and a clerk of that office had nowhere to put it.**

He said it in about four sentences and the four sentences were everything he had, and he had them in his own head and not on a paper, which is the mark of a man who has told it before.

“Nine days up and four days down, and the going up is a cart road to the bottom of a pass and the top of the pass is not a road, it is a barrow ground with a trough on it, and there is a woman there who keeps the top and she did not ask me anything and I did not tell her anything. Two days in a town of about nine thousand people with a river in it and glass works and a bread oven and eleven people on a council, and I was there on the ordinary business of a man who buys things nobody in this building sells.

“**And the light in it does not turn. It comes up out of the east in the morning and it stays where it is and it was still where it was at night and it was still where it was the next morning, and the people there have lived under it for four hundred years and they do not talk about it, and I asked one man what it was and he told me it was a word, and he would not say the word in front of a stranger.**”

He had a figure on a page for that and the figure was not a price, it was a count of the days he had been in the town and a count of the number of people who had asked him what he wanted, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who comes back with a figure is a man who has decided to come back, and that a man who comes back with a price is a man who is selling, and that a man who comes back with both is a man who has been up a pass and has a good head.

**And he was asked for the rest of it and he gave it, and it took about four minutes, and a couple of them in that room have said since that it was nine minutes of the most ordinary account of a place they have ever heard and that they have all been thinking about it for nine weeks.**

“I will give you what I saw and I will not tell you what it means, and I want the two of those kept apart, because I have been asked the second thing eleven times on the road and once by a man who paid for the answer and I did not have it.

“There is a river and the river is used. There are wharves and they work. There is a glass works with a furnace in it and the men in it were doing a job and did not look at me and I stood in the way for a quarter of an hour and nobody told me to move. There is a bread oven that was hot at the second hour of the morning and hot again at the ninth hour of the evening and the same people were on it both times. There are about nine thousand people and I could not get a figure and did not try, and a man of about sixty has a book in a yard that I saw the outside of and did not go into.

“And there is a street market that is not a market day in this kingdom and is a market day there, and about four hundred people were in it, and the bread was cheap and the glass was cheap and I sold nothing and bought two things and paid the same as I would pay in a market at home, which is the part I keep coming back to, because a place that will not be written to and has a market that opens when it likes and sells bread at the price of a market at home is not a ruin and is not a shrine and is not hiding, and I have been in eleven markets in four counties and two of them were dear.

“**There is nothing to report. That is what I have come back with. A man came up a pass and came back and there was nothing to report, and I have a jar, and the jar is the only thing anybody in this building is going to be able to use, and it is a cup and a half of something I cannot put a name to in a form, and the two of those facts are both of those years of my life and I would like the room to have them both.**”

---

**And the jar is what he came back with, and nobody in that building can say what it is, and it is not the light and it is not of the light and it is a cup and a half of something in a jar with a cork in it and a wax that has been broken and put back, and a good half of them in that building have said that if it is what he says it is then this office has a form with no column for the place it came from and a rule that says a person writes it down, and that the two of those things are about four hundred years old and do not know about each other.**

---

**And then he filled in the form, and the form is a return of the office, and it is ruled in five columns with a sixth line at the foot that has no head to it, and the columns ask what the thing is, on what day it was taken up, who had knowledge of it, who keeps it, and how often.**

The first column took him about a minute. He wrote, in a fair hand, *A sample, in a glass jar, sealed, of a substance not known to this office, brought in by a trader and not entered as goods.* And then he stopped and said that he would put the name of the place on the sixth line if they would let him, because there was nowhere else for it, and the woman of thirty-three said that the sixth line had no head and a line with no head is not a column and is not entered and is not kept, and he said that he had understood that and was going to write it there anyway.

The name is Calderwick. He said it out loud in that room at about the third hour of that Wednesday, in a fair voice, once, and about eleven people heard it and a good half of them have said since that they heard it and could not now say whether it had been two words or one.

**The woman of thirty-three wrote it on a leaf of her own hand because there was nowhere on the sheet to put it, and the leaf went into a book of working papers on the second shelf, and the sheet went into the returns press, and the name of the only place anybody in this building knows of that keeps a standing thing and will not be written to was on a leaf in a book of working papers and on a line with no head on a sheet in a press, and neither of those is the form, and the form is the only thing that leaves the building.**

The second column: the day. The third column: *persons spoken with.* And here he stopped for a moment longer than the first column, and then he wrote two names in a fair hand and spelt both of them and said the spelling out loud once so that the room would have it, and the two names are these.

**Osmund Cusk. Nesh Karrow.**

“They are the two people in that town who spoke to me and told me the truth and would not sell me anything,” he said. “That is all of my knowledge and it is not enough to be a return and I have written it because the column asks for it and because I am not a man who writes a column and means something else. I have not put a number in the second column because a number is not what I have got. I have got two names and I have got them in a column with a head on it, and if this office wants a figure of persons it does not have one off me and never will.”

**The woman of thirty-three read the two names twice, and a handful of them in that room have said since that she wrote them out on a leaf of her own hand because she was not going to trust the column, and that the leaf is in a book of working papers on the second shelf in that room, and that the leaf has been looked at by three people in nine weeks and that it is the only piece of paper in that office that has anybody's name on it from that town.**

“Take it away,” said the woman of twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns.

He did not argue. That is the thing four people in that building have said about him since, that he did not argue, and that a man who has come four hundred miles with a cup and a half of something in a jar and who is told to take it away out of a room in under a minute and who does not argue is either a fool or has worked out something, and that the people in that room who have watched him work for two years do not think it is the first.

**He put the cork in and the wax over the cork and put the jar in the satchel and took the satchel up and said, “I will be in this province again before the end of the week and I will come to this room again, and I am not going to be difficult about it, and I am telling you now so that you can put the room in order if your room wants putting in order.”**

And he went out, and the satchel went with him, and the two names stayed on the leaf, and the place-name stayed on a line with no head on a sheet that went into the returns press, and the sample was not in the building, and nothing in that room had been decided, and nobody in that room knew what a sample of anything is, and about four people in that building have said that the strangest thing that has happened in that room in as long as the room has been a room was that a woman wrote down two names that nobody in the office had asked for.

**And the office had asked that county twice for a figure of persons and had never once asked it for a name.**
