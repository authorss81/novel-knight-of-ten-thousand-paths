# Chapter 0237

**The fortieth day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the second hour, and a room at the top of a lane that goes up and not down with a long table in it and about two hundred and forty pieces of paper on it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the top of that lane who came up it for the fourth time in forty days and has been in that room five times and has been in it nine hundred seconds each time, and a city of about nine thousand people that has been asked four things from a town two hundred miles off in about nineteen days, and the light is standing where it was standing on the first day and where it will be standing in about a minute, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that there are two hundred and forty pieces of paper on a table.**

Two hundred and forty of them, laid out in twelves, in a room with a long table in it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp counted them twice and got two hundred and forty both times. A man with a limp counts a table of paper the way a man counts a street of barrows, and he did it standing up, and he did not say the number out loud, and nobody in the room asked him for it.

They are about nine inches by six. The second half of the fourth sheet of a printed series, cut along the fold. A man of sixty-six cut them on the whalebone in about a minute in five cuts. A man of sixty-six has cut about four hundred things in twenty years and every one of them was a strip of paper ruled in five lines, and this is the first time he has cut a thing that somebody else printed, and he cut it with the same tool, and nobody in the room asked him about that and four of the eleven noticed it.

The fourth hour is the hour this city does its counting in and it is not the hour this room counts in, and about nine hundred seconds is the length of the longest silence anybody in that room has ever had with a stranger in it, and it is on the page at the nineteenth day of this count in a yard at the bottom of a lane between a man of sixty-six and a child of nine years old, and the two of those are the same nine hundred seconds and there are two of them and neither of them was about a person.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the top of that lane on the fortieth day with a stick in his right hand and his left hand hanging at his side, and the left hand was warm at the fourth hour and the fourth hour is an hour this city keeps by a furnace, and he had been going to do a thing and did not do it, and it is the same thing he did on the twenty-seventh day of this count with a hand on a wall, and it is the same hand and the same warmth.** The room is nine hundred feet from the only other wall in this city that is warm on both sides, and a man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard has said out loud that you can tell whether a bank is square and he cannot, and that a man who puts a hand flat on a thing is not asking the thing anything and is telling it something, and that he said it once and has not said it since.

Nobody in the city saw him not do it. About four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand people did not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the top of a lane for about a minute and a half with a warm hand and did not put it anywhere, and that it is the first time in forty days that he has come up that lane to be told something and has not been told anything, and that a man of about thirty-one with a satchel was on the road at the same hour and has not been told about the minute and a half and would not have understood it.

---

**And a woman of sixty did not look up for about a minute and a half, and a clerk of forty did not shut his ink pot.** A clerk of forty has shut that ink pot in nineteen years every time there was nothing to write and has left it open on the four occasions when there was, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was in the room for both of the earlier ones and did not notice either, and the reason the pot was open is nine lines on a page in a yard four hundred yards off at the fifth hour, and nobody in that room has ever been told that and will not be.

“You have come up four times in forty days,” she said.

“I have.”

“**Then you have come up four times to be told the same thing and you have not asked me for it once, and the fifth time you come up I am going to have said nothing at all, and I am telling you that now so that you can decide what the fifth one is for.**”

---

**And a man of thirty-four with lime was in the lane outside that room at about the fifth hour and did not come up it.** He has been in that room once, on the ninth day of this count of days, and he has been in the yard at the bottom of the same lane every working day for nineteen years, and he stood at the bottom of a lane that goes up and not down on the fortieth day for about twenty minutes and did not go up it and went back to a batch. A man of thirty-four who does not go up a lane is a man who has worked out that what is said at the top of it is not for him.

**And a man of sixty-six and a man of sixty-one were in that room at about the fifth hour.** Two men in this city stood in one room for the first time in nineteen years on the fortieth day of a count of days that this city keeps, and neither of them had been in the other's yard since they were young, and the woman of sixty did not look up and the clerk of forty did not shut his ink pot, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that the two men who buy their paper and their glue out of one building and have never spoken stood in one room eleven feet apart on the fortieth day.

They had both brought the book. A man of sixty-six brought his from a bench under a roof four hundred yards down the lane with about two hundred bundles of cord behind it. A man of sixty-one brought his from a bench under a roof at the end of a lane past two gates and a slope, and the book on it is about two inches thick. The two of them have a fold in them in the same place, four inches from the bottom, and four of the eleven have seen the two of them at the same time and not one of the four has ever been told what the fold is.

Then the man of sixty-six said it, and it is about nineteen words, and it is the sixth thing he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper.

“**This city is going to have a mark in nine hands and a way of telling another place it is there, and there is exactly one person walking out of this valley who is in a book four hundred miles off, and we are not going to put him in the middle of it, and I would like that in a book of some kind and there is not one to put it in and I have not made one.**”

And the man of sixty-one said the other half, and it is the third thing he has said out loud in nineteen years that was not about a book, and it is a sentence about a person, and it is about four hundred yards long and it was said to a table.

“**A road is a line and two hands and a day and a witness at each end who is not the man keeping it, and I have read that in a book in a hall four hundred miles off and I have had four hundred years of a second column to look at and I have never once seen one of the eleven written in a hand, and I am not going to be the first and I am not going to let a man of thirty-two be one either, and that is the whole of it.**”

Nobody improved on it. A clerk of forty wrote nothing. Two men stood in a room eleven feet apart, and about four hundred people in this city do not know any of it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the top of the lane and had been there for about two minutes and had heard about two sentences of it and has not said a word about either and is not going to.

---

**And then the man of sixty-one said the offer, and he said it in about nineteen words and he said it standing up, and a man of sixty-one has not stood up in that room in nineteen years and nobody in that room asked him to, and he stood up because the thing he was saying is a thing a man says on his feet.** This is the second time in nineteen years that a man in that room has stood up, and the first one was a woman of sixty, and the two of them did not know they were doing the same thing.

“**There is a road. Four days in seven, a bundle, no name, no figure, no hand, no wage, no clerk, and a witness at each end who is not you. That is all of it. I am not able to put it anywhere else and neither is he, and it is in this room because this room is the only one in this city that a thing can be said in.**”

Then the man of sixty-six said the other half, and it is about nineteen words, and it is a document and not a speech.

“**It goes in a book under a board and it is not going anywhere and you are not going to take it, and that is the correct way round, and I have made about four hundred of these and not one of them has ever left this city in anybody's hand and there is no reason there should be a second kind.**”

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp went down four hundred yards of lane at about the sixth hour and stopped at the mouth of the glass yard and did not go in, and nine men were on a batch and a man of fifty-two was at the end of the bench with his hands behind his back and was not doing anything.** A man of fifty-two stood at the end of a bench in a working yard for about nine minutes and did not do a thing wrong and did not do a thing right, and a man of thirty-four with lime said nothing and put a duckboard down on the stone about four feet from the door, and did it without a word, and a duckboard has been under that stranger's feet in that yard for twelve days.

**And a man of about thirty-one with a satchel was turned round at the road gate at about the seventh hour of the fortieth day, and a man of fifty-five in a hut said the four words and did not raise his voice.** A man of fifty-five has turned round a man of about thirty-one on the last of about nine days of a round in about six years, and the four words were the same four words, and the man in the hut did not add anything to them and has never added anything to them.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp asked the woman of sixty one question in that room at about the sixth hour, and it is the ninth thing he has asked anybody in this city in forty days, and she answered it in about eleven words and did not look up, and a clerk of forty entered two lines on the strength of it and did not read them back.**

“**Will it come round again.**”

“**Everything that is written comes round again. That is not a thing this city has decided and it is not a thing this city can stop, and you have been in this room five times in forty days and you have now asked me two questions and both of them were about paper.**”

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp did not write a refusal that night, and did not write anything, and a man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard has said out loud that a man who has been offered a thing by two people in a room is not in a position to write about it for about a day, and that he said it once, to nobody, with a gather going, and that a man of fifty-two on the other end of the same bench did not look up and did not say anything, and that the two of them have gone up and down the same ladder on the same wall for nineteen years and have never once discussed a thing either of them was in the middle of.**

> *You have said no twice tonight and both of them were yours to say.*
> *There is no figure for what either of them cost and there is not going to be one.*

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was in a room with a long table in it and about two hundred and forty pieces of paper on it, and he looked at the two hundred and forty for about a minute, and about four people in that room have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp looked at a number on a table and then at a man of sixty-one and then at a man of sixty-six, and that the three of those looks are the whole of what he did with two hundred and forty pieces of paper, and that a clerk of forty entered two lines and did not read them back.**

**And the four sheets were in his coat that night, and about four hundred people in this city did not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp had been offered a road on the fortieth day of a count of days that this city keeps, and that he had been in a room at the top of a lane five times in forty days and about nine hundred seconds each time, and that he had not been told a thing in it until the fortieth day, and that on the fortieth day he was told two things, and one of the two was a road and the other one was that a man of sixty-six would not put him in the middle of anything.**

And the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works with a bucket in one hand and came down it and went up it and nobody in this city has ever asked him what the bucket is for, and about four hundred people in this city did not know on the fortieth day that the ninth hour of the fortieth day was the last hour of a count of days that this city keeps, and that nobody in it is keeping except two men and one of the two is sixty-six and the other is sixty-one and neither of the two has said a word to the other in nineteen years except about two books and a road and a fold in a page, and the count in the book under the board is nine and the count in the book on the slope is a count of days and has been for four hundred years, and the two of them are about four inches apart in two different books and have not been compared and are not going to be.
