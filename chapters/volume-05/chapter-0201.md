# Chapter 0201

**The twenty-fifth of Fallowmonth, YR 314, a Monday, and not a market day, and the fourth hour, and the same yard Volume 04 ended in with the same forty men on it and the same salt coming over the same lane, and this is the same day later, and the fourth bay is finished and is being pointed, and the road is open, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is on the gravel at the low end of it with a barrow, and a man of forty-seven on the bench of nine because he was elected is on the deck of the first bay with a hod, and neither of them holds anything.**

The fourth bay of the Sedge Reach was finished on the twenty-second of Harvestmonth and has been pointing ever since, and pointing is the one job on a bridge that is never finished on the day it is begun, and the man who began the pointing on the parapet on the twenty-third of Harvestmonth went home at the fourth hour and the joint was half a foot short at the end of it, and it is a foot and a half short now, and nobody in that yard has measured it and nobody is going to.

There is one lane over the first bay and it is the lane the salt comes in on, and it has been the lane for four hundred years, and a waggon of salt went over it at about the sixth hour and will go over it again at about the sixth hour tomorrow, and the waggon is four hundred yards from the reach and the whole town can hear a cart on either.

And in the third course from the low end of that fourth bay there is a line about nine inches long, and it is under about half an inch of lime, and it is in the stone and not across the joint and not in the joint, and it has not moved and has not grown and has not closed, and a man of fifty and a half pointed over it on the twenty-third of Harvestmonth at the joint and not at the stone, and nobody in that yard has put a rule on it and a few of them in that yard have said that they would not.

**Two hundred yards of reaching work has gone over the top of that line in five weeks and about two hundred men have walked on a deck above it and not one of them has stopped, and the mason who is pointing that parapet said one thing about it at the sixth hour and the one thing was a trade fact and not a finding: he is not cutting a new mark into a parapet that has an unfinished joint in it, and when the joint above the line is done, on a day when there is a day to do it in, this parapet will be the same height as the other three and a mark in it will be a mark in a wall of a bridge in a market town and not a thing on a working face.**

About forty men. A mason of fifty and a half, who has been cutting stone for thirty-one years and who does the arches and does the pointing and does not do the laying. Two men of about twenty-six setting a course, one of them from this town and one of them from nine miles up the channel. A boy of fifteen at the bench-board with a lime tub and a rule and a bill, who copies the empty space under a number as well as the number. A carter of thirty with a barrow and a waggon of his own at the top end. Four men with hods. A man of about nineteen carrying a hod badly, hired in on a Friday. And the man of forty-seven with the hod, and the man of forty-seven is not on a trade and has not been on one since the year before last, and he is on that bench because a man in a market asked who was going and he put his hand up, and he is paid fourpence a week out of a private box and not out of the chest, and he does not hold this reach or the Old Toll or the crossing, and he has not used his vote since the day he was elected and did not use it then.

The boy of fifteen has a lime tub, and the rule, and a bill, and the bill is a piece of deal with a list on it of what came in and what went out and what was used, and the boy copies the empty space under a number as well as the number, and nobody in that yard has ever told him to do it and he has done it every day since the spring and four people in this town have noticed.

He got up onto a deck off the gravel in three moves instead of two, and it is a thing nobody in that yard can see, and on this morning about eleven men saw it and about four of them have said that the third move is not a get-up, it is a put-down, and one of those four is the mason of fifty and a half.

The stick is in his hand. It is a ferrier's stick, ash, four feet nine, with a rubber ferrule, and it was cut out of a fence rail in a county that is not on any map, and he cannot put a bar in a joint with it and he cannot lean on it, because leaning on it puts the weight through the wrong hip, and that is everything it is for, and it is for four hundred yards of road and nothing else.

He carried. That is what he did on that reach for five weeks, about forty mornings a month, with a barrow on a run that goes from the top of the gravel up onto the deck and back, and he was faster at the low end of it than either of the two men of twenty-six, and a few in that yard have said so and the mason of fifty and a half has said it twice without being asked and did not say it to him.

**And he has not counted anything out loud. There is a word for what a deck takes before anybody may stand on it and it is not a barrow and it is not a shovel and it is not a figure of anything a person can see, and he has it and he does not say it, and about four men in that yard have noticed that he does not say it and none of them has asked.**

**And about two hundred yards of reaching work has gone past the fourth bay since the twenty-second of Harvestmonth, and the two men of twenty-six set four courses on this morning before the sixth hour, and the mason's rule came off the string course twice and went on twice, and a course of a height goes into a bed of a height and nothing in that yard wants anything else.**

The reaching work on a bridge is not a thing anybody outside a yard can picture. There is a bed cut for a stone before the stone is cut, and the bed is cut by a man with a pick and a line and not by a mason, because a mason will not cut a bed for a stone he has not seen and the man with the pick is the only man in the yard who will. There is a course laid on the bed and a course laid on that course, and every third course there is a stone through the thickness of the wall from one face to the other with its head out two inches past the face, and nine of them went into the fourth bay in the last fortnight, and nobody in this county has ever counted them and nobody is going to. There is lime put in with a trowel in a joint and gone off with a hammer and a chisel and a bucket, and the joint is left a quarter of an inch short of the face and filled on a wet day, and the day it is filled is never the day the course is laid.

And a reach is not the same thing twice. A man who has stood on a deck every day for a fortnight knows what the fourth bay is doing, and a few in that yard said afterwards that on this morning it did two things at once and both of them stopped for about four minutes and then started again, and nobody in the yard said anything about it, and four of them have said since that they could feel it and as many have said that they could not and had it from the others.

**And the salt came over the lane on the first waggon at the sixth hour and went over it again at the sixth hour, and the salt is loaded at the wharf four hundred yards down and it comes up over the first bay because the first bay is on this town's own road and the salt is the one thing in this town that has to come in, and a couple of people in the salt loft and about nine in the yard have said that nobody in Merefen has ever asked where the salt comes from and that this is a county that buys its salt from a coast a hundred miles off up a river and a road it has never had a survey of.**

The man of forty-seven went up onto the deck of the first bay with a hod at about the fifth hour and worked there until the seventh with a hod and did it well. A man of about nineteen who was hired in on a Friday was carrying a hod badly at the other end of the same deck and had been told so by the boy of fifteen and by nobody else, and he put it down twice in an hour and picked it up both times, and the man of forty-seven worked four feet from him for two hours and said nothing to him at all, and four men in that yard have said since that they did not know he could carry one and as many have said that they did not know it was him, and neither of those two facts is in a minute and neither of them is going to be.

**And at about the seventh hour on that Monday he cut a mark into the outside face of the parapet of the fourth bay, in daylight, in front of about twenty men, at the height of a thumb, on the face that gets the weather, with a cold chisel and not a stamp, and it is a line and a bar and a figure, and there is no name in it.**

He said the reason before anybody asked, and he said it to the yard and not to the twenty, and it took about four sentences, and they were these.

“**A place you come back to has to be a place you can stand in. That is all of it. I am not going to be able to come back to this and nobody is going to know that I was ever on it, and that is not a complaint, because a man who wants to be found has not got a road, he has got a claim, and a claim is a thing a person has to keep saying. And I would like there to be one thing in this town that a man could put his hand on and know that it was him and know that it was not a name.**”

Nobody improved on it. About four of the twenty understood all of it and as many again did not, and the man of about nineteen asked the only question anybody asked, and it was a reasonable one and it was asked badly and he answered it.

“Then who is it for.”

“It is not for anybody,” he said. “**It is for the man who comes after me and finds it, and he will not know who I was, and he will know that somebody stood here and cut a mark in a bridge parapet on a Monday and did not write a name, and that is all there is of it and there is not one shilling in it.**”

Then he cut it. It took about four minutes with a cold chisel and a lump hammer and no line and no set square, and the mason of fifty and a half did not hold anything for him and did not offer to, and a man of forty-seven with a hod stood eleven feet off and watched all of it and did not say one word, and afterwards four men in that yard said that the mark was in the stone at the height of a thumb and the figure in it could not be read from thirty feet, and that a mason's mark can be read from thirty feet, and that this one is not a mason's mark, and that none of the four of them is going to say so out loud twice.

**And that is the second time in this county that a mark has been cut on a public thing with a chisel and a hammer in front of a yard of men, and the first one is a line and a bar and a figure in the coping of the first bay of this reach, cut on the sixth of Goatmonth of the year before last by the same man with the same chisel, and it is four hundred yards from this one and it cannot be read from thirty feet either, and nobody in this county has ever joined the two of them and nobody is going to.**

The last time that reason was given out loud it took a valley's bridge down.

A woman with a yoke and a pail found a mark in the coping of a stand over four feet of water in the ninth week of the third cold and did not tell anybody for a day and a half, and the reason she gave afterwards, in about forty words, in a yard, unasked, was that if nobody else has seen it then you are the one who has got it, and if you tell somebody then it is a thing that two people have seen, and a thing that two people have seen is a thing there will be an account of. And a man of thirty-four said the reason out loud in a room with about eleven people in it, and said beforehand that he would say it again at the ford in front of forty people, and a reason given once in a closed room is an account and a reason given twice in the open is a reason. And then about forty people watched a course of stone come off a coping in the order it came off, and a man of thirty-four stood at the shoulder of the man with the limp and did not explain what for, and a man of sixty-one said that under the water is where a thing goes that you are not going to look at again, and the nine stones went into the footing of a second stand and nothing rebuilt it.

**He said the same four sentences in that yard at the seventh hour on that Monday and about twenty men heard them and half of them have said since that they were the same four sentences, and he did not improve on them and he has not got anything to improve them with, and the difference between the two is the whole difference between that valley and this town and it is not a difference in the four sentences.**

The difference is that in that valley there were about nine hundred doors and eleven words put at them and a figure in chalk on the end of a public bench, and everybody in that valley could check every one of those three things against every other one, and here there is a clerk and a minute and a chest with two marks in it and a man of about sixty who has kept that minute for nineteen years and has never entered a name in it that somebody else in the room did not put there first. So a mark cut in a parapet in this town goes into a stone and a mark cut on a bench in that valley went into a figure, and the figure could be got at by anybody who came up the bank, and the stone is going to be a stone in about two hundred years and the four sentences are going to be in four or five mouths and then in none.

**And nobody in this town is going to take a stone out of a reach in a fortnight over a mark, and nobody in this town is going to hold four of them about it, and a man of forty-seven with a hod was asked afterwards by the man of nineteen whether anybody was going to be able to say in fifty years what the mark was for, and said no, and said that a mark in a coping cannot be read from thirty feet and that is all it has.**

And the man of nineteen said, “Then what is the point of it,” and the man of forty-seven said, “I do not know and I have been asked that twice today and I am not going to have an opinion in a yard,” and the man of about sixty in the yard put his rule away in a bag he has not opened in this county for two years, and nobody in that yard improved on any of it, and the pointing went on over the top of it, and the joint on the parapet is a foot and a half short, and the man who began it went home at the fourth hour on the twenty-third of Harvestmonth and nobody has sent for him.

And the salt had gone over the lane and the tide was under the wharf and the road was open, and the fourth bay was being pointed, and about forty men were on the Sedge Reach in daylight on the twenty-fifth of Fallowmonth, YR 314, a Monday, and not a market day, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was on the gravel at the low end of it with a barrow, and a man of forty-seven on the bench of nine because he was elected was on the deck of the first bay with a hod, and a boy of fifteen was at a bench-board copying the empty space under a number as well as the number, and a mark was in the parapet of the fourth bay at the height of a thumb that nobody in this county can read at thirty feet.

And at the low end of the lane over the first bay there was a child of nine years old, standing on the kerb with a bundle on one shoulder that was not large and did not look as though it had been carried far, and the child had been there at the sixth hour on Saturday and at the sixth hour on this Monday, and about four people in the salt loft and a few in the yard have said since that nobody in this town knows where the child came from, and that the child has not asked anybody for anything, and that the child has not said a word to anybody in this town, and that the child is not hungry and is not cold and does not want to go anywhere in particular.

The waggon of salt went round it twice. A carter of thirty said that a child standing on a kerb where a waggon has to swing is a child somebody has lost, and four people said that and nobody went and asked, and the man of thirty-two with a limp looked down the lane at about the eighth hour and saw the child and did not go down there.

**And the mark was in the parapet of the fourth bay four hundred yards up the reach and the child was at the low end of the lane over the first bay, and nobody joined them on that Monday and nobody is going to, and the bundle on the child's shoulder had a corner of paper out of it and the paper was folded and nobody in that yard could see what was on it and the man of thirty-two with a limp did not go and look.**
