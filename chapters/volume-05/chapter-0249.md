# Chapter 0249

**The twenty-fourth day of Goatmonth, YR 315, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the fourth hour and a half, and a crossing four hundred yards up this county's own road and a hundred yards from the market, a bell with a band of forty-one rivets in it, and a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper, and four hundred people in this town who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at that crossing for half an hour and then went up two flights of a stair over a wharf and asked a clerk of about sixty what a bench of nine had decided about it, and was told that it had decided nothing, and did not speak.**

The crossing is in municipal trust and the trust has been in abeyance since before the bench existed. The tolls on it are suspended by an instrument a court has found lawful and has said it has nothing further to say about it at any time, and so the crossing has no owner and no money and no seat on that bench and no glass in the sill and no third seat. A dressed block is in the abutment and forty yards of tarred hemp went into it on the fourth of Hearthmonth of the year before by four men and a boy, and the hemp has not been looked at by anybody in this county since the day it went in, a man who has done work on a thing is allowed to hear what a room has decided about it, and the man who put it in has not been in that room in a year and a half.

The bell is nine stone of green-black bronze with a dull red seam in the metal where the band was closed, and it is chest-high and split lip to shoulder, and it is rung because that is the arrangement. The arrangement has been a boy and a carpenter since the ninth of Hearthmonth, and the boy has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is still not good at it, and the carpenter is a hand and not a keeper and has a bar he is not using. Neither of the two of them is paid by the guild and neither of the two of them is a member of anything, and four people in this county have said since that a bell rung by a person who is not paid for it and a person who is not a member of it is a bell nobody in this county can be asked about, and that is why it is rung.

**And the man who put the hemp in stood nine feet off the abutment and did not go up, and the hemp is under the dressed block and four feet down and nobody in this county has ever dug for it.**

He had a stick and he could not lean on it, because leaning on it puts the weight through the wrong hip, and he stood with it in his right hand and his left hand hanging at his side and he did not put his left hand on the dressed block. A man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that a man who puts a hand flat on a thing is not asking the thing anything and is telling it something, and that he said it once and has not said it since, and a man of thirty-four with lime has not been on the reach four hundred yards down the lane for a year and has not been asked about it by anybody in this county.

**And the sill has no glass in it and there is no third seat, and the stone either side of that sill is dressed to the same line, and four people in this county have said that a sill with no glass in it is a sill anybody may put a hand on, that nobody in this county has ever put a hand on that one, and that the man of thirty-two with a limp stood nine feet off it for half an hour on the twenty-fourth day of Goatmonth, YR 315 and did not put a hand on it either, and has not, and is not going to.**

**And the crossing carries nobody, and it has carried nobody for four years, and four people in this county have said that a crossing that carries nobody and a bridge four hundred yards the other way that carries everything are four hundred yards apart and a hundred yards from a market, and that this county has one of each and has never put the two of them in the same book.**

Nobody noticed the not putting it down. A man of about nineteen with an empty hod went past on the channel road, and a carter of thirty went past with a barrow, and four people in that lane have said that a man of about nineteen who walks past a crossing with a hod and does not look at the sill has looked at that sill before and has not got anything out of it and has not told anybody that he has looked.

---

**And the bell went at the fourth hour and a half and it went the way it goes every working day of this county, and nobody within four hundred yards of it was asked anything by it and nobody in this county is rung at by it.**

The boy had one end of a line and the carpenter stood beside him with the bar he was not using, and the line came down and the bar came up and the note is the same note it has been since the ninth of Hearthmonth, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has heard it four times in a year in three counties and has never once been in the room where it was decided that it should be a boy and a carpenter, because there is no such room, and a man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that a thing nobody can produce a minute for cannot be asked about, and that is not a fault in it.

**And a waggon of stone came up the channel road at about the fifth hour and did not stop at that crossing, and a second one came up at the sixth hour and did not stop either, and the driver of the second one got down and looked at the crossing for about two minutes and got back up and said nothing to anybody.**

The channel road was not a market road before a bridge was open over a reach, and nine waggons have come up it since the spring, the county has never asked whether it wants the stone and has never voted on it and would not know what to do with a vote, and four people in that lane have said that a driver who looks at a crossing for two minutes and says nothing is a man with a list, and that the list is a list of doors and not a list of waggons, and that a crossing with no owner and no money has no way whatever of knowing how many went over it in a day, and that about four hundred doors on one of those lists are about four hundred doors on another one in a county four hundred miles off, and that nobody in any of the four places has ever worked out how many, and that the two men holding the two lists have never met.

A carter of thirty came back down the lane at about the seventh hour with a barrow and stopped, which he does not do, and said one thing, and it was about eleven words, and he said it to the ground.

“**Two waggons and neither of them stopped, and I have been on that road since the spring and I have never once seen one stop at a thing that charges nothing, and I would not mind if it did, and I am not going to say that to anybody either.**”

Nobody asked him what he meant and he did not say, and four people in that lane have said that a carter of thirty has now said two things out loud in two days that neither of them was asked for, that both were about the same road from opposite ends, that the county has a bench that says no in eleven words and a carter who says nothing twice, and that neither of them has ever been in a room with the other one.

---

**And then he went into a town of about four thousand people and up two flights of a stair to a long room over a wharf, and the bench of nine was not sitting because a bench of nine sits on Saturdays, and a clerk of about sixty was in the room because a clerk of about sixty is in that room on a Wednesday.**

The clerk has kept that minute for nineteen years and is not a member and has never been asked to be. There is a chest under the window and the chest has had two marks in it since the ninth of Fallowmonth in the year before last, and the two marks are the only figures in that room, and the clerk said so once, to a man who asked about a joint on a parapet four hundred yards down a lane, and said that a clerk cannot measure a thing. A fair copy goes on a board under the bench where anybody coming up two flights may read it, and there are about four names in a subscription book on a shelf and two of the four are the same man twice, and the crossing has been asked about four times in nineteen years and answered four times and the four answers are in that book, and the book is not a minute and cannot be got out of and is not got out of.

“What has the bench decided about the Old Toll.”

That is about eleven words and it was the only thing he said in that room in about a year and a half, and a clerk of about sixty read it back and did not improve it, and then he said what there was, which is what he has said to four people in nineteen years when anybody has asked him a question about a thing in that room.

“**Nothing. That crossing has been in the book four times and the four times are a toll and not a road, and the bench has never been asked about the road and I have never entered that it has not been asked, because a clerk who enters an absence is a clerk who has entered a decision, and nobody in that hall has ever taken a decision in writing.**”

**And the four times that crossing has been asked about are on a shelf in that room in a book that is not a minute, and there are four names in it, and two of the four are the same man twice.**

The tolls have been suspended since before the bench existed and a crossing with no tolls is asked about by people who have an interest in a toll and not by the county, and four times in nineteen years somebody in this county has put a name to a subscription at a market in favour of putting a toll back on it, and a reeve of this town has taken four names at a market four times a year for nine years and knows the figure and the men do not, and about four people in that hall have said that a book with four names in it where two of the four are the same man twice is a book nobody can be got out of and is not got out of, and that the two marks in the chest under the window are the only figures in that room and neither of them is one of the four names.

A clerk of about sixty said one thing about that book when it was asked for, in about nine words, and he has said it once in nineteen years and does not intend to say it again, and what he said is that a name in a subscription is an offer and an offer is not a minute, and that he has entered no minutes of offers in his life and is not going to start on a Wednesday, and about four people in that hall have said that a clerk who has kept a minute for nineteen years and has never once entered an offer has done the harder of the two jobs and has never been thanked for it and has not asked.

**And on the board under the bench in that room there is a fair copy that anybody coming up two flights may read, and the count the reeve of this town asked for on the sixth day of that month is on a page in the same book, a clerk of about sixty entered it and read it back and did not change it.**

The count is five and one and one and one and one, and it is eight things asked of that bench in nineteen years, and four of the eight were answered yes and three of the eight were refused for a reason and one of the eight was brought up two flights and not put, and none of the five figures is a figure of persons and none of them has ever been added to a figure of persons in that room or in any other, and four people in that hall have said that a bench which has been asked eight things and has answered four of them has not kept a count of anything except its own occasions.

Nobody was in the room. A man of thirty-two with a limp stood in a long room over a wharf on the evening of the twenty-fourth day of Goatmonth, YR 315 and was told by a clerk that a bench of nine has never been asked, and he did not speak.

**And he did not speak, and a man who has done work on a thing is allowed to hear what a room has decided about it, and he had not come to speak and he did not, and four people in that hall have said since that the only man in this county entitled to speak about that crossing is a man entitled to be there and not to speak, and that he has used the entitlement twice in a year and a half and has said nothing either time.**

The clerk entered nothing. That is the first time in nineteen years that a man has come into that room, asked a question about a thing in it, and gone out with nothing entered, and the clerk has entered a fever once by entering nothing about it and has not entered anything since, and a second time in nineteen years is not a habit and is not a rule and nobody in that hall has written either of those two words down.

---

**And the two marks in the chest under the window have not moved, and the tide was under the wharf, and the fourth bay of a reach four hundred yards down a lane was still a foot and a half short on the parapet and nine inches of pointing on it had been carried into a second day, and four hundred people in a town of four thousand do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp put forty yards of tarred hemp into a crossing four hundred yards up this county's own road on the fourth of Hearthmonth of the year before, and that he has been in the same county for that whole time and has not gone near it, and that a bell with forty-one rivets in it went over that lane at the fourth hour and a half on the twenty-fourth day of Goatmonth, YR 315 by two people who are not paid for it and are not members of it, and that nothing about that bell or that crossing is his and has never been his and is not going to be.**
