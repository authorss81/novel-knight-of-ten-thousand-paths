# Chapter 0238

**The fortieth day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the ninth hour, and nine hundred feet of paving between a quay and a lane with a kerb on each side of it and a drain under the edge, and a child of nine years old at the low end of that paving with a bundle on one shoulder, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick about eleven feet from it who had come out of a room at the top of a lane about an hour before with four sheets of paper in a coat, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that a child of nine years old has not gone out through a gate on the fortieth day, and four of them know that it has not gone out on purpose, and none of the four has said so to anybody.**

It had not gone out on the thirty-first or the thirty-second or the thirty-third. It had gone out on the thirty-fourth and the thirty-sixth and the thirty-eighth, and three days together is a thing that has happened about once in four years, and a woman of twenty-eight folded a strip of paper through all three of them and did not say one word about any of them, and nobody in that room has ever been told the arrangement and nobody in that city could tell them.

Then it came down the lane at about the ninth hour and stopped at the low end of the paving and put its bundle down on the kerb, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was about eleven feet off and did not move and did not say anything and did not put his hand anywhere.

It looked at him. It looked at him for about as long as it takes to count nine, the way it has looked at him on four other days in forty, and then it looked at his left hand, which was hanging at his side, and he did not put it behind him and did not open it.

**And the child put its left hand flat on the kerb, at about the middle of it, on the cold stone, in the way anybody puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the stone was cold on the other side of its hand too, and about a minute and a half later a man of thirty-two with a limp felt everything about a thing he has been building for four years stop being something he was holding.**

---

**And before that it had been an ordinary hour of an ordinary day in a city of about nine thousand people.** It had carried a hod eleven times in a glass yard, and eaten a piece of bread off a sill, and walked four hundred yards of lane twice, and stood in a market on about two hundred yards of street where a few hundreds of people were and bought nothing. It had been in a room off a lane and put a mark in the corner of a strip of paper about a foot long in about a second and a half. There is no ceremony in any part of that and nobody in nine thousand people knows that any of it did not happen. A man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works with a bucket in one hand and his other hand on the top of it. A man of fifty-five in a hut with three bars looked at a child of nine years old come past his barrier and did not look at it, and did not ask it anything, and has not counted the tenth and has not counted the tenth since he came into that hut.

**And a man of about forty at a winch stopped winding for the length of that minute and a half and did not look round.** He has been at that end of a winch for twenty-two years and has never once stopped for anything, and he has not worked out what he stopped for and has not been asked and would not answer if he were. A man of fifty-five in a hut three hundred yards off did not write down the one that did not go out either.

A child of nine years old took its hand off the stone and picked its bundle up off the kerb and went down the lane. It did not go out through a gate. It did not say anything to anybody, and nobody in that lane said anything to it. About nine thousand people in this city went on doing what they had done at the ninth hour, and the oven was hot at the ninth and had been hot at the second.

And the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days.

---

**And the eighth of Hearthmonth, YR 315, a Saturday, and a market morning, and at first light there is a crossing with no glass in its sill four hundred yards up a road out of a town of about four thousand people, and the tide was out, and the salt was not coming, and about four people were standing at it, and they had not been asked to be there, and they have said since that they were the four who would have gone out and not the four who would have been sent.**

It is the second time in a year the same four have stood on that abutment. The first of the four is a man of forty-seven who is on a bench of nine because a man in a market asked who was going and he put his hand up, and he is not a road warden and there is one and he is not it, and he has been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and is paid fourpence a week out of a private box and not out of the chest, and has not used his vote since the day he was elected and did not use it then.

The second is a carter of thirty with a barrow, who put the barrow down at the top of the bank on the eighth of Embermonth in the year before and did not get up it.

The third is a man of about nineteen who had been hired in on a Friday in a month two hundred miles of road away and had not been told that he was on the roll of anything.

The fourth is a boy of fifteen with a lime tub and a rule and a bill, and there is no bill on this morning and there has been no bill on this morning for about two months, and he has the bill under his arm and is not going to be given one, and it is not the bill he is waiting for.

**And then a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the low end of the sill, and he was on his feet, and there is no line in the coping on either side of that sill and there is nothing on it and it was nothing before and it is nothing after, and nobody in that county can see it and nobody in that county has ever been able to see it, and a man of thirty-four with lime said so once about nine inches of lime in a stone course on the fourth bay of a reach four hundred yards up the road and was not asked about it again.**

He had four sheets of paper in a coat and a stick he cannot lean on and a page with a seventh line on it that says a person and eleven lines in a notebook in the same coat, and he could not get up the bank. That is the first fact about that morning, and a carter of thirty did not go down the bank either, and the two of them have both done that on the same morning and neither of them said anything to the other about it.

The man of forty-seven said the four things out loud at about the fourth hour and said them again because a witness repeats them, and it is the third time in a year he has said the four things on a bridge, and he said them twice on the eighth of Embermonth in the year before and twice again on the eighth of Hearthmonth in the year after, and four people in that county have worked out that the repetition is not a formality with him and four hundred have not.

Then he said the other thing, and it is about nineteen words, and it is the third time in about a year he has said a thing on a bridge to people he was not sent to, and four of them have it word for word and as many again have said that they were not close enough and have not asked since.

“**I have been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and I want the hour said, and I am going to say the other thing as well and I have never once had the chance to say it. There was no line in this. I have stood at two ends of two crossings and there was a line in both of them and a day in both of them and two hands in both of them, and there is nothing in this one at all, and I am the witness at this end and I have not been asked to be and I am not a road warden, and I am going to have the whole of that said in whatever anybody writes down, and there is going to be nothing in it.**”

Then he said the hour, and he said it twice, and he said the second one louder, and on this morning there were two hours, one at this end and one four hundred miles off at a place where about nine thousand people keep a count of days and no months, and nobody in that county has been able to find out the other one and is not going to.

And a man of about nineteen was told by the boy of fifteen to hold a hod and held it for about an hour and did not know why and did not ask, and he is going to be a foreman before he is thirty and everybody in that yard knows it and nobody is going to say it to him. A carter of thirty took his barrow up four hundred yards of lane with nine sacks of salt on it and did not stop at the crossing and did not look at either end of it.

**And a bench-board four hundred yards up that road had a bill on it and a boy of fifteen was not at it.** A man of thirty-two with a limp is four hundred yards from that board and was not at it and has not been at it since, and nobody in that county has said anything to anybody about the two of them, and a clerk of about sixty has kept that minute for nineteen years and has never entered a name in it that somebody else in the room did not put there first.

**And a woman of about thirty with a pail was going to a door two hundred yards from the crossing, and she is the woman who has told a stranger the whole of the post four times in years, and she did not stop and she did not look at the crossing, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that the woman carrying water to a door on that market morning was two hundred yards from a man standing at a sill.** A man of thirty-two who sells names for a penny was at a corner of it and did not go to anybody, which he has not done in about four years.

**And four hundred yards up that road a toll house has no glass in its sill and no third seat, and a dressed block, and forty yards of tarred hemp, and no owner and no money, and a man of fifty-five in a hut at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards off it has been in that hut nineteen years and has turned round about four hundred people and has let about nine thousand through, and the four words are the same four words as the ones at a gate on a bridge four hundred miles off, and nobody in either county has ever compared them.**

**And a market on two hundred yards of street was four hundred yards off and open, and a man of thirty-two who sells names for a penny was at a corner of it with nothing in front of him, and a man of fifty with a waggon was unloading salt off a cart, and a woman of about thirty with a pail was going to a door.** None of them was told anything and none of them has been told anything since, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that the man with the stick came back to this county on that morning at all, and nine of them have worked out that something happened at a crossing and cannot say what.

**And the fourth bay of the reach was being pointed and about forty men had been on it before the light and were on it again at the ninth hour, and a man of thirty-four with lime is not on that reach and has not been for a year, and a boy of fifteen was on it and had a bill under his arm and did not write anything, and about four people in that county have said since that a reach with forty men on it went on working all morning on the eighth of Hearthmonth, YR 315 while a man came out of a county four hundred miles off at the other end of a thing nobody in that county can see.**

And the road out of that county was open at about the fifth hour and a half of that morning, and the bell was rung at the fourth hour and a half by a boy and a carpenter and not by anybody else, and the fourth bay of the reach was being pointed and the joint on its parapet is a foot and a half short, and there is a line in the third course of that bay about nine inches long under about half an inch of lime that has not moved, and a mark in the coping of the first bay and a mark in the parapet of the fourth bay that cannot be read at thirty feet by anybody in that county, and the market was four hundred yards long and open, and the salt was coming over the first bay of the reach and would be coming over it again on the next market morning.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp got up the bank in about nine steps instead of one, and did not say anything about the nine, and none of the four said a word about it, and one of the four is a man of forty-seven who is paid fourpence a week, and that he has never once in about a year said a word to that man about how he gets up a bank, and that he was asked about it once by a carter of thirty and that he said the same four things.**
