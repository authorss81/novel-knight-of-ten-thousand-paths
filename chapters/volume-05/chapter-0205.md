# Chapter 0205

**Two hundred miles off, in a provincial town, on the third of Embermonth, YR 314, a Tuesday, and not a market day in this county and not a market day in that one either, and the fourth hour, and the Office of Provincial Registration had a room with a stove in it and a woman of thirty-three in the room who was an aid to the register and was not a member of anything and had never been a member of anything, and nobody in that room is the person this case is about and nothing in that room knew that anything had happened four hundred miles from it in a fen town with a bridge over a channel.**

The room is one of eleven in that office and it is the room with the forms in it. The forms are stacked in a press and the press has a lock and the lock is opened at the first hour by a man of about fifty who is a clerk of the press and is not a member of anything, and there are seven presses in that office across four floors and four of them are the forms and five of them are the returns, and the returns are the ones that come back.

**And the whole trade of that room is the *Keeper* column, and the *Keeper* column is a column that asks for a person, and it is the fourth column of five on a form ruled in the office's own house and printed about two years ago, and the columns are *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper*, and *How often*, and there is a sixth line at the foot that has no head to it, and the form does not ask where.**

The woman of thirty-three has filled in that column about two hundred times. She has been in the office nine years and she was eighteen when she came to it, and there is nobody in that office who taught her to fill it in and she has never asked anybody how to fill it in, and a handful of them in that building have said since that she is the best hand in the office at that column and that the two of those things are about the same fact.

You fill it in from the district. There is a keeper for a district and the keeper is a person and the person is whoever is on the roll of the district, and when the person goes out of the district the obligation goes with them and the name stays in the column, and the column does not get emptied, and the column is not the same column as the one the name is on, and the office does not have to know how the two of them are joined because the column is a column and the roll is a roll and the office holds both.

**And that is the whole mechanism, and it is a practice and not a law, and nobody in that building has ever explained it to anybody, and it is printed in nine words in a small type at the foot of the form, and a woman of thirty-three has read those nine words about two hundred times and has said out loud, in a room, twice in nine years, that she does not know what they are for and is not going to find out.**

---

**And there is a file on the second shelf of that room, and in the file there is a sheet of good paper about the size of a broadside, and it went in the file five months ago, and nobody in that office has read it since it went in, and four people have said that a file is for that and one clerk has said that it is not and would not say why.**

The sheet is this office's own. It is a precedent in the office's house and it went round to nine counties in the first quarter of the year, and it asks each of them for a figure of persons in respect of continuing obligations, and it says that the figure is required for the register and that the figure is not to be given by name, and there is a space for the figure at the foot of it and a line for the county.

Eight of the nine counties sent a figure. This one did not.

**And that is not all the sheet says, and all the sheet says is the thing that has made this office into a thing a reader is afraid of, and it is that the figure asked for is the figure of persons and not the figure of names, and a name entered against the return is not returned to the sender and is kept by the office. Nine counties sent a figure. This one sent back a form with the figure space empty and a sheet of four lines with it, and the four lines say that a figure of persons is not a thing this county holds, and the four lines are correct, and the clerk in the press has read the four lines twice and has put them in a file of their own, and some of them in that building have said that the four lines are the cleverest thing any of them has read in the time they have been in that building and that the office does not have a way of answering them and is not going to get one.**

The return came back on the ordinary post in the second week of Embermonth. It is in the tray by the stove and it has been in the tray by the stove for one day, and it is a return, and returns go in the returns press, and it has not gone in the returns press because a woman of thirty-three read the four lines on the separate sheet and then went and stood at the tray for about a minute and a half and then sat down again.

---

**And in the tray by that stove there were eleven other things, and one of them is a refusal in four sheets in a hand two people in that building can recognise, and it came in the ordinary post eleven days ago from a county two hundred miles from that town, and it has not been read, and it is going to stay unread, and a document in a tray in a provincial town is in no county's books at all, and nobody in that room has any idea what is in it, and about four people in that building who were not in the room have said since that there is a thing in the tray that has been in it eleven days.**

Nobody opened it. It is four sheets and it is dated and there is an hour on it as a second thing with a rule between them, which is not how dates are written in that office, and two or three of them in that building have said that whoever wrote it took a great deal of trouble over it and that the trouble is visible from the top of the stairs, and none of them has opened it and none of them is going to and the reason is that it is not addressed to anybody in that building.

**It is addressed to a county. It came out of a county. And a document that comes out of a county and goes into a tray in an office that did not write it is a document that nobody in that room has any business opening, and there is no rule about it and there does not need to be one, because the rule that does the work is that nobody opens the post in an office that is not the office the post is for, and that rule is about four hundred years old and it has never once been written down.**

---

---

**And that office asks for a figure of persons twice in this year and has printed one in eleven years, and about four people in that building have said that this is not an accident and that it has a name in it that nobody uses, and that the name is a return, and that a return is a thing that goes back.**

The office's own returns carry a figure of obligations. They have carried a figure of obligations for as long as anybody in that building can remember and they carry one now and there are nine counties in the second press with a figure of obligations against each of them, and a figure of obligations is a count of things and a count of things is the only kind of paper that cannot be refused by a person, because a person can refuse a thing and cannot refuse an arithmetic.

A figure of persons is a different kind of paper. A figure of persons can be refused by anybody who is inside it and outside it at the same time, and the second column of the form is a refusal entire, and the second column was put there by an office that has been asking for persons for longer than this building has been a building, and the office cannot say whose figure the second column is.

**So the office asks. It asks twice in a year and it will not print one, and it has never once explained that to a county, and eleven counties have sent one, and eight of the nine did not know they were doing anything the ninth did.**

And the precedent in the file on the second shelf, the good paper in the office's house that went round in the first quarter of the year, asks for the figure in a line and a half, and the line and a half begins *that the office be informed of the number of persons to whom any continuing obligation is owed in the district*, and it goes on to say that the number is required for the register and is not to be given by name, and it does not say what the register is and there is no place on the sheet to say it.

---

**And in a drawer in that office there are four sheets of good paper about the size of a broadside that this office wrote, and they are the fourth of a series, and the first three went to three other counties and none of them has been answered, and about four people in that building have said that the three counties and this one are the four that had no county of their own in the register and that the office has been patient about it for four years.**

The terms are these, and they are printed, and the office does not send them in a hurry.

That a hand be given to a man in this county, being a road warden with a vote and not an office, and a room, and a house, and an allowance of four shillings the week, and a clerk.

That the eastern paths be closed by the man so appointed, and by nobody else, and for so long as he holds the appointment, and that no other office is to be given to the man so appointed for the term of the appointment.

That a county be entered in the register of the office making the appointment, by number and not by name, and that the number be not given to the man so appointed.

That no question be asked of the man so appointed about the object of the appointment, in that county or in this one, at any time, by anybody.

**And there is no number on the sheet. The number is not on the sheet and was never on the sheet, and the sheet asks for it, and the office has asked for it in a return, and the return did not come, and the office has asked for it a second time, and a couple of them in that building have said that an office which will not print a number of persons cannot show that any person has not been instructed, and that nobody in that building has ever had to prove that anybody had not been instructed, and that it is the most expensive sentence anyone in that building has ever said and that it was said in a county two hundred miles off and not here.**

And no part of the sheet describes what the paths are, and nobody in that office has ever written down what the paths are, and a few in that building who have read all four of the sheets have said that they do not know, and one of them is a man of about fifty who is a clerk of the press and who said, in a room, in the words he used, that he would rather not know, and that wanting not to know is a thing he is not going to put in a book.

---

**And Iselde Senn, who is Chancellor of the realm and who has been in that building twice in nine years, came into that room at about the fourth hour on that Tuesday, and asked one question, and it was the same question she had asked in that office once before, and she was in the room about four minutes, and a few of the eleven people in it did not know who she was, and one of them was the woman who fills in the *Keeper* column.**

“Is the second asking gone out.”

“It went on the last of the month before last, my lady, to nine counties and to that one in particular, and there is a fair copy of it in the second press and a list of the counties, and I can put the list in front of you in about a minute.”

“Thank you. You need not put it in front of me. **I want a day put in the book against the citation and not against the asking, and I have wanted that since the year of the fever in the third of the year after the Sundering, and the reason I want it is that a precedent set in a year of need is not a precedent until it has stood in a year of no need, and this office writes every instrument it sends to stand, and there is not one instrument this office has issued that has not been cited in a year with no emergency in it, and in the eleventh of those the citing office had no need at all and did not notice, and that is the correct behaviour of an office and it is not a fault of one and it is not a defence of one either. I would like a day in the book so that a man in a year with nothing wrong can see there was something wrong when it was made. Do you keep days.”**

“No, my lady. We keep the year and the county and the figure and the column.”

“**Then you are citing an instrument in the eleventh year that has nothing in it to say what year it belongs to, and the day you stop doing that is the day a piece of paper made in a bad winter becomes the ordinary way this kingdom is written, and I would like it entered that I have said so twice in two offices and that the second time was to a woman who had not asked me anything.**”

And she went out, and the clerk of the press said afterwards that she had come in about four minutes and had not sat down, and that a few in that room had gone on working and one had not, and that the one who had not was the woman who fills in the column, and that she had gone on working in about a minute and a half and had not said one word about it and had written a figure in a column in a fair hand.

---

**And she is frightened of the column, and she has been frightened of it for eleven years, and she has never told anybody in that building that, and four people who know her have each said a different part of it and none of the four parts is all of it.**

The fear is not of the column. It is of the district. A district is a Crown's district and a district has a roll and the roll has names on it, and the column asks which of the names on the roll holds an obligation in respect of that district, and a person on a roll in nine counties is somebody somebody has already decided about once and a column in this office is a second decision about them that they are not in the room for, and about two hundred times she has put a name in it and about two hundred times the name on the roll had not been in the room and had not been asked.

Her want is not about the man in the fen county and she has not been told there is a man in a fen county. Her want, and she has said it out loud in a room of two once, to a man of about fifty who is a clerk of the press and has not repeated it, is that **no family in this country is ever again removed from a record by a name that nobody entered.** That is the sentence and it is nine years old and she has not improved on it and she has not had anybody improve on it, and there is a strip of cut paper in a press in a county two hundred miles off with four hundred and eleven words in it in her hand and cut along the left edge with a knife, and the cutting is this office's own method, and she wrote it two years ago, and nobody in this room has ever mentioned it to her.

And the thing she is doing on the third of Embermonth, YR 314, is putting a county's answer into a file, and a county has answered, and the answer is a form with an empty space in it and four lines beside it, and she is going to enter the return in the returns press with the day on it and the county on it, and the *Keeper* column of that return is going to be filled in by her hand about eleven weeks from now when the district's roll is next sent down, and she does not know what is going in it and neither does anybody else in that building, and she fills in that column about two hundred times a year and has never once known a name before she wrote it.

**And the tray by the stove had a refusal in it in four sheets with an hour on it as a second thing, and the file on the second shelf had a precedent in it in good paper that had gone to nine counties and had been read in none of the nine and had been answered by eight, and the return that was asked for and did not come was going to arrive in about four days on the ordinary post in a bag with a carrier's hand on it, and nobody in that room knew that a man in a fen town had put an empty space in a form on purpose, and the woman who had put the empty space in was four hundred miles away and had said out loud two days before that an office which will not print a number of persons cannot show that any person has not been instructed, and the two of those facts are two hundred miles apart and there is no instrument in this kingdom that can make either of them answer a question that is not one, and nothing was decided in that room on that Tuesday and nobody in that room understood anything at all.**
