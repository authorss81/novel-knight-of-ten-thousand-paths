# Chapter 0236

**The thirty-ninth day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the fourth hour, and a yard at the bottom of a lane past the works and round by a bindle store with a roof on two sides and no roof on the other two and about two hundred bundles of cord on racks, and a man of sixty-six at a bench under the roof with a rule nine inches long and a strip of whalebone and a pot of the thinnest glue and a sheet of printed paper about the size of a broadside with four places in it ruled for a figure, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the mouth of that yard who came down it at about the fourth hour and has not come in, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that a man of sixty-six is at a bench making something he has refused to make for twenty years.**

He did it in four minutes. A man of thirty-two with a limp watched every minute of it from the mouth of the yard, and this is the first time in thirty-nine days a man in this city has done a thing in front of a stranger on purpose, and a man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard has said out loud that a man who is doing a thing on purpose for a man with a stick is not showing off and is getting it right, and that nine men in a working yard can tell the difference, and that he said it once.

The paper is the thinnest that can be had in that city and it is about a foot long and it is ruled in five lines, and the four lines are not for a person and the fifth one is empty when it is finished. He wrote with a pen that had been cut down and not with a quill, and he ruled it with the same rule and the same hand and the same ink. The fourth of the five lines on this one is the same width as the fourth of the five lines on the one he made for a man with a stick on the ninth day of this count of days, and the two of them are about nine inches apart on two benches in two yards four hundred yards apart and neither man has ever seen the other's.

Then he wrote nine words and turned the paper round and did not show it to anybody. A man of sixty-six turned a paper round in his own yard on the thirty-ninth day and put it face down on the bench instead of holding it out, and he has held one out exactly once in twenty years, on the ninth day of this count, to a man with a stick, and that man was not here today and was not told.

**“We do not count what we will not name,”** said the man of thirty-two with a limp, who had come into the yard at about the fourth hour and half, nine feet off, and had read it upside down.

“**That is nine words.**”

“That is nine words,” said Dermit Crane, “**and about four hundred of the eleven-word things are not eleven, and I said in this yard on the sixth day of this count that every one of them is eleven and not one of them is twelve, and I was wrong by a number and I have been wrong by it for twenty years, and I found it out on the thirty-eighth day while I was not doing anything and I am not going to lengthen a no to make it match a shelf.**”

Nobody improved on it. And a man of thirty-two with a limp in a yard in a city of about nine thousand people had been told by four people in that city in thirty-nine days that a form is a document and that a document is a thing a court can do something with, and had been told by a man of fifty-two that he was the same as a piece of paper, and had been handed a strip of paper on the thirty-first day and had lost the thread of it inside nine minutes and had not been able to hold the number of lines on it long enough to check it, and had come down four hundred yards of lane to watch a man of sixty-six do four minutes of work he has watched him do once before, and had lost the thread of it at about the second minute and had got it back at the fourth and had not told anybody either of those two things.

**And he said the other thing while he was ruling the second one, and it is about eleven words, and it is the fourth thing he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper, and a man of thirty-two with a limp heard all of it and has not written it down and is not going to.**

“**I have been asked nine times in nineteen years and I have said no nine times and I have said it in the same words nine times, and I have wanted somebody to come back and ask me a different question for about eleven years and nobody ever has, and I am not complaining and I am not going to be.**”

And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in a yard in a city of about nine thousand people and heard a man of sixty-six say that, and did not answer it, and could not think of a question to put to it. A man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard has said out loud that there are two kinds of silence in a working yard and that a man who has nothing to say to a true thing is the second kind and not the first, and that he said it once and has not said it since.

---

**And then he went to the far end of his own yard where the book was, and the board was off it, and he turned the book round.** A man of sixty-six has turned that book round in front of another person twice in twenty years, the first time on the nineteenth day of this count in front of a child of nine years old, and he did not write anything then.

There is a ruled space at the top of that page and a line ruled across the top of the space, and the space is the same width as every other space in the book, and it is the right size and it is the wrong shape, and a man of thirty-two with a limp looked at it and understood at once that there is no column in the world that this city has ever made or answered for a person, and that the man of sixty-six has not written one in the space at the top of the page and is not going to.

He did not write in it. He turned a page and he wrote a line, and a man of thirty-two with a limp watched the whole of it, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that a count went up in this city for the first time in a count of days that anybody in it is keeping.

Then he said the thing, and it is about nineteen words, and it is the fifth thing he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper, and a man of about thirty-one with a satchel who was at the mouth of that yard at about the sixth hour and had come up the rise that morning heard the whole of it and has not improved on it and has not written it down.

“**That is the ninth line in this book and it is not the ninth thing this city has said no to, and those are two figures and I am not going to put them in one sentence, and neither of them is how many of us there are.**”

“**Say the other one,**” said the man of thirty-one.

“**No. The book says nine and my head says eleven and I have had both of those numbers since the twenty-ninth day and I have not added them and I am not going to, and a refusal count is a count of things this city has refused and not a count of people, and I have said that sentence in this yard twice and I am not going to say it a third time.**”

Nobody improved on it. And a man of thirty-two with a limp worked it out, standing nine feet off a bench in a yard, that eight in a book and a child asked twice was ten, and that the printed sheet that went out of this city on the thirtieth of this count had four tens on it, and that the four places on the sheet in front of the man of sixty-six were empty and would go out empty, and that there were now three figures in three places and that the man of sixty-six had just refused out loud to put two of them in one sentence and had not said a word about the third, and that none of the three was a figure of people.

---

**And a boy of fourteen was in that yard at about the sixth hour and half.** He has the names of eleven places and has been to none of them, and he came in and looked at a printed sheet on a bench and at a strip of paper face down on the same bench and did not ask about either, and he worked out in about a second that they were not the same kind of paper and did not say so. He has said since that a man who looks at two things that are not the same and does not ask is a man who already knows that asking costs, and that he said it once, to a man of fifty with a waggon, and did not improve on it.

Then a man of about thirty-one came down four hundred yards of lane and put his satchel on the bench and took the sheet out of it and looked at the four places in it and did not look at anything else on it. A man of fifty with a waggon has said out loud that a man does not look at the column he cannot read, and that he said it once, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp was nine hundred feet off at the time and has not been told and was not asked.

**And a woman of twenty-eight carried the bundle down to the gate and came back and folded for about an hour and did not ask what the nine words were.** She folded about forty strips in that hour while a man of sixty-six wrote a line in a book under a board and did not say one word to anybody. She has been turning people back at three gates for four years, and folding is what she does when she cannot get a thing to go out, and nobody in that room has ever asked her that and nobody is going to.

**And a man of fifty with a waggon asked the man of about thirty-one one question at about the seventh hour and a half and got a straight answer, and it is the only question anybody in that yard asked about the four places in twenty years, and it is about nine words.**

“**Is it the same sheet.**”

“**It is the same series and it is the fourth one of that series I have carried into this city and the second one of the four has been out of it twice.**”

**And the nine words went flat into a satchel at about the seventh hour with the sheet, and the four places on the sheet were empty, and the object column had a place name and a word in it, and the sixth column had no head on it and the same width as the other five.** The first printed thing that has gone out of this city in a count of days this man can remember went out with four of the same figure in it on the twenty-fourth, and the second went out with four tens in it on the thirtieth, and this one went out with nothing in the four places at all. A man of about thirty-one has carried two of those and will carry this one and has not been told what is in the four places on any of them and has not asked.

He said one more thing at the gate of his own yard, in about eleven words, to a man of about thirty-one and to a man of thirty-two with a limp, and then he sat down and picked up the rule.

“**You are going to want to say to a room that a door declined, and a room has never once had that from anybody and it is not going to have it from this city, and a strip of paper with nine words on it is not a refusal anybody can put in a hand and keep, and that is the correct way round and not the other way round.**”

**And the yard was quiet for about four minutes after the satchel went.** The whole of the thirty-ninth day of this count of days was made in a room of the size of a cart and a bench under a roof, and a strip of paper about a foot long went into it flat with nine words on it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was nine hundred feet off for the whole of it and did not go back up the lane to ask what was in it. A man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that a man who does not go back is not being careful and is being told, and that he said it once and put a gather in.

**And a man of about thirty-one asked one question in that yard on the thirty-ninth day and got a straight answer, and it is the only question anybody in that yard has asked about the object column in twenty years, and it is about eleven words and the answer is about nine.** A man of about thirty-one has carried about two thousand sheets of that series in six years and has never once asked what the object column is for and has asked it once, on the thirty-ninth day of a count of days that this city keeps, to a man of sixty-six, and the man of sixty-six answered it in a yard in about nine words and put the rule down afterwards and picked it up again, and nobody in that yard has ever seen a man of sixty-six put a rule down after answering a question and pick it up again.

**And at about the ninth hour of the thirty-ninth day a child of nine years old came down that lane with a bundle on its shoulder and went out through the road gate and up a far bank and away, and nine people went through behind it, and a man of fifty-five in a hut said nothing.** This is the sixth time in sixteen days a bundle has gone out of that gate without the four words, and the man in the hut has not counted any of the six.

And a man of thirty-two with a limp walked nine hundred feet of paving and turned round at the end of it and walked it back, and the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works and came down it and went up it, and a man of about thirty-one went up a rise with a satchel and a sheet in it with four empty places in it and did not look back, and about four hundred people in this city did not know that a man of sixty-six had entered a ninth line in a book under a board in a yard, and that a man of about thirty-one was carrying three figures to a town two hundred miles off and was carrying none of them, and that the count in that book had gone up for the first time in a count of days that anybody in this city is keeping.
