# Chapter 0231

**The thirty-first day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the second hour, and a room off a lane that goes down to the quay with a bench in it the length of two men and a stove at the end of the bench and nine bundles of cord and reed on a shelf above it, and nine people in that room who carry, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the door of it who has been at the door of it four minutes, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that any of it is happening today, and that is the ordinary condition of a working city and is not a thing anybody has ever noticed.**

The room is not a room anybody made for this. It was a room for cord. The shelf along the back of it is about five feet off the floor and eleven feet long and there have been nine bundles of cord and reed on it for longer than the oldest person in that room can remember, and the bundles are what the lighters bring up and what goes out of the gate in nine pairs of hands four days in seven. There is a bench, a stove, a door on the lane, and a hook by the door with a coat on it. A woman of about fifty keeps the room and has kept it nineteen years and is not in it today and has not been in it on any day that anybody in the lane can remember, and asking her what she does with her day is the same as asking the gate.

**The nine of them are nine and they are the same nine and half of them are women and none of them is paid, and that has been the arrangement for four hundred years and it is not in anybody's book. A man of thirty-two with a limp asked for the number out loud in that room on the thirty-first day and was given it in four words, and it was nine, and it is the ninth time in thirty-one days that anybody has said a figure in this city and been given the right one.**

They were folding. Each of them had a strip of paper about a foot long with five lines ruled across it and nothing written on any of the five, and the fold is made with a thumbnail, once across the middle and once again, and it is the same fold in every one of them. About a second and a half a strip, and nine people for three hours will get through about a hundred and forty. There is no sound in that room except the stove and the folding, and a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the door for four minutes watching it and did not go in, because nobody in that room was going to explain anything to him and there is nothing in that room that anybody knows.

Then a woman of twenty-eight came down the lane with a bundle on her shoulder and went in past him without stopping. She is the one he met on a bridge on the twelfth day. She has been carrying for four years and has been turned back at three gates four days in seven for all of it, and she has not said one word to him in nineteen days since. She put the bundle down on the bench and started folding, and he went in after her and sat at the free end of the bench.

“You are the man with the stick,” she said.

“Yes.”

“**You have been at that door four minutes and you are going to ask me what this is and I am going to tell you that I do not know, and I have been asked four times in four years and I have given four answers and three of the four were wrong, and I am telling you which three so that you do not waste your question.**”

---

**And that was the first hour and a half of the thirty-first day in that room. He folded about ninety strips and was given something out of a cup at the ninth hour by a person he had not seen before who did not ask him anything. The woman of twenty-eight folded a hundred and forty and did not speak to him again that day. Nine people in that room worked out inside half a second that a man who sits down and folds is a man who has decided he is not going to be told anything, and about nine thousand people in this city work that out in about half a second, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is the only one anybody here has met who takes nineteen days to get to it.**

The strips came down the lane at about the fourth hour on a barrow from a yard at the bottom of the lane past the works and round by a bindle store, and he knew the barrow and did not need to be told. Nine people watched him look at the barrow and knew where it had been and none of them said anything about it.

A man of sixty-six was at the other end of that barrow with a rule nine inches long, a strip of whalebone, and a pot of the thinnest glue. He had cut about four hundred and one of the eleven-word things in twenty years and had never in twenty years made one with nothing on it.

“They are not for this room,” he said, to the room, in about eleven words, at about the seventh hour. “**They are for the shelf and the shelf is for the four days in seven and the four days in seven have not changed and nothing about them is going to.**”

And then the other thing, which is about nineteen words, and it is the third thing he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper.

“**And they go to people who are not told anything, which is the ordinary way, and if anybody in this room is ever asked what is in a bale then the answer is that I do not know and it is not a lie, and a man who is asked that is a man who has started asking.**”

Nobody improved on it. A woman of twenty-eight folded a strip of paper and did not turn round.

Then she said the other thing, and it is the fourth thing she has said to him in forty days and the only one of the four that was not a warning.

“**And there is no count of them either. Nine of us go out four days in seven, and the gate lets bundles through and turns people back, and a man of fifty-five in a hut has been in that hut nineteen years and has turned round about four hundred people and has let about nine thousand through, and he has not written one of them down because the hut has no book in it, and there is nobody in this city who would want one. That is the whole of the trade. It is not kept. It is not lost. It was never written.**”

And a man of thirty-two with a limp did not say anything to that, and could not think of a question that it did not close, and a few people in that lane have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in a city forty days and has asked six things in all of them and has got six straight answers and has not once told any of the six that they were being difficult, and that this is a fact about him and not about the city.

Nine hundred yards up that lane a man of about thirty-four with lime said a thing of the same shape to nine men in a working yard on about the same afternoon and did not know the other one had said it.

---

**And the money was footed on the sill of the bench before anybody said a total, and the addition was done by the woman of twenty-eight and not by him, and it took her about a minute and a half and she did it twice.**

Paper was three pence the hundred, and it is the same paper the man of sixty-one buys his in, out of the same building, and the two of them have been buying out of it for nineteen years and have never spoken. The glue was four pence the pot and a pot goes about a fortnight. The cord was two pence the ball and a ball cords nine bales. Three and four is seven, and seven and two is nine pence.

“**Nine pence for a week of that,**” she said, “**and the reed is not in it and the reed has never been in anything, and I am not going to add a thing nobody charges for to a thing somebody does, because that is how a price gets made, and a price gets made out of two things that are not the same thing and then somebody files it.**”

“And the room,” he said.

“The room is not in that either.”

“**The room is two pence a night and bread at the second hour is a penny and that is the woman of about fifty's column and it is hers and I have not been in it and I am not going in it,**” said the woman of twenty-eight, “**and the two nights I have slept in this city are not a column, and a woman of about fifty has already told a man of forty-six that in her own words and I am not going to improve on her.**”

Two and one is three, and nobody said three, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has now been fed and slept and not paid for thirty-one days, and has been given a cup by a person he had never seen before, and has sat on a bench and folded ninety pieces of paper that had nothing on them, and has not been given a wage and has not been given a form and has not been given a question.

---

**And a strip of paper was put into his hand at about the fifth hour by a woman of twenty-eight who did not stop and did not look at him, and it is about a foot long with five lines ruled across it and nothing written on any of the five, and he put it in the inside pocket of a coat, and nine minutes later he could not remember whether there were five lines on it or four.**

That is the whole of the cost. Nobody in that room saw it and nobody in that room would have understood it. Four hundred yards up that lane a man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that there is a thing a man does with a gather that he loses the thread of if somebody hands him something, and that it is not a fault of the gather, and that a man who has been on a batch nineteen years can tell a man who is losing the thread of a thing he is holding from nine feet off, and that he has said it once and has not said it since.

He waited until the sixth hour and took the strip out and looked at it. There were five lines. He had known there were five lines at the fifth hour and had written the number in the air with his thumb on the edge of the bench while he waited, and he did not write it down, because a man who is losing the thread of a piece of paper is a man who has to be able to check the piece of paper, and a man who cannot hold it is not in a condition to check anything, and nobody in that city corrects a stranger, and a stranger holding a piece of paper wrong is a stranger holding a piece of paper.

There is a page in that coat with a seventh line on it that says a person and has said a person since a lane four hundred miles off, and eleven lines in a notebook in the same coat and he cannot stop seeing the eleventh of them, and now a strip of paper with five lines on it and nothing on any of them. The three of them are about four inches apart in one pocket and none of them has met any of the others.

---

**And the shelf is dusted on the first of the four days in seven and not on the other three, and a woman of about fifty does that when she is in and there are four of the seven days when she is not.** A man of fifty-five in a hut and a woman of about fifty in a room and a man of thirty-two who sells names for a penny at a corner are three of the arrangements this city runs on not being written down, and about four hundred people in this city know about one of the three and about nine know about two, and nobody has put any of them in a sentence with any of the others.

**And a man of sixty-one came up a slope at about the seventh hour and went into that room for the first time in nineteen years. There is no book in it. He stood in the doorway and did not come further in, for about two minutes, and then he said three words.**

“They want a column.”

“**They want a column,**” said the man of sixty-six from the doorway behind him, and two men stood eleven feet apart in the doorway of one room, and neither of them had said one word to the other in nineteen years, and about four hundred people in this city know that those two men buy their paper and their glue out of one building and have never spoken.

Then the man of sixty-one said the reason out loud, in about nineteen words, to nine people and a stranger. It is the only thing he has said out loud in nineteen years that was not about a book and the second of two, and he said it to a doorway and not to a man of thirty-two, and the man of sixty-six heard every word of it and has not said one word about it since.

“**A book about the outside is what a message system turns into when somebody starts counting, and I have got one and I have not been able to put it down in nineteen years, and I am not going to be the man who starts a second one about a thing this city has just decided to send forty yards.**”

And then the other half, in four seconds, and it is a sentence about a room he has never been in.

“**And if I did start one, then in about four years there would be a book in this city with a figure in it, and a figure in a book is a figure in a return, and I have read a return once and I did not learn anything from it except that a room will take a number off you and not a name, and that those are the same transaction.**”

Nobody improved on it. A woman of twenty-eight folded a strip of paper and did not turn round. Two men who had known about each other's count for nine years because of a fold in a page four inches from the bottom stood in a doorway and did not say one word, and a fold is a thing a man gets by opening a book at the same place every day for about nineteen years, and neither of those two books has ever been compared with the other and neither is going to be.

---

**And the man of sixty-one went back down the slope at about the eighth hour and did not stay, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went after him as far as the bottom of the lane and did not call out and did not go up, and this is the sixth time in thirty-one days a man in this city has been in front of him and has not been asked anything by anybody.**

**And the nine of them who carry are nine, and about four hundred people in this city have been out of that gate in nineteen years, and nine hundred know the name of one of the eleven places, and neither of those two figures knows anything about the other one.** A man of fifty with a waggon has been to four of the eleven and has never put two of them together in one sentence. A boy of fourteen on a step has been to none of them and has the names of all eleven and gets them from nine people. Four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand people worked that out between them in four hundred years and did it by going.

And the strips went into the bales at about the ninth hour and the bales were corded with about four turns through the loops that are already in them for that, and the shelf was eleven feet long and about nine bundles were on it at the beginning of the day and about nine were on it at the end of it, and the ones that were on it at the beginning went out of the gate at the second hour and the ones that were on it at the end will go out on the next of the four days in seven. A man of fifty-five in a hut at the road end of that lane turned round four hundred people in nineteen years and said four words to each of them and did not say the four words to any of the nine bundles, and about four people in that hut have worked out that a bundle is a bundle and have not said so to him and are not going to.

And a man of thirty-two with a limp walked nine hundred feet of paving at about the ninth hour and turned round at the end of it and walked it back, and he had been doing that since the twenty-fourth. The light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days and where it will be standing on the fortieth. A man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works with a bucket in one hand and his other hand on the top of it, and came down it, and went up it, and nobody in this city has ever asked him what the bucket is for. About four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand people did not know on the thirty-first day that nine people had folded a hundred and forty pieces of paper that had nothing on them, and that a man of sixty-one had said no to a column, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp had been handed one of them and had lost the thread of it inside nine minutes, and not one of the three things was asked about by anybody.
