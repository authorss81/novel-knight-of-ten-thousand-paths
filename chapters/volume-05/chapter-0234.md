# Chapter 0234

**The thirty-seventh day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the second hour, and a road coming down off the eastern side with a man of about thirty-one on it and a satchel and a list, and a hut with a man of fifty-five in it and three bars, and a strip of paper about a foot long lying on a shelf in a room off a lane that goes down to the quay with a mark in the corner of it that nobody in that room put there and that has been on that shelf two days, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick two hundred yards up that lane who has not come down it, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that a second person with a satchel has ever been on that road, and a few of them are two hundred yards off and one of the four is on a ladder.**

He was turned round. It took a minute and a half and a man of fifty-five in a hut did it in four words and did not raise his voice, and a man of fifty-five has turned round four hundred people in nineteen years and this was the first one in nine days that was carrying a sheet of paper in his hand rather than a bundle. The rule is one rule, there is no second rule, and a man with a rule about four words does not have a rule about bundles as well.

Then he worked it out. It took about two minutes and he did it standing on the near bank of the bridge with the sheet in his hand and he did it the way the man of forty-six worked it out about thirteen days before, and he is right, and the reason he is right is that nine people a day go through those bars with a bundle and four hundred people have been turned round at them in nineteen years.

**He put the sheet flat inside a bale of cord and reed and corded it over with about four turns through the loops that are already in it for that, and he said one word while he did it, and the word was *cord*, and a man of sixty-six in a yard at the bottom of that lane said it at about the third hour in the tone of a man saying a price, and the two of them are two hundred yards apart and neither of them has ever seen the other.**

**And the walk up that rise is about two hours from the gate and there is a mile of it with nothing on either side for most of that mile.** A man of about thirty-one came up it on the thirty-seventh day and about four hundred yards from the top he took the satchel off his shoulder and carried it, and put it back on about a hundred yards further on. A man carrying a satchel for a hundred yards is a man whose shoulder has been telling him something for a year, and he is the fourth man in eight years to do it and the three before him all did it about two hundred yards from the top.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp came down the lane at about the fourth hour, and the sheet was in the bindle yard on a bench under a roof with about two hundred bundles of cord on racks behind it, and a strip of paper about a foot long was lying nine inches from it, and the two of them had been put on one bench about nine inches apart and nobody in that yard had put either of them there.**

The sheet is printed and not ruled and it is about the size of a broadside with a little less paper than a broadside, and it is ruled in six columns, five with heads and a sixth with no head at all and the same width as the other five, and four of the six are ruled for a figure. The object column has a place name in it that a man of sixty-six put there in his own hand on the twenty-third day of a count of days that this city keeps.

**And the object column has a word in front of the place name this time, and the word is one that nobody in that yard has ever seen written anywhere, and nobody in that yard asks what it means.** About four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand people have not asked either, and the not asking is four hundred years old and is not a decision anybody in it took.

The word is *eastern*.

A man of thirty-two with a limp read it. He had read it before, in a sheet of good paper about the size of a broadside that is in a bag in the ordinary post in a county four hundred miles off and in a copy on a shelf in that county, and he did not say it out loud, and he did not say what it meant, and he did not say where he had read it. A man with a limp looked at one column of a printed sheet and said nothing at all, and a man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard has said out loud that there is a difference between a man who is reading and a man who is being shown a thing, and that the difference in a man's face is about a second and a half, and that nine feet is the only distance at which you can see it, and that he has said it once.

Then he said the thing that the rest of the thirty-seventh day is made of, and it is about nineteen words, and it is the first thing anybody in that yard has said in four hundred years about what a word on a printed sheet is for, and he said it standing up, in the plainest register anybody in that city has for a piece of paper.

“**Somebody has filled in the object column and it is not the man who printed it, and the man who printed it has never seen that word either, and there are two of us now who have carried a sheet out of this city and only one of us has seen this word and neither of us is going to say what it is.**”

Then he said the other half, and it is the longest a man of about thirty-one has spoken in that yard, and he said it to a strip of paper and not to a sheet.

“**And I will tell you the other half of it because you have been in this city about a fortnight and you have not asked me anything. I do not know what is in the four places, and I have left about two thousand of them at about two thousand doors in six years, and I have never once known what is in the four places, and I have never once been told and I have never once asked, and the list is what I am paid for.**”

Nobody improved on it. A man of thirty-two with a limp stood at a bench with a printed sheet and a strip of paper nine inches apart on it and said nothing.

A woman of twenty-eight came down the lane at about the fifth hour with a bundle on her shoulder, and she is the woman who carried the first strip into that yard, and she stopped at the mouth of it and looked at the printed sheet on the bench and did not go in. A woman of twenty-eight who has been turned back at three gates four days in seven for four years stood at the mouth of a yard and looked at a piece of printed paper and did not go in and did not say why. She went on down the lane and out through the road gate with the bundle on her shoulder, and a man of fifty-five in a hut at that gate did not say the four words.

A man of thirty-two with a limp watched her go and understood, in about a minute and a half, that she had looked at the sheet and worked out that it was the same instrument as the strips, and had decided that a piece of printed paper in a yard is a question, and that she had been asked one already that morning at a gate and was not going to be asked another one in a yard.

A man of thirty-one has said two things in that yard in under a minute and a man of forty-six said four things in nine days in the same tone, and the two of them have not met and they have said the same shape of thing about a list.

---

**And the sheet lay on that bench from about the fourth hour to the seventh hour and nobody filled in the four places.** A man of thirty-four with lime came down the lane at about the fifth hour to get a bundle of reed that had been left against the wall, saw the sheet on the bench, and did not stop. A man who has been in a yard nineteen years has seen four hundred things go out of that yard and has not looked at one of them twice, and the man of thirty-two with a limp has noticed that and has not said a word about it to anybody and is not going to.

A boy of fourteen came to the bench at about the sixth hour and said the only thing anybody in that yard has said about the six columns, and it is nine words and nobody in that lane has improved on it since.

“**It wants people and it will not say people.**”

The object column had a place name and a word in it, and the sixth column had no head on it and the same width as the other five. This is the third printed thing that has been in that yard in twenty years: the first was left by a man of forty-six and came back with four of the same figure in it, the second went back blank, and this one has a word in it that nobody here has. A man of sixty-six has written a figure on a printed sheet once in twenty years and has not written one since, and the fourth place on this one is where the figure goes.

And a man of about thirty-one took the sheet out of the satchel at about the seventh hour and put it flat in the satchel without looking at it for longer than a second. A man of thirty-one who has left about two thousand forms at about two thousand doors in six years has never once looked at one that came back filled in, and he took this one away blank. A man of thirty-one and a man of forty-six will be in the same room in a town two hundred miles off inside about nine days, and neither of them knows the other's name, and about four hundred doors on two lists are the same four hundred doors, and nobody in either of those two rooms has ever worked out how many.

A woman of about thirty with a pail came down the lane at about the sixth hour and saw the sheet on the bench and said nine words to nobody, and four people in that lane have repeated those nine words since and none of the four has improved on them and none of the four was in that yard.

*Somebody has filled that in and it is not the one who printed it.*

---

**And a woman of sixty was told about the word on the thirty-eighth day and a clerk of forty entered two lines and did not read them back.** The man of about thirty-one put a second sheet on the table in the room at the top of the lane at about the second hour of the thirty-eighth day, and the woman of sixty looked at the object column and then looked at the place name in it and then said one sentence, and it is about nine words. A woman of sixty read a word off a printed sheet in a room in a city of about nine thousand people and did not ask what it meant, and she has not asked what anything means in nineteen years, and a clerk of forty entered two lines on the strength of it, and nobody in that room has ever worked out what the two lines were for and the clerk has never once said.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp asked one question in that yard on the thirty-seventh day and it is the eighth thing he has asked anybody in this city in thirty-seven days, and he asked it standing up, in about nine words, to a man of about thirty-one, and the answer is the fourth thing that man has said in nine days in the tone of a man saying a price.**

“**How many of these go out of this city.**”

“**None of them go out of this city. They go out of a gate with a bundle on it, and a gate is three bars and one man, and the man does not count bundles and has never counted bundles and has said four words to about four hundred people in nineteen years and has said them to none of the bundles.**”

**And at about the seventh hour of the thirty-seventh day a man of sixty-six stood in a yard at the bottom of a lane and looked at four places on a printed sheet that are ruled for a figure and are empty, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was two hundred yards up that lane and did not come down it and has not been in that yard since the ninth day, and a man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that a man who has been fed and not paid for thirty-seven days has stopped being a stranger somewhere about the second week and does not know the day, and that the day is the day he first sat down, and that it is not a thing anybody did to him.**

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at a bench with a printed sheet in front of him for about four minutes and worked out four things, and three of them were about the paper and one of them was about the man holding it, and he has not written any of the four down.** A sheet comes round twice. A room takes a figure off you and not a name. A word in an object column is put there by somebody who has never seen this city. And the man reading it is in a book four hundred miles off and is the only person in this city who is in a book.

**And what the four places wanted was not the whole of what happened on the thirty-seventh day and it is not the whole of what happened on the thirty-eighth or the thirty-ninth either. About four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand people did not know on any of the three days that a sheet with a word in the object column was in a yard, and a few of them knew on the thirty-ninth and not before, and the ones who knew on the thirty-ninth were two men in one room and nine hundred feet apart and neither of them knew the other knew.**

And a man of thirty-two with a limp walked nine hundred feet of paving at the ninth hour and turned round at the end of it and walked it back, and the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works and came down it and went up it, and there was a mark in the corner of a strip of paper on a shelf in a room off a lane that went down to the quay, and a man of about thirty-one went up a rise with a satchel and a blank sheet in it and did not look back, and about four hundred people in this city did not know that a sheet had gone out of their city on the thirty-seventh day with a word in the object column that a man of thirty-two with a limp had read before in a document four hundred miles off, and that a man of fifty-five in a hut had said nothing at all.
