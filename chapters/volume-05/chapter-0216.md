# Chapter 0216

**The ninth day, and the fourth hour, and a lane that goes up and not down, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick going up it for the second time with a piece of paper in a coat, and eleven people in a long room on the ground floor, and the fourth hour at the top of a lane is the hour at which nobody in this city will say a thing twice, and the reason is nobody's business and is a woman of about sixty who has said it once in four hundred years of counting.**

He had come up the lane with two questions and he only asked one of them, and about four of the eleven in that room have said since that a man who has come back up a lane three days after a room told him that the room does not answer things has come up it to be answered, and that this is the wrong reason, and that the right reason is that a man who has been told a thing once by a woman of sixty cannot get it out of his head and has gone up to see whether she will say it again.

“Why eleven,” he said, from the same four feet.

The woman of sixty did not look up the second time. That is the fact of the ninth day and four of the eleven have said since that she did not look up, and that on the sixth day she had put her pen down, and that the difference between the two is the third day of a man's asking, and that the pen is on the table in front of her and was not on the table in front of him.

“**I said that once,**” she said. “**I said it once to a stranger in four hundred years and you are the stranger and you have had it, and I am not going to say it twice to a man who has come up a lane to make me say it twice, and that is what twice is for.**”

Nobody in that room said anything for the length of a held breath, and four of the eleven have said since that it was the shortest silence anybody coming up that lane has ever been answered with, and that a woman of sixty who has refused a second time in front of eleven people in nineteen years of sitting there has just done it twice in four hundred years and once in nine days.

“I am not complaining,” he said. “I wanted to know whether it was true the second time as well.”

“**It is the same true,**” said the woman of sixty. “**It does not get truer. I am not the keeper of the truth of it, I am the woman who said it. You are asking the wrong person about the wrong thing and you have come a long way to do it.**”

---

**And then he asked the question he had come for, and it was the ordinary question, and about four of the eleven in that room have said since that it was the right question and that a man who has come back to ask the right question has to be told that it cannot be asked here, and that the woman of sixty took nine seconds to do it and that the nine seconds were the most carefully spent nine seconds anybody in that room has spent in that year.**

“I have been refused.”

“Yes.”

“By the man who makes the forms.”

“Yes.”

“Then I would like to ask it again.”

“**You cannot,**” said the woman of sixty, and put her hand flat on the table, and about four of the eleven have said since that this is the first time in nineteen years that a hand has gone flat on that table, and that it went flat for about half a second and came up again, and that the half a second is the entire history of what a hand on that table is for.

“**Not because I will not hear it. Because there is nothing here that can hear it. What comes out of this room is a form and the form was made downstairs by a man who is not one of us, and if you ask me again I will send him a form, and if I send him a form he will make you a form, and if he makes you a form it will be the same form, because it is eleven words and there is one way of saying it, and you will be holding two of them in three days and they will be worth exactly the same and neither of them will be worth anything.**”

“Then what do I do with the asking.”

“**You keep it,**” said the woman of sixty, “**and in about four years you will want to show it to somebody, and I am telling you now, in a room with eleven people in it, that you will not be able to, and that you will be able to want to, and that the gap between the two is what this place is and it is not a secret and it is not a policy and it is just what happens to a piece of paper when nobody is allowed to keep it.**”

And the clerk of forty entered two lines and did not read them back, and about four of the eleven have said since that he entered them because the woman of sixty did not say do not, and that this is the fourth time a thing has gone into that book on the strength of an omission, and that the clerk has never once written the word omission and has never once needed to.

---

**And at the fifth hour he went down four hundred yards past the works and round by the bindles and the yard door was open, and a man of sixty-six was at a bench with a rule nine inches long and a strip of whalebone and a pot of the thinnest glue, and he did not stop cutting, and this time he said his name before the man of thirty-two had got through the gate, which about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since is the first time in years a man of sixty-six has said his name to a man he had already refused.**

“You have been in the room upstairs.”

“I have.”

“Did you ask her why there are eleven.”

“I did.”

**“She will not have told you a second time.**”

“She told me it is the same true and that she is not the keeper of it.”

**“Then she has been the woman of sixty for about nineteen years and you have been in this yard once, and both of those are correct, and you have now been told the same thing twice by two people who are not the same age and have the same job, and I would like you to notice that you have had nine seconds of it twice.**”

Then he put the rule down.

“Now ask me.”

“I asked for work three days ago and you gave me a form.”

“You did.”

“I would like to ask for it again.”

**And a man of sixty-six looked at a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick for a short count, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that it was the longest a man of sixty-six has ever spent on an asking and that all of them went on whether to charge for the second one, and that he did not charge for it, and that about four people in that city have said since that this is the only time anybody in it has refused to be paid for a refusal, and that a man who has been refused once and comes back is a person, and that the city does not have a form for that.**

“You may ask,” he said.

“I asked for work three days ago and you gave me a form, and I have carried it three days and I have shown it to nobody, and I am asking for it again because a man of sixty with a pen told me upstairs that a person can be asked twice and I would like to find out what happens.”

**“Nothing happens,**” said the man of sixty-six. “**That is the answer and it is the whole of it and I am going to give you the same eleven words in the same order in about four minutes, and the two of them will be the same, and I will not improve on the first one, and you will not be able to tell which of the two you got first.**”

---

**And it took about four minutes and it was the same eleven words and he ruled the paper the same way and the ink was the same ink and the fifth line was empty when it was finished, and a man of thirty-two with a limp compared the two for a while and found that they were the same paper and the same printing and the same hand and that the only difference was that one of them had been in a coat for three days and had a fold in it four inches from the bottom.**

The making of it is the job and a few people in that city have said since that nobody off a road has ever watched it being done twice and that the reason there are eleven words and not nine or thirteen is the whalebone, and that the whalebone is a strip of a thing a whale had and that it is about two hundred years old and that it came here in a crate with glass rods and that nobody has ever been able to find out from whom. He damped the back of the strip, drew it once across the head of the paper, and the five lines came up on it at once and even, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has ruled four things in his life and could not do that with a rule and a straightedge and has not tried to do it with anything else since.

“Could I try that.”

“**No,**” said the man of sixty-six.

“I was not asking for it for a reason. I was asking because I have been in this city nine days, in a room with eleven people in it and four hundred yards of lane and a glass yard and a wharf, and I would like to try one thing.”

**“You may not try that and I will tell you why short, and it is not that you would do it badly. If you learn that, then in about four years there is a man in a county four hundred miles off who can rule a form in this city, and a form is the only thing this place makes, and the only thing this place makes is the only thing it has ever given anybody.**”

And a man of thirty-two with a limp put his hand flat on the bench the way a man puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the bench was warm, and the stone was warm on the other side of his hand too, and a few people in that city have said since that the man with the stick did that in a yard on the ninth day and that nobody there has worked out what he was checking and that the yard is warmer on that side than on the other by the width of a hand and has been for four hundred years, and that a man who has spent four years with his hand on cold stone knows what a warm one is doing.

**They are the same.**

“They are the same,” said the man of sixty-six, “**and there is no number on either, and the number that would tell you which one was the first is the number I am not allowed to write, and the reason I am not allowed to write it is that a number of refusals is a number of refusals and not a number of people, and if a number of refusals ever gets out of this yard and into a hand in another county then this city becomes a figure in a return, and a figure in a return is a thing that cannot be refused, and that is the whole reason for four hundred years.**”

**And then he said the other thing, and it is the second thing he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he said it to the ninth day and not to the sixth and that the two of them together are the whole of what he has said out loud in twenty years that was not about paper, and that nobody in that city has ever added them up, including him.**

“**And I will tell you the thing that goes with it, and it is the only other one, and then I am going to my bench. A refusal that has been made twice is worth the same as a refusal that has been made once, and it is not less, and there are about four hundred men in four counties in your country who have been told the same thing twice and went away and said the place would not be told, and you are the first one in years who has come back and asked for the second one on purpose.**”

---

**And he went up the lane with two pieces of paper in a coat, and a few people in that city have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp came out of that yard a few minutes after the fifth hour on the ninth day holding two identical refusals, and that he did not fold either of them, and that he carried them both flat, and that a man who carries a refusal flat is a man who expects to keep it, and that nobody has ever seen anybody keep one.**

He put them in the coat with the page. The page had a date on it in a fair hand and one word under the date, and the two forms had nothing under anything, and a man of thirty-two stood at the bend in the lane where the wall comes up on the river side and understood that he was carrying three pieces of paper from three places and that the only one of them that anybody in this city had ever written down for him was the one that said no, and that the word on the page was a place four hundred miles off, and that the two of those facts had been in a coat together since the sixth day and had not met and would not meet, and he did not join them, and about four people in that city have said since that the man with the stick has never once said the name of anywhere out loud, and that this is the single most correct thing anybody in this city has done in nine days, and that it is also the reason nobody here is going to know anything about him before he goes.

---

**And the nine days are a thing that happened to him and not to the city, and a few people in that city have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp was in this place for nine days and did nine days of the ordinary work of it, and that the ordinary work of it is carrying a hod for a loaf and being fed at the second hour and the ninth and being in a market at the third and being in a yard at the seventh, and that he was in nine places and was in none of them and that this is not a trick and is not a refusal and is what a man is when he is not refused and is not kept.**

He carried every day from the second to the ninth. The man of fifty with the waggon fed him and the man of thirty-four with the lime on his hands went on putting his own hod down six feet short at the end of a run and picking it up again, and neither of them said why, and neither of them stopped, and about four men in that yard have said since that the arrangement went on for five days and that on the ninth day a man who has been given the same six feet five days running stops noticing them, and that a man who has stopped noticing a kindness is not a man who has stopped needing it, and that about four of them noticed that he had stopped noticing and did not do anything about it.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp worked out in the yard on the eighth day, in about half a minute, that he was the safest person in that city, and worked out all of why straight after that, and about four people in that city have said since that the stranger worked it out on the eighth day and that the working out is correct, and that he did not say it out loud, and that not saying it out loud is the first sensible thing anybody in this city has seen a man do and that nobody here has said so to him and that about four people in that yard have said so to each other.**

He is nothing. That is the trade fact and it is a trade fact and not a comfort: a man in this city who is in no book and has asked nothing and has been refused and has come back and been refused again is a man about whom there is nothing to be shown, and a man about whom there is nothing to be shown is a man nobody can use, and a man nobody can use is the only kind of person this city has ever been good at keeping, and he had worked it out in a yard in half a minute at the eighth hour of the eighth day of a count of days that this city keeps and he does not have, and he did not say it out loud, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that the man with the stick is now the most protected person in this city and that he is protected by the correctness of a form and not by anything anybody did for him.
