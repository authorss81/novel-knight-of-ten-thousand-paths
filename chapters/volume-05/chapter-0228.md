# Chapter 0228

**The twenty-eighth day of a count of days that this city keeps, and the second hour, and a glass works on the low side of the river with a furnace in it that has not gone out in four hundred years, and nine men on a batch, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the mouth of the yard who came down four hundred yards of lane on purpose because a man of thirty-four with lime said there was a man in that yard worth four minutes, and a man of fifty-two on the east wall of that works who has been going up a ladder four times a day for nineteen years and who has a name, and his name is Ivo Marrow, and a few of the people in that yard have said since that a man who has been on a ladder in the same place for nineteen years is the only man in this city whose whole working life has a shape, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what the shape is and that a few hundreds of people in the city could be asked and not one of them would be able to say.**

He was not doing anything. That is the first fact of the twenty-eighth day in that yard and a few of the people who have been in it have said since that a man of fifty-two standing at the end of a bench with his hands behind his back for nine minutes in a yard where nine men are working is not a man who is idle and is a man who has run out of things that are wrong, and that a few of the nine have noticed him and that the five who have not are the five who are nearest the furnace, and that a man of thirty-four with lime has said since that a man who has run out of things that are wrong is the most dangerous man in a glass yard and that he is not going to explain that and that nobody has asked him to.

“You are the man with the stick,” he said, without turning round.

“Yes.”

“**I have been on this wall four times a day for nineteen years and I have never once known what a stick is for, and I have wanted to know for about eleven of those years, and I have not asked, and that is not modesty. There is nobody in this city I could ask and it is not because of the light. It is because of what a man would be supposed to be doing while he asked.**”

Then he turned round, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of fifty-two turned round and looked at a man of thirty-two with a limp for two seconds and did not ask him a question and has not asked him one since, and that this is the fifth person in this city to look at that stick and not ask and that the other four had all been asked something else first.

---

**And the thing he is good at is the wall, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a glass man of fifty-two who has been in that yard since he was seventeen is good at about eleven things and the best one of the eleven is putting the foot of a ladder against a wall that is four hundred years old, and that he does it four times a day and that he has not been asked to do it by anybody in nineteen years and that he does it, and that a wall that a ladder has been leant on four times a day for four hundred years is not a flat wall and is not a straight one and has nine places on it where a ladder will go and about nine places where it will not, and that a man of fifty-two knows the nine and knows which of the nine is the one for a man with a bucket in one hand, and that a man of thirty-four with lime has watched him do it about two thousand times and has never once said so and said so on the twenty-eighth day in nine words and did not improve on it.**

“That is the whole of what he is for,” said the man of about thirty-four with lime, to nobody, in a yard, on the twenty-eighth day, and went back to a gather. “**Four times a day for nineteen years and the only man here who can do it and nobody has ever asked him and he has never once said and about four of us have noticed and about four of us have not, and I have just said it and I would like it noticed that I have said it to a stranger and not to him.**”

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in this city twenty-eight days and has been told about five things in four different yards and not one of the five was a thing anybody in those yards wanted told, and a few of the people in four yards have said since that they have all four noticed that and none of the four has said it to the others, and that this is the ordinary way this city hands out what it knows about a stranger, which is one piece at a time and never with a reason, and that the man of thirty-four with lime is the only one of the four who gave a reason, and that the reason was that the stranger had a limp and would be on a ladder in about four years if he was still here, and that nobody in that yard has said the second half of that out loud and that the man of thirty-four with lime said the first half only, and that this is the third time in twenty-eight days that a man in this city has said a true thing and stopped before the end of it, and that the other two were a man of sixty-six and a man of sixty-one, and that a few of the people in this city have noticed the pattern and about four hundred have not.**

---

**And the man of fifty-two said the two things, and he said them both in the same conversation, in a yard, in his own words, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he has not said the first of them to anybody in this city in nineteen years and that he said it to a stranger on the twenty-eighth day and did not go back over it, and that a few of them have asked him since whether he has ever said it twice and he has not and they did not ask him.**

“**There is a piece of paper with my name on it, and the name of the man that was on it before me, and the name of the man that was on it before him, and it is a list of the third shift and it goes back further than anybody alive, and it is in a drawer in a room in this city, and a few hundreds of people in this city could be asked what the third shift is and not one of them would be able to tell you. I am not asking you for anything. I am saying it out loud once, to you, because you are a stranger and you will be gone, and I have not said it to anybody in this city in nineteen years and I have wanted to say it to somebody in this city for about eleven years and I have not said it to one of them and that is not loyalty, I have worked that out, it is that there is nobody to say it to.**”

And then he said the second one, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that it took four seconds and that it is the shortest of the two and that it is the reason the first one is a thing a man says and not a thing a man has, and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out how a man of fifty-two gets to that in four seconds and that four seconds is about how long it takes a man to say a thing he has had ready for eleven years.

“**And I do not want to die on it. I am fifty-two. There is a brother of mine at the low end of the river who thinks I am on a roof, and if somebody knew what the shift was, and if I did not want to be on it, then I would be able to say that, and there is nobody in this city I could say it to, and that is the whole of why I have said the other thing, and I would like it noticed that it is the whole of why.**”

Nobody improved on it. A man of about thirty-four with lime put a gather into a mould and did not turn round, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-four who does not turn round when a man of fifty-two says a thing like that is a man who has been in that yard nineteen years and has heard a man say a hard thing about four times in his life and has learned not to be in the room for the end of it.

---

**And then a man of thirty-two with a limp asked him the one question he had come down four hundred yards for, and it took him nine seconds to get to it, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he asked it in the plainest register he has, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in this city twenty-eight days and has asked about four things in the first fortnight and has got four straight answers in nine words each and has not once told any of the four that they were being difficult, and that the fifth of the five is the only one he has asked anybody in this city twice.**

“What happens to the shift when you cannot go up.”

“**Then somebody goes up. There are nine of us who can go up and I am the one that has been on the third, and in nineteen years I have had nine days off that ladder altogether, and some hundreds of times somebody has stood where I stand and gone up with a bucket, and the paper says which of the nine and the paper is in a drawer. And if I cannot go up then the paper is wrong, and a piece of paper being wrong in a drawer in this city is not a thing anybody finds out about on the day.**”

“And if the drawer is burnt.”

“**Then this city has four hundred people who know what a third shift is. That is all it takes and that is the whole of what it takes and it has never been done and there is no plan to do it and I have never asked anybody for one.**”

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp then said the thing he had been carrying for nine minutes, which is that the man in front of him had eleven lines in a notebook and eleven names that were not names and a shift, and that he had got there by putting his hand on a wall, and that he is not going to say how, and that a man of fifty-two is the only person in this city he can say any of it to, and about four of the people in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp said about eleven words in a glass yard on the twenty-eighth day and that eleven of the words were not about the light and that the whole of the eleven was a man telling a man in a trade what he could not use, and that nobody improved on it and that a man of fifty-two looked at him for four seconds and then went and put the foot of a ladder against a wall that is four hundred years old and did not say one word about the eleven.**

“You cannot use it,” said Ivo Marrow.

“No.”

“**Then that is the first useful thing anybody has said to me about a list in nineteen years, and I am not going to ask you what it is a list of, and four of the nine of us in this yard have just stopped and looked at the two of you and none of the nine is going to say anything, and I would like it noticed that I have said what I have said and you have said what you have said in a working yard at the second hour, and that a man who says things like that in a working yard is a man who is not in a room, and there is no room in this city that it could be said in, and a few hundreds of people in this city know that and have never had to say it.**”

**And a man of thirty-four with lime put a duckboard down on the stone where a man of thirty-two with a limp had been standing for about twenty minutes, and did it without a word and without looking at him, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-four with lime has been putting a piece of wood under a stranger's feet for five days and that nobody in that yard has ever seen him do it for anybody else, and that the stone in that yard is about two hundred and forty feet long with nowhere to sit on it and nothing to lean on it, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp has a leg that is the same leg it was in a county four hundred miles off and is slower, and that a few of the men in that yard have noticed the slower and none of them has said a word about it and one of them has put a piece of wood down about eleven times.**

---

**And the third shift is at the ninth hour and it is not the only shift, and a few of the people in that yard have said since that this city keeps a light that has not turned for four hundred years and does it in shifts, and that the shifts are in a drawer in a room in this city and not on a wall, and that a drawer is the correct place and that a few of the men in that yard have said that a drawer is the correct place and that a wall is not, and that the reason a drawer is the correct place is that a wall is a thing people look at, and that this is the second time in thirty days that a man in this city has said the correct reason for a correct arrangement out loud to nobody, and that the other one was a man of forty-six with a satchel about an office that will not write down what it is asking about, and that a few hundreds of people in this city have heard one of the two and about four have heard both, and that the two who have heard both are in different yards and have not met.**

**And nine men in that yard work from the second hour to the ninth and nine of them can go up the ladder and about one of them does it four times a day, and a few of the people in that yard have said since that a third shift is not a worse shift and is not a better shift and is not a reward and is not a punishment, and that it is the one nobody puts themselves down for, and that some hundreds of times in nineteen years nine men have been willing and about nine hundred times about nine men have been able, and that nobody has ever written either of those figures down and that a man of fifty-two could write them both in nine seconds and has not and is not going to, and that the paper in the drawer has his name on it and has had it for nineteen years, and that a piece of paper with a name on it that is in a drawer in a city that cannot be written to is the only piece of paper about a man of fifty-two in the world, and that he knows that, and that he said it out loud once this morning to a stranger, and that a few of the people in that yard have said since that a man of fifty-two knowing that and saying it once is not a complaint and is not a want and is the reason the other of the two things he said is believable.**

**And at the ninth hour of the twenty-eighth day a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of that works for the fourth time with a bucket in one hand and his other hand on the top of it, and did not look down at anybody, and a few hundreds of people in a city of about nine thousand people did not know that a man of fifty-two had said two things in a yard that morning and had not said them twice, and the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was sitting on a bench that was too short for him in a room with a stick standing up in the corner where he could reach it, and there were eleven lines in a notebook in the inside pocket of a coat and he could not stop seeing the eleventh of them, and a few hundreds of people in a city of about nine thousand people did not know that either and will not.**
