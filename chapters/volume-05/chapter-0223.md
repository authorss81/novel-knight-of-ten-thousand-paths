# Chapter 0223

**The twenty-third day of a count of days that this city keeps, and the second hour, and a yard at the bottom of a lane past the works and round by a bindle store, and a printed sheet lying on a bench under a roof with about two hundred bundles of cord on racks behind it, and a man of sixty-six who has not written on it and has taken the board off a book that is about two inches thick and has been under the board for twenty years, and a woman of about thirty with a pail who came down the lane at about the second hour and found the two of them like that and went on to the door she had come to, and a few of the people in that yard have said since that a woman of thirty with a pail has been the fifth person in twenty days who has seen a man of sixty-six and a book out at the same time and that four of the five have not asked him one word about it and that the fifth is the one she told about it and that she told a stranger about it in a lane and not to him.**

He had had it there about a day and he had not written on it and he had not thrown it out, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six who neither writes on a thing nor throws it out is a man who has put it in the third place, and that the third place is a bench on the right hand side of his own working, and that the third place has been there for about six years and a few of the people know about it and a few hundreds of people in this city do not.

**And at about the fourth hour of the twenty-second day, after a man of forty-six had gone up the lane and had said twice that he would come back in two days, a man of sixty-six read the five heads of a printed sheet again and then took the board off a book and put the book down on the bench on the other side of the sheet, and the two of them lay about nine inches apart, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that this is the first time a printed thing and a written thing have been on that bench together in twenty years and that the man who did it is sixty-six and did it to answer a piece of printed paper four hundred doors, and that nobody in that yard has worked out that a man who has kept a book for twenty years under a board is the man in this city best placed to fill in four printed spaces, and that the whole of what happens next is that.**

---

**And the book is in one column and the column is a count of days, and the days in it are the days this city keeps from the last renewal, and the count of days is not the thing he was looking at. What is in the book is a line for every time this city has said no to a thing that wanted an answer, and there are eight lines, and the last of the eight was made on the sixth day of this count and it is the one that is a foot long with eleven words on it and a fifth ruled line empty, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six took a book out from under a board on the fourth hour of a day and read it and counted the lines in it out loud, and that he has not counted anything out loud in about a year, and that the only other man in this city who counts out loud is a man of thirty-two with a limp who was two hundred yards up a lane at the time and did not know.**

He said the number out loud twice, in a yard, to nobody, on the twenty-third day, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that both times it was eight, and that eight is the correct number of refusals this city has made in four hundred years that a man of sixty-six has written down, and that the number is right and that it has been right since before the man who keeps the book was in it.

**And then he counted the other one, and he counted it with the sheet in front of him and the book nine inches away, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six counted two numbers in a minute on the twenty-third day and did not put one against the other, and that the two numbers were eight and nine, and that the eight was what the book said and the nine was what he had, and that the difference between them is one, and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out that he was holding both at once.**

The nine was not in the book. The nine was a refusal given in that yard on the nineteenth day of this count by a person of nine years old who had a bundle on one shoulder, and it took the time it takes to draw one breath, and there is no column in that book for it and there has never been a column in that book for it, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has made about four hundred pieces of paper in twenty years and has not one of them with a figure on it, and that on the nineteenth day he kept a pen in his hand and did not write, and that nobody in that yard has asked him about it and he has not offered it and is not going to.

**And this is the whole of the arithmetic and it is about nine inches of a bench, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six in twenty years has never once had two numbers in his hands and that he had two on the twenty-third day and that neither of them was a mistake and that one of them was a copy and that a copy is not the thing it is a copy of when the thing is nine years old and it cannot be written on the paper you have.**

He counted the nine the way he counted the eight, and the way he counted the nine was not by looking at anything, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six counted nine in four seconds without moving his eyes and that he does that the way other men do a sum they have done every day of their working life, and that the ninth of the nine is the one that is in a coat two hundred yards up a lane in the inside pocket of a coat of a man of thirty-two with a limp, and that the other eight are in a room over a shop on the edge of a lane with a bench too short in it, and that the man of thirty-two has shown his to nobody for four days and is not going to.

---

**And then he wrote, and he wrote it in four places, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that it took him a minute and a half and that he did not stop between the four and that the four are the four of the six columns of a printed sheet that are ruled for a figure, and that the fifth of the five headed columns is the one that says what the object is and that the object column is the one he filled first and that what he put in it was a place name, and that a man of sixty-six wrote the name of this city into a printed sheet on the twenty-third day and did not stop at it and did not say anything about it and went on to the four figures, and that a few of the people in that yard have said since that this is the first time in four hundred years that the name of this place has been written by anybody in this place on anything that was not made in this yard.**

One, and one, and one, and one. That is four of the same figure and it is not a sum, and a man of forty-six in a town two hundred miles off who is handed a printed sheet with four figures on it will read four figures on it and the four figures are all the same, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has not written a figure on anything in twenty years and has now written four of them in a minute and a half, and that he wrote four and not one, and that the reason he wrote four is that a man fills in the spaces on a printed sheet, and that a man fills in the spaces because the spaces are there, and that nobody in that yard has ever seen a man fill in a space and it is the first thing a man of sixty-six has done in twenty years that was not on paper of his own.

**And nine is a count of things this city has said no to and it is not a count of people, and that is said once, in a yard, in about eleven words, by a man of sixty-six, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he said it to a yard and not to the sheet and that the sheet is a foot away from him and has four nines on it and no words.**

“I will tell you what that is and then I am going to go back to my bench,” he said. “**That is a count of things this city has said no to. It is not how many of us there are. It is not that. I have never in twenty years had to say that and I am saying it now and I am saying it short.**”

And about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that the man of sixty-six did not say it to a man of forty-six, because the man of forty-six had gone up the lane two days before and was a day and a half off on a road with a list, and that he said it to about two hundred bundles of cord on racks, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp was two hundred yards up a lane at the fourth hour of the twenty-third day and did not hear it and has not been told it and is not going to be.

---

**And a man of about thirty with a barrow and a mule came down the lane on the twenty-third day and stopped outside the yard because there was a man of thirty-two with a limp standing at the mouth of it with a stick and a page in his hand, and a few of the people in that lane have said since that the page had been out of the coat for the length of a conversation and that it has not been out of the coat since the ninth day of a count of days that the man with the page does not have, and that on the twenty-third day a man of thirty-two with a limp took a page out of a coat in a lane and looked at the seventh line of it and put it back without saying a word to anybody, and that the seventh line says a person and has not been finished since a lane four hundred miles off.**

“He has been standing there since the second hour,” said the man with the barrow.

“He has,” said the man of thirty-two with a limp.

“**Then he has been standing in a lane for two hours with a page in his hand and he has not looked at the first six lines, and I have been going up that hill for twenty-three days and I have never once seen anybody in this city look at the first six lines of anything.**”

And he took the mule past him and went up the hill, and about four of the people in that lane have said since that the man with the barrow has now said all that happens in this city in two sentences on four different days, and that he does not know it and would not improve on it, and that the man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the mouth of a yard for two more hours after the mule had gone and did not go down the lane and did not go into the yard and has not been told what is on the bench in it.

---

**And the sixth column has no head on it, and a man of forty-six asked about it twice, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of forty-six who has left about three thousand forms at about three thousand doors in eight years has never once asked what a column is for, and that he asked about that one twice, and that the first time was on the twenty-second day and the second time was on the twenty-fourth and that both times he asked it in the tone of a man asking for a price and not in the tone of a man asking a question, and that a man of sixty-six looked at it both times for a second and a half and said nothing both times, and that a man of sixty-six has said nothing about a column in twenty years about four times and has been asked about this one twice and has answered it neither, and that a column with no head on it is the same width as the five with heads on it and that the only difference is that nobody has ever been told what goes in it and that a few hundreds of people in this city have never once wondered about it and that this is the second thing in four hundred years that a printed sheet has come into that yard and both of them have been about a column that cannot be described.**

**And a woman of about thirty with a pail came down the lane at about the third hour of the twenty-third day and saw a man of sixty-six at a bench with a printed sheet on it with writing in it and a book on the other side of the bench, and she stopped, and a few of the people in that lane have said since that a woman of about thirty with a pail has stopped in a working lane four times in thirty days and that three of the four were because a stranger was in it and that this one was because of a piece of paper, and that she did not go in and did not ask and said one sentence at the mouth of the yard and went on, and that a few of the people in that lane have repeated the sentence since and that it is nine words long, and that it is the only sentence anybody in that city has said in thirty days about a figure of nine and it was said by a woman who carries water and had not seen the sheet.**

**And the nine words were these, and they were said at the mouth of a yard at the third hour of a working day by a woman going to a door, and nobody has said them since, and four of the people in that lane have repeated them without being asked and have not improved on them.**

*Somebody has written a number on a stranger's paper and that is all it is and go to work.*

---

**And on the twenty-fourth day a man of forty-six came back up the lane at about the second hour with his satchel and took the sheet off the bench and put it flat into the satchel without looking at it for longer than a second, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he did not look at it for longer than a second and that a man who does not look at a form he has come back for is a man who has been told what is on it, and that nobody in that yard told him, and that a man of forty-six who has left about three thousand forms at about three thousand doors in eight years and has never once looked at one that came back filled in has had one filled in on a bench in a yard in a city he had been in for three days.**

Then he said, “What is the nine.”

And the man of sixty-six said, without looking up, that it was a count of things this city had said no to, and that it was not a count of people, and that those were two different things and that he had never once had to make the difference until that afternoon.

And the man of forty-six took a stub out of his pocket and turned his list over and wrote it on the back of the list and not on the sheet, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of forty-six wrote it on the back of a list of about four hundred places rather than on the form, and that the two pieces of paper went into the same satchel and one of them has four nines on it and the other one has a sentence on it, and that the sentence is the only part of this that anybody outside this city will be able to act on and it is on the back of a list and not on the sheet and nobody was asked to move it.

**And that is the whole of the twenty-third and the twenty-fourth day, and there is not one other fact about either of them, and a few hundreds of people in this city did not know on the twenty-third that a figure was going to be written and did not know on the twenty-fourth that it had been, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the top of a lane two hundred yards off on both days and was not told and did not ask and has a page in a coat with an unfinished seventh line on it and has not finished it, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works at the second hour and at the ninth on the twenty-third and on the twenty-fourth and came down it twice each day and has never once been asked by anybody in this city what he is doing up there and has never once said, and the lamp was standing where it had been standing since the first day of this count of days and a few hundreds of people in this city did not look at it and about four did.**
