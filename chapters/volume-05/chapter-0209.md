# Chapter 0209

**The eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, a Sunday, and not a market day, and not a working day, and the fourth hour, and four hundred yards up this county's own road there is a crossing with no glass in its sill and about four people standing at it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is on the near abutment and a man of forty-seven is on the same abutment four feet off and a child of nine years old is at the low end of the lane with a bundle on one shoulder, and there is no waggon of salt coming over the lane, because the salt comes off a lighter at the wharf on a working day and the wharf is not taking a lighter off on a Sunday and there is no market, and a lane with a bridge over it that is carrying nothing is the only thing in this county that is different today.**

That last fact was the thing about the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, that some of them in Merefen noticed and as many again did not, and the four of them said afterwards that the lane over the first bay of the Sedge Reach was bare for the first time in anybody's memory, and that a road with a lane over it that is not carrying anything is a road, and that about four hundred years ago it was a road and nobody has known that in this century.

**And about forty men had been on that reach every working day since the spring, and on that Sunday there were four of them, and they came out of the town at about the fourth hour to look at a bridge in daylight because there was a man standing on it doing something that four people had said out loud was nothing, and they stood about for an hour and went home, and a few in Merefen have said that the four of them standing there was all of the watching and that it was about an hour.**

**A Sunday in Merefen is not a day off. It is the day the wharf does not take a lighter off, and the day the four coopers on Weir Street do not open their doors, and the day the reach is not pointed and the salt is not carted and the tide is at the wharf at the fourth hour and goes out again by the ninth. It is the one day in this county's week on which all of the work of a town of about four thousand people is not being done in the street, and a few in that town have said that this is the reason a Sunday is the only day in the week on which a man can be wrong in a yard without a man coming to look at it, and that this is not a religious observation and that it is a trade observation, and that nobody in Merefen has ever put the two together and about four people in Merefen could.**

The mason of fifty and a half did not come. The carter of thirty did not come. The two men of about twenty-six who were setting a course on the Friday were on the coast road at about the fourth hour with a load of lime and were two days gone. The boy of fifteen was at his mother's and had a bill in his head and copied the space under a number out of it for a quarter of an hour and then stopped, because there is no bill on a Sunday.


The tide was under the wharf. The market was not on and the lane was bare and the point on the parapet of the fourth bay is a foot and a half short and there is a mark in the parapet at the height of a thumb that nobody in this county can read at thirty feet, and none of that has anything to do with what was done at a toll four hundred yards up the same road, and two or three of them in this town have said that none of it does and a few have not said it either way.

---

**And he said the four things out loud, in about a minute, and he has said them three times in four years and this is the fourth, and the difference between the four of them is that the first three were said to a room and the fourth was said to a man standing on a bridge in daylight at the fourth hour of a Sunday.**

A line, and two hands, and a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it.

The four of them have been said three times before and this is the fourth, and the first was in a hall over a wharf in a county four hundred miles off at about the fourth hour on a Friday with a man of about sixty standing there who had said the same four words to one man in his life and was not going to say them again, and the second was at a sill in a county that is not on any map in about a minute, and the third was at a joint between two courses of stone at the fourth hour of a Monday, and each of the three was said to a person, and none of the three was said in front of about twenty men, and this one was said on a bridge in daylight to a man of forty-seven four feet away and the two of them were the only people in the county who could be got to that abutment.

The man of forty-seven said it back. He said it in under a minute and he said it in the same words, and about four people have said since that a man who repeats a thing back to you on a bridge is doing a different job from a man who hears it, and that the man of forty-seven has been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and has never once repeated anything, and that on the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, at the fourth hour, he repeated four words and got them exactly right.

“The fourth hour,” he said, “of the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314. And I said yes to it at the seventh hour on the sixth day before, and I am not asking for it to be written down anywhere, and I want it noted that I have not asked.”

“Nobody is going to write it down,” said the man of thirty-two.

“**Then it is a thing four people know and half of them heard the bell go afterwards and two of them have told other people, and in a decade there will be a man in this county who says the date was the seventh, and he will be wrong, and there will be nobody in this county who can tell him he is wrong, and that is the arrangement and I have been in it three times and I have not got a better one.**”

And the man of thirty-two said, “You have got a better one than I have got and I have been doing this for four years,” and the man of forty-seven said, “You have got a better one and you have not written it down, which is the same as not having got one, and I have not said that to be clever and I am not going to say it again.”

---

**And the bell went at about the fourth hour and a half, and it is nine stone of green-black bronze with a band of forty-one rivets round it and a dull red seam in the metal where the band was closed, and it is split lip to shoulder, and it is rung by a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is still not good at it, and by a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper and has a bar he is not using, and the arrangement is not anybody's and nothing in the sound of it belongs to anybody.**

He was about twenty-five yards from it. He had not rung it in four years and he did not ring it. He stood on the bank above the wharf and let a boy of seventeen and a carpenter do it and watched every stroke of it, and about four people in this town have said that the man on the bank watched every stroke of it, and a few have said that he went down to the wharf before the second one, and the two of those are the same afternoon and both of them are true and neither of them is a sentence anybody could put on a wall.

**And the bell went on that Sunday the way it goes on every day of the week including the days nobody is on the reach, and that is the ninth answer, and the man of thirty-two had written it on a page of foolscap on the Saturday with an hour against it, and the hour was the fourth hour and a half, and the page was in a coat, and the answer had been right for about a year and he had only just noticed it.**

---

**And then the man of thirty-two worked out something at about the fifth hour on that Sunday morning, and he worked it out standing up, and he did not sit down, and a couple of them in this county have said that a man of thirty-two worked a thing out standing up in about a minute and a half and did not say one word about it for the rest of the morning.**

He had prepared it. That was everything about the last four years and everything about the last four days. A line, and two hands, and a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it, and nine questions with an hour against each of the nine, and a page in a coat, and a bench that had refused to keep it and refused to witness it, and a clerk of about sixty who had entered two refusals in a minute under a stair and had read them back twice.

**And he did not know how it opened. He had never known how it opened. He had been standing on a bridge at the fourth hour on a Sunday about to do a thing he had done three times and he looked at the far side of a sill with no glass in it and understood, standing up, that the far side of a sill is opened by whoever is at the far end of it, and that the person at the far end of it is not him, and that the only other person in this county was a child of nine years old at the low end of a lane with a bundle on one shoulder, and that he had not asked the child a single question in six days, and that he was not going to.**

**And it was not a small thing and it was not a large thing, and it was the only moment in four years in which a man of thirty-two found out that the thing he had spent four years building was not his to build, and he found it out standing up at a toll on a Sunday morning with a stick in his hand that is for four hundred yards of road and nothing else.**

He had been to three ends of three crossings in four years and he had opened all three. That is the fact and it is a fact about him and about four other people and about nine hundred doors and a bench of nine in a valley that keeps no months. And what he had done at each of them was put a mark in a public thing in daylight, say a reason out loud before anybody asked, and then stand there and be the person who did it while somebody else who was not the person keeping it watched and afterwards could not remember the hour.

**And at the fifth hour of the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, on a bridge four hundred yards up this county's own road, in daylight, with about four people in the county, he worked out that the fourth of those is not his, and that the reason is not the mechanism and is not a rule and is not anything that could be put on a page, and that the reason is that a road is opened at the far end by whoever is standing at the far end, and that the only person standing at the far end was a child of nine years old, and that he had not asked the child a single question in six days.**

Nobody saw him work it out. A man of forty-seven was four feet off and was looking down the road. And then he took the page out of the coat, and he looked at the seventh line of it, and the seventh line said *a person*, and it had been looked at twice and had not been finished, and he folded the page in three and put it back in the coat.

> *A road needs a person at the far end, and the person has to be able to say no. Nobody has told you whether it can.*

He did not write it down. He has had four of those in four years and has written one down, and the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it and he cannot account for where it came from and nobody four hundred miles off can tell him, and there is nobody in this county he could ask, and he did not ask the man of forty-seven, who is four feet off and has said three times in two days that he is not going to be asked a fourth time.

---

**And the child came up the lane at about the fifth hour and a half, and it came the way it has come four hundred yards four times in two days, which is slowly, and with the bundle, and without stopping, and a man of forty-seven stepped out of its way on the near side of the lane and a man of thirty-two did not step out of its way because he was on the abutment and not in the lane.**

It had been at the low end of that lane on the Thursday and the Friday and the Saturday, and it had come up four hundred yards on the Friday and the Saturday and stood at the low end of that sill on both of those days for about an hour, and it had gone back down the lane each time at about the sixth hour, and about four people in this county had watched it go and a few had not, and a handful of them in this county have said since that the going and the coming is the part they cannot account for, and that a child that comes and goes on a fixed errand has a person at the other end of it, and that nobody in Merefen has ever sent for that child and that is not a thing anybody in Merefen would do.

Nobody spoke to it. The child came to the low end of the sill and stopped there, and looked at the stone, and then at the man of thirty-two, and then at the man's left hand, which was on the dressed block with the tarred hemp in it, and the child looked at that hand for in under half a minute.

**And the man of thirty-two saw a child of nine look at his hand, and understood that he had been about to do a thing on a bridge that was not his to do, and that the only person standing between this county and the far end of a road with no glass in its sill was a child who had come four hundred yards four times in two days and had not been asked anything, and some of them in this county have said that this is what he was thinking about for the rest of that morning and that it took him about four minutes and that he has not said it in nine words or in ninety and has said it once in about eleven.**

Then he took his hand off the block and stepped back off the sill and stood with his hands behind his back about a foot from the stone, and the man of forty-seven said, “That is the right of it,” and the man of thirty-two said, “It is,” and the man of forty-seven said, “I would have said so at the fourth hour and you would not have listened,” and the man of thirty-two said, “I would not have,” and neither of them said one word about what the right of it was, and about four people in this county have said that a man of forty-seven on a bridge at the fifth hour of a Sunday telling a man of thirty-two that he is wrong about a thing and being told he would not have listened is nineteen years of it of standing in yards in this town and it is the first time any of it has been about a road.

**And the bell was still going, or had been, or was going again, and the lane was bare, and the reach four hundred yards down the road had four men on it who had gone home, and the salt was not coming, and the child was at the low end of a sill with no glass in it, and a man of thirty-two was standing with his hands behind his back a foot from a stone, and there is no book in this county that has any of it in it, and the child did not speak, and the man of thirty-two did not ask, and the road was not open.**
