# Chapter 0207

**Two hundred miles off, in a provincial town, on the sixth of Embermonth, YR 314, and the second hour, and a man of sixty-one came into the room with the stove in it for the second time in three days, and he had been sent for, and the note that sent for him is in that office's working papers, and it is four lines long, and the fourth line asks for the thing he was told to take away, and a handful of them in that building have said that the note is the office at its most ordinary and that the office at its most ordinary is the one to be frightened of.**

He came with the satchel and without the jar. That was noticed. A man who has been told to take a thing away and who comes back in three days without it has done one of two things and some of them in that room were interested in which, and he said, before anybody asked, that it was in a lock-up in a yard in this town and that it was in a lock-up in a yard in this town because there was nowhere else in nine days that would have it, and that he had paid a man to sit on it and the man had not let anybody in and had not asked what it was, and that he had thought about that arrangement every day since and about four people have said that it is the most careful thing anybody in that building has heard a man describe doing.

**And the fourth line of the note asked for the thing, and the woman of twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns read the fourth line out, and he said no to it in under half a minute and it was not the first time he had said no that week.**

“I have brought you the two names and I have brought you the account of a market and I have brought you a line with no head on it with a place on it, and all three of those things cost me nine days up a pass and four days down and about two years of trying to get into a room over a weigh-house, and you have written me four lines and the fourth of them is the only one you actually want. **I have said no to the fourth one and I will say it again in nine days and I will not get difficult about it, because a man who says no twice is a man who has said no, and a man who says no once is a man who is asking.**”

**And before she said that she had asked him what the thing was worth, because a clerk of the returns in that building is asked that about four times a week and it is the whole of what she is for, and he answered it, and the addition was done on the page by the clerk of the press, who does that without being asked and about whom four people in that building have said that he is the only man in it who can turn a sentence into a column without losing the sentence.**

“I have been offered a hundred and forty marks for it by a house in a port that sells nothing else and buys everything anybody brings them,” he said. “**Forty-eight pence is a mark. Forty-eight, a hundred and forty times over, is six thousand seven hundred and twenty pence. That is the figure and I have had it in a letter for about eleven weeks and I have not taken it and I do not know what I am going to do about it, and I want it understood that I have not taken it and that I have not refused it either, and that a man who has not refused a figure is a man who is going to be asked for it again.**”

“And the house in the port.”

“Does not know what it is either. **That is all of the trade and it is everything I have found out in two years and I will give it to this room for nothing: nobody who has offered me money for that jar knows what is in it, and everybody who knows what is in it is in a place where money is not the thing.**”

The clerk of the press wrote the figure on a leaf of his own and did not put it in the working papers and did not say why, and two or three of them in that building have said since that a man who does an addition all his life and then does not file it has been in that building a long time and is tired of what he files.

“Then we will ask again,” said the woman of twenty-nine, and she was not being sharp and she was doing the exact thing her office does nine times a week, and about four people in that room have said that she said it in the tone of a woman writing a second line under a first one.

**And he was asked for the rest of it and gave about four minutes of it, and the four minutes are the reason the office has a file with a thing in it, and nobody in that room realised while he was saying it that any of it mattered.**

“There is a woman at the edge of that place who carries,” he said. “Not into the town. To the edge of it, and no further. I asked her what she carried and she said it was for somebody else and that she was not going to tell me for whom, and I asked her how long she had been doing it and she said four years, and I asked her what happens at the gate and she said she is asked to turn round, and that they are polite about it, and that being polite about it is everything that happens and has been for four years.

“**And she said the thing I have not been able to put down since I heard it, and I am putting it down now because there is nobody in this room to steal it from. She said: I am not refused because I am nobody. I am refused because of what I am carrying. Those are not the same and everybody who does it knows they are not the same and nobody has ever had to explain it to me before.**”

Nobody in that room said anything for about half a minute, and the woman of thirty-three who fills in the *Keeper* column wrote something on a leaf of her own hand and did not read it back and put the leaf face down in a book on the second shelf, and about four people in that building have said since that a woman who has been afraid of a column for eleven years wrote a note face down in a working book on the sixth of Embermonth, YR 314, and that nobody in that room asked her what it was, and that the not asking was in that room for about half a minute and that it was not entered anywhere, and that there is no book anywhere in that building in which a not asking could be entered.

“Did you pay her.”

“No,” he said. “**I did not pay her and I want that in whatever you make of me, because I have paid four people in nine days and she was the fourth and the only one of the four I would have paid if she had asked.**”

**And the machine of that building is not a mystery and is not a mystery to anybody in it, and a few in four floors could describe it in about four minutes, and it is the reason a thing in a tray by a stove is a thing nobody opens.**

There are about four hundred forms a month out of the press. There are nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls in them, and the rolls come down from the counties once in the season on paper that is good and that has been folded and is soft at the folds, and a roll is a list of names and a district is a Crown's district and there are about forty of them in this province and they were made in about the year after the Sundering by a man in this building who had a table and a list of the market towns, and a district is not a place, it is a set of market towns, and a market town is a place where a thing is bought and sold, and a couple of them in that building have said that all of the province's registration is a map of where people can be counted and that it is not a map of where anybody lives.

**And out of about four hundred forms a month about nine go back, and the nine that go back are the nine that a county has answered, and eight of the nine come back with a figure in them and one of the nine comes back with a space empty and four lines beside it, and the eight go into the returns press and the one goes into the returns press as well, because a return is a return, and the only difference between the eight and the one is what is in a column and there is no column in this building for what is in a column.**

**And on the seventh of Embermonth, YR 314, and the second hour, he came into that room for the third time in four days, and he came with the satchel and without the jar, and a good half of them in that room have said that a man who comes three times in four days to a room he does not work in is not a man with a grievance.**

There was nothing in the satchel. He said so before he was asked, and he put the satchel on the bench and opened it, and it was empty, and a few in that room looked at it, and a woman of about twenty-nine said that a man who opens a bag in a records office has told the office what he is, and he said, “So I have been told once already, and I have come back twice.”

**And they asked him for a figure, and it was the second asking for a figure of persons, and it was put to him the way the office puts everything, in a column, on a printed sheet, with a space for the answer and a line for the county.**

He read the sheet. He read it twice. And then he put it down on the bench and said, “I have two names and I have told you their spelling, and you have written them in a column with a head on it, and there is no figure in this county of any kind that will go in this space, and if you print a figure in this space it will be a figure you have made up, and everybody in this kingdom knows the difference between a figure of persons and a figure of persons you made up, and the difference is nine years old and it is in your own precedent on the second shelf, and I have read that one too, because I am a man who reads things.”

“Then we shall have to send it out with a space in it,” said the woman of thirty-three.

**“Then you shall have to send it out with a space in it,” he said, “and I would like to be in the room when it goes, and I have not been in a room when one of these went out and I have heard of two of them.**”

**And he said one more thing before he went, and it was about the jar and about nothing else, and about four people in that room have written it down afterwards in their own words and the four accounts do not agree and none of the four is a copy of the others.**

“You asked me for the fourth line twice and I have said no twice and I am still here for the third time, and I would like somebody in this room to work out what that is. It is not a sale. I have not got a buyer in this room and if I had I would not use one. **I think it is that you have written to me three times and I have written back three times, and a man can be written to, and a thing cannot.**”

And a clerk of the press said, “It is a man, yes.”

**“Then there is nothing to be frightened of in this room,” he said, “and I have been frightened of it for two years, and I would like somebody who is not me to look into that.”**

Nobody agreed to that and nobody refused it, and a handful of them in that building have said that the not agreeing and the not refusing is the office, entire, and that it has never once had to decide anything because a thing left the building anyway and a thing in a bag on the ordinary post does not need a room to agree to it.

---

**And at about the fourth hour of that Saturday the second asking went out, and it went out in a bag, and the bag went on the ordinary post, and some of them in that building watched it go to the door and a good half of them have said that they have watched a bag go out of that door every week for years and that it is the only thing in that building that anybody has ever been frightened of and that it goes every week.**

It is a return of the office and it is ruled in five columns with a sixth line at the foot that has no head to it, and the columns ask what the thing is, on what day, who had knowledge of it, who keeps it, and how often.

It goes to nine counties, and one of the nine has a return in the press with an empty space in it and four lines beside it, and one of the nine has not answered the first one and both of those facts are in the same press and about four people in that room have said that the press is the only honest part of the building and that the press has no opinion.

**And the space for a figure of persons is on that sheet, and it is ruled, and it has a head, and there is nowhere on that sheet, in any column, in any line, at the head or at the foot, for the name of a person.**

It has been the same form for two years. It was the same form when it was sent to a man in a county two hundred miles from that town in a sheet of good paper about the size of a broadside, with a hand and a room and a house and four shillings the week and a clerk in it, and it asked that county to be entered by number and not by name, and it did not have a number on it, and it has never had a number on it, and the number of persons that instrument was made out to has been asked for in a return and has not come and has been asked for a second time and has not come.

**And nobody in that room on that Saturday connected those two sheets. They were in two presses nine feet apart and both of them are returns of the same office and both of them are ruled to the same line within a hair, and two or three of them in that building have said that a form cannot be sent on, and that an office which builds a form so that the answer has to come back to it has built a form that a place which will not be written to can never answer, and that the office was right to design it that way, and that the office has never once been told that place exists.**

**And then the room went back to what it does, and about four people in that building have said that this is the part of the day that is in the record of the building and that it is recorded because the room does not stop, and a room that does not stop is a machine and a machine does not have a morning in it.**

The woman of twenty-nine entered the third visit in the book of working papers as a visit and put the day against it and nothing else, because there was nothing else. The woman of thirty-three filled in three *Keeper* columns out of a roll that had come down from a county in the west on the day before, and she did them in a fair hand and at the ordinary speed, and about four people who know her have said that she did them better than usual and that she has not been told that and would not have cared.

The man of about fifty went back to the press and locked it at the sixth hour and signed for the day, and a man of about sixty came in at about the seventh hour to take the tray by the stove out into the passage, because a tray is emptied into a passage and the passage is emptied in the morning, and there were eleven things in the tray on that Saturday, and one of them was four sheets in a hand that nobody in that building had read and nobody in that building was going to read, and it went out into a passage with the rest of them, and a thing in a passage is a thing that has not been lost and has not been kept and is not anywhere.

And a man of about fifty who is a clerk of the press and has kept his own book for thirty-one years said, to nobody, that the post goes where post goes, and that this is the most frightening sentence anybody in that building has ever said, and that a few heard it and as many again did not, and that he is not going to say it a second time, and that he is going to go home at the ninth hour and he has been a clerk of the press for thirty-one years and he has never before been frightened of a line of his own and he does not know what to do about it and he is not going to write it down.
