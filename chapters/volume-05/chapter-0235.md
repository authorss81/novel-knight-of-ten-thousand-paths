# Chapter 0235

**The thirty-eighth day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the second hour and the fourth hour, and a room at the top of a lane that goes up and not down with a long table in it and eleven people in it and a clerk of forty with an ink pot he shuts, and a yard at the bottom of a lane past the works and round by a bindle store with about two hundred bundles of cord on racks behind a bench, and a printed sheet in six columns with a place name and a word in the object column and four places ruled for a figure, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the end of nine hundred feet of paving two hundred yards from both of those places, and about four hundred people in this city do not know that a sheet went into a room at the top of a lane on the thirty-eighth day and that one did not and that the one that did has come back down.**

The man of about thirty-one came up the rise at about the second hour with the same sheet, and the object column had a place name and a word in it, and he put it on the table in the long room, and the door did not ask him his name. A man put a printed sheet on that table on the twenty-first day of this count of days and nothing came of it, and he has put a second one on the same table seventeen days later, and the difference between the two is that the object column was empty on the first one and is not empty on this one, and nothing else about the two sheets is different that anybody in that room can see.

**And a woman of sixty did not look up, and a clerk of forty put two lines in a book and did not read them back.** The clerk entered two lines on the strength of an omission, as he did seventeen days before, and that is the fifth time a thing has gone into that book on the strength of what somebody did not say, and the clerk has never once written the word omission and has never once needed to.

“It is not our sheet,” she said.

“It is the same sheet.”

“**It is the same series and it is not our sheet, and you have not been in this room seventeen days ago and I am not going to explain a difference to you that you have not asked me about, and I said once in four hundred years why there are eleven of us and I am not going to say a second thing to a man with a satchel.**”

Then she said the other one, and it is about eleven words, and it is the second time in nineteen years that a thing has come out of that room onto a man's round, and it is shorter than the first one, and she did not look up for either.

“**Take it down the lane. It goes to the man who makes the paper. That is the whole of what this room does and you have now been told it twice and you did not have to be told it once.**”

---

**And the man of about thirty-one took it down four hundred yards of lane and round by the bindle store and put it on a bench under a roof, and the yard was shut.** A man of sixty-six does not shut that yard. The yard is shut on about one day in seven, and nobody in it has ever been able to say where the man of sixty-six is on the day it is shut, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in this city thirty-eight days and has been told four things and got four straight answers and has not once told any of the four that they were being difficult.

The man of about thirty-one came back at about the fourth hour and said what the four places were for. He said it standing up in a yard, to about two hundred bundles of cord and to a man with a stick who was at the mouth of it and had not come in, and he said it in one breath, and it is the plainest description of an office anybody has ever given in this city and he was not complaining and was not accusing anybody and was saying a thing that is true about a room he has never been in.

“**Those four places want a number of people. That is all it is, and it is the same on every sheet of that series in four towns, and I have carried about two thousand of them in six years, and there is a number of people in every one of them and there is nowhere on any of them to write a man's name. You have had a form in this yard with four figures in it and a man has told me the same about that one twice, and I have carried both, and the both of them are the same instrument.**”

“Nobody in this yard has ever asked you that,” said the man of about thirty-one, to nobody, in that yard, on the thirty-eighth day.

“**About four people in four counties have, and three of them wanted to know how many and one of them wanted to know who, and the three are gone and the one is a man of about sixty with a stick at the mouth of my yard, and he is not going to get it out of me on a working day.**”

---

**And what the four places wanted was a number of people, and a number of people is the one thing this city has never put on a piece of paper.** A man of sixty-six has said out loud in a yard that a figure of persons is the only kind of paper that cannot be refused, and that this one has to be refusable or it is a lie with a stamp on it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp heard him say it on the sixth day of this count and has written nothing down since and is not going to.

To put a figure of persons against that object, somebody in this city would have to know how many people here have knowledge of it. Nobody in this city has ever been told what the object is, and the word in the object column is one that about four hundred people in this city have never seen written anywhere, and nobody in that city is going to be told.

“**Then it is a count of nobody,**” said the man of about thirty-one. “**That is a thing I have never had to carry. I have carried a blank and I have carried a figure and I have never once carried a sentence put in one of the four places, and if a man puts a sentence in one of the four places then the office has to have a column for it, and I have never seen that column on any sheet of that series in four towns.**”

Nobody improved on it. A man of thirty-two with a limp at the mouth of that yard worked out, standing still, that there were two answers available to four places on a printed sheet and that one of them was nothing and the other one was a figure, and that a man of sixty-six in this city had said out loud on the sixth day of this count that a figure of persons was the only kind of paper that could not be refused, and that the two of those facts were about nine hundred feet apart and in two yards that had never heard of each other.

**And the only figure of persons this city can put on a piece of paper is one that is already written down, and there is one of those.** A man of fifty-two told a stranger about it on the twenty-eighth day of this count in a glass yard, in about forty words. The sheet it is on is in a drawer in a room in this city. It has his name on it and the name of the man that was on it before him and the name of the man that was on it before him. It is a list of the third shift and it goes back further than anybody alive, and about a few hundreds of people in this city could be asked what the third shift is and not one of them would be able to tell you.

Nobody said it out loud in that yard. It had been said once, in a glass yard, nine hundred feet up the lane, on the twenty-eighth day, to a stranger, by a man of fifty-two, and nine men in that yard had heard it and about four hundred people in this city had not, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was one of the nine and had not said one word about it since and was not going to.

A man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the mouth of it for about eleven minutes on the thirty-eighth day and worked out that the answer to a printed sheet that is two hundred miles off is a drawer in a room in this city, and nobody in that yard worked out the same thing, and the two of them were eleven feet apart and neither of them said it. A man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard has said out loud that a man who is in a yard and a man who is at the mouth of a yard are eleven feet apart and about four years apart, and that it is the only arithmetic he has ever needed.

**And there is a second answer and nobody in that yard said it out loud.** To put a figure of persons against that object somebody would have to put a name to the object, and there is one man in this city who can put a name to a thing by putting his hand flat on it, and he is standing at the mouth of a yard with a stick. The second answer was in that room for about an hour and nobody in the room knew it was in the room, and the man who could have given it was eleven feet off.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp went four hundred yards up a lane to the mouth of a yard at the end of it past two gates on the thirty-eighth day at about the fifth hour.** There was a book on a bench about two inches thick and a man of sixty-one at it. The man of thirty-two did not go in and the man of sixty-one did not look up. A man of thirty-two with a limp has been to the mouth of that yard nine times in thirty-eight days and has been inside it twice, and the man at the bench knows both of those figures and has not been asked for either.

He asked the question he had come for. It is the seventh thing he has asked anybody in this city in thirty-eight days and it is about eleven words and it is the first one anybody in that lane has heard the whole of.

“**Is there anything in this city that a number of people could be written against.**”

Osmund Cusk turned a page over and did not answer it for about four minutes.

“**No,**” he said. “**And you have not told me what for and I have not asked you, and I want you to notice that I did not ask you and that you are about to tell me anyway, and that a man who is about to tell me is a man who has already decided, and I have had two people decide in front of me in nineteen years and one of them was me.**”

Nobody improved on it. A man of sixty-one at a bench put his hand flat on the second column of a book about two inches thick and left it there. He has put his hand on it about nine hundred times in nineteen years, and this is the first time he has put it there while somebody else was in the room.

---

**And a man of thirty-four with lime came down four hundred yards of lane at about the sixth hour to a yard where nine men were on a batch and stood at the mouth of it for about a minute.** He does that about once in a year, and when he does it he is not idle and is a man who has run out of things that are wrong. A man of about thirty-one put the sheet back in the satchel at about the sixth hour and said, to nobody, that he had nine days of his round left and one place on his list he had never been to, and that is the fourth of four things he has said in nine days in the tone of a man saying a price, and the fourth of the four was the only one about himself, and a man of forty-six said the same shape of thing in this yard a fortnight ago and did not hear him and was not told.

**And a woman of twenty-eight was in that yard at about the fifth hour and folded a bundle of cord for about twenty minutes and did not ask what the four places wanted, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-eight has been in a yard with a printed sheet on a bench and has folded cord and not asked, and that she is the only person in this city who is refused at three gates four days a week and is also the only person who has ever stood in a yard with a form in it and not asked a question about it, and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out that she is the most reliable person in the city.**

**And a man of about thirty-one went down four hundred yards of lane and out through the road gate at about the fifth hour and a half with a blank sheet in his satchel, and he was turned round at that gate on the thirty-seventh day and was not turned round at it on the thirty-eighth, and a man of fifty-five in a hut has never written down which of the two was which and has not been asked.**

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp walked four hundred yards down a lane and nine hundred feet of paving and turned round at the end of it and walked it back, and he had been doing that since the twenty-fourth, and about four hundred people in this city have stopped commenting on it and four have not, and the four have not met each other.** One of the four is a man of thirty-four with lime who put a piece of wood under a stranger's feet in a glass yard on about the twenty-third and has never once said why, and one of the four is a man of fifty with a waggon who has fed a stranger for thirty-eight days and does not know that anybody has noticed either thing.

**And the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day of this count of days, and the oven was hot at the second hour and at the ninth, and a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works with a bucket in one hand and his other hand on the top of it and nobody in this city has ever asked him what the bucket is for, and a man of thirty-two with a limp did not ask him on the thirty-eighth day and did not go four hundred yards to a glass yard at all, and about four hundred people in this city did not know that four places on a printed sheet wanted a number of people, and that a city of about nine thousand people has two books in it that could be made into one, and that the other one is in a drawer in a room that about four hundred people in this city could be told to open and that none of them has.**
