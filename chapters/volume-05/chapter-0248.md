# Chapter 0248

**The twenty-third day of Goatmonth, YR 315, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the sixth hour and the seventh hour, and a reach on a river four hundred yards down a lane from a town of about four thousand people with about forty men on it and the road open over it and the salt coming over the first bay, and a yard on Weir Street four hundred yards off with nine men in it, and about four hundred people in this town who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp walked four hundred yards up a lane at the sixth hour and stood at the mouth of that yard for eleven minutes and did not go in.**

The fourth bay was being pointed on the twenty-third as it was being pointed on the eighteenth and on the sixth of the month before that, and the fourth bay is the fourth bay of four, and the crossing season in this county is a quiet one and about forty men are on the reach every working day and the road is open over the top of it and has been open since the eighth of a month in the spring.

A mason of fifty and a half does the arches and does the pointing and does not do the laying. Two men of twenty-six are setting a course. A boy of fifteen is at the bench-board with a lime tub and a rule and a bill, copying the empty space under a number as well as the number, and he has been at that bench-board about two months and the man of thirty-two with a limp has known him for a week of fever. A carter of thirty has a barrow and a waggon of his own at the top end. Four men have hods. A man of nineteen carries a hod badly. And a man of forty-seven is on the deck of the first bay with a hod and has been since the spring and does not hold this reach and has never said so in a room.

**And it is a quiet season, and the quietness is a figure nobody in this county has taken, and there is no column for it.** The reach had thirty men on it through the wet weeks and has forty on it now, and the salt has been coming over the first bay since the spring, and the channel road has brought nine waggons up it since the spring, and the fourth bay is the last of four, and when the fourth is pointed the crossing season of this county is finished until the frost, and the frost is two months off, and nobody in that yard has said the word finished this year.

Three men stopped coming to work in that yard inside a year and none of the three gave a reason. A man of thirty-four with lime has not been on that reach for a year and a carter of thirty has not been up that bank since, and a man of fifty with a waggon has not come to the wharf on a working day since the spring. Four people in that yard have added the three of them up in their own heads and have not said the sum out loud, because a man who counts his yard and then counts it again in a week has made two figures where there was one, and that is the whole of what this volume is about and nobody on a reach can say so in nine words.

**And the joint on the parapet of that fourth bay is a foot and a half short, and it is short because the man who began it went home at the fourth hour, and he has not come back, nobody has asked him, and nobody in that yard has ever measured it.** The clerk of a bench of nine in a long room over a wharf was asked about it once and said that a clerk cannot measure a thing. There is a line in the third course of that bay about nine inches long under about half an inch of lime that has not moved, and nobody has measured that either, and nobody in that yard has said a word about it, and a man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that a man who puts a hand flat on a thing is not asking the thing anything and is telling it something, and that he said it once.

---

**And the salt came over the first bay and down the lane on that Tuesday for the ninth week running, and it is the salt merchant's salt and it is in his waggon and it is not the guild's, and the two of those are a price of two different things and they have never been in one column in this county.**

Nobody counted the barrows of it. A man of fifty with a waggon has been over that lane nine times a week since the spring and has never once been asked for a figure and has never once given one, and four people in this county have said since that a man who is not asked for a figure is a man who is not in a column, and that the whole of what happened four hundred miles off in the spring was a man being given a column, and that nobody in this county has worked out whether a man being left out of one is the same as a man being put in the other one and that nobody is going to ask.

**And the pointing was begun at the sixth hour on that Tuesday and it was not finished on that Tuesday, and that is the ordinary condition of a pointing course and nine men in that yard have said so and not one of the nine has ever said why.**

The man who began the parapet joint at the fourth hour is the man who began the pointing at the sixth, and he is a hand and not a foreman and he does a day's work and goes, and there were nine inches of joint left when the light went and there were nine inches of joint left at the end of that Tuesday and the same nine inches will be there on the Wednesday. Nobody stood looking at it. A boy of fifteen wrote a number in a bill and then wrote the empty space under it, and the empty space under that number is the widest of the four and he copied it, and the bench-board has a line of nine inches on it that is a hand's width from the edge and the two of them are the same width and about four hundred yards apart and nobody has ever compared them.

**And a waggon of stone came up the channel road at the fifth hour on that Tuesday and stopped at the wharf for an hour and a half and went back, and it was not for sale, and four people in this county have said since that the county has never asked whether it wants the stone.**

It came up a road that was not a market road before a bridge was open over a reach, and it went back down it, and the driver did not stop in the market and did not come four hundred yards up a lane to look at a reach. A carter of thirty has a waggon of his own at the top end of that reach and has been on this job since the spring and has watched nine waggons come up that channel road in four months and has not once been asked by anybody in this county whether there should be more of them and has not once asked himself in a way that anybody could hear.

“**A road open over a bridge brings people up a channel road who have not come up it before,**” he said, at the seventh hour, to nobody, in the tone of a man saying a price, “**and this county has never voted on it and would not know what to do with a vote. I have been on this job since the spring and I have not said that to a bench and I am not going to, because a bench that votes on a waggon is a bench that has voted on a road.**”

Nobody agreed with him and nobody refused him, and four people in that county have said since that the not agreeing and the not refusing is the same thing a bench of nine did in a long room over a wharf on the sixth of that month, and that a carter of thirty and a bench of nine arrived at the same place from opposite ends of the county, and that neither of them has ever heard of the other one, and that a third time a bench of nine is asked anything this year it will be asked it by somebody neither of them has met.

**And a woman of about thirty with a pail came through that market at about the second hour and said one sentence at a door in Weir Street and went on, and the sentence was that a man with a satchel had come through this market twice in Hearthmonth and had gone up the channel road both times, and that nobody had told her anything about it, and that she was saying it because the asking after a man with a stick had stopped on the nineteenth of that month and she had nothing else to do with the morning.**

Four people in that lane have said since that a man who comes through a market four times a year and a half twice and goes up the same road both times is on a list, and that the list is what he is paid for, and that the county is on it, and that about four hundred doors on that list are about four hundred doors on another list four hundred miles off, and that the two men holding the two lists have never met and are not going to, and that a woman carrying water to a door is the only person in that street who has said any of it out loud and she said it to a door and not to a person.

Nobody followed her and a woman of about sixty in Weir Street heard the sentence and did not repeat it, and about four people in this county have said since that the asking stopped on the nineteenth of Hearthmonth, YR 315 and that nobody two hundred miles off has ever been told that it stopped, and that a man with a satchel and about two thousand sheets behind him has been in a building in a provincial town every day since and has not been asked about either of the two.

**And a man of forty-seven was on the deck of the first bay with a hod all day and did not come off it, and he had been in a kitchen in Weir Street the evening before and had said the four things, and a carter of thirty was in that kitchen and has said that a man who says four things in a kitchen and then stands on a deck with a hod for nine hours has given the four things to the room and not to the yard, and that the yard has not had them and does not know they exist.**

---

**And at about the sixth hour a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick walked four hundred yards up that lane from the reach to the mouth of a yard on Weir Street, stood there for eleven minutes, and did not go in.**

There are nine men in that yard and there have been nine men in it for two months and they have not been asked about anything. Half the work in that yard is a customer's and not the yard's. A man of thirty-two with a limp has not been in that yard since the eighth day of a month in the spring and has not gone down that road in between and has not sent anything, and four people on that yard have noticed the road and none of the four has said anything about it to the other three.

**And one of the nine was holding a line, and the line was a mason's line, and he held it for four minutes and let it go and picked it up again, and he is twenty-five and he owns the crews and the yard, and there is nobody in that yard who works for him and about nine of them who are on his books.**

The yard is quiet in the way a yard is quiet when there is stone in it. From the mouth of it a man of thirty-two with a limp could see eight of the nine and part of the ninth, and he could hear one voice, and the voice said one thing, and it was a working sentence and not a speech, and it was said at the sixth hour and a half to a man with a barrow and not to anybody else.

“**That is a foot out and it will be a foot out on Thursday, and I have said it twice this week and I am not going to say it a third time, and I am not going over there to look at it.**”

Nobody asked him why. A man with a barrow said the word *right* and went over there and looked at it, and a man of twenty-five went back to the line, and four people in that yard have said since that a man who owns a yard and says a thing is a foot out and does not go and look at it has been in a yard a long time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what the looking is for.

**And that is the second time in a year that he has said one thing to a working yard and not asked a question, and the first time was in a room over a wharf with a bench of nine in it, and he is twenty-five and he told a woman of about seventy where his brother was and did not say anything else, and he has been out on every working day since the spring and has not once said that word to anybody on that yard, and nobody on that yard has asked him.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the mouth of that yard for eleven minutes and worked out in four seconds that he had been expecting not to be asked, and noticed the working out nine minutes after he had done it, and did not do anything about the nine minutes.**

He had a notebook. It was in the inside pocket of a coat over his arm and it has eleven lines in it and one new line and a separate leaf with a seventh line on it that says a person, and he did not take it out, and he has taken that leaf out four times in about five months and has not finished it four times.

**And that is the whole of what he did on the twenty-third day of Goatmonth, YR 315, and the not-writing is the beat, and nobody in that lane and nobody in that yard has any idea that there was a thing to write down.** A man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that a man who does not write a thing down is not a man who is keeping a thing out of a book, and that it is a man who has found out that nobody is going to read it, and that he said it once with a gather going and has not said it since.

A man of about nineteen with a hod came out of that yard at the seventh hour and went down Weir Street with the hod empty, and he has an elder sister of twenty-two in a house two streets off and has not said one word about her to anybody in that lane in a year, and he went past a man with a stick at the mouth of the yard without stopping and without asking anything, and four people in that lane have said that a man of about nineteen who walks past a thing and does not ask it anything has been taught something at home and is not going to say what.

---

**And the fourth bay was still a foot and a half short on the parapet at the seventh hour on the twenty-third day of Goatmonth, YR 315, and the salt was on the first bay and on the lane, and the road was open, and forty men had been on the reach and thirty-nine of them had gone, and the man of thirty-two with a limp went back down four hundred yards of lane with a stick and a coat and did not write down what a man of twenty-five not asking him a question had cost, and four people in this county have said that a man who has started expecting not to be asked has learned it from somebody and that learning it from somebody is a thing that cannot be put in a notebook because a notebook does not have a column for who taught it to you, and that he has not put it in one, and that he is not going to.**
