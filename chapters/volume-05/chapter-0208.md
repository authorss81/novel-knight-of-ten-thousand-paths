# Chapter 0208

**The seventh of Embermonth, YR 314, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour, and the market was on in this county's main street and about forty men were on the Sedge Reach with the road open and the salt over the lane and the fourth bay being pointed and the joint on that parapet a foot and a half short, and the bench of nine had sat at the seventh hour the day before and had gone, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was at a bench-board four hundred yards up the road at the Old Toll making nine questions and putting an hour against each of them, and the eighth of Embermonth is tomorrow and is a Sunday and is not a market day.**

The bench sat at the seventh hour on the Friday and it took about an hour and a half, and a handful of them were in the room, and the clerk of about sixty read all of that minute back twice and the second reading was slower and not better, and the book went back on the shelf under the window and the two marks were in the chest and the door was shut, and some of them in that hall have said that a minute with two refusals in it and a vote in it and a witness's line in it is the busiest piece of paper in this county and that it is nine feet of nothing else at all.

**And a clerk of about sixty who has kept that minute for nineteen years and has never entered a name in it that somebody else in the room did not put there first went up two flights at about the ninth hour on the Friday and looked at a page of foolscap that had been torn out of a copy of a form and had nine lines on it and an hour against each of the nine, and he did not ask what the nine were, and he read them, and he said one thing and went down again.**

“Nine again,” he said. “You did nine in the year before last at a door in this town and the year before that at a sill and the one before that somewhere I have never been, and you have never once asked me whether there was a column for them, and I have never once had to say no.”

“There is not one on the Friday,” said the man of thirty-two. “And there is not one on the Saturday and there is not one on a Sunday either, and that is the reason for the hour and not the hour.”

---

**And the nine, and the hour against each of the nine, and they were made up at a bench-board at the Old Toll in about four minutes with a chalk stub and no book, because his hand does that and a chalk stub does not get into a bag.**

**One. The hour. The fourth hour. He wrote it and left it.**

**Two. The day. The eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, a Sunday, and not a market day, and not a working day, and the reason for that is in the eighth.**

**Three. What the thing is. A road. He wrote it and left it, and a few people who have seen the page have said that a man who can answer that in one word has been thinking about it for about four years.**

**Four. Where it is. A sill, four hundred yards up this town's own road, on the near abutment, with no glass in it and no third seat and a dressed block in the abutment and forty yards of tarred hemp in it, and the coping on each side of that sill has nothing at all on it and had nothing on it for eleven days and nobody in this county has put anything on it and nobody is going to.**

**Five. Who keeps it. He wrote his own county and his own name and then he wrote *me* underneath it, and two or three of them who have seen the page have said that a man who has kept four other things for other people wrote *me* on this one and did not like it and did not cross it out.**

**Six. Who is at this end. A man of forty-seven on the bench of nine because he was elected, and not on a trade since the year before last, and paid fourpence a week out of a private box and not out of the chest, and he does not hold the reach and does not hold the Old Toll and does not hold the crossing, and he has not used his vote since the day he was elected and did not use it then, and on Friday he used it, once, and asked for the hour against it to be the hour he put it up.**

**Seven. Who is at the other end. He wrote *a person* and stopped, and went back to it about an hour later and it still said *a person*, and he wrote under it that he did not have a name for it and that he was not going to put one on a page in a bag, and about four people who have seen the page have said that the seventh line is the only one of the nine that is not finished and that it is the only one of the nine that had two attempts on it.**

**Eight. What it is for. He wrote, *there is no column for this and a clerk of about sixty in this county told me so in a room in Fallowmonth and entered nothing, and I have been carrying that sentence for about a fortnight and it is the eighth answer and it is the only one of the nine that is somebody else's.***

**Nine. What is there when nobody is on it. He wrote, *the sill, and the block, and forty yards of tarred hemp, and four hundred yards of road, and a bell with forty-one rivets in it that goes at the fourth hour and a half whether there is anybody on it or not, and a lane with salt on it twice a day, and a joint on a parapet a foot and a half short, and none of those things has my name on it and none of them has anybody's.***

**And the hour was against each of the nine, and the hours are not the same, and the first one is the fourth hour and the ninth one is the fourth hour and a half, and about four people who have seen the page have said that a man who puts an hour against nine questions in about four minutes is not a man who is asking and that if it is a list then it is the only list in four years that he has made and that the reason for it is that a man of forty-seven asked to be able to say afterwards what hour he agreed at and there was no column for that either.**

---

**And on that Saturday morning a boy of fifteen was at a bench-board on the Sedge Reach with a lime tub and a rule and a bill, and the bill is a piece of deal with a list on it of what came in and what went out and what was used, and the boy copies the empty space under a number as well as the number, and nobody in that yard has ever told him to do it and he has done it every day since the spring and four people in this town have noticed.**

He does it because a figure with nothing under it looks like a figure somebody did not finish, and a bill is a thing other men read when he is not here, and a man of about thirty who is the clerk of the works came past the board on that Saturday and looked at the bill for in under half a minute and said, “You are still doing that,” and the boy said, “Yes,” and the man went and did not say anything else about it and has not said anything else about it.

**And two hundred miles off, in a provincial town, in a press in a room with a stove in it, a form went into a returns press on that same Saturday with a space ruled and headed and empty in it, and a few in that building have said that a county had answered a column with nothing in it and that a boy of fifteen on a bridge four hundred miles away has been copying the space under a number since the spring for no reason anybody can put in a book, and that there is no instrument in this kingdom that can make either of those two answer a question that is not one, and the two of them are about four hundred miles apart and neither of them is a mistake and one of them is a boy.**

---

**And the Old Toll is four hundred yards up the road and it is in municipal trust in abeyance since before the bench existed, and the tolls on it are suspended by an instrument a court has found lawful and has said it has nothing further to say about at any time, and the crossing has no owner and no money and no seat on that bench and no glass in the sill and no third seat, and there is a dressed block in the abutment and forty yards of tarred hemp in it, and the bell is nine stone of green-black bronze with a band of forty-one rivets and a dull red seam in the metal, split lip to shoulder.**

There is no third seat because there were two and one of them rotted out a good many years ago and nobody put anything in its place, and the sill is a stone a foot and a half wide with nothing whatever in it, no glass and no frame and no mark and no chip, and the coping on each side of it has nothing on it at all.

**And that is the place, and the reason it is the place is not a figure and not a mark and not the thing in the stone course in the fourth bay of the reach four hundred yards down this town's own road, and it is that there is no glass in this sill for a person to look through and see whether anybody has come, and a man who is going to stand at a thing and wait needs a thing with no glass in it, and about four people in this county have said that, and as many again have said that it is the reason and that it is a trade reason and that a man who is waiting is not looking.**

**And the point of that crossing, for a man who had been preparing things for four years, is that there is nothing to make. A dressed block is in the abutment and forty yards of tarred hemp is in it and both of those were put there on the fourth of Hearthmonth of the year before by four men and a boy for a job that is not this one. A sill a foot and a half wide with nothing in it. Two seats and the third rotted out a good many years ago. A road four hundred yards down to a bridge with a foot and a half of pointing short on it and a mark in the parapet at the height of a thumb. A lane with salt on it twice a day and a market four times a fortnight and a bell that goes whatever anybody is doing.**

Everything a road wants to be a road is already in this county and has been for about two hundred years, put there by people who were not thinking about any of it, for a toll that a court has suspended and cannot say anything more about at any time. **And about four people in this county have said that this is what a road is and that the only thing a county has to add is a reason, and that this county has never had a reason, and that it has had a dressed block and forty yards of tarred hemp sitting in an abutment for two hundred years waiting for one, and that a thing which is prepared and not agreed is the most ordinary thing in the world and this is the first time in this county that anybody has noticed the difference.**

And a man of fifty with a waggon of his own said one thing about it on that Saturday at about the fifth hour, standing at the low end of the lane where the salt comes over, and a few were within thirty feet of it.

“A road is not a thing you are given and it is not a thing you are refused,” he said. “I have been on both ends of both for most of my working life and I have never once been on a road that somebody had to agree to, and I have never once been on one that anybody had to refuse, and I have carried things over about four hundred miles of them and everything I know about a road is that it is there and I did not make it. **Ask me about a road and I can tell you what it carries and how much and how often. Ask me whether it should be there and I have got nothing, and I have got nothing because nobody has ever asked me, and I would like that noticed by somebody who is about to be asked.**”

---

And the bell went at about the fourth hour and a half, and it is rung by a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is still not good at it, and by a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper and has a bar he is not using, and the arrangement is not anybody's, and there is a page at the head of the yard's book for the week with the hour against it in a hand somebody would know, and nobody wrote a word in it about who rang the bell, and nobody is going to.

---

**And a child of nine years old was at the low end of the lane on that Saturday, and had been there on Thursday and Friday and Saturday, and had been up the lane to the toll on the Friday and the Saturday, and a few in this county have said that a child of nine does not walk four hundred yards up a road with a bundle on one shoulder three days running unless it is waiting for something, and a few have said that a child of nine does exactly that and is waiting for nothing and has said so.**

Nobody asked. That is the fact of the seventh of Embermonth, YR 314, and it is a fact about a town of about four thousand people, and some of them in this town have wanted to ask for six days and a few have not, and one of the four is the man of thirty-two and he did not ask and has not said why and is not going to.

**And the child has a mark on the left hand, and it is a line and a bar and a figure, and it is not a scar and it is not a burn and it is not ink, and a few people in this county have seen it and two or three have said nothing about it and as many have not looked, and the man of forty-seven looked at it on the Friday for about a minute and a half and said nothing at all and has said since that it is a child's hand and that he was not going to be the man who asked a child about a hand.**

And the child did not speak. About four people in this county have said that a child of nine who is waiting for something and does not speak is doing one thing, and as many have said that a child of nine who is not spoken to does not speak, and neither of those is a finding and there is no book in this county that either of them is in.

---

**And it is going on a Sunday, and the reason for it is not the Sunday, and the reason was said out loud on that Saturday at about the sixth hour to four people in a yard and a few were within thirty feet of it and two of them have repeated it since.**

“A Sunday is the one day in this county's week that is neither a working day nor a market day,” he said. “That is why. **And the reason is that a Saturday is the one day anybody in this county can be asked a thing, and the bench sits on Saturdays, and if I went on a Saturday then within four hours there would be a man in that room with a minute, and there would be a question put to me in the ordinary way by a man who is doing his job, and the question would be what it is for, and I have not got an answer for it that will do in a minute, and I would have to stand in that room and be asked it in front of nine people on a market morning, and about four hundred people would be in the street outside.**”

“And the bench.”

“The bench would sit and it would ask and it would be told no, or it would be told nothing, and either of those would go in a book under a stair, and a road that has been in a book under a stair in this county is a thing this county has agreed to and I have not asked it and I am not going to. **And I am not going to be asked either. There is not a clerk in this county who could sit on a Sunday and hold a minute if they asked him to, and there is not a man in it I would want to be asked by. So it is a Sunday.**”

Nobody improved on it. The mason of fifty and a half said one thing about it, which was that a Sunday is the only day a man can be wrong in a yard without a man coming to look at it, and that he had been working six days a week since the spring and would take the Sunday, and went back to his stone.

**And at about the seventh hour on that Saturday the man of forty-seven came up four hundred yards of lane and stood at the near abutment of a crossing with no glass in its sill and put his hand flat on the dressed block, the way a man puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the block was cold, and he took his hand off, and he said, “I have been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and I have never once known where either of them went, and I am going to do it a third time tomorrow at the fourth hour, and I have said yes, and I said it yesterday, and I would like it in the book that I said it before and not after, and there is no book between here and that hall and I am not going up two flights at the ninth hour on a Saturday to enter a line, and so it is said here and about nine of you have heard it.”**

And nine of them had heard it, and four of them have said since that a man of forty-seven who has not voted in two years said yes to a thing on a Saturday at the seventh hour on a Saturday, and that a Saturday is the one day in this county's week when a man can be asked a question and a man said yes, and that this is the only answer anybody in this county has given anybody in about four years, and it was given to a few in a yard at a toll with no glass in its sill, and it was not entered in anything.

**And the page of foolscap with nine lines on it and an hour against each of the nine went into a coat at about the eighth hour on that Saturday, and the seventh line said *a person* and had been looked at twice and had not been finished, and the child was at the low end of the lane, and the bell was rung at the fourth hour and a half by two men who are not him, and the road was open, and about forty men were on the reach, and the salt was on the lane, and the tide was under the wharf, and tomorrow is the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, a Sunday, and the fourth hour, and there is nothing in this county that has been agreed to and something in this county that has been refused and the man who is going to be at the one end of it has written down the other end of it as a person and has not got a name.**
