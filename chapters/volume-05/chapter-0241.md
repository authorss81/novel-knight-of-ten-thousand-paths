# Chapter 0241

**The twenty-fourth day of Hearthmonth, YR 315, a Monday, and the second hour, and the sixth hour, and the seventh hour, and a room with a stove in it in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county that this office has never had a paper out of, and a tray by that stove with four sheets of the office's own printed series in it that have been in it since the sixth of Embermonth in the year before, and a fifth thing that came into that room in the ordinary post on the morning of that day, and four people in that building do not know that all five of them came from the same place, and not one of the five is a question.**

The tray is not a file. That is the first fact about it and it is the reason four sheets of the office's own printed series have lain in it for about five months without one person in that building knowing what was on them. A man of about sixty takes a tray out of that room into a passage at about the seventh hour of the evening and brings it back at about the first hour in the morning, and he has done it for twenty-two years, and nobody has ever asked him what is in it, and a tray is emptied into a passage and a passage is emptied in the morning, and a thing in a passage is a thing that has not been lost and has not been kept and is not anywhere.

He does not look at the sheets. That is not carelessness. It is the trade, and a man who has carried a tray for twenty-two years knows that about four hundred things go through a building in a month and that the whole of his usefulness is that he can tell the difference between a thing that is going somewhere and a thing that is not, and he has never once in twenty-two years been wrong about it and has never once been asked to be right about it.

**And the four sheets are of the office's own printed series.** They are about the size of a broadside with a little less paper than a broadside. They are ruled in six columns, five of them with heads and a sixth with no head at all and the same width as the other five, and about four of the six are ruled for a figure. Nothing at all is filled in on any of the five that have heads, and that is not a thing anybody in that building has ever been surprised by, because a county that does not answer a form does not usually answer it at all, and about nine of about four hundred go back and eight of the nine come back with a figure in them and one comes back with a space empty and four lines beside it.

They came into that room in the ordinary post on the sixth of Embermonth in the year before, and the clerk who takes the post out of that room every morning has never read one of them and is not going to, and a man of about fifty who is a clerk of the press and has kept his own book for thirty-one years has said out loud that the post goes where post goes, which is the most frightening sentence anybody in that building has ever heard said and which he is not going to say a second time.

**And the four of them came from one county in one bag, and four people in that building worked that out on the day they arrived and nobody has said it out loud since.**

The bag was a bag like any other bag. It came up from the south-west with two rolls in it and a bundle of string and a sheet of good paper about the size of a broadside that had been folded once and had been opened, and the four sheets of the printed series were in it between the string and the sheet of good paper, and a man of about fifty took all four of it out on the bench and looked at it for about a minute and a half and then said the one thing anybody in that room said about the bag, and it was that the string was not tied the way string is tied in that province and that it was not untied either, and that somebody had opened it and put it back and had wanted it to look as though nobody had.

Nobody in that building has ever worked out who opened it. It went into the tray with the rest of the things that were in the bag and a tray is not a file and a bag is not a tray and the two of them are the two places in that building where a thing can be for a long time and not be anywhere.

And the sheet of good paper is the one that has been in a press in a county two hundred miles from the room this room is in for about two years, and it has a number of the office against it and nothing at all against a person, and a man of about fifty looked at it on the day it came out of the bag and then put it in the working papers on the second shelf, and four people in that building have said that he did not put it in the returns press and that the returns press is where a sheet of that office's own making goes, and that he has not been asked about it and that he has not offered.

---

**And the machine of that building is not a mystery and is not a mystery to anybody in it, and about four people on four floors could describe it in about four minutes, and it is the reason a thing in a tray is a thing nobody opens.**

There are about four hundred forms a month out of the press. There are nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls in them, and the rolls come down from the counties once in the season on paper that is good and that has been folded and is soft at the folds. A return goes out to nine counties and about nine come back, and eight of the nine come back with a figure in them and one comes back with a space empty and four lines beside it, and all nine go into the returns press because a return is a return and the only difference between the eight and the one is what is in a column, and there is no column in this building for what is in a column.

The forms are ruled in six columns. Five of them have heads. The heads are *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper*, and *How often*, and the fifth of them has been the same head for two years and four people in that building have said that a head cannot be changed without a decision and that no room in that building has ever taken a decision in writing, and that the words at the foot of the form in a smaller type have been the same words for two years and that nobody in the building has ever asked whose they are or where they came from and that about two or three of them think about it perhaps twice a year.

The sixth column has no head on it and it is the same width as the other five. A line with no head is not a column, and is not entered, and is not kept, and a woman of about thirty-three who fills in that *Keeper* column about two hundred times a season said so out loud on the fourth of Embermonth in the year before, in a room, to a man who had brought a jar, and four people in that building have said that she has been the only person in it who has ever said that sentence out loud and that she said it as a fact and not as a complaint.

---

**And then the fifth thing came in on the morning of the twenty-fourth day of Hearthmonth, YR 315, and it was in the ordinary post, and it was a sheet of the same printed series with a thing written on it.**

A woman of about twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns took it out of the sack at about the second hour and put it on the bench and read the object column and then read it again, and four people in that building have said since that she read it twice and that the second reading took her longer and was not better, and that nobody in that building has worked out why a woman who reads about four forms a day read one of them twice.

The sheet is about nine inches by six. It is folded once across the middle and soft at the fold, and the fold is soft because it has been folded and unfolded more than once, and the four places that are ruled for a figure are empty, all four of them, and there is no mark in any of them and no dash and no word for nothing.

**And the object column has a place name in it, and there is a word in front of the place name.**

The word is *eastern*.

Nobody in that room could read the sense of it. Not the woman of twenty-nine, who is asked about four things a week and can turn any of them into a column in the ordinary way, and not the man of about fifty, who is the only man in that building who can turn a sentence into a column without losing the sentence, and not the man of about sixty with the tray, who cannot read it at all and says so and is not asked to.

**The word is one the office printed.** That is the part of it that four people in that building have said since is the strangest thing about the whole morning, and the part that none of them has been able to put anywhere. The type for it has been in a drawer in the press room since the form was made, which was two years ago, and it is set in the line with the heads of the five columns and it is not used in any of them, and nobody in that building has ever asked a printer to strike it and nobody has ever struck it, and a man of about fifty went into the press room at about the sixth hour and looked at the drawer and then came out and did not say anything about it to anybody.

“I cannot read the sense of it,” he said, to a room, and he said it in the ordinary way, and then he said the other half of it and the other half is why the morning of the twenty-fourth day of Hearthmonth, YR 315 is on this page at all.

“**It is our word. It has been in our own drawer for two years and nobody in this building has ever had a use for it, and it has come back to us off a sheet of our own series with a place in front of it that this office has never had a paper out of, and I am going to say three things about that and then I am going to go back to the press.**”

Nobody improved on it. A woman of twenty-nine wrote two lines on a leaf of her own hand and put the leaf in a book of working papers on the second shelf, and she did not read them back, and four people in that building have said since that a woman who reads a form twice and then writes two lines on a leaf and does not read them back has been in that room for about six years and has never once done it before.

---

**And the place name is not a market town, and four people in that building found that out before the seventh hour and did not tell each other they had found it out.**

A district is a Crown's district. There are about forty of them in this province and they were made in about the year after the Sundering by a man in that building who had a table and a list of the market towns, and a district is not a place. It is a set of market towns, and a market town is a place where a thing is bought and sold, and that is the whole of what a district is and it is why all of this province's registration is a map of where people can be counted and not a map of where anybody lives.

The place name in the object column is not a market town. It is not a place where a thing is bought and sold. It is a place with about four thousand people in it on a river, a weigh-house with a door on a lane off it, a bridge four hundred yards up the road with no glass in its sill, and a market four hundred yards long that is on twice a week, and the name in the object column is a name nobody in that building had ever had a paper out of.

A man of about thirty-one carried that sheet to a door. He came down a road and up a rise with it in a satchel and he left it at a door in a town about two hundred miles from the provincial town this room is in, and it came back into the ordinary post nine days later with a place in its object column that had been written in a different hand from the printing, and four places ruled for a figure with nothing in any of them, and he has carried about two thousand sheets of that series in six years and has never once known what is in the four places and has never once asked.

**And four people in that building have said since that a man who has carried two thousand forms to two thousand doors in six years and has never asked what is in the four places is the most ordinary person in the province, and that the most ordinary person in the province has just sent this office a place it has no district for.** And nobody in that room asked the woman of twenty-nine a second question, and she did not volunteer one, and the man of about fifty went back to the press at about the sixth hour and half and locked nothing because there was nothing in it to lock.

---

**And then the sheet went into the tray, because a return is a return, and that is the whole of the office's answer to it on the twenty-fourth day of Hearthmonth, YR 315.**

A return goes into the returns press. That is the rule and it is older than the man who said it and every clerk in that building could say it in nine words and about four of them have said in about nine years that they have never known it broken and that they would not know what to do about it if it were. And a return cannot go into the returns press, because the returns press is ruled for a figure and a thing that is not a figure is not a return, and the woman of twenty-nine said that in about eleven words and then said the other half of it and the other half is the whole of what this office is.

“**It is not a return because there is nothing in the four places. It is not a case because there is no case. It is a sheet of our own form with a place on it that we have never sent a form to, and there is nothing in this building that a thing like that goes in, and I am not going to make one.**”

“Where does it go then,” said the man of about fifty, from the press, and he had come back out of it.

“**In the tray,**” she said, “**because a tray is not a file and nobody has ever had to say so out loud before.**”

Nobody said anything to that. A man of about sixty took a tray out of that room into a passage at about the seventh hour of that Monday evening and there were five things in it, and four of them had been in it since the sixth of Embermonth in the year before, and a man who has carried that tray for twenty-two years did not look at any of the five and has not been asked and would not answer if he were.

And a thing in a passage is a thing that has not been lost and has not been kept and is not anywhere, and a sheet of a printed series with a place in its object column that is not a market town, and a word in front of that place that this office printed itself two years ago and has never used, and four places ruled for a figure with nothing in any of them, went into a passage in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county on the evening of the twenty-fourth day of Hearthmonth, YR 315.

And four people in that building knew that all five things in that tray had come out of the same county, and about nine hundred did not, and one of the four is a man of sixty-one with a satchel and a small hammer he has never used who has been in that building since the middle of last year and can find a thing in it in about four minutes, and he has not said one word about a tray since the middle of last year and has not been asked.
