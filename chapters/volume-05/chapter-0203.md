# Chapter 0203

**The thirtieth of Fallowmonth, YR 314, a Saturday, and a market day, and the market in this county is on a Saturday and on a Thursday and twice a week and that is all of it, and the market was on this county's main street from the second hour until about the sixth, and the bench of nine sat in the long room over the wharf at the seventh hour, and about forty men were on the Sedge Reach four hundred yards down the lane with the road open, and a reeve of this town put a seventh thing on a table and a bench said no to it in eleven words and a clerk of about sixty entered the reason twice.**

It was a market morning and a market day is the one kind of day in this county's week on which anybody in this town can say what day it is, and a few in Merefen can tell you that the thirtieth of Fallowmonth is a Saturday and a market and a Saturday the bench sits, and as many again could not tell you whether the seventh of next month is a Saturday, and there is no figure in that minute for the days and there never has been.

The reeve of this town is a magistrate's elder sister and does not vote on the guild's own matter and has not for nine years, and she came up two flights of that stair at about the sixth hour with a paper in her coat and she put it on the table, and the clerk entered that she put it, and the clerk read the entry back, and that is the third time in nineteen years that a thing has been brought up two flights and put on a table in a minute of that hall, and the other two are on the page and can be read by anybody who comes up that stair.

**The seventh thing was this: that the bench of nine record its consent, in that minute, to a road out of this county, and enter the consent as a consent, and keep the road as a thing it had consented to, and answer for it in a year.**

It took her about four minutes to say and the clerk wrote it as she said it and did not tidy it, and a man of about fifty with a waggon of his own was in the room and had a page of a waggon-book on the table in front of him that he did not open, and a handful of them were in that room, and the tide was under the wharf and could be heard against the piles if nobody said anything for about a minute.

The word she used was consent. She used it four times and the clerk entered it four times, and some of them in that room have said since that the seventh thing was in that word entire and that the bench has never once in nineteen years been asked for its consent to anything and has given it four times to a crossing four hundred yards up this town's own road, and that the fifth asking of those was brought up two flights and not put, and that a bench which has said yes four times to the same thing without being asked has not consented to it, it has been asked and it has not found a reason to refuse, and that is a different thing and the clerk has both of those in the minute in nineteen years of hand.

---

**And then the man in the chair said the reason, and it was eleven words long, and it is the second shortest thing that has ever decided anything in that hall.**

“Two marks in the chest and this ask is not money.”

Nobody in that room argued with it for nine seconds, and the nine seconds were the shortest of that sitting, and about four people in that hall have said since that the first of those nine seconds in the last of Harvestmonth is the only time this hall has ever decided anything and that this is the second and that the two of them together are the only two sentences in nineteen years that this county has ever said out loud about what it is for.

The man of about fifty put his hand flat on the waggon-book.

“**That is correct,” he said. “I have been in nine rooms in eleven weeks about money and in eight of them the money was the argument and in one it was not, and I came to this room on the fourth of Harvestmonth and got the one where it was not, and I have said since that I would like it to be the last I remember, and I have not stopped saying it, and I would like it entered that I said so before I said anything else, and I am not going to argue with a bench that is right in front of a market morning.**”

And then the reeve said the half of it that was in her own mouth, and she did not vote on it and has not voted on the guild's own matter in nine years, and she was there for about eleven minutes and a third of what she said was the reason the no is the right no and not the easy one.

“Then it is a no, and I am glad it is a no, and I want the minute to carry that I said that, and I want the reason twice, because a bench that says no for money has refused a thing it could not afford, and a bench that says no for anything else has refused a thing it does not want, and this county has never in the whole time of the bench got the first and I am not going to let it put the second down in a book under a stair and have it read in a generation as the first.

“**And I want it entered that this is the second time this bench has refused a thing for a reason. Five askings, four yeses and a fifth not put, and a sixth in Harvestmonth with a figure of forty marks on a page and a man who said that was correct before he said anything else, and now this. I would like the minute to say that the two refusals are the two refusals and that there is nothing else in this book under this stair that has ever been refused for a reason, because in about forty years somebody is going to come up these stairs wanting to know what this county thinks about roads, and there is going to be one line in this book that answers it, and I want it to be the right one.**”

The clerk read that back and entered it and read it again, and the second reading was slower and not better, and that has been the practice of that room since the first year.

**And then the magistrate of this county, who is the reeve's sister and has been at that bench for nine years and has beaten two counts of persons with a form's own construction and is frightened of her own answer, put the reason for the no into the minute instead of voting with it, and the reason is four or five words and they are these: that a consent entered in a minute is a consent somebody cannot take back, and a road is a thing that has to be able to be un-consented in about four years.**

“That is a document and not a bench,” she said. “And I have put my reason in a book under a stair four times in nine years and I have not voted on this one, and the reason I have not voted on this one is that a woman who has been frightened of her own answer for nine years does not get to cast the deciding hand on a question that a clerk of this county is going to read out in forty years. **Enter it as a thing I said. Do not enter it as a vote. A vote would make me one of nine and I would rather be a woman in a room saying no than one of nine, and that is the least of my reasons and it is the one I can say out loud.**”

And a man of about twenty-four who came on the roll when the man of thirty-two left the county said, out loud, that he wanted to know what the difference was, and the magistrate said it was the difference between a reason and a vote and that he would find out about forty years from somebody else, and two of them in that room laughed and two of them did not, and one of the two is the clerk.

---

**And one hand went up that has not gone up since the day it was elected, and the clerk entered it, and the clerk has entered the figures of hands in that minute nine times in nineteen years and this is the tenth, and this is the first one that was a man of forty-seven.**

He did not say anything. He had been asked, and he had been in the room at the seventh hour, and the question had been put in the ordinary way and the question was whether the bench consented to a road, and a man of forty-seven who is on that bench because a man in a market asked who was going and who has not used his vote since the day he was elected and did not use it then put his hand up with the rest of them, and a few in that room watched him do it and two of them have said since that they were not looking at him and were looking at his hand.

“Put the hour against it,” he said, when the clerk had the pen down. “Put the hour I put it up and not the hour you write it. And put that I did not say anything, because I did not say anything, and a man who has not voted in two years ought to have it written down that his first vote had no speech in it, and in about forty years somebody will want to know whether I was carried or whether I was quiet, and I was quiet.”

**The clerk wrote it. He read it back twice. And it is in the minute in a clerk's hand, and about four people in that hall have said that a man of forty-seven was paid fourpence a week out of his own box for that vote and that he is not paid anything else, and that the chest under the window with two marks in it did not move while he did it.**

---

---

**And a market morning is the reason that hall is over a wharf and not up a lane, and about four people in this town have said that the wharf is where the money changes hands and the hall is where the money does not, and that a town of four thousand people which keeps one room over a wharf for its refusals is a town that has worked out what it is for.**

The market on that Saturday was salt, fish, peat, one load of glass, four barrels of something out of the low country, and a waggon of stone out of a quarry eleven miles up the channel that was not for sale and stopped at the wharf for about an hour and a half and went back, and a few in Merefen could tell you that the last time there was glass in a market in this town was a market in Embermonth two years ago and that it is a bigger fair this month than the one before, and that nobody in this town has asked why, and that the answer is that a bridge is open and a road open over a bridge brings people up a channel road who have not come up it before.

**And the reach was busier on that Saturday than on any working day in the month, because a market morning in this town is a morning when about four hundred people come up from the eleven steadings and the four hamlets and stand about in a street, and about forty men were on the Sedge Reach with the road open and a waggon of salt over the lane at the sixth hour and the fourth bay being pointed and the joint on that parapet a foot and a half short, and the man of forty-seven with a hod had come down off the deck at about the fifth hour and stood at the top of the lane and looked at the market for about a quarter of an hour and then went back up onto the deck, and four men in that yard have said that he does that on market mornings and as many have said that he does not do it on the other mornings, and neither of those has been said in a room.**

---

**And the clerk had all of it in front of him in nineteen years of hand, and a couple of them in that room and he among them, and he read the count out because nobody else in that hall has ever read it out and because the seventh thing had been put on a table with a word in it that had never been used in that room before, and the count is this, and it is on a page under that stair, and it is not in the minute because a count of askings is not a thing a minute holds.**

The bench of this county has been asked five times for the crossing four hundred yards up this town's own road, and has said yes four times, and the fifth asking was brought up two flights by the reeve of this town on the last day of the year before and was not put, and the minute says that she did not put it and that the reason is a sentence a carpenter said in that hall in the fourth of Frostmonth, and it carries that sentence word for word, and there is no second version of it in this county or in any other and there is not going to be one: that a bench asked three times and saying yes three times is not a bench, it is a subscription.

The sixth was a road to a valley four hundred miles off with a figure of forty marks on a page and the addition done out at every line, and the bench said no in about a minute and a half in eleven words, and the reason went in twice, and a man of about fifty said that was correct before he said anything else, and there is a sentence about not being able to be listened to without a reason that has nothing to do with the asking in that minute as well.

And now the seventh, and the eleventh words, and the reason twice.

**And the clerk said the thing he says when a number has been read out in that room, which is that five askings and four yeses and two refusals and a fifth asking not put is nine things that happened in a room over a wharf in nineteen years, and that there is no figure anywhere in that minute for the roads this county has or has not got, and that the number of times this bench has been asked is on a page under that stair and the number of roads in this county is a thing a man stands on the Sedge Reach at the fourth hour and looks at, and that a clerk cannot put that in a book, and that he has tried twice in nineteen years and has twice been told by a man of fifty-eight four hundred miles off, through a woman with a door on a lane, that a figure of days does not go in a book, and that he believes them and does not know why.**

---

**And then a man of thirty-two with a limp asked the bench for one more thing, and it was not money, and the man in the chair said no to it in a way the clerk has entered in full because he said afterwards that it was the politest thing he has ever had to enter.**

The thing asked was that the bench witness it. That one of the nine stand at this end of the road and be the witness at this end and not the person keeping it, which is the only arrangement this county has ever had for a road and has used twice in nineteen years, once at four hundred yards up this town's own road and once at a sill in a county four hundred miles off where the witness was the same man and was paid nothing and wrote nothing down.

“We cannot,” said the man in the chair. “**We have been asked to keep a road and we have said no, and a body that has just refused to keep a thing cannot be asked to stand at the end of it and say it saw. A witness is a person, and a person can be asked what it saw, and if it says something nobody else saw then one of two things happens and the clerk writes one of them down. This is a bench. A bench answers in a minute, and the minute is under this stair, and in about forty years the two of those will have got into each other and a man who is stood at the end of a road will be a line in a book.**”

“Enter it,” said the clerk, to the man in the chair, and then to the man of thirty-two, and he entered it in about eleven words, and he entered that it was said politely and that nobody in the room had been raised in their voice, and a good half of them in that hall have said that the second half of that entry is the first time in nineteen years a clerk has entered how a thing was said.

**And the clerk entered that this is the first time a bench of nine has refused to be a witness for a road rather than to be a keeper of one, and he entered it because the man of thirty-two asked him to enter it and not because the bench asked, and a man of about sixty said out loud that the difference is the whole difference between a body and an office and that he had been a clerk for nineteen years waiting for somebody to put it in a book.**

And the man of forty-seven put his hand flat on the table.

“Then I will be there,” he said, “on the Sunday, at the near end, and I will be there as a man of this town and not as this bench, and I will be there because there is a line in the wall and I have been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and I am the only person in this county anybody can put at the end of a thing, and if you enter that, enter that I said it after the vote and not before.”

The clerk entered it. He read it back twice.

And the child was on the fourth tread of that stair for about an hour of that sitting with a bundle on one shoulder, and the door was open behind the clerk because he had left it open, and a few in that hall noticed and a good half of them have said since that a child on a stair on a market morning is the only thing in Merefen that nobody in that room has a column for, and that the two marks in the chest are the only figures in the room and neither of them is about a child.

---

And the market had gone in at about the sixth hour and the tide was under the wharf and the road four hundred yards down the lane was open and about forty men were on it, and there is a road going out of this county, and nobody in this county has agreed to it and nobody in this county has refused it, and both of those are on a page under a stair, and a clerk of about sixty read all of it back twice and the second reading was slower and not better, and there are two marks in the chest and neither of them moved, and on the next Saturday the bench sits again, and on the Sunday after that a man of thirty-two with a limp is going to stand at a sill four hundred yards up this town's own road with a man of forty-seven at one end of it and something at the other, and it is going on a Sunday, and a Sunday is the one day in this county's week that is neither a working day nor a market day, and the reason for it is not the Sunday. The reason for it is that a Saturday is the one day anybody in this county can be asked a thing, and a man who is going to be somewhere in four days is not going to be asked anything, and neither is that bench, and there is not a clerk in this county who could sit on a Sunday and hold a minute if they asked him to.
