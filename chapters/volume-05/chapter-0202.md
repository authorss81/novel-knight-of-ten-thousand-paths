# Chapter 0202

**The twenty-seventh of Fallowmonth, YR 314, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the ninth hour, and the tide was under the wharf and a lamp was lit in the long room over it, and a clerk of about sixty who has kept the minute of that bench for nineteen years was asked to open the chest, and a man of forty-seven on the bench of nine because he was elected was in the room and had not been asked to be and came because the clerk sent for him.**

The bench of nine sits on Saturdays. That is the arrangement and it is older than anybody in that room and it has not been broken in nineteen years, and a Wednesday is not a day this body does anything in a room, and the two men who came up two flights on that Wednesday were not a bench and were not sitting and there is no minute of a Wednesday in that chest and there is not going to be one.

What there was in the room was a minute, a roll, a chest, a clerk, and two men who are on the roll, and one of them is a man of forty-seven who is on the roll because a man in a market asked who was going and he put his hand up.

**And the clerk sent for the man of forty-seven at the second hour of that Wednesday with a message of nine words, and the message was that there was a matter on the roll and it wanted the two of them, and the man of forty-seven came up two flights and did not ask what it was about and has not asked since.**

The room is long and it is over a wharf and the floor is board and the boards take the tide up through them in a wet week, and there is one table down the middle of it and a lamp at the top end, and a chest under the window with a lock on it that has not been broken in nineteen years and has not been opened more than about four times a year, and there are two marks in that chest.

There have been two marks in that chest since the ninth of Fallowmonth. Nobody has added to them and nobody has taken any out, and a handful of them in that hall have said that a chest with two marks in it and a bench that has never had a sum to the pound is all the public money there is of a town of four thousand people, and that this county has no council and has never had one, and that the eleven men and women who meet on a Saturday in a room over a wharf are not a council and have never claimed to be, and the difference between the two things is the difference between a body and an office, and this hall is the only room in the county where anybody can be refused.

The clerk turned the key and counted the two marks out onto the table in front of the two of them, because a man who opens a chest in front of other men has to say what is in it, and he said it in four or five words, and one of the two marks had been in that chest longer than the other and the newer of the two was in it from a year in which somebody paid for a roof, and neither of them had been counted in that room in nineteen years and the clerk could tell from the wear on the metal which one was which without looking, and did not say so.

The roll is on the table and it is a sheet and it is nineteen years old and it has eleven names on it, and the clerk has kept it and the roll is not the minute and the minute is in a book and the roll is a sheet and the two of them have never agreed and some of them in this hall have said that the sheet is the older document and it is.

Eleven names. A man of fifty-nine who is the chair and has held it since before the fever. A man of about forty who carries. A woman of about thirty-seven who is a magistrate of this county and is the reeve's sister. A woman of about fifty-one who is a miller. Two men of about thirty. A man of about sixty with a level who has kept his own book for two years and has been paid sixpence twice out of a box, and who is not a member of this bench and never has been, and whose own book is not in this room. A man of about thirty-two who is a road warden with a vote and not an office, and has no other office, and the vote is on the roll and the office is not, and there is a difference between the two of them that this bench has never once been asked to explain and has explained twice in nineteen years in a minute that anybody coming up that stair may read.

**A man of forty-seven who was elected. A man of about twenty-four who came on the roll when the man of thirty-two left the county and has not voted on anything and has not said so and nobody has asked.**

That is the roll, and it is a roll of a bench that is not a bench and has known it longer than anybody in the room has been alive, and about four people in that hall have said that the only thing this county has ever given that man of thirty-two is that line on that sheet, and one of those four is the clerk.

---

**And the clerk called him road-warden, and he meant it kindly, and there is no post of that name in this county.**

“Road-warden,” he said, writing, and then he stopped with the pen an inch off the paper, and looked up, and said, “That is not what it says on the sheet. I have been putting that word on the roll for nineteen years because it is the word you use at a counter and in a lane and it is not the word on the sheet, and I have never once had anybody correct me about it, and you are the fifth person in this county to be called it and the first one who has been in this room while I said it.”

“Go on,” said the man of thirty-two.

**“It is not a post. There is no road warden in this county. There is a road warden on a sheet of paper with a vote on it and no office and no pay out of the chest and no second office for the term, and that is a thing a Crown's office offered a man in a sheet of good paper two years ago and that office does not know this county has a body corporate in it, and you are the only road warden in Merefen and you hold nothing, and I have been sorry about that word on this roll for a very long time and I could not correct it because I did not know which of the two I was correcting.”**

The man of thirty-two said, “Write the sheet and not the word,” and the clerk wrote the sheet, and the word did not go in, and the clerk read the line back and it was the line and not the word.

**And the man of forty-seven said out loud, in that room, at that hour, to nobody, in about eleven words, a thing that nobody in that room had ever asked him for.**

“No, that is not me either,” he said. “**I am not a road warden. There is one and I am not it. I have not been on a trade since the year before last and I am paid fourpence a week out of my own box and not out of that chest, and I hold the reach and the Old Toll and the crossing. I do not hold one of them and the reason I have never said so in this hall is that nobody in this hall has ever asked me, and you are all still not asking me.**”

The lamp moved. The clerk put the pen down and picked it up again, and in under a minute went by, and the man of thirty-two with the limp did not say anything, and the man of forty-seven did not say it a second time, and the clerk did not enter it and told them afterwards why, and the reason is the rule he has kept for nineteen years.

**“I have never entered a name in this minute that was not put there by somebody else in this room, and I am not going to start on a Wednesday, and that man has just said nine words nobody asked him for and if I write them down they are my opinion in a book under a stair, and this county has had about enough of those to last two generations. You did not ask him. I did not ask him. It goes in the room and it does not go in the book.**”

And he was right, and two or three of them in that hall have said since that a clerk of about sixty who has kept a minute for nineteen years was the only person in that room who understood what was being done in it, and the man of forty-seven has never said a word about it to anybody and has told two people in a yard since that he had not voted on a Saturday for two years and had now told a room a thing that was not on the sheet, and that he does not know which of the two was braver and is not going to be told.

---

**And then there was the matter on the roll, and it was one line, and it took about four minutes, and it is in the minute and the minute is a Saturday document and this was a Wednesday, and the clerk wrote it on a loose leaf with the day on it and the day is a Wednesday and the leaf is in the minute and the minute has a Wednesday in it for the first time in nineteen years.**

It was a witness's line. It said that a man is to stand at one end of a thing and is not to be the person keeping it, and it did not say what the thing was, and the reason it did not say what the thing was is that a minute in that hall does not hold a road, and one of them in this town have said that the oldest entry in that minute that anybody can still read is one where somebody wanted to know where a thing was and a clerk of about sixty wrote *no answer* in the column and read it back twice.

“Put the hour against my name,” said the man of forty-seven, “and put the hour I said yes and not the hour you write it down.”

“It is the same hour.”

“It will not be the same hour in a year,” he said, “and in a year somebody will come up that stair and want to know when I agreed to it, and I want the answer to be the hour I agreed and not the hour a clerk was in a room. **And I have done this three times now and I have never once known what I was agreeing to, and I would like that in the line as well, because I have been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and I could not tell you this morning where either of them went.**”

The clerk wrote it. He read it back twice. The first reading was at the ordinary speed and the second was slower and not better, and that has been the practice of that room since the first year, and the clerk has said out loud to four different people in nineteen years that the second reading is not for accuracy and is so that a man in that room knows what it says before it goes in a book he cannot get out of.

**And then the clerk asked him what the thing was for, because a clerk asks, and he asked it in the ordinary way and it was a fair question and about four people in that hall have said that the honest answer was no.**

“There is no column for it,” the clerk said, when he had been told, or something of the kind, or the plainer version of it, or the clerk said it after and not at the time, and the clerk could not remember which, and would not be helped. “**You are asking me to write down what a thing is for, and there is nowhere in this minute to put what a thing is for, and I have a rule about the empty spaces in this book that I did not make and that I keep, and the rule is that I do not rule a line across one, and the reason I keep it is that a clerk who fills an empty space with an opinion is how a county ends up believing it decided something. I will put the hour, and the fact, and that a witness said yes before he did it, and I will put nothing at all in the rest of the line, and that is not a refusal and I would like it noticed that it is not a refusal.**”

---

**And a Saturday sitting is a different animal from a Wednesday, and a couple of them in that hall have said so in nineteen years and none of them has ever written it down, and it is the reason a thing can be refused on a Saturday and only refused on a Saturday.**

A Saturday sitting begins about the seventh hour because the market is on from the second until about the sixth, and the market is a market in this county twice a week and it is on a Saturday and on a Thursday and that is all of it, and a market day is the one day in this county's week on which anybody in this town can say what day it is. On a Thursday the hall does not sit and there are a few in Merefen who could tell you that a Thursday is a market and no more than that. On a Saturday the hall sits, and the hall has sat on Saturdays since before the clerk came to it, and on a Saturday any person in this town may come up that stair and stand at the bottom of it and wait, and a good half of them in a generation have done it and two of them were asking for a road and one of them was asking for nothing at all and has not been identified.

**And a Saturday is the only day this bench can be asked a thing and give an answer that will be in a book, and a Wednesday is not a day it can be asked anything, and the reason for that is not procedure and it is not the clerk's rule, it is that a market is the one day in a week when this town is in the street together and a thing that is refused in a room is refused in a room and a thing that is refused on a market morning is refused in front of the people who live here.**

And about four people in that hall have said that a bench that can be asked is a body, and that a body that cannot be asked is an office, and that the whole difference between the two is the Saturday.

---

And the reeve came up two flights on the Friday with the seventh asking in her coat, and she did not put it on the table, and the clerk entered that she came up with it and did not put it, which is the third time that has happened in that minute in nineteen years, and the Saturday sitting is on the thirtieth of Fallowmonth, and that is a market morning, and the market is on from the second hour until about the sixth, and the sitting is at the seventh, and a man of fifty who has a waggon of his own has said that he will be there because he has said he will be there.

And a child of nine years old came up one flight of that stair on that Wednesday at about the fourth hour and stood on the fourth tread and did not knock, and a handful of them in that hall have said since that the child did not knock and that nobody sent the child down and that the child stood there until the clerk came out of the room and saw it, and the clerk said that a person standing on a stair is not an entry in a minute, and a person standing on a stair is a person, and he went back into the room and left the door open behind him and the child did not come up.

**And nobody in that hall asked the child where it came from, and some of them have said since that the child was there again on the Thursday and a few have said that they had not noticed it, and the clerk has entered nothing about it because there is nothing to enter and because a person is not a thing that happened, and he said that out loud to two people in that hall, and what he said was that the whole trade of that room is putting down what happened and that a child on a fourth tread is a child on a fourth tread, and that if he wrote it down it would be because he had been in the room too long.**

**And the clerk left the door open behind him when he went back in, on the Wednesday and on the Thursday, and nobody in that hall has said why, and the man of thirty-two with the limp was in that room on the Wednesday at the ninth hour and the child was on the stair at the fourth hour, and those are about five hours apart in two rooms nine feet apart, and he did not know the child was there and has not been told and has not asked, and the roll in front of him has eleven names on it and a line on a loose leaf with an hour against a man's name in it, and neither of those has a space on it for a child and neither of them is going to get one, and a man of about sixty in that room has said out loud that he would not rule one if he could.**

And a man of forty-seven on the bench of nine because he was elected went back down two flights at about the tenth hour and told two men in a yard that there is a road going out of this county in about ten days and that he is going to be standing at the near end of it, and he said that before he did it and not after, and about four men in that yard have said that a man of forty-seven who has not voted since he was elected has just said out loud that he is going to be somewhere, and that a thing on a roll is not a thing you keep, and that the two of those sentences have never been said in that yard before and are not going to be improved on.
