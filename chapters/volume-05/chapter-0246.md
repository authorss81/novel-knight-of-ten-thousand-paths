# Chapter 0246

**The eighteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 315, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a lane off Weir Street with a weigh-house under it and two rooms over that weigh-house and a door on that lane, and a copying season in this county that ended eighteen days ago with two women still copying in it, and about four hundred people in this town who do not know that a reeve of this town came up two flights of that stair for the sixth time in nineteen years today and was told no in nineteen words by a keeper of records and watched the no go into a book as a question and not as a finding.**

The copying season in this county is Hearthmonth and it has been Hearthmonth for longer than anybody in that room can remember, and it ended eighteen days ago, and the two women in that room are still copying. A woman of about thirty is a second copyist and has been in that office six years and has wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last and has said out loud in a room of two that she is not going to get it and does not want it and that the two are the same thing. A woman of twenty-nine keeps the office and has four years and a half of a body corporate with a door on that lane and fourpence a year on a lease in a tradesman's hand.

A man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick came down that lane at about the second hour and went up the stairs, which is a thing he has done nine times in four months, and he is not on the roll of anything in that office and has no office and is not paid by the season.

**And the money was footed on the sill of the table before anybody said a total, and the addition was done by the woman who owns the room and not by him.** She weighed the coin on a small stone balance and put the weight in a column in a book, and then said the column out loud, and he did not add it and did not offer to and has not offered to in nine visits.

“A sheet copied for a person who cannot come to the office is a penny,” she said. “A second sheet for the same person is a penny. A bundle of waxed thread to close a folded sheet with, which we go through about four times in a season, is twopence. One and one is two, and two and two is four pence.”

Nobody said four. A woman of about thirty wrote it into a column in a second book and did not read it back, and that has been the practice of that room since the first year.

“**And the two nights this office was open at the ninth hour in the season are not a column,**” she said, “**and I am not going to add them for anybody. A house that works does not count its own days, and an office that works does not count its own hours, and the day somebody starts writing down the nights in this room is the day this room has become a thing somebody can be served with about.**”

**And then she said the other half of the column out loud, and it is the reason the two figures in this room are not in one column, and about four people in that county have said that she has said this part four times in four years and a half and has never once said it as a rule.** The office is fourpence a year. The two rooms over that weigh-house are two shillings a month each, and they are the salt merchant's and not the office's, and both of the two are on a lease in the same hand on the same sheet. Twenty-four and twenty-four is forty-eight pence. And she did not add the four to it, and the reason she did not add the four to it is that one of the two is a price for the right to keep what a county cannot be shown it has lost, and the other is a price for a floor that takes a tide up through it in a wet week, and those are two different things and a price gets made out of two things that are not the same thing and then somebody files it, and the second copyist wrote that down in a book of working papers and did not read it back.

“Now the sheet,” she said.

---

**And a sheet of a printed series came into that room in the ordinary post on the twenty-fourth day of a month in the spring, and it had been at the bottom of a bag and it had been folded once and opened, and it is about nine inches by six and it is soft at the fold, and it is ruled in six columns, five of them with heads, and the sixth column has no head on it and is the same width as the other five, and about four of the six are ruled for a figure.**

There is a word in the object column that nobody in this county can read the sense of, and a name behind the word that is not the name of this county and not the name of any place anybody in that room has ever had a paper out of, and there is a district name on the sheet, and there are four empty places in it, and a district is not a county and there is no such thing in this county and about four people in this town have worked that out since the spring and none of the four has said so to the other three.

**And a second sheet of the same series came to the same room on the sixth day of this month in the ordinary post and went up two flights of that stair to a bench of nine, and a bench of nine said no to it in eleven words, and the reason went in twice, and the second sheet is in a press in a room over a wharf and has not been answered, and the two sheets have been in a drawer together since the seventh and nobody in that room has taken either of them out and looked at the other one.**

A woman of about thirty picked the first of the two up, looked at the object column, and put it down on the table on the side of the table the fair copy is not on, and did not take it to the press, and did not take it to the bench, and did not ask a question about it in a room of two.

“There is nothing in the four places,” she said.

“There is a district,” said the woman of twenty-nine.

“**A district is about nine market towns and eight of the nine have a counter in them where a man can be found at one, and the ninth is this one, and the office has sent it to this one because this is the only one of the nine that has anything to send it to. And a sheet with four empty places in it is not an answer. It is a hole in a form, and the four holes are the same size as each other, and about four people in that building have never once asked why a hole should be the same size as another hole.**”

Nobody said anything to that and the man of thirty-two with a limp was at the window and had not asked either of them a question, and a second copyist of about thirty has said since that a man who stands at a window in a room where two women are working does not interrupt, and that it is the first thing she was taught and the last thing she will ever be taught, and that she has not worked out whether he is being careful or whether he has nothing to say.

---

**And then a reeve of this town came up two flights of that stair at about the fourth hour, and it is the sixth time in nineteen years and she has put three of the six.**

She is a magistrate's elder sister and she came up on the sixth of this month with a sheet of printed paper and put it on a bench of nine, and this time she had nothing in her hands. She does not vote on the guild's own matter and has not voted on it in nine years. About four people in that building have said since that a reeve who comes up two flights with nothing in her hands is a person who has come to ask and knows she is going to be told no before she is up the stair.

“Can this county give that office a number of its people.”

She said it standing, and it is eleven words, and it was the only thing she said in that room, and a woman of about thirty who is a second copyist has said that the reeve said it in the tone of a man asking about a price and that the tone is the reeve's and not the office's and has been the reeve's for nine years.

**And she was told no in about nineteen words, and the nineteen words are the whole of what that office has to say about a figure of persons, and they are said by a person who has been keeping records for four years and a half and who has no office and is not a clerk and is a body corporate that can be sued and cannot be reached.**

“**No. There is no column in this office for a number of people and there never has been.**”

And then the other half, which is longer, said once, and about four people in that county have said since that the nineteen words are the ones anybody in that building would remember and the other half is the one the county runs on and does not know it runs on.

“**I have been asked this four times in four years and a half and I have answered it four times and I am not going to answer it a fifth time in different words. And a number of people in a column is the one kind of paper in this kingdom that cannot be refused, and I have a door on a lane and four years and a half of a body corporate that can be sued, and I have never once worked out what a county is supposed to do with a paper like that.**”

Nobody improved on it. A second copyist of about thirty wrote two lines on a leaf of her own hand and put the leaf in a book of working papers on the second shelf and did not read them back, and about four people in that county have said since that a woman who writes two lines and does not read them back has been in that room six years and has never once done it.

“Then what goes in it,” said the reeve.

**“Nothing goes in it. What goes in it is the question, and it goes in as a question, and the reason goes in under the rule, and the office in a provincial town two hundred miles off will get a question back where it expected a figure and it will not know what to do with a question and that is not my doing and it is not their doing either, it is what a form is.**”

---

**And the fifth entry went into a register in her own hand, and all five of the entries begin with a district name, and all five of them are entered as a question and none of them is entered as a finding, and that is the whole of her trade and the difference between a register and a day-book is the difference between those two things.**

The register is a book. It is not the press and it is not the day-book, and the day-book is the only book in that office nobody can be served with, and the register is entered with a question and the day-book is entered with a rule. The first of the four entries is five months old and the second is four months and the third is two months and the fourth is a fortnight, and the fifth went in at about the sixth hour of the eighteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 315, in a fair hand and under the rule, in about four minutes, and it is the first of the five that names a day.

A woman of twenty-nine read it back and the reading was slower and not better, and that has been the practice of that room since the first year. And then she put the pen down and said the other half, and it is the longest thing she has said out loud in that room in four years and a half, and it is the only one of the long ones that was about the five entries and not about the office.

“**A keeper of records who enters a thing she means to do has made a plan. I have five questions in that book and not one of them is a finding, and I am going to keep it that way as long as there is a person left in this county who has not been asked, and the day there is nobody left to ask is the day the register becomes a roll and I would like somebody to write down that I said so and put the day against it.**”

Nobody wrote the day down. A second copyist of about thirty looked at the register and then at the wall and did not take a leaf out of the working papers, and has said since that she did not write the day down because it is not her column, and that a woman who writes down a thing that is not in her column is a woman who has started to keep a roll.

And a reeve of this town went down two flights of that stair with nothing in her hands, having come up with nothing in her hands, and she said one thing on the top step and it was longer than the nineteen words and she knew that before she was up the stair, and it was not to anybody in the room.

“**Then this county is not a number. I have said that a bench of nine is a county and I am not going to take it back, and I have said it in a market four times a year for nine years and about half the names in that book are the same four men and the book is not a minute and cannot be got out of, and if there is a room in this county that can put a figure on the two of us then I have been wrong for nine years and I would rather find that out in a room than in a market.**”

---

**And he was given the register at about the sixth hour, because he had asked to see it and she had said yes in four seconds, and he stood at the table with it open in front of him for about a minute and read the fifth entry, and about nine minutes later he could not remember whether the fifth entry said *question* or *finding*, and the difference between the two is the whole of that room and the whole of the eighteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 315, and he did not ask her to read it again.**

A man of thirty-two with a limp is holding three paths open and cannot stay interested in the whole length of a conversation, and in a county that has decided to be counted it arrives as a way of being given a thing and losing the thread of it, and it has arrived four times in about a fortnight in two counties. He stood there and waited, the way a man waits, and then he put the register down on the table on the side the sheet is on, and the sheet has four empty places in it and the register has five entries in it and he put the two of them about four inches apart on the same table, and neither of them has met the other one and neither of them is going to.

A man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard four hundred miles off has said out loud that you can tell whether a bank is square and he cannot, and that a man who puts a hand flat on a thing is not asking the thing anything and is telling it something, and that he said it once and has not said it since. About four people in that lane have said that a man of thirty-two with a limp has now put a book of questions down next to a sheet with four holes in it about four inches apart in a room with a door on a lane, and that the two of those are the same size and the same shape and the same absence, and that he has not written the number of either of them down and has not been asked to by anybody in that room.

---

**And nobody in that room asked him where he had been, and he did not say, and nobody in that county has asked him and he has not offered it, and one of the two people in that room is a woman who is not going to ask.**

The not askings stand at nine. There is a book in that county nobody can be served with and it has nine entries of the not asking in it and there is not a tenth in it and there is not going to be a tenth, and the tenth would be a question asked of a man of thirty-two by a woman of twenty-nine in a room she owns, and she owns the room and the day-book and the door on the lane and the fourpence a year and the press with four hundred documents in it, and a keeper of records who asks the only person in this county who is in a book somewhere else has made a plan, and she has not made one, and a second copyist of about thirty has said out loud in a room of two that the not asking is the only thing in four years and a half that either of the two of them has been asked to do by anybody and has not done.

Nobody asked him about the morning. He did not offer it. A sheet of a printed series with four empty places in it and a district on it went down to a press on a table in a room over a wharf and was not answered, and a bench of nine has said no in eleven words about a word and not about a road, and a keeper of records has entered five questions in a book that can be sued and cannot be reached, and a market four hundred yards long was open from the second hour until about the sixth and a man of fifty with a waggon came through it and did not stop, and a reach four hundred yards down the lane was open with the road over it and the salt coming over the first bay, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that any of that happened on the eighteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 315, and there is no figure in that county of how many people do not know.
