# Chapter 0239

**The eighth of Hearthmonth, YR 315, a market morning, and the fifth hour and after, and about four hundred yards of lane from a crossing with no glass in its sill to a house on Weir Street two doors from a coopers, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who got up nine steps of a bank at about the fifth hour and a half and then did not get up any more, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people did not know for about two hours that he was on the ground, and two of the four were on the same abutment nine hundred feet away and did not see it happen and have not been told.**

The fever is Stage 3's and it was earned by the stage and it is not a symptom of anything else and it is a week. That is the whole of what happened on the eighth of Hearthmonth, YR 315, in a town of about four thousand people on a river. A carter of thirty found him, and a boy of fifteen was standing four feet off with a bill under his arm and did not have a bill to write and did not write one.

A carter of thirty got him up the bank with a barrow and did not have to lift him, because he got on it himself and was not asked, and he pushed it up four hundred yards of lane at about the pace a barrow goes, and the two of them did not say one word to each other on the way, and that is the only thing either of them has said about it.

The second and the third days were the worst. The fever is not a fever anybody in that county has been able to name, and a woman of about sixty in Weir Street has had two in forty years, and one of the two was a man who had been in a mine and this one was not that, and she said so out loud to a boy of fifteen on the second day and he did not write it down.

**And the boy of fifteen held the tools for seven days.** That is the whole of what a man of forty-seven said about it, in about nineteen words, on the seventh day, standing in a kitchen, to a woman of about sixty and a carter of thirty, and it is the only time anybody in that county has used the word tools about anything belonging to a man of thirty-two with a limp, and he said it in the tone of a man saying a price, and about four hundred doors on two lists in two counties are the same four hundred doors and nobody in either county has ever used that word about anything.

The stick was a ferrier's stick, ash, four feet nine, cut out of a fence rail in a county that is not on any map, for four hundred yards of road and nothing else, and he cannot lean on it because leaning on it puts the weight through the wrong hip. A boy of fifteen carried it up Weir Street under his arm for seven days and set it in a corner where a man lying on his back could reach it, and did it without a word and without being asked, and did not once in seven days put it anywhere else.

The notebook is a thing that has been carried for four years and has had four pages filled in in four years, and one of the four has no hour against it and no name in it. A boy of fifteen who copies the empty space under a number as well as the number held it for seven days and did not open it, and he was asked about it once by a woman of about sixty and said that a man of forty-seven had said he would be asked about it.

**And the four sheets of paper were in the inside pocket of a coat and the coat was on a chair, and the four sheets had four empty places in them and a place name and a word in the object column and no name anywhere on them.** A woman of about sixty put the coat on the top shelf on the second day, because there is a boy of fifteen in that house and there are four sheets in that coat, and she did not ask what was in them.

On the second day a woman of about thirty with a pail came to Weir Street with a woman of about sixty from a lane two hundred yards off, and the two of them talked about a thing at a door for about a quarter of an hour while the woman of about sixty washed, and a woman of thirty with a pail has walked two hundred yards to a house in Weir Street to talk about a thing at a door and has not asked one question about a man of thirty-two with a limp in the next room. The man of about sixty who keeps the loft over the weigh-house has been in that house four mornings this week and has not asked either. About four hundred people in that town do not know that the man with the stick is in Weir Street at all, and nine of them have worked out that he came back and cannot say from where.

And a man of forty-seven came on the third day and looked at the four sheets and did not ask what was in them and said, in about nine words, in a kitchen, to a woman of about sixty and a carter of thirty and a boy of fifteen.

“**I have been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and I am going to be the witness at the end of a week, and a witness is not a man who asks, and I have been asked twice this week why I do not ask and I am going to say the same thing to both of you and it is the four things.**”

And he said the four things and he said them again, and nobody in that kitchen improved on them. A carter of thirty and a woman of about sixty and a boy of fifteen heard all four, and none of the three asked him a fifth thing, and a woman of about sixty has said since that she wanted to and that she did not, and that she has been the keeper of two rooms over a weigh-house for nineteen years and has never once wanted to ask a man a question more than she wanted to hear the answer.

**And the tax of holding three paths arrived the way it has arrived for nine days, and in a fever it is a way of being unable to be given a thing without losing the thread.** On the fourth day he was given a cup of something at about the fourth hour and had it in his hand and then asked the woman of about sixty what the cup was for, and she told him, and about nine minutes later he asked her the same question again. She answered it both times in the same words, and she did not say the same words the second time, and the second time she said it slower, and nobody in that house has worked out why.

On the fifth day the stick was handed to him by the boy of fifteen, because a man lying on his back cannot reach a corner, and it was taken back about a minute later because he could not hold it, and neither of them said anything on either occasion, and the boy put it back in the corner where a man could reach it, and that is the whole of the two things that happened on the fifth day of that week.

The leg is the same leg. It is not worse, and nobody in that house has said that it is worse, and his right leg was the leg it was in a county four hundred miles off, and a boy of fifteen moved the stick twice in seven days and both times were on the side the leg is on, and the boy was asked about it once by a carter of thirty and said the stick went in a corner where a man could reach it and that that was the whole of why.

On the sixth day he sat up. On the sixth day he was given the coat, and he put his hand in the inside pocket of it, and he did not take anything out of it, and he did not look at what was in it, and a boy of fifteen was standing four feet off and saw that and has not said what he thought about it.

**And on the third day a man of fifty with a waggon came to Weir Street with a barrow of salt and left it against the wall of the lane and did not knock, and a woman of about sixty gave a piece of bread to the boy of fifteen and not to the man in the next room, and about four people in that county have said since that a man of fifty with a waggon came up Weir Street once in that week and left a barrow and went down again, and that he has fed a stranger every day in a city of about nine thousand people for forty days and has never once been thanked for it, and that he did not knock on a door in Weir Street because nobody in that county has ever told him that a man in a bed is a man to knock on.**

**And nobody asked him about the morning.** A woman of about sixty and a carter of thirty and a boy of fifteen and a man of forty-seven were in that house at some point on each of the seven days, and not one of the four asked him where he had been and he did not say, and one of the four wanted to, and the wanting is not on the record, and the man of forty-seven is the only one of the four who could have asked and did not.

And on the seventh day the boy of fifteen gave the notebook back, and he gave it back flat and in the right order and with the first leaf turned to the first page, and a man of thirty-two with a limp took it and did not open it and put it in the inside pocket of a coat. The right order is a thing a person can only get right by not having opened it, and a man of forty-seven was in that house on the seventh day and said four things about a set of papers and was not thanked by anybody and did not want to be.

**And on the sixth day a woman of about sixty read the four things off the wall of her own kitchen out loud to a boy of fifteen, which she has done about four times in forty years, and the boy wrote them on the back of a bill he is not going to be given, and the four things are not a rule and are not about the light, and a man of forty-seven has said the same four things in three counties in about a year and has never once said where he got them.**

**And on the seventh day a man of forty-seven came and said the four things and asked whether the four sheets were to be kept, and a woman of about sixty said that they were not hers and were not anybody's in that house, and he said that was the correct answer and that a man of forty-seven had been told the correct answer about four things in a year in three counties and had believed it every time.**

**And on the seventh day a woman of about sixty who owns two rooms over a weigh-house came in and said one thing and went out.** She said that there was a thing in his coat that was not hers and that she had put the coat on the top shelf and that he could have it when he was up, and he did not say thank you, and she has said since that a man who does not say thank you for a coat he is going to get back is a man who has been given a coat by somebody else before, and that she has not been told who and did not ask.

**And a room in a town of about four thousand people with a door on a lane was forty feet from the room he was in and was not a room anybody offered him, and a woman of twenty-nine who keeps two rooms over a weigh-house for four years and a half did not come out of it once in the seven days, and about four people in that county have said since that a woman of twenty-nine who owns a door on a lane four feet from a sick man's room did not use the lane once in a week, and that the salt merchant of sixty-eight who owns the loft has been in that house four mornings and has not asked either, and that nobody in that county has ever worked out whether the two of them know that the man with the stick is forty feet away and neither of them has said.** A bench of nine that has refused a road twice has put both refusals twice in a minute under a stair in that town and has not been asked anything since, and the clerk of about sixty who has kept that minute for nineteen years entered no line about a fever.

**And a woman of twenty-nine with a door on a lane is forty feet from the room he was in and did not come out of it once in the seven days, and a second copyist in a provincial town two hundred miles off has a letter she has not opened and a line in a day-book she has wanted since the summer before last and does not want, and about four people in two counties have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp was in a bed in Weir Street for a week in the same fortnight in which nobody opened a letter and nobody said a word, and that the two of those facts have never been in the same sentence in any county and are not going to be.**

**And a market was four hundred yards long and open on the eighth of Hearthmonth, YR 315 and again on the next market morning, and the salt was coming over the first bay of the reach, and the fourth bay was being pointed, and the joint on its parapet is a foot and a half short, and a bell with forty-one rivets in it was rung at the fourth hour and a half by a boy and a carpenter, and a yard on Weir Street four hundred yards up this county's road had about nine men in it and did not have a man in it who was in it in the year before.** Toban's yard has been about nine men for about two months, and the nine have not been asked about anything, and one of the nine has been out on every working day and has not once said his brother's name, and nobody on that yard has asked him why, and that is a cost and it is not a thing anybody in that yard has said out loud.
