# Chapter 0229

**The twenty-ninth day of a count of days that this city keeps, and the second hour, and a man of about forty-six with a satchel coming back up a lane past the works and round by a bindle store with a printed sheet in the satchel that is the same sheet he carried out of this city on the twenty-fourth, and the four figures on it are all the same figure and the figure is nine, and a few hundreds of people in this city did not know that a sheet had gone out on the twenty-fourth and a few hundreds of people in this city did not know on the twenty-ninth that it was coming back, and the two of those are the same four hundred people, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick was nine hundred feet off at the mouth of a lane at the second hour and has not come down it and did not come down it at the third hour or the fourth, and a few of the people in two yards nine hundred yards apart have noticed that and have not said so.**

“It is the same sheet,” he said, at the gate, before he had got through it, and a few of the people in that hut have said since that a man of forty-six said *it is the same sheet* to a man of fifty-five in a hut the way a man says a price, and that a man of fifty-five has been in that hut nineteen years and has turned back a few hundreds of people and has never once been told that anything came back. “**It came back on the twenty-seventh. The series goes round twice. It goes round twice everywhere, and it is the same sheet in every town, and a man in a room turns it over and puts it in a pile and the pile goes round again, and I have carried the same sheet to nine doors in eight years and I never once knew it until this week and I am telling you that so that you do not think a man came back for a second time because he wanted to.**”

And a man of fifty-five in a hut did not say the four words, and a few of the people in that hut have said since that the four words were in his mouth and did not come out, and that this is the first time in nineteen years a man has gone through that gate carrying a bundle without the four words, and that nobody in that hut has worked out why and that the reason is that a bundle is a bundle and the man in the satchel was carrying a bale of cord and reed on a barrow and not a sheet, and that a man of fifty-five with a rule about four words does not have a second rule and does not need one, and that the gate is a machine for letting liars through and the price of that is a man of fifty-five in a hut who cannot ask a man where he has been and has worked it out about a hundred times in nineteen years and has worked it out about four hundred and one times on the twenty-ninth.

---

**And the money was footed on the sill of a bench in a room the size of a cart on the second night and the addition was done by the woman who owned the room and not by him, and it is the second column he has had in this city and it is not added to the first one, and the room on the second night is the room on the first night and it is the same price and there is only one price for it.**

The room was two pence. Bread at the second hour was a penny. A bundle of waxed thread to close a folded sheet with, which he buys about four times a year and has never once bought in this city before, was twopence. Two and one is three, and three and two is five pence.

“**Five pence and the same room and the same two for it, and the two nights are not a column and I am not going to add them for you, and a man who counts his nights in a town is a man who is going to be asked for a night by somebody in about four years and I would rather he had not had the habit,**” said the woman who owned it, and weighed the coin, and put the weight in a column in a book, and then said the other half of it, and a few of the people in that room have said since that she said it on the first night as well and that a woman of about fifty has said the same two sentences to two men nine days apart and has not noticed and a few of the people in that lane have.

And a man of thirty-two with a limp slept in that room on the twenty-ninth and had slept in it for twenty-nine nights and had not been charged for the twenty-eight of them and was not going to be, and a few of the people in that lane have said since that this is the sixth stranger this year in this city who has been fed for weeks and not paid and that not one of the six has been asked to sign anything, and that a man of thirty who sells names for a penny in that city has said out loud that the arrangement is the only protection the city has and that nobody knows it is one, and that the man of thirty has not been asked and did not say it to a stranger and that a man of thirty-two with a limp heard it from a woman of about thirty with a pail and has written it down and has not written down the rest of what she said.

---

**And at the fourth hour of the twenty-ninth day a man of forty-six was in a bindle yard and a man of sixty-six was at a bench with a printed sheet on it with four figures on it, all the same figure, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six put the sheet on the bench on the twenty-ninth and that he had it out of the satchel before the man of forty-six was through the gate, and that a man of sixty-six who has had twenty years to get used to a piece of printed paper on that bench has not got used to it and that the reason he is not getting used to it is that the last time that sheet was on that bench it went out of the city, and that a man who has been working for forty years does not put a thing on a bench that went out of the building on the twenty-fourth without thinking about the twenty-fourth.**

“The first one came back,” he said.

“**It came back on the twenty-seventh clipped to a second one and I have said what I am going to do about the second one and I said it in a yard and I am not going to say it twice, and the second one went back in that satchel with nothing on it and it is going to go back again on the same sheet with something on it, and I am not able to do one and not the other and there is nobody in this city who could tell me to do one and not the other, because there is nobody in this city who could tell me anything.**”

“Then what are you going to write.”

“**A figure. The sheet has four places in it for a figure and I am going to write a figure in the four of them, and it is going to be the same figure in each, and I have not got a second figure and there is not going to be one.**”

---

**And a child of nine years old came down the lane at about the fourth hour and a half of the twenty-ninth day, and a man of sixty-six asked it the same question he asked it on the nineteenth day of this count, in the same yard, in the same words, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that this is the second time a man has asked a child of nine years old a question in that yard in four hundred years and that the first time was nine days ago and that both of the times were the same question and that the man asked it the second time on purpose and had decided to before he came down the lane.**

Then he said the reason out loud, and he said it in about eleven words, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six said a reason out loud in that yard for the first time in twenty years and that the reason was about a number and that the man of forty-six heard it and that the man of forty-six wrote nothing and that the man of forty-six is a man who has never once written anything down in eight years except a list of about four hundred places and a sentence on the back of it.

“**I wrote a figure on the twenty-third that was one higher than the number in my book and I have known since the twenty-third that it was and I have not been able to do anything about it, and I am asking it a second time because the figure has to be right this time and there is one refusal in this city I have never been able to put on a page and I am going to find out if it still stands.**”

**No**, said the child.

It took the time it takes to draw one breath. And a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six who has written about four hundred pieces of paper in twenty years has now been told no twice by the same person and that the second no was the same length as the first one and that nine days of a count of days that this city keeps have gone by in between and that a man of sixty-one who keeps the other count has a book in a yard at the end of a lane past two gates that has a line on it that is fourteen years old and that neither of those men has ever compared, and that this is now three books in this city that are about a fold in the page and about four inches from the bottom, and that a few of the people in this city have worked out that the three of them are the same shape and about four hundred have not and that the man of thirty-two with a limp is one of the four.

---

**And the arithmetic was done out loud, in a yard, at about the fifth hour of the twenty-ninth day, by a man of sixty-six, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has added two numbers together in that yard for the first time in twenty years and that he did it because a form wanted a figure, and that the two numbers were eight and two, and that eight and two is ten, and that the two is a person of nine years old who has been asked twice, and that the ten is not a count of people and it is not a count of rooms and it is not a count of doors, and that it is a count of times this city has said no, and that a few of the nine have said since that a man of sixty-six said that in about nineteen words and that it is the only time anybody in that yard has put a count and a person next to each other and that the two of them being next to each other is the whole of what has happened in this city in ten days and that nobody in that yard has worked out that it is a document.**

Eight in the book. And the child, twice.

**One, and one, and one, and one. That is four of the same figure and it is not a sum, and the figure on that sheet is ten, and there are four tens on it, and the first one had four nines on it, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has written eight figures in two days on two sheets of the same printed series and that eight and eight is sixteen and that he has not added those and that nobody outside this city is going to add those unless a man in a room in a town two hundred miles off does, and that a few hundreds of people in a city of about nine thousand people do not know that any of it has happened and are not going to.**

---

**And the man of forty-six asked what the ten was, and the man of sixty-six said the same thing he said on the twenty-fourth in about eleven words, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of forty-six has now heard the same sentence twice and has not improved on it and has not asked it a second time, and that a man of forty-six who has left about three thousand forms at about three thousand doors in eight years has never once been told the same thing twice by the same man and that this is the first and that the man with the satchel noticed and that a few of the people in that yard have noticed the man with the satchel noticing.**

And the man of forty-six took the stub out of his pocket and turned his list over and wrote it on the back of the list and not on the sheet, the second time, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he has now got two sentences on the back of a list of about four hundred places and that both of them are a count of things a city has said no to and not a count of people, and that the two sentences are in the same hand and the same place on the paper and about four inches apart, and that a man who has two of the same sentence written about four inches apart on the back of a list is a man who is going to be asked to explain it by somebody in about a year, and that he will not be able to, and that he has known that since the twenty-third and has not stopped writing them down.

Then he said the thing that is all of the twenty-ninth day, and he said it standing up, in a yard, in about nineteen words, and he said it about a room in a town two hundred miles off and not about this city, and about four of the people who have been in that yard have said since that he said it in the tone of a man saying a price and that he has said four things in that tone in nine days and that the four of them are the truest four things anybody in that city has heard from outside it in four hundred years.

“**A series goes round twice and the room has both of them, and a room with two returns from one district is not a room with a problem. It is a room with a pile. And nobody in a room adds two figures from one district together, and nobody in a room asks why they are different, and I have been doing this eight years and I have never once seen anybody in a room in a town two hundred miles off do anything at all with a figure I have carried up a road, and that includes the twenty-third and that includes this, and I am telling you it includes this because I would like somebody in this city to know it before I am on the rise, and I am not going to be able to tell you again because I will not be here.**”

**And a child of nine years old took its bundle off its shoulder and put it on again and did not go out that afternoon, and a few hundreds of people in a city of about nine thousand people did not know, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the mouth of that yard and did not come in and did not call out and did not ask the child a second question himself, and a few of the people in that lane have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp has now watched a person in this city be asked the same thing twice and has not asked it once, and that a page of foolscap in his coat has a seventh line on it that says a person and has not been finished since a lane four hundred miles off, and that he took the page out of the coat at about the fifth hour of the twenty-ninth day and held it and did not write on it and put it back, and that a man of thirty with a barrow and a mule was up the hill at that hour and did not see it and has not been asked.**
