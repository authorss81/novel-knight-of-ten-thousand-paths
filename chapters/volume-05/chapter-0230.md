# Chapter 0230

**The thirtieth day of a count of days that this city keeps from the last renewal, and the second hour, and a road coming down off the eastern side with a man of about forty-six on it and a barrow and a bale of cord and reed on the barrow and a satchel on his back, and the light is standing where it was standing on the first day of this count of days and where it stood on the twentieth, and there are four figures on a printed sheet inside that bale and the four of them are all the same figure and the figure is ten, and the first one had four nines on it and went out on the twenty-fourth and came back on the twenty-seventh and went out again on the twenty-ninth, and this city did not know on the twenty-fourth and did not know on the twenty-seventh and did not know on the twenty-ninth and about four hundred of them worked out something on the twenty-eighth and about four hundred have not.**

The man of fifty-five in the hut said nothing. That is the whole of the second hour of the thirtieth day at the road gate and a few of the people in that hut have said since that a man of fifty-five in a hut has now let two bundles out of that barrier in nine days without the four words and that nine thousand people have gone through it since he came into it and that the two of them are the only two he has thought about and that he has not thought about either of them.

Then the man of forty-six went up the rise, and a few of the people in that lane have said since that he was not looking back and that a man of fifty with a waggon had gone up the same road nine days before him with glass rods under sacking and had not looked back either, and that a man of thirty with a barrow and a mule went up it on the twentieth with nine small glass things in straw and did not look back, and that there have been three men up that road in twenty-nine days and none of the three has ever looked back and that this is the ordinary condition of a road with a market at the bottom of it and not a thing anybody has ever noticed and is not going to.

**And the twenty-eighth was a day and the twenty-ninth was a day and the thirtieth is a day, and this city keeps a count of days from the last renewal and no months, and the oven was hot at the second hour and at the ninth on all three of them, and nine hundred feet of quay worked four jobs at once on all three of them, and a market on about two hundred yards of street was open on all three of them, and a man of thirty was at a corner of that street with nothing in front of him who says anybody's name in that street for a penny, and a few of the people in that city have said since that the three days are the three ordinary days of a city that has not been asked anything in four hundred years and that the only thing that was different about any of the three is that a man of fifty-two said two things in a glass yard on the twenty-eighth, and that nine people in that yard heard both of them, and that a few hundreds of people in this city did not hear one of them and are not going to and do not know that there was anything to hear.**

---

**And at the fourth hour of the thirtieth day a man of fifty-two on the east wall of a glass works came down a ladder for the fourth time and put the bucket where it goes and said three things, and he said all three of them while he was working and did not stop and did not look up, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of fifty-two has said three things in a working yard on one afternoon and that not one of the three was to a man of thirty-two with a limp, and that the man with the stick was nine feet off and was the only person in that yard who knew all three of them were about him, and that nine men in that yard have heard all three of them and a few hundreds of people in this city have heard none of them, and that nobody in that yard improved on any of the three and a few of the nine have not stopped working, which is the correct way to receive it in a working yard.**

“**We have begun filing a person as a class,**” he said. “**That is the first time in four hundred years this city has done that, and we did it in answer to a form, and we did it out of nothing. Nobody in this city decided to. A man with a satchel asked a boy a question twice because a figure was wrong by one, and the boy said no twice, and a man of sixty-six wrote down a count of times and not a count of people, and that is a person in a class, and a few of the nine of us in this yard have worked that out in the last day and a few hundreds of people in this city have not and will not.**”

He put a gather in.

“**And a man can be stood in a place and a man can be asked. That is the whole of the difference and it is four hundred years old and I have watched both sides of it from the same ladder. A column is a place to stand a man in. It does not ask him and it does not hear him and it is the same whatever he says, and I have been in one nineteen years and I would not know today if it had changed on me.**”

And then he said the third one, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that it took him nine seconds and that the nine seconds were the longest of the three and that he looked up once in the middle of them and went back to the work and finished, and that a few of the men in that yard have said since that a man of fifty-two looked at a man of thirty-two with a limp for a second and a half in the middle of the longest thing he has said in nineteen years and that the second and a half is the whole of what he looked up for.

“**And you are the same as the paper, and you have not noticed, and you have been fed and not paid for thirty days and there is nothing to show anybody about you, and that has been the best thing that has happened to you in this city, and it is not kindness, it is a line in a form with your whole life on the wrong side of it, and the man who filled it in did it because a figure was wrong by one and not because of you, and I would like it noticed that a man of thirty-four in this yard has been putting a piece of wood under your feet for five days and neither of us has ever once said why, and that is the same arrangement and it is a smaller one.**”

Nobody improved on it. A man of about thirty-four with lime turned a mould and said nothing, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-four who says nothing at the end of that is a man who has decided that the best answer a person in a working yard can give is to go on working, and that a few hundreds of people in this city have never been in a working yard and have never been given an answer like it and are not going to be.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood nine feet off with a stick he cannot lean on and did not say a word for a minute and a half, and a few of the people who have been in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in this city thirty days and has asked about four things in the first fortnight and has got four straight answers in nine words each and has not once told any of the four that they were being difficult, and that this is the first thing anybody has said to him in thirty days that he has not been able to answer, and that he did not try to answer it, and that a few of the men in that yard noticed that he did not try to answer it, and that one of the four is a man of fifty-two who put a gather in and went on with the work and has not said one word since.**

**And he could not use it, and he has not written it down and is not going to, and a few of the people in four counties four hundred miles off have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp has been told in a glass yard that a person who cannot be shown is a line on a form, and that a man who has spent four years learning to be answered by a document has just been told that the not being in one is the same arrangement and a smaller one, and that there is nobody four hundred miles off who could use either half of that, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp would have liked the sentence about the wood under the feet and has not said so to anybody and is not going to.**

“A child of nine,” he said, after the minute and a half.

“**Yes,**” said Ivo Marrow.

“You put that in a count.”

“**No. A man of sixty-six put that in a count and he asked it twice because a figure was wrong, and if you want to take it out of the count you will have to go nine hundred yards and take it out of it yourself and you will not be able to, and I have not said that to be unkind and I would have said it about any of the nine of us in this yard.**”

Nobody said anything else. And a few of the people in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp asked one question in a glass yard on the thirtieth day of a count of days that this city keeps and that it was the only one in thirty days that was not about a thing, and that it was about a child of nine years old, and that a man of fifty-two answered it in nine words, and that a man of thirty-four with lime did not turn round, and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out why a man who has been in a city thirty days and has been protected by the correctness of a form asked about a person of nine years old and not about the form.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp walked nine hundred feet of lane at about the seventh hour of the thirtieth day and turned round at the end of it and walked it back, and a few of the people in that lane have said since that he had been doing that since the twenty-fourth and that a few of the people in that lane have stopped commenting on it, and that a man of thirty with a barrow and a mule came down the lane at about the seventh hour and passed him and did not say anything, and that a man of thirty with a barrow and a mule said the whole of what a city is in nine words on the first day of a count of days to a stranger on a hill road, and has not said it since, and did not say it on the thirtieth, and that a few of the people in that lane have noticed the not saying and that a man of thirty-two with a limp noticed it and that neither of them has said one word about it to anybody and that neither of them is going to.**

**And the eleven lines were still in the notebook in the inside pocket of a coat and a few of the people in that room have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp had them out on the table at about the seventh hour and looked at the eleventh of them and did not write anything under it, and that the eleventh of them is a man of fifty-two on a shift nineteen years old, and that on the thirtieth day that man said three things in a working yard nine hundred feet off and the man with the stick was nine feet from him when he said them, and that a few hundreds of people in this city did not hear one of the three and will not, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp is the only person alive who has both the notebook and the three things and that there is nobody four hundred miles off who could use either, and that he has not written it down, and that about four people in that room have said since that a man who has not written it down is a man who has decided that a thing he cannot use is not improved by being written, and that this is the second time in a month he has decided that and that the first one was a line on a page that says a person, and that the two of them are about four inches apart in the same pocket and have not met and are not going to.**

---

**And at about the ninth hour of the thirtieth day of a count of days that this city keeps and that a man of thirty-two with a limp does not have, a child of nine years old came down a lane with a bundle on one shoulder and went out through a gate and up a far bank and away, and nine people went through behind it, and about four people in that lane have said since that a child of nine years old went out of this city on the thirtieth day and that it did not go out on the nineteenth and that nobody in this city has been told which of those two days was a duty and that the man of sixty-six who could have said was nine hundred yards off at a bench with a rule nine inches long in his hand and was not asked, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the end of that lane and did not go with it and did not call out and did not ask it anything, and that this is now the third time in twelve days that a person in this city has been in front of him and has not been asked anything by anybody, and that a page of foolscap in his coat has a seventh line on it that says a person and was looked at on the twenty-third and on the twenty-ninth and has not been finished on either, and that he has now been in this city thirty days and has not written under it once and that about four people in that lane have noticed that a man who has not written under a line for thirty days is a man who has stopped expecting to, and that a few hundreds of people in a city of about nine thousand people did not know any of it on the thirtieth day and will not.**

**And a man of fifty-two went up a ladder on the east wall of a glass works for the fourth time with a bucket in one hand and his other hand on the top of it, and a few of the people in that yard have said since that a man of fifty-two has been putting the foot of that ladder against that wall four times a day for nineteen years and that the wall is four hundred years old and is not flat and is not straight, and that there are nine places on it where a ladder will go and about nine where it will not, and that he knows the nine and knows which of the nine is the one for a man with a bucket in one hand, and that nobody in this city has ever asked him and that a few of the men in that yard have watched him do it about two thousand times and said so once.**

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp watched the feet go against the wall. That is the last thing that happened in this city for ninety days that anybody in it wrote down, and it was not written down, and a few of the people in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp had eleven lines in a notebook in the inside pocket of a coat and could not stop seeing the eleventh of them, and that the eleventh of them was a man of fifty-two on a shift nineteen years old, and that he had been told that morning that the two of them were the same arrangement, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp had been in this city thirty days and had been told a thing about himself that he cannot use and about a person of nine years old who is not named and has not been named in this city for four hundred years, and that the light was standing where it had been standing on the first day, and the oven was going to be hot at the ninth hour and would be hot at the second hour on the thirty-first, and the count in a book in a yard nine hundred yards off was eight, and the number on a printed sheet in a bundle of cord and reed on a road two hundred miles off was ten, and nobody in a city of about nine thousand people had put the two numbers side by side and was going to, and a man of fifty-two's feet went against a four-hundred-year-old wall in nine places out of the nine that will take a ladder, and the bucket was in his hand, and the light was standing where it was standing.**
