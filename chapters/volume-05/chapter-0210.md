# Chapter 0210

**The eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, a Sunday, and not a market day, and not a working day, and the fifth hour and after, and four hundred yards up this county's own road there is a crossing with no glass in its sill, and about four people were standing at it, and the road out of this county was opened at about the fifth hour and a half of that Sunday morning by a person who was not Aren Kest and who is nine years old, and there are a few people in this county who know that it was, and none of them knows how.**

The child did not do it with the block and did not do it with the hemp and did not do it with the sill. The child put a left hand flat on the sill, at about the middle of it, on the cold stone, in the way anybody puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the stone was cold on the other side of his hand too, and about a minute and a half later a man of thirty-two with a limp felt everything about a thing he had been building for four years stop being something he was holding.

**And there is no man in this county who can say what happened, and about four of the four hundred people in this county have said the same two sentences about it, and the two sentences are these, and neither of them improves on the other.**

Nobody has measured it. Nobody in Merefen can see it. There is no line in the coping on either side of that sill and there is nothing on it and it was nothing before and it is nothing after, and the only thing anybody in this county can point at is a child of nine years old who put a hand flat on a stone and took it off again and stood there.

**And a man of forty-seven on the bench of nine because he was elected was standing four feet off with his hands behind his back, and he had said the four things out loud at the fourth hour and had said them again because a witness repeats them, and he watched all of it, and about four people in this county have said that what he said afterwards was nine words and that it is the only thing said in this county on that Sunday that anybody has written down anywhere except a page of foolscap in a coat.**

“That was not him,” Bram Ottery said, on the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, at about the sixth hour, to the people who were on that abutment and about four of whom have repeated it. “**I have been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and I have never once seen the far end open and I did not know it went open from this end, and I want the hour said, and I said yes at the seventh hour on the sixth day before and the hour it happened was the fifth hour and a half and I am not going to have anybody in this county say the two were the same.**”

**And the four who were on that abutment at about the fifth hour and a half of that Sunday morning were a carter of thirty who had put a barrow down in the middle of a bay in this town in Harvestmonth and had not been able to account for what he was looking at, a man of about nineteen who had been hired in on a Friday in the same month and who had not been told that he was on the roll of anything, a salt merchant of sixty-eight who owns a loft four hundred yards from that bridge and who has let two rooms over a weigh-house for four years and a half to a body corporate that answers to nobody, and a boy of fifteen with a lime tub and a rule and a bill and nothing to do on a Sunday.**

None of them had been asked to be there. A few people in Merefen have said that the four of them are the four who would have gone out, and that the four of them are not the four who would have been sent, and that this is the only observation anybody in that county has made about anybody in it in four years that is worth anything, and that it was made afterwards and not at the time.

The carter of thirty had a barrow and put it down at the top of the bank and did not go down the bank. The man of about nineteen had come down four hundred yards of lane on the Sunday morning to look at a man standing on a bridge and had stayed for an hour and a half and had not understood one word of it and had not asked a question, and a couple of them in that county have said that a man of nineteen who stands on a bridge for an hour and a half without asking anything is going to be a foreman before he is thirty and that everybody in that yard knows it and nobody is going to say it to him.

The salt merchant of sixty-eight had been on the bridge about four minutes and had gone back to his loft, and a couple of people in Merefen have said that the man who owns the room that a keeper of records works in was on a bridge in Merefen on that Sunday and that the woman who works in his loft did not come out, and that the two of those facts are the arrangement and that the arrangement has been the arrangement for four years and a half and that the man does not know it and the woman does.

**And the boy of fifteen stood at the low end of the sill with his bill under his arm and looked at the child for about four minutes and then went back up the lane to the bench-board, and there is no bill on a Sunday, and he stood at the bench-board for about half an hour with the bill under his arm and did not write anything, and about four people in this county have said that a boy of fifteen who copies the empty space under a number stood at a board on a Sunday with nothing to copy and did not go home, and that nobody in this county has been able to say what the boy was waiting for and that it was not the bill.**

Nobody said they were the same. Most people in Merefen had not thought about it and two or three have, and the four had all come to the same place, which is that a witness repeats four words at the fourth hour and a road opens at the fifth hour and a half, and that those are an hour and a half apart, and that in a decade a man in this county will say a road out of Merefen went on the eighth of Embermonth at the fourth hour, and that he will be half an hour out and that there is nobody in this county who will be able to correct him.

---

**And then the child came back across the sill and up the near bank, and it came the way it had come four times in two days, which is slowly, and it stopped in front of the man of thirty-two with the limp and it did not say anything for a moment, and then it said one sentence, and it is the only sentence that child has said to anybody in this county, and a couple of them have it word for word and a few have said that they were not close enough and have not asked since.**

“Is that the far one,” the child said.

Nobody in this county knows what that sentence means, and two of them in Merefen have given four answers to it in nine days and none of the four has convinced anybody, and one of the four is a mason of fifty and a half and his answer was that a child who has been somewhere and is asked whether a place is the far one has been somewhere, and the second is a carter of thirty and his answer was that it is a child's way of asking whether a thing is finished, and the third is a woman of twenty-nine with a door on a lane and her answer was that it is a keeper's question and that she would want to know what was being kept, and the fourth is a man of forty-seven on a bench of nine and his answer was that it did not matter and that he was not going to be the man who asked.

**“That is the far one,” said the man of thirty-two with a limp, and the child looked at him for about as long as it takes to count nine and then went and picked its bundle up off the low end of the sill, and about four people in this county have said that a man of thirty-two answered a question he did not understand, in about three words, to a child of nine, and that it was the correct answer, and that he has not been asked about it since and does not want to be.**

And the child went back down four hundred yards of lane to the low end of it and stopped at the low end of it, and it was there at about the seventh hour and it was there at about the ninth hour when the tide was out, and it was there on the Monday, and it was there on the Tuesday, and a handful of them in this county have said that a child of nine that comes to a place on a Sunday and is there on the Monday is a child that is not going anywhere, and a few have said that they had not noticed, and neither of those is in a book.

---

**And the child gave him a page, and it did not give it to him, it was holding it and he took it, and it was a page torn out of something and folded in three and soft at the folds, and a few people in this county have seen that page and as many again have not, and it is not in a minute and it is not in a book under a stair and it is not in a coat any more, because it went into a coat at about the sixth hour on the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, and the coat is on a man of thirty-two with a limp who is in this county, and the county is four hundred miles from the place the page came from, and there is no road between the two of them.**

There is a date on it. The date is the fourteenth of Harvestmonth, YR 314, and it is in a fair hand and it is the date of an ordinary day in an ordinary county in a kingdom that keeps a calendar, and it is thirty-five days before the page was handed to him, and about four people in this county have said that a person who is sent a thing by somebody four hundred miles off does not expect it to be thirty-five days old on arrival, and that a bag in the ordinary post is the only instrument in this kingdom that everybody trusts, and that the two of those are the same fact.

**And there is one word on it under the date, and the word is the name of a place, and the place has a child's mark on it and it has no name on it, and a few people in this county have read that word and as many again have not, and not one of the first four has said it to anybody, and there are four people in that county who have said since that they heard the word spoken out loud in a provincial town two hundred miles off on the fourth of Embermonth, YR 314, in a room with a stove in it, by a man of sixty-one with a satchel, and that they did not know then what it was and that nobody has told them since, and that those are two facts about the same word and neither of them is going anywhere.**

Nobody read it aloud. A man of forty-seven was four feet off and did not read it aloud. A man of thirty-two with a limp read it in under half a minute and put the page back into the coat in three folds, and a few people in this county have said that he put it away in a hurry, and that a man who puts a page away in a hurry has been shown something he does not yet have a way of holding, and that a few in this county have said that a man of thirty-two putting a page away in a hurry on a Sunday morning at a toll is a thing a county could do without, and that nobody in that county went and asked him.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp went up four hundred yards of lane to the bench-board on that Sunday at about the sixth hour with the page in the coat and stood at a board where there is no bill, and a couple of them in that yard have said that he stood there for about a quarter of an hour and that he did not sit down, and that a boy of fifteen was there and neither of them said anything, and that a man of thirty-two who has not been in a yard on a Sunday in four years was in one on the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, and that the yard was bare and the lime was standing in the courses and the stone was the same temperature on both sides of everything in it.**

He did not write it down. He has had four of those in four years and has written one down, and the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it and he cannot account for where it came from, and there is nobody in this county he could ask about it, and he did not ask the man of forty-seven, who is not a road warden and has said so out loud in a room, and he did not ask the boy of fifteen, who is fifteen, and he did not ask himself, which is the one that is going to matter in about ninety days and that he has written nothing about anywhere.

And at about the seventh hour the tide was going out and the salt was not coming and the bell had stopped and the four of them were still on the bridge and the child was at the low end of the sill with its bundle on one shoulder, and a man of thirty-two with a limp came back down the lane from a bench-board where there is no bill, and stopped about ten feet short of the child, and about ten feet is the distance a man keeps from a thing he has been told in the last two hours is not his, and he had not thought about the ten feet and a couple of people in that county have said that he put it there without thinking about it and that the four of them have not worked out what the distance was for either.

**And the child did not say the word, and did not say where the page came from, and did not say who had sent it, and did not say its own name, and was not asked any of those things by anybody in this county, and a few people in this county have wanted to ask it its own name for about six days and as many have wanted to and have not, and there is a bench of nine in this county that has refused a road and refused a witness and put both of those twice in a minute under a stair, and that bench has never once been asked about a child, and neither has the clerk of about sixty who has kept that minute for nineteen years, and a few in that hall have said that a clerk would not rule a line across a child's name if he could and that he has said so out loud once and is not going to say it again.**

---

And the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, is a Sunday, and a Sunday is the one day in this county's week that is neither a working day nor a market day, and the market was not on and the lane was bare and the salt was not coming over the first bay of the Sedge Reach, and the reach was open with four men on it who had gone home, and the fourth bay was being pointed and the joint on its parapet is a foot and a half short, and there is a line in the third course of that bay about nine inches long under about half an inch of lime that has not moved, and a mark in the coping of the first bay and a mark in the parapet of the fourth bay that cannot be read at thirty feet by anybody in this county.

**And the road out of this county was opened at about the fifth hour and a half of that morning by a person who is nine years old and who is not Aren Kest, and the page that came with it is in a coat, and the page has a date on it and one word under the date, and the word is a place, and the place has a child's mark on it, and there is no name on the page and there is no name on the child, and nobody in that county read it aloud, and a few people in that county know the word and as many again have not, and a man of forty-seven on the bench of nine because he was elected was standing four feet off at a sill with no glass in it and had said the four things out loud at the fourth hour and had said them again because a witness repeats them, and the hour it happened was the fifth hour and a half and he would not have the two put in the same hour, and the child went back down four hundred yards of lane and was still at the low end of it at about the ninth hour when the tide was out.**

**And there is a mark in a child's hand and there is a page in a coat and there is no name on either of them, and the word on the page was not read aloud by anybody in that county on that Sunday, and it is the eighth of Embermonth, YR 314, and the ninth hour, and the bell was rung at the fourth hour and a half by a boy and a carpenter and not by anybody else, and the road was open, and nobody in Merefen agreed to it and nobody in Merefen refused it, and the child was at the low end of the lane.**
