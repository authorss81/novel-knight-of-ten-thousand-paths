# Chapter 0204

**The second of Embermonth, YR 314, a Monday, and not a market day, and the sixth hour, and there was a bag on a cart at the wharf in Merefen with three sheets in it and one of the three sheets had a space on it for a figure of persons and nothing whatever in the space, and a man of about fifty with a waggon of his own was standing beside the cart and Mara Vey was on the wharf steps with the bag in her hand and a man of thirty-two with a limp was four hundred yards up the lane at a bridge where nobody can be paid.**

The road out of this county is a lane for four hundred yards and then a county road for eleven miles up the channel and then two hundred miles of other counties, and it is made, and it is about four feet wide in places, and a waggon of stone goes up it four times a day out of a quarry, and about forty men a day go over the first bay of the Sedge Reach at the low end of that lane, and there is no toll on it anywhere in this county and there has not been one for longer than the oldest person in this room has been alive.

The bag is going to a provincial town two hundred miles off, and the county is putting three sheets in it, and the three sheets are the office's own form, filled in at every line except one.

**The form is a Count of continuing obligations in respect of a district, and it is ruled in five columns with a sixth line at the foot that has no head to it, and the columns ask what the thing is, on what day, who had knowledge of it, who keeps it, and how often. The columns do not ask where. There is no column on that sheet for a place, and a handful of them in this town have said that this county has beaten that form with its own construction once, in a moot hall, in about four minutes, and that the winning of it was to answer a question about persons with a figure, on the ground that a figure is not a person and no person is in default of a thing they were never asked about.**

The return went back with the first column filled in and the second filled in and the third filled in and the fifth filled in, and the fourth column, which is headed *Keeper*, has a figure in it and no name, and the sixth line at the foot, which has no head to it, has nothing in it at all.

**And the space for a figure of persons is empty, and it is empty on purpose, and the reason is in a document that goes in the same bag, and the document is four lines long and it was written in about four minutes by a keeper of records who has four years and a half of an office that can be sued and has never once been asked a question by anybody in this county and did not ask one.**

*That the office has asked this county twice for a figure of persons and has not been given one, and that a figure of persons is not a thing this county holds, and that an office which will not print a number of persons cannot show that any person has not been instructed, and that this office declines to supply a number in order to assist another office in showing a thing it has not shown.*

**And the space is empty because it is empty, and not because the answer is in it, and a magistrate of this county beat the same form in a moot hall in about four minutes and told a room that a figure under a head count and a figure over one are the same figure, because neither of them is a count, and some of them in this town have that sentence and two of them are on the reach four hundred yards up the lane.**

---

**And what it cost, and the addition was done on the page because his hand does that, and the total was said after it was footed and not before.**

*Two men, two days, at fourpence the day.* 4, twice over, is 8. 8, twice over, is **sixteen**.

*Two horses, two nights at a stable, at threepence a horse a night.* 3, twice over, is 6. 6, twice over, is **twelve**.

*A bed and a supper for two men, two nights, at twopence.* 2, twice over, is 4. 4, twice over, is **eight**.

*Three sheets for a return, at twopence the sheet.* 2, three times over, is **six**.

*Sixteen and twelve is twenty-eight. Twenty-eight and eight is thirty-six. Thirty-six and six is **forty-two pence, which is three shillings and sixpence**.*

And it is three shillings and sixpence in this county's money, and there is no item in it for the room, and there is no item in it for the keeper, and there is no item in it for the second copyist's hour at eightpence the hour, and the second copyist said so before anybody asked her, and said that this office has entered a figure of its own keeper's time exactly never and is not going to start on a Wednesday with a bag going out of the county.

**And this county's own price of two hundred miles of road and two men and two horses and two nights is not a figure, and the two are not added, and there is no way on this earth to add them, and the man who wrote the addition on the page has done it in four counties in four years and has never once tried to do it in the other direction.**

“Three shillings and sixpence,” said the man of about fifty, “for two hundred miles, and I have carried a waggon up that road for a figure of that sort every week of my life, and I want it entered that I said that, and I want it entered that I am not being paid for the road. I am being paid for the bag. A man is paid for carrying a thing and he is paid the same whether the thing is a bag of paper or a bag of salt, and if he is paid for the road then somebody in a decade will work out that a road pays, and there is no such thing.”

---

---

**And a road out of a county is a thing a person can picture in under half a minute and cannot build in four years, and the difference between those two things is what a bag in a cart is for.**

There are eleven miles of it up the channel and it was made about forty years ago out of the gravel of a quarry, and it is four feet wide in two places and six in most, and it has a crown on it in one stretch and nothing at all on it in another, and the nothing-at-all stretch is a mile and a half where somebody widened it in a wet year by putting the hedge back nine feet and the nine feet is still settling. There is a ford at about the fourth mile that is made and unmade every winter and the county repairs it in a fortnight in Fallowmonth and the same fortnight every year, and the man who does it is a man of about thirty and is paid out of a box and the box is not the chest under the wharf window.

And there is a mile of it at about the seventh mile where the metalling is gone and what there is instead is a stone surface laid by somebody for a length of about forty yards, and no person in this county knows who laid it or when, and about four people have said that a road is a thing that somebody built and maintained and a thing that somebody used, and that this county has never had anybody in it who was paid to think about the eleven miles as a road rather than as a way to a quarry, and that eleven miles is a short road and the reason it has taken forty years is that nobody in this county has ever needed it to be anything but a way to a quarry.

**And a few in this county have said that the road out of it is the only thing here that anybody would call a road, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in this town since he was seventeen and has walked or ridden or pushed a barrow over nine of those eleven miles in four years and has never once been asked by anybody in this county what the road is for, and that on the second of Embermonth, YR 314, a bag went up it.**

---

**And the second copyist of that office is thirty and is on a warrant of the keeper's own that is not in the press, and she stood on the wharf steps with the bag at her feet and did not touch it, and two or three of them in Weir Street have said since that she would not put her hand on a bag that was going out of the county.**

“It is a bundle of nine things on a second shelf with a piece of tarred hemp through it,” she said, “and the tenth came in the ordinary post in Harvestmonth and was opened by the keeper and not by me, and I said the day I open the next one is the day this office is a body corporate that goes through a county's post, and it is the first of Embermonth and nothing has come, and I have looked at the shelf about four times a day since and it is the same nine things and the hemp has not moved.

“**And I am not touching this bag, because a bag going out is not the same as a thing coming in and I have been six years in an office and I know which of the two I would rather be the woman who did.**”

And the keeper of records said, “It is a form with an empty space in it and there is no law against a county posting a form,” and the second copyist said, “There is no law against it either and that is everything I have been saying to you for six years,” and the keeper said, “No, it is not,” and read the four lines again, and about four people in that room have said that the two of them have had that argument four times in two years and have got to the same place four times.

**And there is a strip of cut paper in a press in a room up two flights of that stair that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is the account of nobody, and there is a half tile in the same press that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is a tile, and a man of about sixty in this county who swept round a tile on a coping for forty-one years and wanted it lifted is four hundred miles off and cannot be sent for, and neither of those two objects is in the bag and neither of them is going to be.**

---

**And then he gave her the figures before she asked, and he has done that four times in five weeks, and she has never once asked for one of them, and there are nine entries in that day's book that are the not asking and there is not going to be a tenth, and entering a tenth would be entering a question.**

The figures were these, and he gave them to Mara Vey before she asked. A leg that is the same leg it was in June and does not get up a bank any better and is slower now and about a month out of a fever and a week of fever behind it. A line of about nine inches in the third course of the fourth bay of the reach, under about half an inch of lime, that has not moved, that he looked at once and has not measured, and that a man of forty-seven has said he will not have measured and a man of about sixty with a level will not put a rule on, and that he is not going to be a man who puts a figure in a book about a thing he has looked at twice. A mark cut in a parapet on the twenty-fifth of Fallowmonth at the height of a thumb in front of about twenty men with no name in it and a reason given in four sentences, and the reason is in four or five mouths in this town and in no book. A road out of this county on the eighth of Embermonth, a Sunday, at the fourth hour, with a man of forty-seven at one end of it and a witness at the other, and a bench of nine that has refused to keep it and has refused to witness it and has entered both of those twice in a minute under a stair, and the last of those refusals is in a clerk's hand and a clerk of that county has kept that minute for nineteen years and has never entered a name in it that somebody else in the room did not put there first.

He gave her all of it in about four minutes and it was slower than the writing and not better, and that has been the practice of that office since the first year.

**And he did not tell her that he cannot. He has not told her that on any of the four times and she has never once asked him for it, and the not asking stands at nine, and about four people in that room have noticed that neither of them has ever once referred to the morning and neither of them is going to.**

She read the four lines on the separate sheet twice, and stopped at the third one.

“Eightpence an hour is the county rate for me and you have left me off a bag that is going two hundred miles,” she said. “That is not modesty and I do not want it read as modesty, and if it is going in a document it goes in as an omission and not as a kindness.”

“It goes in as an omission,” he said. “And the omission is the finding, and it is in a document and not in a column, and a magistrate of this county wrote that a finding is a document and not a form about eleven years ago and it has been in a press ever since.”

“That was another woman's document and she signed it and I have not signed anything,” she said, “and I am not going to, and that is not a complaint and it is the only thing in this conversation I am certain of.”

---

**And the man of about fifty put the bag on the cart himself and would not let the second copyist touch it and would not let the keeper touch it, and a couple of them in Weir Street have said since that a carrier of fifty who has a waggon of his own and a firm of two and a rate on about a hundred and forty miles of haulage in four counties carried a bag of paper up onto his own cart and would not have three women in a room touch it, and that he said one thing about it and then said nothing else about it for an hour.**

“A bag of paper goes up that road two hundred miles and it is going to arrive and it is going to be read by people who are not going to be in a hurry about it,” he said. “**That is not a complaint. I have been up a pass twice in eleven weeks and walked nine days and I did not go up it to ask anybody anything, and I have said in a hall that the day I go up it to ask is the day I have a reason to be listened to that has nothing to do with the asking, and I am not going up there. I am going along this road with a bag, and I am being paid for the bag, and the two hundred miles is the longest haul I have done in a month and I will be glad when it is behind me and I will not be asked to do it again in a hurry.**”

**And he went up the four hundred yards of lane and over the Sedge Reach with the cart and the road was open and about forty men were on it and the salt was on the lane, and at four hundred yards up the road there is a bridge with no glass in the sill and no third seat and no owner and no money, and the tolls on it have been suspended by an instrument a court has found lawful and has said it has nothing further to say about at any time, and he went over it and paid nothing, and a good half of them in this town have said that a man who cannot be paid at a bridge is a man who has to go round and that he does not go round, and that the arrangement of that bridge is the reason this county has no toll on any road in it and that nobody has ever written that down.**

**And Mara Vey went up the two flights of that stair to a room with a door on a lane and a press in it, and the man of thirty-two went up four hundred yards of lane to a bridge, and the two of them went in opposite directions at about the same hour on a Monday and neither of them said one word about it and about four people in Weir Street and a few in that hall have said since that she had told him in nine sentences in a room of two in Harvestmonth that she was not going to walk up that road with him, and that she has not said it again, and that a person who has said a thing once and is not going to say it again is not a person who has been contradicted, and that nobody in this town has heard her say it twice and nobody is going to.**

And a child of nine years old was at the low end of the lane with a bundle on one shoulder and did not say a word to anybody in this town, and the man of about fifty put a fish pie and a piece of bread on a stone at the kerb at about the seventh hour and did not hand it to the child and did not speak to the child and got back in his cart, and the child was still there at the eighth hour and the food was gone.

**And the bag went out of Merefen at about the seventh hour on the second of Embermonth, YR 314, and two hundred miles of made road took it, and nobody in this county agreed to the road and nobody in this county refused it, and the road is four feet wide in places and a waggon of stone goes up it four times a day, and about four hundred yards up that lane there is a mark in a parapet at the height of a thumb that will be a stone in about two hundred years, and at the low end of the lane there was a child with a bundle and a corner of folded paper out of it, and the two of them went out of this town in opposite directions on that Monday and nobody joined them and nobody is going to.**
