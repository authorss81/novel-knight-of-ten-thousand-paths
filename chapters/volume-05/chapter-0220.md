# Chapter 0220

**The nineteenth day, and the fourth hour and a half, and a yard at the bottom of a lane with a roof on two sides and no roof on the other two and about two hundred bundles of cord on racks, and a man of sixty-six at a bench with a rule nine inches long, and a child of nine years old standing at the bench with its bundle on one shoulder and a mark on its left hand, and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick at the mouth of the yard forty yards off who did not come in, and this is where the ninth asking in four hundred years was made and where the ninth thing this city has refused in four generations was given, and one of them took the time it takes to draw a breath and the other took no longer than that, and neither of those figures is anybody's estimate of how long it took to be important.**

He did not make it a thing. That is the first fact about the ninth asking and four people in that yard have said since that it was not dressed and was not put in a room and was not entered, and that a man of sixty-six who has entered about four hundred refusals in twenty years made the ninth one standing at a bench in a yard with a child of nine years old and about two hundred bundles of cord on racks behind him and a man with a stick at the mouth of the lane.

**“You have not been out since the twelfth,”** said the man of sixty-six, and the child did not answer him, and he went on as he had gone on the first time, and four people in that city have said since that a man of sixty-six checks a date in his own head and then decides what the date is for, and that on four hundred occasions it has been a door and a loaf and once it has been a question, and that nobody in that yard has ever known which it was going to be until he got there.

Then he said, “I want you to take one out to the west gate this afternoon.”

And the child looked at him.

**“I am not going to tell you what it is and I am not going to tell you where it goes and you are not going to ask me, and I would like that entered as well, and I would like the two of them entered together, and there is nobody in this city I can enter them with.**”

“**No,**” said the child.

It took the time it takes to draw one breath. Four people in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six has entered about four hundred refusals in twenty years and that the shortest of the four hundred was nine seconds and that the shortest of anything that has ever been said in that yard was a second and a half and was said by a child of nine years old to a man of sixty-six, and that about four hundred years of a city making careful, correct, eleventh-word decisions came to that, and that nobody in that yard improved on it, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp at the mouth of the lane did not move and did not call out and did not say one word, and that the not saying was four seconds long and that four seconds is about the time it takes a man of thirty-two to work out four things at once and he had a great many of them to work out and did not get through one.

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp did work one of them out, standing at the mouth of that yard, and it was this, and nobody in the lane has said it since and he has not said it either: a man of sixty-six had said *a man of twenty-eight* and not a name, and the only person of twenty-eight in that city who is turned back at a gate four days a week is a woman who has been carrying for four years, and a man of sixty-six had known that, and had not sent her, and had sent the child instead, and had not said why, and had said in advance that he was not going to say why, and four people in that city have said since that a man of sixty-six put a woman of twenty-eight's four years of being turned round at three gates into nine words in a yard to a man he had met once and that the nine words did not explain anything and that the putting of it in was the thing, and that this is the only time anybody in this city has put a person's difficulty in a sentence and a man of thirty-two with a limp is the only man alive who has worked out what it meant and he did it in nine seconds at the mouth of a yard and has not written it down.**

---

**And the man of sixty-six went to the far end of his own yard where the book was, and the board was off it, and he turned the book round, and four people in that city have said since that a man of sixty-six has turned that book round in front of a stranger once in nineteen years and that a stranger has never seen the page and could not have read it standing where he was and that the turning round was not for the stranger and was not against the stranger and was the act of a man putting a thing away.**

The book is in two columns and it is about two inches thick and it has a board over it and four people in that city have said since that the board is there for the sun and the rain and not for anybody, and that it has been there since before the man who keeps it was born, and that this is a thing nobody in the city has ever checked.

He turned to a page, and there was a ruled space at the top of it and a line ruled across the top of the space, and the space was the same width as every other space in the book, and four people in that city have said since that a man of sixty-six looked at a space in a book and that a person watching him could see that the space was the right size and that it was the wrong shape, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what shape a space for a person is, and that there is not a column for it anywhere in the world that this city has ever made or answered, and that a form in a town two hundred miles off that has a space for a figure of persons and nowhere at all for the name of a person has exactly the same trouble, and that neither of those two things is in the same book and nobody has joined them and nobody is going to.

He did not write. That is all he did and four people in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six kept a pen in his hand and did not write, and that he is a man whose whole work is writing down the things this city says no to, and that he has never once in twenty years not written, and that the silence is the first of its kind in this yard and that there is nobody in the city he could ask about it because there is no office in the city that does that and nobody in it appoints anybody to anything.

Then he shut the book and turned it round with the spine toward himself and put the board over it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the mouth of that yard and had a form in a coat and a page in a coat and a line in his palm and had watched a man of sixty-six not write, and four people in that city have said since that the man with the stick has now watched two men in this city fail to put a thing in a book on the same day, and that one of the two stopped because there was nothing to write and the other one stopped because there was no column to write it in, and that this is the second of two in a city of about nine thousand people and that they are nine hundred yards apart and nobody has joined them and nobody in either yard has heard of the other.

---

**And then the man of sixty-six said the thing, and it is said once, in trade terms, to a man of thirty-two with a limp at the mouth of a yard, and about ninety words long, and it is the only time in nineteen years that a man of sixty-six has said anything to a man he had already refused, and four people in that city have said since that they have all four heard it and that none of the four has been able to say one word about it, and that about four of the eleven in the room at the top of the lane have never heard it and will not.**

**“A man can be asked twice and can be asked it a different way the second time. A column cannot be asked anything at all. That is nine refusals in four generations and eight of the nine were given by eleven people in a room, and a room is a column. The ninth was given by a person. It is the only one of the nine I have never been able to put on a page, and I am not going to find a way to put it on a page, and that is not a complaint, it is a fact about a book.**”

He said it once and went back to the bench and picked up the rule, and did not say it again then or on the twenty-second or on any of the other days of a count of days that this city keeps and that nobody in it is keeping except two men and one of the two is sixty-six and the other is sixty-one, and four people in that city have said since that a man of sixty-six has said one thing in about ninety words in nineteen years and that the ninety words are the only sentence anybody in this city has ever said that a person on the outside of it would agree with, and that four people in that city have said since that they do not entirely agree with it, and that they cannot say which part, and that they have had nineteen days to think about it and have not got it.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the mouth of that yard with four words ready that he had had ready for thirteen days, and he did not say them, and the child of nine years old was at the bench with a mark on one hand and a bundle on one shoulder and did not go out that afternoon, and the ninth asking in four hundred years had been made and the ninth refusal in four generations had been given, and there was no form for it, and there is no column for it, and the number did not go up anywhere, and four people in that city have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp had a question ready for thirteen days and the city produced the answer in front of him, and that the answer was a person and not a column, and that a man who has spent four years learning to be answered by a document stood forty yards off and was answered by a person and did not open his mouth.**

Nobody in that city knew what had happened. That is the fact of the nineteenth day and four people in that yard have said since that a man of sixty-six did not write a line, and that a child of nine did not go out, and that a man of thirty-two did not call out, and that not one of the three of them said a word about it to anybody, and that a city of about nine thousand people went on doing what it had done at the second hour and at the ninth hour, and that the lamp was standing where it had been standing since he came down the eastern road, and that the count in a book on a bench under a roof was the same count it had been on the eighteenth day.

**And it was the same count, and nobody looked, and that is all there is to what a person can be asked twice about: on the nineteenth day a person was asked and said no and the thing that said no has not gone into anything, and the man who makes the things that say no has a book with a page for every one of them except that one, and he put the board over it and turned the spine toward himself and picked up a rule nine inches long, and the child of nine years old took its bundle off its shoulder and put it on again and did not go out that afternoon, and about four hundred people in that city did not know any of it and will not, and one man four hundred miles off is going to be told a number in nine weeks or so and the number will be right.**

---

**And the child did not go out that afternoon, and the reason it did not go out is that it said no, and the reason nobody in that city has done anything about a child not going out on a working day is that about four hundred people in it did not know, and the two of those are the same fact and there is no third thing to say about it, and four people in that city have said since that a city of nine thousand people has missed the ninth thing that has ever happened to it by the width of a breath and that this is the ordinary way a city misses a thing and that the ordinary way is not carelessness.**

It stayed in the yard. It put the bundle down on the bench beside a rule nine inches long and a pot of the thinnest glue and it stood about four feet off and it did not touch anything, and a man of sixty-six worked on at the bench and did not tell it to go and did not tell it to stay, and four people in that city have said since that a man of sixty-six and a child of nine worked in the same yard for about three hours on the afternoon of the nineteenth day and that neither of them said one word for about nine hundred seconds and that this is the longest silence either of them has ever had in that yard and that the man counted it and the child did not, and that the man has not put the nine hundred seconds anywhere.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the mouth of that yard for about three hours and did not come in and did not go away, and a woman of about thirty came down the lane at about the sixth hour with a pail and saw him and said, “You are the man with the stick,” and he said yes, and she said, “You have been standing there since the fourth hour and a half,” and he said that he had, and she said, “**Then you have been standing in a lane for three hours and a man with a limp does not stand in a lane for three hours, and I am not going to ask you why and you are not going to tell me, and about four people in this city have not asked you why and I am the fifth and I am telling you that I am not the sixth.**”**

She went on down the lane with the pail. And four people in that city have said since that a woman of thirty counted three hours of a man standing in a lane and did not ask, and that the man did not go in, and that between the two of those facts a child of nine years old was in a yard with a man of sixty-six saying nothing to each other about nine hundred seconds, and that this is the second time in nineteen days that a woman in that city has told a stranger the whole of a thing in nine words and then gone to work.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp had four words ready at the fourth hour of the nineteenth day and had them ready at the fourth hour of the sixth day, and a page of foolscap in a coat has a seventh line on it that says a person and has said a person for thirteen days, and the four words and the seventh line are about the same thing and he has not put them together and would not be able to say how they go together, and about four people in four counties have noticed the not asking and none of them has asked him, and the fifth of those is a city that has made the not asking into a trade, and the sixth of those is a woman of thirty with a pail who told him in a lane at the sixth hour on the nineteenth day that she was not going to be the one to ask and that about four people in that city had not asked and that she was the fifth, and he has not written any of it down, and he did not go into that yard, and the child of nine years old was in there with a mark on one hand for about three hours and came out at the ninth hour with the bundle on its shoulder and did not go out through a gate and did not go out through a lane to a gate and went home, or to wherever a child of nine in that city goes at the ninth hour, and about four hundred people in a city of about nine thousand did not know that anything at all had happened in it that day, and a man of sixty-six put a board over a book and turned the spine of it toward himself and picked up a rule nine inches long, and the count in that book was the same count it had been on the eighteenth day, and it was going to be the same count on the twentieth and on the ninth and on every other day of a count of days that this city keeps and that nobody in it is keeping except two men and one of the two is sixty-six and the other is sixty-one and neither of the two has said a word to the other in nineteen years or longer.**
