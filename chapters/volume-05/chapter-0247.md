# Chapter 0247

**The twenty-second day of Goatmonth, YR 315, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the third hour and the fourth hour, and a lane off Weir Street with a weigh-house under it and two rooms over that weigh-house and a door on that lane, and about four hundred people in this town who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp said nine sentences in that room today to a woman who had not asked him for one of them and that she did not accept any of the nine, and that one of the two of them has had a question of her own for about five months and has not asked it until today.**

The copying season had been over for twenty-two days. A press in that room has about four hundred documents in it and about four hundred of them have gone out of it since Hearthmonth, and a county that stops copying when the season ends is a county that thinks the work was the season, and neither of the two women in that room has said so, and one of them has four years and a half of a body corporate and the other six years in the office, and neither is paid by the season.

A fair copy and a working lie on a bench about four inches apart. The fair copy is four hundred and eight words of the account of a valley of about four hundred people in a county that is not on any map, and the working is a second sheet off a board under a stair, and the working is the only piece of paper in that office anybody has ever had to be shown and about four people in four years and a half have been shown it and every one of them was shown it in the rain. A man of thirty-two with a limp has been in that room nine times and has looked at both of the two of them and cannot say which is the dangerous one, and a man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard four hundred miles off has said out loud that a man in a room is eleven feet from a man in a yard and four years apart and that it is the only arithmetic he has ever needed.

He came up the stairs at about the second hour with a stick in his right hand and his coat over his arm, and he put the coat on the back of a chair that is not the chair the coat was on in the spring, and neither of the two women in that room remarked on it.

---

**And there are two presses within four hundred yards of each other in this county, and about four people in this town have worked out what the difference between them is, and none of the four has said it, and both of the women in that room have thought about it.**

The one in that office has about four hundred documents in it and every one of them can be produced, on the order of a body corporate, to a person who asks in a form, and the office is fourpence a year and can be sued and cannot be reached. The other is in a room four hundred yards up Weir Street with a bench-board in it and a boy of fifteen at the bench-board, and there are four sheets of printed paper in it with four empty places in them and a place name in the object column and a word in front of the place name that nobody in this county can read the sense of, and that press belongs to a yard and not to an office and nobody can be served with anything out of it and nobody has ever tried.

A woman of twenty-nine read the four things off the wall of her own kitchen once in forty years and a boy of fifteen wrote them on the back of a bill he is not going to be given, and a man of forty-seven has said the same four things in three counties in a year and has never once said where he got them, and four people in two counties have said since that the four of those are standing in a kitchen in a fen county and in the mouth of a man in a hut and on a page in a city about four hundred miles off and that the three that are the same are the same by accident.

Nobody in that room has ever put the two presses beside each other, and a second copyist of about thirty has said out loud in a room of two that a press you can be asked for and a press you cannot are the same press with two different heads on, and that the county has both of them, and that the county has had both of them since the spring, and that she is the only person in four hundred yards who has said it and she has said it once.

---

**And he said them, and it was nine sentences, and he said them once, and he said them to a woman who had not asked him for one of them, and a second copyist of about thirty was in the room for the first one and about four and a half of the nine and has said since that she was not hiding and was not leaving and that a room of two becomes a room of three four sentences into a thing and there is no way in that room to be out of it and not rude.**

He said them in the plain way and he did not improve on any of them and he did not say a tenth.

“**I have been treating a refusal as a thing that had not happened yet.**”

“**I have said that out loud in three counties in about five months and I have never said it to the person it is about, and I have said it to a man of forty-seven four times and to a yard wall and to a bench-board and to nobody.**”

“**I was in a place about four hundred miles off for ninety days and it said no nine times in a book and eleven times in a man's head, and I sat there the whole of it waiting for the no to become a thing that had not happened.**”

“**A man in a room two hundred miles off filled a figure of persons into a column on a Thursday because a form wanted one, and he is right that a number of persons is the one kind of paper that cannot be handed back, and he is a careful man who has been afraid of a column for eleven years and did it anyway because a thing left the building and nobody in that room had to agree to it.**”

“**A bench of nine in this town said no to me on Saturday in eleven words about a word and not about a road, and a woman who keeps a door on a lane put five questions in a book and would not put a number in it, and both of those took about four minutes and I have not written either of them down.**”

“**And the thing I have been treating as not-happened is not one place and it is not an office and it is not that city, it is all of them at once, and I have been doing it by being patient, and patience is a way of not being told anything twice.**”

“**I did not know that a no was a person until I was in a place that had nine of them and every one of them had been given by somebody who could not be asked a second time.**”

“**There is a notebook in the inside pocket of the coat on the back of that chair and there is a page in it with a seventh line that says a person, and I have looked at that line four times and finished it four times not, and I have not finished it this morning either, and that is the ninth sentence and you did not ask me for any of them.**”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds and a second copyist of about thirty has said that four seconds is the length of that silence and that she counted it and that she counted it because it is the trade.

---

**And she did not accept it, and the reason she did not accept it is in her own words and it is longer than the nine sentences and it is not an apology and it is not a kindness, and it is the whole of what a room with a door on a lane is for.**

“**No, and I am not going to accept that, and I would like the not-accepting entered in the same place the nine sentences are, which is nowhere.**”

He did not say anything. A second copyist of about thirty has said since that she watched a man of thirty-two with a limp be told no by a person who is not a bench and not a reeve and not a magistrate and has no vote and no office, and that he took it standing up, and that he did not ask why, and that the not-asking took about a second and a half and that she has thought about the second and a half more than about the nine sentences.

“**That is a finding. A finding is a thing this office enters, and a finding has a day against it and a person against it, and you have put a person in a column and given the column a head, and the head is a thing you decided about, and you decided it about yourself at a table in a room with a door on a lane without asking anybody whether the room was the right place to put it.**”

“**I have five questions in that register and not one of them is a finding, and I have not got a reason for that in any form I could put in a book and I have stopped looking for one, and the reason is that a question can be asked a second time and a finding cannot. And you have handed me a finding about yourself and a date I have never had, and I cannot enter it, and I am not going to enter it, and if you had wanted it entered you were in the wrong room and I would have told you so if you had asked me to.**”

And then she said the other half, and it is longer, and it is the only thing she has said in four years and a half that was about what a room costs.

“**And I am not going to be kind about it, because a kind thing said at that table is the most expensive thing in this building and I have four years and a half of a body corporate that answers to nobody and I am not going to spend one sentence of it on a thing nobody asked me for.**”

---

**And he did not say that she was right, and he did not say that he had been wrong, and no apology of any kind was available in that room on that Monday, and the reason is not a rule and is not a wall and is a sentence she said out loud in a gateway in another month, and it was standing in that room the whole of the time, and nobody in that room referred to it, and it is not referred to here.**

A man of thirty-two with a limp and a woman of twenty-nine in a room of four hundred documents have not said one word to each other about the morning, and he has not offered it and she has not asked it, and the not askings stand at nine and there is a book in that county nobody can be served with and there is not a tenth in it and there is not going to be a tenth, and the tenth would be one of the two of them breaking first.

Nobody improved on the nine sentences. A second copyist of about thirty wrote nothing down, and did not take a leaf out of the book of working papers, and has said since that a woman who writes down a thing that is not in her column is a woman who has started to keep a roll, and that she has four years and a half of the same view and got it in this room.

**And the second copyist said one thing in that room on that Monday, and it is longer than either of the other two things said in it that day, and it is the only thing she has ever said in six years that was not about a post she did not want, and she said it to the window and not to either of them.**

“**I have been in this room six years and I have watched two people not say a thing to each other for four months and it is the longest sentence anybody has ever said in it, and I am not going to be the one who breaks it, and if either of you want to break it you will have to do it in a room with no press in it.**”

Nobody answered her and she went back to the copying and did not say anything else, and about four people in that building have said since that a woman who has wanted a post with her name in a day-book for two years and has said she does not want it is the only person in that room who can be trusted with a silence, and that the trust is not a kindness and is not given and does not have to be asked for.

---

**And then she asked him a question, and it was a question and not a finding, and she told him so before he had answered it, and that is the last thing that happened in that room on the twenty-second day of Goatmonth, YR 315 and it is the question that has been in that register in one form or another for about five months.**

“**Go and stand at the window. I have got a question of my own and I have entered it four times and I have had no answers at all, and you are the only person I have ever stood in a room with who has been in a room with a man carrying a list of about four hundred doors, and that man said out loud that he did not know what was in the four places on a sheet and had never once asked. I am asking you what is on that list and on a second list in this county that is the same.**”

“I have not seen the second one,” he said, after about four seconds.

“**Then you will not be able to tell me, and neither can I, and two men have carried about four hundred doors each for six years and eight years and nobody in either county has ever asked one of them a question about it. That is my question and you have now had it and you have not answered it and I am not going to make you.**”

He did not know whether the two lists were the same four hundred, and he said so, and he said it in four words and he did not guess, and a man of thirty-four with lime has said out loud that a man who is asked a question he could have guessed at and does not guess at is a man who has been asked something worth asking.

**And nobody added them together, in that room or in any other, and they are on two pieces of paper in two counties two hundred miles apart and nobody has put one of them beside the other one, and there is no column in this office for a number of doors and there never has been, and a man of thirty-one with a satchel has carried about two thousand sheets of a printed series in six years and has never once known what is in the four places and has never once asked, and none of the three of them has ever been in a room with either of the other two.**

He went out of the door on the lane at about the fourth hour and down forty feet of lane to a house in Weir Street, and he did not write the nine sentences down, and the notebook went into the inside pocket of a coat with a page in it that has eleven lines and one new line and a seventh line on a separate leaf that says a person, and there is no line in any of the three of them about a woman in a room refusing a sentence, and a man of forty-seven came to that house in the evening and said the four things and asked nothing, for the fourth time in a year, and a woman of about sixty heard all four of them and did not repeat them.
