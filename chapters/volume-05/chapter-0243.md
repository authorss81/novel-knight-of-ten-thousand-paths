# Chapter 0243

**The second of Goatmonth, YR 315, a Tuesday, and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a lane off Weir Street in a town of about four thousand people with a weigh-house in it and two rooms over that weigh-house and a door on that lane, and about four people in that town do not know that a man of sixty-one stood at that door for about forty minutes on that Tuesday and was not let in and did not go away, and one of the four is a man of about nineteen who came down four hundred yards of lane to see what a man was doing at a door and did not ask and has not asked since.**

The copying season in that county is Hearthmonth and it is over by nine days, and two women in that room are still copying, and about four people in that town have said since that a county that stops copying when the season ends is a county that thinks the work was the season, and that neither of the two women in that room has said anything about it, and that one of them has four years and a half of a body corporate and the other has six years in the office and neither of them is paid by the season.

The room is over a weigh-house and the weigh-house weighs carts and has a floor of boards that take the tide up through them in a wet week, and there is a bench against the wall that is too short for a man of thirty-two with a limp and a table that is the right height for a man of thirty-two and not for a man of thirty-two with a limp, and a press with about four hundred documents in it, and a shelf, and a door on a lane that anybody in that town may knock on and about four people in a generation have.

**The office is fourpence a year.** That is the whole of it and it has been fourpence a year for four years and a half and it is on a lease in a tradesman's hand and it is paid for the year, and the two rooms over that weigh-house are two shillings a month each and they are the salt merchant's and not the office's and the two of those figures are in two different books in two different counties and about four people in that town have said since that a price gets made out of two things that are not the same thing and then somebody files it, and that nobody in that county has ever filed either of them.

The deed is thirteen words and it is a thing a tradesman copied out of a common form and got wrong in the ninth word and did not know he had got it wrong, and it says, *for the keeping of what this county cannot be shown it has lost*, and a woman of twenty-nine has read it out loud to about four people in four years and a half and it has not improved in the telling.

---

**And a man of sixty-one stood at that door at about the fourth hour of the second of Goatmonth, YR 315, and he had been in the house he works in since the middle of last year and had come up about two hundred miles of road with a satchel and a waxed cloth and a small hammer in it that he has never used and has never explained.**

She saw him from the window and she did not go down and she did not open the door, and about four people in that town have said since that a woman of twenty-nine who keeps two rooms over a weigh-house watched a man of sixty-one come down a lane and stand at her door and did not go down, and that she has refused him four things in two years and that this is the fifth, and that the fifth is the one he came about.

**He did not knock.** That is the fact of about forty minutes of a Tuesday afternoon in a town of about four thousand people, and it is the fourth time in two years that man has come up that lane and not knocked, and about four people in that town have said that a man who does not knock at a door he has been refused at four times is a man who has decided that the door is not going to change, and that two of them think that is respect and two of them think it is something else, and that the woman inside has not worked out which and has not asked him.

He stood about nine feet off the door, which is the distance a man keeps from a thing he has been told twice is not his, and he had not thought about the nine feet and about four people in that town have said that a man of sixty-one put it there without thinking about it and that a man of about nineteen also put nine feet there without thinking about it on a different Tuesday in the same year, and that the two men have never spoken and that about four people in that town have noticed the same distance twice and have not said so to each other.

At about the fifth hour he said one thing to the door, and it was about nineteen words, and it is the only thing he said in about forty minutes, and about four people in that town have said since that it was said at a door and not to a window and that the door was shut the whole of the time.

“**I have been in a building two hundred miles off eighteen months and I can find a thing in it in about four minutes, and there is a thing in it about a place that is yours, and it is in a file, and a file is a thing somebody has decided about, and I am not able to tell you what is in it and I am not going to try.**”

Nobody inside said anything. He put the satchel down on the step and stood about nine feet off the door and picked it up again in about a minute, and a woman of about twenty-nine behind a window with a door on a lane watched a man of sixty-one pick a satchel up off a step and put it on his shoulder, and about four people in that town have said since that a man who has come two hundred miles to say that he cannot tell you something has said the only sentence that anybody in this county has heard about it, and that nobody in this county has asked him a question and that the man of about sixty-one did not wait to be asked one.

---

**And what he had come about, and did not say, and could not have said if he had been asked, was in a press in that room, and it was four entries in a register that the office keeps and nobody can be served with.**

The register is a book. It is not the press and it is not the day-book and the day-book is the only book in that office nobody can be served with, and the register is a different book, and the difference is that the day-book is entered with a rule and the register is entered with a question, and about four people in four years and a half have said that a keeper of records who enters a thing she means to do has made a plan, and that a keeper of records who enters a question has not.

There are four entries in that register and all four of them are in her own hand and all four of them begin with a district name, and a district is a word she learned off a printed form that came in the ordinary post about five months back, and a district is not a place in this county and there is no such thing in this county, and the word is on about nine hundred sheets in a provincial town two hundred miles off and on about four of them in a tray in a passage and now in a file in a room with the working papers on it.

**And all four of the four are entered as a question and none of them is entered as a finding, and the difference is the whole of her trade and four people in this county have said that they can tell the difference between the two on a page and that they have never once been able to tell the difference between the two in a room.**

The first of the four is about five months old. The second is about four months. The third is about two months. The fourth is about a fortnight. And four people in this county have said since that a woman has been entering the same question four times at intervals over about five months and has not told anybody in this county or in two hundred miles of it, and that a woman who enters a question four times is not doing it for the answers, because there have been no answers, and that about four people in that town have worked out what the four entries are for and none of the four has asked and one of the four is a man of about nineteen who came down four hundred yards of lane to see what a man was doing at a door.

She has not told the second copyist. That is the whole of what happened between the two of them about it and about four people in that town have said since that the second copyist asked her once, about three months back, in that room, whether there was anything in the register she wanted, and that she said no, and that the second copyist is a woman of about thirty who has wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last and has said out loud in a room of two that she is not going to get it and does not want it and that the two are the same thing, and that a woman who does not want a post is the only person in that room who can be trusted with a question.

**And the other thing on that bench is a fair copy and a working, and they are about four inches apart, and about four people in that county have said since that a fair copy is a thing anybody may check line by line and a working is a thing a person has done, and that the two of them have been on that bench for about a year and a half and that the county has never been shown either of them and was never asked to.**

The fair copy is the account of a valley of about four hundred people in a county that is not on any map, four hundred and eight words of it, and the working is a second sheet off a board under a stair, and the working is the only piece of paper in that office that anybody has ever had to be shown and about four people in four years and a half have been shown it and every one of them was shown it in the rain. And about four people in that town have said since that the two of them are about four inches apart on that bench and that neither of them has ever met the other, and that one of them is the only paper in that room anybody could be sued for producing and the other is the only paper in that room nobody could be sued for producing at all, and that a body corporate can be sued and cannot be reached, and that those are not the same and everybody who has worked in the room knows it.

And a page of foolscap with a seventh line on it that says *a person* has been in a coat in this county for about five months and has been looked at four times and finished none of the four times, and a woman of twenty-nine has not asked about it, and about four people in that town have said since that a keeper of records who has not asked a question about a page in a coat is a keeper of records who has decided that the page is not hers, and that she is right about that and has not said so.

---

**And a man of about nineteen came down four hundred yards of lane on that Tuesday to see what a man was doing at a door, and he stood about nine feet off and did not ask, and he has an elder sister of twenty-two in a house two streets off and has not said one word about her to anybody in that lane in about a year, and about four people in that town have said since that a man of about nineteen who does not ask questions at a door is a man who has a sister and is used to keeping quiet in a house.**

---

**And a letter has not been opened, and it is the tenth of a kind, and four people in two counties have said since that the day it is opened is the day this office is a body corporate that goes through a county's post, and that the woman who said it has not said it twice and has not been asked to.**

The tenth is on the shelf over the bench and it has been on that shelf since about the summer before last and it is from a county four hundred miles off and it has a place on it, and a second copyist of about thirty walked past it about four times a day for about two years and did not open it and did not say why, and about four people in that town have said since that a woman who has walked past a letter four times a day for two years is not refusing it and is not not refusing it, and that the not opening is the only arrangement in that room that nobody in it has ever had a name for.

---

**And about two hundred yards off, on that same Tuesday, the reach was open with about forty men on it and the fourth bay was being pointed and the joint on that parapet was a foot and a half short, and nobody measured the joint, and the salt was coming over the first bay on the lane and had been coming over it since the spring, and about four people in that town have said since that a reach with forty men on it is the only thing in this county that anybody can point at and say has been going on, and that this county has never asked its bench for a figure of days and is not going to.**

**And a man of about thirty-one with a satchel was about two hundred miles from a door in that town on that Tuesday and did not know it, and he had about four hundred places on a list behind him, and those four hundred places are the four hundred doors on a man of forty-six's list in a county four hundred miles off, and the two men have never spoken and are not going to, and about four hundred doors is a number that is on a list and in a satchel and in a room two hundred miles from here and in a county four hundred miles from here, and nobody in any of the four places has ever worked out how many, and one of the four places is this one and it does not know that it is one of them.**

---

**And the man of sixty-one was still on the lane at about the sixth hour and then he was not, and about four people in that town have said since that he went down about two hundred miles of road with a satchel and a waxed cloth and a small hammer he has never used, and that he came up it to tell a woman something he could not tell her and that he told her that he could not tell her, and that four people in this county have said since that a man who has been refused four things in two years and comes up a road a fifth time to say that he cannot help is a man who is not asking for a fifth thing, and that nobody in that county has worked out what he is asking for, and that the man of about sixty-one has not been asked and would not answer if he were.**

And a man of thirty-two with a limp was four hundred yards up that road in a yard on Weir Street on that Tuesday and did not come down it, and a man of about nineteen came down it to see what a man was doing at a door and stood about nine feet off and did not ask, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people did not know that a man of sixty-one had stood at a door on a lane for about forty minutes and had not knocked and had said nineteen words to a shut door, and that four entries in a register in a room over a weigh-house are four questions and not four findings, and that the two of those are the same afternoon and about nine hundred feet apart and nobody in either place has been told about the other one.
