# Chapter 0260

**The fifth day of Mudmonth, YR 316, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the fourth hour and a half and the seventh hour, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane from a market in a town of about four thousand people on a river, with about forty men on it and the road open over it and the salt coming over the first bay, and a weigh-house at the north end of that lane with a cart arch into a yard about forty feet from a door, and about four hundred people in this town who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp said nine words to nobody in that arch at about the fourth hour on the fifth of Mudmonth, YR 316, and that nobody answered him, and that the nine words are not written down anywhere and are not going to be.**

He came into this county on the twenty-ninth of Thawmonth, YR 316, a Wednesday, after four days, three of them on a hired horse and the last stage in a hired cart because of the leg. The stick is ash and four feet nine and cut out of a fence rail in a county that is not on any map, and he cannot lean on it because leaning on it puts the weight through the wrong hip, and he has not said that out loud to anybody in this county and has not been asked.

There were about nine men on the reach every working day from the twenty-ninth of Thawmonth to the fifth of Mudmonth and about forty on the fifth, because the mud month is the month the ground is bad and the fourth bay is being pointed into a third week, and the joint on its parapet is a foot and a half short because the man who began it went home at the fourth hour one day and has not come back, and nobody in that yard has ever measured it and a clerk of a bench of nine was asked about it once and said that a clerk cannot measure a thing.

**And about four hundred yards down that lane there is a crossing that carries nobody and has carried nobody for four years, and about four hundred people in this town know that it is there, and about four people in this lane have said since that a crossing that carries nobody is a crossing that carries nobody and is not anything else, and that it carried nobody for four years before anything happened to it, and that those two sentences are the whole of what this county knows about it and that the county is not going to be told any more.**

He did not go up to the crossing. He went down the lane to the weigh-house, because the weigh-house takes the tide up through its boards in a wet week and it had not been a wet week, and because a man who has been in a building for nine days and was not sent for can go somewhere and not go somewhere else and nobody in that lane has ever asked him which of the two he was doing. The office in the two rooms pays fourpence a year for them and the salt merchant charges two shillings a month for each of them, and about four people in that lane have said since that those are two prices for one set of rooms and that nobody has ever put them in a column together and is going to.

The door on that lane was shut. It is shut most days. There is a cart arch into a yard about forty feet from it, and the arch is a wagon arch and it takes a loaded waggon and it is the only way in to a yard that has no other way, and four people in that lane have said since that a wagon arch is not a door because nobody closes it, and that a man standing in a wagon arch at the fourth hour on a Tuesday is standing in the place where a man who was going to knock would stand, and that about four of them have said that and none of them has said it to him.

**He stood in it for about a minute and a half and he said nine words to nobody and he got no answer.**

Nobody in this lane has ever worked out what the nine words were. About four people in that town have worked out that he said something, and about two of them have worked out that he said nine words, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that anything was said in a wagon arch on the fifth of Mudmonth, YR 316. The door on that lane was shut when he passed it at about the fourth hour, as it is most days, and he did not knock and did not go up the lane.

And the four words he has had ready since the sixth day of a count of days he does not have are still ready, and the nine words he said and the four words he has not said are not the same four words, and four people in that lane have said since that a man who has nine words he said to nobody in a wagon arch and four words he has not said at all is a man carrying two things and has been carrying both since the spring, and that nobody on that reach knows he has either of them, and that a man who is asked what the nine words were would not be able to put them into the same order twice.

---

**And a Tuesday in Mudmonth in that town is a day the market does not come, and a market is on a Saturday and on a Thursday and on nothing else, and the lane is busy in a way a lane is not busy on a Thursday, and four people in that town have said since that a month in that county is carried at thirty days and has about eight market days in it and about twenty-two that are not market days, and that the two market days of a week are the two days anybody in the county can be found at, and that a thing that has to be found at a person is a thing that is done on a Saturday or on a Thursday, and that the bell goes on a Tuesday.**

A man of about nineteen came down the lane with an empty hod at about the sixth hour and went through and did not stop at anything, and he has an elder sister of twenty-two in a house two streets off and has not said one word about her to anybody in that lane in about a year, and four people in that town have said since that a man of about nineteen who buys one thing and goes is saving for something and that nobody in that market has asked him what for and is not going to, and that on the fifth of Mudmonth, YR 316 he did not even buy.

A woman of about thirty with a pail came up the lane at about the seventh hour and put the pail down outside a door and knocked once and went on to the next one, and four people in that lane have said since that she does about nine doors on that lane and has done it for about four years and that a woman who does nine doors is the only person in that lane who can say what is shut and what is not, and that on the fifth of Mudmonth, YR 316 one of the nine was a door forty feet from a wagon arch, and that it was open, and that a door that is shut at the fourth hour is not a door that is shut all day, and that nobody in that lane has ever checked, and that she has not said so to anybody and is not going to, and that a man standing in a wagon arch forty feet off had his back to it and did not see her and she did not speak to him.

---

**And the bell went at about the fourth hour and a half, and it is nine stone of green-black bronze with a band of forty-one rivets in it and a dull red seam split lip to shoulder, and it went by a boy and a carpenter, and neither of them is paid by the guild and neither of them is a member of anything.**

The boy has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is not good at it. He takes the weight on the wrong stroke about one time in nine and the carpenter waits for it, and the two of them have not changed the order in two years and three months, and four people on that reach have said since that the people of this county who hear that bell and the people who do not have never been counted apart and are not going to be.

A man of thirty-four with lime in a glass yard four hundred miles off has said that a person who maintains a thing and a person who authorises a thing are two different people and that a county needs both, and that this county has one of the two and has had it since the crossing stopped carrying anybody, and that nobody in this county has ever put the bell and the crossing and the man with the stick in one book, and that this is the second time a man with a stick has stood under that bell and it is the first time he has asked anybody anything about it.

He asked four people.

He asked the mason of fifty and a half, who does the arches and the pointing and does not do the laying.

“**The boy rings it and the carpenter rings it. That is the answer.**”

He asked a man of about twenty-six setting a course.

“**That is the steward business and I do not know it.**”

He asked a carter of thirty, who has a waggon of his own and has said two things out loud in two days to nobody and would not mind a toll and is not going to say so to anybody either.

“**It has always been that pair and I was not here before them.**”

He asked a man of forty-seven who is on the deck of the first bay with a hod, and the man said the same four things he had said four times in a year, and none of the four of them was about the bell.


**And the man of forty-seven came to Weir Street on the seventh day of a fever and on the twentieth day of Hearthmonth and on the sixth of Goatmonth and on the twenty-second of Goatmonth, and has not asked him one question on any of those four days, and the man of forty-seven has now said the same four things to a man with a stick five times in a year and has said them to a yard wall and to a bench-board and to nobody besides, and has been asked about none of them and has said nothing, and four people in that town have said since that the four things are not about the bell and that a man who repeats himself in public without being asked to is a man whose repetition has become a thing other people can use, and that nobody in this county has used it and is going to.**

**And nobody in that county can answer who keeps it, and that is the finding and not a gap, and four people on that reach have said since that a bell rung by two people who are neither paid for it nor members of it is a bell nobody in that county can be asked about, and that is why it is rung, and that about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people have heard it at the fourth hour and a half about four hundred times and have never once asked.**

---

**And the fourth bay was being pointed on that Tuesday and it was not finished, and about nine inches of that pointing went into the week, and the mason of fifty and a half did it and two men of about twenty-six set a course and a boy of fifteen at a bench-board with a lime tub and a rule and a bill copied the empty space under a number as well as the number, and nobody measured the joint and nobody measured the line about nine inches long in the third course under about half an inch of lime that has not moved.**

He stood at the bench-board from about the fourth hour and a half to the fifth hour with the coat over his arm, and the boy of fifteen was still at it, and there was a number on the stone and a rule under the number and a space under the rule, and the space was the widest of the four, and the boy of fifteen had copied all three of them and had not asked what any of the three was for. A man of thirty-two with a limp read a number, a rule and a space that a boy of fifteen had copied, and put nothing at all into the widest of the four, and wrote nothing at all, and the boy did not see him do it and has not been told.

He took the leaf out. It is a separate leaf in a coat over his arm and it has a seventh line on it that says a person, and it has said a person since a lane in a city four hundred miles off, and he has looked at that line five times in five months and did not finish it, and on the fifth of Mudmonth, YR 316, at about the fifth hour he looked at it for the sixth time and did not finish it, and put it back flat with the first leaf turned to the first page, and the right order is a thing a person can only get right by not having opened anything.

**And forty-one days counting both ends have gone by since a Thursday in the last week of a year, and they are forty-one days in which a man with a stick was two hundred miles off on four of them and a clerk in a building put two files in one pigeonhole and a man of forty-four read a schedule out loud and a woman of thirty-eight said nineteen sentences and a woman of thirty filled in a column she is afraid of, and four people in this county have said since that a man who has been in the county five months and has been called something in four counties has asked four people on a reach about a bell and has not written a figure in a space that is the widest of the four, and that neither of those two things is a decision, and that the two of them are the same not-decision, and that a man who decides not to write a thing down has made a plan, and that he has not made one and is not going to.**

And the salt was coming over the first bay and down the lane for the ninth week running, and it is the salt merchant's salt and it is in his waggon and it is not the guild's, and the two of those are a price of two different things and they have never been in one column in this county, and the tide was under the wharf at about the fifth hour, and about nine waggons had come up the channel road since the spring, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that a bell with forty-one rivets in it went at the fourth hour and a half on the fifth of Mudmonth, YR 316 by a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is not good at it, and by a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper, and that nobody in that county can be asked who keeps it, and that it went on the Wednesday, and that it goes on.
