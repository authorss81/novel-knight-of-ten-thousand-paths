# Chapter 0298

**The eighteenth day of Fallowmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the fourth hour and the sixth hour and the eighth hour, and a made road about four hundred yards long in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, with a standing finish on the end of it, and a yard with about two hundred feet of wall on one side of it and a mill at the low end of the water below the yard, and about four hundred people in that valley who do not know that a woman went down that road in the morning and came back up it in the afternoon.**

She got up before the mill did. That is the whole of what anybody in that valley could have said about the morning if anybody had been up, and nobody was, and the reason nobody knows she went is that a valley of about four hundred people wakes up at about the fifth hour and she was back before it.

The road is about four hundred yards long and it was made by hand and it has a standing finish on the end of it, and the standing finish is the only straight thing in that valley, and the far end of that road is the far end of that road. There is nothing at it. There is about nine feet of grass and the two stones that hold the finish and the weather, and there has never been a letter on a post at that end and there is no post.

**She stood at it for about as long as it takes to look at nine feet of grass twice, and she did not get down off the horse, and then she turned it round and came back up four hundred yards of her own making, and she came back with nothing.**

---

The yard has about two hundred feet of wall on one side of it and a gate that does not shut and a boy of about nine in it with a barrow and about nine pounds of stone in the barrow, and the stone was going down to the mill and the man at the wheel would not have looked up for it.

She came up the yard at about the sixth hour and put the horse in and came out of the saddle and stood, and the boy went on with the barrow and did not stop, and nobody in that yard asked her where she had been.

She said it to the boy and not to the yard, and she said it in the plain way, and it was the only time in her life she has said it and she is not going to say it again, and she said it to the only person in that yard who was not going to answer it.

“**I have wanted two years to be able to put a day against one thing in a book nobody will show me, and there is a man who was going to come and he has not, and I have found out that the wanting was the whole of it and there is nothing on this side of it, and I am not going to say that to anybody except you and a man with a barrow.**”

The boy went on down the yard with the barrow and did not put the stone down and did not look at her, and nobody has ever asked him what he made of it and nobody is going to. A boy of about nine with nine pounds of stone in a barrow heard the whole of a woman of thirty-six at about the sixth hour of an ordinary afternoon and carried on to the mill, and that is the correct response to it and there is no other one in that valley.

---

Nobody in that yard thanked her. Nobody told her it would be all right. Nobody gave her a reason, and a reason was available in that valley in about four different kitchens that afternoon and none of it was given to her, and about four people in that valley have said since that a woman came back up a road she had made and told one person nothing was on it and was not asked one question about it and has not asked one since.

The man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work came in at about the seventh hour and said her name, which is the first time it has been said in that yard.
“**Bessa,**” he said, and then he said, “**Bessa Quill, you have been down the road.**”

“**I have,**” Bessa Quill said, and the woman of thirty-six who keeps the bridge over the river with about two hundred and forty feet of stone on it did not say one word about why. A man said a woman's whole name in a yard and asked her one question and got one word, and the one word was true, and the question was not the one anybody wanted to ask and was not asked by anybody else in about four hundred people.

And nothing was entered anywhere, and the day-book of an office forty feet from a wagon arch in a county two hundred miles off did not get a tenth entry on that day and does not get one on any day, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.

---

The ride is four hundred yards and it takes a woman on a horse about a minute and a quarter, and the road has a crown on it that a man of thirty-four cut in a wet summer with sixteen men and no work, and the crown holds the rain on one side and sends it down the other into a ditch that was dug for it, and a road that was dug by the people who use it is the only road in that valley and there is no law about it and no office and nobody in that valley has ever called it anything but a road.

She went down it at about the fourth hour and she was at the standing finish at about the fourth hour and a quarter and she stood there, and the weather was the weather of the fourth month of that autumn and it was about four degrees off cold and there was nothing to hear on that road but the water in the ditch, and about two hundred feet below the finish the river goes under a bridge with about two hundred and forty feet of stone on it and the stone is about two feet thick at the fourth span and the woman who keeps that bridge was the only person in that valley who could have said what is under the fourth span and she did not say it to anybody and she was not asked.

And the man at the mill came out into the cold at about the fourth hour and a half for about four minutes and looked up the hill at a woman on a horse four hundred yards off and went back in. He has never once been asked about the second cold and nobody is going to ask him, and he saw a woman go down a road and come back up it and said nothing about it to anybody, and there is a man at the low end of the water in every one of the four hundred years of that book.

And a man of eighty-one was outside the door of that room over the cross at about the seventh hour and said one ordinary thing to a woman of about thirty of the ninth generation who has carried a slate for eleven years, and the ordinary thing was about the weather, and nobody in that valley has ever asked him a second question about the second cold and nobody is going to.

---

The man of thirty-four was still in the yard at about the seventh hour and she said one more thing to him, and it was not a thank you and it was not a reason and it was seven words.

“**Nobody is to be told I went.**”

“**I am not going to tell anybody,**” the man of thirty-four said.

“**That is not the same thing,**” Bessa Quill said, and the two of them have never raised it since and are not going to, and a woman who keeps a bridge got a man in a yard to promise her a thing in nine words and then told him that a promise and an entry are not the same thing, and a valley of about four hundred people has an instrument for the second of those and not for the first, and the instrument is a book nobody will show her.

And the man at the mill came out of the mill door at about the seventh hour and a quarter and shut it behind him and stood in the cold for about two minutes and said nothing to anybody, and went back in, and about four hundred people in that valley do not know that three people said three short things in a yard that afternoon and that about two hundred of the ninth generation were inside the mill and heard none of them.

---

At about the eighth hour the man of fifty-eight was in the room over the cross with the book under the trestle and the trestle against the wall, and there is one finding in that book in a hundred and forty years and it is at the head of the first spread under the four lines on a line he ruled himself, and he did not enter anything that evening and nobody asked him about the one that is in there, and a bridgekeeper of thirty-six was at the bridge at about the ninth hour and did not come up to the room and was not asked to.

The woman who gave up standing in that aisle in writing was not asked anything. The man of fifty-five was not asked anything. The man of eighty-one was not asked anything. Nine people were on twenty-nine benches and about two hundred of the ninth generation were at the back and the aisle four feet wide went the length of the room and had a bench along it with a back to it, and nothing was stood in it and nothing has been stood in it for three generations, and a hundred and forty is cut into the end of the bench at the back of the right-hand row and nobody in that valley has ever worked out that a hundred and forty in a bench and a hundred and forty years in a book are two numbers about the same room and has not been asked.

The mill went on at the low end of the water and a man who cannot leave his wheel was in it, and about two hundred people left that valley in the year after the ninth cold and the first three families out were the three that said no, and a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work has sixteen men in that valley and about four of the sixteen have nowhere to work this season and one of the sixteen is nine years old.

And about four hundred people in that valley went to bed that night not knowing that the road had been gone down and come back from, and the road is still there and the standing finish is still on the end of it, and about two hundred and forty feet of stone is still on a bridge over the water with a woman who keeps it, and there is no column anywhere in that valley for a road that was gone down and come back from, and there never has been, and nobody in that valley is going to look for anybody on it again.
