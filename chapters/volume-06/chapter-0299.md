# Chapter 0299

**The eighteenth day of Embermonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the fifth hour, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane from a market four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people on a river, with about forty men on it and the road open over it, and a toll house a hundred yards from the end of that market, and a yard on a street four hundred yards up that county's own road with nine men in it, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that the salt has been over the first bay of one crossing since the spring and over neither of the others.**

The tide brings the salt over the first bay at about the second hour and takes it off again about the seventh. It is the salt merchant's salt and it is in his own waggon and the guild has none of it and no claim on it, and two prices of two different things have never once stood in one column in this county.

The fourth bay was finished in the summer and pointed into a third day, and there is nothing left on it that is on a bill, and a man of fifty and a half who does the arches and does the pointing and does not do the laying has been on the second bay for about a fortnight putting a finish on a finish, and nobody in that yard has asked him why, and a reach does not stop for a market and a reach does not stop for a quiet season either.

In the third course of the fourth bay, under about half an inch of lime, there is a line about nine inches long. It is in the stone and not across a joint. It has not moved. Nobody has measured it, nobody has ruled across it, nobody has laid a rule on it, and no person in that county has put a finger on it or is going to, and about forty men worked about nine feet off it every working day for a fortnight and none of them has ever mentioned it to another of them, and there is no column in this county for a thing in a stone.

---

**At the fourth hour and a half the bell goes.**

It is nine stone of green-black bronze with a band of forty-one rivets in it and a dull red seam in the metal where the band was closed, split lip to shoulder, and it is chest-high, and it is rung by a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is still not good at it, and by a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper and has a bar he is not using.

The arrangement has been a boy and a carpenter since the ninth of a month in the year before. The boy takes one end of a line and the carpenter takes the other and they pull it together on the count of four, and the count of four is the arrangement and not a rule and it is not written down anywhere, and the boy is still not good at it and the carpenter has never once said so to him and is not going to.

**Neither of the two of them is paid by the guild and neither of the two of them is a member of anything.** About four hundred people in this town have heard that bell about four hundred times this year and have never once asked who keeps it, and there is no answer in this county and the answer nobody in this county can give is the finding, and a bell rung by two people who are neither paid for it nor members of it is a bell nobody in this county can be asked about, and that is why it is rung, and nobody in this county has ever said any of that out loud.

It goes on the fourth hour and a half on a Saturday in a market month and it went on the fourth hour and a half on the Sunday before that and it goes on, and about four hundred people in that town did not hear it this morning and would not have said so either way.

---

There is a yard on a street four hundred yards up that road with about nine men in it, a bench-board standing four feet off the ground and about nine feet along it, and a press in a room at the end that is the yard's own and cannot be served with anything by anybody. A bundle has been lying in that press for years with nothing written on the outside of it. It is not in the yard's book.

The man of twenty-five who owns that yard said one thing at about the third hour and it was not about the bundle.

“**We are not short this month,**” he said, to a man of about nineteen with an empty hod at the end of the bench-board.

“**I have not said we are,**” the man of about nineteen said.

“**You have been standing at that end since the second hour,**” the man of twenty-five said, and went back to the end of the board, and an owner of a yard with nine men in it and no work told a man with an empty hod what he had been doing for an hour, and the man with the empty hod did not deny it, and nobody in that yard has asked either of them about it and nobody is going to.

At the end of that yard a man of about sixty was packing lime against a heap with a lid on it, with his back to everybody. On the bench-board a boy of fifteen copies the rule under a number as well as the number, and that rule is the widest of the four, and he has copied the space under it eleven times and has never once been told what belongs there, and nothing belongs there, and that is what the space is for.

The man of about nineteen with the empty hod came down off the board and put the hod against the wall and came back up.

“**There is nothing on the bill,**” he said.

“**I know there is nothing on the bill,**” the boy of fifteen said.

“**Then why are you copying the space.**”

“**It is under the number,**” the boy said, and the man of about nineteen looked at the space for about as long as it takes to look at nine inches of nothing, and went back up to the board without answering that either.

And the man of about sixty with the lid on the heap said one thing over his shoulder without turning round, and it was the only thing he said in that yard that day, and it was to nobody.

“**Somebody has to know where the lime is.**”

And three men said three ordinary sentences about a hod, a space and a heap of lime in about an hour, and none of them said anything about a press in a room at the end of the yard with a bundle in it that has been there for years, and there is a boy of nineteen in that yard who has eleven weeks left to serve before he asks about being a journeyman and has not asked.

---

A man of fifty-five was in a hut at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards down that road, and about nine times that morning he turned the bars down and said four words to whoever was going through and nothing else, and he has been in that hut nineteen years and nobody in this county has ever asked him where the four words come from, and he has never said and is not going to.

A carter of thirty came through the bars with a barrow and a waggon of his own and the four words went out to him and he went on without stopping, because that is what a barrier is for, and the man of fifty-five turned the bars back down and said the same four words to the next one, and a man who has said the same four words to about four thousand people in nineteen years has never been asked one question about them by anybody in this county and is not going to be.

---

A hundred yards from the end of that market there is a crossing on that county's own road and it carries nobody and it carried nobody for four years before anything ever happened to it, and it carries nobody now, and it is not a crossing that has gone wrong and it is not one anybody in that county has called anything, and about four hundred people in that town have driven past it in about four years and none of them has ever thought about it and none of them is going to.

There is no owner and no money and no glass in the sill and no third seat, and the door at the low end has been open since the spring, and there is a bench inside with its back to the door and a man of thirty-two with a limp was on it at about the fifth hour with his stick across his knees and had not lit anything because there is nothing in that building to light.

The sill is a foot and a half wide. There is nothing in it and there is nothing under it. Four men and a boy put forty yards of tarred hemp into the abutment at the near end of that crossing on the fourth day of a month in the year before, four feet down, for a job that is not this one, and **nobody has dug for it and nobody is going to dig for it**. There is no writ in this county and no seal and no office, and nothing in this county could take a statement about it if somebody wanted to make one, and a hemp four feet down under a dressed block in a county of about four thousand people is a hemp that a man with a stick is the only man in the county who could ever have got at, and he has a permanent limp in the right leg.

The dressed block is squared and about nine inches by six by four and it is in the abutment with the mortar round it, and there is a line in the face of it about nine inches long, in the stone and not across a joint and not in a joint, under no lime of its own. It has not moved. Nobody has measured it and nobody is going to, and about four people in that lane have said since that a lane is a lane and a river is a river and a market is a market, and that not one person in that county has ever put a thing seen at one of those places into the same sentence as a thing seen at another of them, and that nobody in that county is going to.

---

A woman of about thirty with a pail went up that lane at about the fifth hour and stopped at nine doors and said one thing at each of them, and at the door of that toll house she said that the bell had gone at the fourth hour and a half as it does, and she said it to the door, and there are eight doors behind her in that lane and nobody in that lane has ever asked her why she says things at doors.

The man of thirty-two with a limp did not get up, because getting up is about four seconds longer than sitting down, and he said one thing to that door and it was the only thing he said in that building that day.

“**It is on the same count,**” he said.

And about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has been in that county for about a year and has never once been asked anything about that bell said it was on the same count as every other morning, and that the boy and the carpenter who ring it would not have said so and would not have been asked to, and that nobody in that county has ever worked out that a thing which cannot be asked about is a thing everybody in the county can answer for.

**And he was not asked. Nobody in that lane has ever asked him about a bell and nobody in that county is going to, and about four hundred people in that town have heard that bell about four hundred times this year and not one of them has ever put the two facts together, and the two facts are a bell and a man and they are a hundred yards apart.**

---

Nobody walked any part of that Saturday with anybody. The tide was under the wharf, and about four hundred people went home from a market with a bell in the air behind them and about nine men went home from a yard with a bench-board and a press in a room at the end of it, and a man of forty-seven was not in that market and has not been in it this month.
