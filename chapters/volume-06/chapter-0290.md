# Chapter 0290

**The eighth day of Harvestmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the third hour and the fifth hour, and a market four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people on a river with about four hundred people in it, and a lane at the north end of that market with a wagon arch in it and two rooms over a weigh-house, and a yard on a street four hundred yards from a wharf, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that a man in a plain coat came up the county's own road that morning with a sheet in a bag and that the sheet says that a thing they were frightened of has stopped being written down.**

A courier is not a carrier. A carrier has a round and a firm and a rate that is not printed in the county he is standing in, and a courier has a bag and a place to put it and no round at all, and the two of them have been in this county four times between them in about nine years and have never met and are not going to.

**He came up the county's own road at about the second hour on a Saturday with about four hundred people in the market and did not stop in the market, and he had a bag with a number of an office on it and nothing at all against a person, and about four hundred people in that town saw a man walk past them and did not find out what he was.**

The office in that number is in a provincial town two hundred miles off. There is no writ in this county and no seal and no office and a body corporate with a door on a lane can be served with anything at all, and a courier is the one kind of visitor that does not need a county to have anything, because he comes to a door and not to a court.

---

He went up the lane to the wagon arch and then to the door on the lane, and he put the sheet on the table in that room and said that it was for the keeper and had been for seven days, and the keeper of records is twenty-nine and has run that office four years and a half out of a deed of thirteen words and she took it off his hand and did not read it standing up.

The courier went out again and was not asked anything and about four hundred people in that town did not know he had come.

The sheet has a number of an office on the head of it and the seal of that office and the word *copy* in the corner, and it is a copy of a minute of a court and it is one sheet and it is the whole of what was sent.

---

**She read it once, and it took her about two minutes, and the second copyist of about thirty was in that room and had been in that room for three days, having come back down a made road in a cart on the fourth of a month with a bag of her own, and neither of them has said one word to anybody about the three weeks and a day the other one of them was not in that room.**

The sheet says four things and only four things, and they are in the ordinary way and they are on a printed series and there is a head on each of them.

A temporary injunction has been accepted against forced culling. It holds to a day and after that day it holds if a local council renews it in public. The classifications already entered are not taken back by it. **And the office that made them has gone out of the ordinary way and will not be circulating the schedule and has said so in one line at the foot of a sheet of its own printed series, and there is no name against that line and there is no office to ask.**

**And that is the whole of what came to a county of about four thousand people on the eighth of Harvestmonth, YR 316, and the county read it in a room over a weigh-house at about the third hour on a market Saturday and about four hundred people in that town do not know that it came, and the county did not know that a thing it was frightened of had a schedule at all until forty days before the courier came, and it has not got over that and is not going to.**

---

The reeve came up the two flights at about the fourth hour and stood in that room and did not sit down.

“**Where did that come from,**” she said.

“**A man with a bag,**” the keeper of records said. “**He said it was for me and had been for seven days and he did not give a name and I did not ask him for one.**”

“**You did not ask him for one.**”

“**No.**”

**“Is that the thing with the bell in it,”** she said.

**“It is a minute of a court,**” the keeper of records said, **“and it does not name a bell and it does not name a crossing and it does not name this county at all.”**

**“Then it is about something else.**”

**“It is about a place in a column,**” the keeper of records said, “**and a column is not a thing anybody in this county can be shown, because there is no column in this county for it and the only two kinds of paper in this building are a claim and a day-book.**”

And the reeve did not improve on that and went down, and a woman who gets to a true thing in a county of about four thousand people by saying the wrong one first has been doing it for as long as she has held the office and says it is the only thing in the office she is good at, and she did it four hours after a courier came up a lane, and nobody in that lane was in the room and nobody has worked out since that the two things are in the same building.

---

And the market outside went on from the second hour to about the seventh, and a salt waggon came up the channel road and stood at the end of it about an hour, and a woman of about thirty with a pail went up that lane at about the fifth hour and stopped at nine doors and said one thing at each of them, and at the door forty feet from a wagon arch she said that a man in a plain coat had come up the lane that morning and had not stopped in the market, and she said it to the door, and there are eight doors behind her in that lane and nobody has ever asked her why she says things at doors.

**And about four hundred people in that town stood in a street four hundred yards long and did not know that a sheet had gone up a lane and into a room over a weigh-house and that the sheet said a thing they had been afraid of had stopped, and that is the whole of what the eighth day of Harvestmonth, YR 316 did in a county of about four thousand people on a river.**

---

**And nobody put a question to anybody in that room or out of it that day, and the day-book of that office stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and there is no tenth coming on that Saturday or on any Saturday after it, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.**

The magistrate was not in that room and has not been told and is not going to be until the bench sits on a Saturday at the seventh hour, and a bench of nine has been asked eight things in nineteen years and the count is on a page under a stair four hundred yards from that lane and it is eight, and a thing that is not asked is not a refusal and is not a yes, and a courier with a bag is not a thing anybody has ever put to a bench of nine and is not going to be.

And the magistrate has a construction ready and has had it for about a year and did not use it on that Saturday and is not going to use it on a courier, and nobody in that county has worked out what the construction is for and nobody in that county is going to.

---

**And the office pays fourpence a year. It has paid fourpence a year for four years and a half and that is the whole of what it has ever cost that county to be a thing with a door and a number on it, and there is no other figure in that building and nobody in that building has ever added one to it.**

A courier is not entered anywhere either, and a man who comes up a lane with a bag and puts a sheet on a table and goes out again is not in the day-book and is not in the register and is not on the fair copy nineteen years old on that wall, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that a person came and that a person leaving is the only kind of visit a county can be sure of.

A scale under that room has not been used since the fourth of a month, and there is a press in that room with two unserved books in it and a day-book and a half tile that weighs a shade over ten ounces, and a fair copy on the wall nineteen years old in a keeper's own hand that says no combined figure is entered in that office, and nobody has read it out loud in that room including the keeper of records.

**The second copyist put the courier's sheet into the press in that room at about the fourth hour, because that is what the press in that room is for and because that press can be served with anything at all by anybody who walks up that lane, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who puts a minute of a court into a press that can be served with it has done the ordinary thing and has also made a decision, and that the two are the same thing and only one of them is written down.**

---

And a man of thirty-two with a limp was in that room at about the fifth hour and asked for the sheet and the keeper of records gave it to him and did not ask him what he was going to do with it, and the not-asking is not a courtesy and is not a rule and is the ordinary habit of a room, and a room hears a thing once.

He had been in that room about a quarter of an hour and had not asked one question in it and there was nothing in it he had come for.

**He put the sheet into his coat and went down the lane and up a street to a yard and put it into a press in a room at the end of that yard, and nobody asked him to and nobody in that room knows what he is looking at.**

---

The yard has nine men in it and a bench-board four feet off the ground and about nine feet long, and the press is in a room at the end of it that belongs to the yard and cannot be served with anything, and a bundle has been in that press for years with nothing on the wrapper and it is not on the yard's book and is not going to be.

**“It goes in on the shelf and not on the book,**” the man of twenty-five who owns that yard said, **“and I have never asked you to take anything out of it and I am not asking you now.**”

“**What are you doing with that,**” a man of about nineteen with an empty hod said, from the end of the bench-board, and he had not been in that room before that afternoon and nobody had told him a man was in it.

“**Nothing,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said.

And the man of about nineteen went back to the end of the bench-board and did not say anything else about it, and has not said anything about it since and is not going to, and about four people in that yard have said since that a man of about nineteen asked a stranger a question on a Saturday and got one word for it and was satisfied, and that about four hundred people in that town have asked about nine hundred things that week and got nothing at all for any of them.

And nobody in that room asked him what the sheet was or where it had been or what he had read on it, and a man of about nineteen with an empty hod went past the end of the bench-board and did not stop, and a boy of fifteen at that board copied the empty space under a number as well as the number and has copied it eleven times and has never been told what goes in it, and a man of about sixty was at the end of that yard packing lime with a lid on a heap and had his back to all of them.

---

Nobody walked any part of that Saturday with anybody. He went down that lane on his own and up that street on his own, and a yard is nine men and a bench-board and a press in a room at the end of it, and about four of the nine noticed a man go through that yard and about four did not, and the tide was under the wharf, and the salt was over the first bay, and a bell a hundred yards up that county's own road goes at the fourth hour and a half whether anybody is on the crossing or not, and a crossing four hundred yards up that road carries nobody and has carried nobody for four years, and a county of about four thousand people read a minute of a court on a market morning and does not know whether to be glad, and a sheet that says so is in a press in a room in a yard that nobody can be served with, and about four hundred people in that town went home that day knowing none of it.
