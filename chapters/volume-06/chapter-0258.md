# Chapter 0258

**The eleventh day of Thawmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the third hour and the fourth hour, and a room with a bench in it and no window in that room in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that the man who buys a loaf in their market on a Saturday morning was in a room with no window in it at about the third hour of that Saturday with a sheet in front of him and two prices of the same thing on it, and that he was offered the smaller one in exchange for a thing he had already said he would do.**

He had been in that building nine days and nobody had sent for him, and four people in that building have said since that a man who is not sent for is a man who is not on anything, and that a man who is on nothing in a building where things are convened is a man who is in the way in the only sense the word has, and that nobody in that building has used the word at him and that about four of them have thought it.

The room with the bench in it is on the ground floor at the end of the passage with the stove and it has no window and there is a candle in a tin holder screwed to the wall and the holder is not the only reason it is the room people are put in, and four people in that building have said since that a room with no window is a room a person cannot be in for long, and that a thing that cannot be gone on with for long is a thing done quickly, and that both of those are the trade and neither of them is the law.

**And a man of about forty came in at about the third hour and put a sheet on the bench and did not sit down, and he is not a clerk of the Crown and he is not the woman of thirty-eight and he is not the man of forty-four, and four people in that building have said since that there are five people in that building who can put a sheet on a bench in front of a person and that four of the five have said what the sheet is and one of the five has not.**

The sheet is the ordinary one. It is a sheet of the office's printed series, and it is folded once across the middle, and on the back of it is the form of oath, and the front of it has three things on it: the matter, the person, and what the person may be sentenced to.

**“The matter is entered in the provincial court out of a deed of affiliation and it names you,” the man said. “It has been standing for a year and a bit. It is not a serious matter. It is a matter about a body that answers to nobody and about a road that four people say you made and forty people say you did not. The sheet says what a person in that case may be sentenced to. It is on the back. I am not going to read it to you twice.**”

“I am not asking you to read it twice.”

“**Nobody ever asks me to read it twice. They ask me to read it once and slowly and then they ask me what it means and that is the same thing with more of your face in it.**”

He said one thing in that room that day and it was in his own voice and it was nineteen words and it was the only thing about the offer that he said, and he has not said it since to anybody, and four people in that building have said since that a man of about forty has one sentence about an offer and says it the same way in every county in this province and that nobody has ever found out whether it is true in any of them.

“**A person who gives evidence in the proceeding is not sentenced for the matter the evidence is about. That is the whole of it.**”

---

**And the man of about forty said the other thing at about the third hour and a half, after the sheet was on the bench and before anybody had sat down, and it was his second thing that day and it was twenty-four words, and it is the only thing he has said in that building in about nine years that is about his own work, and he said it to the bench and not to the man.**

“**If you do not take it the matter stands, and a matter that stands is a thing I carry, and I am carrying four.**”

Nobody answered him, and four people in that building have said since that a man who offers a person the smaller of two prices of the same thing and is refused has said the only sentence in the trade that admits what the offer is for, and that what the offer is for is that the sheet should not have to be read twice, and that the person in front of him heard that and has not said whether he heard it, and that a clerk's four words in a room in a county two hundred miles off is the same shape of sentence and nine hundred miles from here in a valley on a road that is not made, and the two of them have never met.

And the man of about forty has carried four matters in about nine years and there is no line on the sheet for the ones a man does not take, and four people in that building have said since that the only record of four matters that are standing is a man of forty who is carrying them, and that a county of about four thousand people cannot be served with a man either, and that both of those things have been true for nine years and neither of them is anybody's fault.

---

**And the sheet has two prices of the same thing on it and nobody in that room added them, and a man of thirty-two with a limp looked at the two for about a minute and a half and did not add them and did not ask why they were different, and four people in that building have said since that this is the second time in about a year that two figures out of one place have been looked at in that province and not added, and that the first time was said out loud in nine words and this time was not said out loud at all.**

The sheet gives what a person in that case may be sentenced to, and it gives it twice, and the two are a matter of course in the trade and not a trick: the first is what it is and the second is what it would be if the person gave evidence. The fine is a mark and a half the first time and one mark the second time, and a mark is four shillings and a shilling is twelve pence, and those two figures are on the sheet in words and not in figures, and nobody in that room wrote them down and nobody added one to the other and nobody asked what the difference of them was, and those are two prices of one thing and there is no column in that building for two prices of one thing.

“**I will give evidence.**”

The man of about forty did not write it down at once. He looked at the sheet for about two seconds and a half and then said one thing, and it was ten words, and it is the ordinary way a thing is said in that trade and nobody in that room improved on it.

“**That is not the offer. The offer is the reduction.**”

“**I have said what I will do and I have said it before you asked me and I am not going to say it again. You may write down that I said it and you may write down that I said it first. You may not put the reduction on me and call it a thing I asked for.**”

---

**And two hundred miles off there was a market on that Saturday morning four hundred yards long with salt and fish and peat in it and one load of glass, and a man of about nineteen came down the lane with an empty hod and bought a piece of bread and did not stop at anything else, and a woman of about thirty with a pail was at the corner of it and was at the same corner on the last market Saturday of a year, and four people in that town have said since that the two of them have not spoken in about a year and that neither of them has said one word about the other to anybody, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp was not in that market and has not been in it on a Saturday since the spring and that nobody in that market has asked him where he was.**

---

**And what he wrote down took about a minute and a half and it is the fourth of the entries in a book in that building and it is the ordinary entry, and four people in that building have said since that a man who declines a reduction and offers his evidence anyway is entered as a witness who declined, and that the entry does not say why he declined and that the form has no line for it.**

*Witness attended. Offered reduction of sentence. Declined. Evidence offered before the asking.*

That is the entry, and it went into a book in the ordinary way, and four people in that building have said since that the fourth of the four lines is not on any form in that office and that the man of about forty put it there because it is what happened, and that a clerk who puts what happened into a book that has no line for what happened has done the one thing in that building that cannot be got out of a room, and that about nine hundred people in that building do not know that it happened on the eleventh of Thawmonth, YR 316 at about the fourth hour in a room with no window in it.

**And a sheet of the office's printed series is a penny, and a second sheet for the person the matter is entered against is a penny, and a clerk's fee for entering a witness is fourpence, and a witness's day is eightpence. A penny and a penny is twopence, and twopence and fourpence is sixpence, and sixpence and eightpence is fourteen pence, and fourteen pence is a shilling and twopence, and that is the whole of what a man of thirty-two with a limp cost that building on the eleventh of Thawmonth, YR 316, and the two prices of the same fine are not in it and are not added to it and are not going to be.**

---

**And he was not on the witness list and he has never asked to be on it, and four people in that building have said since that a man who turns up in a county with a summons posted to a door and then sits in a passage for eleven hours and then says *I will give evidence* to a man in a room with no window in it is a man who has offered a room the thing it is convened for and has not been given the room, and that nobody in that building has said that to him and about four of them have said it to each other on a stair.**

And the woman of thirty-eight is two floors up and was not in that room and has not been told that he was in the building on that Saturday and four people in that building have said since that a Crown officer who has asked in nineteen sentences for a record to show what was asked would want to know that the only person in the building who has offered evidence was in a room with no window in it and not in the chamber, and that she is not told, and that nobody has said she would want to know.

**And on the eleventh of Thawmonth, YR 316, at about the fifth hour, a man of thirty-two with a limp came out of a room with no window in it into a passage with a stove at the end of it and stood for about a minute and a half, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that he was offered the smaller of two prices of the same thing by a man who was not surprised, and that he took the larger one, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp has four words ready that he has had since the sixth day of a count of days he does not have and did not say them in that passage, and that nobody in that province knew he had them and that about four hundred people in this county do not know that he has them either.**
