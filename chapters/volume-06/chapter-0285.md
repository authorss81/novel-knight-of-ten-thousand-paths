# Chapter 0285

**The tenth day of Rainmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the first hour and the second hour and the fourth hour, and a room with a stove in it on the first floor of a building in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and four people in that room, and a tray that went up two flights on the Friday before and came down again that morning, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a woman of thirty-three who is an aid to the register and is not a member of anything was handed a copy of a piece of paper at about the second hour on that Saturday and found her own name in a column with a head on it, and that she had never entered it.**

The copy was made on the ground floor at about the third hour on the Friday before, by a man of about fifty who has been a clerk of a press for thirty-one years, and copying one filled form off another is the ordinary work of his trade and is not a thing anybody asks him about.

A copy is made because a thing produced in a room has to be produced twice or it cannot be produced at all, and what a man does at a stone is to put the filled sheet face down on a clean one, rule the six columns again over the top of it, and go along the lines with the same hand. It takes about nine minutes. It is not a tracing and it is not a print and it is not a copy of a copy, and it is the same nine shapes at a different weight, and about four people in that building have said since that a man who can do that in about nine minutes has done it more than once.

---

The room with the stove in it is on the first floor and it has one window and the window is nine feet off a wall, and about four hundred forms a month are read in that room at waist height under light that is on the floor, and the stove is not lit in that room in that season and was not lit that morning.

**The woman of thirty-three is an aid to the register and is not a member of anything and has never been, and she has filled the fourth column of that office's own printed series about two hundred times in a season for eleven years, and the fourth column is headed *Keeper*, and she has been afraid of it since she was twenty-two and has said so out loud in a room once and about four people in that building heard it and two of them heard it twice and nobody has done anything about it and nobody is going to.**

The woman of about twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns has been in that room six years and has wanted the sixth column ruled for all of them.

Perry Hask, 34, is a second clerk of the returns, and on the Thursday before he had put a tray on that table and had been told that the thing on it was a sheet, and had asked for a column in the press room at the end of the passage and had been given nothing.

The man of about sixty with a tray has carried a tray in and out of that building for twenty-two years and put it down where it had been before and went out again without saying anything.

---

She read the first column and the second and she did not read the third.

**The fourth column is headed *Keeper* and there is a name in it, and the name is hers, and the hand is a hand she has been looking at in about four hundred forms a month for eleven years, and the nine shapes in it are the nine shapes she puts in that column when she fills it from a roll in about a minute and a quarter without looking up, and a particular stroke is left short at the top in it the way she leaves it short at the top.**

And it is the wrong weight.

That is the whole of what she had at about the second hour of that Saturday and it took about two seconds and there is no column in that building for it. **A copy of a hand is the right shapes at the wrong weight. She has been writing that column for eleven years and she has never once seen her own nine shapes come off a nib at somebody else's speed, and a woman who can tell the weight of her own hand is a woman who has been thinking about her own hand for a very long time and has not been asked.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

**And she said the thing she has said since she was twenty-two, and it is the same sentence and it has not got one word more in it than it had in that month, and nobody in that room improved on it and nobody has improved on it anywhere since.**

“**Nobody enters a family by a name nobody entered.**”

“**I have that on a wall,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, “**in a fair copy, in a keeper's own hand, nineteen years old, and nobody has read it out loud in this room including me.**”

“**Then you know how long it has been there,**” the woman of thirty-three said.

And a woman who has said a thing once and been told that it is on a wall in a fair copy has learned two things in about four seconds, and neither of them was that she had been right, and nobody in that room said anything about the second one.

---

And then she asked a question, and it is a question, and it is the first question anybody in that building has asked about that column in eleven years, and a man of about thirty-one with a satchel said in a yard and in a room and in a market that four places are ruled for a figure and no place for a name, and nobody has ever asked him what the fifth is for either.

“**I would like to know what that column is for, and I have wanted to know for eleven years, and nobody in this building has ever said it out loud.**”

Perry Hask put his pen down and did not look up, and a man who puts a pen down when a question is asked has not decided to answer it and has decided not to answer it in a hurry, and those are two different things and only one of them is in anybody's job.

**Nobody answered it.** The woman of about twenty-nine turned a sheet over and did not say that she had not understood the question.

“**It is a column,**” she said, after about half a minute. “**I can tell you that it is a column, and that is not what you asked me, and I have known that since you finished asking.**”

“**That is all right,**” the woman of thirty-three said.

“**I am not saying it is not all right,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, and went back to her sheet, and about four people in that building have said since that a clerk who says I am not saying it is not all right in a room where a woman has just asked a question has answered a different question and knows it and has answered it anyway, and that the different question is the only one in that room that anybody can answer.

The man of about sixty with a tray came back in with an empty tray and said that there was tea in that room if anybody wanted it and went out again, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building and about one of the six columns on them has a head on it and about one of the six does not and nothing has ever come back into either.

**And then she put the copy down on the table and squared it with two fingers and did not take it and did not ask anybody to take it, and it lay there for about nine minutes, and at the end of the nine minutes the woman of about twenty-nine put it in a tray with about nine other sheets, and neither of them said one word about that, and a sheet that is put in a tray by a person who has not been asked to put it in a tray is a sheet that has been given up, and nobody in that building has ever entered one of those.**

---

**And behind her there is a case with a shelf on it, and on that shelf is a book, and one leaf of that book is face down, and there are nineteen words on that leaf in her own hand, and she wrote them on the sixth of a month in a year before last, and nobody in that building has asked her to turn it over and nobody is going to.**

Four people in that building have said out loud that a person who turns a leaf over is a person who has made a decision about what comes next, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building and not one of them has ever had a line in it for what a person is going to do next, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a woman stood at a case on a Saturday morning and put a hand flat on a book and took it off again without opening it.

---

And the room went on. The presses on the second floor started at about the first hour and went on until about the fifth, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building in a week of presses and a Saturday is the day the week is counted, and a woman of thirty-three filled in four more of them before that morning was out, and read each one back once, and put each one in a tray, and the fourth column of each one is headed *Keeper* and she filled that in from a roll in about a minute and a quarter without looking up, and there was nobody in that room watching her do it and she did not check whether anybody was.

---

**And a woman of about thirty came into that town in a cart at about the first hour on the Friday before and went up two floors to a room at the end of the second-floor passage and has been in it two nights, and the town had nowhere to put her and a clerk of the Crown had a bed made up in that room on the Thursday, and nobody in that building has said the word for what that is and there is no column in that proceeding for a person.**

And the copy went back on the tray at about the fourth hour and the tray went up two flights, and about nine hundred people in that town went on working on forms at about four hundred a month into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls, and a market was on in that town and about three hundred people were in it, and the woman who had come two hundred miles was in it and got four ordinary sentences and gave back four ordinary answers and told nobody in it that she was two hundred miles from the only room she has ever worked in.

And the woman of thirty-three went back to the register and is going to fill in that column again on Monday morning from a roll in about a minute and a quarter without looking up, and she is not going to stop, and nobody in that building has asked her to and nobody in that building is going to.
