# Chapter 0274

**The fifth day of Longlight, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour in a chamber on the third floor of a building in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and about the fourth hour in a fen county's own market and four hundred yards up that county's own road, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that a line about four hundred yards of made road went into a record in a chamber two hundred miles off on that Saturday morning with a name in the fourth column of it, and that a sheet of the same printed series came back down the same road in a carrier's bag in the same afternoon and was put in a sill on a crossing that carries nobody, and that nobody in that county was told anything about either half of it.**

The record is read at the second hour on Saturdays and on Wednesdays, and it is read by a man of forty-four who is a member of the Culling Order and a classifier, and he reads the object column first and has said out loud in a room that he is not going to write in it twice in a day, and he did not explain himself and nobody asked him to.

The schedule is a week late and has been for about a month, and a schedule that is late is a schedule with room in it, and the room in this one is at the bottom where the lines are ruled and empty.

And the line that went in at about the second hour and a quarter on that Saturday is a line about four hundred yards of made road in a county two hundred miles off, and the place went in the object column, and the place is written the way that office writes a place, and there is a word in front of the place name on it, and the word is in the same type as the heads of the five columns.

The man of forty-four read the place in about four seconds and did not read the word out, because the word is in the column and the columns are read by their heads and the head of that column is *Object*.

The other four places against that line are blank, and one of the four is ruled for a figure and one is ruled for a date, and the sixth column has no head on it and is the same width as the fifth, and about four of the six columns on that sheet are ruled for a figure and there is nowhere on the whole of it for the name of a person.

The man of forty-four had not been asked what is in the four places. Nobody in that chamber has ever asked him, and he has never asked, and about four people in that building noticed afterwards, on four different days, in four different rooms, and did not know the other three had noticed and are not going to be told.

---

The witness for that place was produced at about the third hour and it was the ordinary thing, because a place in a classification is not a thing that can be witnessed by anybody who lives in it.

A classification of a length of made road rests on a witness record, and a witness record is a form of the office's own printed series with a hand in the fourth column, and the fourth column is headed *Keeper*, and the hand is nine shapes and the shapes are the same in the same half-minute on about four hundred forms a month.

The sheet was a form of that series and it was about nine minutes old and it was the work of a man of about fifty who has been a clerk of a press for thirty-one years and has entered about eleven thousand forms and has never once been asked what is in the four places.

“**Is that yours,**” a man of about thirty who is a clerk of that room said.

“**It is,**” the man of about fifty said.

**“Whose hand is the fourth column.”**

**“Mine,” the man of about fifty said, and he said it the way a man says the time, and he did not put the sheet down to free his hand, and he did not look at it while he said it.**

Nobody said anything else, and about four people on a stair in that building have said since that a man who has just had a name put in a column in his own hand and has not been asked about it goes downstairs and asks a clerk a question about something else, and that the two questions are the same question and neither of the two men has worked out that and is not going to.

The man with the satchel came through that passage at about the third hour and did not stop on the landing, and a man who carries a form down a stair and up a stair and does not stop has a thing he is not sure of, and the not stopping is the whole of the method.

The name in the fourth column was read out because a name in a column is read out when the column is read, and it was the name of a woman of thirty-three who is an aid to the register on the first floor of that building and is not a member of anything and has never been, and who has filled that column about two hundred times in a season for eleven years and has been afraid of it since she was twenty-two.

Two floors down and a floor down, at about the first hour, which is before any of it, she had filled in two of them from a roll in about a minute and a quarter each without looking up, the way she does the four hundred, which is to say she read the first two columns and wrote the fourth with no pause anywhere in the third.

Nobody in that building went up or went down on that Saturday, and a leaf on the second shelf of a case behind her is face down and has been since the sixth of a month in a year before last and has nineteen words on it in her own hand, and she did not turn it over, and nobody has asked her to, and she has not come up two floors and she is not going to.

---

---

And the market in that county is on twice a fortnight and the fifth of Longlight was one of the two, and about four hundred people were in the street, and the street is the same width it has been for about two hundred years, and nothing that has ever happened in this county has happened in that street at a width other than that.

A salt waggon came up the channel road about the third hour and stopped at the end of it and about nine waggons have come up that road since the spring, and a man of about thirty-one with a satchel came through the crowd with about two hundred sheets of that series in the satchel and about four hundred places on a list behind him, and he has carried about two thousand sheets of that series in six years and has never once known what is in the four places and has said so out loud twice in about a week, in a yard and in a room, in the tone of a man saying a price.

He said it a third time on that Saturday to nobody at all, in the middle of a market, at about the fourth hour, with the satchel open and a sheet in his hand.

**“Four places ruled for a figure and no place for a name,” he said, to a woman buying salt. “I have carried that shape for six years and I could not tell you what a person is meant to do with it, and I have stopped trying, and I would rather say so than be asked twice.”**

She said she was sorry for him and went on with her salt, and about four hundred people in that street did not hear it and about nine did, and a man of about thirty-one with a satchel put the sheet back in the satchel and went on up the lane and did not go as far as the crossing because he had nine more drops in the county and the fourth one was at a mill.

And a bag came up that county's own road in the ordinary post on the afternoon of that same Saturday and a carrier of about fifty with a firm of two carried it up the lane himself because the firm of two were at a market four miles off.

A bag in the ordinary post comes back to a county with everything in it that was ever sent to it, including one sheet of about nine inches by six that nobody in that county had sent and that had a number of an office against it and nothing at all against a person.

He carried it up the lane and put it in the sill of the crossing.

The toll house is a building that people cut through on the way to somewhere and not a building anybody goes into, and it has been that since before the bench existed, and a carrier with a bag is not a man who stops at a building like that to ask what is wanted with it. He put it in because the sill is where a thing goes when it is raining and there was no other sill on that lane, and he weighted it with a stone off the bank because the wind on that lane comes off the water and has taken a sheet off a sill before.

The sill is a foot and a half wide and it has no glass in it and no third seat, and there were two men in the toll house that carries nobody and has carried nobody for four years, and a carrier of about fifty with a firm of two put a sheet of the office's own printed series in the sill of it and did not ask anybody what to do with it and nobody in that lane was there to tell him.

A carrier is not a man of letters and a crossing that carries nobody is not a place a bag is brought to, and about four people in that town have said since that the whole of the way a sheet of paper travelled on the fifth of Longlight, YR 316 is a thing a room decided and a man with a firm of two carried out on a Saturday and did not ask, and that neither of those two men has ever wanted to know what the other one did, and that the sheet is on a sill in a building nobody in that county goes into, and that is the only reason it was not taken in the rain.

And the whole of what it cost that county to be put in a schedule it did not know existed was this.

A sheet of the office's own printed series for one classification, a penny. A second sheet of the same series for the person the matter is entered against, a penny. A clerk's fee for entering one classification, fourpence. A carrier's hand for two hundred miles, and there is no rate for two hundred miles printed in this county and the man who carries bags here has never been asked what a thing like that costs and would not be asked. A stone off a bank four hundred yards down a lane, and there is no rate for a stone in this county and there is not going to be one.

*A penny and a penny is twopence. Twopence and fourpence is sixpence.*

**Total: six pence. And that is the whole of what it cost two hundred miles of road to put a name in a column about a crossing that has carried nobody for four years and has not been asked about anything in that time, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that a sheet is in the sill, and the man of thirty-two with a limp is in that town and does not know it either, and he is not going to be told, and it is going to be in a sill until the weather takes it.**
