# Chapter 0257

**The tenth day of Thawmonth, YR 316, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, and four rooms in one building in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and about four people in that building who worked out the same thing on the same day about four hours apart and did not know the other three had worked it out, and about nine hundred people in that building who do not know that any of it happened, and one of the nine hundred is a woman of about thirty-three who is an aid to the register and who was at a press on the second floor at about the fourth hour and heard a sentence about a column and did not say anything back.**

The sentence the woman of thirty-three heard was this, and it was said at about the fourth hour by a man of about fifty to a man of about sixty with a tray, and neither of them said it to her, and she was four feet off with a sheet in her hand A woman who is four feet off and hears a sentence about a column and says nothing back has not been asked anything, is not going to be asked anything, and has not been asked anything since she was twenty-two.

“**A man can stand in a room for two hours and be asked nothing and go out having done the work and not know that he was never asked.**”

The man of about sixty with a tray said nothing to that and carried the tray out at the seventh hour and has not said anything about it since, and four people in that building have said since that the sentence was not about the man of about fifty and was not about the man with the tray and was about the man of forty-four, and that the man of about fifty and the man with the tray are the only two men in that building who could have said it, and that he said it to the other one and not to the man it was about.


---

**And a woman of about thirty-three who is an aid to the register filled in the fourth column of a form at about the sixth hour on that Friday with nobody in the room but a man of about sixty with a tray, and the fourth column is headed *Keeper*, and she has filled it about two hundred times in a season for eleven years. Two hundred is a count of askings. It is not a count of persons and it is not a price and it is added to nothing.**

The form had come back out of the tray that morning with a space empty in the fifth column and a figure in the second, and the fourth was the one a room puts its hand in, and she filled it from a roll in about a minute and a quarter without looking up, and the man of about sixty with a tray was in the room and did not watch her do it and she did not check whether he was watching.

She has been afraid of that column since she was twenty-two. She said so out loud in a room, once, in nineteen words, and four people in that building have heard it and two of them have heard it twice, and nobody has done anything about it and nobody is going to, and there is no form in that office for a person to say that she is afraid of a column, and there is no column in that office for it, and a woman of thirty-three has therefore said the thing she is afraid of in a room to about four people and has gone on filling it in.

**And on the second shelf of the case behind her is a book with a leaf in it turned face down, and the leaf has nineteen words on it in her own hand, and she wrote them on the sixth of a month in a year before last and she has not turned it over, and nobody in that building has asked her to and nobody is going to. Four people in that building have said out loud that a person who turns a leaf over is a person who has made a decision about what comes next, and nobody has asked her anything, and she was at a press on the second floor at about the fourth hour that day and heard a sentence about a column and said nothing back.**

She put the filled sheet on the bench with the other three and did not look at the fourth column again, and the man of about sixty with a tray took the four of them at about the seventh hour in a tray, and four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that the hand that filled the second column of a return on the fourth of Goatmonth, YR 315 in about a minute and a quarter is the hand that filled the fourth column of a form at about the sixth hour on the tenth of Thawmonth, YR 316, and that neither of the two is a decision and that both of them are the same hand, and that the same hand is a woman of thirty-three who has been afraid of that column for eleven years and has said so in nineteen words and has filled it in this year and last year and the year before.

---

**And a Friday in that building is the day the about four hundred forms of a month come back into one room, and the fourth hour is when they come, and the light in that room at the fourth hour in Thawmonth is on the floor and not on the walls. A room where the light is on the floor is a room in which people read things held at waist height, and the four hundred forms are read at waist height, and nine hundred of them a week are read in four floors by about forty people, and none of the forty is asked what is in the four places.**

About nine of the four hundred come back. Eight of the nine come back with a figure in them and one comes back with a space empty and four lines beside it, and all nine go into the returns press because a return is a return, and the only difference between the eight and the one is what is in a column, and there is no column in that building for what is in a column.

Nobody in that room on that Friday connected the eight to the one, or the one to a man on the second floor, or a man on the fourth floor to a woman on the first floor, and the sentence about a column was said at the fourth hour on the second floor and the eight came into the room with the stove at the fourth hour, and about four hours apart in the same building four people noticed the same thing and did not know it.

**And that is the first of the four, and it is at the fourth hour on the second floor, and nobody in that room has said since that the man of about fifty knew who it was about, because a man who has entered about eleven thousand forms in thirty-one years does not say a name.**

---

**And the second of the four is at about the fourth hour and a half in the press room, and it is a boy of nineteen who has been told a thing at second hand and does not know what it is about and has eleven weeks and five days to serve.**

He was told by the man of about fifty, in about nine words, at the door of the press room, and the man of about fifty told him that a man had been in the chamber that morning and that the man had read a schedule out loud from beginning to end and had not been asked a question, and the boy of nineteen listened to that and said four words.

“**Asked a question by who.**”

The man of about fifty said nothing and went down. A man of about fifty has asked one question in about a year and it was not this one. A boy of nineteen who asks *by who* has got hold of something in nine words that the four grown persons in that building have not got hold of, and the boy did not have it and was not going to.

**He went back to the stone and set nine lines of a column that has a head on it and he thought about the two hours for about a minute and a half, and then a woman of about thirty came and stood at the end of the stone and waited, and he set the sheet. A boy of nineteen who has been in a room for two hours of somebody else's work does not put that in a book, does not tell his mother, has told nobody at all, and will not.**

---

**And the third of the four is at about the fifth hour in the room with the stove, and it is a woman of about twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns, and she has wanted one thing in six years and on that Friday she wanted the other one, and the other one is smaller.**

She has wanted the sixth column ruled for six years. On the tenth of Thawmonth, YR 316, at about the fifth hour, she found out that a man can stand in a room for two hours and be asked nothing, and she sat down. A woman who has wanted a column ruled for six years and has not asked for it has spent a day thinking about a man who was not asked anything. Those are not the same thing, and a woman who has wanted one for six years knows the difference between what she wants and what she has heard, and she went on wanting the same thing after hearing it.

She said one thing at about the fifth hour and it was to the stove, and it was ten words, and it is the only thing she said in that room that day.

“**I have wanted a rule in it for six years.**”

Nobody asked her which one she wanted ruled and nobody asked her what *it* was, Ten words to a stove in a room with two people in it is the ordinary way a thing gets said in that building when the person saying it does not want to be asked a second question, and a second question is the only thing in that building that makes a want into a finding, and she has been avoiding one for six years.

---

**And the fourth of the four is at about the sixth hour and it is the man of about sixty with a tray, and it is the only one of the four that anybody has ever asked about, and he was asked by a woman of about twenty-nine about a thing that is not a column and that he answered in the ordinary way.**

He has carried that tray out of that room at the seventh hour of the evening and back at the first hour of the morning for twenty-two years. He has never been asked whether he wanted the tray. He said one thing about it at about the sixth hour to a woman of about twenty-nine who was in the room, and it was twelve words, and he did not say it as a want and did not know that it was one.

“**Nobody ever asked whether I would rather it went at the sixth.**”

She said nothing back and went to the tray and looked in it. A man who has carried a thing for twenty-two years has just told a woman that the hour is wrong, and the hour has been wrong for twenty-two years, and a man who says a thing about an hour in a room where four hundred forms a month are worked has said a thing about a column without knowing there is a column in it. Nobody in that room joined the two together and nobody in that building is going to.

**And the tray went out into the passage at about the seventh hour on the Friday with four things in it and came back at about the first hour on the Saturday morning, and it is a tray and not a file and a bag is not a tray, and the two of them are the two places in that building where a thing can be for a long time and not be anywhere, and about nine hundred people in that building do not know that four separate people said something on that Friday and that the four somethings are one thing, and that the one thing is a form with four places ruled for a figure and nowhere on the sheet at all for the name of a person, and that nobody in that building has ever asked what is in the four places, and that a man of forty-four stood in a room on the eighth of Thawmonth, YR 316 for two hours and was asked nothing and read a schedule and has not noticed that he was not asked and is not going to.**
