# Chapter 0282

**The third day of Rainmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour and the sixth hour and the seventh hour, and about four hundred people in a market in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and nine people in a long room over a wharf at the north end of it, and a lane four hundred yards off with a satchel going up it at the sixth hour, and a bench of nine that has been asked eight things in nineteen years and was asked nothing at all on that Saturday, and a count of eight on a page under a stair in that room where anybody coming up can read it, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that nine sheets that went out on the Monday before came back up the same lane at the sixth hour and went down it again inside nine minutes.**

The market in that town is on twice a fortnight and the third of a month was one of the two, and the street it fills is the width it has been for about two hundred years, and nobody in that county has ever widened it and nobody has ever had the money to and about nine people in that town have wanted to and have not said so to anybody.

---

The room fills at about the seventh hour and not before, and about nine people go up the outside stair two at a time, and a clerk of about sixty is at the end of the long table with a minute he has kept for nineteen years and has never entered a name in that anybody else in the room did not put there first.

There is a bench along the wall under the window and nine men would sit on it if nine men came, and four came, and the magistrate sat at the table with her hands flat on it and the reeve sat next to her and did not vote on the guild's own matter and has not for nine years.

A man of fifty-five was in for the fire and had a stick he did not need and has not put down.

**And nothing was asked of the bench of nine on that Saturday, and the count on the page under that stair is eight and was eight before it and is eight after it, and a thing that is not asked is not a refusal and is not a yes, and the minute for that morning has no asking in it and did not have one when it was ruled.**

---

A man of about thirty-one who is on that bench asked one thing before the salt, and it was about a gate, and the answer was no, and the no went in without a reason and about nine people in that room have said since that a bench of nine has been asked eight things in nineteen years and has not been asked anything since the sixth of a month and is not going to be asked anything on that Saturday by anybody.

“**Whose gate,**” the man of about thirty-one said.

“**Nobody's,**” the magistrate said, and that was the whole of it.

The salt was the first item and it took eleven minutes, and a carter of thirty with a barrow and a waggon of his own was in the room for it and did not speak, and the salt that is in that minute is the same salt that is in it about four hundred times in nineteen years.

The second item was a hedge. There is a hedge four miles up the county's own road where two fields meet corner to corner and the two fields are held in two different names and the two parishes either side of it have never been one parish in about four hundred years, and about nine people in that room found the two of them and argued about them for about twenty minutes on the ordinary way, and one of the nine wanted it in the minute and one of the nine did not and the clerk entered that it had not been agreed and did not write down how many had wanted it.

A clerk of about sixty entered that the bench had not voted, and entered nothing about how many of the nine had wanted it, and there is one line in that minute for a thing that was not decided and there is no second line for a thing nobody said, and he has never put anything in the second place and has never been asked why.

A man of about thirty-one who is on that bench said one thing about the hedge and it was fifteen words and it was said in the ordinary way.

“**Both of them want it and neither of them has been to the other end.**”

“**Then it stays,**” the magistrate said, and about nine people in that room went on at it for another nine minutes, and the second item took twenty in all, and the clerk of about sixty entered that the bench had not voted and entered nothing about how many of the nine wanted it.

And the magistrate did not use the construction. She has had it ready for about a year and she did not use it on the hedge and nobody in that room knew she had one, and a magistrate who has had an instrument ready for a year and does not use it on a hedge is a magistrate who is waiting for a thing worth using it on, and nobody in that county has worked out what that thing is and is not going to.

The bench rose at about the eighth hour. The clerk read the minute back once, at the end, and only once, and the reading took about a minute and a quarter, and he did not read it a second time and there is no practice in that room of reading anything twice and there never has been, and a clerk who reads a minute once is a clerk who does not want to hear what it sounds like the second time, and he has an answer ready for that and nobody in this county has ever wanted it.

---

**And outside, in that street, four hundred people did the ordinary Saturday of about four thousand people, and a salt waggon came up the channel road at about the third hour and stopped at the end of it and stood there about an hour, and about nine waggons have come up that road since the spring and there is a man who writes down what comes up that road and what goes down it and has written nine lines this year.**

The salt went into a store at the north end of the street through a door that is four feet nine high because the last cart that went through it was four feet nine high, and the door has not been made higher since and the man who wrote down the nine lines did not write down the height of the door and has never been asked.

A woman of about thirty with a pail came up the lane at about the fifth hour and said one thing at nine doors and moved on before the knuckle had finished ringing in the frame, and at the door on that lane forty feet from a wagon arch she said that a man had been up the lane twice in three days and had not knocked at either, and she said it to the door, and there are eight doors behind her in that lane and nobody has ever asked her why she says things at doors.

---

**And at about the sixth hour, half an hour before any of that, a man of about thirty-one with a satchel came up that lane with the collection and was in it for about nine minutes, and the lane was wet to the hub from a shower that had gone over about the fourth hour, and about four hundred people were in a market four hundred yards away and did not see him go up it.**

A collection is not a delivery and it is the same nine in the same order.

He came up the lane and he stopped at nine places and at seven of the nine the sheets had gone, because a sheet left on a step in a wet week is a sheet that has been in the rain since the Monday, and at the eighth there was a sheet on a step that had been turned over by somebody and put back, and at the ninth of the nine he put his hand in a sill and took a sheet out of it.

The sheet had been there since the Monday and it had a stone on it and the stone was a stone off the bank and the stone was not in the satchel either, and he left the stone on the sill and took the sheet and put the sheet in with about nine others and went on down the lane and did not look at any of them.

A satchel of that office's own printed series goes back down the lane and down the county's own road and into a building two hundred miles off, and everything in it is read, because that is the trade and because a round that collects is the only thing in the mail that anybody anywhere is certain has come from somewhere.

**And the sheet that came out of that sill had this county in the object column and a date in it eleven days after the Monday, and it went into a building two hundred miles off at about the seventh hour on that Saturday, and about four people in that building read it that evening, and none of the four asked where it had been, and there is no column in that office for where a sheet has been, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a county of about four thousand people is in a folder because a man with a satchel put it on a step in the rain and took it back off.**

---

The man with the satchel went up that lane alone and came down it alone, and about four hundred people in that market did not notice a man going either way, and nobody has ever asked him what he does up there and he has never said.

---

**And there is a room over a weigh-house at the end of that lane with a door on the lane and a number on the door and a year against the number at fourpence, and a keeper of records is twenty-nine and has run that office four years and a half out of a deed of thirteen words, and a second copyist is about thirty and is on a warrant that is not in the press, and there is a third post in that day-book and it is on a warrant held by a second keeper of twenty-six and it is not signed, and the two women have never spoken about it and are not going to.**

The room over that weigh-house is two rooms and the tide comes up through the boards of the weigh-house under them in a wet week, and it was not a wet week, and the light in there is on the floor and about four hundred forms a month go out of a building two hundred miles off and are read in that room at waist height under it.

The scales under that room have not been used since the fourth of a month, and there is a press in it that can be served with anything at all by anybody who walks up that lane, and a press four hundred yards up Weir Street that cannot, and a day-book that is the only book in that office nobody can be served with, and a half tile in that press that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is not in a column anywhere in this kingdom.

The second copyist goes on Monday.

Nobody in that office has said why she is the one and not the woman of twenty-nine and not the woman of twenty-six, and the warrant she travels on is a warrant to produce and not a warrant to enter anything, and it names a room and a day and does not name a person, and the man who wrote it out wrote it in about four seconds and put it in an envelope and gave it to a man of about thirty-one with a satchel and the man of about thirty-one put it in with about nine others and did not read it.

**And the second keeper of twenty-six is not going and the third post in that day-book is still not signed, and the woman of about thirty has wanted a line in that book with her own name on it since the summer before last and has said out loud that she is not going to get it, that she does not want it, and that those two are the same thing, and the two of them have never once raised either of those, and the day-book is the only book in that office that cannot be served with, and it stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked and there is no tenth coming on that Saturday or the Monday.**

Nobody walked any part of that Saturday with her either. She was in that room over the weigh-house from the first hour to the ninth with a sheet in front of her and came out of it once, and about four hundred people in a market four hundred yards away did not see a woman go anywhere, and a bench of nine had sat for about an hour that morning over a wharf and been asked nothing, and a clerk of about sixty had read a minute back once, and the tide came up under the wharf at about the tenth hour and went back at the third, and the salt was over the first bay, and a bell a hundred yards up a road went at the fourth hour and a half, and on the Monday she went down that lane on her own at about the sixth hour to a cart and got into it, and by then a county was in a folder two hundred miles off with a district on it and nothing in that building to say how it got there.
