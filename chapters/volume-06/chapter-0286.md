# Chapter 0286

**The eleventh day of Rainmonth, YR 316, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the third hour and the sixth hour, and a room with a stove in it on the first floor of a building in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and five people in that room on a Friday when there are four of them on a Saturday, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that the last kind thing anybody in that room was ever going to be offered at that table was offered at about the third hour of that Friday and was refused.**

A building of four floors and seven presses is quieter on a Friday that is not a market day, and about four hundred forms a month go out of it in every week of the year, and a Friday is the day they go out on, and so a Friday morning in that room is a morning with about nine of the four floors idle and one floor working and the stairs empty both ways and the stove not lit and the window shut.

---

**A man of about fifty came up two flights at about the second hour and stood in that room for about four minutes and said nothing at all, and he had a sheet under his arm and he put it on the table and picked it up again and took it back down with him.**

He has been a clerk of a press for thirty-one years and has entered about eleven thousand forms and has never once been asked what is in the four places, and he has not been thanked for anything at any time in about two years, and a man who comes up two flights and says nothing has come up to find out whether anybody in that room knows, and he went back down having found out that nobody in that room had said anything about his hand.

And he did not come up again, and the four minutes are the whole of it, and a man who has found out twice that nobody in a room is talking about him is a man who will not come up a third time, and that is the only kind of decision a man makes in that building that is not written down anywhere and is not in any column at all.

---

The woman of thirty-three filled in two forms that morning and read them both back once and put them in a tray, and the fourth column of each of them is headed *Keeper*, and she filled that in from a roll in about a minute and a quarter each without looking up, and there was nobody in that room watching her do it and she did not check.

The woman of about twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns turned a sheet about the second hour and did not say anything, and has wanted the sixth column ruled for six years and has not asked for it in a form, and Perry Hask, 34, wrote a figure in a second column in about a minute and a quarter and read it once and put it in a tray.

**And the man of about sixty with a tray came in at about the third hour with tea for four and put it on the table and said that there was tea in that room if anybody wanted it and went out again, and he has done that about nine times a week for twenty-two years and has not once been asked whether anybody wanted it and nobody in that room said anything on that Friday, and a man who brings tea into a room five times a week for twenty-two years is a man who is not a person to anybody in the room and knows it and has never once said so.**

---

**And at about the third hour the woman of about twenty-nine said a kind thing, and it is the only kind thing anybody in that room has said since the copy of that sheet came off a tray on the Saturday before, and it is five words, and she began it as something else and heard herself begin it as something else and stopped in the middle of it.**

“**It was not your hand.**”

And then the room was a room with four people in it and a jug of tea going cold on a table, and the woman of thirty-three said the third thing, and it is the whole of what she said that day, and nobody in that building improved on any part of it then or afterwards, and it is the last time a kind thing is refused at that table.

“**I have been afraid of that column since I was twenty-two and I filled it in this year in about a minute and a quarter without looking up, and you have now put my name in it again, and both of those are the same hand, and neither of them is mine. Do not thank me. I would like somebody to write down that I did not stop it and there is nobody in this building I can ask.**”

---

Nobody thanked her. Nobody improved on it. Nobody in that room said that she had been right, and nobody said that she had been brave, and nobody said that it was not her fault, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building and there is no form in the office of that Crown that has a line for any of those three things and there never has been.

And the clerk of the Crown of that building was in the passage outside that room at about the third hour and a half and did not come in, and a man who stands in a passage outside a room where something has just been said is a man who has heard it through a door, and he was not asked and has not been asked anything about it and is not going to be.

**And nobody asked her anything, and that is the whole of the rest of that Friday, and a woman who asks a room to write down a thing about herself and is not asked a question back has learned what a room is for in about a second and a half, and the asking is not the part that is expensive and the not being asked is.**

The woman of about twenty-nine put her pen down about four seconds after the last of it and then picked it up again and went on with what she was doing, and a clerk who puts a pen down and picks it up again has not entered anything and has not refused anything and is going to carry on exactly as she was, and this is the ordinary outcome of a thing nobody in a room can put anywhere.

“**Do you want it entered,**” Hask said, after about a minute and a half, and it was the only sentence anybody said in that room for a minute and a half and it was the sentence of a man offering a form and not a kindness.

“**No,**” the woman of thirty-three said.

“**I had to ask,**” Hask said.

“**You did not,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, without looking up, and two clerks in a room have agreed on one thing in about a minute and a half, and the thing is that a man offering a form is not a man offering a kindness, and that is the whole of the difference between the two, and it took them a minute and a half and it has taken other people years.

Perry Hask did not look up. He has wanted one thing in eleven years in that building and said it out loud once, on the Thursday before, in eleven words, to a man of about fifty at a stone, and he was not thanked for that and was not refused and has not mentioned it since, and a man who has been given nothing once and has not asked again is a man who has worked out that asking twice is a different trade and not one anybody pays for.

**And the tray came back in at about the fourth hour with three sheets on it and the man of about sixty with a tray said that there was nothing on them for anybody in that room and went out again, and a man who says there is nothing on them for anybody in that room has said the most careful thing anybody said in that room all day, and he said it to nobody, and he is on a rate for carrying trays and not for saying things.**

---

And at about the fourth hour she stood up and put the two forms she had filled in into a tray and went out of that room and up one flight and along a passage and down one flight to a room at the end of it with a bed in it that a clerk of the Crown had a made up on the Thursday, and she did not knock and the woman of about thirty was not in it and the bed had been slept in and the room was cold.

“**I am going to sit down,**” she said, to a room with nobody in it, and shut the door, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that a woman two hundred miles from the only room she has worked in for eleven years sat down on a bed that a clerk of a Crown had had made for her on the Thursday because a building of four floors and seven presses had nowhere else to put a person who came two hundred miles.

---

**And behind her there is a case with a shelf on it, and a book on the shelf with one leaf face down, and nineteen words on that leaf in her own hand, written on the sixth of a month in a year before last, and nobody in that room asked her about it and nobody in that room is going to.**

Four people in that building have said out loud that a person who turns a leaf over is a person who has made a decision about what comes next, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building and not one of them has ever had a line in it for what a person is going to do next, and she was at a press on the second floor at about the fifth hour that Friday and heard nothing said about a column and said nothing back.

---

And the jug of tea went cold on that table and the man of about sixty with a tray took it out at about the sixth hour and did not wash it, and a tray that is not washed between the fourth hour and the sixth is a tray that has been stood on, and it is not a rule in that building and there is no column for it.

**And a table in that room has had a kind thing offered at it once since the twenty-second year of a woman's age and has refused it, and a table where a kind thing has been refused once does not get one offered at it again, and nobody in that building has worked out how to do that on purpose and nobody in that building has had to.**

Nobody walked any part of that Friday with anybody. About nine hundred people in that town went to work on about four hundred forms a month into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river did not know that a woman two hundred miles off had been asked a question in a room and had not been asked a question back, and that she was going to fill that column in on Monday morning from a roll in about a minute and a quarter without looking up, and that nobody in that building knows that she knows and nobody is going to tell her that they know.
