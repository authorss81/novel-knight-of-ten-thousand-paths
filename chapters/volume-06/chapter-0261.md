# Chapter 0261

**The sixth of Mudmonth, YR 316, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour, and a chamber two floors above a room with a stove in it in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and a long table in it that is not the table of three, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that a length of made road in a county they have never heard of went into a table at about the second hour of that Wednesday as a thing that can be acted on, and that a woman of thirty-three who was at a register two floors below that morning does not know it either, and that the column it is going to want is a column with a head on it, and that the head is the office's own, and that nobody in that building has ever asked anybody what is in it.**

The thing on the table is a schedule and it is about two hundred and forty lines long and it is a week old and it is late, and a schedule that is late is a schedule with room in it, and the room in this one is at the bottom, where the lines are ruled and empty.

The head of the record it goes into says: *Read into the record, at the second hour, of the office of the Culling Order, of the classifications of the kingdom made under the standing instruction, to be complete before the first of Longlight.*

That is the phrase and it is on the head of a record in a smaller type than the schedule and it is not a threat and it is not a doctrine, and the only two people in that chamber who could have said it out loud did not say it out loud.

The line that went in at about the second hour has a place in it. The place is a ford on a made road and there are about nine of the two hundred and forty lines with a ford in them and this is the tenth, and the place is written in the object column because the object column is the only one of the six that can be filled in without a person being in the room, and a ford is a thing that can be written about with nobody standing in front of it.

The other four places against that line are blank and one of the four is ruled for a figure and one of the four is ruled for a date and the sixth column has no head on it and the same width as the fifth, and about four of the six are ruled for a figure and nowhere on the whole of the sheet is there a place for the name of a person.

**And a classification with a place in it and nothing else is a count of places, and a count of places is a thing the office can send out, and a thing the office can send out is a thing somebody can be asked to do something about, and the fourth column is the one that turns a count of places into an instruction, and the head on the fourth column is the office's own and it has been there for two years.**

The fourth column is headed *Keeper*. It is the only one of the five heads on that sheet that is a person and it is the only one anybody in that building is careful with, and the care goes into the handwriting and not into the asking. Nobody in that room has ever asked the man who reads the schedule what is in the four places, and he has never asked, and about four people in that building have worked that out separately and about four hours apart and none of them has said it to the others.

The clerk who took the line down was a man of about twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns and has been in that room six years, and he wrote the place in the first column in about four seconds and the day in the fourth in about two and then stopped with the pen on the rule and stopped for about a minute and a half, and the reason he stopped is that there is nothing in the sheet for what he is about to do and there is a great deal in the sheet for what he has just done.

“**A place is a thing a person can be at,**” he said, to nobody, and then he put the pen down.

Nobody in that room asked him what he meant and about four of them have since worked out that he meant the fourth column and not the first, and that the sentence is about the fourth column, and that he has said nothing else about it and is not going to.

---

**And that building is four floors and about forty people in it and seven presses across the four of them, and about four hundred forms a month go out of it and nine counties are behind the four hundred, and there are four hundred and nine parish rolls behind the nine counties, and every one of those figures is the office's own and none of them is a figure of persons about anybody, and about four people in that building have said since that a number that a building uses about itself is not a number about a person and that the two kinds are kept in two different places and that nobody in that building has ever been shown where the line between them is because there is not a line, there is a rule on a wall that has never once been read out loud.**

The light in the room with the stove in it at about the second hour in that month is on the floor and not on the walls, and a room where the light is on the floor is a room in which people read things held at waist height, and about four hundred forms a month are read at waist height in that building, and not one of the about forty people in it is asked what is in the four places.

---

**And at about the second hour and a half a woman of about twenty-nine who is the second clerk of that room wanted the sixth column ruled and has wanted it for six years, and she said one thing at that hour and it was to a press and not to a person, and it was fourteen words, and it is the only thing she has said in six years that was about the shape of the sheet.**

“**There is a column the width of a person's name and it is empty.**”

Nobody answered her and nobody has asked her which column and she did not say, and four people in that room have said since that a woman who has wanted a column ruled for six years and has said fourteen words about it in six years is a woman who is not asking for a column, because asking for a column is a decision and saying fourteen words to a press is not a decision, and that the two are the same want wearing two coats and that the one she is wearing on a Wednesday is the one that costs her nothing.

**And a boy of nineteen at the stone asked her one question and it was five words and it was the only question anybody asked anybody in that room that day about any of the six columns.**

“**Whose column would it be.**”

She said nothing and went back to the stone, and the boy set the sheet, and four people in that room have said since that a boy of nineteen who asks *whose* has got hold of the thing four grown persons in that building have had in front of them for two years and have not picked up, and that the boy did not have it after he asked and was not going to.

---

**And two floors down and a floor down and at about the first hour, which is before any of it, a woman of thirty-three who is an aid to the register and is not a member of anything and has never been came in and did what she does at about the first hour every day of the week that there is one.**

She is the person in this kingdom who fills in the fourth column. She has filled it about two hundred times in a season for eleven years and she has been afraid of it since she was twenty-two and she said so out loud in a room once, in nineteen words, and about four people in that building have heard it and two of them have heard it twice, and nobody has done anything about it and nobody is going to, and there is no form in that office for a person to say that she is afraid of a column, and there is no column in that office for it.

A woman of about thirty-three therefore has said the thing she is afraid of in a room to about four people and has gone on filling it in, and on that Wednesday morning at about the first hour she filled in two of them from a roll in about a minute and a quarter each without looking up, and the roll is a roll of forms that have come back out of a tray, and about four hundred of them a month come back out of a tray in that building, and the fourth column is the one a room puts its hand in.

**And on the second shelf of the case behind her is a book with a leaf in it turned face down, and the leaf has nineteen words on it in her own hand, and she wrote them on the sixth of a month in a year before last, and she has not turned it over, and nobody in that building has asked her to and nobody is going to. Four people in that building have said out loud that a person who turns a leaf over is a person who has made a decision about what comes next, and nobody has asked her anything, and she has said nothing this morning about the fourth column and is not going to.**

She put the two sheets on the bench with the others and did not look at the fourth column again, and the man of about sixty with a tray came in at about the fourth hour and took the four of them out, and the tray has gone out into a passage at that hour and come back at the first hour of the morning for twenty-two years, and the man who carries it has never been asked whether he would rather it went at the sixth and has said so once, in twelve words, to a woman of about twenty-nine who was in the room and did not act on it.

**And about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that the hand that filled the second column of a return on the fourth of Goatmonth, YR 315 in about a minute and a quarter is the hand that fills the fourth column of a form two hundred times in a season, and that neither of the two is a decision, and that the same hand belongs to a woman of thirty-three who is at a bench two floors below a chamber at about the second hour of that Wednesday while a man of about twenty-nine two floors above her has a pen on a rule and is not writing.**

---

**And the fourth column of that line is still empty at the fourth hour and it will be empty until a man of about fifty comes up two floors with a sheet, and neither the man of about twenty-nine nor the woman of about thirty-three nor the boy of nineteen nor the man with the tray knows that, and about four people in that building have said since that a column that is empty in a room is not an empty column, it is a column that is about to be filled in by somebody who has not been asked to fill it in and does not know that is what he is coming up two floors to do.**

---

**And the man of about twenty-nine was in that room for two hours before the line and about four hours after it, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who is a clerk of the returns has six years in a room with a stove in it and has read about eleven thousand sheets out of about four hundred trays and has never once been asked to read one out to anybody, and that a man who reads a sheet up to himself in about a minute and a quarter has worked out that the sheet is the only party that never interrupts him, and that this is a thing about a man of about twenty-nine and nothing at all about the office, and that about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people could be told that and would not know what to do with it.**

A woman of about twenty-nine came in at about the third hour with a sheet and put it on the bench and said nothing and went out, and the sheet was one of the eight with a figure in it, and the figure was a figure and not a name and there is no column in that building for what a figure was, and about four people in that room have said since that a figure goes into a press and comes out of a press and goes back into a tray and there is no point in the four floors at which anybody looks at one and says anything, and that a building in which nothing is said about a figure is a building in which a figure can be anything at all.

The schedule is a week late, and there is a rule in that building about a schedule that is late, and nobody in that chamber applied the rule to a line with an empty column in it, and about four people on a stair have said since that the rule about a late schedule is a rule about a schedule and not about a person, and that a rule which can only be applied to a person is not a rule and is a want, and that the office has had two of those for two years and has printed neither of them.
