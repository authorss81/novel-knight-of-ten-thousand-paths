# Chapter 0269

**The thirteenth day of Greenmonth, YR 316, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour, and a wet morning, and a yard on Weir Street in a fen county with about nine men in it and a bench-board and a press in a room that belongs to the yard and cannot be served with anything, and four sheets of printed paper in that press that have not been sent to anybody, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that a fifth refusal of the same thing was written in that yard on that wet morning at about the second hour in front of about nine men by a man of thirty-two with a limp, and that he put a warm hand on nothing at all.**

The yard belongs to a man of twenty-five who owns the crews and is not in it that morning, and the yard's rule is that nothing that belongs to a man goes in that yard's book and only a thing set.

Nobody in that yard has said one word about a family in about a year and about four of the nine men in it have noticed that and about four of them have not noticed anything, and the yard's owner is one of the nine and is not in it that morning.

The bench-board is four feet off the ground and about nine feet long and it is not a table and there is a rule in that yard that a thing is set on it and not at a table, and the reason is that a table is a thing four people sit at and a bench-board is a thing two people stand at, A county in which nine men set four hundred things a day on a board four feet off the ground has never once had to be told what a room is for.

**And a man of about sixty was packing lime at the end of the yard at about the second hour of that morning, in the wet, in an open yard, because wet lime is packed and dry lime is dusted, and he has packed lime at that end of that yard for about nine years, and he is not on the yard's book. The man who packs the lime is the only man in that yard who is not paid out of it, and nobody in that county has ever worked out why.**

---

**And a man of about nineteen came down that lane with an empty hod at about the sixth hour on that morning and went through and did not stop, and he has an elder sister of twenty-two in a house two streets off and has not said one word about her to anybody in that lane in about a year. A yard is a place where men who are related to each other stand nine feet apart and do not say so, and a yard that carries nine men and about four families in it has never once had a conversation about a family in it. This is not a rule anybody made. It is what a yard is, and a man of about sixty packing lime at the end of it has been in that yard nine years and has heard the whole of it.**

---

He wrote it on the bench-board because there is nowhere else in that yard to write anything.

The sheet is of the marshal's own office's printed series and it is a fifth sheet of the same kind, and the four of the kind that are in that press are in a bundle in a corner of the room and have not been sent for about two years and are not going to be, and this one is going in a bag in the ordinary post because a thing that is in a press in a yard cannot be served with anything and a thing that is in a bag in the ordinary post can.

The object column of that series has a place in it and the place is the crossing four hundred yards up this county's own road, and in front of the place name there is a word, and the word is *eastern*, and the word is in the same type as the heads of the five columns, and the type came out of a case in a press room in a provincial town two hundred miles off about six weeks ago and was struck once by a boy of nineteen who does what he is told, and nobody in that office has ever said what the word is for, and no character in this county has asked.

**And he wrote the word in because it is the word that goes in that column on that series, and he did not know what it was, and about four people in that yard have said since that a man who fills in a column writes down what the column wants and not what he knows, and that there is no line on that sheet for a man to write down that he does not know what a column is for, and that the absence of that line is the only difference between that yard and an office in a town two hundred miles off and that nobody has ever measured the difference.**

He did not put a hand on anything.

That is the whole of it and there are about nine men in that yard within thirty feet of him and it is daylight and it is a wet morning and his left hand is warm at about the fourth hour every day and has been since a lane in a county that is not on any map, and it was about the second hour and he did not put it on a sill, a gate, a bench-board, a stone, a wall, a door or a man.

He wrote it standing up, braced with his left forearm along the top edge of the board, because a man cannot get at a bench-board at that height from a seat and there is nowhere in that yard to sit and write. The pen went over the grain on two of the lines and he did not start again on either of them.

Nobody in that yard noticed him not doing it, because a man not putting a hand on a thing is not a thing anybody in a yard looks up for. A man of thirty-two with a limp has come into that yard about four times since the spring and has not put a hand on anything in it on any of the four, and the fourth of the four was the second hour of a wet Friday morning in front of nine men. The hour was not chosen. About four of the nine men in that yard have worked out what the hour was and about none of them has ever worked out why it was not chosen.

**And the weather came in again about the fourth hour and the man of about sixty packing lime put a lid on the heap and came in out of it and stood at the wall with his back to about nine men. A man who packs lime stands with his back to a yard for nine years and knows what nine men say behind him without hearing it, and he has never once turned round, and nobody in that yard has ever asked him how he does it, and a man with his back to a yard for nine years is the closest thing that yard has to a person who keeps it and is on nobody's book and is paid out of nothing. The finding about the bell in that county is a finding about this man. Nobody in that county has said so and nobody is going to.**

He came in out of the lime and looked at the sheet on the bench-board from about nine feet off and read it upside down without moving his head, which is a thing a man does at a bench-board and not a thing a man does at a table, and he said nothing about it. A man who reads a sheet upside down from nine feet and says nothing is a man who has read four hundred sheets upside down in nine years. Nine men in that yard have watched him do it about nine hundred times, none of them has ever thought about it, and four of them have.

---

**And he wrote it in about eleven minutes and he did not write it well and he did not write it badly. A refusal written on a bench-board in the wet by a man who cannot lean on anything takes eleven minutes, and eleven minutes is longer than a bench of nine has sat in this county in a year. The man of about sixty packing lime heard every word of it in the wrong order and about four of the nine men heard every word of it in the right one, and neither of those two sets of people will ever be asked about it.**

The first column of that sheet is what is given, and what is given on that Monday was a road, and the second column is to whom, and the second column is the same second column this county has used for four years and a half and it says *to the persons who may require this office to produce the same, and to no other, and to no person, there being none*, and about four people in that yard have said since that a man writing a refusal in the second column of a printed form in a wet yard has copied out of a press a sentence he did not write and could not sign and that the sentence has been in that county for four years and a half and has been produced once and has been about a man with a stick more than once, and that nobody in that yard knows what it means and that nobody has asked and that a yard is the only room in that county in which that sentence has been read out loud by a man who is not a keeper of records and did not know he was reading a rule.

---

**And a man of about twenty-six setting a course on a reach in another lane asked him one question at about the third hour, and it was at a bench-board and not in a room, and it was about the post and not about the hand, and it was five words.**

“**Is that one going out.**”

“**That one is going out.**”

The man of about twenty-six did not look at the bag when he said it and he did not look at the man with the stick when he said it, and then he went back to the course he was setting, and about four people in that yard have said since that two men agreed about a bag in five words each at a bench-board in the wet, and that a thing two people agree about in ten words is a decision, and that a decision made in a yard in the wet on a Friday is a decision with no room in it anywhere, and that there is no column in this county for a decision and there never has been and that is the whole of what a bench of nine is for and does not know it.

---

**And a sheet of that printed series is a penny, and a second sheet of the same series for the person the matter is entered against is a penny, and a sheet into the ordinary post in a bag is twopence, and a clerk's fee for entering a refusal is fourpence, and the bench-board is the property of a yard and there is no rate for the use of a bench-board and there has never been one. A carrier's hand for two hundred miles is not in that column at all, because no rate for it has ever been printed in this county. A figure nobody in a county can check is a figure nobody in a county should enter. The office two hundred miles off enters one every day.**

*A penny and a penny is twopence, and twopence and twopence is fourpence, and fourpence and fourpence is eight pence.*

**Total: eight pence. And that is what a fifth refusal of the same thing cost that yard, and about nine men in that yard do not know the figure and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that a bag is going out of it in the ordinary post with a word on the front of a place name that nobody in that town has ever said out loud.**
