# Chapter 0267

**The twenty-seventh day of Mudmonth, YR 316, a Wednesday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the seventh hour and the eighth hour and the ninth hour, and the same room over a cross, and the same aisle four feet wide between the two middle rows, and a man of fifty-eight standing in it at fifty-eight years of age to be asked what he swore, and about four hundred people in that valley of whom about nine were on benches that morning, and a stranger in the room who has been in it two nights and has a form in a coat and has not taken it out and is not going to, and about two hundred of the ninth generation behind the back row who came to hear a rule being read and are getting something else.**

The question was put by the man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work, and it took him about four minutes to get round to it, and he had had it a long time.

“**Stand in the aisle. I am going to ask you what you swore and you are going to answer and I am not going to ask you anything else.**”

**And about four people in that valley have said since that a man who can say *I am not going to ask you anything else* in a room of about thirty people is a man who has been in that aisle himself, and the man of fifty-five said so out loud in about nine words and then said nothing else about it and is not going to.**

The man of fifty-eight went and stood in the aisle.

The aisle is four feet wide and the benches on both sides of it are bolted down and the floor under it is the same floor as the rest of the room, and a man of fifty-eight who keeps the only roll in a valley of about four hundred people stood in the middle of four feet of floor in front of a room that has not had anybody in it for three generations, and the first thing he did was turn round and look at the bench at the back of the right-hand row and then look at the number cut into the end of it, and the room waited while he did it, and about two hundred of the ninth generation at the back of that room had not known that there was a number on the end of that bench and about nine of them worked it out inside about two seconds each.

“**What did you swear.**”

---

**And the man of thirty-four walked to the front of the room and stood about two feet off the end of the first bench and did not put a hand on the man of fifty-eight, and about four people in that room have said since that a man who has said *I am not going to ask you anything else* and has not touched the man he is asking is a man who has thought about it in a yard and not in a room, and that a man of about thirty-one of that valley had said four words on that morning about a question being asked as a question and not as a thing with a person in it, and that the two of those are about the same thing and about four people in that valley have worked it out and about none of them has said it to the other one.**

The two hundred at the back of that room came about nine feet further forward when he got into the aisle and about nine of them did not, and about four people in that room have said since that a room full of people moves forward when a man of fifty-eight stands in four feet of floor in front of them and that this is the first time in three generations that anything in that room has moved, and that the nine who did not move are the ninth generation and that they will not move for a man of fifty-eight and that they will move for about two hundred people going out of a valley in a year and about four of them will be in it.

He took about nine seconds to say it. Nobody counted. A man who says eighteen words in nine seconds in a room of thirty people will be asked about those nine seconds for the rest of his life, and the rest of his life in that valley is a long one.

---

He said it. It is in the first spread of the only roll in that valley in his own hand and it is eighteen words and it has been in the room for nineteen years and about four hundred people in that valley have never heard it said out loud and about two hundred of them heard it on that morning and will not forget it and will not write it down.

*I swear to keep the book, the room, the day, the mark and the hand, and nothing else.*

Nobody said anything for about a minute and a half and then a woman of about thirty of the ninth generation who has carried a slate for eleven years asked whether the last two words of it meant what she thought they meant, and he said that they did, and that she was the first person in nineteen years to ask him anything about it, and that she was not going to get a second answer and should not want one.

---

---

**And about four people in that valley have said since that the room did one thing after the finding was entered and the one thing was to go on exactly as it had been going on, and that a man of thirty-four began taking the benches out of the floor at about a quarter past and nobody helped him and about nine people went out of the room in about four minutes and the two hundred went out in about nine, and that a finding in that book is not an occasion and has never been treated as one because about four hundred people in that valley have had a book with a figure in it about nine times in a hundred and forty years and every one of the nine was a Tuesday of ordinary work.**

Nobody in that room said anything about the entry and about four of the nine who were still on the benches said nothing about the reading and about two of the four said nothing about the asking, and about four people in that valley have said since that a room in which four people are silent about the three things that happened in it is a room that has settled something, and that a room in which nobody is silent has settled nothing, and that the two are not the same and that this valley has had both of them about nine times in a hundred and forty years and has never worked out which of the two was the settling.

---

**And then he entered it, in his own book, in his own hand, and the entry is at the head of the first spread under the four lines, because the four lines are a rule about keepers and a fact about a keeper goes under the rule about keepers, and he ruled a line to do it, and the line is about the width of two fingers, and it is the first line that has been ruled across in that book in a hundred and forty years.**

It is the first finding in that book in a hundred and forty years. There are three figures in it in a hundred and forty years and the last of them is a figure of people with no name against it and a margin note in a man's own hand saying *eight named and one not found*, and there is a four-word answer in a minute in a clerk's hand that is not in that book and has never been copied into it, and there is this.

**And about four people in that valley have said since that a man can enter a finding in his own book in his own hand and that the fourth line at the head of that first spread permits it, and that the fourth line permits it because he did it himself and not because anybody asked him, and that nobody asked him, and that he asked.**

---

**And he offered it in the room about four minutes after he had been asked, in the same words twice, and the words were that it should go in the book in his own hand under the four lines, and nobody had asked him to offer. A man who has been asked what he swore and has then asked the room to let him write it down has done two things in nine seconds, and both of them were a decision and neither of them was a request, and a request can be refused and a decision cannot. About four hundred people in that valley have never seen a decision in that room and were not asked for one on that night.**

The man of thirty-four said yes and the man of fifty-five said yes and the woman of sixty-six said nothing and the man of eighty-one said yes and the man of sixty-one at the mill said yes without getting up, and about four people in that valley have said since that nine people on benches in a valley where the ninth generation is about two hundred strong and the eighth generation is about nine people old is a room in which a yes is a formality and that the formality is nine generations old and is written at the head of the first spread, and that the first of the four lines says the keeper of it is a man and not an office and that a formality is not an office and that a formality has no keeper.

---

Nobody asked him. He offered in that room about four minutes after he had been asked and nobody had asked him to offer, and about four people in that valley have said since that a man who is asked what he swore and then offers to have it written down in the same evening is a man who has been waiting six years for a room to say it in and has been told by this book that he cannot be asked a second time, and that he knows that, and that about two hundred of the ninth generation heard him say that he knows it and did not understand one word of it.

Nobody in that room asked whether he could stand it afterwards. There were about thirty people in it and two of them were strangers and one of the strangers had come nine days up a made road with a stick and a form in a coat and could have asked and did not. The not-asking is not a kindness and is not a rule. It is the ordinary habit of a room. A room hears a thing once and a person can be asked twice, and that room had just used up the once.

---

**And at about the ninth hour the room emptied the way it empties, and the woman of sixty-six stayed on the second bench until the fire was down, and the man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work began taking the benches out of the floor, which takes about an hour and a half and which nobody has ever asked him not to do.**

The book went under the trestle and the trestle went against the wall, and about four people in that valley have said since that the trestle goes against the wall because the man of fifty-eight has put it there every night for nineteen years and that on the morning after a finding was entered in the book the trestle went against the wall nine minutes faster than usual and that about four of them noticed the nine minutes and about none of them has said so.

He did not go down the hill to the mill that night, and a man of sixty-one who cannot leave his wheel came out into the cold at about the ninth hour for about four minutes anyway, and about four people in that valley have said since that those four minutes were the whole of what that valley got out of an evening that a man of fifty-eight has wanted for six years, and that nobody in the valley has ever been told what was in it and that nobody is going to be told and that a valley of about four hundred people has gone to bed on the ninth hour of a night with a hundred and forty cut into the end of a bench and a number ruled across the first spread in a hundred and forty years and not one of them knows that anything has been entered anywhere at all.
