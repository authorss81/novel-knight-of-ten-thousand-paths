# Chapter 0300

**The twenty-fifth day of Embermonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour and a half and the fifth hour and the seventh hour and the eighth hour, and about four hundred people in a street four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and a long room over a wharf at the north end of that street with nine people in it, and a page under a stair in that room, and a yard on a street four hundred yards up that county's own road with a bench-board in it and nobody at it, and the last day of that year.**

The bell went at the fourth hour and a half. It goes on a Saturday in a market month and it went on the Sunday before that and it goes on, and about four hundred people in that town did not hear it this morning and would not have said so either way.

The market went from the second hour to about the seventh and a salt waggon came up the channel road and stood at the end of it about an hour, and a man who writes down what comes up that road and what goes down it wrote one line, and there have been nine lines in that book this year and one of them is a waggon of salt that is not the guild's and is not in the guild's book and is never going to be in it.

---

A man of forty-seven was at the end of a bench-board four feet off the ground in a yard on a street four hundred yards up that county's own road, at about the fifth hour, with a man with a stick about nine feet off him, and a yard wall behind one of them and nobody else in the yard.

His name is on the roll of a bench of nine and all that gives him is a vote and no office. He is not on a trade. He has not voted since the day he was elected and did not use it then either. He has stood as a witness at two ends of two crossings in a year. Fourpence a week comes out of a private box and not out of the chest. He does not hold the reach, the Old Toll or the crossing, and nobody in that county has ever asked him a single question about any of it.

He said the four things. He has said them five times in a year and that was the fifth, and about four hundred people in that town have heard one of the five and none of the four hundred has heard two, and nobody in that yard has heard any of them before and nobody in that yard has said one word about them.

The man with a stick asked him the same thing he has asked four times. He got the four things and he asked nothing else, because there is nothing else to ask a man who has said the same four things five times, and a man who asks a question a fifth time and gets the same answer a fifth time has not been told anything by the asking, and the man who told him nothing has been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year and has not been asked one question by anybody in this county about either of them.

And the first of the four was about not being asked a single thing in two years, and he put it at the end of the other three in the ordinary way, and nobody in that yard wrote any of it down, and the clerk of a bench of nine was four hundred yards away in a long room and was not told, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was standing in that yard and did not write it down and was not asked to, and a man of forty-seven has said a thing about a room in which nobody recorded it and has said nothing else about it since.

---

Nobody is in that room until about the seventh hour, and then about nine of them go up the outside stair two at a time, and a clerk of about sixty is at the far end of the long table with a minute he has kept for nineteen years.

There is a bench along the wall under the window and nine men sit on it, and there is a chest under the window with two marks in it that have not moved since the ninth of a month in the year before last, and a long table, and four feet of bare floor between the long table and the wall with a bench along it and a back to it, and nobody is standing in those four feet this morning and about nine people in that room have ever noticed that they are there.

A bench of nine has been asked eight things in nineteen years. Four yes, three no for a reason, and one brought up two flights and not put, and the count of the eight is on a page under that stair where anybody coming up can read it, and it has been eight since the sixth of a month in a year in which the year turns, and a thing that is not asked is not a refusal and is not a yes.

**And a woman of thirty-eight came up the outside stair at about the seventh hour on her own and put a sheet on the long table face down and did not sit down.**

She is of the office that convened a proceeding in a chamber in a provincial town two hundred miles off in the second month of this year, and she has been in that building nine years and has written a finding at the end of nine of them, and what she has at the end of every one of the nine is a figure, and she has known since the fourteenth of a month in Thawmonth that it would be and entered the room anyway, and being correct is the most expensive thing available to a person in a chamber.

She turned the sheet over. It is the term of a temporary injunction against forced culling and it holds to a day, and after that day it holds if a local council renews it in public, and the day is about nine days off, and there is no council in this county and there is no writ and no seal and no office in this county at all.

And she asked the bench of nine a thing, and it was a thing, and it was not a figure of persons, and the county could refuse it.

“**That this bench renew it, in public, here, this morning,**” she said, “**and that the renewing be a matter of the county's own record and not of a court two hundred miles off.**”

And the magistrate had a construction ready and had had it ready for about a year, and she did not use it, and nobody in that room knew she had one, and nobody in that room has ever written it down for her, and she is not going to say it in this county and is not going to have it said.

And she said the bench's no. She said it at waist height, in the ordinary way, and it was eleven words, and about nine people in that room heard all of them.

“**No, because we have never been asked for anything at all.**”

Nobody improved on it. Nobody said anything else. The reeve sat next to her and does not vote on the guild's own matter and has not for nine years, and four of the nine men on that bench had put a hand up once in their lives and it was not in that room, and about nine people in that room have said since that a bench of nine which has been asked eight things in nineteen years said no in eleven words to the first thing it was asked by anybody who was not in this county, and that the no had a reason in it, and that a reason is the first half of a thing that can be written down.

**And the clerk of about sixty wrote it down, and the line he wrote it on is the line for a thing that was not decided, and he entered that the bench did not vote, and under that he entered the reason in his own hand, and that is the reason on the page twice, once in the mouth of a bench and once in the ink of a clerk, and a clerk who has kept a minute for nineteen years has no line in that minute for how many times a thing has happened in it, and he has not gone looking for one.**

“**Then it is entered,**” the woman of thirty-eight said, and nobody thanked her and nobody in that room said that the county was right, and a refusal with a reason in it is the only kind of refusal that can be entered without somebody having to decide what it means, and that is what nine years of asking for a room to show what was asked has come to in a county of about four thousand people on a Saturday morning in a market month, and about four people in that hall have said since that a woman of thirty-eight came two hundred miles to be refused in eleven words by a bench of nine on a market Saturday and was not thanked for asking and was not thanked for being answered, and that being correct is the most expensive thing available to a person in a chamber and this is what it came to.

And the man of forty-seven was on that bench and was asked nothing, and has been asked nothing at every one of the nine sittings he has sat in, and has not used his vote since the day he was elected and did not use it then, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that he said the same four things at the end of a bench-board four hundred yards up that road four hours before he sat on that bench and said nothing at all in the room.

---

A clerk of the Crown had come up the outside stair with the woman of thirty-eight and had not been in that room in nineteen years, and he sat on the stool at the end of the table for about forty minutes and wrote four lines and did not ask her one question, and she did not ask him one either, and for about forty minutes neither of them was the office and both of them were the room, and there is no line in that minute for that and there never has been one.

“**You will want it minuted that it was refused,**” the clerk of the Crown said, at about the eighth hour.

“**I will want it minuted that the bench refused it,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**That is a different line.**”

“**It is the same line with a person at the front of it,**” she said, and went down the outside stair, and a Crown officer who has asked for nine years for a record to show what was asked got a refusal entered against a bench of nine with a person at the front of it, and she did not correct it and did not stay to see it written.

And the man of about thirty-one who is on that bench said nothing at all, and the clerk of about sixty put two different things on one line, a thing that was not decided and an absence of a vote, and those are two different things and there is only one line for them, and nobody in that room has asked him about it and he has an answer ready.

---

At about the eighth hour the clerk of about sixty went out of the room and down four steps and turned round and went under the stair.

The page under that stair is about nine inches by six and it has a figure at the top of it in a hand that does not write figures very well, and there are eight marks under the figure, and the marks are not even and about four of them are heavier than the other four because the pen was nearer the nib on those four, and nobody in that hall has ever entered that count on a form, and a form is what that county has for everything else it knows, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that it is there.

He put a ninth mark under the eight. The ink was the same and the pen was the same and it took him about two seconds, and he did not enter the number nine anywhere else, and there is no line in that minute for what the figure at the top of that page is a count of, and a count of askings is a count of askings and is not a price and is not a count of persons and is added to nothing.

**Nobody in that hall has ever entered that count on a form, and nobody in this county has ever worked out that a room can be asked nine things in nineteen years and refuse the ninth and be written down doing it, and that the page under that stair is the only record of it in this kingdom.**

---

The bench rose at about the eighth hour and about nine people went down the outside stair two at a time. The woman of thirty-eight went down behind the last of them and did not ask one more question and was not asked one more question, and the clerk read the minute back once, at the end, and only once, and the reading took about a minute and a quarter, and nobody in that room asked for it again and it was not offered again.

The tide was under the wharf and had been since the tenth hour and went back at the third. The salt was over the first bay. A yard on Weir Street had a bench-board in it with nobody at it. A man of forty-seven went up the county's own road on his own at about the eighth hour with a stick he does not need and has not put down, and nobody walked any part of that Saturday with anybody, and he is not on a trade and holds nothing and has been asked nothing, and there is no more of that to write down.

**And this county has four thousand people in it and about four hundred of them were in a street this morning, and it has two crossings in it, and the county says the two of them are four hundred yards apart, and there is a line in the stone of the third course of the fourth bay of the one that carries a road over a river and there is a line in the face of a dressed block in the abutment of the one a hundred yards from the end of that market, and both of them are about nine inches long, and neither of them has moved, and neither of them has ever been measured, and nobody in this county knows of the other one, and about four hundred people in that market went home on the last day of that year not knowing that there are two, and there has never been a book in this kingdom with the two of them in it, and there is not going to be one.**
