# Chapter 0279

**The nineteenth day of Longlight, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour, and a long room over a wharf in a town of about four thousand people on a river with about four hundred people in the market under it, and nine people in that room and a tenth in it who is on no roll of anything and has never once spoken in that room, and an aisle four feet wide between the long table and the wall, and a woman of seventy-two standing in it, and a clerk of about sixty with a minute he has kept for nineteen years, and the eleventh day after the eighth, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp was in a room over a wharf on that Saturday and told one person one thing and that the one person writes things down for a living and did not write it down.**

Nine people were in that room at about the fourth hour and the bench had not sat and does not sit until the seventh hour on a Saturday and on nothing else in the week, and a room that fills at the fourth hour on a market morning is a room that four hundred people in the street have walked past the door of.

The aisle is four feet wide. It is between the long table and the wall and it goes the length of the room and there is a bench along it with a back to it, and every person who comes into that room steps over that aisle and nobody calls it anything else, and it has no use and there is nothing at the end of it and it has never had a use in the nineteen years the minute has been kept.

And about four hundred people in this town come into that room about nine times a year and about nine people in that room have ever noticed the four feet of floor between the table and the wall, and about four of the nine have noticed that it is the only part of the room that nobody has ever stood anything on, and about none of the nine has ever asked anybody what it is for.

The clerk of about sixty was at the end of the table with the minute in front of him and the other book beside it under his coat, and a bench of nine has been asked eight things in nineteen years and the count is on a page under that stair in that room and it is eight and it was eight before that Saturday and it is eight after it, and a thing that is not asked is not a refusal and is not a yes.

He had the other book there because the first of Longlight had been four weeks before and he had a second kind of minute to finish writing up, and about four people in that room knew that a clerk of about sixty now keeps two kinds and none of them knew what the second kind was for and none of them asked.

---

**And at about the fourth hour a woman of seventy-two who had come in with nobody and had sat at the end of the bench got up and stood in the aisle four feet wide, and she said one sentence, and it was twenty words, and it was said in the ordinary way at the height of a woman who is not asking anybody for anything, and nobody in that room improved on it, and nobody asked her a second question, and nobody has asked her one since and is not going to.**

**“A hearing is a column with a roof on it, and the roof is the part that does the damage.”**

That is the whole of what she said. She has no vote and no office and is not on the roll of anything and is not a member of anything and came into that room on that Saturday because there is a fire in it and about nine people were sitting in front of it.

The reeve said nothing. The magistrate said nothing. The man of fifty-five who was in for the fire said nothing, and a man of forty-seven said nothing, and about four people in that room have said since that a woman who stands up in four feet of floor in a market morning and says twenty words about a hearing to nine people and a stranger has done the only thing anybody in that county has done about a hearing in about four years, and that the bench of nine was not convened and the woman of twenty-nine four hundred yards away was not told and the man of about four hundred miles off was not asked, and that the ten people in that room are the only people in this kingdom who have heard it and about four of them did not understand a word of it and would not have understood it if they had.

She sat down again inside about a minute and a half and a man of about thirty-one who was on that bench asked her whether she wanted the fire drawn nearer, and that was the whole of the second half of the afternoon.

---

**And the clerk of about sixty entered it in the minute, in his own hand, on the day, at the hour, with no head over it, and he entered it at about the seventh hour when the bench sat, and he did not put a head on it, and a head on a thing in that minute is a thing that minute has done four hundred times in nineteen years and there is a column for it and the column is about two inches deep and has a rule at the bottom of it and the rule is no more faded than the rule at the bottom of any other.**

He read that minute back once at the end of the sitting, and only once, and the reading took about a minute and a quarter, and there is no practice in that room of reading anything twice and there never has been, and about four people in that lane have said since that a clerk who reads a minute once is a clerk who does not want to hear what it sounds like the second time, and that he has been doing it that way for nineteen years and nobody has ever asked him and he has an answer ready and it has never been wanted.

And the sentence was referred to twice afterwards by two different people in two different rooms and neither of them improved on it, and it was read once, and a thing read once in a room in a county of about four thousand people is a thing that nine people know about and about four hundred do not, and there is no column in that county for a sentence and never has been.

---

**And the eleventh day after the eighth of Longlight is that Saturday, and on that Saturday a man of thirty-two with a limp sat in a room over a wharf with a stick across his knees and the fever out of him two days before, and a version of the last eight months in which he did not do it read to him as the reasonable one.**

It is not a vision and it is not a voice and nothing in that room came into it. It is a version, and he could see the shape of it, and the shape was the shape of a thing that would have worked.

In that version the sheet is in the sill of a crossing for nine more days and then the weather takes it, and the forty yards of tarred hemp are in the dressed block four feet down where four men and a boy put them, and there is no line in that block at all, and a man of fifty-eight is in a room over a cross four hundred miles off with a book on a trestle and a trestle against a wall at the ninth hour, and a sheet of eleven lines stays in a drawer in a building two hundred miles off with his mother on the eleventh of them, and there are no three forms on any table in this county and nobody in this county or in that one ever tells him that, and a bench of nine in a fen county is asked nothing in that month or the next, and a man of fifty-five in a hut at a barrier of three bars says the same four words to about four hundred more people and is never asked where he got them, and a man of thirty-two with a limp in a room over a smithy cannot get up a bank and does not try to, because there is no reason on that side of it to try, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river go on not knowing that any of it happened.

**And that version is the shape of a thing that would have worked, and he could see all of it, and the part of it he could not get past is that in that version he is a man who cannot climb and does not have to, and that a man who cannot climb is only a difficulty in a story where something is wrong with a stone, and that the whole of what his leg is, in that version, is a thing about his leg.**

He sat with it for about a quarter of an hour. Nothing came. There was no light and no voice and no panel and nothing in the room at all, and about nine people in that room did not look at him and about four of them looked at him and thought he was asleep, and he was not asleep and he has not slept through an afternoon like that since the spring.

About the room, in that quarter of an hour, and none of it about him. **The salt was the first item and it took eleven minutes and it was the same salt that is in that minute about four hundred times in nineteen years, and a carter of thirty with a barrow and a waggon of his own was in the room for it and did not speak and the reeve did not vote on the guild's own matter and has not for nine years.**

The second item was a hedge. It is an ordinary hedge four miles up the county's own road at the corner of two fields, and the two fields either side of it are not the same field and the two parishes either side of it are not the same parish, and about nine people in that room argued about a hedge for about twenty minutes on the ordinary way, and the magistrate did not use the construction she has had ready for about a year, and a clerk of about sixty wrote down that the bench had not voted and did not write down that nobody had voted, because those are two different things and there is only one line for them.

And nobody in that room knew that the hedge the bench argued about for twenty minutes on that Saturday is the hedge a stone was set back into four weeks before, and that four people in that field had worked out that a bench of nine has no standing at a parish boundary and had said it to nobody, and that a clerk of about sixty had been in that field and had held a book shut under his arm while the whole of it was said out loud in front of him.

He got up about the eighth hour with the stick and it took him about four seconds longer than the chair did, and the bench had risen by then and about nine people were going down the outside stair two at a time, and a clerk of about sixty was at the end of the table with the minute shut and the other book under his coat.

---

He told the clerk of about sixty. That is the whole of it and it took about eleven seconds and it was forty-four words and it was said in a room with the fire in it and about four people in the room were still on the stair at the bottom of it and heard none of it.

**“There is a version of the last eight months in which I do not do it, and in that version nothing happens to anybody, and I can see the shape of it, and it is the shape of a thing that would have worked.”**

The clerk of about sixty did not write it down.

**And that clerk has entered four hundred things in a minute in nineteen years and has entered the absence of a vote once by not entering it and has entered nothing at all when a man asked him in writing what the bench had decided about a crossing, and he has never once been asked by anybody why he does not write a thing down, and he has an answer ready, and nobody in this county has ever wanted it, and the sentence he was given on that Saturday is the fourth thing a person has said to him in about a year that he did not enter, and the day-book of an office forty feet from a wagon arch has nine entries in it of a thing that was not asked and did not get a tenth on that Saturday, and a tenth is not a gift and nobody in this county is going to give him one.**

He put the minute under his arm and the other book under his coat and went down the outside stair behind the last of them, and he did not say anything about it, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river went home on that Saturday from a market with a tide under the wharf and the salt over the first bay and a fourth bay finished and pointed, and a bell a hundred yards up a road that goes at the fourth hour and a half whether anybody is on the crossing or not, and a bench of nine that was asked nothing, and a woman of seventy-two who said twenty words in four feet of floor and was asked nothing either.
