# Chapter 0288

**The twenty-fourth day of Rainmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour, and a chamber on the third floor of a building in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and about nine people in that chamber at the second hour of the second sitting of a proceeding, and a clerk of the Crown with a margin, and a man of thirty-two with a limp standing in front of a table of three, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that he spoke before he was asked and that the room let him.**

The second sitting was entered on the Friday before and there is a card for it on the board in the passage on the second floor and the card is the same colour as the fifth one and not the colour of the four that are gone, and the woman of thirty-eight took that one without asking anybody anything either, and a person who has taken two rooms off that board without asking is a person who has been told what is in both of them is available.

---

**There are about nine of them in that chamber. The woman of thirty-eight is in one of the two chairs and is thirty-eight and is of the office that convened this. A clerk of the Crown is on the stool. Perry Hask, 34, is in the second chair and is there because he is a clerk and not because he is anything else. A man of about fifty who has been a clerk of a press for thirty-one years is at the back. A man of about thirty-one with a satchel is at the back. Alish Renn is at the back and has been in that town since the ninth of a month and is at the back again.**

The room has no window. That is the room, and every other room on that floor with a table in it has a window nine feet off a wall, and this one is at the end of it and takes its light off two candles in tin holders on the wall and has taken its light off candles in that room for about nine years, and a room with no window is the only kind of room in it where a thing said cannot be seen to have been said by anybody in the street, and this is a trade arrangement and not an accident.

He was sworn on the back of a return and the oath is the same sheet the returns go out on and it is not the front, and a man who has been sworn three times on the same piece of paper has been made to sign a page of a form three times in his life, and a finding is a piece of paper with a day on it.

And nobody asked him anything for about a minute and a half, and the minute and a half is on the clerk's margin, and a minute and a half of a room is not a long time and is the longest thing that has happened in that chamber, and the clerk of the Crown did not fill it and did not hurry it and entered nothing at all in it.

And then he said it, before anybody asked him for any part of it, and before the order of the marshal's office was read, and it is eleven sentences, and he did not improve on one of them and he did not stop in the middle of one.

“**On the eighth day of a month in Longlight, at about the second hour, I put my hand on a thing in a crossing four hundred yards up a county's own road. There was a sheet in the sill of that crossing with a woman's name in the fourth column of it, and I did not know the woman. I knew the hand. I put a hand on that crossing on the strength of a line somebody else wrote and I had asked nobody. There was no keeper at my shoulder and there was no rope and there was no second person in that room anywhere. I was not asked and I did not ask. It took the anchor and not the link, and forty yards of hemp that four men and a boy had put into that stone four feet down went with it. Nobody in that county heard anything happen and a bell a hundred yards off went at the fourth hour and a half the same morning. I have not been able to get up that bank since the seventh day of that month and I am not going to. There is a man of fifty-eight four hundred miles off who is not coming back and nobody in that valley is going to be told why. That is what I did and that is all of it, and I have a sentence ready and I am not going to say that one either.**”

---

**And a clerk of the Crown made a stroke in the margin against each of the eleven, and there are eleven strokes in that margin and the space after the eleventh one is ruled and it is empty, and there is no rule after a twelfth one because a clerk of the Crown does not rule a space for a thing that has not been said.**

**And the woman of thirty-eight said one thing, and it was eleven words, and it was said at waist height to the clerk of the Crown and not to the man who had spoken, and a woman who has spent nineteen sentences asking that a record show what was asked has just heard eleven sentences and answered them with eleven words addressed to the man writing them down, and that is the correct procedure, and about four people in that chamber have not worked out yet whether that is the whole of it.**

“**Enter that he was not asked, as a thing that happened,**” she said.

“**There is no line for it,**” the clerk of the Crown said.

“**Then put it in the margin,**” she said, “**and the margin is the part people read.**”

And the clerk of the Crown wrote it in the margin under the eleven strokes, in about four seconds, in a hand that is not the office's, and it is the twelfth mark in that margin and it is the only one of the twelve that is not a stroke against a sentence, and nobody in that chamber has read that margin since and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a room wrote down the fact that it had not asked a man a question.

He did not read it back. He did not read it back twice, and there is no practice in that chamber of reading anything twice and there never has been, and a clerk who writes a thing down once and stops is a clerk who has decided that the room has heard it, and a room which has heard a thing and has it in a margin is a room that can go on without hearing it again.

Nobody improved on any of the eleven. Nobody asked him why he began the way he began, and nobody in that chamber is going to, and there is no line in that proceeding for a reason a witness had before he stood up.

And he had four words ready that he has had since a day he cannot count, and they were ready in a room with no window in it in a town in this province in the second month of this year, and he did not say them in that room and he did not say them in this one, and a man who has carried four words since a day he cannot count and has not said them in two rooms has not been asked about them in either and is not going to be.

**A witness who says the thing nobody asked for first has given the room the finding before the room can make one, and this is the correct procedure, and it is also the only procedure anybody in this kingdom has ever heard of, and a woman of thirty-eight who spent nineteen sentences in Thawmonth asking that the record show what was asked has just watched the best possible version of that ask being performed in front of her by a witness, and it did not work.**

She did not stop him. She has not stopped anybody since the second of a month in Thawmonth and she entered the room as a room in the minute that afternoon, in her own hand, and the finding at the end of that proceeding is a figure, and she has known that since the fourteenth of a month in Thawmonth and entered the room anyway, and being correct is the most expensive thing available to a person in a chamber and nobody in that chamber has said so to her and she has not said it to anybody.

---

**And the order was read at about the fourth hour, and it is an order of the marshal's office, and the man who wrote it did not stand up to read it, and a clerk of the Crown read it out and it is a sheet and it has a number of an office against it and nothing at all against a person, and a man of about thirty-one with a satchel said, in eleven words, that he had never once known what was in the four places, and nobody in that room asked him a question about it.**

And nobody asked the man who wrote the order a single question, and that man has never been surprised by anything in about nine years and is not going to be surprised by a question, and he was not in that room and there is no line in that proceeding for a person who is not in the room, and the only person in this kingdom who would have got a different thing out of that room is not in it and did not come and will not.

A woman of about thirty has been in that building for three weeks and she is at the back of that chamber and the office has entered a second sitting and has entered no third, and a warrant to produce names a room and a day and does not name a person, and a person who came two hundred miles to produce a page is now a person an office cannot say it is keeping, and there is no column in that proceeding for a person, and the list on the wall has a column with a head on it and that column is not one anybody in that chamber is being asked to fill in on that afternoon.

---

And a man of about fifty at the back of that chamber has been entered as a witness twice and asked nothing twice, and a true thing about a column that he said out loud in that building in about two years was not thanked then and has not been thanked since, and the thanks is what makes a thing a finding, and a man who is not thanked for saying a true thing is a man who has said it for nothing, and a man who has said a true thing for nothing in a building of about nine hundred people is a man who has done the whole of the work and gone home and not known that he was never asked.

Nobody walked any part of that Saturday with anybody. About a hundred and forty people were in a market in that province and about four hundred people were in a market in a county two hundred miles off, and about nine hundred people in that town did not know that a room heard a man say eleven sentences about a thing he had done in a county they have never heard of, and that the finding at the end of it is a figure, and that the figure is the ordinary work of an office that has been doing this nine times in nine years.
