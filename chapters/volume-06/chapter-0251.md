# Chapter 0251

**The twenty-fifth day of Goatmonth, YR 315, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and a half and the sixth hour, and a market four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane from it with about forty men on it and the road open over it and the salt coming over the first bay, and a bell with a band of forty-one rivets in it that went at the fourth hour and a half by a boy and a carpenter, and about four hundred people in this town who do not know that a thing with a door and a number on it has been named in a room two hundred miles off, and one of the four hundred is forty feet from a door on a lane and is not the one it will come to.**

The market was on from the second hour until about the sixth, and there was salt in it and fish and peat and a load of glass that had come up the channel road behind nine waggons since the spring, and a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at a bench-board on the reach until the fourth hour and a half and then went down four hundred yards of lane to a house in Weir Street with a stick and a coat over his arm, and about four hundred people in that market worked out in the ordinary way that he has been in this county five months and has been called something in four counties and has never asked what any of the four means, which is a thing about four people and not a thing about him.

**And the ordinary post came up the lane at about the sixth hour, and it came eleven times a week in the season and nine times a week now, and one of the things in it was a sheet folded once across the middle with a seal on the fold that was not wax and was not a mark, and the sheet was put on the sill of a table in a room over a weigh-house at the north end of that lane, and a woman of twenty-nine read it standing up.**

The weigh-house under those two rooms takes the tide up through its boards in a wet week and this was not a wet week, and the two rooms are the salt merchant's and are two shillings a month each, and the office that sits in them pays fourpence a year, and no clerk in that office has ever added one of those three figures to another of them and is not going to.

The sheet is a summons. It is printed on the office's own printed series, and the series is ruled in six columns, five of them with heads, and the heads are *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper*, and *How often*, and a sixth with no head on it and the same width as the other five, and about four of the six are ruled for a figure.

The heads of the five are not on the front of this sheet. They are on the back of it, in a smaller type, above a form of oath, and a person who swears on this sheet is sworn on the back of a return, and the words at the foot of the back in a smaller type again are nine words and they have been the same nine words for two years and nobody in that building has ever asked whose they are or where they came from, and about two or three of them think about it perhaps twice a year.

**And a body corporate is named in it, and the thing that is named is a body corporate with a door on a lane, a number, and the capacity to sue and to be sued, and it was made out of a deed of thirteen words: *for the keeping of what this county cannot be shown it has lost.***

Thirteen words, and there is no officer in them and no name and no person, and a body corporate made out of a deed that names no officer is a body corporate that a person can be served and that has nobody to receive the service, and four people in that lane have worked that out in the ordinary way on the evening of a market day and none of the four has said it to the other three.

The summons wants three things, and it wants them in the order it wants them. It wants the register produced. It wants the day-book produced. And it wants a person having knowledge of the thing in the object column to attend on a day in Rainmonth at a room in a provincial town two hundred miles off. It does not say what is in the object column, and it does not say what the register is a register of, and it gives a date and a room and a person to swear in front of, and those three are the whole of it.

**And there is a line on the sheet for the person served and a line for the office, and the two lines are the same length, and one of the two is ruled for a figure and the other is ruled for a figure too, and neither of them is ruled for a name.**

Nobody had ever been served at that door before this morning. That is the whole of what four years and a half had bought that office, and it is not a trick and it was not planned and it is the shape of a thing rather than a thing. In four years and a half nobody had come up that lane with a paper in a hand, because a county of about four thousand people cannot be served with anything, has no writ and no seal and no office, and a bench of nine has been asked eight things in nineteen years and has not been asked a ninth.

And a woman of twenty-nine read a summons at the sill of a table and did not sit down, and four people in that lane have said since that a woman who reads a paper standing up is a woman who is in a hurry to be past it, and that the copyist in that room reads standing up because standing up is faster and the two of them have never compared notes about it and neither of them is going to.

---

**And the carrier who brought it up the lane did not wait for it to be read, and what the service cost is a column of four items that a woman of twenty-nine kept on the sill of that table in pencil and added up in front of the second copyist, because a woman who keeps a day-book adds a column up in front of somebody.**

A carrier of about fifty with a waggon of his own has carried the ordinary post up that lane for nine years and he came up it on the twenty-fifth day of Goatmonth with the sheet on the seat beside him and a waggon he had to keep one hour because a house on that lane is not a house and has no yard to stand in, and he left the sheet on the sill and went back down four hundred yards of lane at about the sixth hour and a quarter.

A sheet of the office's printed series is a penny. A second sheet for the person served, which is this office's own practice and is not the practice of the office that sent it, is a penny. A carrier's hand for four miles is twopence. A waggon kept an hour is four pence. **A penny and a penny is twopence, and twopence and twopence is fourpence, and fourpence and fourpence is eight pence, and the whole of the service of a summons on a body corporate in this county came to eight pence on the twenty-fifth day of Goatmonth, YR 315.**

The fourpence a year that the office pays is not in that column and the two shillings a month that the salt merchant charges for each of the two rooms are not in that column either, and those two prices are the price of the same two rooms and they have never been added to one another in this office in four years and a half and are not going to be.

**And a second copyist of about thirty looked at the four items for about four seconds and said that the carrier kept the hour, and a woman of twenty-nine said the carrier kept the hour, and neither of them said anything about the other three items, and four people in that lane have said since that a column of four items added in front of somebody is not a receipt and is a decision, and that the decision was to let the second copyist see the arithmetic, and that nobody has entered the arithmetic anywhere except on a sill in pencil.**

---

**And what she did about it took about nine minutes and it is the whole of the twenty-fifth day of Goatmonth, YR 315 in that room, and she did it in the ordinary way and did not improve on it afterwards.**

The register came off the shelf first and she did not open it, and that is the second thing about the nine minutes. A keeper of records who is served with a summons to produce a register takes the register off the shelf and puts it on the table and does not open it, and the register has five entries in it and all five of them begin with a district name and all five of them were entered as a question and none of them was entered as a finding, and the fifth one went in under the rule at about the sixth hour of the eighteenth of Goatmonth in about four minutes and is the first of the five that names a day.

She put it on the table with the spine toward her and did not turn it round, and four people in that lane have said since that a book put on a table spine toward the person who owns it is a book that is being kept shut in a room where there is nobody to hand it to.

**And there are two presses within four hundred yards of that door in this county, and about four people in that lane have worked out what the difference between them is, and neither of the two women in that room has said it, and a summons was put down on a table four feet from the one that can be served with and neither of the two women looked at the other one.**

The press in that office has about four hundred documents in it and every one of them can be produced, on the order of a body corporate, to a person who asks in a form. The other is in a room four hundred yards up Weir Street with a bench-board in it and a boy of fifteen at the bench-board, and there are four sheets of printed paper in it with four empty places in them and a place name in the object column and a word in front of the place name that nobody in this county can read the sense of, and that press belongs to a yard and not to an office and nobody can be served with anything out of it and nobody has ever tried.

A body corporate that can be served with a summons is a body corporate that can be served with anything at all, and the four sheets in the other press cannot be got out of it by anybody, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has stood in this county for five months with a hand on the outside of that press for about four minutes and did not open it, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that a seal was put on a fold at the sixth hour and that a boy of fifteen at a bench-board four hundred yards up the same lane did not know anything about it and has not asked and is not going to.

She did not enter a name. That is the third thing and it is the whole of it, and she did not write *no person* either, and four people in that lane have said since that those two are the only two answers available to a person standing at a table with a line ruled for a figure and a person in the room, and that a keeper of records who has five questions in a register has had about five months to work out that there is a third answer and has not entered it and is not going to.

What she entered was the day, and the hour, and the door. Three items, in that order, on the fair copy and not on the working, and the fair copy is the copy that anybody coming up that lane may read, and the working is the copy that stays in the office, and the two of them lie on a bench about four inches apart and a man of thirty-two with a limp has stood in that room nine times and has looked at both of them and could not say which of the two is the dangerous one.

**And a second copyist of about thirty came into that room at about the sixth hour and a half with a tray in her hand, and put it on the table, and looked at the summons, and did not ask what it was, and four people in that lane have said since that a second copyist of six years who sees a summons on a sill and does not ask what it is has understood something about it in about a second and a half, and that what she has understood is that a question about a summons is a thing that gets entered.**

“**Is that a summons.**”

“It is a summons,” the woman of twenty-nine said.

“**To us.**”

“**To the body corporate.**”

“**There is a difference and I have never worked out what it is and I have been in this room six years.**”

---

**And a thing nobody in that room had done in about two years was done at about the seventh hour, and it was done without any decision, and it is the only thing in the twenty-fifth day of Goatmonth, YR 315 that anybody in that lane has found out about since.**

There was a letter on the second shelf. There have been ten letters on that shelf in about two years and a second copyist of about thirty has walked past that shelf four times a day since the summer before last, and she has not opened the tenth of the ten, and the nine before it were opened inside a week of arriving and it has been on that shelf since a day nobody in that room has written down.

She said one thing about it and it was in her own voice and it is the only thing she has said in six years that was not about a post she did not want, and she said it to the shelf and not to the woman of twenty-nine.

“**The day I open the eleventh is the day this office is a body corporate that goes through a county's post, and I have said that out loud once and I am not going to say it again, and there is a tenth one here and I have known that for about two years.**”

Nobody answered her and she did not open the tenth one, and the woman of twenty-nine did not tell her to open it, and four people in that lane have said since that two women stood about eleven feet apart in a room over a weigh-house on the last day of a year and neither of them said the obvious thing to the other one, and that the obvious thing was that a body corporate that has been named in a room two hundred miles off is a body corporate that can be served, and a body corporate that can be served is a body corporate that a letter can go to, and that this office has had a door on a lane for four years and a half for exactly the other reason, and that a seal on a fold is not a knock.

**And at the seventh hour the lane was quiet and the market had gone in at about the sixth and the reach had forty men on it at about the fourth hour and a bell with a band of forty-one rivets in it had gone at the fourth hour and a half by a boy and a carpenter who are neither paid by the guild nor members of anything, and about four hundred people in this town do not know that a sheet of printed paper was put on a sill at the sixth hour and read standing up, and one of the four hundred is a man of thirty-two with a limp in a house forty feet from that door and he did not know until the sixth hour of the following day and he found out from a man of forty-seven who had not been asked to carry anything and who said it out loud in a kitchen once and did not say it twice.**
