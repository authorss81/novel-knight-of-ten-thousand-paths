# Chapter 0296

**The fourth day of Fallowmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the third hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, and two rooms over a weigh-house at the end of a lane in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and about four hundred people in that town in a street four hundred yards long, and a reach four hundred yards down another lane with about forty men on it, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that a cart brought a sheet up that lane on the previous afternoon and that a woman of twenty-nine has had it on a table since and has not turned it over.**

She is twenty-nine and has kept that office four years and a half, out of two rooms over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, on a deed of thirteen words, for fourpence a year, and in that office the word keeper is a thing a person is and not a thing a person can do to anybody else.

She turned it over at about the third hour on a Thursday morning in the fourth month of that autumn, and it is a sheet of a printed series of that office's own making, two hundred miles off, six columns and five heads, and the first column has a place in it written the way that office writes a place, which is a set of market towns, and the second column has a figure in it, and the head over the second column says *Date*.

---

**And the figure is a figure of persons, and there is no name in the sheet anywhere, and the set of market towns in the first column has a word standing in front of it on the copy in that building and there is nothing standing in front of it on this one, and nobody in that building has been asked why.**

The figure is larger than the number of people in that town. She knows the number of people in that town the way everybody in a town of four thousand knows it, which is badly, and by three or four hundred either way, and this is about nine hundred more than what she would have said.

“**That is more than us,**” she said.

“**It is what they have,**” the reeve said. She had come up the two flights at about the third hour without knocking, and she does not vote on the guild's own matter and has not for nine years.

“**It is more than us.**”

“**Then somebody has counted the wrong thing,**” the reeve said, and got to a true thing by saying the wrong one first, and has been doing it for as long as she has held the office, and says it is the only thing in the office she is good at, and the true thing was the second thing said in that room and it took about a minute and a half to arrive at.

“**There is nothing in that number to be wrong,**” the keeper of records said. “**It is a figure against a set of market towns and there is no way in this county to make it less. You cannot refuse a figure. You cannot check one. There is no writ in this county and no seal and no office and nothing at all to show a thing to, and I have looked.**”

“**Then what do we do.**”

“**Nothing,**” the keeper of records said, “**and nothing is the whole of what this office has ever been able to do, and it has been doing it for four years and a half, and I have a book for it.**”

“**You have a book for doing nothing.**”

“**There is a day-book,**” the keeper of records said, “**and nothing in this building can be served out of it, and there are nine things written in it that nobody asked, and there is not going to be a tenth, and if one came it would not be a present.**”

And the reeve went down the two flights and did not improve on that, and nobody in that building or in that town has worked out since what a keeper of records said to a reeve at about the fifth hour of a Thursday in the fourth month of that autumn, and that about four hundred people in a street four hundred yards away were doing a market at the time.

---

The sheet says that the record entered against that set of market towns is contested, and it does not say by whom, and it does not say what the record is, and it gives a day by which cause may be shown to an office two hundred miles off, and the day is about eleven weeks off, and there is no line on that sheet for a person and no column in that office that a county can send an answer in.

A county of about four thousand people with three kinds of paper in it has looked at a fourth, and there is nothing a person in that county can put on it. A claim in that office is a claim about a person, and the thing that is contested is a figure, and the two of those cannot be made into one another by anybody who has not got a column for it, and there is no column, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that there is a day.

She put the sheet on the shelf with the register and not in the press, and the shelf is the shelf and not the press, and the press in that room can be served with anything at all by anybody who walks up that lane and a press four hundred yards up Weir Street cannot, and nobody in that lane was in the room when she did it, and a woman who has now twice decided where a piece of paper goes has entered neither decision and is not going to.

---

A man of thirty-two with a limp came into that room at about the sixth hour, stood in it about a quarter of an hour, and asked nothing in it at all.

He had a coat over a chair and the chair was nine feet off him and there was something in the inside pocket of that coat, and he did not take it out, and a man owns a coat and puts paper in it and does not put paper on a table, and nobody in that county has asked him about the coat and nobody is going to.

He took it out at about the sixth hour and a half. There were eleven lines on a page with no words in them, and the eleventh of them is a man on a shift nineteen years old, and he cannot stop seeing it and did not see it that afternoon, and there is a separate leaf with a seventh line on it that says *a person*, and he looked at that leaf for about a minute and a quarter and put it back.

He has looked at that leaf five times in five months and finished never, and that afternoon was the sixth time, and it is not finished, and a man who has looked at one line of paper six times in six months has not finished it, and there is nobody in that county he could finish it with, and the seventh line is a question, and the sixth time is the closest he has come to it, and nobody in that county has ever been told there is a question.

His left palm was warm at about the fourth hour and had been warm at about the fourth hour in that county for two years and nobody in that county knows that.

---

---

The register is on the shelf with the sheet and it has five entries in it and all five begin with a set of market towns and all five were entered as a question and none as a finding, and the fifth is dated, and it has been about four months, and nobody in that office has read that register out loud to anybody in four years and a half, including the woman who keeps it.

A question is a thing that goes out. A finding is a thing that is produced. A county with five questions in a book and no answer to any of them has a register and not a roll, and a register cannot be sworn to and cannot be produced in a room a long way off without somebody putting a day against it, and there is nobody in that office who is going to, and about four people in that town have said since that a body corporate with a door and a number on it has been asked five questions by an office two hundred miles off and has answered none of them and has not asked anybody to, and that this is the only kind of paper in this kingdom that cannot be refused, and that nobody in that county has ever said so out loud.

**She got the register down at about the seventh hour and put the contested sheet beside it and looked at the two of them together for about four seconds, and then she put the sheet back on the shelf and the register back on the shelf, and nothing was entered, and the day-book was not opened at all that day.**

Four hundred yards down that lane about forty men were on the reach with the road open over it and the salt over the first bay since the spring, a man of fifty and a half was on the second bay with a bar he was not using, and a boy of fifteen was at a bench-board with a bill under his arm and no bill on the board, and four hundred yards the other way a yard on Weir Street had about nine men in it and a press in a room at the end of it that no one can serve anything on, and a bundle has been in that press for years with nothing written on the outside of it, and it is not in that yard's book and never will be, and nobody in that yard has spoken to anybody in the lane above it this year.

---

Nobody walked any part of that Thursday with anybody. About four hundred people were in a street four hundred yards long and did a market, and a salt waggon came up the channel road and stood at the end of it about an hour, and a woman of about thirty with a pail went up that lane at about the fifth hour and stopped at nine doors and said one thing at each of them, and at the door forty feet from a wagon arch she said that a cart had come up the lane on the Tuesday and had gone to a room at the end of it, and that nobody had asked the cart what it had brought, and she said it to the door, and there are eight doors behind her in that lane and nobody in that lane has ever asked her why she says things at doors, and a reach four hundred yards down that lane had about forty men on it with the road open over it and the salt over the first bay, and the tide was under the wharf, and a bell a hundred yards up that road went at the fourth hour and a half, and a county of about four thousand people was entered in a schedule it did not know existed nine weeks before the day it was told about it.
