# Chapter 0291

**The ninth day of Harvestmonth, YR 316, a Sunday, and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, and two rooms over a weigh-house at the end of a lane in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and a reach four hundred yards down another lane with about forty men on it, and a market street four hundred yards long with nobody standing in it, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that the one office in that county that anything at all can be served with stood open on a Sunday, and that a person came up that lane and knocked on it.**

A market street with nobody in it on a Sunday is not an empty street. It is a street with the trestles folded and the awnings chained and about nine sweeps in it, and the sweeps are there on a Saturday as well, and a town of about four thousand people does not stop on a Sunday, it goes to about nine doors and to a field about a mile off and to a mill, and about four hundred of them were out of the house and nobody in the county knew where.

---

A number is cut into that door and the year of the deed is cut in under it, and the sum is fourpence a year, and fourpence a year is the entire cost, to that county, of four years and a half of being a thing that can be named. No other sum has ever been written down in that room and no one in that room has ever thought of adding one to it.

It is open on a Sunday because a person came up the lane and knocked on it at about the first hour, and a body corporate that can be served with anything by anybody who walks up a lane cannot shut on a Sunday without deciding something, and the woman who keeps it did not decide anything, and she went down and opened the door.

There was a woman at it with a pail.

“**I said at this door on Friday that a man had come up the lane,**” she said.

“**I did not hear you,**” the keeper of records said.

“**You were not in,**” the woman said. “**I said it to the door.**”

“**I am in now,**” the keeper of records said.

And the woman put the pail down on the step and stood there, and there are about nine doors in that lane and she had been at eight of them before this one that morning, and she had said one thing at each of them, and the thing she said at this one is that she said a thing at this one on Friday, and the keeper of records is twenty-nine and has run that office four years and a half out of a deed of thirteen words and did not ask her why she says things at doors.
“**What did you tell the other eight,**” she said.

“**That a man had come up the lane,**” the woman said, “**and that a room was open on a Sunday, and that I have not told anybody else that there is a room.**”

And she took the pail up and went on down the lane, and a woman who says one thing at nine doors and has now told a keeper of records that she has not told anybody else there is a room has said a second thing in a year, and the second thing was said to the one person in that county who could do something about it and did not.

---

The second copyist is about thirty and she goes on the Monday at about the sixth hour in a cart down to the market and then on down a made road, and she had been in that room three days and had been in the only room she has ever worked in for about six years, and she has wanted a line in the day-book with her own name on it since the summer before last, and she said that once, out loud, in a kitchen in that county, and nobody in that building has said it since and nobody is going to.

She was not packing. There was nothing to pack. A woman who is going two hundred miles and has been in a room three days and has nothing in a bag has not been in three rooms.

“**Is the cart at six,**” she said.

“**It is at six,**” the keeper of records said.

“**I have not written to anybody,**” the second copyist said.

“**You do not have to,**” the keeper of records said.

“**That is not the same thing,**” the second copyist said, and the keeper of records did not answer it, and about four people in that town have said since that two women in a room over a weigh-house have said two sentences on a Sunday morning about a cart and neither of them is about the cart, and that the third post in that day-book is on a warrant held by a second keeper of twenty-six and it is not signed, and that the two of them have never once raised it and were not going to on a Sunday.

The day-book is the only book in that office nobody can be served with. It has nine entries in it of a thing that was not asked, and it is on a shelf with the register and not in the press, and the register has five entries in it and all five begin with a set of market towns and all five were entered as a question and none as a finding, and the fifth is dated, and it has been about four months.

“**You have not read that minute again,**” the second copyist said, at about the second hour.

“**No,**” the keeper of records said.

“**It came on Saturday and it is the first paper that has come up that lane for anything in four years and a half, and you have not read it again.**”

“**I read it once on Saturday and I put it in the press on Saturday, and a press in that room is a press anybody can be served with, and I did that because that is what the press in that room is for.**”

“**And this morning.**”

“**And this morning I have taken it out of the press,**” the keeper of records said, “**and put it on the shelf with the register, and the shelf is the shelf and not the press, and I did that at about the first hour and there was nobody in the room and there is no line in that office for where a thing is put.**”

And the second copyist was in the room and saw her do it and said nothing about it, and a woman who sees a decision made and says nothing about it is a woman who has decided the same thing, and the two of them have not said one word to each other about where that sheet is.

---

The reeve came up the two flights at about the third hour on a Sunday, which is not a thing she does.

“**They were in your room on Saturday,**” she said.

“**They were in my room on Saturday,**” the keeper of records said.

“**With a bag.**”

“**He had one on his shoulder,**” the keeper of records said.

“**Was it a warrant.**”

“**It was a minute of a court,**” the keeper of records said, “**and a minute of a court is not a warrant and it is not served and there is no seal on it against anybody and it did not name this county.**”

“**Then it is somebody else's business,**” the reeve said, which is the way she gets to a true thing, and she has been doing it for as long as she has held the office and says it is the only thing in the office she is good at.

“**It is about a place, sitting in a column, two hundred miles off,**” the keeper of records said, “**and there is nothing in this county that a column could be shown to. This building keeps a claim and a day-book and that is the whole of the paper.**”

“**A third kind,**” the reeve said.

“**There is a third kind on a shelf,**” the keeper of records said, “**and there is a register, and a register is the second kind twice over, and nobody has ever put a day against a question in it in four years and a half except one.**”

And the reeve went down the two flights and did not improve on any of that, and a woman who gets to a true thing in a county of about four thousand people by saying the wrong one first did it on a Sunday morning in about ninety seconds, and nobody in that lane was in the room and nobody has worked out since that a thing was decided on a Sunday in about a minute and a half with nobody in the room to see it.

---

Four hundred yards down the lane there were about forty men on the reach and the road was open over it and the salt had been coming over the first bay since the spring, and the fourth bay was finished and pointed and had been finished for weeks and nobody in that yard had anything left to do on it that is on a bill.

A man of fifty and a half who does the arches and does the pointing and does not do the laying was on the third bay with a bar he was not using, and a boy of fifteen at a bench-board four feet off the ground had a bill under his arm and nothing on the board.

“**There is no work on that,**” the man said.

“**There is a number under it,**” the boy said.

“**Then put down what is on the board,**” the man said, and came down the ladder, and the boy put down the number and then put down the empty space under it, which is the widest of the four things on that board, and he has copied that space eleven times and has never once been told what goes in it, and nothing goes in it, and that is what the space is there for.

A man of about thirty-one with a satchel came up the market end of that lane at about the fourth hour with about nine sheets in the satchel and did not go down it, because a carrier's round in that county is on a market morning and this was not a market morning, and about four hundred people in that town did not see him go past.

And the bell went at the fourth hour and a half, and it goes on a Sunday, and nobody in that county has ever asked who keeps it, and there is no answer in that county and the answer nobody can give is the finding.

---

A man of thirty-two with a limp was in a room over a smithy and had been in it since the sixth day of a month in a year in which the year turns, and the fever had been out of him eleven days and his right leg had not got up a bank since the seventh and was not going to.

He had a form folded once in a coat and the coat was over a chair and the chair was nine feet off him, and he did not take it out on a Sunday.

His left palm was warm at about the fourth hour, and it had been warm at about the fourth hour in that county for two years, and nobody in that county knows that it is warm, and it is the only thing about him that two hundred miles off in a room with a stove in it would have been felt by anybody in it, and it is not felt in a room with a stove in it and was never going to be.

---

Nobody walked any part of that Sunday with anybody. About four hundred people went to about nine doors and to a field about a mile off and to a mill, and a room over a weigh-house stood open from the first hour to the fourth and then shut, and a room over a smithy had a man in it and a form in a coat on a chair nine feet off him, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane had about forty men on it, and the tide was under the wharf, and the salt was over the first bay, and on Thursday of that week a man with a satchel will come up that lane and put nine sheets of a printed series on nine sills in it, and the ninth of the nine is the same sill it went on nine days before with a stone off the bank on it, and the stone is still on that sill, and nobody has gone back for it.
