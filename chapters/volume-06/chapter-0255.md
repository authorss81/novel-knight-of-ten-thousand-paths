# Chapter 0255

**The eighth day of Thawmonth, YR 316, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour, and a chamber two floors above a room with a stove in it in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and a long table in it that is not the table of three, and a schedule of about two hundred and forty lines on it, and a record with a head on it that says which office the schedule is for, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that the thing which is going to be classified was read on the eighth of Thawmonth, YR 316 in about two hours and ten minutes by a man of forty-four who was not asked anything.**

He came in at about the second hour and he was early and there is a thing about that, which is that a man who is early to a reading of a schedule has done the reading before in his head, and four people in that building have said since that a man of forty-four who has been in that room twice before and is early both times is a man who is afraid of a column and not of a person, and that nobody in that building has ever worked out how those two are different and about four of them have stopped trying.

**Ansel Rudd, 44, a member of the Culling Order, and a classifier, and the office is on the head of the record in a line with the other heads and it is not the office that sent the return or the office that wrote the proceeding or the office the woman of thirty-eight works for, and four people in that building have said since that there are four offices in one building and that two of them are on paper and two of them are on a floor, and that nobody has ever put a floor and a paper together in that building and one of the two on the floor is reading a schedule and one of the two on paper put that body corporate in a pigeonhole twelve days ago.**

The head of the record says: *Read into the record, at the second hour, of the office of the Culling Order, of the classifications of the kingdom made under the standing instruction, to be complete before the first of Longlight.*

That is the phrase, and it is on the head of a record in a smaller type than the schedule, and it is not a threat and it is not a doctrine and it is not spoken in that room by anybody in a voice, and the one person in that room who could have said it out loud did not say it out loud until about the fourth hour and then said it to a clerk about a tray.

---

**And the room for two hours is a room with a man reading at one end of a long table and a clerk copying at the other, and the copying is the whole trade. A schedule of two hundred and forty lines read aloud and taken down by hand is the ordinary way of moving a fact from one office to another, and it has been that way for a long time, and nobody in that building has ever asked what happens to a schedule between the reading and the copying.**

The clerk was a young man with a bad wrist who had the good hand on the pen, and he had done about two hundred and thirty lines in about two hours. Two hundred and thirty lines in two hours is a rate, and it is the rate a man with a satchel has kept for two years in the same province, and nobody has ever put the two rates in one column, because there is no column that takes a rate of copying and a rate of carrying.

The woman of thirty-eight sat at the end of the table with a sheet of her own and wrote down three things in the two hours and the three things were the time, the place and the number of lines. A Crown officer who has asked in nineteen sentences for a record to show what was asked has spent two hours writing down when and where and how much. The three are not the nineteen. She has not noticed that they are not the nineteen and she will not find out until a room somewhere makes a finding of it.

The light came in at about the fourth hour on a window that faces a wall nine feet off, and a man of forty-four read to the end of the line and then to the end of the page and then turned the page, and the turning took about a second and a half and it happened about two hundred times. A page is the only thing in a schedule that anybody turns. A person does not.

---

**And he read what was in front of him, in the order it was printed, for about two hours and about ten minutes, and he read it the way a man reads a roll of names at a door, and four people in that building have said since that a man who reads two hundred and forty lines aloud in a room is doing a thing that is older than any office in that building, and that a person who is going to argue with a line cannot argue with a line that is being read.**

He read the object column first. That is the trade and everybody in that room knows it and four people in that building have said since that the object column is the first one anybody fills in because it is the only one of the six that can be filled in without a person being in the room, and that the office prints them in that order for that reason and has never said so and does not have to.

The object column on that schedule is about nine inches wide and it has a place in it in most of the two hundred and forty lines and the places are rivers and fords and lines of stones and places where a road goes under a bridge, and in about nine of the two hundred and forty lines there is a word in front of the place name, and the word is in a type that is the same type as the heads of the five columns, and four people in that building have said since that a word in an object column in front of a place name is a word the office put in the case and has never struck, and that nobody in that building has ever asked what it is for and that two or three of them think about it perhaps twice a year.

He read the nine of them as he read the other two hundred and thirty-one, and he read them the same, and four people in that room have said since that a man reading nine lines out of two hundred and forty the way he reads the rest is a man who is not making a point, and that there is no way to tell from the outside whether a man is not making a point or cannot see the nine.

---

**And he said three things in that room and every one of the three was about a column, and he did not say them together, and the woman of thirty-eight was in the room for all three and did not improve on any of them and did not ask him a single question about any of the three.**

The first was at about the third hour and it was about the first column.

“**The object column is the only one of the six a person can fill in alone, and that is not an accident, and I am not going to write in it twice in a day, because a thing written twice in a day is a thing somebody is going to be asked about in about a fortnight.**”

The second was at about the third hour and a half and it was about the fifth.

“**That head has been the same head for two years. I am not going to change it. A man who changes a head in his own hand is a man who has made a decision, and I have not made one, and I would like it noticed that I have not.**”

The third was at about the fourth hour and it was about the sixth.

“**There is no head on the sixth and so it is not a column, and anything a person puts in it is a thing this building decided not to keep, and about four of us have known that for two years and about four hundred forms a month go out of this building with a blank one on them.**”

Nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him and four people in that building have said since that a man of forty-four who says three true things in a room about three different columns and is not answered to any of them has been in nine rooms in nine years and has been answered to none of them and has not found out why, and that the reason is on the sixth column and is not about him.

---

**And the woman of thirty-eight asked him one question in that room and it was at about the fourth hour and it was not about the four places and it was the only question she asked him, and he answered it in twelve words and did not explain it and she wrote the twelve words down.**

“**Is a crossing a place or a thing.**”

“**It is a thing. A place is what the thing is at.**”

She wrote it on her own sheet under the three items and did not read it back. A woman who has spent nineteen sentences asking for a record to show what was asked has just been given the answer in twelve words by a man who was reading at the time. The twelve words are a definition, and there is no column in that building for a definition, and a definition is the only thing a record cannot carry and the only thing a person can use.

**And he went back to the line he was on and neither of them said anything else about it, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that the description which is going to be written down in a field in this county in about twelve weeks is going to be made by a person who has never been to the county, out of a column with a head on it, on a word at the front of it that the office printed and has never used.**

---

**And the want was said at about the fourth hour to a clerk who brought a tray in at that hour and was about to go out with it, and it was thirty-four words, and it is the only thing he said in that room that was about a thing he wanted, and he did not say it again in that building in about nine days and is not going to.**

“**A classification that is late is a classification that can be argued with, and I have argued with nine of them in a year and I would rather be late than argue with ten.**”

The clerk said nothing and went out with the tray, and four people in that building have said since that a clerk who hears a want in a doorway and carries a tray out of a room has taken it out of the room with him and that is what a tray is for, and that nobody in that building has asked that clerk what he heard and about two of them have wanted to and have not.

**And nobody in that room asked the man of forty-four what is in the four places on the form, and nobody noticed that nobody had asked him, and the reason nobody asked is that everybody in that room has been in a room with a form in it for about nine years and has never once wanted to know what is in the blank parts of a thing they fill in every day, and about four people in that building worked that out on the ninth and tenth days of Thawmonth, YR 316, separately, and about four hours apart, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that there was anything in it to work out.**

**And the schedule went into the record at about the fourth hour and a half, and it is a schedule of about two hundred and forty classifications and a frequency against each and a keeper against each, and it is in a record in a room in a building two hundred miles from a fen county, and the second bay of a bridge four hundred yards down a lane from a market is in the object column of it, and the word is in front of the place name, and the word is the same type as the heads of the five columns, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that any of that is true and are not going to be told.**
