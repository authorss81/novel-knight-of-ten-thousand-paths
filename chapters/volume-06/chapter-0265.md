# Chapter 0265

**The twenty-fifth day of Mudmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the nine days before it, and a made road, and a man of thirty-two with a limp on it alone, and a stick that is ash and four feet nine and cut out of a fence rail in a county that is not on any map and that he cannot lean on because leaning on it puts the weight through the wrong hip, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that he went out of that county on the second day of that month at about the sixth hour and got to the other end of the road on the twenty-fifth and that nobody walked any part of it with him, and that two hundred miles off and two floors up a sheet with a name in it went into a table on the day he left, and that he does not know that and has not been told and is not going to be told in this county.**

Nine days each way is a body's travel and not a thing anybody can be asked about afterwards, and about four people in that town worked that out separately in about a week and none of them has said it to him.

He left at about the sixth hour on the second day of that month with a stick, a coat, an object under a pound, and a form of the office's own printed series folded once inside the coat, and he went the first two days badly because of the leg and then he got the trick of it back and the trick is to put the weight down before the leg arrives and not after, and he has had four years of the trick and he has never once been able to say where he learned it.

**And about the fourth day he came to a place where the road goes under a bridge, and he stopped under it for about ten minutes, and there are about nine such places on that road and every one of them is in a list in a building two hundred miles off.**

A bridge on a road is a place where a road is under something, and the office prints the name of the bridge and not the name of the road. A man who has walked nine hours under nine bridges has been under the whole of a list and has not seen one line of it, and he stood there for the ten minutes reading the water and not the list, and could not have said afterwards what was on it.

**And the four places where the road turns are the whole of what a man knows about the people who made it, because a road that has to turn turns badly, and this one turns four times in nine days and two of the four are worse than the other two and nobody who made it ever came back.**

He stopped at each of the four. At the second one he got down off the bank on his good side to look at the inside of the bend, and the stones there are laid in courses and two of the courses have run and been relaid by somebody with a different idea of the turn, and a man of thirty-four said in a yard in a winter in a year before last that a road is a thing you dig and has never said it again, and the four bad turns are the only part of a made road that is about anybody.

On the sixth day the weather turned and it came in from the west and he was out in it for about nine hours and about four miles of that road have no hedge on the west side because the ground will not take one, and he was cold for about five hours of it and then was not. A man who walks nine days in the open comes to a point on the sixth day where he stops being cold and starts being a different man, and nobody has ever asked anybody about that point, and there is no column for it anywhere in this kingdom.

He slept in nine different buildings in nine days and in six of them somebody gave him a bed without asking him anything and in three of them he paid and in one of the three a woman of about thirty with a yoke on her shoulders told him the price before he asked and would not take less. A man walking nine days alone is asked eleven things and answers two of them. The eleven are the same eleven in every county in four hundred miles and nobody has ever printed them.

On the fourth day a woman at a door asked him which county he was walking out of, and he said the one behind him, and she said that was not a county anybody walks out of on purpose, and he said it was on purpose, and she shut the door on that, and it was the closest anybody came to asking him anything for the whole nine days.

---

**And on the fifth day he said two things out loud, and neither of them was to anybody, and he did not write them down, and about four people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that he said them, and a man who has had four of those in four years has written one of them down and the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it.**

*Somebody stood in that aisle once and got an answer worth having.*

*Nobody has ever worked out who it was or what the answer was.*

He said them at a stile in a wind and there was nobody within about a mile of the stile, and when he has gone back over that fifth day in his own head since, which he has not, the part he is sure of is that he waited to say them until there was nobody. He did not write them down. There is a page in that coat with eleven lines on it and no words in them, and there is a separate leaf with a seventh line on it that says *a person*, and he has taken that leaf out five times in five months and has not finished it five times, and this is not that leaf and this is not one of the eleven, and about four people in that town have worked out that a man of thirty-two with a limp keeps three things about four inches apart in one pocket and that none of them has ever met any of the other ones and is not going to.

---

He came into the valley on the ninth day at about the seventh hour with about an hour of light left, and the first thing about the place is that there is no market in it and no market day and no calendar on any wall of it, and nobody who lives in it has ever needed one.

That is not a figure of speech. A man who has come nine days up a made road out of a county with a bench and a market knows what day it is, because that county has calendars and a carrier and a court that sits on a day, and a man who has been asked to keep a straight count knows what day it is without a calendar at all, and both of those are ordinary and neither of them is anything to do with the place he came into.

There are about four hundred people in it and eleven steadings and four hamlets and a stream that four people in that valley call the Burn and nobody else does. A room over a cross with the door off one side of it and a bench in it that is too short for a man of thirty-two and a stone at the front of the room with a cross cut in the top of it and about nine generations of weather on it.

**And a bridge over a river with about two hundred and forty feet of stone on it and a mill at the low end of the water and a man in it who cannot leave his wheel, and a road about four hundred yards long made by hand with a standing finish on it, and a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work who said in a yard in a winter in a year before last that a road is not a thing you are given and a road is a thing you dig, and nobody in that valley has ever counted the length of it and nobody has ever asked him to and about four people in that valley have said since that a man who says a thing once in a yard and never says it again has about nine words in a yard and that they are not the same nine words as the nine on the end of the bench and that nobody in that room has ever put the two figures side by side.**

The woman who carries the water up the outside stair told him where the room was and he had asked for the book and not for the man.

“Room over the cross,” she said. “Second door. It is not locked, it never is.”

“Does the man who keeps it know I am coming.”

“He will know at seven. He knows at seven when nobody is coming.”

That was the whole of it. The woman who carries water up a stair is the only person in that valley who goes up and down a hundred times a day and knows every door in it, and she knew the door and the hour and nothing whatever about the man who kept the book in it, and she had not asked him his name and he had not given it, and neither of them thought that was strange, because in a valley of about four hundred people a man with a stick who asks for a book and not for a man is a man who has come about four hundred yards up a made road and is not going to be asked anything else before he is sitting down.

---

**And a girl of nine with a barrow came down the made road at about the eighth hour of that ninth day going the other way and did not stop, and she is nine years old and is the ninth generation and about two hundred of that generation are in a book from the day they were born. A girl of nine with a barrow, going the other way on the ninth day a man came up, is the only person on that road who was going somewhere before he was.**

Nobody in that valley has ever asked her where the barrow is going and she has never said, and the barrow had about nine pounds of stone in it and she was taking it down to the mill and the man at the wheel would not have looked up for it in nine hundred years.

A man of eighty-one came out of a door about nine hundred yards short of the room over the cross and looked at the road for about a minute and a half and went in again, and about four people in that valley have said since that a man of eighty-one who was not asked to come and came is a man in that valley who has spent nineteen years being the one who comes, and that nobody has ever asked him what he came for and he has never said and that both of those are the arrangement this valley has had since the second cold of the ninth generation.

---

**And a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work was at the top of the made road at about the sixth hour of that ninth day with a barrow and no load in it, and he did not ask the man with a stick anything and the man with a stick did not ask him anything, and about four people in that valley have said since that two men stood about eleven feet apart on a made road for about four minutes and neither of them said one word, and that a valley of about four hundred people has a man with sixteen men and no work in it and a stranger with a stick and four feet nine in it, and that the sixteen men are all in that valley and the stranger is not from any county that is on a map, and that about four of the sixteen have nowhere to work this season and one of the sixteen is nine years old.**

The valley had a smell of wet stone at that hour that a man who has come nine days up a made road can smell from about a mile and it was the first thing about the place that was not a building. A valley is not a set of steadings. It is a smell and a sound of water and a wall nine hundred feet above a road, and the wall is four hundred feet of retaining wall at a byre nobody asked for, and the byre is not for anything that is in it.

He came into the room over the cross at about the seventh hour and about nine people were in it and none of them looked up, and the man of thirty-four came in about nine minutes behind him and also did not look up. A room of nine people in a valley of about four hundred does not look up for a stranger and does not look up for one of its own either. That is not unfriendliness. It is the only arrangement any of them has ever heard of for a room in which nine people sit.

---

He did not go to the byre that night. He slept in a room with a fire in it that a man of about thirty-one offered him without being asked and without a condition, and that is a thing this valley has been doing for nine generations with anybody who comes up that road, and nobody in that valley has ever written that down either.

He did not know, at about the ninth hour of that first night, that the man who had offered him the fire would be asked in a room over a cross two days later what he had sworn in that room over a byre nineteen years before, and that the answer would go into a book that had had no finding in it in a hundred and forty years, and that a woman of sixty-six in that room would be asked nothing at all, and that a man of thirty-four would say that he was not going to ask her anything else.
