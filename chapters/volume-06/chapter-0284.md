# Chapter 0284

**The ninth day of Rainmonth, YR 316, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour, and a chamber on the third floor of a building in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, with a table of three and a list of eight ruled lines nailed to the wall and a form of oath printed on the back of a return, and a board at the end of that chamber with two folders on it a foot apart, and about six people in that chamber, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that anything was sat in it.**

There are two chairs in that chamber and they are both for the office, and there is a stool at the end of the table with a clerk on it, and there is a place in front of the two chairs where a person stands who is not sitting, and a room arranged like that is a room where two people are in charge and one person is writing and a fourth is standing up.

The room was taken on the fourth of a month off a board in a passage on the second floor where the days of a quarter are nailed up a card at a time, and the card for the ninth of a month in this year was the only one on that board with no other matter entered against it, and the woman of thirty-eight took it without asking anybody anything, and about four people on that staircase have said since that a person who takes a room without asking is a person who has been told what is in it is available, and that nobody on that staircase has ever asked her, and that there is no column on a booking board for a reason.

---

**The list has eight lines and a nail through the top corner of it, and six of the eight people named on it are in that chamber, and one of the eight is a body corporate with a door on a lane and a year against a number at fourpence and a deed of thirteen words that names no officer, and there is no person against that line, and the woman of thirty-eight looked at that line for about a second and a half on the second of a month in Thawmonth and did not fill it in and did not write *no person* on it either, and she did the same thing again that Friday and nobody in that room has asked her about either.**

The seven of them that are people are a woman of thirty-eight of the office, a clerk of the Crown, a second clerk of the returns, a man of about fifty who has been a clerk of a press for thirty-one years, a man of about thirty-one with a satchel, a man of thirty-two with a limp, and a keeper of records of twenty-nine in a county two hundred miles off.

The keeper of records is not in the room and was not sent for, and there is a line with her name on it and a hand on it that is not the office's, and a clerk of the Crown entered it in about four seconds and did not say when, and a name on a list is not an asking and a name on a list is not a summons either, and a person who is not in a room is a person who is a line.

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp is in that room because his name is on the sixth line, and his name is on the sixth line in a hand that is not his and is not the office's, and nobody in that chamber has said when that was done and nobody in that chamber is going to.**

---

The oath is on the back of a return and a clerk of the Crown said it without any weight in it at all.

“**You are sworn on the back of that sheet, and it is a return going out on the Monday like any other, and what you say will be on the reverse of it and not on the front.**”

“**Nobody minds,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**Nobody minds,**” the clerk said, and entered it, and shut the book, and a clerk who enters a thing and says nothing more about it is a clerk who has decided not to be the man who said it, and nobody in that building has worked out why, and there is no column in it for why.

---

**And there is a second copyist in that room and she is not there as a copyist, and she came two hundred miles for it and there is no accommodation in that building for a person who came two hundred miles for it and nobody in that building has thought about that at all.**

Alish Renn is thirty and is a second copyist in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane in a fen county, and the paper she is standing here on is not in the press of that office, and there is a line in that day-book she has wanted since the summer before last and has told nobody in particular that she will not have and does not want and that both of those are one thing.

And a clerk of the Crown wrote her name on the sixth line of a list on a wall and it is the only name on that list that any person in that room asked to be put there, and it was asked for by an office, and a woman of twenty-nine who runs that office did not know it had been asked for and is two hundred miles off and has not been told.

The warrant she travelled on names a room and a day and does not name a person, and the man who wrote it out wrote it in about four seconds and gave it to a man of about thirty-one with a satchel, and she read it in a cart on the second day of a month and got to that town on the Friday morning at about the first hour.

“**Is there a seat,**” she said.

“**There is a chair for the person before it,**” the woman of thirty-eight said, “**and a stool for the clerk, and a bench along that wall that is in the room because the room is that shape.**”

“**That is three chairs and nine people, then.**”

“**There are six of us.**”

And about four people in that building have said since that a woman who counts chairs in a room she has been in for four minutes and does not ask who the other three are has understood the list faster than anybody who wrote it, and that a list of eight with six people under it is not a room with three chairs in it and nobody in that chamber has said so.

---

**And the delegation cannot arrive, and it was not sent for, and there is no delegation in that chamber, and there has not been one in that building on the ninth of a month in four years, and the matter it was entered for is on a board at the end of that chamber in a folder with a card on the front of it, and the card has one word on it, and the word is *stood*, and it has been *stood* four times and the four cards are not on that board any more and the fifth is.**

The second folder on that board is a foot from the first one and is on the same board and is on the same sheet of wood and has a card on the front of it too, and the word on that one is *sat*.

**Nobody in that chamber has read the delegation and about four of them have read the file, and one of the four is a second clerk of the returns who entered a sheet of one page on the Thursday before as a sheet because there is no column for anything else, and the file is still open and has not been corrected and has not been removed, and nobody in that building has asked him about it, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that two matters were in one room on one Friday and that the room was the same room for both of them and that the word on one card is the opposite of the word on the other.**

---

And the woman of thirty-eight is thirty-eight and is of the office that has been convened for a public inquiry into a body corporate in a fen county, and she is not a magistrate and not a clerk and not a registrar and there is no other word for what she is in that building or in that province, and she put her own name at the top of that list in the office line on the second of a month in Thawmonth and it was the first name anybody had ever put on that sheet.

She asked for it once, in nineteen sentences, standing, with the list on the wall behind her, at about the third hour of that second day: that a thing heard be entered as a thing asked and not as a thing found, and that the record show what was asked. A clerk of the Crown wrote it down and read it back once and she said *that is right*, and nobody in that room said that a want with a day against it and a person against it is the thing she has just spent nineteen sentences saying cannot exist, and that has not been said to her since and is not going to be.

**And on the ninth of a month she entered that chamber as a room, in the minute, in her own hand, and it is the first thing she has written in that minute and it is the only sentence in it, and a Crown officer who has asked for nine years for a room to do one thing has got the room and has written the room down, and neither of those is the thing she asked for.**

---

A man of about fifty from the press on the ground floor stood up when his name was called and was sworn on the back of a return and was not asked anything.

A man of about thirty-one with a satchel stood up when his name was called and was sworn on the back of a return and was not asked anything.

**And neither of them was thanked, and the man of about fifty has not been thanked for a true thing about a column at any time in about two years, and a man who is sworn and not asked is a man who has been entered, and being entered is the whole of what a witness list is for, and a witness list is a list of people who are not in the room, and about four of the people on that list were in the room, and the sheet on the wall has no line under it for that.**

---

And at about the third hour a man of about thirty who is a clerk of that room came in with a pigeonhole under his arm and put it on the table and took it away again, and the pigeonhole has about nine hundred sheets in it of that office's own printed series going round twice into nine counties, and nobody in that chamber has ever counted them and the man who carried it did not count them and the clerk of the Crown wrote nothing about them.

**And the woman of thirty-eight looked at the four cards on the board in that passage once, on the fourth of a month, and has not gone near the board since, and the clerk of the Crown has not mentioned the board to her, and the two of them work in the same building and have sat in the same chamber eleven times.**

---

**And that Friday came to a column, and a clerk of the Crown read that column out at about the sixth hour in the ordinary way, and the items in it are added in pence on the page before the total is said and not after.**

A clerk's fee for entering a sitting is fourpence. A sheet of the office's own printed series for the list that is on the wall is a penny. A second sheet of the same series, which that office prints itself and charges for anyway, is a penny. A light kept burning in a room with no window for one day is twopence. A second copyist of about thirty two hundred miles in a cart and four days on the road, and there is no rate for a cart in this county and there is not going to be one, and a man who travels on a warrant is paid his wages and not his road, and his wages are on a sheet in a drawer in that building and the sheet is not one of the five items and the five items are not added to it.

*Fourpence and a penny is fivepence. Fivepence and a penny is sixpence. Sixpence and twopence is eight pence.*

Total: eight pence. And a clerk of the Crown read that out in a room where the person the matter is entered against is two hundred miles off in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, and did not say the figure to that person and has not sent it, and the fourpence a year that that office pays is in a different column in a different county and is not in this one, and nobody in that chamber has added one to the other, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that any of it was sat.

And the finding at the end of that proceeding is a figure, and the room went on for about nine weeks after that Friday, and about nine hundred people in that town went on working on forms at about four hundred a month into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls, and nobody in that building has ever entered that what was asked in that chamber is not a column and that what will be found in it is.
