# Chapter 0295

**The twenty-ninth day of Harvestmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a chamber on the third floor of a building of four floors and seven presses in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, with a table of three in it and a board at the end of it with two folders on it a foot apart, and about nine hundred people in that town of whom about three hundred are in a market, and about four hundred people in a county two hundred miles off who do not know that the ninth of nine sheets went onto nine sills in that county on the seventeenth of that month and came off them again on the twentieth.**

Two chairs in that chamber belong to the office. A clerk sits on a stool at the near end of the table. Between the two chairs and the foot of the table there is floor for one person to stand on, and a person who stands there is not sitting down.

That chamber has no window at all. Every other room on the floor with a table in it looks at a wall nine feet off, and this one stands at the end of the passage and is lit by two candles in tin holders screwed to the plaster, and has been lit that way for about nine years, and it is the arrangement that lets a thing be said in a room and not be seen to have been said by anybody going past the door.

---

The board at the end of that chamber has two folders on it and they are a foot apart and they are on the same board and on the same sheet of wood.

The first folder on that board is the matter entered in the year before last for a day in Rainmonth, for a delegation that cannot arrive. Four cards have come off that board and the fifth is on it, and the word on the fifth is *stood*. Nobody sent for the delegation and nobody is going to, and there has been no delegation in that building on that date in four years and there is not one in that chamber.

A foot away from that one is a second folder, on the same board and on the same board-piece, and there is a card on the front of that one as well, and the word on that card is *sat*.

**And nothing has ever been sat in it.**

It is a file of one sheet. It was opened in the second month of this year beside the hearing and not against it, and nobody made the opening and nobody has ever known it was a companion file, and the sheet in it has a district on it, and the district is a set of market towns, and the district is the district a return went out against, and that is the first time in about nine years that anything in that building has joined a thing to a thing by accident.

Nobody in that chamber has read the delegation and about four of the people in that building have read the file, and one of the four is a second clerk of the returns who entered a sheet of one page into a ruled book as a sheet because there was no line for anything else, and the file is still open and has not been corrected and has not been removed.

---

**And the wrapper is new, and the card is new, and the sheet inside is the same sheet.**

The wrapper on that folder is the wrapper this quarter, and the card on the front of it was written this quarter, and the word on the card is the word that goes on the front of a card on that board when a matter has been heard, and there is no line on that board for what a matter is.

The woman of thirty-eight came into that chamber at about the second hour and stood at the end of the board with a sheet of her own under her arm and read the card on the second folder, and about four seconds of it, and then she read the date on the new card, and then she put her hand flat on the board and left it there for about four seconds, which is a thing she has done in four different buildings in nine years and has never once been asked about in any of them.

“**That folder has been through a sitting,**” a man of about thirty who is a clerk of that room said, from the table.

“**It has not,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**The card says it has.**”

“**The card was written this week and the sheet in it is the sheet that was in it in the second month, and the card is the only new thing in the folder and it is the only thing in the folder that anybody can produce.**”

And the man of about thirty said nothing else about it, and went back to the table, and a woman of thirty-eight stood at a board with her hand on it and said a matter that was never sat has a card on it that says it was, and she did not take the card off and did not put a new one on and did not open the sheet, and the three things she did not do are the only three anybody in that building has ever done about that file, and doing nothing to a file is a thing a person decides and not a thing a person fails to do.

**Nobody thanked her for that either. Nobody improved on it. And the two proceedings were never joined in one sentence by anybody in that building, and are not going to be, and a chamber that has held one thing at the fourth hour and a half on one Friday and one other thing at the second hour on a Saturday four months later has held two things, and the two of them are four feet apart on the same board and about four hundred miles of country from a lane with a wagon arch at the end of it.**

---

At about the fourth hour a Crown officer's minute was made up for the quarter's list and a clerk of the Crown read the column out in the ordinary way, and the items in it are added in pence on the page before the total is said and not after.

A sheet of the office's own printed series for the quarterly list is a penny. A clerk's fee for entering a finding is fourpence. A light kept burning in a room with no window for one day is twopence. A sheet of the office's own printed series for the copy of the minute that goes on the back of the list is a penny. A new wrapper for a folder, and a wrapper is not a form of that series and is not charged for as one, and there is no rate in that county for a wrapper.

*A penny and a penny is twopence. Twopence and fourpence is sixpence. Sixpence and twopence is eight pence.*

Total: eight pence. And a room is not a fee and there is no rate in that column for a room, and a woman of thirty-eight entered one in a minute in the second month of this year in her own hand and it is not one of the four items, and the four items are not added to it, and the nine pence that a column on the ground floor came to on the seventeenth of that month is in a different room on a different day and is not in this one, and nobody in that building has added one to the other of them, and being correct is the most expensive thing available to a person in a chamber.

---

A clerk of the Crown came in at about the third hour with the wrapper for that folder in his hand and the woman of thirty-eight did not look at it, and he put it down on the end of the table and stood there.

“**The wrapper is this quarter's,**” he said.

“**I have read the card,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**Then somebody ought to read the sheet under it.**”

“**I have read the sheet under it. It has a district on it and it is not a return and it was never sat. None of those three things is a reason to take a card off a board.**”

“**It is a reason to put a new one on.**”

“**It is a reason to put a new one on and a worse reason to take the old one off,**” she said, “**and there is no line on that board for a reason, and if there were one I would have to write the reason on it, and I have not been asked for a reason by anybody in that building this morning.**”

And the clerk of the Crown put the wrapper back under the folder and did not write anything on a card and did not take anything off the board, and a woman of thirty-eight held a clerk of the Crown in a room with no window in it for about a minute and a quarter and neither of them said the word *file* once, and a folder with a card on it that says a matter was sat is still on that board with a card on it that says a matter was sat.

---

The booking for that chamber is on a board in a passage on the second floor with a card for each day of a quarter nailed on it, and the four cards that used to be on that board for the ninth of a month in four years are not on it, and the card that is on it is the same card and the same hand, and there is one word on it and the word has not changed, and a woman of thirty-eight took it off the board on the fourth of a month without telling anybody in that building that she was going to.

Four years running, that chamber has been nobody's choice for the ninth of a month, and in four years not one person on that staircase has wondered whether the room was free because nobody wanted it or because nobody knew what was in it, and the board in the passage carries the days and carries nothing else.

And a second copyist of about thirty went down a lane two hundred miles off in a cart on the sixteenth of that month at about the sixth hour and got into it and has not written to anybody about any of it, and the office she came from has not written to her and has not been asked to, and a person who is producing a document in a proceeding that has not sat again is a person an office cannot say it is keeping, and no column in that proceeding is for a person.

Nobody in that building has mentioned her in the chamber since. About four people in that building have said since that a woman went home in a cart and the building has gone on working at about four hundred forms a month into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls and has not once asked whether the warrant she travelled on is to be produced again, and the warrant names a room and a day and does not name a person, and nobody has asked about the warrant either.

---

The list went out at about the sixth hour on nine sheets of the office's own printed series and went down two flights and out of the front door and into a cart, and about nine hundred copies of a form of that series go into nine counties in a year and this is not one of them and has not been counted with them, and one of the nine has a set of market towns in the first column of it and nothing at all in front of the name of the place, and a man of about thirty-one with a satchel has carried about two thousand sheets in six years and has never once known what is in the four places.

And about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that nine sheets went onto nine sills in their county on the seventeenth of that month and that the ninth of the nine was put under a stone that had been sitting on that sill since the twenty-eighth of a month in Longlight with nothing on it, and that the stone was still on that sill when the satchel came back on the twentieth, and that a county of about four thousand people has not seen the ninth of the nine and has not asked for it, and the cart that took a list out of that building on that Saturday afternoon will be two hundred miles nearer by the fourth hour of a Tuesday in a month that has only just begun.
