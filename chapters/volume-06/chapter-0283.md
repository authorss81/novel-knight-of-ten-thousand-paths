# Chapter 0283

**The eighth day of Rainmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the first hour and the third hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a building of four floors and seven presses in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county with about four hundred forms a month going out of it into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls, and a room on the third floor with a nail in the wall and a sheet of eight ruled lines on it, and a press room on the ground floor at the end of the passage with the stove, and a passage on the second floor with a tray in it that goes into a passage and comes back, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a file has been opened on a sheet that came back up a lane in a county two hundred miles off on the Saturday before.**

The tray goes into the passage at about the first hour and comes back at about the third, and it goes into the passage because a tray is carried and not posted and the passage is where a thing is carried to when a person wants it to be somewhere else without having to say so, and a tray that goes into a passage and comes back has been at a door, and nobody in that building has ever asked at which door, and there is no column for it.

---

**Perry Hask, 34, is a second clerk of the returns in that building, and he is the person the tray came back to, and he is a man who has been in it eleven years and has wanted one thing in all of them and has not asked for it in a form.**

A second clerk of the returns is a post because there is a first clerk of the returns, and the first clerk of the returns is a woman of about twenty-nine who has been in that room six years and has wanted the sixth column ruled for all of them and asked for a word instead on the ninth of a month in the year before last, and nobody answered her and the word went in, and asking for a head is a decision and asking for a word is not, and she knows the difference and has known it for nine years and made the smaller ask on purpose.

A man of about thirty who is a clerk of that room said *Is that yours* to a man of about fifty on a Saturday in Mudmonth, and the man of about fifty said *It is*, and that is the whole of what either of them has ever said to the other one about it, and neither of them has ever said anything about it since.

---

The file had one sheet in it and the sheet had a district on it, and the district is written the way that office writes a district, which is a list of market towns, and there is no person on it, and it is not a return, and a return is the only thing that goes into that book.

The book is a ruled book about nine inches by six and it takes about four hundred forms a month in about nine counties, and each form in it is a line, and a line is a place and a date and a figure and a figure and a figure, and about four of the five places in a line are ruled for a figure and nowhere on the whole of a line is there a place for the name of a person.

He had the sheet in his hand for about two minutes before he asked anybody anything, and he asked a man of about thirty who is a clerk of that room, and got an answer that was a question with one word fewer in it than the one he had asked.

“**Is that a return.**”

“**It is a sheet.**”

“**It is not a return, and it is not a minute, and it has a district on it and nothing else, and there is no line in that book for a sheet.**”

And the man of about thirty who is a clerk of that room did not improve on that and went back to his own bench, and a clerk who is told a thing is not one of three things and does not ask what the fourth is has saved himself a morning, and he will be asked the fourth one day and not by anybody in that room.

---

**And Hask turned it over, and the back of it was blank, and the front of it had six columns and five heads on it, and the heads were *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper* and *How often*, and the sixth column was the same width as the other five and had nothing at the top of it, and about four of the six were ruled for a figure.**

He is a clerk of the returns and he has read about nine hundred forms a month in eleven years and he had never turned a sheet over before in that room and he turned this one over and the turning took about a second and a half.

**And it went into the book on the line for the district, and it went in as a sheet, and about four people in that building have said since that a man who has been handed a thing that is not a thing and has entered it as what it is has done the only thing a book can be asked to do, and that the book will now carry a line that is not a return for as long as the book is kept, and that nobody in that room will ever look at that line again and that the line will be there.**

---

And he went down to the press room at about the fourth hour, and the press room is the smallest room on that floor and the warmest, and there is one window in it and the window is nine feet off a wall, and a man of about fifty was at the stone with a sheet under his hand and did not look up.

He has been a clerk of a press for thirty-one years and has entered about eleven thousand forms and has never once been asked what is in the four places, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building and about four of the six columns on them are ruled for a figure and about one of the six is a column a person can only be entered into and not refuse, and there is no line on any of them for a person to refuse on.

He put his hand flat on the stone for about a second, which is a thing he has done twice in about two years and has not been asked about either time, and then he picked up a sheet.

“**I have been handed a sheet with a district on it and there is no column for it, and I have entered it as a sheet.**”

“**There is no column for it,**” the man of about fifty said, and put a sheet under the press, and did not say anything else about it.

“**I would like there to be a column for a sheet.**”

“**There is one,**” the man of about fifty said, and put a sheet under the press, and then looked up for the first time in about a minute and a half, and “**there is one**” and “**there is no column for it**” are two different sentences and he had said the first one and then the second one about four seconds later and about four people in that press room have noticed that and none of them has said anything to anybody about it, and a man who contradicts himself about a column at a stone is a man who has decided something and does not know what.

“**I did not mean that,**” he said, at about the fourth hour and a half.

“**You did,**” Hask said.

And the man of about fifty went down two flights and did not come back up that day, and about four hundred forms a month went out of that building on the ground floor without him being asked about anything, and nobody in that building has worked out which of those two sentences was the decision.

And that is the whole of what Perry Hask said in that room about a thing he wanted. **It is eleven words and it is the only thing he has said in about eleven years in that building that was about a thing he wanted, and the man of about fifty gave him back none of it and did not thank him and did not refuse him and has not mentioned it since, and nobody in that building has ever worked out whether a man who asks for a column and is given nothing has been answered.**

---

**And the booking is done on a board in a passage on the second floor with a card for each day of a quarter nailed on it, and the cards for the ninth of a month in this year and in three years before it are the same card, in the same hand, and there is one word on them and the word is the same, and there were four of them on that board and there is one now and the woman of thirty-eight took the fifth off the board on the fourth of a month and put a card of a different colour on the ninth, and she did not come down to the board and did not look at it and has not asked about the four.**

A room that has been free on a day for four consecutive years is a room nobody has wanted, and nobody in that building has ever asked the question in the other direction, and there is no column on a booking board for a reason.

The card of a different colour is for a chamber on the third floor with a table of three in it and a witness list and a form of oath and a clerk of the Crown, and the woman of thirty-eight has been in that building nine years and has written the finding at the end of nine of these, and every one of the nine is a figure of something and not one of the nine is a room, and the card of a different colour does not say any of that and a clerk of the Crown did not ask her any of it when she took it.

---

And a man of about thirty-one came through that building at about the third hour with a satchel and about nine sheets in it, and he had come up a lane in a county two hundred miles off on the Saturday before and had taken one of those nine off a step in the rain, and he did not read it and does not know that a county of about four thousand people is in it, and he has carried about two thousand sheets of that series in six years and has never once known what is in the four places and has said so out loud three times, in a yard and in a room and in a market, and about nine hundred people in that town have heard none of the three.

The sheet which has a county in it and a date on it came off a step in the rain rather than out of a tray or out of a pigeonhole, and nobody in that building has ever worked out how a thing gets to a form, and the answer is that a thing gets to a form the way a thing gets to a form anywhere, which is that a person puts it there and does not say.

---

And a market was on in that town that Thursday and about nine hundred people live in it and a market day in that town is a day about two thirds of them are not at work on, and nobody in that building went out to it and nobody in that building has ever gone out to it, and the four floors worked from about the first hour to the seventh and a tray went up two flights and came back down again and a sheet went into a book on a line for a district, and about nine hundred people in that town went on not knowing that a county of about four thousand people had been entered against a place name by a man with a satchel who thought he was collecting the post.
