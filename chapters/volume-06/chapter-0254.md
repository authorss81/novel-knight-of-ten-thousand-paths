# Chapter 0254

**The second day of Thawmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the third hour and the seventh hour, and a room two floors above a room with a stove in it in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and a chamber in it with a table of three and a witness list and a form of oath and a clerk of the Crown, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who came into that building on the morning of the day before and has been sitting in a passage with a stick across his knees ever since and has not been sent for, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that a man who buys a loaf in their market has been on a floor of a building two hundred miles off for a day and a night, and one of the eight lines on a list in that building is a body corporate and one of the eight is empty and nobody in that room has said which of the two is going to happen first.**

He came into that building at about the sixth hour of the first of Thawmonth, YR 316, and it had taken him four days and it was not a crossing and there is no crossing in this and nothing in that county or in this province can open one. Three days on a hired horse and the last stage in a hired cart because of the leg, and he slept the first night in a town that is not this one, and the year turned on the road on the first of Thawmonth between a posting house and a bridge, and the man who turned the year over was a carter with a load of lime and did not know he had done it.

**And a chamber is a room with a table of three in it and a list on the wall, and the woman who convened it came in at about the second hour and a half and put her own name at the top of the list in the office line, and it is the first time a name has been on that sheet, and she is thirty-eight years old.**

**Hester Cray, 38, of the Crown, of the office that has been convened for a public inquiry into a body corporate in a fen county.** She is not a magistrate and she is not a clerk and she is not a registrar and there is no other word for what she is in this building or in this province, and four people in that building have said since that a woman who convenes a thing is a person the thing happens to and not a person it happens for, and that this is the first time in about nine years that anybody in that building has convened anything, and that the last time anything was convened there it was for a delegation that cannot arrive and nobody came and it was not adjourned and it was not refused.

The table of three is the table of three. Two chairs are for the office and one is for the person before it, and the third chair is a clerk's chair and it is at the end and it is a stool, and four people in that building have said since that a room with two chairs and a stool in it is a room where two people are in charge and one person is writing, and that every other room in that building has the two chairs and no stool.

**And the oath is on the back of a return, and a witness list of eight ruled lines is on the wall with a nail through the top corner of it, and four people in that building have said since that a person who swears in that room is sworn on the back of a form that says what a thing is and who keeps it, and that the two of those are not about the same thing, and that nobody in that room has noticed and that noticing it is not in anybody's job.**

“**You are sworn on the back of that,**” the clerk of the Crown said, without any weight in it at all. “**It is the same sheet the returns go out on. It is not the front.**”

“Nobody minds,” said the woman of thirty-eight.

“**Nobody minds,**” the clerk said, and wrote it down, and did not read it back, and four people in that building have said since that a clerk who writes a thing down and does not say it out loud again is a clerk who has decided not to be the man who said it, and that nobody in that building has ever worked out why, and that there is no column in it for why.

---

**And what the woman of thirty-eight wanted is nineteen sentences long and she said it once, at about the third hour, standing, with the list on the wall behind her, and nobody in that room improved on it and one of the two people in that room wrote it down.**

“**I would like the record of this to show what was asked and not what was found, and that is the whole of it, and I have never said it out loud in a room before this morning and I am not sure I am saying it correctly now.**”

“**I have been nine years in this and I have written the finding at the end of nine of these, and every one of the nine is a figure of something and not one of the nine is a room.**”

“**A thing that is heard has to be entered, and that is not a rule anybody made, it is what a book is. A book is read by people who were not in the room, and about nine hundred people a month go through that door and none of them was in this room. You cannot hear a thing and leave the book as it was, because the next person comes and reads the same page and the page has not moved. A room that hears a thing and writes a finding is a room that has been told what it found, and it did not find it.**”

“**So a thing heard is entered, and the entry has the day on it, because the entry was made on a day. And an entry with a day on it in a proceeding with a date against it is a finding.**”

“**A finding is the only kind of paper in this kingdom that can be produced in a court four hundred miles off. A question cannot be produced, and I have tried to produce one, and I have had it refused by a clerk in four words, and I have carried the refusal for eleven months and I still have it. I have also produced a finding about a thing I did not see, and I have watched a room read it back and believe it.**”

“**So I know what a question is worth in a court and it is nothing, and I know what a finding is worth, and I have not once in nine years read the finding at the end of one of these and thought that it described a room. There has been a witness list in every one of the nine and there has never been a column in any of them for what was asked.**”

“**I have never once been in a room that could do what I have just asked this room to do. I do not know whether it is possible. I have looked for it in the standing orders and in nine sets of minutes and there is nothing in any of them that says a record may show what was asked.**”

“**I have not found it and I have not stopped looking, and I want it entered that I asked for it, because a want that is not entered is a want I had, and a want I had is a thing about me and about nobody in this room.**”

“**I am not asking you to agree with me and I am not asking you to think it is a good idea. I am asking you to write down that I asked, in the order I asked it, and with the words I used, and I am asking for it now while there are two of us in the room and one of us is on a stool, and I have not been refused, and I have not been warned, and I would like it entered that I asked in front of a person and not in a report.**”


She did not say four hundred as a figure of anything. It is a distance and not a column, it is the distance she has been wrong about twice, and nobody in that room has asked her about either.
---

**And the clerk of the Crown wrote it down, and it took about forty seconds, and four people in that building have said since that a woman who says nineteen sentences into a room and has them written down has had a want entered, and a want with a day against it and a person against it is the thing she has just spent nineteen sentences saying cannot exist, and that nobody in that room said so, and that the clerk read it back once and not twice and that the not reading it twice was the correct operation and that nobody has said so either.**

She read it back once. She did not read it out a second time, and the reason she did not is in the trade and not in the room, and a clerk who reads a thing out loud twice to a person who has just said it is a clerk who has told that person the person needs it twice, and four people in that building have said since that a Crown officer who has asked for a record to show what was asked has got nineteen sentences read out loud once, which is once more than that woman of twenty-nine has had a thing of her own read out loud to in four years and a half.

The woman of thirty-eight read it as it was read back and said one thing.

“**That is right.**”

And she did not ask for it to be taken out, and four people in that building have said since that a woman who asks for a record to show what was asked and is told that the asking is entered is a woman who has got what she asked for and knows what it cost, and that the cost was nineteen sentences and about forty seconds and a name at the top of a list in the office line, and that she has not asked for the cost and is not going to.

---

**And the cost of convening a room is a column, and a clerk of the Crown read it out at about the sixth hour in the ordinary way, and it is five items and the five items are added in pence and the total is said after the addition and not before.**

A sheet of the office's own printed series is a penny. A sheet of the same series for the list that is on the wall, which this office prints itself and charges for anyway, is a penny. A clerk's fee for entering a proceeding is fourpence. A light kept burning in a room with no window for one day is twopence. A carrier's hand for four miles is twopence. **A penny and a penny is twopence, and twopence and fourpence is sixpence, and sixpence and twopence is eightpence, and eightpence and twopence is ten pence, and the whole of convening that chamber came to ten pence, and a clerk read that out in a room where the person the proceeding is against was not present and had not been told the figure.**

The fourpence a year that the office in that county pays is not in that column, and the two shillings a month that a salt merchant charges for each of the two rooms over a weigh-house is not in that column either, and the two shillings are the price of each of the same two rooms and they are never added to the fourpence in any register in any building, and there is a county two hundred miles off where a woman of twenty-nine has added neither to the other and has said out loud once that she is not going to.


---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp spent the whole of that day in a passage with a stick across his knees, and a man with a satchel came down that passage at about the fourth hour and did not stop, and that is the only conversation he had in that building on the second of Thawmonth, YR 316 and neither of them said anything twice.**

Nobody sent for him. That is the finding of a day and a night in a passage and it is not a finding anybody entered, and a man of thirty-two with a limp came into that building on the strength of a summons that was posted in a county with no writ and no seal and no office, and he came into it because a sheet went out of a press in a room in this province and got itself to a lane, and there is no line on that sheet for a person who turns up.

He had a stick that is a ferrier's stick, ash, four feet nine, cut out of a fence rail in a county that is not on any map, and he cannot lean on it because leaning on it puts the weight through the wrong hip, and he sat with it across his knees for about eleven hours and four people in that building have said since that a man who sits with a stick across his knees in a passage for eleven hours is a man who is waiting to be told he is in the wrong building, and that nobody in that building has told him that, and that one of them looked at him twice and neither of the two times was to say so.

**And his left hand was warm at about the fourth hour and a half and he put it flat on the stone of the passage wall for about two seconds and nothing came back, and four people in that building have said since that a man who puts his hand on a wall in a passage is a man who is looking for a door and that there are four doors off that passage and none of them is one, and that the stone in that building is not the stone in a county two hundred miles off and that a hand does not know the difference and a person does.**

He did not open a door. Nothing opened for him, and he did not ask anybody anything, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that a man of theirs sat in a passage two hundred miles off for a day and a night and put his hand on a wall and did not open anything, and one of the four of them has said out loud that a man who carries a road for four hundred yards and nothing else does not open doors, and that the two of those facts are the same fact and that nobody has worked out how.

**And the list on the wall has eight lines on it, and four of the eight have a person against them and four do not, and one of the four that do not is a body corporate in a fen county with a door on a lane and fourpence a year and a deed of thirteen words that names no officer, and the woman of thirty-eight looked at that line for about a second and a half and did not fill it in and did not write *no person* on it either, and two hundred miles away in a fen county a woman of twenty-nine did the same thing to a different line on a different sheet on the twenty-fifth day of a month, and the two of them are two hundred miles apart and neither of them has been told that the other one exists, and that is the whole of what has happened between them and nothing has happened between them.**
