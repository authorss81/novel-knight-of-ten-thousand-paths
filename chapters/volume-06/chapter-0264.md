# Chapter 0264

**The sixteenth day of Mudmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour and the fifth hour, and a lane four hundred yards from a market in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and about forty men on a reach with the road open over it and the salt coming over the first bay and the fourth bay being pointed into a second month, and four hundred yards up that lane a crossing that carries nobody and has carried nobody for four years, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that two hundred miles off and two floors up a sheet went into a table at about the third hour of the Saturday before this one with a name in the fourth column of it, and that the man who is going to be asked to hold one end of a road is in that lane at about the fifth hour and is going to be asked and does not know it yet.**

He came into the lane at about the fourth hour because the market had gone in and the lane was empty of it, and a man who has been in a county for six weeks does not go up a lane to look at a thing, he goes down one, and there is a weigh-house at the bottom of it with a door on a lane and a cart arch into a yard about forty feet from that door.

The door was shut, as it is most days, and he did not knock, and he went on up the lane past four hundred yards of nothing to a thing that carries nobody.

**There is no owner and no money and no glass in the sill and no third seat, and it is in municipal trust in abeyance since before the bench of nine existed, and a dressed block is in the abutment with about forty yards of tarred hemp in it put there by four men and a boy on the fourth of Hearthmonth of the year before, and a bell a hundred yards off is nine stone of green-black bronze with a band of forty-one rivets in it and a dull red seam split lip to shoulder, and it goes at the fourth hour and a half by a boy and a carpenter, neither of them paid by the guild and neither of them a member of anything, and about four hundred people in that town have heard it about four hundred times and have never once asked who keeps it and are not going to be asked again.**

He stood at the near end of it for about nine minutes and did not put a hand on anything.

What he was there to do is a thing this book has done before and does not do often. A road is a line and two hands, a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it. He had the line. He had both hands. He had a date, and the date was a Tuesday in a month this county calls Mudmonth, and the date was four days off, because a road that is a line and two hands and a date is a road on the date, and not on any other.

The witnesses were the difficulty, and the difficulty was that there is nobody standing at either end who keeps anything.

**And the far end is four hundred miles off in a county that is not on any map, and the last time a man went up that road the far end was a child of nine with a barrow, and this time the far end is a man of fifty-eight who keeps a book, and that change is the whole of what has happened in this county since the spring, and a witness is not the person keeping a thing, and a child of nine was the better witness of the two, and the reason is that a child of nine had not been asked to keep anything and a man of fifty-eight has.**

He went and asked the man of forty-seven.
---

**And the second bay is being pointed into a second month and the salt has been coming over the first bay since the spring, and the joint on the parapet is a foot and a half short because the man who began it went home at the fourth hour one day and has not come back, and nobody in that yard has ever measured it, and a clerk of a bench of nine was asked about it once and said that a clerk cannot measure a thing, and that sentence is on a page somewhere in that county and is the only sentence anybody in that county has ever said about a joint.**

There is a boy of fifteen at a bench-board on that reach with a lime tub and a rule and a bill who copies the empty space under a number as well as the number. A boy who copies the empty space under a number is doing the only thing in this county that a form does, and is doing it for nothing, and does not know that he is doing it. Nobody on that reach has told him. Four of them have thought about telling him and have not.

A woman of about thirty with a pail came up the lane at about the seventh hour and put the pail down outside a door and knocked once and went on to the next one, and she does about nine doors on that lane and has done it for about four years. A woman who does nine doors is the only person in that lane who can say what is shut and what is not. On that Saturday one of the nine was a door forty feet from a wagon arch and it was open. Nobody in that lane has ever checked.

---

He found him on the deck of the first bay at about the fifth hour with a hod and about nine others on the reach, because it is a working day and Saturday is a working day and the market is a morning and the market has been over for an hour.

The man of forty-seven is on that bench of nine because a man in a market asked who was going and he put his hand up, and he has not been on a trade since the year before last, and he does not hold the reach and does not hold the crossing and has not used his vote since the day he was elected and did not use it then.

**He has said the same four things five times in a year, and four of them were to a man with a stick, and the fifth was to a yard wall and a bench-board and nobody besides, and he has been asked nothing about any of the five and has said nothing. A man whose repetition has become a thing other people can use is a man in a document and not in a conversation, and nobody in this county has used it yet and is going to.**

He was asked one question and it was a question and not a thing with a person in it, and he said yes, and then he said one other thing, and it was nine words, and it was not one of the four things, A man who has repeated himself four times in a year without being asked has been asked one question in that year, and has answered it in nine words that are not the four. This is the first time in a year that has happened. Nobody on that reach has worked out what it means. Two of them have.

The man of forty-seven put the hod down in the lime and wiped his hands on his thighs and looked at the man with the stick for about four seconds before he answered, and the four seconds were the first thing that had happened in that conversation that either of them had arranged.

“**Nobody has ever asked me to hold an end.**”

The man with the stick had the date on the back of his hand in pencil because he had no paper for it, and he looked at the mark and then at the crossing four hundred yards up the lane and then at the man of forty-seven, and he said the date, and he said only the date, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has said out loud before in a yard to nobody.

“Tuesday.”

**And the man of forty-seven has not asked the man with a stick what the end is for, and the man with a stick did not tell him, A man who agrees to hold an end of a thing without being told what the end is of has agreed to be a witness and not a keeper. Those are two different things, and the difference between them is the only thing in this county that has changed since the spring, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know it has changed at all.**

The man with the stick had come to the reach with a line and two hands and a date in his coat and had not come to the reach to explain any of it, and there was no way to hand a man of forty-seven the fact of an end without handing him what the end was, and the man with a stick had not written that down anywhere and had not said it out loud to himself, and so what the man of forty-seven had been given was the holding and not the reason for it. He noticed the difference. He was not a man who noticed differences often. He was a man who had said the same four things five times and let the fifth one stand in a wall.

---

**And the wagon arch into that yard is about forty feet from the door on that lane and it takes a loaded waggon and it is the only way in to a yard that has no other way, A wagon arch is not a door, because nobody closes it. A man standing in a wagon arch at the fourth hour on a Tuesday is standing in the place where a man who was going to knock would stand. A man of thirty-two with a limp had stood in it on a Tuesday in Mudmonth and had said nine words to nobody and got no answer for them. About four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that a man in that lane has said anything at all this year.**

---

**And there is a man of about nineteen who came down that lane with an empty hod at about the sixth hour on that Saturday and went through and did not stop at anything, and he has an elder sister of twenty-two in a house two streets off and has not said one word about her to anybody in that lane in about a year. A man who buys one thing and goes is saving for something, and nobody in that market has asked him what for and is not going to, and on the sixteenth day of that month he did not even buy.**

He went up the lane at about the fifth hour and a half on the Saturday and the market had been over for about an hour and the reach had about nine men on it and about forty in one week of the mud month because the ground is bad in the mud month, and he stood at the near end of a thing that carries nobody and did not put a hand on it and did not knock on a bell a hundred yards off and did not go in, A man who stands at a crossing that carries nobody for nine minutes and then goes up a lane has come to look at it, has not found anything, and is going to come back. Nobody in that county has asked him what he is coming back for.

---

**And on that Saturday evening a man of thirty-two with a limp went up a lane to a house and a press in it and did not read the summons that has been sitting in that house on Weir Street since the spring, and did not send anything to anybody, and put a sheet of paper into an inside pocket of a coat, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that he is going to be nine days from that lane and that nobody is walking any part of that road with him, and that the whole of what he is carrying is a stick, a coat, an object under a pound, and that county's own form, which is ruled in seven and has a column in it for a witness at this end and a column in it for a witness at that end, and that he has taken it out of a drawer in that county because it is the only paper in the county with those columns on it, and that about four people in that lane have said since that a man who takes a printed form out of a drawer in a county he is going to walk out of has decided something and does not know what.**

The bell is a hundred yards off that lane and it has gone on being rung at the fourth hour and a half for four years over a crossing that carries nobody, and the boy who rings it has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is still not good at it, and there is nobody in that county to ask about either the bell or the boy and has not been for four years. The man with a stick did not ask. He stood in a lane four feet wide and worked out that a county which has rung the same bell over the same empty crossing every market day for four years has decided something about that crossing that it has never said out loud, and he had no way at all of finding out what, and he did not go up the lane to try.

He had asked four people about it on the fifth of Mudmonth, YR 316 and got four answers and none of them had been an answer, and he has not asked a fifth and is not going to.
