# Chapter 0281

**The twenty-eighth day of Longlight, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour, and a toll house four hundred yards up a fen county's own road that people cut through on the way to somewhere and do not go into, and a round of nine drops going up that lane at about the second hour with a satchel and a list of about four hundred places behind it, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that nine sheets of a printed series went to nine doors on that Monday and that the ninth of the nine went into a sill with a man sitting in the building, and that he read it and put it down again and told nobody, and that there is no column in that county for a thing a man has told nobody.**

A round is not a delivery. A delivery has a person at the end of it and a round has a step.

---

The carrier is a man of about thirty-one and he has carried about two thousand sheets of that office's own printed series in six years and he has never once known what is in the four places, and he has said so out loud three times, in a yard and in a room and in a market, in the tone of a man saying a price, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river have heard none of the three.

The order of the nine is on the list and the list is behind him and about two thirds of the nine on it in this county are in a hand he does not know and the other third are his own and he made those himself in the winter. He does the nine in the order on the list. He has done them in the order on the list for six years, and the order is not the order they would be in if a man were being quick, and it has not been the quick order since the year before last, and nobody has ever asked him about it and he has never offered.

A sheet goes where a thing goes when it is raining. That is the whole of the rule and it is not written down anywhere and there are about four people in that lane who could state it and none of them has ever been asked to.

---

**He put one in a cresset against a door, and one on a step under a shutter, and one inside a door that was not shut, and one on the sill of a shop with a stone off the sill because the wind on that lane comes off the water. He did all four in about nine minutes and did not knock at any of them and nobody opened a door at any of them.**

The fifth went on a window sill inside a window that is painted shut four days a year, and a woman of about thirty-three who lives behind that window came out at about the third hour to fetch a pail and found it and took it in and did not look at it, and a sheet of a printed series is not a thing a person picks up and reads on a step.

The sixth went into a cresset that had water in it and he moved it to the step and did not say anything about that to anybody.

At the seventh he waited.

“**You are the second one this week,**” a woman said, from a door about four feet off him, and she was not the woman with the pail and she had not asked him anything and he had not asked her anything.

“**I am,**” he said.

“**I have had the other one off that step and I have not read it,**” she said, and shut the door, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that a sheet of a printed series was taken off a step in that county and read by nobody and that the woman who took it off has not thrown it away and has not put it anywhere and has it, and that a county of about four thousand people has a sheet of paper in it that a person is carrying about with and is not reading, and about four people in that lane have said since that a thing in a house that nobody is reading is the same as a thing in a sill and is the same as a thing in a press four hundred yards up Weir Street, and that there is no column in this county for a sheet anybody has picked up.

The seventh and the eighth were on the same step about forty yards apart and a woman of about thirty with a pail came up that lane at about the third hour, nine doors, one sentence at a door, and saw two sheets on two steps and did not stop for either of them, and she has never been asked about the two and is not going to be.

The eighth was on a step about forty yards from the ninth and a man who came out of that door to look at the weather looked at it for about a second and a half and said one thing about the rain to nobody and went in again.

“**That will be gone by the third hour,**” he said.

And he went in, and about four hundred people in that town have heard a man make a true remark about a sheet on a step in this county and only that one, and the man who made it did not know he had said anything and has not mentioned it and is not going to.

The ninth of the nine was the one at the end of the lane, and he put it in the sill of the toll house, and he weighted it with a stone off the bank, and he did not go in, and there was a man in the building.

He did not see the man. The door of that building is open at the low end and has been open since the spring, and there is a bench inside it with its back to the door, and a man going up a lane at about the second hour with a satchel is looking at sills and not at doorways. He was in and out of that sill in about four seconds. He put the sheet down, he put a stone on it, he looked at the sky to see whether it was going to rain, and he went on down the lane with about eight sheets still to go and did not turn round.

---

The sill is a foot and a half wide and it has no glass in it and no third seat, and there are two stones in it, one of which is a stone off the bank and one of which has been in that sill since the spring and is not a stone off the bank.

The man of thirty-two with a limp was on the bench with his stick across his knees. He had been in that building since the second hour and he had not lit anything and there is nothing in the building to light, because the toll house is a building people cut through and the third seat has been gone about two years and the fire was never in it.

He did not get up. He read the sheet from where he was sitting, and the reading took him about a minute and a half, and he read it once.

Six columns and five heads. The heads are *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper*, and *How often*, and the sixth is the same width as the other five and has nothing on it at the top of it, and about four of the six are ruled for a figure.

The object column is the first of the six and it has a place in it, and the place is in it the way that office puts a place in, and there is a word standing in front of the name of the place, and the word is cut in the same sort as the heads of the five columns, and he has now seen that word in four places in five months, in a case, on a sheet, in a bag and in a field four miles off, and he has not once found out what it is for and is not going to keep looking.

The second column is empty. The third is empty. The fourth is empty. The fifth has a date in it and the date is the ninth of a month he can name, and it is eleven days off, and it is a Friday, and a thing on a Friday is not a market day and he knows that about this county the way he knows the tide.

**And he knew the series. He has had one of them in his hand. A summons on the same printed series went out of that office in the last month of a year and got itself forty feet from where he was sitting and he has seen the head of it, and this is not a summons, and a sheet is not a summons because a summons has a person served on it and there is no person on this one and there is no person on any of the nine.**

The place in the object column is this county. It is a set of market towns and it is written the way that office writes a set of market towns, and the sheet does not say which of them, and a man of about fifty who has been a clerk of a press for thirty-one years said in his own office about two hundred miles off, some months ago, that he does not know what a district is, and nobody answered him, and that is the whole of what anybody in this kingdom has ever said about one out loud, and the man who put a district into that sill has been up this lane eleven times and does not know that is what it is either.

Forty feet from where he was sitting there is a lane with a wagon arch at the north end of it and two rooms over a weigh-house at the end of that lane, and the place in the object column of that sheet is that lane, and a door on that lane has a number on it and a year on it at fourpence, and the office it is entered against is in a provincial town two hundred miles from here, and about four hundred people in this town do not know any of that and the two of the nine hundred people in that town who do know it are not going to say so in a room with a bench in it.

He put the sheet down on the sill and left it on the sill with the stone on it, because a thing that has been put in a sill by a man with a satchel is a thing that belongs to the sill, and taking it off it would be a decision and he has not made one.

---

**And at about the fifth hour, with the wind behind him and the door open at the low end of that building, he said two lines out loud to a room with a bench in it and nobody in it, and he did not write them down, and he has had four of those in four years and has written one of them down, and the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it.**

*A man may be the only one who knows and still be the wrong one to say it.*

*There is nobody in this county to say it to and there is not going to be.*

He did not write them down. He has not written down what he did on the eighth of a month in this year and he is not going to, and there is a coat in a room over a smithy and the coat is where a paper goes and about four people in this county have noticed over about a year that a man who owns a coat puts paper in it and does not put paper on a table, and none of them has asked him about the coat and none of them is going to.

Nobody in that lane heard him. The round had gone on down the lane about a quarter of an hour before and a man with a satchel does not stop at a building he has been to eleven times, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river went on past a toll house at about the fifth hour of a Monday that is not a market day and did not know that a thing was standing in a sill forty feet from a door with a number on it.

---

A man of fifty-five sat in a hut at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards down that road and turned round about nine times on that Monday and let them all by, and he said the same four words to each of them and added nothing to them, and he has been in that hut nineteen years and nobody in this county has ever asked him where he gets the four words and he has never said and is not going to.

“**Anything for the road,**” a carter of thirty said, coming through the bars.

“**No toll here,**” the man of fifty-five said. **“There has not been one since before you were asked to sit here.**”

And the carter went through and the man of fifty-five turned the bars back down and said the same four words to the next one, and he did not see a man with a stick that Monday. A man with a stick had gone up the lane at about the second hour and had come back down it at about the sixth and the hut is three hundred yards down the road and a barrier of three bars goes up across a lane and a man who is not going through it is a man who is not on that lane.

And a bell a hundred yards off goes at the fourth hour and a half whether there is anybody on that crossing or not, and it went at the fourth hour and a half while a man of about thirty-one was putting nine sheets on nine doors, and nobody in that county has ever worked out who keeps it, and the answer nobody in that county can give is the finding and is not a gap, and a bell rung by a boy and a carpenter who are neither paid for it nor members of anything is a bell nobody in the county can be asked about, and that is why it is rung, and nobody in that county has ever said any of that out loud.

---

And a woman of about thirty with a pail went up that lane at about the fifth hour and stopped at nine doors and said one thing at each of them and moved on before the knuckle had finished ringing in the frame, and at the door on that lane forty feet from a wagon arch she said that nine sheets had gone to nine doors that morning and that nobody had knocked, and she said it to the door and not to anybody, and there are eight doors behind her in that lane and nobody in that lane has ever asked her why she says things at doors.

Nobody walked any part of that Monday with him. He went up that lane at about the second hour and down it at about the sixth, and it is a lane and there is nobody on it at either hour, and about four hundred people in that town did not see a man go or come, and the tide was under the wharf, and the salt was over the first bay, and a bell a hundred yards off went at the fourth hour and a half, and nine sheets are on nine doors in that county, and the ninth of the nine is in a sill with a date on it eleven days off, and a county that keeps a register of claims and a day-book of things that were not asked has three kinds of paper in it and a date nobody asked for is not one of the three.
