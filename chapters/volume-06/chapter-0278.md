# Chapter 0278

**The seventeenth day of Longlight, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane in a fen county, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp carried a wet sheet of a printed series up that lane on that Thursday and put it on a table in that room and took it away again, and that a woman of twenty-nine said eighteen words about the shape of it, and that nobody improved on the eighteen words, and that the day-book of that office has nine entries in it of a thing that was not asked and did not get a tenth, and that the not-asking on that Thursday is not in the book and is not going to be.**

The market was on in the main street and the lane to the weigh-house was wet to the hub and about nine men were on a reach four hundred yards down it with the road open over it and the salt coming over the first bay for the tenth week running.

He went up the lane at about the first hour and a half and did not knock and the door was open and it has been open since the spring.

Two people were in that room and neither of them had been there all morning. The keeper of records is twenty-nine and has run this office four years and a half out of a deed of thirteen words at fourpence a year, and keeper in the office line is employment and is not jurisdiction and she has never once confused the two. The second copyist is about thirty, is on a warrant that is not in the press, and has wanted a line in a book with her name in it since the summer before last. There is a third post in that day-book and it is on a warrant held by a woman of twenty-six and it is not signed, and the two of them have never spoken about it and are not going to.

He put the sheet on the table.

The name in the fourth column had a leg on it from the weather and the corner of it had gone soft, and the head of the fourth column is the office's own and it has been the same head for two years, and a man who changes a head in his own hand has made a decision.

“**Does that sheet come from you,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said.

**“No,” the keeper of records said.**

“**It is a form of your series.**”

**“It is a form of our series,” she said, “and we do not have it, and neither do I think anybody in this county does, and I have not asked anybody in this county and I am not going to.”**

The second copyist did not look up. A second copyist who does not look up at a sheet put on a table in front of her is a second copyist who has decided that a sheet on a table is not a thing she is responsible for, and that decision is about four years old and about nine people in that building have made it and none of them has ever written it down.

He turned it round so that the object column was nearest her and pushed it about four inches towards her, which is a thing a person does, and the four inches were the whole of his argument.

**“That is our shape with a place in it and no figure, and we have never entered one.”**

Eighteen words. She said them in the ordinary way, at waist height, in a room where the light is on the floor and about four hundred forms a month are read at waist height under it.

**Nobody improved on them. A man of about fifty in a press room two hundred miles off has said a true thing about a column in eleven words to a boy of nineteen and was not thanked, and this is a different true thing about the same shape in eighteen words to a man with a limp, and neither of the two men has ever heard of the other one, and the two of them are the only two people in this kingdom who have said a true thing about that column out loud, and neither of them has been thanked.**

He did not thank her. She was not thanked and did not want to be, and there is a form on the office's own printed series on which a person could have been thanked and it is not one of the four places ruled for a figure and the sixth column has no head and is the same width as the fifth.

“**There is a register in this room,**” he said.

“**There is a register in this room,**” she said.

**“How many things in it.”**

**“Five,” the keeper of records said. “Every one of them begins with the name of a district and every one of them is a question, and not one of them is a finding, and the fifth of them has a day against it.”**

She said that in about four seconds and she did not say anything else about it, and a register with five questions in it and no finding in it is a register that cannot be sued for, and a body corporate cannot be sued for a question, only for a finding, and a person can be asked a second time and a column cannot, and a room that is asked a second time is a second hearing, and about four people in that lane have said since that the only way this office can be got at is a finding and the only way a finding arrives is a room, and nobody in that room has ever asked the room to be one.

---

He took the sheet off the table and put it back inside his coat and it is in that coat now and it is not in the press on Weir Street and it is not on the shelf with the finding on it, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that a piece of paper is in a coat in a room over a smithy that a man pays for by the week and that nobody has ever asked him a question about.

And there is a leaf in the press in that room with something in it that this office has had for about a year and a half and did not write and is not going to write another of, and it is not a form and it is not a question and it is not a finding, and it is the only other piece of paper in that building that is about a person, and the keeper of records did not mention it and he did not ask, and a man who has not been asked about a thing in a press has not been told and has not been told and the not-telling is not a courtesy and is not a rule and is the ordinary habit of a room.

There is a fair copy on the wall in that room nineteen years old, in a keeper's own hand, and it says that no combined figure is entered in that office, and it has never once been read out loud in that room by anybody including the keeper of records, and a rule in a fair copy on a wall that nobody reads out loud is a rule that is obeyed and not known.

“**There is a rule on that wall,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said, after about a quarter of an hour.

“**There is a rule on that wall,**” the keeper of records said.

“**You have not read it to me.**”

**“Nobody has read it to anybody in nineteen years,” she said, “and you have been in this room about a quarter of an hour and you have not asked me what is in it, and that is correct, and I would have said no.”**

The scales under that room had not been used since the fourth of that month, and there is a press in it with two unserved books in it and a day-book and a half tile that weighs a shade over ten ounces, and about four people in that lane have said since that the half tile is in a press with two books that cannot be served with anything and weighs a shade over ten ounces, and that nobody in that building has ever worked out what the weight of it is for and it is not in a column anywhere.

The second copyist turned a sheet about the fourth hour without being asked and did not say anything, and a woman of about thirty who has wanted a line in a book with her name in it since the summer before last has said nothing in that room on that Thursday either and is not going to, and a man of thirty-two with a limp did not ask her, and nobody asked anybody, and the day-book of that office is the only book in that office nobody can be served with and it has nine entries in it of a thing that was not asked and did not get a tenth.

At about the fourth hour he went down the lane and past a wagon arch and up the lane to a crossing four hundred yards off and he did not go inside the toll house and he did not put his hand on anything, and the sill was as it was and the block was as it was.

A woman of about thirty with a pail went up the lane at about the fourth hour and stopped at nine doors and said one thing at each of them and moved on before the knuckle had finished ringing in the frame, and at the door on that lane forty feet from a wagon arch she said that a man had gone up the road twice this week and had not been up the road at all the week before, and she said it to the door and not to him, and there are eight doors behind her in that lane and about four hundred people in that town and nobody has asked her why she says those things at doors.

There is a man of fifty-five at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards down that road who says four words to everybody who comes through it, and he said them to a man with a stick on that Thursday and did not add to them, and the man with a stick did not ask him for a fifth thing and did not ask him where he got them and is never going to.

The fever was on its ninth day and he was out for about two hours and then he was not out for about two hours, and nine days of a thing that is not worse and is the same one and that he has had twice in about two months and that somebody else in this county is holding the tools of, and a yard on Weir Street has nine men in it and a bench-board and about four men who have noticed that a man who used to stand at that board for an hour and a half every market morning has not been there for two days and about four who have not, and a man of about sixty packing lime has been at the end of that yard every working day of that week with a lid on a heap and a crack in it in the same place, and a boy of fifteen has been at a bench-board with a bill under his arm and no bill on the board, and a man of fifty and a half has been up a ladder and down it.

Nobody in that yard was told and nobody asked, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river went on to the end of that Thursday the way they go on to the end of every Thursday of a wet season, and the tide was under the wharf, and about nine waggons had come up the channel road since the spring, and about four hundred people in that town do not know that a man has been upstairs for two days and that he came down for two hours and went up again and that the two hours cost him the rest of the week, and that a woman of twenty-nine in a room forty feet from a wagon arch said eighteen words to him and was not thanked and did not want to be, and that a sheet with a name in it went back into a coat and out of a room that cannot be served with anything, and that the sheet is the only copy anybody in this kingdom knows of and it is in a coat.
