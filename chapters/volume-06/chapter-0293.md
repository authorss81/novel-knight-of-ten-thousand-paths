# Chapter 0293

**The seventeenth day of Harvestmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the first hour and the third hour and the fifth hour, and a press room on the ground floor at the end of a passage in a building of four floors and seven presses in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, with about four hundred forms a month going out of it into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls, and a case against the wall with a drawer in it, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a form of that series went out of that building that morning with nothing standing in front of the name of the place in the first column of it.**

That building turns out something near four hundred of its forms every month, and on any one of them six spaces are ruled and five of the six have a head over them, and four of the six will take a figure and one will take a person, and not one of the six will take a person declining to be a person.

The man at the stone is about fifty and he has been a clerk of that press for thirty-one years and about eleven thousand forms have gone through his hands, and not one of those thirty-one years has anybody in that building asked him what is in the four places.

---

The sheet on the stone at about the first hour is the same series as every other sheet that building makes. Six columns and five heads, and the heads are *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper* and *How often*, and the sixth column is the same width as the other five and has nothing at the top of it.

The first column is the object column and it is the first of the six and it has a place in it, and the place is in it the way that office puts a place in, which is a set of market towns, and in front of the name of the place there is a space about the width of a word.

**And the word that goes in that space has been in a drawer in that press room for about two years and was struck once, on a list that went out to nine counties in the second month of this year, and it has not been struck since, and there is a boy of nineteen in that building who set it once and does what he is told and has eleven weeks to serve before the next sitting and wants to be a journeyman.**

He put his hand flat on the stone for about a second, which is a thing he has done four times in about two years, and then he picked the sheet up and looked at the space in the first column and at the drawer and did not open the drawer.

The second column has a figure in it. It is a figure of persons against a set of market towns and it is a good figure and it is a figure the office is very good at, and the head over that column says *Date*, and the head over that column has said *Date* on every form of that series for about two years.

---

At about the third hour a woman of thirty-eight came down two flights and stood at the end of that stone, and she is of the office that convened a proceeding in a chamber on the third floor of that building in the second month of this year and has been in that building nine years and has written a finding at the end of nine of them, and what she has at the end of every one of the nine is a figure.

“**Say the true thing,**” she said, not as an order.

“**I have said it before,**” the man of about fifty said, “**and nobody thanked me for it.**”

“**Say it again.**”

And he said it, and it was twenty words, and it is the whole of what he has said out loud in that building about a column in about two years and it is the second time it has been said and he did not improve on it.

“**A figure of persons stands under a head that says Date, and there is no place here for a name.**”

And the woman of thirty-eight said thank you, and she said it in the ordinary way, at the height of a person thanking a man for a thing he has done well, and about four people in that building have said since that the thanks was the worst thing that happened in that press room in about two years, and that it was two words, and that the man who said the true thing had been waiting for one of them for about two years and got it on a Monday from the office that prints the form.

A clerk of the Crown wrote it in a margin in about four seconds, in a hand that is not the office's, and a thing in a margin is a thing anybody can produce, and a true thing said in a room and thanked by the office has a day on it and a person against it, and that is a finding, and there is no form in that office of that Crown that has a line for a finding that was not asked for.

**And nobody in that press room thanked her for thanking him, and nobody asked her whether she had understood what she had done, and she had been in that building nine years asking that a record show what was asked and had come down two flights and thanked a man for saying a thing nobody asked him to say. The two of those are in the same building on the same Monday and they are not in one line of type and there is no column in that office that would put them side by side.**

The man of about fifty went back to the stone and put a sheet under the press and did not say anything else about it, and that is the last time anybody in that building has said anything to him about a column.

---

At about the fifth hour the woman of thirty-eight asked him to set the sheet again for the copies, and he said that the first column wanted a sort and she asked him which sort and he told her.

“**There is a word in that drawer that has been in it about two years,**” he said, “**and it was struck once, on a list, in the second month, and it has not been struck since.**”

“**Then set it,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**It is not going to be struck,**” the man of about fifty said, and he did not say why, and nobody asked him. The boy who struck it once is nineteen and has eleven weeks to serve before the next sitting and wants to be a journeyman and **was not in that room when the room was told what the drawer held**, and he has not been in that press room since the second month of this year, and nobody in that building has asked him where he is.

It went out of that building at about the sixth hour with the first column of every copy empty ahead of the name of the place, and about nine hundred of that series go a year into nine counties and none of these nine is one of the nine hundred and no one has added it in, and a man of about thirty-one took them down to a cart in a satchel without reading a word of them, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a county of about four thousand people two hundred miles off is inside nine of them with nothing whatever standing in front of its name.

---

A woman of about twenty-nine who is a clerk of the returns came down at about the fourth hour and stood at the end of that stone and asked how many copies were going out, and the man of about fifty said nine, and she asked whether the returns book had a line for them and he said it did not, and she stood there for about four seconds.

“**Then somebody will write them on the back of something,**” she said.

“**Or on nothing at all,**” the man of about fifty said, and put a sheet under the press.

And she went up without saying anything else, and a clerk who has asked in that building for one thing for six years and has not asked for it in that form watched a man of fifty offer a second way of doing a thing and did not take either, and nobody in that press room saw her not take either.

And a man of about thirty who is a clerk of that room came down at about the fifth hour to say that a tray had come back with nothing on it for anybody in that room, and went up again without waiting, and a man of about sixty with a tray has carried a tray in and out of that building for twenty-two years and has not once been asked whether anybody wanted it.

---

A man of about thirty-one with a satchel came through that building at about the fifth hour on his way down to a cart and stopped in the passage outside that press room because a man of about fifty was standing at a stone with a sheet under his hand and not working it.

“**Is that going out today,**” the man with the satchel said.

“**Nine of them are,**” the man of about fifty said.

“**I will take them.**”

“**They are not done.**”

“**I will take them when they are,**” the man with the satchel said, and went on down the passage, and a man who has carried about two thousand sheets of that series in six years and has never once known what is in the four places stopped in a passage to offer to take nine of them early, and the man at the stone told him they were not done, and neither of the two of them said one word about a column or a drawer or a word in a drawer.

---

**And that Monday came to an account, and a clerk of the Crown read it out in the ordinary way, and every item in it is added in pence on the page before the sum is given and not after.**

A sheet of the office's own printed series for the contested record is a penny. A second sheet of the same series, which that office prints itself and charges for anyway, is a penny. A third sheet of the same series, for the copy of the minute that goes on the back of it, is a penny. A clerk's fee for entering a contested record is fourpence. A lamp kept burning in a press room with one window in it, for one day, is twopence, and there is no rate in that county for a lamp and there is not going to be one.

*A penny and a penny is twopence. Twopence and a penny is threepence. Threepence and fourpence is sevenpence. Sevenpence and twopence is nine pence.*

Total: nine pence. And no figure out of another county and no figure out of another room is in that column, and the wages of a boy of nineteen at that press are on a sheet in a drawer in that building and the sheet is not one of the five items, and nobody in that building has added one to another of them, and being correct is the most expensive thing available to a person in a room.

---

Nobody walked any part of that Monday with anybody. A man of about thirty-two with a limp was two hundred miles off in a room over a smithy and did not know that a form had gone out of a press with his county's road on it in the first column and nothing in front of it, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river had gone to a market that was not on and had come back, and the presses on the second floor started at the first hour and went on to the fifth, and a woman of thirty-three filled in the fourth column of two of them that day from a roll in about a minute and a quarter without looking up, and nobody in that room was watching her do it.
