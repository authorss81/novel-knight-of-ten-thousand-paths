# Chapter 0277

**The fourteenth day of Longlight, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the seventh hour, and the second hour of the morning after, and the seventh hour of that morning, and a room over a smithy in a town of about four thousand people on a river with a table in it and three pieces of paper on it, and about four hundred people in that town who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp put three forms of the same printed series side by side on a table on that Monday and found that they were the same shape, and that one of them was a sheet he had read out of a sill, and that the finding is not a relief and is not a rescue and is the opposite of both, and that his mother is on the eleventh line of a list he has had in his head for about four months and has never had on a table.**

The fever let him up on the fourth day and he went down the stairs in two stages and stopped on the landing both times, and a stick that is ash and four feet nine and cut out of a fence rail in a county that is not on any map is not a thing a man can lean on in a room, and he leaned on a table instead, and a table is deal and is not warm.

He put three things on it.

The first was the sheet out of the sill, and the weather had got a ninth of an inch into the name in the fourth column and the leg of it had run, and there is no rule in this kingdom against a name in a column going soft in the weather.

The second was a blank form of the same printed series out of the inside pocket of a coat, which is a thing he has carried since Mudmonth and has never had a reason for except that it is the shape of the thing a person is asked to fill in.

The third was this county's own form, ruled in seven, which is what the office over a weigh-house with a door on a lane has used for four years and a half, and which has a ruled space about two inches deep at the bottom of it with a name in it, entered by a second copyist of about thirty in about nine seconds.

And a man of thirty-two with a limp put three forms of the same printed series on a table and squared the two printed ones against each other and the third one against them, and the second column of the first is the same width as the second column of the second, and the fifth of the first is the same width as the fifth of the second, and the sixth column of both has no head on it and is the same width as the fifth, and about four of the six on both are ruled for a figure and there is nowhere on the whole of either of them for the name of a person.

He did that in about four minutes and it took him the whole of the four, and the two of them had been on that table about nine minutes.

---

**And then he went and got the fourth thing, and the fourth thing was not on the table, and it has not been on a table since the fourteenth day of a month four months ago in a room with no window in it in a provincial town two hundred miles off where a man who is not surprised put it on the table and then put it back in a drawer in about a quarter of an hour.**

Eleven lines, and each of them ruled for two things, and the two things on every line are in two parts, so that the top half of the first column on any line is a hand and the bottom half of it is a road. Eleven of one and eleven of the other, cut to the same depth, set the same way up the page, and doing two entirely different jobs.

The eleventh line has two marks on it and they are the only marks on the sheet. A woman's and a boy's. And about a year between the first of them going out and the eleventh coming back.

**And there is no third column. There is no column for what became of anybody. And a man in a room said so before he was asked, and he did not ask, and the not asking took about four seconds, and that man is not in this county and is not going to be, and he has not been asked anything since and is not going to be, and the not asking is not a decision anybody took.**

He sat with it for about a quarter of an hour. He did not write it down. He has had four of those in four years and has written one of them down.

---

**And what he worked out was this, and it took him until about the second hour of the morning, and it is a complication and not a vindication, and he did not get anything out of it that he wanted.**

The first column of that list is a hand.

A hand is not a person. A hand is what a person is when a column has a head on it and the head is *Keeper*, and the shape of the loop and the nine shapes and the stroke left short at the top are the shape of a person putting down the only thing about themselves that a form will take.

So the first column of that list is the fourth column of a form of this office's own printed series, in a book in a drawer, written out longhand by somebody who wanted a drawer to be a drawer.

**And the second column of that list is a road, and a road in a column with a head on it is a place, and a place is not a journey, and a journey is a road with a day on it and an outcome at the end of it, and this one has neither.**

It has a hand and it has a road. It does not have what became of either of them.

**And a document with a keeper in it and no outcome in it is not a record of a journey. It is a record of custody. And a record of custody is a list of what is held and by whom, and the eleventh line has two marks on it because two people were with the eleventh thing, and a mark is not a name, and the two marks are the only marks on the sheet, and nothing else in this kingdom says what a mark is.**

So the thing he has been reading as a record is a list of what was taken.

He let that sit where it was for about nine minutes and then he took it apart, because a man who lets a thing sit for nine minutes and then takes it apart has decided to take it apart, and the deciding happens about nine minutes before the doing and nobody in the room can see it happen.

It is a form. It has a keeper and it has a place and it has no outcome. The office has printed about nine hundred copies of that shape in nine counties and has four hundred and nine parish rolls behind the nine counties, and about four of the six columns on the shape are ruled for a figure, and the sixth column has no head and the same width as the fifth.

**And a form that is not printed with a column for what became of anybody is not a form that somebody forgot. A column is a decision. A form is a set of decisions that have been cut into a forme and printed nine hundred times, and the decisions are older than the forme, and about four people in that room two hundred miles off have had the forme in a drawer for two years and have never once asked what a set of decisions is for.**

---

**And his mother is on that line, and it is the eleventh, and it has been the eleventh for about four months, and he did not find that out on the fourteenth of Longlight and he knew it in a room with no window in it.**

He has never had a name for her and he has never been given one and he has never asked, and the sheet says *You have a mother* in the plain way and does not say anything else, and the eleventh line has a woman's mark and a boy's mark on it, and the boy is about one-and-twenty and he was thirty-two in a month in this year, and the arithmetic of that is a thing he has not done and is not going to do tonight.

She is not saved and she is not lost. There is no column for it. The only thing in this room that knows anything about the eleventh line is a man who looked at a list and did not say anything for about a minute and a half and then read it again, and a man is not a column.

And there is a page in that coat with eleven lines on it and no words in them, and the two of them are the same length, and neither of them knows about the other one, and he has had the page for about four years and has never once put the two side by side and about two hundred miles off and nine days up a road he has stood in a room with the other one in it and did not have a coat open.

He did put them side by side at about the third hour of that morning, in his own head, and the two of them are the same length and one of them has words in it and one of them does not, and the one without words is his, and the one with words is in a drawer, and a man who puts his own page next to a drawer is a man who has stopped reading his own page as a record of anything.

He squared the three forms together at about the fourth hour and stacked them with the object columns at the same edge, and he did not write anything down, and a man who has had four of those in four years has written one of them down and the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it, and this one had a date in it and the date is the eighth of Longlight and the date is in a room four hundred miles off and not in this one.

---

The yard was at its best about the seventh hour on the morning after and there were about nine men in it and the owner of it was twenty-five and had come in from the wharf and had not said anything about the four sheets in a press in a room that belongs to the yard and cannot be served with anything.

“**You look as though you have been somewhere and come back,**” the man of twenty-five said.

“**I have been upstairs and come down,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said.

**“You have been upstairs for three days and I have not asked you about it and I am not going to,” the man of twenty-five said, “and I would rather you did not tell me, because if you tell me then I know it, and then I am a man who knows a thing about a man in his own yard, and there is no way in this yard to be a man who knows a thing about a man in his own yard.”**

He said it at a bench-board, standing up, with about four men within thirty feet of him, and about four hundred yards down a lane a door was open and has been open since the spring and nobody in that lane has said anything about it.

The lime went on the heap at about the seventh hour and the man of about sixty stood in the open at the end of that yard and watched nine men do nine things and was spoken to by none of them, and a yard that has one man in it who is paid out of nothing is a yard that has somebody in it who can be left alone in a way the other nine cannot.

**“I will tell you what I have learned this week and I have learned it from a lid,” he said. “A lid that is the same lid in Thawmonth and in Mudmonth and in Longlight is not the same lid. There is a warp in it that was not in it and it is going to take the ninth of a ton of lime off the heap before the season is out, and there is nobody in this county I could say that to who would not want to know why I know it.”**

Nobody asked him why he knows it. Nine men in a yard have heard about the lid about four hundred times and not one of them has asked him anything, and a man of about sixty who packs lime is the only man in that yard who is not paid out of it, and the yard has decided in the ordinary way over about nine years not to ask him, and the deciding was never made by anybody and is the closest thing that yard has to a rule.
