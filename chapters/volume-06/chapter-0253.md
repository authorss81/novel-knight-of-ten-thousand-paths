# Chapter 0253

**The twenty-seventh day of Goatmonth, YR 315, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the third hour and the sixth hour, and a market four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and a room over a weigh-house at the north end of a lane with a door on that lane, and a press with about four hundred documents in it that anybody can be served out of on the order of a body corporate, and four hundred people in this town who do not know that a letter which came in the ordinary post about two years ago was opened at about the third hour on a market Saturday morning by a second copyist of about thirty who has walked past it four times a day since the summer before last.**

The market was on, as it is on a Thursday and on a Saturday and on nothing else, and there was nothing in it that had not been in it in the spring, and a woman of about thirty with a pail was at the corner of it selling nothing that anybody bought twice, and a carrier of about fifty with a waggon of his own came up the lane at the second hour and went straight on past the door on the lane because he had nothing for that door, and that is the ninth week running, and a carrier who has nothing for a door can go up a lane of four hundred yards and come back, and nobody in that lane has ever asked him to knock.

**And the tenth letter was opened at about the third hour, and the whole of the third hour is that, and it took about a minute and a half to open and about four seconds to read.**

There have been ten letters on the second shelf of that office in about two years. The nine of them were opened inside a week of arriving and the tenth has been on that shelf since a day nobody in that room has written down, and it is a sheet folded once across the middle and soft at the fold, and there is nothing on the outside of it but the name of the office, and a market town in a district of about nine market towns, and a date about two years old, and a mark on the back of the fold that is a line and not a seal and was made with a ferrule.

There is no sender. That is the first fact about it and it is the only fact about it that has held for about two years, and a second copyist of about thirty who has walked past that shelf four times a day has known it for about two years and has not opened it and has not asked about it, and a woman of twenty-nine in the same room has not asked about it either, and there is no not-asking entered and there is not going to be one.

The copyist opened it and put the letter on the table and read it standing up, which is a thing she does and is a thing the keeper of the room does not do, and the two of them have never compared notes about that and neither of them is going to. A copyist reads standing up because standing up is faster. A keeper reads standing up because a bench in that office is for two people and not for one.

---

**And what was in it is one question, in a fair hand, nine lines long, and there is no second thing in it and no enclosure and nothing at the foot of it and no name at the top of it or the bottom of it.**

The question is this. Somebody in a market town has been sent nine forms of the office's own printed series. He has sent back one. He would like to know whether the other eight are still wanted, and if they are not he would like to be told so in a line he can keep, and if they are he would like to know when they are coming again, because he has a shelf and there is a woman in his house who is seven and there is a thing he has been told he must have in writing.

A county does not write to a county. There is no form in that office for a letter from a market town asking a question about a form, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building into nine counties and nine of the four hundred come back, and there is no register of the three hundred and ninety-one that do not, and there is no column for a form that has gone out and not come back, and the office has been counting what comes back since the press was made and has never once counted what goes out.

**And the second half of the question is the half that matters, and it is one sentence, and it is the only sentence in nine lines that is about a column, and a second copyist of about thirty read it twice and did not say anything about it either time.**

*And if there is a place on the sheet for the name of a person, will you tell me whether I am to write my own in it, or leave it, because I have left it, and I have left it nine times.*

Nobody improved on it. A keeper of records who has entered five questions in a register over about five months and has had no answers to any of them stood about four feet off and read it over the shoulder of a second copyist of about thirty, and read that one sentence, and read it twice, and said nothing either time, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who has not said one word about a thing for about five months read a sentence about a column twice and said nothing about it, and that saying nothing twice is the same as saying nothing once and takes longer.

She did not answer it. That is the whole of it and it is a fact about the twenty-seventh day of Goatmonth, YR 315 in that room, and a keeper of records who is asked a question about a column by a stranger and has no answer and has a register of five of her own has about nine minutes in which to say so, and she said nothing in the nine minutes, and a second copyist of about thirty did not look up.

---

**And what the second copyist did with it is the only thing in this chapter that nobody in this county has an opinion about yet, and it is the best piece of machinery in a volume about forms, and it happened because a woman of about thirty had been the second copyist for six years and had wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last.**

The day-book is the only book in that office nobody can be served with. That is the whole of why it exists and it has existed for four years and a half and it has nine not-askings in it, and there is not going to be a tenth and the tenth would be a question asked of a man of thirty-two by a woman of twenty-nine in a room she owns. A post in the day-book means a place in the book where a person's own entry stands under that person's own name, and a second copyist of about thirty has wanted one since the summer before last and has said out loud that she is not going to get it and does not want it and that the two are the same thing.

She had a letter with no sender in it and a day-book with a post in it that nobody holds, and she did the ordinary thing, and the ordinary thing took about four seconds.

**She entered it. She wrote a head at the top of a fresh page and under the head she wrote the day and the hour and the place the letter came from, and under those she wrote a line that says the letter carried no sender, and that is four items in a column and the column is footed and the four items are the day, the hour, the town, and the absence. A head invented by a second copyist of thirty in a room with a keeper in it is the only thing that office has had made in it by anybody in four years and a half. The head has one line under it, and no second column, and no figure, and no name, and a keeper of records has looked at it and has not said whether it is right.**

The head says *Received without a sender*. Four words, in a fair hand, in a smaller hand than the hand of the woman of twenty-nine, because the second copyist of about thirty has a smaller hand, and the two hands are in the same book on the same page and the difference between them is about a quarter of an inch and a person who has not been in that room cannot see it and about four people who have been in that room can.

A thing entered in a day-book has a day against it. That is the whole of it and it is not a rule, it is what a book is, and four people in that lane have said since that a keeper of records who entered five questions and not one finding did it because a question can be asked a second time and a finding cannot, and that a letter from a stranger that has been entered under a head of a second copyist's own invention has a day against it and a person against it and is a finding about a market town in a district of about nine market towns, and that nobody in that room has ever entered a finding and that one was entered at about the fourth hour on a market Saturday by a woman who was not asked to enter one.

---

**And the eleventh does not exist, and the finding of that is the last thing anybody in that lane worked out in about two years and it was worked out by the woman who has said the thing about the eleventh once and has not said it since.**

The copyist said one thing about it at about the fourth hour and it was eleven words and it was in her own voice and she said it to the day-book and not to the keeper, and she has not said anything of that length in six years and did not say anything of that length in six years before that.

“**It was never going to be the eleventh that did it.**”

Nobody asked her what she meant and the keeper of that room did not ask her. A woman of thirty has spent two years being afraid of a letter and has opened the tenth one and got nothing, and the thing she was afraid of was never a number. It was a head of her own, and she has got one now, and it is on a page in a book that a county of about four thousand people cannot be served with.


---

**And the rest of that Saturday in that room was the ordinary work of a copying season that had been over for twenty-two days, and a county that stops copying when the season ends is a county that thinks the work was the season, and neither of the two women in that room has said so.**

A press in that room has about four hundred documents in it and about four hundred of them have gone out of it since Hearthmonth, and a fair copy and a working lie on a bench about four inches apart, and the working is the only piece of paper in that office anybody has ever had to be shown, and four people in four years and a half have been shown it, and every one of them was shown it in the rain.

The letter went on the bench with the rest, unfolded, face up, and it stayed face up for about two hours, and the copyist copied a column of nine names out of a roll that week with the letter four inches from her right hand and did not turn it over, and a woman who does not turn a thing over is a woman who has decided that the thing is not a thing that gets turned over, and nobody came in.

A keeper of records of twenty-nine worked on the fifth entry in a register for about a quarter of an hour that afternoon, and the fifth entry is a question, and it is the first of the five that names a day, and it went in under the rule on the eighteenth of Goatmonth in about four minutes, and she has entered the same question four times over five months and is not going to enter a fifth. A woman who enters a question four times and does not enter a fifth is a woman who has found out that a question is the only kind of paper that can be put in a book twice without making a person answerable, and she found that out in about five months and in a room with nobody in it.

**And she said nothing about the letter to the copyist, and the copyist said nothing about the register to the keeper, and the two of them worked in a room of eleven feet by nine until the light went at about the fifth hour. Two documents were made in that room on a market Saturday in Goatmonth by two women who were in the same room, and neither of them said one word to the other one about either, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that.**

**And at about the sixth hour the lane was quiet and the market had gone in and the reach had about forty men on it and the road was open over it and the salt had been coming over the first bay since the spring, and a bell with a band of forty-one rivets in it went at the fourth hour and a half by a boy and a carpenter, and about four hundred people in this town do not know that a question from a market town about a line on a form was written into a day-book at about the fourth hour on a Saturday under a head that a woman of thirty made up, and that the answer to it is going back to a market town in that district on the ordinary post in about three weeks, because a question is a thing that goes out and a finding is a thing that is produced, and that there is no column in that office for a person who would rather not have answered it.**
