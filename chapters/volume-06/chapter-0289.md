# Chapter 0289

**The first day of Harvestmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a chamber on the third floor of a building in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and another chamber two floors above the same room with a stove in it with a long table in it and a record on the head of that table and nobody at either end of it, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a court sat that morning, and about four hundred people in a county two hundred miles off who do not know it either.**

**The court accepted the injunction at about the fourth hour. It is a temporary injunction and it is against forced culling and it has a day on it, and a clerk of the Crown read the term of it out in the ordinary way at about the fourth hour and a half, and it holds unless a local council renews it in public, and a clerk of the Crown reading a term out loud is the only place in that kingdom where a thing that has to be renewed in public is ever going to be described, and that a room that can be entered a second time is a second hearing, and that the second cheapest thing in this kingdom is forgetting a thing in the same building on a different date.**

Nobody in that chamber said anything about the renewal and nobody in that chamber has any power to renew it.

“**Is there anybody in this county who can renew it,**” the woman of thirty-eight said, at about the fourth hour and a quarter.

“**There is nobody in this county at all,**” a clerk of the Crown said. “**This is a room about two hundred miles from any county that is on any paper I have.**”

“**Then write that down,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**It is in the minute already,**” the clerk said, “**in the ninth line, and I read it out about four minutes ago, and nobody in this room heard the ninth line.**”

And about four people in that building have said since that a Crown officer who has asked for nine years for a record to show what was asked has been told that the thing she asked for was in a line she did not hear, and that she believed him about it, and that the not hearing and the believing are the whole of what nine years does to a person in a chamber. and about nine hundred people in that town do not know it exists, and there is no council in that room and no council was written to and nothing in that proceeding asks anybody in a county to do anything at all.

---

And a sheet of that office's own printed series went out of that building with the minute on the back of it, and the minute has the term of the injunction in it, and the sheet went down two flights and out of the front door and into a bag, and a bag goes in the ordinary post, and about nine hundred copies of a form of that series go into nine counties in a year and this is not one of them and has not been counted with them.

The clerk of the Crown made it up at about the fifth hour and read it back once, and only once, and a clerk who reads a minute back once is doing the correct operation, and there is no practice in that chamber of reading anything twice and there never has been.

Nobody in that building put a courier in a bag and nobody thought of one, and the only thing anybody in a chamber can do with a thing that has stopped is put it in a bag, and a bag is ordinary, and a county two hundred miles off is a place a bag goes to whether anybody in the building sending it has ever been there or not.

---

**And two floors up there is a chamber with a long table in it that is not the table of three, and a record on the head of that table with a schedule of about two hundred and forty lines on the desk beside it, and the second hour came and went, and the third hour came and went, and nobody read anything into that record.**

The head of that record is the same head it has carried since the eighth of a month in the year before, and it is the head that says a schedule is to be complete before a date, and the date on it went by a month ago.

**And the first of Longlight was a month ago, and about nine people in that building have read that head about nine times in a month, and about four of them have worked out that the date on it has gone, and nobody has said so to anybody, and there is no column in that building for a head that is out of date, and a schedule that cannot be completed before a date that has been is not a schedule that is late. It is a schedule that has stopped.**

The clerk with the bad wrist was at the other end of that table with the good hand on the pen and about two hundred and thirty lines to go and nothing to copy, and he sharpened the pen and put it down and picked it up and sharpened it again, and a man who sharpens a pen twice in a morning has understood something about the morning and is not going to be the one to say it.

And the man of forty-four who is a member of the Culling Order and a classifier is not in that room, and his room is two doors down it and the stove in it is cold, and he has not been in that building since the Friday before, and nobody in that building has asked him where he is and nobody is going to and about four people have noticed the cold stove and two of them have not connected it to a man.

He read about two hundred and thirty lines in about two hours every Saturday and every Wednesday for about a year and argued with nine classifications in that time, and his want that the schedule be complete before a date was said once, in thirty-four words, at the fourth hour of the eighth of a month in the year before, to a clerk who was carrying a tray out of the room, and it is spent, and he did not say it again, and he is not going to say it again in any building in this kingdom.

**And a man who is not in a room on the morning his work stops is a man who has stopped coming, and about four people in that building have each found out on their own that he has not been in since the Friday, and about two of them have said so to the other two and the other two have not said anything, and a schedule that stops being late because the man who reads it has stopped coming is the same thing as a schedule that has gone out of the ordinary way and is not the same thing, and nobody in that building has ever worked out which of those two it is.**

---

**And that morning came to a column as well, and a clerk of the Crown read that one out at about the sixth hour, and the items in it are added in pence on the page before the total is said and not after.**

A sheet of the office's own printed series for the injunction is a penny. A clerk's fee for entering an injunction is fourpence. A light kept burning in a room with no window for one day is twopence. A messenger's hand for two hundred miles, and there is no rate for a messenger for two hundred miles printed in this county and the man who carries bags here has never been asked what it costs and would not be asked. A copy of a schedule of about two hundred and forty lines for the Crown, and there is no rate for a copy of two hundred and forty lines in this county and there is not going to be one.

*A penny and fourpence is fivepence, and fivepence and twopence is seven pence.*

Total: seven pence. And the fourpence a year that a body corporate in a fen county pays is in a different county and is not in that column, and the two shillings a month that a salt merchant charges for each of the two rooms over a weigh-house is not in that column either, and nobody in that building has added one to another of them, and being correct is the most expensive thing available to a person in a chamber, and a total of seven pence is the whole of what that came to.

---

**And at about the fifth hour a clerk of the Crown asked the woman of thirty-eight a question at the end of that proceeding and it was a question about a column and he asked it the way a clerk asks a thing he has to enter, and she answered it in one word.**

“**Is that a column.**”

“**Yes,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

And that is the whole of what she said in that chamber about a column, and a Crown officer who has spent nineteen sentences asking that a record show what was asked has been asked a question about a column at the end of the thing and has answered it in one word, and that the answer is correct, and that a correct answer to a question about a column is the ordinary work of an office and is not what she came into that room for.

---

**And the clerk of the Crown read the minute of that morning back at about the sixth hour in the ordinary way, and it took about a minute and a quarter, and the four sentences in it were the term of the injunction, the date it was made, that a thing asked in that chamber had been entered as a thing asked, and that a witness had spoken before he was asked, and a clerk of the Crown who has written a sentence into a margin that has no line for it read the minute back once in a room where the woman who asked for it was sitting, and she said *that is right*, which is the second time in nine years she has said those two words to a thing read back to her, and the first was on the second of a month in Thawmonth about nineteen sentences, and nobody in that chamber has worked out why she keeps saying it.**

And a woman of about thirty went down two flights at about the fifth hour and out of the front door of that building with a bag of her own, and the office entered no third sitting and there is nothing to hold her for, and nobody in that building said that the reason she was able to go was that the thing that was being held was over.

She came two hundred miles in a cart on the Friday of a month and got to a town of about nine hundred people at about the first hour and was put in a room at the end of a second-floor passage because the building had nowhere to put a person, and a warrant to produce named a room and a day and did not name a person, and she was in that town for about four weeks and she is going home in a cart and she has not written to anybody about any of it and nobody has written to her and nobody is going to.

**And a keeper of records two hundred miles off does not know that a woman has been in a building of hers for four weeks and is not going to be told, and a second keeper of twenty-six is in that county with a warrant for a third post in a day-book and it is still not signed, and the two women have never spoken about either of those and are not going to.**

---

**And at about the fourth hour and three quarters a man of about thirty-one with a satchel asked one question, and it was the only question asked by anybody in that chamber all day that was not asked of a witness.**

“**Is the schedule still going out.**”

“**The schedule is not going out,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**Then somebody should be told,**” the man said.

“**Nobody is to be told anything about it, and that is in the ninth line too,**” the woman of thirty-eight said, and about four people in that building have said since that a woman who has been refused twice in about four minutes by a line in a minute has gone back to her chair, and that she had read the ninth line in the minute in front of her and had not been told and had not asked, and that the not asking is in the office and not in the ninth line.

And a man of about fifty at the back of that chamber was entered as a witness for the third time and asked nothing for the third time, and a true thing about a column that he said out loud in that building in about two years was not thanked on that morning and has not been thanked since and is not going to be, and a boy of nineteen with eleven weeks to serve before the next sitting wants to be a journeyman and stood at the foot of those stairs at about the sixth hour and did not go down to the press room and did not say why, and a word is not printed on a list any more and is not struck in a case any more and a man who set it once is standing at the top of a staircase about four months later, and nobody in that building has ever worked out what the difference is between those two facts and about two of them think about it perhaps twice a year.

---

**And about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that a thing they were frightened of has stopped being written down, and about four hundred people in a provincial town two hundred miles off do not know either, and an office that has gone out of the ordinary way has not been written to and does not know that it has, and nobody in that building has told it and nobody is going to, and about four hundred people in a county of about four thousand people on a river are going to hear about it from a man with a bag on a Saturday, and nobody in that building has entered that a man with a bag is a thing a county cannot refuse.**
