# Chapter 0276

**The twelfth day of Longlight, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour, and a crossing four hundred yards up a fen county's own road that carries nobody and carried nobody for four years before anything happened to it and carries nobody now, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that the forty yards of tarred hemp that were in a dressed block in the abutment of that crossing are not in it, and that nobody in that county has looked, and that nobody is going to, and that the man who put them in there did it himself about a year ago and has not been near the place since.**

That is not a refusal and it is not a shape. A thing done at the second hour of a Tuesday seven days after a promise was said out loud in a field four miles away can be set down in about nine words, and every one of the nine would be a lie, and so it is not set down, and the fourth day after it is the first day anybody in that county knows anything about it, and the reason that county knows anything at all is that the weather took a sheet out of a sill and not that anybody said so.

What the weather did was take the sheet out of the sill.

---

**The sill is a foot and a half wide and it has no glass in it and no third seat, and it has nothing in it, and it had nothing in it on the day before this one, and it has nothing under it now.**

Forty yards of tarred hemp went into the abutment at the near end of that crossing on the fourth of a month in the year before, by four men and a boy, and it went in four feet down, and it was put in for a job that is not this one, and nobody has dug for it and nobody is going to dig for it, and a hemp that is four feet down under a dressed block in a county of about four thousand people that has no writ and no seal and no office and cannot be served with anything is a hemp that a man with a stick is the only man in the county who could ever have got at, and he has a permanent limp in the right leg.

The dressed block is squared and it is about nine inches by six by four and it is in the abutment with the mortar round it, and there is a line in the face of it about nine inches long. It is in the stone and not across a joint and it is not in a joint. It has not moved. Nobody has measured it, nobody has ruled across it, nobody has laid a rule on it, and no person in that county has put a finger on it or is going to.

And a line about nine inches long in a dressed block is the same length as a line about nine inches long in a stone course in another structure in this county, and that is a fact about two pieces of work done by the same hands in the same trade, and no person in this county knows of the other one, and the two have never been in one book and are not going to be.

There is no column in this county for a thing in a stone. A county that keeps a register of claims and a day-book of things that were not asked and a page under a stair with a count of askings on it has three kinds of paper in it and a line in a dressed block is not one of the three and cannot be put in any of them, and there is no office two hundred miles off that would take it either, because a line in a stone is not a place and not a person and not a date and not an hour, and the sheet it would have to go on has four places ruled for a figure and nowhere at all for the name of a person and no head on the sixth column at all.

---

**And he could not get up the bank, and that is the whole of what a man of thirty-two with a limp got out of a crossing on the fourth day after, and it took him about four minutes and it did not take him up it.**

The fourth day after is a Saturday and a market day and about four hundred people were in a street four hundred yards away and about nine men were on a reach in that lane with the road open over it, and the salt came over the first bay about the second hour and went down about the seventh, and a man of fifty and a half who does the arches and does the pointing and does not do the laying was on that reach all day because a reach does not stop for a market, and a reach has about forty men on it every working day whatever else in the county is happening and nobody in that yard has ever asked the yard to have a Saturday off.

A man of about thirty-two with a limp was not on it and had not been on it for a week and about four men in that yard had noticed and about four had not, and a boy of fifteen at a bench-board with a bill under his arm copied a number under a number and a rule under a number and the empty space under the rule, which is the widest of the four, and he has copied that space eleven times and has never once been told what goes in it, and nothing goes in it, and that is what the space is for.

He got about two feet and sat on the stone with the mortar on and put his hands out in front of him, the way he had sat on it on the Monday, and he stayed there for about nine minutes, and about four hundred yards down the lane a market was on and about four hundred people were in it and a bell a hundred yards off that goes at the fourth hour and a half whether there is anybody on the crossing or not, and nobody in that county heard anything at all happen on the morning of the twelfth day of Longlight, YR 316, and nobody heard anything the day before it either, and nobody heard anything on the day of it.

And a bell that goes at the fourth hour and a half whether there is anybody on the crossing or not is a bell this county can be asked about and cannot answer for, and the answer nobody in that county can give is the finding, and nobody in that county has worked out that the answer is the bell and not the crossing, and nobody is going to, and the two of those are the same fact seen from two ends and this county has never put them in the same book.

A man of forty-seven came up the lane at about the fourth hour and a half with a hod he was not carrying anything in and he was in the toll house for about eleven minutes and nobody asked him anything, and he did not say the four things, and the four things have now been said five times in a year and there is no sixth occasion on any list anybody keeps.

**“You have not asked me a single thing in two years,” he said, to nobody in particular, “and I have never once taken it as a kindness, and I have never once taken it as anything else, and I would like it recorded that I have thought about it a good deal more than the other people in that room have.”**

Nobody in that toll house recorded it. The clerk of a bench of nine was four hundred yards away in a long room and was not told, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was in the toll house and did not write it down and was not asked to, and a man of forty-seven has said a thing about a room in which nobody recorded it and has said nothing else about it since.

A woman of about thirty with a pail went up that lane at about the fifth hour, nine doors, one sentence at a door, and moved on before the knuckle had finished ringing in the frame.

**“They have been in and out of that house all week,” she said. “I did not see who and I am not going to stand about it.”**

That is the whole of what any person in that lane knows about the morning of the twelfth of Longlight, and it is the ninth of those sentences in about a year and about four hundred people in that town have not asked her a single one of the nine why she says them, and she has not offered.

---

A man of fifty-five sat in a hut at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards down that road and has been in that hut nineteen years, and on that Saturday he turned round about nine times and let them all by and he did not see a cart and he did not see a barrow and he did not see a man with a stick, and he has never once been told what is four hundred yards up the road from him and is not going to be.

“**Somebody went up there on Tuesday,**” he said, to a carter of thirty who was passing.

**“They go up there all week,” the carter of thirty said, and did not stop, and has a barrow and a waggon of his own and has said two things out loud in two days to nobody and would not mind a toll and is not going to say so to anybody either, and a man of fifty-five has been the fourth person in a week to say something ordinary to him and got an ordinary answer and has not noticed the shape of it.**

---

**And that is the fourth day after, and this is what he said out loud on the bank at about the fifth hour to a county that heard nothing, and it was not said to anybody, and he has had four of those in four years and has written one of them down, and the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it.**

*Somebody in this county once had a reason for a thing that was put in a dressed stone.*

*Nobody wrote it down and nobody is going to ask him and he is not in this county.*

He said them standing up with the wind behind him and he did not write them down and he was not in a room, and about four hundred yards down that lane about nine men in a yard were standing in it and about four hundred people in a market were standing in it and neither of those two groups knew that anything had been said, and the second of those two lines is about a man who is four hundred yards away and cannot be asked and has not said it in this county in a year and is not going to.

---

And on the third day of that illness he could not get out of bed, and on the fifth day a man of about thirty came up the stairs of a room over a smithy with a pail of something and put it on the table and went out again without asking him a question, and nobody had sent him and he said so at the door, and a man of thirty-two with a limp did not ask him his name and has not since.

The fever runs about nine days and it is the second one in about two months and it is the same fever and it is not worse. A county that has a bench and a market and about forty men on a reach four hundred yards down a lane does not stop in a week of weather, and the fourth bay is finished, and the salt went over the first bay for the tenth week running, and a man of fifty and a half who does the arches and does the pointing and does not do the laying went up a ladder and came down it again, and a boy of fifteen was at a bench-board with a bill under his arm and no bill on the board.

Nobody in that yard was told that the man who used to stand at it for about an hour and a half every market morning was ill, and nobody in that yard asked, and a yard is a place where a man can be away for nine days and about four men notice and about four do not, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp has not been out of a room over a smithy since the twelfth day of Longlight, and that the only reason anybody in this county knows anything about the fourth of that month is that the weather took a sheet out of a sill.
