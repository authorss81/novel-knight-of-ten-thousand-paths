# Chapter 0270

**The sixteenth day of Greenmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the fourth hour, and a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane in a fen county, and a press in it that can be served with and a press four hundred yards up Weir Street that cannot, and a bag that went out in the ordinary post on the Friday before, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people on a river who do not know that a name went into a column of that county's own form at about the fourth hour of that Monday, and that the column has a head on it, and that the man whose name it is is four hundred miles off in a valley in a county that is not on any map, and that he does not know, and that about four hundred people in that valley are not going to be told.**

The bag went on the Friday and it cost eight pence, and the eight pence was added and footed in that yard and is not in this room. A bag in the ordinary post is the first thing that has ever left that yard in the ordinary way. The yard has been sending nothing anywhere for about two years, and a yard which sends nothing can be ignored and a yard which sends something cannot.

There were two people in the room and there would have been three.

The keeper of records is twenty-nine and has kept this office four years and a half out of two rooms over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, out of a deed of thirteen words, at fourpence a year, and in which keeper is employment and is not jurisdiction, and the second copyist is about thirty and is on a warrant that is not in the press and has wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last.

---

**And the scales under that room have not been used since the fourth of that month, and there is a press in it with two unserved books in it and a day-book and a half tile that weighs a shade over ten ounces, and there is a fair copy on the wall in a keeper's own hand that is nineteen years old and that says no combined figure is entered in this office, and that fair copy has never once been read out loud in that room by anybody including the keeper of records. A rule in a fair copy on a wall that nobody reads out loud is a rule that is obeyed and not known, and that office has obeyed it for nineteen years and has never once printed what it costs.**

The press in that room can be served with anything and the press four hundred yards up Weir Street cannot, and there is a bag of ordinary paper on the shelf with the findings on it, and about four people in that lane have said since that the only thing that separates a claim that can be produced from a claim that cannot is about four hundred yards of street and whose ground it stands on, and that this office has known that for four years and a half and has never written it down because writing it down would be a second column with something in it.

---

The county's form is ruled in seven. **What is given. To whom, in the county's words. By whose hand. The day. The hour. A witness at this end. A witness at that end.**

**And the seventh of those is a ruled space about two inches deep with nothing in it, and the rule at the bottom of it is no more faded than the rule at the bottom of the third, and it has been in this office's use for four years and a half and it has been filled with nothing on nine occasions. A column left empty nine times by nine different people who all decided not to put a person in it is not a column nobody can use. It is a column nobody has been shown how to use.**

The man of thirty-two came in at about the fourth hour with the form folded once in a coat and put it on the table and did not sit down. A man who puts a sheet on a table in that office and does not sit down has been in nine rooms with a clerk in each of them and has learned the shape of the table. A man who sits down in that office is a man who is about to be at something for a while.

He filled the first column in about four minutes and the second in about two and the third in a second and then he stopped with the pen on the rule and stopped for about a minute and a half.

“To the persons who may require this office to produce the same,” he said, reading it back, “and to no other, and to no person, there being none.”

Nobody in that room had heard him say that before. He read the second column back first and he did not have to, and reading it back is what a person does in a room in which they are about to do a thing they cannot undo.

The day went in. Then the hour, separately, with the rule between them, because the fourth of the seven is the day and the fifth is the hour and they are two columns and there is a fair copy on the wall in this room nineteen years old that says no combined figure is entered in this office and that has never once been read out loud in that room by anybody including the keeper of records.

**And a witness at this end is a man of forty-seven who is on a bench of nine because a man in a market asked who was going and he put his hand up, and that name went into the sixth column in about four seconds and nobody in that room asked him anything. A name in the sixth column of that form cost four seconds. A name in the seventh column is going to cost something else, and neither of them is a price and neither of them is added to anything.**

---

**And the seventh column got a name in it at about the fourth hour and a half on that Monday, and it is the name of a man of fifty-eight four hundred miles off who keeps the only roll in a valley of about four hundred people, and he did not enter it, and the second copyist of that office entered it, and she entered it in about nine seconds and did not look up and did not say anything about it and has not asked for it to be taken out and is not going to.**

The keeper of records said one thing about it and it was at about the fifth hour and it was not a refusal and it was not an objection and it was about the office and not about the man, and it was fourteen words.

“**A name in a column of this office is a claim about a person.**”

Nobody improved on it and nobody answered it and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine has said in fourteen words in about four years and a half the whole of what a claim is, and that the claim in that sentence is not the one on the table and is the one about the ninth line of a second column in a book four hundred miles off, and that this office has had that one in a leaf in its press for about a year and a half and did not write it and is not going to make another, and that the two of those are the same sentence with two different people in it and that about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know that either of them exists.

The third column says by whose hand, and the hand is a second copyist of about thirty, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who has wanted a line in a book with her name in it for two years has put her own name in the third column of a finding of this office and her own name against a different person's name in the seventh, and that those are two names on one sheet and one of them is a claim about a person and the other one is a hand, and that nobody in that room has ever made the difference between a hand and a person in a column and that the difference is nineteen years old and is on the wall and has never been read out loud in that room.

**And the man of thirty-two did not argue with the fourteen words and did not thank her for them, and he was in that room for about an hour and a half, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who does not argue and does not thank is a man who has heard the sentence and has not got an answer to it, and that he has had four words ready since a day he cannot count and did not say them in that room, and that nobody in that lane knows he has them and that about four hundred people in that town do not know that he was in a room over a weigh-house at all.**

---

**And the finding was read back once, at about the sixth hour, by the second copyist, and the reading was slower than the writing, and that has been the practice in that office since the first year, and nobody asked for it a second time and nobody has asked for it a second time in nine years.**

The keeper of records did not read it. The second copyist read about nine lines of it and stopped twice and neither stop was at a place anybody had asked her to stop at, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman reading a finding back once and stopping twice is a woman who has found two things in a document of her own office that nobody has asked her about, and that she did not say what they were, and that a person who has found two things in a document and has not said what they are is a person who is going to be asked about them one day by somebody, and that the day is not this one.

---

**And the day-book of that office is the only book in that office nobody can be served with and it has nine entries in it of a thing that was not asked, and there is not going to be a tenth, and nobody entered one on that Monday, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man who has been in that room for an hour and a half and has not been asked one question has not put a tenth in a book and a woman of twenty-nine who wanted to write down that she did not use a thing she has had ready for about a year has not put one in either, and that the two of them were in the same room at the same hour on the same Monday and neither of them asked the other one anything and neither of them is going to.**

The finding went on the shelf with the number against that office against it and nothing against a person, and the shelf is the shelf and not the press, and the press is for the two unserved books and a day-book and a half tile that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is the account of nobody.

**And about four hundred people in a valley four hundred miles off went on that day as they go on every day of it, and nine of them were in a room over a cross on that day, and one of those nine is a man of fifty-eight who has entered one finding in a book that has had none in a hundred and forty years and is not a member of anything, and he does not know that his name is in a column four hundred miles off, and nobody in that valley is going to tell him, and about four hundred people in that valley keep a memory of a man who came up a road nine days and read a book aloud and stood in an aisle and answered a question, and not one of them knows that anything has been done about it.**

And the bag was in the ordinary post with a word in front of a place name on it that nobody in this county has ever said out loud, and a name in a column of a form in a room over a weigh-house is a claim about a person, and a claim about a person is the only kind of paper in this kingdom that can be produced in a room a long way off, and there is no column in this office for what is done to a claim after it has been produced, and about four hundred people in a town of about four thousand people do not know any of it and are not going to be told.
