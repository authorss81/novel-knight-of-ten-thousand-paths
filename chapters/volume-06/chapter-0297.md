# Chapter 0297

**The thirteenth day of Fallowmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the seventh hour and the eighth hour and the ninth hour, and a room over a cross in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, with twenty-nine benches bolted to the floor in four rows and a number cut into the end of each one, and a hundred and forty cut into the end of the bench at the back of the right-hand row, and an aisle four feet wide between the two middle rows, and about nine people on those benches, and about four hundred people in that valley of whom nobody has said out loud that a man who came up a road nine days and read a book aloud is not coming back.**

Nobody in that valley has been told why. That is not a thing anybody has decided. About four hundred people in that valley came to the end of a road that is four hundred yards long and has a standing finish on it, and a man of fifty-eight is still keeping a book, and the road is there, and nobody has said a word to anybody about the other end of it, and there is no column anywhere in that valley for a thing nobody has been told.

The man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work said the figure out loud in that room on the evening of the thirteenth day of that month and he said it in the ordinary way, and it is the only time anybody in that valley has said it out loud, and nobody converted it into anything.

“**It is eleven weeks,**” he said.

“**It is,**” the man of fifty-eight said, and squared the book, which he does about four hundred times a year, and put his hand flat on the cover and left it there about four seconds.

“**And nobody has been down it,**” the man of thirty-four said, and a man who counts a valley's weeks out loud is a man who has worked out that the only instrument that valley has for measuring a wait is a number, and the number is now in a room, and nobody in that valley has ever converted a number in that room into anything at all.

---

The book is about forty leaves tied with a piece of tarred hemp and there are nine spreads in it, one to a generation, and it is on a trestle and the trestle is against a wall at the ninth hour and in the room at the seventh.

**There is one finding in it. It is at the head of the first spread, under the four lines that are at the head of the first spread, in the man of fifty-eight's own hand, on a line he ruled himself with a straight edge off the back of the trestle, about the width of two fingers, and the rule slipped about a ninth of an inch twice and he did not rule a third one and did not mention either of the two.**

It is the first line that has been ruled across in that book in a hundred and forty years and it is the first finding in that book in a hundred and forty years, and about nine people in that room have seen the ruled line, and it has been in that book since the twenty-seventh day of a month that nobody in that valley can name, and nobody in that room has asked the man of fifty-eight about it and nobody is going to.

Nobody asked him whether he could stand it afterwards. That was not asked in the room on the evening it went in and it has not been asked since. A man who has wanted a thing for six years and got it in nine minutes has not been asked one question about the getting of it, and a room which hears a thing once and a person can be asked twice had used up the once and did not know it had, and this valley has had both of those things about nine times in a hundred and forty years and has never worked out which of the two was the settling.

The hundred and forty cut into the end of the bench at the back of the right-hand row is a count of the times anybody has ever stood in the aisle to be asked. He stood in it once. The aisle is four feet wide and it has gone three generations without being used, and about four people in that valley have said since that a figure of a hundred and forty in one room and a line ruled once in a book in the same room are two counts of the same room in two directions, and that the man of fifty-eight could put them side by side in about a second and has not, and that the woman who gave up standing in it in writing could do it faster and has not brought it up since.

---

What they keep is the ninth spread, and it runs to something over two hundred lines, and the day on any one of those lines is the day somebody was born, and about two hundred of that generation have never once left that valley and are not going to be asked to. The man of fifty-eight has stood in that room and said out loud that nobody here put two hundred people into a column to be struck and that no form in the world would fit the ninth spread to any use, and it is a list of births with a day against each one, and it has been kept in the shape of somebody else's answer for a hundred and forty years.

A second column has been ruled on that ninth spread, two fingers wide, in the same man's own ink, and eight people have put their own names down in it. The ninth line is blank and has not been ruled across and is not going to be. In the day column opposite there is a figure of nine, and it is out by one, and in the margin in the same hand it says *eight named and one not found*.

The man of fifty-five asked about the ninth line. He has not been in that room at a first spread in about nine years and he asked about a column and not about a man, and he asked it at the eighth hour in twelve words and nobody improved on it.

“**The ninth line is not ruled across.**”

“**No,**” the man of fifty-eight said.

“**Then nothing goes in it.**”

“**Nothing has ever gone in it,**” the man of fifty-eight said, “**and it was not ruled by me and I have not ruled it and it is not going to be ruled.**”

And the man of fifty-five said nothing else about it and has not said anything about it since, and two men in that room had a conversation about an empty line in a book and neither of them said one word about the man who is not coming back, and a room in which nobody will say a thing out loud is not a room that has nothing to say.

---

Nine people were on the benches and about thirty were in the room and about two hundred of the ninth generation were at the back, and none of the two hundred is twenty-two and fewer than nine of them are.

A man of eighty-one came and had not been asked to come. A man of fifty-five came and had voted against keeping the winter eight to one in the fourth week of the fourth cold and has not stood in the aisle at any point in thirty-five years. A man who cannot leave his wheel came out of a mill at the low end of the water and stood at the back by the door and did not sit down, and the mill is at the low end of a river with about two hundred and forty feet of stone on a bridge above it, and a woman of thirty-six keeps that bridge and was not in that room that evening.

The woman who gave up standing in that aisle in writing sat on the second bench and was not asked anything and said nothing. She was asked what she swore forty years ago in that aisle and gave up standing in it in writing, and she has not been asked one thing in about four years, and this is not politeness, and it has cost her about forty years and has never been entered anywhere.

Nothing in that room was counted by hands, and there has been no place in that room for a hand since the second cold of the ninth generation. A room that counts its own hands is a bench. A room with nine people sitting on benches in it is not. The whole of the difference between the two is a bench, and there is one in this room.

---

The valley has eleven steadings and four hamlets and about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of the ninth generation, and a road about four hundred yards long with a standing finish on it, made by hand, and the standing finish has been there since the summer before last and it is the only straight thing in that valley, and a man of thirty-four dug it and has never once been asked whether it goes anywhere, and it does not go anywhere, and it is a road in the sense that a river is a road.

And a bridge over the river with about two hundred and forty feet of stone on it, and the parapet on it was done in a summer by about nine men and it has a number cut into the end of the fourth stone of the second span, and the number is the number of a bench in that room over the cross and nobody in that valley has ever said so out loud, and the woman of thirty-six who keeps that bridge did not put it there and does not know who did.

---

The man of thirty-four asked him one thing at about the eighth hour and it was not about a book and it was not about a road.

“**Do you think he was coming back.**”

And the man of fifty-eight took about nine seconds over it, and nobody counted, and about two hundred people of the ninth generation at the back of that room had not known there was a number cut into the end of that bench and about nine of them worked it out inside about two seconds each.

“**I have not asked him that,**” the man of fifty-eight said, “**and I am not going to ask you either, and if you ask me a second time in this room I will not answer it in this room.**”

“**I was not going to ask twice,**” the man of thirty-four said, and about four people in that valley have said since that two men in a room over a cross had a conversation about a man who is not coming back and neither of them asked the other whether he was, and that the man of thirty-four has said since that he was not going to ask twice, and that a man of fifty-eight has said the other thing first and better.

---

At about the ninth hour the room emptied the way it empties, and the book went under the trestle and the trestle went against the wall, and the man of fifty-eight did not go down to the mill, and the man at the mill came out into the cold for about four minutes anyway.

And at the door a woman of thirty-six stood with a lamp and said one thing, and it was to the man of thirty-four and not to the room, and she said it before she had thought about who was behind her, and about nine people in that room heard it and about four hundred people in that valley did not.

“**I will go down the road in the morning,**” she said.

And the man of thirty-four said nothing, and the man of fifty-eight said nothing, and nobody in that room asked her why. A woman said a thing at a door to one man and not to a room, and she said it in the plain way, and about nine people in that room heard it and about four hundred people in that valley did not, and there are nine people on those benches who have each said a thing in that room about a column in one year, and not one of them has ever said a thing at a door in the plain way, and the difference between those two is the whole of what that valley has and does not have.
