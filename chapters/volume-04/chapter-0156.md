# Chapter 0156

The seventh of Goatmonth, YR 313, was a Thursday and it was not a market day, and the county post went up the hill and did not come down, and about forty men were on the Sedge Reach with the fourth cart of eleven tons on the gravel approach and the pointing begun for the third time in nine days.

He made the nine up on the fourth step of the stair over a weigh-house at about nine minutes past the fourth hour of the afternoon, with the book out and his knee against the rail, and it took him nine minutes, and seven of the questions took him about four seconds each and the two he could not took him the rest of the time and neither of them got a figure.

He wrote the hour against each one because the trade says to, and because he has never once been able to find a reason not to, and because in four years and a half nobody has ever been able to find a reason for him to stop.

*One. What the place is called.* A valley with a river in it that the people there call the Burn, and a book that a man of fifty-eight has kept nineteen years, and the man's name, and the fact that the valley is in no map in this kingdom and in no book in this county. That is enough to name a road and a man who has never named a road has to be able to do it in four seconds, and it took three and a half.

*Two. The moment it turned on.* A man wrote a letter because he wanted a book read, and he had wanted it for six years, and in the sixth year he wrote down the reason he could not fix it by wanting it less, and that is a choice and it has not been taken back and it is not going to be.

*Three. What is still standing of it.* The letter. Nineteen years of a book. A man with a hearth.

*Four. Who is at the other end.* A stone room over a byre, and a roll nine generations deep, and about two hundred people on it from birth, and no writ in the valley and no bell in it and no office, and a woman of forty-one — no. He wrote the fourth answer and then struck a word out of it, and the word was *holder*, and there is a title in this country for the person a community appoints to authorise one named contact, and it carries consent in it, and he does not know that anybody in that valley has appointed anybody to anything, and a man who writes *holder* into a page about a valley he has not been in is a man who has put a Crown's word on a thing because there was a space for it.

*Five. What the going and the coming are called here.* He could not answer this one.

*Six. What a day is.* He could not answer this one either, and he had known since Tuesday that he could not, and the not-answering took the best part of the four minutes because a man who sits on a step trying to convert one country's day into another's will go on trying past the point where it is worth anything, and he stopped, and he wrote *no frame* in the two spaces, and *no frame* is a figure and it is the first figure of its kind he has ever entered.

*Seven. Somebody on this side who can say it was made and not what it was for.* That one took four seconds and it had a name in it before he had finished the sentence, and the name is not in the book and will not be in the book, because the second of that county's six terms is that no name is to be written in the line's book at the north end and he has been in this county long enough to know that a line's book is a line's book, and the term was not written about him and it is about everybody.

*Eight. What it is for.* No figure.

*Nine. Whether anybody at the other end has been asked.* No figure, and under this one he wrote a sentence, and it is the only sentence in the whole nine that is not a question.

*Nine. Nobody there has been asked. I am going anyway. I wrote that down so that it would be in front of me at the fourth hour tomorrow and I would have to read it, and I have read it, and I am still going, and a man who writes a thing down so that he will be frightened by it in the morning has understood about half of what that does.*

**And the witness at this end is a man of forty-seven who is on a bench because he was elected and is paid fourpence a week out of a private box, and he is the only person in this county who can be produced about this and cannot be got at.**

Bram Ottery came up the two flights at about the seventh hour on the seventh of Goatmonth because Aren Kest asked him to and gave no reason, which is a thing that has happened to that man about four times in four years and a half and that he has never once said was strange.

“You want me to stand somewhere and say I saw it,” he said.

“I want you to stand somewhere and say what you saw. If you saw nothing, that is what you say.”

“That is a very thin job.”

“It is the only job there is.” Aren Kest put the notebook on the sill, open, at the page with the nine on it, and did not turn it round, and the man looked at it for as long as he liked, which was about four seconds, because Bram Ottery is not a man who reads other men's pages.

“What am I standing next to,” he said.

“An abutment. Forty yards of tarred hemp went into it on the fourth of Hearthmonth of the year before with three other men and a boy, and there is a dressed block in it, and it is the crossing four hundred yards up the road with no owner and no money and no glass in the sill and no third seat.”

“I know the crossing. I have voted for the money for it four times and I was not in the room for the fifth, and I would like that minuted, because I heard at the wharf on Saturday that the fifth was put.”

“It was put and it was refused and the number of yeses is four and the number of times it has been asked is five, and that is in the minute in the clerk's hand and you can read it.”

“Then I will say this,” said Bram Ottery. “I will stand at the abutment on Friday at about the fourth hour and I will say that a man I know was at that crossing at that hour and that I saw him and that I do not know what he did there, and I will not be able to tell anybody in nine years that he did the thing he came to do, because I did not see the thing he came to do, and if anybody asks me what I witnessed I will have to say I witnessed a man being on a bridge, and that is a very thin job, and I am willing to do it, and I would like it written down that I said I was willing to do it before I did it and not after.”

A man with a vote and not an office asked another man with a vote and not an office to stand on a bridge and say nothing, and the other man said yes and wanted it written down that he had said yes first.

It is in the minute, and it is the first entry in that minute that anybody has ever entered at the request of the person it was about, and the clerk wrote it in the ordinary way and did not know what it was, and the reeve initialled it because that is what the reeve does, and the chest has two marks in it and has had two since the ninth of Fallowmonth.

**And a letter from a valley four hundred miles off went into a county council's minute on the seventh of Goatmonth and was read out on the Saturday, and the man who read it out had not read it before and had four things of his own on the agenda.**

Marda Oakes came up the two flights at about the eighth hour on the seventh with a folder and a card with a name and a county and a room and no seal and no office, and she had read the letter on Wednesday in a county council room in about four minutes, which is the ordinary speed, and she said the thing that had to be said about it before anybody else got a hand on it.

“This is a letter from a county that is not in this county's book, to a filing office, asking for a man to read a book, and it is dated inside a month of its own year and not in ours, and there is no carriage offered and none accepted, and the man says he will not pay one because he does not know how.”

“Then it is a request for a favour with no money in it,” said the reeve, “and this bench has a rule about those and the rule is Toban's sentence and I am not going to say it.”

“I am not asking this bench for a favour. I am handing it a letter that came to a door on a lane and that I read because I am the clerk of the council and the council has to know what is put in front of it.” She put the folder down. “I have brought it to Saturday. I would like it read out, and I would like the reading minuted with the hour, and I would like the minute to say that it was received and that nothing was done with it, and I would like the minute to say that in those words, and not *referred*, because *referred* is a word that means somebody is going to come back to it.”

Nobody argued with her about that, because she stopped in the middle of a sentence a year ago in a long room over a wharf and said why, and the second copyist entered the half sentence, and the reason has not changed.

So it was read out on the Saturday in a room over a weigh-house by a bench of nine that has a vote and not an office in it, and the minute says that the letter of a man of fifty-eight from a valley not in this county's book, asking for a man who can read a hand, was received, and that nothing was done with it, and that no person in this room knew whether such a valley existed, and that the hour was the second.

It is the shortest minute this county has ever entered about a thing four hundred miles away, and it is one of about nine, and none of the nine has a name in it, and the ninth not asking is in a different book in a different room and it stands.

**And he was not in that room, and he did not ask to be, and the county did not send anybody, and the delegation for the ninth of Rainmonth is not to be sent for and the hearing is not adjourned and is not refused and it stands.**

**And at about the ninth hour of the seventh of Goatmonth he was in the rooms over a cooper in Weir Street with a bag, a lamp, a notebook, and a coat, and there were four things in the coat and he knew exactly what they were, and the fourth of them is a rule in a fair copy in a room two hundred yards away.**

The first is a road. The second is a number, and the number is one hundred and seventeen days and a printed three weeks and ninety-six days of error, and it is in a leaf of returns in a press in a room with a door on a lane and it is in this notebook as well and the two are not the same record and neither is the original.

The third is a half tile in a bag made by himself behind a kiln with a saw mark on the back of it, and there is one of it in a press in this town that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is the account of nobody, and he has never asked whether the other one arrived and is not going to.

The fourth is a sentence somebody else wrote four months ago, in her hand, in a fair copy, on a page that cannot be served.

He did not write it down. He has never written it down. A thing written down in two books is a thing in a file, and he has spent four years learning that a file is a thing a person can be produced from, and the sentence is about him, and he has not told her, and there is no page in this notebook with it on.

He put the notebook in the bag. He did not take the lamp. He stood in the doorway for about a minute and a half, which he does, and then he went down the four steps on one leg and a hand on the frame and out into Weir Street in the rain, and the stick came with him because it goes four hundred yards and there are nine hundred yards of road in this county that he knows of and he is going to four hundred miles.

---

**And at about the fourth hour and a half on the seventh of Goatmonth the bell went four hundred yards up the road, and it goes every day of the year at the fourth hour and a half, and a Monday is the day that far end keeps, and this was not a Monday.**


The arrangement is a boy of seventeen and a carpenter, and it has been that since the ninth of Hearthmonth, and it was a boy and a ferrywright before that, and the page at the head of the yard's book for the week carries the hour against it in a hand somebody would know, and nobody wrote a word in it about who rang it and nobody is going to.

He did not go up the road to hear it and he did not ring it. He was on the deck of the third bay of the Sedge Reach at the fourth hour and a half with a stick in his hand and a cold chisel in his belt and a bag on the gravel, and the bell is four hundred yards from the third bay and about nine hundred from the parapet with the mark in it, and the arrangement has nothing to do with him, and nothing in the sound of it is his, and that is a Monday fact and this was not a Monday, and the two of those are in a day-book separately and are not in one sentence, because a book is a place where things get joined whether they were joined or not.

---

**And he went down Weir Street at about the sixth hour and the yard was shut with the churn still propping the top hinge, and the boy of fifteen was on the bench-board with the rushes, and there was nothing on the board that said anything about Friday, and he did not put anything on it.**

A thing set goes in a yard's book. A contract is a thing set. The salt is a thing set. The name is a thing set. The rest of it is not a thing set, because it is a figure a county in a market room agreed it could not pay, and a yard that puts that in a book has admitted a debt it cannot call in, and a book that knows what a man has decided not to do is an office. That rule is nineteen years old and it is on the wall in a hand belonging to a man who is dead, and the boy of fifteen copies the empty space under a number as well as the number, and the empty space under a date on a bill for a mill-race job is not the same thing as an empty space where a man should be.

The boy said, “You have got the bag.”

“I have got the bag.”

“You are not taking the shave horse.”

“I am not taking the shave horse.”

The boy went back to the board. Nobody in that yard asked him where he was going and nobody in that yard has ever asked him anything, and the rule is on the wall, and he read it when he was seventeen and it is the reason he has never once in two months asked what the five lines in that book are for.

He went up to the rooms over the cooper and put the bag down and stood in his own doorway for a moment, and the leg would not take him up the four steps and he got up them on one leg and a hand on the door frame, and the room had a lamp in it and a stick cut out of a fence rail leaning in the corner by the bed, and a notebook on the sill with nine lines in it and three of them with no figure in them at all.
