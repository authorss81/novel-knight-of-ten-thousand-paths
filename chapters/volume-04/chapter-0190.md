# Chapter 0190

**The eighth of Harvestmonth, YR 314, a Wednesday, and the third sheet of the finding is what it cost, and it is in the county's money, and the addition is done on the page before the total is read out.**

*Of the second copyist, one hour, at the county's rate of eight pence the hour:* **eight pence**.

*Of paper, two sheets, at twopence the sheet:* 2, twice over, is **four pence**.

*Of the room, one hour:* **nothing**. The office pays fourpence a year for the room under a lease entered in a tradesman's hand and paid for the year, and there is no rate in this county for the use of a room for one hour by its own keeper, and the reason there is no rate is that a rate for a room is a figure of a thing and a rate for a keeper's hour is a figure of a person, and no combined figure is entered in this office.

*Of the keeper of this office, one hour:* **nothing**. This office has never entered a figure of the keeper's own time and is not going to start, and the reason it is not going to start is on the wall in this room in a fair copy in a keeper's own hand, and it is nineteen years old, and I have never once been able to agree with it and I have never once been able to replace it.

*Eight and four is twelve pence, which is twelve pence.*

“A body corporate that can be sued can afford twelve pence sixteen times a year,” said the keeper of records, “and cannot afford it once if anybody comes, and I have had that sentence the wrong way round since the hearing and I have had it the right way round since about the fourth hour yesterday, and I would like the second copyist to put *that* in the finding and not in the third sheet, because the third sheet is a cost and a finding is not a cost, and because a document that says what a thing is worth is a different object from a document that says what it is.”

And then the fourth line of the third sheet, which is not a figure and is in no column, and which was read out once and is entered here whole, because it is the part of this document that is about a person and the person is not going to be named, and this is the only instrument in this case in which a person can be a cost without being a claim.

*And it is entered on this sheet, against no person and in no column, that a body corporate that cannot say what a thing cost is a body corporate that has no standing to be asked what it cost by anybody else. This office has a claim number against it and not a name, and the name is not going in, because a name in a column of this office is a claim about a person and this office has one of those already in a leaf in its press and it did not write it, and it is not going to make another. And so the cost of this finding falls on the only person this office can name and cannot put in a column, and the person is going to be thirty-eight in about nine years, and by then there will be a second keeper in this room and neither of them will know who decided any of it, and the second keeper will be told that the hearing was held and not adjourned and not refused and that the seventh column is empty and the reason is under the rule, and the second keeper will be told all of that in about four minutes and it will be in a hand she has not seen before and it will be one sentence long, and that is what this office is for and it is all it can do.*

Nobody said anything about that and it was not improved on and it is the fourth of five things in this document, and the keeper of records has entered four things in this office in nine weeks and had entered nothing in nine years.

And then the second copyist said the thing she had been in the office six years for, and she said it in about ten seconds, and she said it to a finding and not to the keeper, and she had not planned to say it that day and has not said anything about it since.

**“I have wanted a post with my name in the day-book since the summer before last, and the reason I have not asked for it is that I did not want a warrant, and a warrant is a thing a clerk is given by a keeper. I want a line. One line, in that book, with my name against it, and nothing attached to it, and I do not want it to say what I do and I do not want anybody to be able to take it away.**

“And I have worked out this morning, reading a finding of ours back, that I am not going to get it, and that I do not want it, and that those two are the same thing, and it has taken me six years to find out and I am not bitter about it and I would like the finding to know that I said it here and not anywhere else.

“Because that is what the day-book is. It is the only book in this office nobody can be served with and it is the only book in this office with a keeper's name in the head of it, and if a second copyist's name goes in the head of it then whoever holds the day-book in nine years is holding it for her and not for the office, and that is a worse instrument and not a better one. **A finding goes on a shelf with a number against this office. A name goes in a head of a book. Those are two different objects and I have wanted the second one for six years and I have just worked out that the first one is what this office is for, and I want it in the finding, and I am not going to ask for it to be put in a column, because there is no column and I am not going to make one.**”

The keeper of records read that back to her word for word and put it in the finding in the second column under the words *and to no person*, and the second copyist did not ask her to take it out and is not going to.

---

**And the finding was read back once, at the ninth hour, by the second copyist, and the reading was slower than the writing and not better, and that has been the practice since the first year, and then the sheet went on a shelf with the number against this office against it and nothing against a person.**

The day-book has it in four entries and not three, and it is the fourth time in four years and a half that a day and an hour have gone in separately in this office, and the reason they go in separately is a form with seven columns and the fourth is the day and the fifth is the hour, and a day-book is not a form but this office has kept to the form nine years longer than it has had to and nobody has ever asked it why and the standing reason is in a fair copy on the wall and is nineteen years old.

*Claim number, the number of the account.* Against no person.

***That a hearing was heard on a day and at an hour against no person.* The day.**

*That a hearing was heard on a day and at an hour against no person.* The hour.

***That no person came and that no person was sent, and that the resolution that says so was entered on the twentieth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, and that the delegation is not to be sent for.***

And the third sheet went on the shelf with it, and the second sheet went on the shelf with it, and the third sheet is the only one of the three that has a sentence in it that is not a fact, and the shelf is the shelf and not the press, because the press is for the two unserved books and a day-book, and a day-book cannot go on a shelf with a finding, and there is a shelf.

And the list of things sent is ruled in four columns and has no fifth, and there are two inches of bare paper at the right-hand edge of it, and the second copyist ruled it short in this office about two years ago and it has not been ruled in since and it is not going to be, and there is a thing on the face of the paper in the lane outside this door that this office sends things to about four times a week and it does not send anything back, and the person who keeps it has not been asked to and would not say yes to anybody who did, and the second copyist has ruled the page and the bare paper is bare and the two inches have been bare for two years and are going to be bare in nine.

A water-barge hand of twenty-seven out of a county four hundred miles off stood in the rain on a market morning and read a fair copy of four hundred and eight words and a working on a second sheet off a board under a stair, and asked for a copy, and nobody had to be asked. The copy went out on the ordinary post and cost a sheet at twopence and four pence of carriage, and it went back to a board under a stair four hundred miles from this door where anybody coming up may read it, and the reason it went to a board and not to a man is that a man can be shown a document and a board cannot, and this office wrote nothing about the county it came from on the wrapper, and the reason it wrote nothing is that a wrapper goes to a filing room.


**And there is a bundle on the second shelf with a piece of tarred hemp through it, and there are nine things in it, and every one of them came in the ordinary post from a county four hundred miles off that has nothing in it, and they are all the same county, and the county is the one whose delegation is not to be sent for, and the ninth of them came in on the sixth. The reason there are nine is that this office answers everything that is not a question, and there is not one instrument in this kingdom that can make a county answer a question that is not one, and so the letters go where letters go, and a bundle with a piece of hemp through it on a second shelf is what a body corporate looks like from the inside on a Wednesday in the harvest.**

And in the year before last a sheet came back from a provincial town two hundred miles off in an office that has a press in it, cut along the left edge with a knife, four hundred and eleven words, and the cut is the office's own method and it went into the press entire and uncopied with the cut band beside it, and the second copyist has never read it in this room and the keeper of records has read it about four hundred times, and the clause that landed on her was the number of persons the offer was made out to, which is not on the sheet, and the Archive has asked for that number in a return with no name in it and no word of how it came by it, and the number has not come, and it is not going to come out of that office, because an office that will not print a number of persons cannot show that any person has not been instructed, and that is the other half of the same arrangement this office has had in its press for four years and a half in a column headed *Keeper* in a hand that is not the man's, and the two of them are four hundred miles apart and neither of the people who put them there has ever heard of the other one.
And there is one item in the ordinary post of that Wednesday that is from a county four hundred miles off, and it was not opened, and it is not going to be, and the keeper of records said so out loud to an empty room in about four words, which is a thing she has done about four times a year since the first year, and the room heard her, and the letter has been on the table since the junior clerk brought it up about the third hour and it is going to be on the table on Thursday and Friday and it will go on a shelf with the other things from that county that are not opened, and there are nine of those, and she has never opened one of them and has never written to a person in that county, and the distance between this room and the place those letters come from is about four hundred miles, and there is nobody in this county who knows where he is, and that is not a thing she has written down anywhere and is not going to.

And the day-book went into the press with the two unserved books in it, and the press has a half tile in it that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is the account of nobody, and there is a body corporate with a door and a number in this county and its deed is thirteen words long and it was made in this room, and the room is a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, and the scales under it have not been used since the fourth of it.

The keeper of records stood at the table for a while with her hands on the back of it and did not pick anything up.

**“I want to put one thing in the record of this and I am going to say it out loud first because I have found out in the last two months that a thing I have only said out loud does not exist in a few years, and a thing I have only written down gets argued with.**

“This office is a body corporate with a door, a number, and the capacity to sue and to be sued, and it was made four years and a half ago out of a deed of thirteen words, and the words are *for the keeping of what this county cannot be shown it has lost*, and there is no office in this kingdom that can be told what to do and no writ that can be served on one and no bench that can make one sit down. That is all a deed does when a county is not going to have a Crown's hand in it. And every other instrument in this case in four years and a half has been a form with a space for a name in it, and this one is a deed with no name in it at all, and the reason this office could hold a meaning this morning and the county council four hundred miles off could not hold a road is in those thirteen words and not in the cleverness of anybody who wrote them.

“And I have entered four things in nine weeks and I have held one meaning that nobody handed me and that I cannot hand back, and in a few years this office is going to be holding three of those and a woman is going to read them and not be able to find out who decided any of them, and that is what the deed bought, and I have never once said that out loud in a room before this morning and I am not going to say it again in nine years.”

She did not write it down. She had said out loud first, and the second copyist was in the room, and the second copyist has wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last, and the two of them had agreed some months ago that a thing said out loud in this room goes in the book that is not the day-book and is not in the press, and it went there, and the book has six years in it and no name in it and cannot be served and cannot be shown to anybody, and it is the only object in this room that nobody can be got at, and it weighs nothing.

And then she put the lamp out, and the room was a room, and there are two chairs in it, and one of them has not been moved since the ninth of Rainmonth, and it has not been moved because nothing has needed it moved, and she looked at it for about a second and a half and left it where it was, because a body corporate that has agreed to hear a thing cannot un-agree and can only decide what it is now, and what it is now is a room with a keeper in it at the second hour and nothing at the other side of the table, and a second chair is not a mistake in a room, and it is going to be the first thing a person looks at when somebody walks in, and there is going to be somebody, or there is not, and the woman who left it where it was has not decided which and has written it in no book, and the door on the lane was shut, and the scales under that room have not been used since the fourth of it, and the tide came up under the wharf four hundred yards off at about the tenth hour and went back at the third.
