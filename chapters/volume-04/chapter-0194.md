# Chapter 0194

**The fourth morning of the four days had forty people at the ford at the fourth hour, and there is no fourth day of digging in it, and the notice on the end of the back bench in the room over the cross says three days in chalk with a figure struck through it, and the man of thirty-four did not rub the three out, and about two hundred people in this valley came up that bank in four days and stood at the top of it and looked down and went away again, and nine hundred did not come at all.**

The notice was the man of fifty-eight's. It went up on the first morning and it said *four days, the second, the third and the fourth, the fourth hour*, and on the second evening a man of thirty-four went up to the room over the cross with a rag paper and the man of fifty-eight and the man of about thirty-four were on the bench with the hundred and forty cut into the end of it and neither of them said anything for a while.

“Change the four to a three,” said Wystan Roole, “and put a bar through the four, and leave the three legible, and do not rub anything out.”

“That is a figure in a public stone and there is no column for it and there is nobody to enter it against.”

“Then it is a figure on a public stone that nobody entered,” said Iven Sarr, “and that has been the arrangement in this room for a hundred and forty years and I am not going to start keeping it better on the fourth day of four.”

---

**And the fourth day was a course, and a course of a height is the only thing this valley's stone goes into, and sixteen men put one across the top of a four-foot face at three feet in a day and a half and cut nothing to do it with.**

That is a whole day and a half of four hands and a fifth hand and a man of fifty-one who will not let anybody hurry the last stone and cannot say why, and a man of thirty-four who stood at the shoulder of a man of thirty-two with a limp for four days and did not explain what for and was asked nothing and answered nothing.

And the four days at the shoulder of it is a thing that has to be said about once and not improved on, and it is said here by the man of thirty-four to eleven people in a yard in the warm week and it went into four kitchens and nowhere else.

“He carried. That is everything he did for four days, and forty people watched him do it, and nine of them have said since that he was better at it than the sixteen. He is not a man of this valley and he has not been in this valley seven months and he carried stone up a bank nine hundred times in a cold, and he was faster at the low end of the run than a man of twenty-six because he has got a limp and he has learned to use it, and I have watched him do that for four days and I have not said one word about it to him and I am not going to.

“**And he stood at my shoulder for four days with his hand on a rope, and nobody asked him what the rope was for, and I did not explain it, and eleven people were close enough to see it and four of them have asked me since what it was for, and I have told all four the same four sentences and not one of the four sentences is the reason.**”

The four sentences were about a rope, and about a knot, and about what a rope round a stone does to a stone, and about how a man of thirty-four did not want anybody in this valley to be able to do it themselves, and none of the four of them was the reason and the man of thirty-four has never given the reason and was never asked it twice by the same person.

**And the ninth generation asked four questions about a lid in a day and a half, and got four answers, and three of the four answers were that it did not matter and the fourth one is the reason the hole is still a hole in the warm week.**

They asked whether a course wants a course under it. They asked whether three feet is a figure or an opinion. They asked whether the barrow run should come off the course and go round the low end the way it goes round the face, and were told that it should not, and that a barrow run that goes round a thing forever is not a barrow run, it is a habit, and that there is no wheel in this valley and there has not been one in nine generations and that the day there is, somebody is going to have to come and take this course up, and four of them said out loud that they would like to be the somebody and nine of them said nothing.

And the mill man of sixty-one came down on the second of the two days with his barrow and could not leave his wheel and did not go back up it, and he put about nine barrows of stone into that course and then went and stood at the low end of it and looked at the run for about a minute and a half and said one thing to a man of fifty-one, and the man of fifty-one repeated it to four people in the warm week and would not repeat it again.

“He said, if the water comes up over that run in the warm week it will find the eleven yards on the low end first, and the eleven yards are made ground and there is not one stone in it, and the eleven yards will go before the run does, and then the run will be on its own with nothing holding it at the low end, and then it will want a wall.”

**And a wall at the low end of a barrow run at a ford in this valley is a thing that is not going to be built in the next nine years by sixteen men, and everybody at the low end of it that day knew that, and a man of thirty-four wrote nothing on a rag paper about it, and there is no book in this valley with a wall at the low end of a barrow run in it.**

The stone for the course came off the field wall at the top of the bank that the wall at the Low Holm had already had, and off a wall that was coming down anyway two steadings up, and the number of barrows it took is a figure nobody in this valley keeps and the girl with the barrow kept it.

She counted them going up and she counted them coming down and the two numbers were not the same, and the man of thirty-four asked her for the two numbers at about the seventh hour of the fourth day and she gave him both, and he wrote nothing down, and she said that he always writes nothing down and that she does not mind and that she will be doing it in the warm week.

**That is the only account of the four days that anybody in this valley has that is in figures, and it is in the head of a child of nine, and it is the only one of the nine accounts of the four days that anybody has ever been able to check, and it does not agree with any of the other eight.**

“And the two numbers are not the same because the barrows are not the same going up as they are coming down,” said a man of about fifty-one, “and the difference is the sand and the loose, and you have counted a barrow of loose as a barrow of stone, and I am not going to tell her that, because she has counted nine hundred and something of them in a day and a half in a cold and she is nine.”

---

**And at about the eighth hour of the fourth day the man of thirty-four put his hand on the bar at the top of the course and told the forty people on the bank what the four days had been for and what the hole was, and it took about four minutes and he did not put a roof on it and he did not make it a thing anybody could carry away.**

The course went in at three feet and the hole is under it and the hole is four feet and there is a foot of loose in it and the loose came out of the face on the fourth day and went back in on the fourth day in about a quarter of an hour while about forty people watched it.

“This is a hole in a bank with a barrow run over the top of it,” he said. “That is all it is and it is not going to be anything else in my lifetime. It is not a grave and it is not a mark and it is not a place anybody is going to come and look at. **In nine years there will be a woman in this valley who will not be able to tell anybody what this was, and there is nothing I can do about that and I have thought about it for two years.**

“And the course on it is a course in the same stone as the wall at the Low Holm and the second stand at the ford, and in a few years nobody will be able to tell those three things apart, and that is all I have got to say about the four days.”

The man of about forty-one who farms three steadings asked, and he had asked the same question in the fourth week of the fourth cold in a room with a hundred and forty cut into the end of the bench he was sitting on.

“Will it still be there.”

“It will be there while that course is,” said Wystan Roole. “And that course is nine inches and it is the same stone and the same lime and the same height, and it will be the footing for a stand one day, or it will be a lid on a hole. I do not know which and I am not going to be told.

“**And I am going to say the other half, and I have not said it out loud in nine years, and I am saying it in the open in front of forty people because I said beforehand that I would, and the other half is that the ground under that ford has moved about a foot a year since I was twenty-five, and I have not known why in the whole time, and I am not going to know it either, and I have put a bar across a hole in it and I have done what I know how to do.**”

Nobody said anything. The man of thirty-four had his hands behind his back and eleven people were close enough to see that, and four of them have said since that a man with his hands behind his back at a bank in a valley that does not get dark is the only thing they have ever been afraid of in this valley that was not weather, and the other seven said the same about the taking down of a coping in the fourth week of the fourth cold, and neither account is better than the other and nobody has ever put the two side by side.

---

**And the four days stopped, and the man of thirty-four took the bar off his shoulder and put it down in the barrow that it had come in, and the sixteen of them stood about eleven yards from the hole on a bank in the ninth hour of a day in a cold in a valley that does not get dark, and about two hundred people had been at that bank in four days.**

He put his hand flat on the top of the course, the way anybody puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the stone was cold, and he took his hand off and said four words to the man of fifty-eight and to about eleven people who were within thirty feet of it, and the four words were that the sixteen would be back.

“Not in the warm week,” said the man of fifty-eight.

“**In the warm week, and I will be here, because I have nowhere else to be until a man of thirty-four says a date, and there is not one in this valley, and I have written none.**”

The girl with the barrow was the last one off the bank and she took her barrow over the course and it went across, and the course held it, and she said nothing about it, and as many again in this valley have said since that they were not surprised, and one of the nine is a man of about thirty-one of the ninth generation who said that a course of a height in this valley's stone holds a barrow or it does not and that he had known which about four hours before the bar went on it.

> *The line is open. What is under it is not mended by the man who dug it.*

He did not write it down. He has had four of those in four years and has written one down, and the one he wrote down has no hour against it and no name in it and he cannot account for where it came from and nobody four hundred miles off can tell him, and there is nobody in this valley he could ask, and he did not ask the man of thirty-four, who was a bar's length away and had his hands behind his back and has never said one word about what the four days were.

**And on the fifth morning there were eleven people in the room over the cross at the fourth hour, and the man of fifty-eight had the ninth spread of the only roll in this valley on the trestle at the front with a rag paper under it, and a stick was against the wall between the second bench and the third where a man of thirty-four had put it in the fourth week of the fourth cold, and nobody in that room had moved it, and four people looked at it and nine did not.**

The girl with the barrow came up the outside stair during all of it, did not knock, and stood on the fourth tread, and the fourth tread says so under her and under nobody else. She counted the benches from the top of the stair through a doorway in a minute and a half, got twenty-nine, and did not go in, and she counted the stick as a tenth object in a room where everything in it is counted, and she did not ask about it and nobody asked her.

“The four days are not a figure of people,” said the man of fifty-eight, with the pen on the rule and not moving, and four people in that room did not understand a word of it and nine did.

“Three figures have gone into that book in a hundred and forty years and they went in on the twenty-fourth day of the second cold and about four hundred, and about a hundred and eighty-nine, and nine. And there is no column in this book for a figure of days, and I am not going to rule one, and the four days are going to be in nine hundred doors and in a child's head and in about four hundred people who stood on a bank, and there is not one of those that anybody can hold.

“**And a figure of people goes in this book and a figure of days does not, and that is the first distinction anybody in this valley has ever drawn and I have been the man who draws it for nineteen years and I have never had to say it out loud in a room before.**”

The clerk of about sixty wrote that in the minute in a room over a mill, and it is in the minute, and nobody has ever been served with that minute and nobody ever will be, and the man of thirty-four was not in the room and has never read it and was never asked what he thought of it and said once, in a yard, that a man who will not write a figure down is the only kind of person in this case that has ever been worth listening to, which was said about him and not by him.

And the warm week was eleven days off and there were nine weeks behind it, and the sixteen were to come back to a hole in a bank, and the man of fifty-eight asked the question that four people in that room had been asking for two days, and it was the only question anybody asked, and it went in the minute and it has not been answered.

“About four of the eleven that went over the pass on the fourth day of this cold will come back in the warm week,” he said. “I am entering that, and not the other seven, and not where the other seven are, and I have not got a figure for the other seven and I am not going to make one out of seven, and there is a difference and the difference is that a figure of people that somebody can check is a figure and a figure of people that nobody can check is a rumour with a mark on it.”

“Then what do I write when four come back.”

“Four,” said the man of fifty-eight. “And not a word about the other seven, and not one of them at the door, and if a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow and a dog comes up this bank in the warm week and says where her brother is, then she is at a door and not at a book, and this room has nothing to do with that.”

**And the chalk on the end of the back bench in the room over the cross said three days with a bar through the four, and the man of fifty-eight took the rag paper out of his coat on the fifth morning and did not write anything on it, and about two hundred people went up a bank in four days and nine hundred doors in this valley had eleven words put at them once in nine generations and were never asked anything else, and there were about nine weeks in front of sixteen men and a hole in a bank at the ford and nobody in nine miles had written the nine weeks down anywhere.**
