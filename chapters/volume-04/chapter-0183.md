# Chapter 0183

**The fourth hour of the fourth day is not an hour in this valley, exactly, because there is no hour here and there has never been an hour here, and the light in a valley of about four hundred people comes up out of the shoulder of the bank and stays there and does not get dark at any time anybody keeps. The sixteen of them came at the fourth hour because a man of thirty-four said fourth hour at eight doors a day for nine days and it is the only time anybody in this valley has agreed to since the fourth day of a cold.**

About forty people came up the bank. That is more than the room over the cross takes and more than the market takes and it is the largest number of people who have stood in one place in this valley in the whole nine weeks of the man of thirty-four's term, and nobody organised it, and about eleven of them were of the ninth generation and about four of them were of the tenth and had never been in a room with a fire in it in their lives.

The woman with the slate came and stood at the back. She has carried it for eleven years and gave a number off it at a gate in the second month of this cold and has never been asked for it and kept it, and she came up a bank in the cold to stand and look at a coping.

The man of eighty-one did not come. He had said he was not going to watch the fifth and he did not, and there is nobody in this valley with any standing to say he was wrong about that, and he was the only one of the about nine people who had seen it in advance who was not there.

And a man of fifty-eight was there, and a man of thirty-two was there, and a young man of twenty-two was there because there are about nine people in this valley who come to a room when there is a fire in it, and he came up a bank in the cold which is a different thing and he did not say which one it was.

---

**And the man of thirty-four would not begin until every one of them was there, and then he said the nine sentences again, and this time he said them to the forty people and not to a stranger, and the reason was that a reason given once to a room of nine is an account and a reason given twice in the open in front of forty is a reason, and he had learned the difference in a cold room in the second month of this valley from a man who had read four words off a leaf.**

“I am going to say it again because thirty of you did not hear it in that room and I am not going to have nine stones off a coping on the word of about nine people.

“A stand is not made by a man. A stand is made by two ends and a day and somebody who was not either of the two ends. Take those three away and you have got a pile of stone in a cutting. I have built four of those in nine years and I know what they are worth.

“That mark is a line and a bar and a figure and I cannot read the figure and I am not going to guess. It says a day. A day in it means somebody meant it to be findable, and there is nobody in nine miles who could tell you which day, and I laid that coping so I am not a witness, because a witness is somebody who is not the one keeping the thing.

“And there is another end to it, about four hundred miles off, and something at that end has agreed to it, or it is not a line, it is a mark on a stone. Nobody out there has been asked for anything by anybody in this valley. I do not say anybody has done anything wrong. I say the other end is not there, and that is a different thing from wrong, and in about nine years somebody is going to tell a child that mark meant something, and the child will be the one shown it.

“And it is the highest thing in nine miles that a person stands on. A barrow rests on it. A pail rests on it. A girl of about nine rests on it every day of her life and she does not know. Any of you can put a hand on it and not one of you can tell me what day it is, and in nine years there will be a story on that coping and there will be no way back to this morning.

**“So the course comes off. Not the mark. The course, all nine stones. And the nine old ones do not go in the beck and do not go in a wall and do not get carried off, and I have known where they go since this morning and I am telling you before I start: they go in the footing at the ford, in the order they come off, under the water, where a barrow cannot go and a nine-year-old cannot get a hand.**

“A mill man of sixty-one said to me yesterday that under the water is where a thing goes that you are not going to look at again, and he is right, and I have thought about it for a day and a half, and it is the honest end and not a tidy one, and I would like it noticed that a man of thirty-four has decided to put nine good stones in the water rather than break them and rather than put them in a wall that a barrow goes over four times a day, and that is a trade decision and not a moral one, and the moral part of it is finished and took four days.”

Nobody in about forty people said anything, and about eleven of the ninth generation had understood most of it, and the man of thirty-four had worked out in the last four days that the reason they had understood it is that it is the only argument anybody in this valley has ever heard that was about their own hands.

---

**And he began, and he did not let the man off the road take a stone, and he gave the reason before anybody thought of asking for it.**

“You are going to want to,” he said, to the back of the right-hand row, in a voice nine other people could hear. “I have watched you for five months and you get up onto things in three moves instead of two and I have never once worked out how you do it and I have decided I am not going to find out. You are not laying a stone in this valley this morning.

“And the reason is not that I do not want the help. The reason is that if a man off the road lays a stone in this valley then in nine years there will be a thing in this valley that a stranger built and forty people watched, and the only difference between that and the mark is which side of it the light is on.”

He put a bar under the first stone and the sixteen came in behind it.

And the first stone off a coping is the easiest stone in the world to take off a coping, and it took longer than it should, because the lime putty has gone to stone in four months and the whole nine has to come away as a course and not as nine stones, and a man who has never taken a course off a coping in nine generations does not know that a coping is a course and not a row of stones, and the sixteen of them learned it in nine minutes and it was the man of thirty-four who showed them and not the man with the chisel.

Nine stones, in order, the fourth one from the far end last of the first four. Between the fourth and the fifth there was about four seconds in which nobody said anything, and nine people in the crowd counted, and the girl counted the four, and the man of twenty-two counted the four and did not say what he counted.

The fourth stone is a different stone to the other eight. It came off the byre coping at the third generation's house and it is a hundred and forty years old and it is squared on one face, and the face that was squared had a square on it about four inches across, and about a hand's width down from the square there is a line, and a bar across the top of the line, and a figure cut into it that four people in that valley looked at for about a minute each.

Nobody rubbed it out. That is the thing about the ninth day of this cold, and there were about forty people watching and nine of them noticed it, and nine of them said afterwards to somebody they had talked to the next day that the fourth stone was not rubbed, and the fourth stone went into the footing at the ford with a stranger's mark in it that four people in this valley could still read in the flat light for about four months until the water came up over it.

**The man of thirty-four put it on the ground himself and stood looking at it for about a minute and a half with his hands behind his back, and a man of thirty-one of the ninth generation who farms four steadings said, in a voice about four people could hear, “You could chip it out,” and the man of thirty-four said, “No,” and the man of thirty-one said, “I know,” and nobody said anything else, and the four of those two sentences went into about nine years of this valley's version of things.**

And the nine stones went down the bank on a barrow with the fourth one in the middle, and the man who makes nails took the barrow and did not ask to and was not asked, and a girl of nine walked beside it counting, and twenty people who had come up the bank to watch a coping came down it instead, which is not a thing anybody in this valley has ever done, because there is nothing to come down for.

And it took the four of them about nine minutes to get the barrow down a bank that four people had been getting stone down for four months, and a man of thirty-four took the front of it and a man of thirty-one of the ninth generation took the back of it without being asked, and a man of fifty-eight came down behind them carrying nothing and stood at the bottom and waited, and the man of thirty-four said afterwards that the man of fifty-eight had stood at the bottom of that bank for about nine minutes in the cold doing nothing, and that he had asked him about it in the fourth week and the answer had been nine words long and had not been about the form.

“Nine,” she said, at the ford, when they were down. “And the one in the middle is the one that had the thing on it. I counted it twice at the cutting and I have counted it once here and I am not going to ask anybody what it was.”

She put her hand flat on the top of the fourth stone on the barrow, the way anybody puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the stone was cold, and she took her hand off and went and got her barrow out at the ford in about ten minutes, which is what she had come for.

And the footing of the second stand at the ford is a course of stone set in the bed and it goes in under four feet of water, and eleven of the nine stones are bigger than a man's two hands and three are not, and they went in in the order they came off, and the man of thirty-four set every one of them himself and would not let anybody else touch that course, and the reason he gave was in four sentences on a wet bank.

“The bed is what holds those and not the stones. You put nine stones in a course in a bed and the course is the bed's, and if the nine are not in the order they were in then in nine years a man will lift one to look at the joint and the bed will come up with it. **I have laid a footing twice in nine years and I laid the second one wrong and there is a man in this valley who knows and he is not going to tell you which of us it was.**”

Nobody in that valley has ever worked out whether the man of eighty-one knows, and he was not asked, and he has said nine times in nine years that he has been in that room sixty times and never once heard what was in it.

And the new course went on, and it went on for eleven days, and the ninth stone came into the bed on the first morning of it, and the two men who had put it in and the two men who had knocked it out had a go at it in front of forty people and it went in on the fifth try and nobody cheered because there is not a cheer in this valley in nine generations, and the people who had been in the cutting for four hours went down the bank without saying anything, and four of them were crying and five of them had a cold.

They bedded it on a bed of what there was, which was lime putty out of the last of the three loads and about four barrows of sand out of a bank, and the sand came out of the same place the barley comes out of in the warm week, and there is nowhere else in this valley to put it.

The sixteen of them put nine stones down in it in about two hours and a half, and the man of thirty-four did not let anybody cut one to fit, and he said the reason for that too, and he said it to the nine of the ninth generation who had been in the bank all morning, and it is the best thing anybody in this valley has said about stone and it is said once.

“Every course in every wall I have ever laid has been cut to. It takes half the stone off and it takes half the time. You get a straight face and you get a joint that is a joint and not a crack, and a wall goes up in half the time and stands for a hundred and forty years. And in this valley you cannot do it, and the reason you cannot do it is that the stone here is squared on two faces and not on four, and a stone squared on two faces wants a course of one height all the way along, because that is the only bed it will go into. Cut one course thick at the bottom and thin at the top and you have got a joint that opens when it dries, and a joint that opens when it dries is a joint that is not a joint, and that is where a wall comes apart in a thaw. I have seen it happen four times and I was in two of them.”

He knocked the ninth stone off the bed with the butt of his bar and it was about a finger off and he put it back and knocked it off again.

“This one is a finger off and it is going to stay a finger off until the sixth one is laid, and when the sixth one is laid the ninth will come in on its own, because that is what a course of one height is for. **A man from a county that cuts its stone comes here and cuts, and a cut course in this valley is a hole with a good face on it, and he will do it in the warm week and the wall will come apart in the second year and everybody will say the stone was bad.**”

Nobody said anything about that for four minutes. The man of thirty-four put the ninth stone back a third time, and the mill man of sixty-one said, from about six feet off, in a voice nobody had heard him use in about nine years, “Then you have not been getting it wrong,” and the man of thirty-four said, “No,” and that was the entire conversation between them, and it happened on a bank at the fourth hour on the fourth day of a cold in its ninth generation with forty people in it.

And the ninth stone came into the bed about an hour later on its own, without anybody touching it, in front of everybody, and the man of thirty-four stepped back and looked at the course and did not say one word about it, and about four people who have been in this valley their whole lives said afterwards to somebody they had talked to the next day that they had seen a stone go into a wall by itself, and neither of the two of them who had told it said *and it was the right way round*, and in about nine years there will be a woman in this valley who says that a stone in a coping at the Low Holm once went in on its own, and what she will mean by it is not what happened, and that is the ninth account in nine years of four or more accounts of a coping, and none of them is checkable against the other three, and the man of thirty-four knew that on the bank at the fourth hour and took his hands behind his back and said nothing.
