# Chapter 0184

**The new coping went on in eleven days and it is not the coping that came off, and everybody in this valley knew that by the fourth of the eleven and about eleven of them could have said how before it happened.**

The nine stones that came off the far abutment on the fourth day at the fourth hour were a stone off a wall that was coming down anyway, a hundred and forty years of a byre coping a woman had done in lime with a man working beside her, and seven pieces of field stone that a man of thirty-four picked out of a face himself in about half an hour on the first morning and did not cut.

The old course was a foot deep and bedded in lime putty and it was the only work on that stand that a man of thirty-four had ever done in this valley that he was going to be asked about, and he said so on the first morning of the eleven, to the sixteen, standing in a cutting with his hands in a belt that was not a belt.

“It is a foot deep because a foot is what I had and it is what I wanted to be able to lean a pail on, and I have never once looked at that coping and thought about a hundred and forty years, and neither has the woman with the yoke, and the only person in this valley that looks at a coping and thinks about a hundred and forty years is a man of eighty-one who will not come and look at it.

“The one on now is nine inches and it is bedded in what is left of what there was, and it is going to be this valley's coping and not mine, and in a few years there will be a man in this valley who cannot tell the two apart, and that is all of it, and I am not going to be thanked and I would like it noticed that the ninth stone came in on its own and about four people saw it.”

---

**And on the sixth day of the eleven the water in the cutting came up over the ninth stone of the old coping at the ford and did not go down again for nine weeks, and the mark in the fourth one went under about four feet of it on the fourteenth day, and there is no instrument in this valley that could raise it and nobody asked for one.**

Nobody in the valley said anything. The man of thirty-four said one thing on the sixth evening to the man of thirty-two and it was about four words long and it was not for anybody else.

“They will go down in the warm week,” he said. “Not in nine weeks. In the warm week, and about a foot off the top of that stone will come out, and by then that coping will have been under a load all winter and stood it, and so it will have been asked.”

He did not improve on it and nobody asked him to, and it is a trade fact and not the mechanism and it is not the thing anybody in this valley is afraid of, and he has not said it a second time.

---

**And then the nine weeks, which began on the fourth day at the fourth hour and are all of what he has to do with them, and there is no second road in this valley and nobody in it has offered him one and nobody is going to.**

There were sixteen men and about thirty under the cross. There was a mill at the bottom that had a wheel and had never once been able to get anything out of its own valley, and there were nine roofs up the bank with a hole in each of them, and the ninth generation would not let the bridgekeepers put them up. There was a ford with four feet of water in it and a barrow that went round it. And there were four hundred yards of ground between the top of the bank and the ford that had never had anything made on it, and a man of thirty-four said on the first morning of the second week that they were going to make it, and about eleven people asked him what it was for, and he told them, and the answer took him about eleven seconds.

“It is for a barrow,” he said. “That is all of it. Not for a cart. There is no cart in this valley and there has not been one in nine generations and I am not going to pretend to you that there will be. For a barrow, four hundred yards, once a day, from the top of the bank to the ford, so that a woman with a yoke does not put a pail in four feet of water in the warm week, and so that a mill has a way to the water, and so that a man can get a stone to the bottom of a valley. Four hundred yards. It is the smallest road anybody has ever wanted and it is not a road.”

It is not a road. It is the only true line in nine miles and about eleven people in this valley have said so out loud, and none of them is of the ninth generation, and a man of thirty-four has been refused twice for a road and has built four hundred yards in nine weeks, and it is the wrong thing and it is the only thing there is.

**And the nine roofs went on being holes, and he asked a man of the ninth generation in the fourth week and got an answer in eleven seconds that he has not improved on since and has not written down.**

The man was about twenty-four and he had put his hand on the wrong plate in the second month of this valley in front of eleven people and had been told why in a yard and had got another plate, and he was on the ground work because he wanted to be and was not asked.

“Why will you not have your roof done.”

“By the sixteen.” He did not stop working. “Because a roof in this valley is not a thing you can be given. My grandfather's roof was put on by his brother and by two men from the second house, and it took a week, and they ate at our table for a week, and one of them is the man who taught me to set a plate. That is what a roof is. It is not a thing sixteen men under a cross do on their way past because there is a hole in it.

“And I know what you are going to say, and you can say it, because you have not said it and I have been waiting three weeks. You are going to say there is a woman with a yoke putting a pail in four feet of water. And she has a stand with a nail in it now and the stand holds, and I have been over it four times a day with a barrow and it has not moved, and I would rather have a stand than a roof and I am not being unkind, I am telling you that a stand is four days and a roof is a family and there is not one family in this valley that has asked the sixteen for anything since before the man of thirty-four was born.”

“That is a hard sentence.”

“It is my mother's and she said it to me in a kitchen in the second month of this cold and I have had it four months and nobody has ever asked me why, including him, and I would like somebody to ask me why, and there is nowhere to put the answer to it, and now you have asked me about the roof and not about that, and I am going to go back to the barrow.”

And the four hundred yards came down to the ford in the fourth week, and on the last morning of it the woman of the Low Holm walked all of it with her yoke on and a pail in each hand, at the pace of a woman who has been walking round a thing for nine years and does not trust it, and she went over the second stand and came back over it, and that is the first time in nine generations that anybody here has walked four hundred yards of made ground, and about four people watched her do it and as many again did not know it had happened.

And the mill came off, and the mill man of sixty-one came off it and went down the four hundred yards for the first time in nine years with a barrow, and he got a quarter of the wheel up the bank in one go and had to stop, and the sixteen of them watched him do it and about four of them had a hand on his barrow before he asked, and he said no, and they took their hands off, and he got the rest of the way up alone, and a man of thirty-four said afterwards that he had wanted to say four things about that and had said none of them, and one of the four was that a wheel which has stood still since the first cold and is turned again in a cold is a different sound from a wheel that has been turned, and the difference is in the first quarter of a turn, and he had heard it once in a county four hundred miles off and had never had occasion to hear it here.

There is no figure for any of it and there was never going to be. A valley of four hundred people, eleven steadings, four seed houses, one slate and one market a season that everybody can date and nobody can name a day of, and in the fourth week of the fourth cold of a cold in its ninth generation, about two hundred of them are in a book under a byre, and about nine hundred doors have had eleven words put at them, and a stand went down at the ford in four hundred yards of ground, and the only record of any of it is a page with three figures at the foot of it in a hand that varies, and a trestle in a yard with a figure in chalk on the end of it that will be off inside a year, and about four hundred memories that do not agree with each other and cannot be got to.

And the counting of it is the thing he was not good at, and it is the thing he has never once done before in his life, and it took four men to teach him in about four days and a man of thirty-four to correct him in public on the ninth day of the third week.

A course is counted in loads. A load is counted in barrows. A barrow is counted in shovels, and the shovels are not a fixed number because a barrow holds what the heap holds and the heap holds what is under it, and the man who calls the loads calls them out loud in the yard, in a voice the whole yard can hear, because a course that has not been counted is a course that has not been built, it is a wall that somebody has had a go at.

He had a word for it and it was not theirs.

In the county he came from, a man loading a deck counted load points, and a load point was not a barrow and not a shovel and was not a figure of anything anybody could see. It was what a deck took before anybody was allowed to stand on it, and it was in a guild's book, and a man who wrote it in wrong was a man who had put a span over a deck and got a figure he could not check, and it was the whole of a trade that had been in four counties since before he was born.

**He said the word on the second morning of the third week, out loud, in a yard, in front of about eleven people, and the man of thirty-four stopped what he was doing.**

“Say that again.”

“Load points.”

“No I do not mean say it again, I mean say what it is, and say it the way you would say it to a man who has never been in a county that has a word for it.” He wiped his hands on a rope. “Because I have been listening to you for three weeks and I have worked out that you count. You count everything. You counted the stones in a wall with the girl who counts them, you counted the loads coming up the bank on the first morning and you counted them out loud in a county's way twice before you noticed you were doing it, and I have not stopped it because it is a good thing to do, and I am going to stop it now because you are about to count my course and your number is not my number, and if I let you call it out in that yard then about thirty men under the cross are going to learn a figure they cannot check.”

So he learned the valley's. A barrow, a heap, a shovel, and a man who says the number out loud, and a course is that number with the number of courses in it, and a stand is that number with the number of courses in it, and the mill is that number with everything in it, and the four hundred yards is that number, and none of it goes in a book, because a book of this valley is a day and a mark and a hand and a figure of people.

And on the ninth day of the third week he called it wrong in a yard with sixteen men in it, and he called it wrong by a figure that would have been found in about four months by a woman with a yoke putting a pail in the wrong place.

He was counting what they carried. Three, four, five, six, and then eight, and a man of twenty-six put a barrow down at the head of the bank and said eight, and there had been seven.

“You are counting what we brought up,” said Wystan Roole. “And we are not building what we brought up. We are building what we laid, and those have been two different numbers since the first load of the first wall anybody ever laid went into a bed, and the difference between them is the hearting, and the hearting is the small stuff and the gaps and the piece of stone you put in on the end because the barrow would not take a whole one, and every shovel of the hearting is a shovel that is not in your figure and every one of them is in the course.

“Count the laid course. Not the heap. Go along it with a finger on the top stone and count what is in the bed and not what came up the bank, and do it out loud, and if the number you get is smaller than the number of barrows then you have got a course in your hand and not a number, and you will get that every single time and it is the only check there is.”

He went along it. He got a figure that was lower than the figure of barrows by about a fifth, and it was the right one, and sixteen men heard the difference, and half the ninth generation heard a man off the road get a thing wrong out loud and take the correction without a word, and the man of thirty-four said afterwards that it was the ninth day of the third week and that it was the first day of his nine weeks that he had got anything out of the man he had gone to the bank to get it from.

And on the fourth of the third week the man of thirty-four put a stick against the wall of the room over the cross and told him what it was for, and neither of them called it anything.

“You have carried that four hundred miles and you have not put it down once in about five months and you have not leaned on it, and I have watched you not lean on it for about four of the months, and there is nowhere in this valley to put a stick down.” He put his hand on the end of it. “So put it against that wall and do not bring it on a job. It is not a thing you can lean on and you know it, and there is a man of eighty-one who said so to you on the third day of a wall in the second month of this valley and he was right, and I am not saying it as a finding. I am saying a wall is a wall and you can lean a stick on it.”

The stick went against the wall of the room over the cross between the second bench and the third, on its side, and it was still there at the end of the fourth cold, and four people in that valley noticed it, and nobody asked about it, and the girl counted it as one more object in a room where everything in it was counted, and did not ask.

And the bare fact of the stranding was said out loud in that room once, in nine sentences, by the man of thirty-four, on the fourth of the third week, and no figure of any kind was attached to it and it is the first time in this case that a man has been stuck somewhere with no way out of it and had it said.

“You are not getting out of here before the warm week and you know it and I know it and I have known it since the second day of this cold. I have not asked you about it and I am not going to, because a man who is asking a stranger when he is going home is asking a thing that is not about the work, and I have sixteen men and no work and I have spent that on the mill.

“And I am not going to build you a road either, and I want it said that I thought about it on the first of the four days and I am not going to pretend I did not, and the reason I am not is not that I would not. It is that a road to the top of this bank that a man did not ask for is not a road out of this valley, it is a road to a place where a man stands, and there is nothing at the other end of it that anybody in this valley has agreed to, and I have spent nine days taking a stranger's mark out of a public stone for exactly that reason and I am not going to do the same thing with four hundred yards of ground.”

He put his hand down off the bench.

“So you are here until the warm week. And on the fourth day of the next cold eleven of the ninth go over a pass, and about four of them come back in the first warm week, and I have been in this valley nine years and I have never once known which four and I am not going to start.”

And on the fourth of the fourth week the four hundred yards came down to the ford at about the ninth hour, and the man of thirty-four put the last barrow-load of stone on the bed of the second stand and about eleven people went over it with barrows, one at a time, the way you cross anything anybody has built for you in a valley where nothing has ever been built for you, and the mill man of sixty-one went over it with an empty barrow and came back over it with a full one and did not say anything about either, and a woman was at the ford with a barrow waiting for her turn.
