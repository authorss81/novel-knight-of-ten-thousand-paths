# Chapter 0193

**On the third morning of the four days the bottom of the face was four feet down and a step, and the sixteen had a bar to it before the forty arrived, and eleven people in this valley have said in three different kitchens that the sound it made was the sound of a bar on a thing that had never been cold, and none of those eleven was on the bank when it was made.**

The bar went in about a finger and stopped. That is all that happened. A man of fifty-one put a bar to it the way you put a bar to a stone you have not seen before, and it went in about a finger, and it stopped, and the bar came back up with a white line along the point of it that was not frost and was not anything else that anybody on that bank could put a name to in the fourth hour of a morning in a cold.

Nobody said anything for about a minute and a half. The barrow run stopped behind them and forty people stood on a bank in a valley that does not get dark at any hour anybody keeps and watched a man of fifty-one look at a bar.

“That is the thing,” said Wystan Roole.

He said it from the top of the bank and he did not come down and he did not say anything else about it, and four people within thirty feet of him heard the two words and as many again did not, and the man of fifty-one heard both of them and has never repeated them to anybody, and the man of thirty-four said afterwards that he had waited two years for two words and that he had thought about the wording of them for nine years and came out with two.

Then he came down into the face and stood on the step and looked at the bottom of it for a while, and the white line came off the point of the bar onto his glove, and he put the bar down and stood on the step and said nothing for two minutes.

“Right,” he said. “That is what we are here for. Sixteen, on the bar, in courses, from the low end. Nobody is to be on the bottom of this face that is not needed on it. And I want the run going again in four minutes and I want it going the same way round the low end.”

---

**And the ninth generation asked four questions that morning and got four answers, and one of the answers was given by a man of thirty-two who has been in this valley about seven months and has not been asked anything and answered four questions in a morning without being asked any of them, and eleven on that bank noticed that and as many again did not.**

They asked whether the bar goes in the same every time, and the man of fifty-one said it did not and that a bar goes in where the joint is and that there was no joint in it and that was all the information he had.

They asked whether the face wants widening at the bottom, and the man of thirty-four said it did and that it would have it in the afternoon and not before, and that a face that is four feet at the top and four feet at the bottom is a hole and a face that is four feet at the top and six at the bottom is a face.

They asked what happens if it goes under the mill.

“The mill is four hundred yards up the bank and the mill has a wheel on it that has not turned since the first cold and a floor that a man of fifty-one and a man from a county four hundred miles off laid on stones in two days, and nothing is going to happen to the mill today, and if anything does, it will not be because of a bar, it will be because the ground let go, and I have been watching that ground since the second winter and it has not let go yet.”

And the fourth question was asked by a woman of about thirty-five of the ninth generation, who had come down the bank for the second day running and who is one of the four hundred people who have said out loud that the best wall anybody in this valley has ever seen is holding up a byre and that the floor of it is still a dinner plate.

“Then that is the thing that has been letting go,” she said. “Not the water. The water went quiet in the first cold and it has not come back, and there is a bank behind that byre with a beck coming out under the wall of it for sixty years, and my father has said for nine years that a byre is not a thing you stand a wall behind.”

“No,” said Wystan Roole. “It is not, and I have not got anything for you, and I have had nine years.”

**He said that in front of forty people and it went into four kitchens and eleven mouths, and the man of thirty-four did not improve on it and has not improved on it since, and the woman of about thirty-five has asked him about it twice since the fourth day and has been told twice that he has not got anything, and she has told him that she is going to keep asking and he has told her that he is not going to stop her.**

---

**And at the sixth hour of the third day the ground came back into the face, and the man of thirty-one of the ninth generation said it before the man of thirty-four did, and forty people came off a bank in less time than they came off it on the second morning, and nobody was hurt, and that is the only thing about the third day that anybody in this valley will be able to tell anybody else in a generation with an account anybody could check.**

The whole of the loose in the face, which had been a slope of about a foot from the top down to the step, went into the bottom in the time it takes to say a sentence, and it did not make a noise, and eleven people have said that the thing they remember about it is not the ground and is the silence, and one of them has said that the silence was worse than the noise would have been, and that is the only one of those accounts anybody has ever improved on.

“Off the face,” said a man of about thirty-one of the ninth generation, from nine feet above the top of it, and about forty people were off the bank in about three seconds and the man of thirty-four was the last of them and he was not slow, he was carrying a bar.

He got out and stood at the top with the bar in his hand and looked at the bottom, and the face had a foot of loose in it again in the time it took him to walk to the top and come back, and it had stopped.

“Nobody goes down there again until I have looked at it,” he said. “And I am not going to say I know what that is, because I have not got it and I have been two years getting to this bank and I have got four feet and a bar and no more.”

“And if it comes up again.”

“Then the face is four feet too deep in four days, and we will fill it to three and put a bar across the top and go home, and I will write the four days on the end of that bench and put *three feet* under it, and there will be a man in this valley in nine years who will want to know why.” He wiped his hands on a rope he was not using. “And he will not be able to check it against anybody, and that is the arrangement and I have been in it since I was twenty-five.”

---

**And the face did not come up again, and the sixteen had the bottom of it open by the seventh hour, and about forty people were on a bank in a cold working, and the man of thirty-four stood at the shoulder of a man of thirty-two with a limp for the whole afternoon, with a rope.**

Not a rope round a stone. A rope between them, round a stone about the size of a melon that had come out of the face on the first day, and the man of thirty-four's hands were on one end of it and the other end was round the man of thirty-four's own forearm at the elbow, and the man of thirty-two had the other of it, and the sixteen worked the bottom of the face in a course of a height with nothing cut to it, and eleven people in this valley were close enough to see it and as many again were not close enough and have said since that they were never told what it was for.

**And a man of fifty-eight came up the bank at about the fifth hour of the third day with a piece of rag paper and did not write anything on it, and eleven people saw him do it and about four of them asked him why, and he gave the same answer to all four of them and it is the reason there is no account of the four days in any book in this valley.**

He had a pencil and the rag paper was about the size of a hand and he held it down against the wind with a stone and he looked at the day column of the ninth spread in his own head and did not put anything on the paper, and the man of thirty-four came down off the bank and asked him about it and he said it out loud on a bank in front of four people.

“Three figures have gone into that book in a hundred and forty years and they went in on the twenty-fourth day of the second cold and about four hundred, and about a hundred and eighty-nine, and nine, and the last one of them is a figure of people and I did not put a name against it because the four lines at the head of the first spread say a name goes in because the person did it.

“**A figure of people goes in that book. A figure of days does not, and I have never put one in and I am not starting on the third day of four with a man of thirty-two standing at the bottom of a face.** There is no column for it and I have not got a second book and I am not going to take a leaf out of this one, because the day I take a leaf out of that is the day that book is two books, and I have kept one for nineteen years.”

He put the paper in his coat and did not say anything else about it, and the man of thirty-four said afterwards that a man who will not write a figure down is the only kind of person in this case who has ever been worth listening to, and that he had wanted the four days written down since the ninth day and had not asked twice.

Nobody asked. That is the thing about the four days and it is the thing that four accounts in four kitchens agree on without anybody having to check, which is that nobody asked, and that a man of thirty-four said beforehand that he was not going to explain what for, and that a man of thirty-two said nothing at all, and that a person in this valley will say, in a generation, that the four days were done with a rope, and will not be able to say what the rope was for, and will not be able to find anybody who was not asked and therefore does not know.

**And on the second evening of the third day the man of thirty-four called a stop on it at the sixth hour, and forty people stood on a bank in a cold and were told there would not be a fourth day of digging, and the fourth day was not the day anybody had written on the end of the bench.**

“Sixteen, and the rest of you can go home, and the four of you stay.”

Four people stayed. The man of thirty-four, a man of fifty-one, a man of about twenty-six of the ninth generation, and the man of thirty-two with a limp, and it was the first time in seven months that a man of thirty-four had kept a man of thirty-two behind when the others were let out of a yard, and four people noticed it and as many again did not, and the man of thirty-four said afterwards that he had not thought about it and had not decided it and would have sent him home with the rest of them if he had thought about it.

Then he said why there was not going to be a fourth day of digging, and he said it in eleven sentences and it was the last thing he said out loud in the four days, and it is why the hole in that bank was still a hole in the warm week.

“**You cannot fill a face in a cold. That is all of it, and I have been a fool for three days, and the four days wrote themselves.** A fill goes in loose in a cold because the ground it is going to sit on is frozen and cannot be keyed, and in the warm week the ground under a fill lets go of what is on it and the fill goes down into itself, and if a barrow run goes over the top of a fill that is going to do that, then the barrow run has got a hole in it, and the hole in the barrow run is at the ford, and the ford is where about eleven people in this valley get their water and about four hundred people get everything else they have.

“So it is going to stay open. We will put a course across the top of it at three feet and two barrows of stone on that, and the run goes over the top of that and does not know what is under it, and the top of it will not hold a barrow in the warm week and neither will the four hundred yards of made ground above it, and I have not got anything better than that and I have had three days and about forty people and a rope to try and get.

“And when the warm week is out and the ground has done whatever it is going to do, the sixteen of us will come down here and take that course up and fill it properly, in a warm week, with the ground keyed to it, and I will tell you now that that is nine weeks off and I do not know what this valley looks like in nine weeks and neither do you.”

Nobody asked him what it looked like now. A man of twenty-six of the ninth generation, who farms one of the eleven steadings and whose mother has a slate she has not given back in eleven years, said, “Then the four days on the bench are three,” and the man of thirty-four said, “Then the four days on the bench are three, and the fourth day is the day we put a course across the top of it and I will not rub the three out, because a man who rubs out a figure on a public stone in this valley is asking a question in a place with no writ and no bell and no office.”

---

And at about the ninth hour of the third day a man of about forty-one who farms three steadings and has a bar with a hundred and forty cut into the end of a bench somewhere four hundred yards up this bank put his bar down and stood still, and then put it on his shoulder again.

Nobody asked him that either.

---

**And then a man of thirty-four, from the top of a bank, in the ninth hour of the third day of the four days, with about forty people on a face and a barrow run behind them, said four words and about forty people put their barrows down.**

“Put your barrows down a minute.”

Nobody moved for a moment and then the run stopped, and about forty people stood on the top of that bank and in the four hundred yards of made ground above it, and nobody in this valley had ever stopped for anything that was not work in nine generations, and there is a fire in a room over a cross about forty times a year and nine people turn up to it and this was not that.

**The Burn makes a noise where it goes over its stones. It has made that noise every day for nine generations. A man of eighty-one has heard it every day of his life and a man of nine can hear it from the top of this bank. And in the first cold it stopped below the ford, and it did not stop above the ford, and it is four hundred yards of this valley where a river does not make the noise it has made every day for longer than anybody has been keeping a book.**

It was not making it.

Eleven people heard it. The man of thirty-four heard it and put his hand flat on the bar he was holding, and the man of fifty-eight was on the bank and heard it and came down four steps, and a man of about twenty-two of the ninth generation who has not been in that room in nine months heard it and said one word out loud, and nine grandmothers of the ninth generation had been saying a thing about the water for a whole generation and nine of them were not on that bank and none of them ever found out that the four days of a stranger in a valley in its ninth generation were the four days it came back on.

Nobody said anything about it. The man of thirty-four looked down about four hundred yards of river and then up at the bank and about eleven people were looking at him and nine were looking at the water.

“Right,” he said, after a while. “That is not ours. Nobody is to go and look at it and nobody is to say to anybody that they heard it, and I will tell you why I am saying that, and it is not that it is ours. **It is that a woman in this valley will say, at a table with four people at it, that the man who dug the hole made the river come back, and there will be no way in nine miles to go back to the ninth hour of the third day and find out whose it was.**”

That is the second time a man of thirty-four has said a version of that sentence and it is the last time, and four people in this valley have the two versions and as many again have said that the second one is better, and the man of fifty-eight said in a room in the warm week that it was not better, it was later, and that he has a book with three figures at the foot of the ninth spread in it and that a sentence is not a figure and does not go in.
