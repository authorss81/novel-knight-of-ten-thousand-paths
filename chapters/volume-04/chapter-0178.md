# Chapter 0178

There are eleven steadings in this valley and about four hundred people in them, and the ninth generation is about two hundred of those, and no two hundred people in a valley of four hundred can be asked anything in a room, in a market, or out of a book, and the reason is nine generations old and it is not a custom, it is a shape: the room over the cross takes forty and there has never been a month when forty would come, and the market takes about eleven people who can tell you which day of what it is, and the roll is in a room over a byre and the man who keeps it has spent nineteen years not letting it out of that room for reasons that are now on the page in six lines on the back of a form.

So the asking went on foot.

It went on foot because there are about thirty men under the cross and sixteen of them came, and because there is no cart in this valley and there has not been a cart in this valley in nine generations, and because a valley of about four hundred people has about four hundred pairs of feet in it and that is the only carrier anybody has ever had.

The method was four men and a woman at a door, and it was worked out in about half an hour by a man of thirty-four on the outside stair of the room over the cross on the twentieth day and it has not been improved on since, and it is the entire arrangement.

Four men because a man alone at a door is a man asking. Two men is two men asking. Four men and a woman is four men and a woman with nowhere to put anything, and the reason it is four and not five is that the fifth man would have brought a slate. The woman was the woman of the Low Holm, who has a yoke on her shoulders most of the day and who has never thanked anybody and never refused anybody and who is the eighth generation, and she asked one question about the job and it was how long it takes, and she was told nine days if they went at it and eleven if the weather turned, and she said that was a long time to be somebody's messenger and went and got a yoke.

The woman with the slate was asked at the door like everybody else, on the twenty-third day, on a day that is not a day anybody in that valley keeps, at about the sixth hour in the evening, and there is no word for what she was doing there because it is not a thing anybody has done before.

---

**And this is what was said at the first door, and it is on the page in full, and it was said at about nine hundred doors in nine days in the same words in the same order, and the reason it is printed here once is that it was printed once in nine miles and not nine hundred times, which is what this valley has never done with anything.**

The four men and the woman stood on the step and did not come inside, and one of the four said it, and it was a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick he had brought four hundred miles for four hundred yards of road and had not put down since the eighth of the month he came out on, in a county four hundred miles off.

“Six things, and then I will stop and you can ask me whatever you like and I will answer all of it.”

*One. There is a thing under this valley that is nine generations old and it is holding.*

*Two. It is straining. That is a thing a bridgekeeper of thirty-four has felt in a road since the first winter of this cold and cannot name, and I am telling you it is not his word and it is mine, and I cannot name it either, and there is nobody alive who can.*

*Three. There is a man here who can take it out. It would be done in a season. And the winter would come down.*

*Four. There is a book under a byre in this valley with about two hundred of you in it from the day you were born, and it says you are the people of a winter. You are not. You have never been asked what you think about a winter and you have never had a say in whether you were put in it, and nobody who has ever kept that book is in it, not one, and there is a form under the floor of the room at the back of that byre with eight columns in it and the valley wrote four lines at the head of the first spread about nine generations ago saying that there is nobody appointed to answer for the men that go out and that there never will be, and that is the best thing anybody in this valley has ever written down and nobody has ever found out why it was written.*

*Five. It does not make you safe. There is a count of what this valley owes in a file in a county about two hundred miles off. It is a figure. It has no name in it. It is the only thing in the world anybody could produce against anybody in this valley, and it will still be there in nine years whether the winter is here or not, and I have been in a room where a legal argument was answered with a figure in a woman's hand and no seal and no name and I have seen what a figure is for, and a figure is the only kind of paper that cannot be refused.*

*Six. It has to be stood to. Not by me and not by the man of thirty-four and not by the man of fifty-eight. By the about two hundred people in the book. All of you or as many of you as will stand to it. And nobody is going to be put in a column about it and nobody is going to have a stroke cut on a bench about it and it is not going to be read out in the cold in that room, and if you ask me afterwards what I said I will tell you and I will not have written it.*

Nobody in that doorway said anything at all.

“If I stood to it,” said a woman of about thirty-five, “what would you write down.”

“Nothing,” said Aren Kest. “There is nowhere to write it and I am not going to make a place. I am going to say a thing at about nine hundred doors and then four of us are going to remember what we can and a man of fifty-eight is going to put a figure in a book, and the figure will be a figure and it will not have a name in it and in nine years somebody will be able to look at it and not be able to tell you who stood to what. I know what that is. I have spent four years and a half learning that a thing written down twice is a thing two people can be got at, and I am not going to be the reason there is a second copy of this valley.”

“Then how will anybody know what I said.”

“They will not,” he said. “That is the answer and I have been in this valley about fifteen weeks and it is the first time in my life I have said a true thing to a person and known that it was the whole answer and I have not been frightened of it, and I would like you to notice that the woman who is the keeper of the only book in this valley is a man of fifty-eight and he did not do this and I did, and you may tell him that and he will put it in a minute and get it wrong.”

**And the woman with the slate was asked at the door like everybody else, on the twenty-third day, and she said yes in nine seconds, and her reason is the best reason anybody gave in eleven days, and nine doors heard it behind her and not one of them will ever be able to say who said it.**

She was in the fourth house from the mill and she had the slate under her arm as she always has it, and one of the four said the six things, and she did not ask any questions.

“I will stand to it,” she said, “and I would like you to notice that the reason is not that I believe you and it is not that I do not. It is that I have kept a number eleven years and nobody has ever asked me for one and kept it, and in eleven years not one person in this valley has ever wanted a figure off me that was going anywhere, and you have been to about nine hundred people in nine days and there was not one figure in any of your six things. Not one. You have asked this entire valley six things and not one of the six is a number, and I have been sitting here for eleven years with a number and nowhere to put it, and the only way a number of mine is going to be worth eleven years is if there is something in this valley worth counting.”

Nobody at that door said anything for four seconds.

“That is a better reason than the one I gave my own household,” said the man of thirty-four, from the step behind her, “and I would like it recorded that a woman who has been keeping a number in her head for eleven years and has never once been asked to give it away is the first person in this valley who has understood the sixth thing, and I have been one of the four of us since the first morning and I did not get there.”

“You got there on the first day,” said the woman with the slate, “and you are the only one of the four of you who ever said out loud that we were not writing anything down, and I heard you say it in a yard on the first morning and I have thought about it every day since, and it is the only sentence anybody has said to me in this valley in four years that was about my own work, and I have not said thank you for it and I am not going to, because if I say it you will put it in your head and start doing it, and there are four of you and there is one of me and I have thought about that arithmetic as well.”

**And the word came at the fourth door, and it came out of the valley and not out of him, and he did not translate it, and it is what he has been in this valley for.**

He had it ready and he did not use it, because a man who has been in a valley for about fifteen weeks has watched about nine people use eight words for one thing and has worked out that there is no word in that valley for agreeing, and has also worked out, from four words said in a market in the ninth week of the first cold and a fifth word said out loud in a room over a byre on the eleventh day of this cold, that the absence is not an accident.

At the fourth door there was a man of about fifty at a gate and the man of thirty-four put his hand on the gatepost and started to say *will you agree* and got about four words into it and stopped.

“Use the word,” said Wystan Roole. “You have been standing on my bank for nine weeks using four words from a county that has an office in it and every one of them has come out of your mouth like a thing a man reads off a board. I do not know what any of them mean and I have never asked and I am not going to, and you have just run out of one in the middle of a sentence at a gate and there is a word for it and it is about a foot thick.”

“There is a word in the county I came from.”

“Then use the word in the county you came from, and the man at this gate has never been to the county you came from, and he has a gate and a cold week and about nine minutes before you are finished on his step, and he is going to hear your word and hold it, and there is not one of us who is ever going to be able to say what he heard.”

**The word is the word this valley has for a bridge. It is in about forty mouths in nine miles and it is in no book in this valley, and it is not a rare word, and it is not a trade word, and it is about a foot thick, and a man of thirty-four used it before he noticed he had used it and did not take it back.**

“Will you stand to it,” said Aren Kest.

The man at the gate looked at the stick in the other man's hand for a while.

“That is a bridge word,” he said.

“It is a bridge word in this valley and it is not one in the county I came from and I am not going to put the other word on it, and I have been asked about that in the last nine seconds by a man of thirty-four who is right and by about nine hundred people this week who are not going to be asked.”

“You have been asked by four of us.”

“You have been asked by four of us and nine hundred people are about to be asked by four of us, and after this morning nobody is going to be able to tell you what the other eight hundred and ninety-nine said, and that is not because we cannot remember. It is because we are not writing it down, and I have been in this valley about fifteen weeks and that is the first arrangement anybody in it has ever made that I have understood, and I have understood it because of a man of fifty-eight and a door four steps down under his own byre, and I am not going to explain it on your step.”

The man at the gate stood in his own gateway for a moment longer and then he said, “Then I will stand to it,” and Wystan Roole said nothing at all, because a man of thirty-four who has been asking a valley for a road for nine years and has been refused twice knows exactly what a thing sounds like when it is the first time.

---

**And that is the second asking entire, and it began on the twentieth day of a cold in its second winter and it took eleven days and not nine, and they went to nine hundred doors, and every door got the six things in the same order in the same words, and four men and a woman did it.**

And it came into the room over the cross on the thirtieth day, in the only way anything from those eleven days can come into a room, which is that a man of thirty-four counted the doors in his own head at the end of it and gave the figure to the man of fifty-eight, and the man of fifty-eight took it and put it nowhere.

One figure. About nine hundred doors, and about four hundred people, and the doors of the same household counted once.

**And he did not write it down, and he gave the reason before anybody asked him for it, and the reason is that nine hundred doors is not a figure of anybody. It is a figure of walks. A man of fifty-eight has wanted a figure in that book since the year he was thirty-nine and there is about two hundred lines in the ninth spread that are the figure of a valley and he has not got it out of anybody yet, and a keeper who puts a number of doors in a book of days has put a number of his own boots in it, and in nine years that would be the first line in nine spreads anybody could not get out of.**

And the man of thirty-four said the thing about the eleven as he said it, standing at the trestles, and it is the last thing said in that room that winter that was not about a wall.

“Eleven of the ninth went over the pass on the fourth day and there has not been one of them back and it takes a week to the top and then they are gone and about four come back in the first warm week.” He put his hand on the trestle. “And the six things you have all been told say *about two hundred people in the book*, and I have been to about nine hundred doors in eleven days and I have not been to one of those eleven, and I cannot ask them and you cannot ask them and the man off the road cannot ask them, and until they are asked the thing is not stood to by all of you, and it is not stood to by any of you either, because a thing that nobody has ever asked a question has not been found out about by anybody, and I said the rest of that in a cold room eleven days ago and I have not improved on it since and I am not going to.”

Nobody in that room had an answer to that and nobody in that room was going to have one before the warm week, and the man of fifty-eight turned the last leaf of the ninth spread over and left it blank, and the light in the room did not do what light does in a county that keeps its hours, and it did not get dark at any hour anybody kept, and a girl of about nine was at the top of the outside stair with her hands in her sleeves and she had not knocked and she was not going to, and there is no hour in this valley at which anybody would be told that she was there.
