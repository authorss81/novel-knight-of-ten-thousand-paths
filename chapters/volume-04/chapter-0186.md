# Chapter 0186

**The council of this valley met in the room over the cross on the fourth day of the fourth week of the fourth cold, and it met there because there is no other room in nine miles with a fire in it and twenty-nine benches in it, and it is not a bench and has known it for longer than anybody in it has been alive, and nine people had heard it said out loud in that room in the third month of this cold and four had heard it in a yard and had told nobody.**

Nine people sat. The keeper of the only roll in a valley of about four hundred people sat at the front on a bench, because he keeps it and it goes in the first spread, and a man of fifty-eight, and he is not a member of anything and has not been able to tell anybody in this valley what to do in nineteen years and has not been asked to since a stranger asked him to read a leaf. A man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work. A man of fifty-five who had come up out of the yard the week before and said his own name. A woman of sixty-six of the eighth generation who has been at four looms for fifty-one years and who was asked what she swore forty years ago and answered, and it took nine minutes, and it was the worst of her life, and she has told the ninth generation about it and told them it was survivable, and it was survivable, and she was wrong, and she knows she was wrong, and she has never told anybody which of the three was the wrong one until the fourth day of the fourth week of the fourth cold. The woman of about thirty who has carried a slate for eleven years. The woman of the Low Holm, with a yoke on her shoulders, who came in and left it in the doorway because the bench in the second row had a barrow wheel against it. A man of about thirty-one of the ninth generation who farms four steadings and had not wanted to come and came. A man of about sixty-one who could not leave his mill and had not wanted to come and came and had a hand on the rail of the seat.

And a man of eighty-one who had not been asked to come and came, and had been in that room sixty times and had never once heard what was in it.

And a young man of twenty-two at the back of the right-hand row, on the bench with a hundred and forty cut into the end of it, who had not come for this and had not been asked for it and had come because there are nine people in this valley who come to a room when there is a fire in it.

And the aisle is four feet wide and it runs from the door to the front, and it was empty all the way through both days, and nobody stood in it, and nobody was asked to, and four people in the room could see the whole length of it without turning their head.

---

**The motion was put in about forty words by a man of fifty-five, and it went in the minute in a clerk's hand, and the clerk is a man of about sixty who has kept the minute of this valley for twenty-two years and is not a member of it and has never been asked to be.**

“That the council be asked whether it is going to keep the winter, and that it be asked what happens if it does not, and that both things be put in this minute, and that the second of them be put in this minute whether it is answered or not.”

Nobody moved for about four seconds.

“I am not asking you to vote on the first one,” said Torrin Hatch. “I am asking you to be asked it. You are nine people and there are four hundred in this valley and two hundred of them are in a book under a byre and eleven of them went over a pass on the fourth day of this cold and cannot be asked, and four of those eleven are going to come back in the first warm week and I have been in this valley nine years and I have never once known which four.

“So this council cannot answer for the valley and it has never been able to answer for the valley, and I am the one who has been saying so in yards for thirty years and four people ever heard me. I am asking this body to be asked a thing it cannot answer, in a room, in writing, in front of a clerk, so that there is a piece of paper in this valley that says a thing about a winter and not about a man.

“I am not asking for a decision. I am asking for a record. And I would like the record to say that the council could not answer, because that is the first time in nine generations that anybody in this valley has written down anything that nobody could answer.”

---

**And before the second question was put, the man of about thirty-one said the thing that four people in that room had been thinking about for an hour and had not said, and he said it badly and knew it was bad before he had finished the first sentence.**

“We have nine people on this bench and one of us is of the ninth generation and he is here because he farms four steadings and his father did, and not one of us is on the roll under that byre as a representative of anything, and there is no such thing, and I have looked for it since the first of the cold and there is no box on this bench for it and there is no way of putting us in a room and asking us, because four hundred people cannot be asked in a room and nine hundred doors is a thing five men did in eleven days and it is not a council and it is not a minute and it is not a thing anybody can stand behind.

**“So I am going to say the thing that is under the room and nobody is going to say it. We are deciding about a winter that two hundred people are in a book for, and none of those two hundred is in this room in a seat, and one of us is in the room at the back on a bench with a hundred and forty cut into the end of it and he is not in a seat either. And if we vote today then the vote is this bench voting about two hundred, and not one of us was chosen by the two hundred and none of us can be, and the minute will say that we decided, and the minute will be in a room over a mill and nobody will ever be served with it.**

“That is not a reason not to vote. That is the reason I want it in the minute.”

The clerk wrote that in as well, and it took him a minute, and he wrote it in his own hand and not as a finding, and the man of thirty-one looked at what he had written and did not ask to have any of it changed, and has not asked in nine years.

And then the second question was put and it went round the room and it was put nine times and it was answered four times, and the four answers are in the minute, and the other five were not, and the clerk wrote *no answer* five times in a valley with no writ and no bell and no office.

“What happens if it does not.”

The woman of sixty-six at four looms said it first, in four seconds, and it is the only thing said in that room in the nine weeks that anybody has written down.

“I do not know. I am sixty-six. My grandmother stood in that aisle and she told me about it four times and never once told me what it was for, and I have been at four looms for fifty-one years and I have asked about the winter twice, both times of a woman with a slate, and both times I was told to leave it, and I do not blame her and I would do the same. And I am going to say the other half of it, in a room, on a day this council has put in a minute, and nobody has ever asked me for it and I have been waiting forty years and there is no room in this valley for waiting.

“Forty years ago I was asked what I swore in that aisle, in the cold, in front of the whole valley, with a man at the back with a slate and a book behind him, and it took nine minutes, and it was the worst of my life, and I will not say what I said. I told the ninth generation about it because they were owed it, and I told them it was survivable.

**“And it was survivable. And I was wrong. And what was wrong was not the asking. The asking was right and it had never been done and it should have been done to me in a kitchen with a fire in it. What was wrong was the man with the slate and the book behind him, and a book does not know the difference between what a person said and what was true, and that is why I have spent four years telling the ninth generation that I would not lie to them about it, and why I have not once told them all of it, and why what I tell them is a sentence at a time.**”

Nobody wrote that down either, because there is nowhere to write it, and four people in that room had heard her say it all once before, in a cheese house, in the first month of this valley, to a stranger.

The man of thirty-four said it second, and it took him eleven seconds and he gave all of it.

“I have built four walls and a stand and four hundred yards of ground in this valley and I can tell you exactly what every one of them does and I could not tell you what a single one of them is for. I have asked this valley twice for a road and been refused twice, and in the second month of this cold I worked out that no council here ever decided not to build one, because four hundred people had never once wanted to be anywhere.

“And a body that has never decided anything cannot be asked to decide about a thing it has never been asked about. If you ask it, it will say yes. It will say yes because saying yes is what it has done the four times it has been asked, and you will go out of this room with a minute with the word *yes* in it about a winter that not one of the nine of you could describe, and that is worse than not having a minute, because in nine years somebody is going to read that minute and believe it.”

And the man of eighty-one said it third, and it was the one that was about the room and not about the winter, and it had been building in him since the third month of this cold and it came out in nine sentences.

“About forty of my generation are dead and nine of the eighth are in this room and I am the only one in this valley who has been in that aisle and been asked and come out of it, and I am eighty-one, and I am going to be asked nothing and I am not going to stand in it.

**“The ninth generation has not stood in it. The tenth has not. The eleventh has not. The aisle over the cross has now gone three generations without being used and the benches are still bolted to the floor and the hundred and forty is still cut into the end of that one and nobody has ever been struck off one of them and the number has not moved in the whole time the man of fifty-eight can remember and it is not going to move in mine.**

“And the tenth generation is coming of age in this cold. Not the ninth. The tenth. Forty of them, and none of them has ever been in a room with a fire in it except four of them, and the four of them are in here, and I counted forty of them out of my own head in half an hour by the fires and I have not told anybody and I am telling a room now because a room is what I have got.

**“And nobody is going to be asked. I have been in that room sixty times and I have watched thirty of the eighth generation and the ones before them stand in that aisle and be asked, and I have watched a hundred and forty of the ninth go past a door that was open in a cold week, and in a few years a hundred and forty more are going to go past it, and there is a stroke cut into the end of that bench for every single one of them who has ever stood in it, and nobody has ever been struck off one, and that is the only roll this valley has ever kept of what it has done to anybody.**”

The room over the cross was quiet and the stove ticked and the light in the one window about the size of a man's hand did not do what light does in a county that keeps its hours.

And then the man of fifty-eight had the form out, and it came down four steps and out of a room about four feet by six with a lamp, and the people in the room over the cross had never been within nine feet of it and four of them had not known it existed a year ago.

The stone is a piece of the byre coping squared on one face by a man of fifty-eight in the dark, and it is lying across the sheet about a hand's width on the diagonal and it is not square to the boards, and he put it down where it had been, on the diagonal, and nobody in that room knew it was crooked and four people who had been in the back room knew.

“This is the only thing anybody has ever sent this valley,” he said. “I have told you that. It is a printed sheet, it is about two hundred years old, it is headed with the name of an office four valleys and a long way over a pass, and it has eight columns on it and three of them are answered and two of them are empty.

“The third column asks for the number of men going out of the quarter and it is answered *four in this year* in a hand that is not mine and not my father's and does not vary the way a hand varies, and there is no year in this valley and there has not been one since before the first of the nine, and I am not going to turn that number into a length of time.

“The fourth column asks for the number of those that have gone out and not come back, and it is empty. The fifth column asks for the name of the person appointed in the quarter to answer for the men that go out, and it says *none appointed* in a slow careful hand, and the reason nobody is appointed is written at the head of my own book in four lines a hundred and forty years old, and my great-grandfather wrote them having read that column on that floor, and I have read those four lines nine hundred times and never once knew what they were a rule about until a man of thirty-two stood on that step and asked me.

“The seventh column says *withdrawn* and there is a rule drawn across the head of it, and a man does not rule a line across a space on a form unless he is going to write one word in it, and it has not been ruled across.

“The eighth column says *answer received*, and it is empty, and the rule at the bottom of it is no more faded than the rule at the bottom of the third column, which means it has not been filled in for a very long time and nobody ever came back and looked.

“And at the foot of it there are nine printed words, and the same nine words are on a form printed about two hundred years later in a county four hundred miles off, and I know that because he told me in that room and he would not describe the other one to me, and the reason he would not is that a man who describes a document to a room has made as many copies of a thing nobody in this valley can see as there are people listening to him.”

He put his hand flat on the sheet, which is what he does, and took it off again.

“So. There is one document in this valley that anybody from anywhere has ever sent us, and I have had it in my hand and read it out and put six lines on the back of it, and it is about the number of men that go out of a quarter. **It is not about a winter. Nothing anybody has ever sent this valley is about a winter. And that is the answer to the second question, and it is not an answer, and I am going to hand it to this council as what I have got.**

“What happens if it does not keep the winter. Nobody in this room knows. Nobody in this valley knows. The thing underneath has been held nine generations and there is no book about it that anybody has read, and I have read everything anybody in this valley has written since my great-grandfather's time, and it is a book of days and marks and hands, and there is not one word in it about a cold, and there is not one word in it about the ground.

“And I have a third figure to give the room and I gave it in the second month of this cold and it is the first time in a hundred and forty years there has been a figure in that book, and it is four hundred, and about a hundred and eighty-nine, and nine. And the nine is three households that said no, and I put no name against the nine, and I have not been asked to and I would not have if I had been.”

And that was the end of the first day, and the clerk read the minute back, and nine people in a cold room listened to about four hundred words in a hand that is not the keeper's hand and is not any hand in this valley except his own, and the last thing in the minute was the second question, and the clerk had written it in four words, and the four words were: *No answer in the room.*

And the meeting sat again on the following morning, and the fire was lit at the front at the fourth hour and not anywhere else, and the aisle was four feet wide and it was empty.

And a woman of about thirty took the slate out from round her neck and put it on the trestle at the front and said a figure out loud, and it was her figure, and she had kept it eleven years and had never once been asked for it and had given it twice at a gate in the second month of this cold, and a man of fifty-eight said that the ninth spread of the roll in the room over the byre has about two hundred of the ninth generation in it, and the two figures were not the same, and neither of them was struck out, and neither of them was offered up to be corrected, and there was nobody in that room who could say which of the two of them was the ninth generation, and there is nowhere in nine miles that either of them can be put, and the two of them are going to be in this valley as long as either of them lasts.
