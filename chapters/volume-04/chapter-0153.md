# Chapter 0153

The first of Goatmonth, YR 313, was a Friday and it was not a market day, and a sheet of ordinary writing paper with a fold in it had been on the table under a limestone weight for four days.

Four days is not long. It is one Thursday and one Friday and two market mornings that this county does not have, and it is long enough that everybody who came up those two flights had looked at it, and nobody had put their hand on it, and by the fourth day it had become a thing in the room in the way a thing that has been left too long becomes a thing in the room.

Alish Renn opened it. She is thirty and second copyist and on a warrant of the keeper's own that is not in the press, and she said the thing that has to be said before a document is opened, which she has said twice in four years and a half and which is the same sentence both times because she has never improved on it.

“A document with an address on it is a different animal from a document without one,” she said, “and this one is addressed to a person in an office two hundred miles off, and to no office, and it has come back with a knife through the seal, and there is no office in this country that can say what a person in an office ought to have done with it, and I am going to open it because it has been on this table for four days and because a thing that is not opened is not a record, it is a piece of paper in a room.”

“Enter the reason,” said the keeper of records.

“The reason is entered. The reason is that it has no standing here and that leaving it shut does not improve its standing and that I am the second copyist of this office and the standing of this office is that a person having knowledge is the person whose knowledge it is, and it is not mine and it is not yours, and I am opening it because it is in my hand and not because it is mine.”

It is four hundred and eleven words. It is in a hand that is not a clerk's and is the hand of a man who was in this room on the fifth of this month and has not been in it since, and nobody in the room said so and two people in the room could not stop themselves from looking at the hand.

Alish Renn read it out. She read all of it, including the address, and she read it in the voice she uses for documents, which is not the voice the keeper uses for facts and is slower and does not do anything to the words.

*To Sabin Dain, aid to the register at the Office of Provincial Registration. And to no office.*

*This is a refusal. I am writing it instead of saying it, and I am writing it because a spoken no is a thing that can be repeated by whoever heard it and a written no is a thing that can be produced by the person it was written to, and you are the only person in this business who has ever written the reason down before asking for the thing.*

*One. I am not taking the grade. It is of the grade used in the eastern counties, with a district, with a clerk, with a stipend of eleven marks a year, and with the right to send men. It is a command and it is not a custody. I have been in a county for a year and a rolling in which the man who holds a length of water answers to the people on both sides of the water and to nobody else, and a bench there has governed nothing on purpose for nineteen years, and I have spent two years refusing to be made answerable for a road I keep, and I am not going to be made answerable for a road I do not keep in a rank that has the word warden in it and a command underneath it. I have no argument against a command. I have one against a command that a person appointed by nobody can hold and that a person appointed by a community can refuse.*

*Two. I am not taking the sealed record. A record of myself, kept by your office, produced to any body that asks your office a question about me, not produced to me at any time while it is sealed, the seal yours and not mine. I have spent two years not making a record of that kind and I have been worse at refusing it than I am at building bridges, and this letter is the fourth attempt and the first one that is a document. I have no argument against your office. I have one against a record a person cannot see, and I put that in writing nine years ago in a fair copy and I have not improved on it and I am not going to now.*

*Three, and this is the part that is for you and not for the file. The sheet says the offer was made out in the ninth month of the year three years last past and has been held open since, and that the number of persons to whom it has been made out in that time is not stated on the sheet. I have been about four months thinking about that sentence and I have arrived at the conclusion that it is the only sentence on the sheet that matters, and that the eleven marks and the district and the clerk are the price of not printing it, and that a woman with a filing system and a horror of unnamed things wrote that sheet with the number left off on purpose, and that I am not going to be the one who tells her she did it on purpose, because I do not know that she did, and because if she did then there is a number in her office and if she did not then the number is the sheet's and the sheet is the office's and the office has had three years.*

*Four. There is a warrant of this grade in a sealed envelope in a press in the provincial town. I am not going to ask anybody to go and fetch it. If it is produced to me I will send it back and I would like the return entered whichever way it goes, and if it is not produced then nothing is lost that I can measure.*

*Five. The eastern paths are not to be closed by me. That is not a bargaining position and is not held against anything, and I will not trade on it. I do not know how to close a road and I am not going to learn how. A county of clay with no office in it, four hundred miles off, had its road closed in four lines of writing in the middle of this year by a bench that asked for it, in a hand that is not a clerk's, and it contained no name, and the fourth line gave back a term I had helped write nineteen years ago, and it was correct, and I read it in a room with about nine people in it and I did not say the fourth line out loud, and that is everything I know about closing a road and it was done by a bench of nine that has been asked four times and has said yes four times and is not a bench.*

*Six. There is an earlier letter of mine in a bag going the other way, written in the middle of the summer, which neither accepts nor refuses this and carries no returnable date and no number and no copy in this county. I am not withdrawing it. A thing that is not withdrawn is a thing that can be produced, and I would like this county to be able to produce both of them.*

*And there is no thanks in this letter, and you did not offer a thing that needed any, and I would rather a woman who writes the reasons down first did not have to be polite to me in a document she is going to keep.*

---

**Nobody in that room apologised to anybody, and nobody in that room had anything to apologise for, and that is not because anybody in the room is careful. It is because there is nothing in the document that a person could apologise about, and a document that cannot be apologised about is a document that is either very badly written or extremely well written, and nobody who reads it can tell which, and the woman who wrote it is two hundred miles off and did not write it to be read aloud in a room over a weigh-house in a fen town on a Friday morning.**

The keeper of records had her hand flat on the table and she did not pick up her pen for about a minute and a half, and the minute and a half is the whole of the silence in that room on the first of Goatmonth.

“Three,” she said at last. “That is the paragraph that has just landed in this room and the other five are a document.”

“It is a paragraph about a sheet in another office,” said Sarn Oyelaran.

“It is a paragraph about a number that is not on a sheet, and this office has been living on the other side of that number for four years and a half, and I would like everybody in this room to understand what has just happened, and I would like it entered, and I am going to say it slowly.”

She turned round in the chair and addressed the room and not the page, which is a thing she does perhaps twice a year.

“That office has held an offer open for three years. The offer is of a grade, and a district, and a clerk, and eleven marks a year, and the right to send men, in exchange for the eastern paths being closed by the man who holds them. The sheet it is written on states the date it was made out and states that it is held open, and it does not state how many people it has been made out to, and I have had a copy of that sheet in this room for three years and I have read it perhaps forty times and I never once thought about the number of persons, because there was nothing on the page to think about.

“And in this room, in this press, there is a whole leaf of returns of the same series with a real man's name in the fourth column of it, in a hand that is not his, and the office relied on that column and the county never knew, and the county's minute cannot be served and there is no Crown's hand in that county at all, and that is the whole reason a man in that county is still carrying a thing he did not agree to. **That is what an office that will not print a number of persons looks like from the inside, and I have had the inside of it in this room for four years and a half and I have been reading the same words on the same page.**

“An office that will not say how many people an instruction has been made out to is an office that cannot say that a person has not been instructed. That is the whole of it. It is not a threat and it is not a plot. It is the ordinary way an instruction becomes a standing thing about everybody in a country, and it happens in a filing room, in daylight, by people who are pleasant about it.”

She wrote for about two minutes, and then she read it back, and it is the entry, and it is entered in the ordinary form with the date and the hour and the claim number against the office and nothing against any person.

“Entered, on the first of Goatmonth, YR 313, sixth hour, against no person: that a document addressed to a person in an office two hundred miles off, and to no office, has come back to this county opened, and that it refuses a grade and a sealed record and declines to trade on the eastern paths, and that its third paragraph is that the number of persons to whom the offer has been made out is not stated on the sheet, and that this office has held a copy of that sheet for three years and has entered nothing about the number, and that the whole of the entry is that an office which does not print a number of persons cannot show that any person has not been instructed. And that this office has had the other half of the same arrangement in its press for four years and a half, in a leaf, in a column headed *Keeper*, in a hand that is not the man's, and has entered nothing about that either.”

“And the answer,” said the keeper, “is a letter asking for the number.”

“Yes,” said Alish Renn. “And no name in it.”

“No name in it,” said the keeper of records, “and no word of explanation of how we came by the number we are asking for, and no reference to the leaf, and no reference to this document, and I want that written on the outside of the letter so that whoever in that office opens it knows that this office has not told them anything, and I want it written in the plainest words in the envelope and not in the letter, because a letter is a thing that can be produced and an envelope is a thing that gets thrown away.”

Then she stopped, and put the pen down, and said the thing that makes her the best keeper of records in this county and has cost her something four times.

“And I am not going to say the name of the woman who wrote it. She is in an office two hundred miles off, she is thirty-three, and this letter was not addressed to me, and if I say her name in this room this afternoon then a person in this county who is not the man who wrote it will know that the man who wrote it is in this county, and that is a name going into a conversation in a county that has not had one since the twenty-seventh of Fallowmonth, and I will not do it, and he would not want me to, and the reason I know he would not want me to is that he wrote *and to no office* at the top of it and meant it.”

Sarn Oyelaran wrote that down.

“It is against the office,” he said. “It has to be against the office.”

“It is against the office and it is against nobody and I have done that before and I will do it again,” she said, “and if the day comes when this office has to put a name in a line to get a thing done, then the day has come when this office should not have been given the door.”

---

**And a man in the lane asked about the writing on the back of a printed form, and was refused about it, and the refusal was four words and it was in a gateway and not in this room.**

It was a carter with a barrow of lime who wanted to know why the yard at the top of Weir Street had a crown's mould of grey wax nailed over a doorway, which is a thing a man sees every day of his life and thinks about twice a year, and who had in his barrow a form of the same series as the leaf in the press, filled in by somebody, and who wanted to know what the office wanted with the back of it.

Alish Renn was in the lane and she answered him, and what she said was: “I cannot say that here.”

“I am asking you what is written on it.”

“I know what is written on it and it is in a document in a press in a room with a door on this lane, and I cannot say it here, and you would not be able to un-hear it, and there is a woman in this county who has spent two years making sure that office has nothing of hers to quote.”

“That is four words,” said the carter.

“It is five,” said Alish Renn, “and one of them is *cannot*, and that is the one I would like you to keep, and you may have the rest of the conversation free of charge, and I have not got any more of it.”

The carter took his barrow up the street. Nothing was printed on the back of a form in a gateway, and nothing was explained, and the lane is not a room and a gateway is not a hearing, and the man who wrote on the reverse of that form has never been asked to explain it and did not come to Merefen to.

---

**And the return went out on the Friday post at about the eighth hour, in a bag, and the bag is on a man who is carrying it at his own cost, and there is no name in it, and the cut band went into the press whole.**

That is the last of the first of Goatmonth. The band is a strip of paper with a seal on one end of it and a knife's stroke down the left edge, and it weighs nothing, and it is the only object in this case that an office has answered a person with, and it is going in the press unserved with a claim number against nothing on a wrapper, and in about four years and a half a woman in a filing room in this county will go into that press for something else and will see a strip of cut paper and will not know what it is, and that is the correct outcome and there is nothing anybody can do about it and nobody in this room tried.

And the letter with the four hundred and eleven words in it went into the same press, entire, and not copied, because a second hand on a man's own refusal is a second man's opinion of it, and this office does not keep second opinions.

The lane door made its noise at about the ninth hour and Rowan Petch took the bag down the two flights and out and away, and a woman of about sixty-six in the lane asked him whether the post was going up the hill today, which it was not, and he said so, and she said that was a pity because she had a jar to send down, and he said he would come back on Monday, and she said Monday, and that was the whole of it.
