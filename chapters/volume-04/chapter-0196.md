# Chapter 0196

**The winter came down over eleven weeks, and it did not come down like a thing coming down. The water came up, and the ground under a valley of four hundred people that had not moved in nine generations moved, and it moved all at once, and four hundred yards of the best ground anybody in that valley had ever seen went to a slope in nine days, and there is no figure in this valley for any of it and there is not going to be.**

A generation is a winter and a winter is a generation and the ninth is the one that has been keeping. The fourth cold ended and the warm week was nine days and the warm week ended, and after the warm week there was no cold in this valley for the first time in nine generations, and there is no word in nine miles for the eleven weeks that followed it because they had not happened before.

The man of fifty-eight did not enter the eleven weeks in the book. He was asked for it three times in the warm week by three different people and he gave the same answer to all three, and the answer was four words long and it is in a minute in a room over a mill.

“A week is a week of this cold.”

**And he said the other half to eleven people in the room over the cross on the fourth morning of it, and it is the reason there is no account of the eleven weeks in any book in this valley, and nobody in the valley improved on it.**

“Nine hundred doors had eleven words put at them once in nine generations. The four days had a figure in chalk on the end of a bench in a room with twenty-nine benches in it and a hundred and forty cut into the end of one of them. The thirty-one days of the warm week have a figure of money in the ninth spread of the only roll in this valley, in my hand, and it is the first figure of money that has ever gone in there.

“**And a figure of weeks does not, and the reason is not that weeks are not worth keeping. The reason is that a week of this valley is a thing a person standing up in a cold room says a week is, and there is no authority in this valley that could be asked, and the day there is one is the day somebody in this valley can be made to answer for what happened in the eleven weeks, and I would like to know what I am going to say then before I put a number against it.**”

Nobody in the room said that he had just refused to be the only man in the valley who could be made to answer for anything, and four people understood all of it and nine did not, and one of the four is a woman of sixty-six at four looms who has been asked what she swore forty years ago in the cold in front of the whole valley with a man at the back holding a slate, and she said one sentence about it at the second hour and it went in the minute.

“Then when somebody comes, make them ask a person.”

---

**And in the third week the four hundred yards of made ground went to a slope, and the ninth stone of the new coping at the Low Holm came off it, and the barrow run at the ford held a loaded barrow on the fourth day of that week and did not go down, and a man of thirty-four stood on it for an hour and a half and did not get off it once.**

The four hundred yards was the best ground anybody in this valley had ever seen and a man of eighty-one had watched a barrow go down it four times and said that he had never once done it himself and had watched thirty of the eighth generation do it. It is a slope now. It is not a scar and it is not a bank and it is not a road. It is four hundred yards of ground that has stopped being the thing a man of thirty-four made in nine weeks with sixteen men and about thirty under the cross and a barrow once a day, and there is no instrument in nine miles that could tell anybody which part of it went and which part of it was always going to go.

“**It is not the hole,” he said, to eleven people on the top of it. “I want that in a kitchen before the end of the month. The hole is filled and keyed and a man of thirty-two looked at it four times in a warm week and said it was square four times, and this is not the hole and it is a mile and a half up the bank of it. Nine generations of ground that has not moved in nine generations, and it is all going to move at once, and there is not one wall in this valley that has not been up for less than all of that, and I have not got anything.**”

And the ninth stone of the new coping at the Low Holm came off on the fourth day of that week, on its own, in the way the ninth stone of a course of one height comes into its bed on its own, and about eleven people were on the bank and about four of them said afterwards that they had seen it, and the man of thirty-four picked it up and turned it over and there was nothing on it, and he put it back in the bed and it came out again in the afternoon, and he left it on the bank on the fifth day.

**Nobody in this valley has ever asked a question about a stone, and there is nobody in this valley who is going to, and the stone is on the bank at the Low Holm and the barrow run goes round it.**

---

**And in the fifth week six byres came down and the stores in one of the four seed houses went under four feet of water, and the seed came up, and the seed went into the room over the cross and into the aisle, and the aisle is four feet wide and has not had a person in it for three generations.**

Six byres in a valley in a warm week is not a tragedy. It is a hole in a roof, a floor that has not been walked on, a door a family shuts behind them and does not open until there is somebody in the yard. Four of the six were on ground that a beck has been coming out under for sixty years and the fifth was the best wall anybody in this valley had ever seen holding up a byre, and the sixth was a byre with about forty foot of new coping on it done by a man of fifty-one in a good week and nothing else done to it in nine generations.

The seed house was at the low end of one of the eleven steadings and it had a stone floor and a door that shut and four feet of the Burn in it in four days, and the seed was barley and the seed on a piece of rag paper in the man's hand, and the man had it up the bank in two days with his brother and a barrow and eleven people helping and nobody asked.

It went into the room over the cross because that is the only room in nine miles with a fire in it about forty times a year, and it went into the aisle because there is nowhere else in that room for nine bushels of barley, and four people carried it in and nobody stood in the aisle while they did and the aisle is four feet wide and there has not been a person in it for three generations.

**And the clerk of about sixty, who has kept the minute of this valley for twenty-two years and is not a member of it and has never been asked to be, wrote in the minute that the seed had gone into the aisle of the room over the cross on the sixth day of the eighth week, and that is the first entry he has made in twenty-two years that was not something said out loud in a room in front of him, and he asked the man of fifty-eight about it twice before he made it.**

“Write it,” said the man of fifty-eight. “It is a thing that happened in a room in this valley and there is nobody else who is going to write it down and there is no column for it and I am not ruling one.”

---

**And in the sixth week four of the nine roofs that had been open since the summer of the second cold came in, and the fifth is a roof on a house that is not there, and the six that were not open are the only roofs in nine miles that anybody has stood under in nine generations, and the mill went in the seventh, and the ford moved twice in the eighth and did not move again.**

A roof in this valley is for a family. That is a sentence a man of about twenty-four said on a bank in the fourth week of the fourth cold and his mother gave him in a kitchen in the second month of that cold, and there is not one family in this valley that has asked the sixteen for anything since before the man of thirty-four was born, and the four roofs that came in were four roofs over four houses of which three are not there any more and the fourth is a house with about four foot of floor in it and a woman of sixty-eight in it who has a bank behind it that went.

The mill went on the low side. The wheel had been off since the fourth week of the fourth cold and had been carried up four hundred yards in one go on a barrow by a man of about sixty-one who could not leave it and would not let anybody else touch it. The building lost the wall on the water side in a wet week in the seventh week of the eleven and there is no stone in this valley for a wall at a mill race and there is not going to be one, and the man of about sixty-one sat on the mill step for two days and then asked four people in one day whether he was going, and said that he had not decided, and on the ninth day he decided, and the reason he gave for it was in about nine words and it was about the race and not about the winter.

**And the ford moved twice in the eighth week, a foot each time, which is a year's worth in eight days, and it has not moved since, and there are about four people in this valley who have a barrow and know what a moved ford is and about four of them have noticed, and none of them has said a word about it in a room, and the man of thirty-four has not said it in a room either.**

He said it in a yard, to four people, on the ninth day of the eighth week, and he said it once and did not say it a second time, and the reason he said it in a yard and not in a room is that a room has a clerk in it.

“It has stopped. Nine good stones are in the footing of the second stand under four feet of water in the order they came off, and the footing is in the bed of that ford, and the bed is where a ford is, and the ford has gone down about a foot a year since I was twenty-five and it has gone down twice in eight days and has not gone down at all for the rest of the week.

“**And I am not going to say that is because of a hole in a bank that a man of thirty-two and sixteen of us filled in a warm week. I have got four trades' worth of reasons a ford might stop and I have not got the one that is true, and I have been nine years on this bank and I am not going to spend the nine years of a man of fifty-eight on a thing I cannot check.**”

**And in the ninth week the ninth generation was asked one more time, in the room over the cross, on a morning with a fire in it, and the asking was not refused, and what they said was four words long, and it is in the minute in a clerk's hand and it is entered as a fact and not as a defeat and the clerk read it back twice.**

The room over the cross has twenty-nine benches bolted to the floor in four rows with a number cut into the end of each one and a hundred and forty cut into the end of the one at the back of the right-hand row, and the aisle is four feet wide, and in the ninth week of the eleven weeks there were nine bushels of barley in it and thirty people in the room and none of them standing in it.

Nine people were on the bench. A man of fifty-eight who keeps the only roll in a valley of four hundred people and is not a member of anything. A man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work. A man of fifty-five who had voted against keeping the winter eight to one in the fourth week of the fourth cold and had come to that room four more times in as many years and had not stood in the aisle at any point. A woman of sixty-six of the eighth generation who was asked what she swore forty years ago and answered, and who has offered in writing in a minute nobody can be served to stand in that aisle at sixty. A woman of about thirty who has carried a slate for eleven years. The woman of the Low Holm, with a yoke on her shoulders, who had a barrow run going over a hole four feet from her front door for four days and had gone over it four times a day and had said nothing about it. A man of about thirty-one of the ninth generation who farms four steadings. A man of about sixty-one who could not leave his mill and came. A man of eighty-one who had not been asked to come and came.

**And about two hundred of the ninth generation were in the room and not one of them was in a seat and not one of them could be, and a young man of twenty-two was at the back of the right-hand row on the bench with the hundred and forty cut into the end of it, and the clerk wrote the order they were asked in before anybody argued for it, and the order was the order they were sitting in, and the young man of twenty-two was asked seventh.**

The question was put by the man of thirty-four and it took him about four minutes to get round to it and he had had it ready for nine weeks.

“Eleven weeks. About nine hundred doors and eleven words and a figure of nine at the bottom of a page in a book under a byre and nine against it, and eleven of you over a pass that cannot be asked, and about four of them back, and nobody can say where the other seven are, and four hundred people in this valley and about two hundred of you in a book from the day you were born.

“**We are not asking you to keep it. There is nothing left to keep. I am asking you one thing and it is the only thing I have got and I have had it ready for nine weeks. I am asking you what it was.**”

Nobody answered for about a minute and a half. The man of thirty-one of the ninth generation put his hand flat on the end of his bench and said, “Ask it as a question and not as a thing with a person in it,” and the man of thirty-four said that it was a question and that he had not been able to work out how to make it one and that he was asking it anyway.

The man of fifty-eight read the minute back. *That the council has asked the ninth generation what the winter was, and that the ninth generation has answered, and that the answer is four words long, and that the answer is entered as a fact about this room and is not entered as a defeat and is not entered as a pledge.*

“Put the four words in the middle of the page,” said the man of fifty-eight, “and not at the end, and not in the day column, because it is not a figure of people and it is not a figure of days and it is not money, and there is no column in this book for a thing that is a fact and not a count, and I am not ruling one at eight in the morning on the ninth week of the eleventh.”

The clerk read it back twice. The second reading was slower and not better, and that has been the practice of this valley since the first year.

**We said yes once.**

Nobody said anything about it for about a minute. The man of fifty-five said, “That is the only true sentence anybody has said in this room in the two years I have been coming to it, and it is four words and it is not a pledge and it is not a refusal, and I am not going to say another word about it, and I have not stood in the aisle and I am not going to.”

A woman of about thirty-five of the ninth generation asked what the nine was, and the man of thirty-four told her, in about four sentences, what he had made it out of in about a minute on a step in the cold with a rope-belt on, and that the three households hold eight people and that there is a ninth he cannot find, and that the ninth line in the second column of the ninth spread of the only roll in this valley is empty and is not going to be ruled across, and eleven people in that room looked at the man of thirty-four and four looked at the man of fifty-eight.

**And the clerk wrote the four words in the middle of the page, and read them back twice, and shut the minute, and the fire went out at the ninth hour, and nine bushels of barley were in the aisle of the room over the cross, and a girl of nine came up the outside stair during all of it, did not knock, stood on the fourth tread, counted the benches through a doorway, got twenty-nine, and did not go in.**

And in the tenth week about two hundred people in this valley began to find out where the pass was.

Nobody in nine miles told them. There was a market four times a year and about eleven people came to it and one of the four was a man of about fifty with a waggon of his own who had been up the low country twice in eleven weeks, and on the third market after the warm week he said in front of nine people that there was a cart road at the top of the pass and about nine days on it and a county road at the bottom of it, and that was all he said, and the county that keeps the road at the bottom of it has never been asked a question by anybody in this valley in nine generations and does not know this valley is on a map.

About two hundred people heard it in about a week and nobody has a figure for the number that are going and there is not going to be one, and a woman of about thirty took a slate out from round her neck and said a figure out loud, and a man of fifty-eight said that the ninth spread of the only roll in this valley has about two hundred of the ninth generation in it, and the two figures were not the same, and neither of them was struck out and neither of them was offered up to be corrected, and there is nowhere in nine miles that either of them can be put.
