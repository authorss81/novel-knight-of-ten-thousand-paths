# Chapter 0195

**The warm week came eleven days after the fourth day of the four days and it lasted nine days, and on the fourth morning of it sixteen men and about thirty more came down to a hole in a bank at the ford at the fourth hour with two bars and a rope and a bucket, and they took a course up off a hole in about two hours, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was let into it because he is the only man in nine miles who can say whether a bank is square and there is no instrument in this valley that can.**

He had been standing on the top of that bank for four days with a barrow and a rope and there had been no place on the ground where a man of thirty-two could be told to stand, and on the fourth morning of the warm week a man of thirty-four came up the bank with a bar in his hand and said three sentences to him and they took about eleven seconds.

“You can tell whether a bank is square. I cannot. I have got four men here who cannot and one of them is fifty-one and the other three are under thirty and have all been wrong in front of me this month.

“**So you are not laying a stone. You are the man who says whether it is square, and you will say it about four times a day, and I will move whatever you tell me to move, and I am not going to ask you whether you are sure, because if you are not sure I want to know that and not have it kept.**”

He said yes in about four seconds and did not argue and has not argued about it since.

**And the reason he is in the book for the four days and the two hours is a rope and four sentences and none of the four sentences is the reason, and this is the second half of the thing that was done in the fourth week of the fourth cold, and the two halves are in the mouths of eleven people in this valley and not one of those eleven has the other half.**

---

The course came up in about two hours. It is nine inches and it is a whole course and it comes up the way a course comes up, all of it at once, with a bar under it and four men on the bar, and it is the second time in this valley in a hundred and forty years that a course has been taken off anything, and the first was nine stones off a coping on the fourth day of the fourth cold in front of about forty people, and nobody rubbed that out and nobody has ever found where the two of them are going to end up.

The four of them that could not say whether a bank was square said it four times a day. He said it in the morning and it was not square and nine barrows of stone came out of the top of it. He said it at the second hour and it was square and they left it. He said it at the fifth hour and it was not square on the low side and a man of thirty-four came and stood there for a minute and a half and then moved a stone himself, which is the only stone that man of thirty-four has moved in a face in four days and the second one in a month, and the man of fifty-one said something about it that four people heard and the man of thirty-four did not ask what.

And on the second morning he said it four times and the fourth time he did not say anything at all, and the man of thirty-four stopped the work for a minute and waited, and the man of thirty-two with a limp looked at him and said, “It is square and it will not stay square, and I have not got an instrument for that and I am telling you the two things separately because the second one is mine and the first one is everybody's.”

“Then we fill it on the low side first and leave the high side loose,” said Wystan Roole, “and the barrow run goes over the top of it either way, and if the high side goes in the warm week I will be here and I will know it because I will be standing on it. Put the loose in the middle and not on the low side, and put the biggest stuff on the top, and I have got no reason for that and I am not going to give you one.”

---

**And the barrow run went over the top of it the whole time, and the road was open the whole time, and about eleven people used it while it was being made, and the finding of that morning is the whole of that week's work and it is not a finding anybody made on purpose.**

A barrow is a barrow and a woman of the Low Holm has a yoke on her shoulders and a pail in each hand and has crossed the run four times a day since the first cold. She went over the works on the first morning at about the fifth hour, with the barrow run open on one side because a man of thirty-four had put nine barrows of stone on the other side of it and had left four feet of width, and she went over in the four feet, and she went over again an hour later, and again in the afternoon, and eleven people in that valley counted the four times and as many again did not, and she said nothing about it to anybody and has not since.

“A road that is being made and a road that is open are two different things and most of this valley has only ever had the second of them,” said a man of about twenty-six of the ninth generation, who farms one of the eleven steadings, and who is the ninth generation's own answer to a question about a rafter that a woman of sixty-six at four looms answered in a cold in the second month of this valley.

“They are the same thing while there is four feet of width left,” said Wystan Roole. “And there is going to be four feet of width left every day I do this, and if there is not then stop me. That is the whole argument and I have had it with nine people in four days and I have won it with a woman with a yoke and a barrow and I did not know I was having it.”

**And on the second morning a man of thirty-two with a limp counted a barrow run out loud in a yard in a county's way and got four words into the fourth barrow and stopped, and about thirty people heard him stop, and he has not counted anything out loud anywhere in this valley since and has said why once.**

The word is load points. It is not a barrow and not a shovel and it is not a figure of anything a person can see, and it is what a deck takes before anybody is allowed to stand on it, and it is in a guild's book in a county four hundred miles off, and a man who wrote it in wrong was a man who had put a span over a deck and got a figure he could not check. He said it out loud in a yard in the third week of the fourth cold and a man of thirty-four stopped what he was doing and made him say what it was, and told a yard of about thirty men under the cross that they were not going to learn a figure they could not check, and he learned the valley's number instead, which is a barrow, a heap, a shovel, and a man who says it out loud in a voice the whole yard can hear.

On the second morning of the warm week he got four words into the fourth barrow. He said the figure the way a man says a figure he has said about four hundred times, and then he stopped in the middle of the yard with his hand on the barrow and about thirty people stopped with him.

“Say it,” said the man of thirty-four.

“No.”

“**Say it or do not begin it. There is no third thing a man of thirty-two can do in my yard and there has not been one since the third week of the fourth cold.**”

He said it. It was a figure nobody in the yard could check, and it was about a fifth lower than the figure of barrows, and it was the right one, and a man of fifty-one in that yard had said in the third week of the fourth cold that the hearting is what is going to be under a thaw and that the right figure is lower than the figure of barrows by about a fifth, and nobody in the yard joined those two things except the man of thirty-two and a man of fifty-eight who was standing on the outside stair of the room over the cross about four feet away with a piece of rag paper in his hand.

“Then that is the number,” said a man of about twenty-six of the ninth generation. “Say it that way and I will call it.”

“No,” said the man of thirty-two. “I have got a figure and I have got yours and I have not got a way of putting the two of them side by side in this valley, and the day somebody here can check my figure against yours is the day my figure is worth anything, and there is not one person in nine miles who could do it and I have looked.

“**So I am not going to say it again, and I want the yard to have it in a voice and not out of a man, and that is your number and not mine, and the reason I am not going to use mine is that a man of thirty-four told me once that about thirty men under a cross are not going to learn a figure they cannot check, and he was right, and it took me two years to find out he was right and I have had it in four days.**”

Nobody argued with him. A man of thirty-one of the ninth generation said he thought a man who could give a figure ought to give it, and the man of thirty-two said that he could and that was all the trouble, and four people in that yard have said since that the second half of that sentence is the reason this valley did not have a number in it, and one of those nine is a woman of about thirty-five who said it in a kitchen and was told by her own brother that she was making a thing of a man talking, and she said she knew and that she was making a thing of a man talking.

**And that is everything a man off a road did in a valley of nine generations of a craft, in nine weeks of work and four days of digging and four days of filling, and nobody in that valley can say when he stopped, and the one thing he is certain of is that he stopped.**

---

**And on the third morning of the warm week the sixteen of them stopped at the top of the bank and looked at four hundred yards of made ground that had been the best ground anybody in this valley had ever seen and had been nothing but a barrow run once a day, and a man of thirty-four said the thing that has been in his mouth for two winters and got it out of it in nine sentences.**

“About two hundred people in this valley are going to want to be somewhere, and I have been wrong about that for nine years and I have said so out loud on a bank in the third month of this valley and I have not been wrong about anything else in the nine years of it, and the reason I was wrong is that nobody in this valley has ever wanted to be anywhere, and that is not a reason. That is a habit, and it is a habit that is nine generations old, and the day it stopped being a habit was the day a man of thirty-two stood at the bottom of a hole with a rope in his hand.

“**So I am going to say it and I am saying it on a bank in the open: there is going to be a road out of this valley. Not a road to the ford. A road, up this bank and off the top of it and away, and it is not going to be four hundred yards and it is not going to be for a barrow, and I have been refused twice for it since I was twenty-five and I am not asking anybody for anything this week and I am telling you it is going to happen.**”

Nobody said anything for a minute. Nine people in that bank had heard him refuse his own request twice and could remember both times and about eleven could remember one.

“We are not building it this warm week,” he said. “We are filling a hole. The road is for after the hole, and the road is for the sixteen and about thirty under the cross and nobody else, and it is going to be built out of what comes out of this bank and out of the wall that is coming down two steadings up, and there is no money in this valley and there is not going to be any and I am not going to put a figure on the end of a trestle in chalk.”

“Why not,” said a man of about thirty-one of the ninth generation, from nine feet above him.

“**Because the trestle falls down and the chalk is off it inside a year and I will be here.** I have been here since I was twenty-five. That is the first time in nine years that I have been able to say a figure of mine out loud and put nothing against it, and I would have said the other thing in the second month of this valley and I did not and a stranger wrote it in a book that stays in a coat, and that is the whole difference between this valley and a county four hundred miles off, and I have had nine years to work out and eleven people in this valley know it.”

---

**And the wages of thirty-one days went on a page in a book that stays in a coat, and the addition was done on the page, because his hand does that, and the man of fifty-eight put the same figure in the ninth spread of the only roll in this valley in a hand that varies, and the reason he was allowed to is a rule that is a hundred and forty years old and is at the head of the first spread.**

*Sixteen men, thirty-one days, at three pence the day.* 3, sixteen times over, is 48. 48, thirty-one times over, is **1,488**.

*Two barrows of salt at fourpence.* 4, twice over, is **eight**.

*1,488 and eight is 1,496 pence, which is thirty-one marks and eightpence.*

That is the wages of sixteen men in a county four hundred miles off where a day of an unskilled man is three pence, and it is in a county's money and there is no coin in this valley and there has not been any in nine generations, and the sixteen men are not on the list and neither is he, because there is no money in this valley to put either of them on a list with, and the thirty-one days were eaten out of eleven kitchens and the standing of a mill that has not ground since the first cold and a quantity that four households call a root and the rest of this valley calls a word they do not spell, and not one household was asked twice.

The two are not added. There is no way on this earth to add them and a person who tried would be doing arithmetic on a thing to make it go away.

And it went in the book, and the man of fifty-eight said why, and he said it to eleven people in a room over a byre and it went in four kitchens.

“**A figure of people goes in this book. A figure of money does not, and a figure of days does not, and I have had three figures in a hundred and forty years and the three of them are about four hundred, and about a hundred and eighty-nine, and nine. I have entered a fourth and it is the first one I have entered in nineteen years that is not a figure of people, and I have entered it because a man of thirty-two has written in his own book that a figure that a man off a road leaves in a valley is a thing that means nothing, and he is right, and I have nineteen years of a book and no other room.**”

Nobody improved on it. The clerk of about sixty, who had come down four steps to ask, wrote the figure in the minute of a valley in a room over a mill and did not ask what it was a figure of, and the man of fifty-eight said that was the correct thing to do and that the clerk had been doing it for twenty-two years.

---

**And the man who makes nails was on the barrow run for the four days of the four days and for the four days of the warm week, and he did not look out past the top of the bank once, and about four people noticed and as many again did not, and the man of thirty-four did not say one word about it to him and has not.**

He had said on the sixty-third day of the nine weeks that he would put him back on it in the warm week, and he had said it in a yard in front of sixteen men, and he had not put him on it for one day of the four days of the four days and had put him on it for the rest, and the reason is in a yard and it took nine sentences and it is not about nails.

“**I did not take you off that run because I do not trust you with it. I took you off it because I had built the only ground in this valley a man can stand on and look out of, and in four months there is going to be a queue for it, and I was not going to have the man at the front of the queue working it for me in a cold while I have sixteen men who want the work.** It was in the fourth week of the fourth cold and it is the same reason now and it will be the same reason in four months and I am not going to say it to you again.”

The man of about thirty-one said, “He is going to be at the front of the queue.”

“He is,” said Wystan Roole. “And he has a wife and a mother-in-law of sixty and he has wanted to go out of this valley for nine years and he is one of three that said no at nine hundred doors in the second month of the second cold, and the first family out of this valley is going to be a family with a reason and a place to go, and he is the one of the three with a place to go.

“**And I would rather he went third than first, and I have known that since the second month of this valley and I have not said it to him, and I am not going to say it to him, and the reason I am not is that the day I say it to him it is a thing I have put in him and not a thing he has done.**”
