# Chapter 0197

**About two hundred people in a valley of about four hundred people have begun to find out where the pass was, and the sixteen men and about thirty under the cross have been putting a road up the bank and off the top of it since the fourth week of the twelve weeks, and on the eleventh day of it a woman of forty-one came down off the top of the pass with a barrow, asked the man of thirty-four for nothing at all, and told him what she wanted, and she told the bridgekeeper and not the stranger.**

The road is a barrow road. Four hundred yards was a made ground and a made ground is for a barrow once a day, and what the sixteen put up the bank in the four weeks after the asking is nine hundred yards of the same thing with a crown on it and a ditch on the low side where a ditch can be got, and it is for a barrow and a cart and nothing heavier, and there is a turning place at the top of it that is about forty feet across and it is the first turning place in nine generations and there is no name for it.

Nobody can say who made it. That is not a mystery and it is not a modesty and it is not anybody's arrangement.

**It is because the man of thirty-four refused it, three times, in three yards, in front of eleven people each time, and the three refusals are the only reason it is a road and not a thing with a man's name on it, and he has said so out loud once and will not say it again.**

“I will not have it said. I have been asked three times in four weeks by three people who wanted to say it and I have said no three times, and the third time a woman of about thirty-five asked me in front of a waggon and I said no to her in four seconds and she called me a fool in front of eleven people and she was not wrong.

“**A road in this valley is made by sixteen men and about thirty under the cross and by nobody else, and there is no money in this valley and there are no wages and there is a chalk mark on a bench that says three days with a bar through the four, and that is the whole record. And the day there is a man in this valley whose name goes on a road it is a road that belongs to him, and in a few years it will be a thing people say a man made, and they will say it at a table with four people at it.**”

---

The woman of forty-one came down off the top of the pass on the eleventh day with a barrow and a trough-hook and nine days of nothing to do with the barrow, and she has kept the top of that pass for about two years and before that she kept a road at another end of another country four hundred miles off, and she is the granddaughter of the woman of about forty-one who keeps that other end, and she is not the same person, and the two of them have never met, and the road at that other end has been shut in four lines of writing since before the girl of the barrow was born and nobody in this valley has ever read those four lines and nothing in the twelve weeks of the four days and the eleven and the four has opened it.

She had not been asked about the road in about two years and she had not asked either, and the reason she came down was not the road.

“I have kept the top of this pass for two years and nobody has come up it, and there is a trough and a barrow and four of the ninth go up it twice a year with beasts and come back, and that is all the trade there is. And I have not asked anybody for anything in two years and nobody has asked me for anything, and I have wanted to be asked about the road for two years and I have not asked, and I have worked out in the last four weeks that those two things are the same arrangement and that it took nine generations to make it and about four hundred years to make the other one and both of them are in this valley at the same time on the same bank.

“**So I am going to say the one thing I have come down for, and I am going to say it to you and not to him.**”

She looked at the man of thirty-four and did not look at the man with the limp, and eleven people on the bank noticed that and as many again did not.

“I want the road made. And I want it made so that in a generation there is not one person in this valley who can tell you who made it. Not because it is anybody's business. **Because a road that a man made is a road that a man can be asked about it, and there is nobody in nine miles to ask, and the day there is, that man is a thing that stands in a gap in a fence, and I have watched two of those in my life and both of them were men who were good with their hands.**”

And the man of thirty-four said, “That is the second half of a thing I have been saying out loud for two winters and I did not know anybody else in the world was saying it, and I have been saying it badly and in the wrong order.”

“No,” she said. “You have been saying it about a mark in a stone. I am saying it about a road, and the road is nine hundred yards long and it is going to be used and a mark in a stone is not, and I would rather be wrong on the right thing than right on the wrong one.”

---

**And the valley's memory of him is not a fond one, and it was said out loud in the open at about the fourth hour of the ninth day of the road, in front of eleven people, by a man of about twenty-eight of the ninth generation who farms one of the eleven steadings and who had a roof put in on the sixth of the eleven weeks and has not had a floor since.**

He said it to a child. The child was the girl of the Low Holm, who is of the ninth generation, and about four other children were on the bank, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was eleven feet off with a barrow and heard all of it.

“That is the man that took the winter out,” he said. “And there are nine roofs open in this valley and there were nine roofs open before he came and there are nine roofs open now, and four of them came in in the eleven weeks, and the mill went, and the ground he made with sixteen of us went to a slope in nine days, and I am not saying a word against him and my roof is on and it is on because of the sixteen and I know that.

“**And my daughter is nine and she will be told that a man came and the winter went, and she is not going to be told what it cost, because there is nowhere in nine miles to tell her, and in a generation there will be a woman in this valley who will say that the man came and the winter went and nothing else, and she will not be wrong, because the other half is not hers and it is not anybody's.**”

Nobody said anything about it. The man of thirty-four was not there and had not been asked and the man of fifty-eight was not there either, and the girl of the Low Holm put her hand flat on the top of a barrow and then took it off, and as many again in that bank have said since that they were at the fourth hour of the ninth day and heard it, and the man of about twenty-eight has not said it again and does not know that four kitchens have it and one of the four is a house a mile and a half up the bank where a man of fifty-eight lives with nine spreads in a room over a byre.

**And nobody in that valley has called him anything, and a man of fifty-eight has said out loud in a room that a sentence in this valley about a man off a road is the second one in two years, and that he does not know what to do with two of anything.**

---

**And the man of fifty-five came up the new road on the eighth day of the twelve with eleven people behind him because he had come, and he stood at the top of the turning place and did not say anything about the road, and he said one thing, and then he stood there and let eleven people wait.**

“The promise was nobody's to hold,” he said. “I have said that in this valley for thirty years and about four people ever heard me, and I said it in a room of nine in the fourth week of the fourth cold and I voted against keeping the winter and I lost eight to one and I have not said one word about that since and I am not going to.

“**And there is nothing to hold. It went with the rest of it. And I have been a man whose want was to be relieved of a thing and I have been relieved of it by a man of thirty-two who I have spoken to about four times, and I have not been asked to pick anything up, and I am not going to be, and I would like that in the minute and there is no column for it.**”

The clerk of about sixty was not there. The man of fifty-eight was not there. About eleven people were on that turning place and nine of them have said since that a man of fifty-five said a true thing about himself out loud in the open and that nobody wrote it down, and the man of thirty-four said afterwards that the reason nobody wrote it down is that there is nobody in nine miles to write it down, and that is not a tragedy and it is the arrangement, and that if a man of fifty-five had stood in the aisle instead of on a turning place there would have been a stroke cut into the end of the back bench before the fourth hour and there was not, and that this is the fourth generation of this valley that has gone three generations without using that aisle and that the number cut into the end of that bench has not moved in the whole time the man of fifty-eight can remember and nobody has ever been struck off one of the twenty-nine.

---

**And the market in the fourth week of the twelve was the largest anybody in this valley has seen and there is no figure for it and there is not going to be one, and a man of fifty-eight stood at the edge of it for about an hour and a half and counted nothing.**

That is the finding of the twelve weeks and it is the reason a man of fifty-eight has a book with three figures at the foot of the ninth spread in it and a figure of money in a fourth column and no figure of anything else.

“The last market before the warm week had about eleven people at it,” he said, to a man of thirty-four, in a yard, on the fourth morning of the road. “This one had everybody who is going and a good half of everybody who is not, and I stood at the edge of it for an hour and a half with a piece of rag paper in my hand and I did not write one figure down.

“**And I want you to understand what that is, because I have been a man with a book for nineteen years and I have never once not written something down. There is no column in that book for how many people went to a market and did not come back, and there is no column for how many of them are going, and a man who puts a figure on a piece of rag paper in a yard is a man who has made a column whether he means to or not, and I have not made one and I am not going to.**”

Two hundred is a figure the valley has and is not a figure the valley made, and the man of thirty-four did not make it and did not want it, and the ninth spread has about two hundred of the ninth generation in it, and a woman of about thirty has a figure she has carried on a slate for eleven years, and the two figures are not the same, and neither of them has been struck out.

**And the first three families to go out of this valley were the three that said no, and nobody in this valley arranged that and nobody in this valley is going to claim it.**

The man who makes nails went on the fourth day of the next cold with his wife and his mother-in-law of sixty and about four barrows of nails, and he is the first customer a nail has ever had in this valley and he is the reason a stand has a nail in the coping, and he had wanted to go out of this valley for nine years and had said so at a market in front of eleven people in the second month of the second cold, and four hundred people in a valley of about four hundred people have heard that sentence and can say when they heard it, and the sentence went into a minute nobody can be served.

The man of about fifty went on the sixth day with a dog and one barrow and no family, and his grandmother stood in the aisle over the cross when the benches were put in and it took nine minutes and it was the worst nine minutes of her life, and he is the man who put a hurdle across a step in a doorway in the ninth week of the first cold and said no in about four seconds, and nobody in that valley has ever had that sentence improved on and eleven people have it word for word.

The man of about forty went on the ninth day with his mother and a brother of twenty-six and a sister of nineteen, and he is the man who gave his reason at a door in the second month of the second cold before the six things had been finished, and it was one sentence, and it is the sentence about not being a man who changes his mind for a piece of paper, and eleven people in this valley have it word for word and nobody has improved on it.

**Those are nine people and one dog and they are three households and the figure of nine that a man of thirty-four made on a step in the cold in about a minute with a rope-belt on is one higher than the number of people in them, and the ninth line in the second column of the ninth spread of the only roll in this valley is empty, and it is going to be empty in nine years, and the man of fifty-eight has not ruled it across and is not going to.**

And the first family out was a family with a reason and a place to go, and the man of thirty-four had said that in a yard in the fourth week of the fourth cold, and he said afterwards that he had not arranged it and would have given anything in the wages of the whole time he does not get to have arranged it, and that he had put the man of nails off a barrow run for fifty-nine days for exactly the opposite reason and had not told him, and that the man of nails came back to the run on the fourth day of the four days of the four days and worked it for four more days and never once looked out past the top of the bank, and that about four people noticed and as many again did not.

---

And on the ninth day of the twelfth week the man of fifty-eight went down four steps to the room about four feet by six with a lamp in it and put the ninth spread of the only roll in this valley on the boards and looked at the second column, which is a hundred and forty years old in the ruling of it and eleven days old in the writing, and there are eight names in it in eight hands and the ninth line is empty.

He did not rule it across. He did not write a figure in the day column and he did not take a leaf out, and about four people in that room had not been in it in nine weeks and none of them said anything, and a man of fifty-eight said one thing to the book and not to them.

“A name in a book of this valley goes in because the person did it themselves, and there are eight of them and they are in here, and the ninth line is empty and it is going to be empty, and a man of fifty-eight is not going to be the man who fills it and a man of thirty-four is not going to be asked.”

And on the ninth day of the twelfth week about two hundred people in this valley who had not gone were still in it, and the barrow run was open over a hole that had been filled and keyed, and the four hundred yards of made ground was a slope, and a barrow went up nine hundred yards of new road to a turning place forty feet across that had no name and came back down, and the man of thirty-four stood at the low end of a cutting with his hands behind his back and watched it come down, and there is not a cheer in this valley in nine generations and there was not one that day either, and eleven people in nine miles have said out loud that it is the first time anybody has gone out of this valley and come back, and one of the eleven said the wrong half of it, and nobody corrected her.
