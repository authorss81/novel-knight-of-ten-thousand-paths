# Chapter 0154

The fourth of Goatmonth, YR 313, was a Tuesday and it was not a market day, and one bag came up the two flights with two things in it that nobody in this room expected to arrive together, and the second of them came out of the bag first because it is the smaller of the two and it was on top.

A sheet of writing paper, folded in two, with a county on the outside of it and a seal, and no address, because a returnable paper is not addressed to a person.

Alish Renn took the seal off and looked at the head of it and then put it down flat on the table with both hands, the way you put down something hot, and said the name of the office on it out loud, and the room heard it.

*Office of Provincial Registration. Returnable. Count of continuing obligations in respect of a district. To be returned by the bench or the body corporate of the district. Space for a figure.*

There is no space for a name. Not a small one and not a ruled one. The five columns of that series are *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper*, *How often*, and this paper has six, and the sixth is the one at the foot, and the sixth is a line with no head to it at all, and the line is for a figure.

The date on it is returnable on or before a day in the next month of this year, and the day is a Monday, and the day was named in advance by somebody two hundred miles off who has never heard of this county.

Mara Vey read the whole of it twice and then went and got the wrapper it came in, because the answer to a paper is very often on the thing it came in.

The answer to a paper is on the reverse of its own sheet, in the office's own hand, in a smaller type, and it is the same office's hand that is on the reverse of every form of that series, and nobody in this room has ever read it out loud and one of them has read it and knows what it is about.

*For the purpose of this return, a continuing obligation in respect of a district is held by a person who has gone out of that district and whose name is entered against that district in any record of the district.*

Nine words. It has been printed at the foot of that form since the first of a month in a year two years before the one this volume is in, and it is in a smaller type than the rest of the sheet, and a man with a barrow of lime asked a woman in a lane on Friday what was written on the back of one of these and she said she could not say that here, and she was not hiding it, and there is a carter in this county who does not know it.

“You may not say what is on the back of it in a room either,” said Alish Renn, from the window, without turning round. “That is not me being careful. That is that it is a thing that has to be cited in a return and refused about in a gateway and put in no mouth in a room, and those are the three places it can go and this is not one of them.”

“Nobody has said a word about it in a room,” said the keeper of records, “and I am going to say nine words about it in a room, and I am going to say them in a return, and I would like the room to notice the order those two things happened in.”

---

**And the second thing out of the bag was a letter from a valley four hundred miles off that is in nobody's book in this county, and it is four hundred miles off and the letter says so, and it came out of the same bag as a Crown instrument from an office that has a table in it.**

Nobody in the room read it and nobody in the room was going to read it, because a letter with a county on the outside of it that is not this county is not a letter this office opens, and the keeper of records looked at the cover and then looked at the junior clerk and then did the thing that is the whole of her office.

“Then it is for the county and I am not going to open it because the county is not this office and the county is not on this bench and the county has a council that meets,” she said, “and a thing that is not opened is not a record.”

“It is a letter about a book,” said Rowan Petch, who had put the bag down and had not gone.

“Then it is a letter about a book that the county has a council about, and the council meets on a Saturday, and the Saturday is in four days, and I will hand it to the clerk of the county council myself and write down the hour that I did it, and I would like that in the minute, and I would like somebody else in this room to write down that I said it.”

Aren Kest was in the room. He has been in that room about nine times in four years and a half and this is the fifth time in a month, and he came up the two flights at about the seventh hour with a stick and a tool bag and had been in the room about a minute when the letter came out of the bag.

“You could read a hand,” said the keeper of records, to him, and then stopped, and did not finish the sentence, and the sentence was not finished because of what is in the letter and not because of anything in the room.

---

**And Lysa Fenn beat it in nine minutes, in a room over a weigh-house, on a Tuesday, with the form's own construction, and she is frightened of her own answer and said so out loud in front of nine people.**

She came up the two flights with a wet coat because it had begun to rain on the stair and she would not give a bag to a boy, and she did not want a chair, and she had read the thing in a filing room in the county town four days earlier, which is what a county of nine thousand does with a paper that cannot be served, which is that it gets a copy of it from the man who copied it.

“If a body corporate enters that company,” she had said about a different thing in a different month, and the principle is the same and it has never once been improved on.

“A count of persons is a return of a figure,” she said. “A figure is not a person. No person is in default. A thing against which nobody is in default is not refused and is not enforced, and it is therefore spent the moment it is answered, and it requires no further returnable date, and there is nobody in this kingdom who can appeal it because there is nothing in it to appeal.”

“That is your ninth of Greenmonth,” said the keeper of records. “And your twenty-first of Wolfmonth. And it is going to be your answer to this one as well and I would like you to hear yourself say it before you do.”

“I have heard it,” said Lysa Fenn. “And I am going to say the rest of it and this is the part I do not like.

“The discovery this time is not that nobody is in default. It is that **the paper is not returnable by this office at all.** A count is returnable by the bench or the body corporate of a district. This is a district with a bench in it and I am its magistrate and the bench is a bench and it has a clerk. And the paper has come to this room. And this office is not that bench, this office is a filing office with a door and a number, and a door is not a district, and a number is not a bench, and a returnable paper with no party in it expires, and this is the third time in four years I have made that discovery and the second time I have made it about this office.

“In nine months somebody in the county town will write to somebody in this county and say that the Bellweather Claims Archive, a body corporate, can be sued and can be shut and can be in default, and the letter will be right, and I will have to answer it, and the answer will be that this office expires instruments, and everybody will be very pleased, and in about four years a body corporate with a door will be able to expire anything at all by finding something about it, and it will be me that handed them the method, because the method is nine words long and it is mine.”

She wrote the answer in the ordinary form on the reverse of the return, with the hour, and the return went out with a space for a figure and nothing in it, and no name anywhere on the paper, in any hand, of any kind.

“Then we are at nothing,” said Alish Renn. “Again.”

“We are at nothing twice in a year,” said the keeper of records, “and nothing is a figure and I cannot put it on a line, and I have four years and a half of this office to tell you that I have got very good at nothing and that it has never once been the same as a zero, and a zero is a figure and a figure is what that office wants.”

---

**And the junior clerk read the letter, because the county council is not meeting for four days and the county council has no clerk on a Tuesday, and a bag is not going to wait four days in a room with a door on a lane.**

Alish Renn read it out. She read the whole of it, twice, because she is the second copyist of this office and the first thing the second copyist of this office is for is to know what is in the bag before anybody acts on it.

It is from a man of fifty-eight. It is from a valley four hundred miles off, and the valley is not on any map in this kingdom and is not in the county's book, and the letter says four hundred miles in the first line, and the reason it says it in the first line is that the man has been asked how far away it is four times in his life and has given four answers.

*Keeper of records at Merefen in the north, or the clerk of the press, or whoever opens the bag.*

*I am fifty-eight and I keep the winter book of a valley four hundred miles from you, and I say four hundred miles because a man in a county that is not on any map has been asked how far away it is four times in his life and has given four answers, and this one is the best of them.*

*The book has been in my keeping nineteen years. It was in my father's and it was in his father's, and it is a roll of who has gone out of this valley and who has come back to it and who was called a carrier for going. It is nine generations deep and it is the only roll in this valley, and there is no writ in this valley and no bell in it and no office, and a valley that expects people to leave builds a road, and this valley has not built a road, and the reason it has not built a road is on the roll.*

*I have wanted it read by somebody who was not me for six years. I have had two readings. The first was a man who came through with a cart in the third year and read the first four generations in an evening and got the fifth one wrong in a way that has been in a copy ever since. The second was a woman who read it over two nights and told me on the second morning that she had been wrong about the first, and I have never stopped hearing that sentence.*

*I am not asking for anybody to come. I am asking for a man who can read a hand. There is one in your county and he is not the man I am asking for, and I know that before the ink is dry, and I am writing it in this letter because a man who is not asked does not come, and a man who is asked to cross a thing will cross it, and I have read a great many other people's books and I have never once wanted a man to cross anything on my account.*

*If a hand in that book is one you can read, I would like to know what it says, and I would like to know it in this valley and in a room, and I would like the ninth generation told what it signed, and I would like to be in the room for that, and I am not going to be able to arrange it, because a man of fifty-eight who has kept a book for nineteen years is a man this valley will not stop asking to read out of.*

*Nobody has asked me why I want it read. That is the first thing that is wrong, and it has been wrong for six years, and I have not been able to make it right by wanting it less.*

*The carriage is not at your expense and I will not pay it, because I do not know how to pay a person and a valley has no coin, and a thing that cannot be paid is a thing that can be refused, and I would rather have a refusal I can enter.*

*I am Iven Sarr, and that is my own name, and it is in the book in my own hand, and I have never once had to explain it to anybody.*

The room was quiet for a little while after that, and the thing the room was quiet about was not the four hundred miles and was not the book.

“He says there is one in this county who can read a hand,” said Sarn Oyelaran, “and he says it in a letter to a filing office, and he is asking that keeper to go and read it, and she is the one person in this room who can, and it is three days’ post each way and about nine weeks of winter on the far end, and the county cannot afford a carriage and the valley will not pay one.”

“I am not going,” said the keeper of records.

“You would be back inside a season.”

**“A keeper does not travel.”**

Four words, and then she did not say anything else for about a minute, and when she did speak again it was in the other voice, the one that is not for facts, and what she said was not the reason.

“The ninth of Rainmonth is a Tuesday and it is at the second hour and it is a hearing this office entered in its own resolution against no person, for a delegation that is not to be sent for, and it stands whether anybody comes or not, and the room will be shut, and I will be the only person in it, because that is what a hearing against nobody looks like in practice and I have known that since the twentieth of Hearthmonth and I have not been able to put it down in a way that does not sound like a complaint.”

“You could go to the valley and come back and be here for it,” said Sarn Oyelaran.

“No,” she said, “because I would be the woman who went, and the minute would say she went and was away for a season with no date of return in it, and in about four years a person would read that minute and see a keeper of records who left this office and came back, and the only question anybody would have about it is what she was doing.”

Then she wrote four lines in the day-book, with the date and the hour and the claim number against the office and nothing against any person, and the four lines say that a letter has been held entire, that it is from a county that is in nobody's book in this one, that it is not opened further, and that the hour it was read to the room is on it. And then she said the thing that was going to be the standing instruction of the whole of the next nine months in this office, and she said it to the room and not to a book, because a keeper who enters a thing she intends to do has made a plan and a plan in a day-book is a thing other men find and act on.

“The letter is not going to be answered this week,” she said, “and the reason is not a figure and it is four words and they are *it is not ours*.”

And then the two of them went down to the press together, and Alish Renn ruled a line under the entry in the ordinary way and stopped the ruling two inches short of the width of the page, and the two inches is at the right-hand edge of the page and nothing is ruled in it, and there is nothing in this office that says what is going in it.

Because a Crown instrument of the Office of Provincial Registration and a letter from a valley four hundred miles off that is in nobody's book came up the same two flights in the same bag on the same Tuesday morning, and a clerk on a Tuesday wrote at the foot of the wrapper that they had arrived together, and that they were a coincidence, and that no person in that room was to do anything with it, and that the only thing she had done about it was to rule a line and stop two inches short of the edge of a page.
