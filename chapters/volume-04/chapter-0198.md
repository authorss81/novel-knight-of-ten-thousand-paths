# Chapter 0198

**The fourteenth of Harvestmonth, YR 314, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the fourth hour, and forty men on the Sedge Reach in Merefen with stone in four carts and a road that was open, and a carter of thirty put a barrow down in the middle of the fourth bay and stood looking at the third course from the low end of it and could not account for what he was looking at.**

The Sedge Reach is four timber bays and it has been the town's crossing for longer than anybody in Merefen has kept anything, and the third of those four bays was down in the spring and the fourth was down in the summer and they are being put in in stone, eleven tons of it, in four carts, out of a quarry eleven miles up the channel, and the work goes on in daylight with the road open because the road cannot be shut. There is one lane over the first bay and it is the lane the salt comes in on, and it has been the lane for four hundred years and it is going to be the lane when this is finished.

About forty men. A mason of fifty and a half, who has been cutting stone for thirty-one years and who does the arches and does the pointing and does not do the laying. Two men of about twenty-six setting the courses, one of them from Merefen and one of them from nine miles up the channel who has a barrow of his own and is faster on it than anybody in the yard. A boy of fifteen at the bench-board with a lime tub and a rule and a bill, who copies the empty space under a number as well as the number, which is what the boy does, and who has been on this reach since the spring. A carter of thirty with a barrow and a waggon of his own at the top end. Four men with hods. A man of about nineteen who is here because his father is here and is carrying a hod badly and has not been told so by anybody except the boy.

**The stone came in four carts a day out of a quarry eleven miles up the channel, and it came one cart behind another all morning, and the carts were unloaded with a crane and a gin and four men on a rope, and eleven tons went into the ground in this reach in the spring and the summer and the autumn, and the pointing goes into the joints on the day after the stone and not on the day, and a joint that is pointed on the day is a joint that is pointed twice.**

And there is one more thing about that yard on that morning that four men have said since and that the minute of the bench of nine does not hold, because a minute cannot hold it and has never been asked to.

A reach is not the same thing twice. A man who has stood on a deck every day for eleven days knows what the fourth bay is doing, and the fourth bay is doing two things at once, and on the fourteenth of Harvestmonth at about the fifth hour both of them stopped for about four minutes and started again, and nobody in the yard said anything about it and nine men have said since that they could feel it and four have said that they could not and that they had heard it from the others.

**And the tide was under the wharf at about the fourth hour and about four hundred yards downstream from the reach,** and the wharf was taking a waggon of salt off a lighter, and the reach and the wharf are four hundred yards apart in a town nine miles long and the whole town can hear a cart on either of them, and nobody in Merefen has ever had to be told what is happening on the reach because it is happening in the street.
 Two men of about twenty-six setting the courses. A boy of fifteen at the bench-board with a lime tub and a rule. A carter of about thirty with a barrow and a waggon of his own at the top end.

**And a man of forty-seven on the roll of the bridge guild with a vote and not an office, who has not used the vote since he was elected and did not use it then, and who had been at the deck of the fourth bay for about eleven days and had a barrow on it about forty times a day.**

The line was in the third course from the low end of the fourth bay. It was about nine inches long. It was in the stone and not across the joint and not in the joint and not under it. It was not a crack, because a crack in a new course in a wet fortnight is a crack you can get a nail in, and this could not be got at with anything at all and two men tried it and gave up. It did not move. Two men put their thumbs on the two ends of it and pushed and it did not move.

Nobody had been on that stone when it was laid except the two men of about twenty-six, and the mason, and a boy with a lime tub, and the four of them had been in the same place at the same time for eleven days and there was no way on this earth to find out which of the four of them had done it, and none of the four of them said that they had.

---

**And the carter told about four men and did not tell the rest of the yard, and the four men told the man of forty-seven, and the man of forty-seven came and looked at it and did not put a rule on it.**

He is a road warden of this county's bridge guild. He is on the roll. He has a vote and it is not an office, he does not hold the crossing, he does not hold the Old Toll four hundred yards up the road, he does not hold the reach, and he has not voted since the day he was elected and has not said anything about that in this hall or any other and is not going to.

He put his hand flat on the stone on either side of the line, the way a man puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and the stone was the same temperature on both sides of it, and he took his hand off and stood there for a while.

“Is it a crack.”

“No,” said the carter. “I have put a nail in a crack in the coping of a wharf in Hushwater and a nail in a crack goes in about a finger. I have tried a cold chisel at both ends and neither of them went in and I am not a mason and I know what a mason looks like when he is looking at a thing he does not know.”

“That is a line,” said the man of forty-seven.

And a man of about sixty came across the deck with a rule in his hand and did not put it down, and nine men within thirty feet of it stopped what they were doing, and the man of sixty is the guild's man with the levels and has kept his own book for two years and has been paid sixpence twice out of a box, and four people in that yard have said since that he did not come across for the stone and that he came across for the man of forty-seven, and the man of forty-seven has not confirmed it and has not denied it.

“Let me have that rule.”

“I do not think you are going to use it,” said the man of forty-seven.

**“I have not said what I am going to do with it. I have said that I want it.”** And then he put the rule back in the bag and said the thing that four people in that yard said afterwards was the single most useful sentence anybody had said on that reach in eleven days, and the sentence is this.

“A figure on a thing in this county goes in a book somewhere, and a book in this county with a figure in it about a bridge can be produced by anybody who has a copy of it, and I have kept my own book for two years for exactly that reason and I have been paid sixpence twice out of a box and I have not been able to make the second payment any easier by having a better rule than anybody else in this yard.

“**A thing that has been measured once gets measured again in a year, and the second one is a decline, and a decline is a survey, and a survey goes in a form.** And I have told that to one man in my life and he is not in this yard and he is not going to be told by me twice.”

Nobody on that deck improved on it and nine men within thirty feet heard it and four of them have said since that it was the first time in their working lives that anybody had given them a reason for a thing they had always done, and one of those four is the mason of fifty and a half, who has been cutting stone for thirty-one years and who said, to nobody, that he had been doing it the whole time and had never once had the words.

**And then forty people on that reach asked the same question in four different ways for about an hour, and the four who had been on that stone were asked separately, and their four accounts do not agree, and that is a fact about four men and not a mystery that anybody on that reach was going to be able to close in a day.**

The two men of about twenty-six were asked first and separately. One of them said he had laid the course on the eighth and the other said the ninth, and neither of them was carrying a paper and neither of them had ever been asked to carry one, and the mason said the eighth, and the boy of fifteen said the ninth and said that he had written the ninth on the bill and could go and get the bill, and went and got it, and the ninth was on it, and the ninth was on it in the boy's own hand and the boy's hand is a good hand for a figure of a day.

“Then somebody put a line in a stone in a wet fortnight on a Tuesday,” said a man of about twenty-four who had been hired in on the Friday and had not asked for anything else in a fortnight.

“**Then somebody did,** said the man of forty-seven, “and it was not any of those four, and I know that because I was on the low bank of this bay at the fifth hour on the ninth of Harvestmonth from about the second hour to about the fourth with a barrow and nobody came past me but the two of them and the boy, and I have not said that to anybody until this afternoon and I am not going to say it to anybody else, and I would like it noticed that I have said it to a man of nineteen who hired in on Friday and cannot get a vote on this guild whatever he does.

“That is not because I am protecting the yard. That is because there are four accounts and three of them are wrong and one of them is right and I know which one, and the one I know is mine, and a man who has a thing in him that only he can check is a man who has to say it out loud before anybody builds a thing on it, and four people in this county have never done that and two of them are dead.”

**And the course was not taken up. A boy of fifteen thinks he saw a line in a stone and a man of forty-seven has a fact about a Tuesday afternoon, and neither of those is a reason to pull a course out of a reach in a fortnight, and the mason of fifty and a half said so in about nine words and the man of forty-seven agreed with him and said that he wanted it minuted that he agreed with a mason about a stone in a guild reach.**

Nobody minuted it. There is no minute of a reach and there is no clerk on a reach and the man of forty-seven said that he had been in this trade nineteen years and had never once known a thing about a stone to be written down anywhere, and that he was not asking for it now, and that the reason he had said his piece out loud in a yard of forty men was that a mason and a carter and a boy of fifteen and two men of twenty-six were about to work over that stone for the rest of their lives and that four of them were owed a thing they had not been asked for.

**And a mark in the parapet on the upstream side of the first bay, and it is a line and a bar and a figure cut into the stone at the height of a thumb,** and it has been there since the sixth of Goatmonth, YR 313, and nobody in this county can read the figure, and nine people in this town have said out loud that it is a mason's mark, and it is not a mason's mark, and there is a girl in this town who put her hand flat on it in the summer to see whether it was warm and it was not, and she has not asked anybody about it and nobody has asked her.

That is a different thing and it happened four months ago and four hundred men have walked past it since, and a mason's mark is a thing you can read from thirty feet and this one you cannot, and a man of sixty with a level has said in a yard that he does not know what it is and is not going to, and that the two of them are not the same thing, and that a yard of forty men is a bad place to be asked a question in.

And the seam in the fourth bay is the only one of the two that was put there by a person, and four people in this town know that and one of them is a man of forty-seven on the roll with a vote and not an office, and he has not said so to anybody and has not written it down and has not used his vote about it.

**And the seam was not measured, and it was not pointed, and it was not cut out, and it was not filled, and the course it is in was bedded on a course and the course it is in is bedded on a course and the bed holds them, and the work went on over the top of it at about the fifth hour, and about forty men were on that reach in daylight with the road open and the lane over the first bay in use all day with salt on it.**

That is all of it, and it took four minutes to happen on the fourteenth of Harvestmonth, YR 314, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and there is a carter of about thirty who saw it first and a man of forty-seven who would not have it measured and a man of about sixty who would not put his rule down, and the four of them have not seen each other since and one of them is in a county four hundred miles off and none of them knows it.

---

**And the reaching of the fourth bay went on for another nine days, and the seam did not move and did not grow and did not close,** and on the twenty-third of the same month the mason of about fifty and a half had it pointed over, at the joint and not at the stone, with lime and a little of the same lime that is in the course, and the man of forty-seven stood and watched that happen and said one word about it, which was *why*, and the mason said, “Because it is eleven days old and the rain is coming and a line in a stone is not a joint and I am not going to leave a stone in a reach in a county that has no owner of it for eleven days.”

That is a trade reason and it is the reason the seam is still visible in a stone course in a bridge in Merefen and is not a figure and is not a column and is not in any book, and the man of about sixty with the levels said afterwards that a mason in a hurry is the only man in a yard who can make a thing illegible without making it wrong, and that he had been watching that reach for eleven days and had not seen anybody else do it.

**And the pointing was not finished on the day it was begun, which is the one thing about this trade that has never once been different anywhere it has ever been done, and the man who began it on the twenty-third went home at the fourth hour, and a boy of fifteen copied the empty space under a number on a bill as well as the number, which is what the boy does, and the joint was half a foot short at the end of it, and it is still a foot and a half short this day and nobody in that yard has measured it either.**

And the reach was open on the fourteenth of Harvestmonth, YR 314, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and it is open now, and forty men have been on it every working day since the spring, and the lane over the first bay had a waggon of salt on it at about the sixth hour and had a waggon of salt on it at about the sixth hour on the twenty-third and had a waggon of salt on it on the fourteenth of Fallowmonth, YR 314, a Monday, not a market day, and nobody in that town has ever asked where the salt comes from, and nobody in that town has ever been told anything about a valley nine days over a pass that a carrier of about fifty has been up twice in eleven weeks.
