# Chapter 0182

**The four days were four days of a stretch of cold that has no name in this valley, and the tenth word of the eleven was *nobody*, and a valley of about four hundred people heard all of it at about nine hundred gates in nine days and then did the only thing a valley does with a thing it has been told twice, which is to wait and see who else turns up.**

They turned up.

Not all of them. About thirty came up the bank in the three days before the fourth hour on the fourth day, and they came for different reasons and none of the reasons was the coping, and the coping was the reason all thirty of them gave.

And the first of them was the woman who had found it, and she had a day and a half of it to account for, and she accounted for it in a yard in about forty words, to four people, without being asked, and then she went and moved a pail out of the wet and did not say anything else about it.

“I found it on the second day,” she said, “and I had a yoke on and a pail in my hand and I put the pail down where I put it every day, which is on the coping, because you cannot set a pail on a sloping stone and you can set it on a flat one, and my hand went down on a line.

“And I stood in a cutting at about the ninth hour with a yoke on my shoulders and a pail at my feet and a mark under my hand, and I want to say what I did, because I have been thinking about it for a day and a half and I have not said it out loud to anybody and I have told my own house and they are not going to be asked either.

“I did not tell anybody for a day and a half. And the reason is not that I was frightened of it. **The reason is that the first thing you want to know about a mark is what it is, and there is nobody in nine miles to tell you, and so the second thing you want to know is whether anybody else has seen it, and if nobody else has seen it then you are the one who has got it, and if you tell somebody then it is a thing that two people have seen, and a thing that two people have seen is a thing there will be an account of.** And I did not want an account of it in a valley with about four hundred people in it.

“And then on the second morning I went past it again and I put my hand on it, and it was not warm, and I came up the bank and I told the man of thirty-four, and he came down and looked at it and went back up and I have not seen him say a word to anybody about it since, and he is the man I told.

“And I have not been thanked for telling him and I have not been thanked for not telling him, and I do not want to be thanked for either, and I have said four things about it in a yard and that is the whole of what I have got.”

The man of thirty-four heard all four of that and said nothing about it, and about four days later he told a man of thirty-two that it was the reason he had not slept, and he did not tell him it was the reason he had not slept, and the woman knew that he had not slept and did not say so.

The man of eighty-one came on the second of the three and stood at the top of the cutting for about twenty minutes without going down into it, and he is eighty-one and has been in this valley since before there was a stand over that beck, which there is not now either, and he asked one question and it was the question of a man who has been alive for eighty-one years in a place with no office in it.

“How many times has it been done before.”

“It has not been done before,” said Wystan Roole, from the far bank, where he had gone to look at a stone he could not get at. “It is the first time anything has been taken down in this valley in nine generations. There is nothing to have a figure about.”

“Then you are doing it in front of everybody for the first time and you will get it wrong in a way that four people in nine years will remember, and that is the correct arrangement and I would like it noticed that I have said so in about four seconds and I am not going to stand here and watch, because I have watched four of these in sixty years and I have never once watched the fifth.” He went back up the bank.

**The mill man came on the third of the three, and the mill man is about sixty-one and cannot leave the wheel, and he came because somebody had told him there were men on a bank, and he had a question about the fourth stone, which is the one off the byre coping at the third generation's house and is a hundred and forty years old and is squared on one face.**

“That stone is not ours to lay,” he said. “That stone has been in a wall at the third generation's house for a hundred and forty years and it is going back in a coping on a stand that has no name, and a hundred and forty years is a long time, and the mill has had stones off that house twice in my lifetime and neither of them went back.”

“They go in the footing at the ford,” said Wystan Roole. “Under the water. All nine of them, in the order they come off, and I have not decided that until this morning and I have just decided it, and the reason is that I am not going to break nine good stones over a chisel mark and I am not going to put nine good stones in a wall where a barrow goes over them four times a day, and I am not throwing them in the beck. In the footing, under the water, where a barrow cannot go and a nine-year-old cannot get a hand.”

“Under the water is where a thing goes that you are not going to look at again,” said the mill man.

“Yes,” said Wystan Roole. “And I have thought about that for about a day and a half and I have decided that it is the honest end and not a tidy one, and I would like a man of sixty-one to have said it out loud in front of thirty people, and I am not going to pretend to him that it is a good place for a stone. It is the place a stone goes when the valley has decided that the thing about it is not worth a wall.”

**And on the second of the four days a girl of about nine put her hand flat on the mark, and she did it the way anybody puts a hand flat on a thing to see whether it is warm, and she had not been asked to and would not have done it if she had been.**

She is nine. She has a barrow she most days does not go and get out at the ford, and she has counted four hundred and six stones in a wall in two goes round it and nine through-stones in the same wall and nine treads on an outside stair, and she has never once asked anybody what any of the numbers were for, because then she would have to be told.

Her hand went on the fourth stone about four inches in from the end and stayed there for four seconds, and there were nine people on the bank and the cutting was in daylight, and the sun in this valley does not do what sun does in a county that keeps its hours and it came over the shoulder of the bank anyway at about the ninth hour and put a hand's width of light on the face the weather gets at.

“It is not warm,” she said, to nobody, and took her hand off and went and got her barrow out at the ford.

Nobody asked her anything then and nobody asked her anything afterwards, and the man of thirty-two was standing nine feet off with a stick in his hand and did not go and ask her what her hand was doing on that stone, and he has been asked by about four people since why he did not, and the answer he gave all four of them was the same four sentences and none of them was the reason.

---

**And on the second of the four days he sat down at the top of the cutting with a book in his coat and did the thing he has done three times in this valley, which is to get to a page and not write a figure on it.**

**The page is the back of the book and there is a reason written on it in his own hand about why he is not going to write a drift figure, and he wrote the reason the first time and it is about four lines long and it is that he has got a number and no frame, and the second time he added nothing to it, and the third time he left the page alone afterwards for the first time in this case and went and did something else.**

The two numbers are still on the page above the reason and they have not moved. Three weeks and two sleeps at the fourth week. The valley's own count, which is a week of the cold, and which nobody in this valley keeps to a day and which there is no authority in nine miles that could be asked. He has not added them together. He has not converted either of them. He has been in this valley about twenty weeks, and there is now a third thing on the same page that is not a figure either, and it is that there is a date in a county four hundred miles off that nothing he is carrying has a road to, and he is not going to write it down in a valley that keeps no months, because the day he writes a date from another calendar on a page of that book is the day the book is two books.

And here is the thing about those four days that nobody in this valley can be told, because there is no instrument in four hundred miles that could carry it, and he has not told anybody and was not going to and it goes on the page because the page is the only place in this book where a thing like that is allowed to happen.

He had a place to come back to for four days. It was about four hundred miles off and about nine days on a road in a county that keeps its days, on a bridge with four timber bays and a parapet on the upstream side, and the mark in that parapet was the same mark, cut with the same chisel, line and bar and a figure, at the ninth hour of a day that county uses, in front of twenty men, and a girl of about eleven came up off the near bank with her hands full of rushes and put her right hand flat on it to see whether it was warm, and went back down the bank with the rushes.

He had made the one in the coping at the Low Holm in the third cold because that one was four hundred miles away and a man with a limp cannot get to a place in four seconds, and a place you come back to has to be a place you can stand in, and there was one thing in nine miles he could stand in and he had cut it in the third cold with sixteen men looking at it and had not told them and had not been asked.

He had not gone to the four hundred miles. He had come out of that county at the fourth hour on a day it keeps, on a sill with no glass in it, with a witness at this end who was a man of forty-seven on a bench because he was elected, and the date of the coming back is written four hundred miles away in a resolution he was in a room for, and there is no way in this valley to get to any of it that does not involve a road this valley has not got and a man in it who cannot get up a bank without a stick, and there is a pass that takes a week to the top of it, and a cart road on the other side of the pass, and about nine days on that.

He had not gone. He had about five months of a valley behind him and four days of a place to come back to, and the four days were two days gone, and he was not going to be able to go on the fourth day either, and there was nobody in the valley who knew there was a bridge with a mark on it four hundred miles off and there was nobody in it who could carry the news if there was.

He shut the book and put it back in his coat and got up the bank, and the cold was coming off the beck, and the stand was four feet above it with a stranger's mark in the coping of it, and he had not gone, and the four days were two days gone.

And then, on the fourth of the four, at about the sixth hour, the man of thirty-four came up the bank and asked him the only question he was going to be asked in nine weeks, and he asked it standing, and there were about nine people within thirty feet of it and all nine heard it.

“You have been sitting on that bank for four days,” he said. “I have watched you. I have not watched anybody for four days in nine years and I am not going to pretend that I was doing it for a reason, I was doing it because I laid a coping with a mark in it and I could not sleep.

“I am going to ask you all of it and I want a short answer and I want it now and I am asking you in front of nine people on purpose, because if I ask you in a doorway then in nine years there are two of us and one of us says we did not.”

“I have made my arrangements.”

“I do not care about your arrangements and you have not got any, and I have known that since the second day of this cold and I have not said anything about it for four days, and the reason I have not said anything is that a man who has just been told he is stuck is not a man you can give a job to, and I have sixteen men and no work and I was not going to spend that on a man's face. That is the reason and I would like it entered that I gave it before I asked.” He wiped his hands on a rope he was not using. “And I have thought about whether I am a coward about it, and I am not, because there is a thing at the bottom of this valley that is going to be taken out in the warm week, and there is a stand going in at the ford in nine weeks, and there are four hundred yards of approach to make first, and I have never in my life had a second pair of hands that could read a floor, and you showed me three faults in one in four seconds in the second month of this valley, and you have been here five months and you have had nothing to do but sit on banks.”

“About nine words,” said Aren Kest. “The mill at the bottom. And I am not the man who pays you. There is no money in this valley.”

“There is no money in this valley and there are sixteen men with nine years of a craft in them and about thirty more under the cross, and I have never once in nine years been able to get a man in this valley to stand in a room and be told what to do like anybody else, because there is no room in this valley that anybody stands in for that, and the room over the cross has a fire in it about forty times a year and nine people turn up to it, and in about nine years one of them put eleven words at nine hundred gates and got it done, and that was four days ago and it was not me who did it.”

He turned round and looked at the nine people on the bank.

“Sixteen of us, fourth hour, on the coping and then the ford. The mill man first, because the mill is going to be the first thing anybody in this valley can get out of, and the rest of you can be late.” He put the rope in a belt that was not a belt. “And I am going to tell him the rate I pay, and it is nothing, and I am going to tell him that out loud in front of everybody, and the reason I am going to tell him is that a man who is paid nothing gets asked no questions and a man who is paid something gets asked what the something is, and I would rather be asked what nothing is.”

And that is the four days entire, and on the morning of the fourth of them about forty people came up the bank in a flat grey light that does not get dark at any hour anybody keeps in this valley, and they came to watch a course of stone come off a coping, and the man of thirty-four stood at the low end of the cutting with his hands behind his back and waited for all of them to get there before he said anything, because he had learned that in a cold room in the second month of this valley and it had taken him four years to learn it and he was not going to be told about it twice.
