# Chapter 0152

The twenty-seventh of Hearthmonth, YR 313, was a Monday and it was not a market day, and the county post came up the hill at about the sixth hour, and it is three days either way, and a thing that is in the post on a Monday in this county is in the post in the same county on the Thursday and is in a filing room two hundred miles off on the Tuesday of the week after.

Rowan Petch is twenty-six and has been four years in the filing room of the provincial court and is this month on the county post at his own cost, which is a thing he does twice a year and which nobody has ever thanked him for, because thanking a man for carrying a bag is a thing you do to a man who is carrying something of yours.

He came up the two flights with the bag and put it on the table, and there were four things in it, and three of them were for this county, and the fourth had been out in a bag since the sixth of this month and had come back in this one.

It came back because it had been opened. That is what is on the outside of it. A paper band round the neck of a bag, sealed, and the seal is not broken but it is *cut*, along the left edge, with a knife, in one stroke, the way a man cuts the throat of a seal when he wants the person it was sent to be able to see that it was opened and not that it was never shut.

Every lesson of the last eleven months of this case has been about a seal.

Inside the band there is a sheet of ordinary writing paper with a fold in it, and on the fold, in a hand that is not a clerk's and that two people in this room recognise and neither of them says so, it says *To Sabin Dain, aid to the register at the Office of Provincial Registration. And to no office.*

“You are to enter that it came back opened,” said the keeper of records, from the middle of the room, before anybody had sat down. “And I want the bag, and I want the band, and I want the hour on the outside of the bag and not on the paper, and I want the door on the lane shut.”

“I have not opened it,” said Rowan Petch.

“I know you have not. I am telling you what I am going to enter so that the entry is not about you, and there is a difference, and you have been in filing rooms long enough to know that the difference is the whole of what anybody in a filing room does.”

And the sheet is still on that table on the Wednesday and still on the table on the Thursday, because nobody in that room has any standing to open a thing addressed to a person in an office two hundred miles off, and the Archive is not that person's post, and a keeper of records who opens a letter that was not addressed to her is a woman who reads other people's post, and there is no version of this in four years and a half in which that has been done in this room.

---

**The fourth thing in the bag was a notice of four lines and it had come down a road four hundred miles off, in a fair hand, and it is not the answer to anything, because nothing was asked of the county it came from.**

It came down on the Friday. It is dated four days before that, which means it was written while it was on the road, which means the road took nine days for four lines and a keeper of records has written that on the wrapper because a document's own date and a document's own arrival are the two halves of every question anybody ever asks about a letter.

Four lines, in a fair hand, and the hand is not a clerk's hand and is not the hand of the man who keeps the minute in the county the fair copy came from.

*That the line at this end is shut, and was shut in writing, and the shutting is lawful, and is not to be disputed, and is not to be re-opened by anybody from the north.*
*That the book at this end is shut and is kept shut and is not to be asked for by anybody from the north.*
*That the hand at this end is released from it, and is not to be sought, and is not to be sent for.*
*That the line at this end is the whole of what this county has to say to him.*

Nobody in the room had asked that county anything. That is the finding and it took about nine seconds and it is on the page because Alish Renn entered it, and she entered it in the plain form with the date and the hour and the claim number against the office and nothing at all against any person.

“Nothing was asked of it,” she said. “There is a resolution of the twentieth of this month in this room that says the delegation is not to be sent for and that no person is to be sent to ask anybody in that county anything, and the resolution is a hundred days old and it has been kept, and this county has kept it, and about four hundred people in a county that is not on any map can be entirely certain that nobody came, and that is the one thing this case has been able to give them, and it is on the second of a month in a fair hand, and it came down the same road as a company's first letter and it is not about that letter.”

“Then what is it about,” said the junior clerk.

“It is about the fact of there being a north,” said the keeper of records. “**A county that has been cut off has to send something down the road about once every year or people start, and there are four lines in this and every one of them is about a thing not being asked, and that is a letter a county writes to itself and sends to prove that it is still there.** And it is correct, and every line of it is correct, and the second of them is the same sentence the third line of the autumn notice said, and the fourth of them is the same sentence the fourth line said, and I have the autumn notice in the press and I have checked both and I am not going to improve on either.”

She read it twice, which is her habit, and then she did the thing she does, which is to copy one sentence of it out entire on a sheet of plain paper and put it in the press with a claim number against nothing on the wrapper.

The sentence is the fourth line.

*That the line at this end is the whole of what this county has to say to him.*

“Four words of that are the same four words a bench of nine put in a minute of its own on the twenty-fourth of this county's last year,” said Alish Renn, “and I am not going to say which four, and the second copyist of this office is not going to write them down, and if the office is ever asked which four, the answer is going to be that the office does not know and has not looked.”

---

**And the letter that is not answered is not in the bag and never has been in the bag, and that is the second half of what this Monday is.**

It went into a bag on the twenty-first of this month. Four lines. To any county that has kept a seed of a thing it does not grow, asking for a jar, with the carriage paid and the jar paid for and the name of whoever keeps it promised to a book that cannot be served and not to a book that can.

It went to a post office, because a letter with no name on it goes to a post office and not to a person, and a post office writes to the last address it holds for a county, and the last address this office holds for that county is a roll of households with no address on it and no person at the end of it, bought for a farthing of a bushel of salt by a man on a coast who wants a canal wharf.

So the letter is in a bag in a county with no writ, addressed to a county four hundred miles off a road that is shut in writing, and it has not been delivered, and it is not going to be delivered inside this year, and the notice that came down on Friday does not mention a jar, a seed, a wheat, a jar's price, or the name of anybody who keeps seed, and nobody in that county is in default of anything, because the letter is not a demand and a person cannot be in default of a thing they have not got.

Alish Renn made a sheet for it.

That is the second half of the Monday and it is not a dramatic thing and it is the most careful piece of work done in this room in four years and a half.

The sheet is ruled in four columns, in her hand, and the four columns are *what was sent*, *the day it went*, *to whom in the words it was sent*, and *carriage paid or not paid*. And there is no fifth column, and the ruling stops at the fourth, and there is a space of about two inches at the right-hand edge of the sheet with nothing ruled in it at all, and a heading that stops four words short of the width of the paper.

“There is no column for what came back,” she said, to nobody in particular, and the room knew what she was doing and nobody helped her.

“There could be one,” said Sarn Oyelaran. “It would be the ordinary thing. Every office in this country has a column for what came back and I have never seen a returnable paper without one, and if you rule it you can write *nothing* in it, and *nothing* is a fact, and a fact is what this office is for.”

“Then somebody writes *nothing* in it in about four years,” said Alish Renn, “and it is true when he writes it, and then in about forty years somebody reads a ruled column with the word *nothing* in it and says the office asked, and that is a thing this office does not do, and if you want to do it you may do it on your own sheet and I will keep it in this press and label it, and I will not have it on the same page as the other.”

Eleven lines went on the sheet and the eleventh is a jar of a wheat, four hundred miles, carriage paid, and there is two inches of bare paper at the right of it with nothing ruled in it, and the two inches is not a mistake and is not going to be ruled in.

**And a hall four hundred yards from that yard read the sixth line of the fair copy out loud on the same Monday, because a hall reads things that are about it.**

Bram Ottery is forty-seven and is on that bench because he was elected, and he is not on a trade, and he is paid fourpence a week out of a private box that a reeve's predecessor filled twice, and in four years and a half of that bench there has never been a week on which the fourpence was late and there has never been a year in which it was enough.

He asked for the sheet to be read again and the bench-clerk read the sixth line again.

*And a line with nothing in a money column, and it is the only one of the six. Four men of a yard from a fen county four hundred miles off, on the council's account, at the guild's rate, with none. The last twenty-seven days of them were paid in ninety-six bushels of salt at nine pence the bushel and it lands clean. The rest of them is at none, and it is in this council's book and in the guild's book, and neither of those books can be made to answer to anybody in any other county.*

“Four men of a yard,” said Bram Ottery, “in this town.”

“Four men of a yard in this town, at this guild's rate, and the yard is named, and the street is named,” said the reeve, “and the guild's first hand four hundred miles off objected to the two names being on one page and then would not have either of them taken off, and the county in question proceeded with its eyes open and entered the objection, and I am telling you the names because you are on this bench and this is a matter of this bench and I would like it minuted that I told you.”

The room did not make a sound, and about nine men in it knew what a yard in this town was, and one of them was the bench-clerk, and none of them looked at the door.

“Then I will say the thing that is in the room,” said Bram Ottery, “and I will say it once and I would like the clerk to put it down exactly. There is a council four hundred miles off that has written down in a book that cannot be served that it owes this town four men's wages, and it has written it in a way that no court in this kingdom can get at, and it has put the word *none* in the space where the money should go, and it has done that with its eyes open, and it is not a secret, because it is nailed to a board under the stair in this hall.

“And this bench has a chest with two marks in it and has been asked five times for money and has said yes five times and has given two marks, and in four years and a half this bench has not once asked a yard in this town what it is owed by anybody. Not because we are proud. Because nobody ever thought of it.”

“Enter it,” said the reeve.

“Enter it exactly as he said it and not one word of mine in it,” said the bench-clerk, “and he is right that it is wrong and I am going to rule a column on this minute for it, and the column is going to be blank, and I would like the reeve to initial the blank, because in about nine years somebody is going to come and look at this minute and see a ruled column with nothing in it and think there is a sum.”

The reeve initialed the blank column.

Nobody in that hall has yet said out loud that a yard four hundred yards down Weir Street has a crown's mould of grey wax nailed over a doorway nobody can see from the road, and a bench-clerk has ruled a column and a reeve has initialled it, and a council four hundred miles off has written *none* in a money column in a county that is not on any map, and the four men of that yard are at this moment six miles from here on a job that will be paid at the end of the week.

---

**And the post is paid at his own cost and this office has never asked him for a penny of it and is not going to, and the figure is on the wrapper because a man who pays his own way should have it written down once.**

He did it on the back of the wrapper with a pencil, because there was nothing else to write it on, and he read it out because the room had gone quiet again.

“A carrier at ten pence a day is ten. A horse’s keep at four is four. Ten and four is fourteen a day, and I have been on the road nine days out of the eleven I have been on it, and fourteen nine times is a hundred and twenty-six. Four sheets at twopence is eight. A hundred and twenty-six and eight is a hundred and thirty-four pence, and that is two marks and three shillings and twopence, and I have paid it, and I would like it on the same page as the list and not on the wrapper, because the wrapper is a thing that goes to a filing room.”

“Move it,” said the keeper of records.

And then, because there were nine lines above the jar on that sheet and the room had read them, she read the one that had to be read, and it was not in her voice for facts.

*That the Marches' contract was costed in that county and came to three thousand two hundred and forty pence, and paid in two hundred and eighty-eight bushels of salt at nine pence the bushel and two hundred and sixteen bushels of seed at three pence the bushel, and the two together are the contract and not two payments, and the one fifth of the whole, six hundred and forty-eight pence, was payable on the return and was given up in a gateway by the first hand of that county's guild for a reason about a promise, and it is not in any book, and a yard four hundred miles from here has been out of pocket six hundred and forty-eight pence since, and the same figure is the price of two hundred and sixteen bushels of seed in the same line of the same account.*

She put her hand flat on the page.

“Those are two different sums of money in two different counties and they are the same figure, and I am entering that this office has noticed it, and that it has done one single thing with it, which is to write it down, and that what it has not done is arithmetic.”

“And you have read that column out loud in a room,” said Rowan Petch, “to a man who is on the post at his own cost and who will be asked about it in a tavern in a month.”

“Yes,” she said. “And I would rather you heard it from me than worked it out, and I would rather a clerk heard it in a room than a keeper’s boy, and there are nine people in this case who have worked it out and none of them is a person who would have said so.”

Rowan Petch went down the two flights with the bag at about the ninth hour, and the bag was lighter by one sheet, and he had four villages left and no carriage money, and the notice went into the press with a claim number against nothing on the wrapper, and the bare two inches at the right-hand edge of the list of things sent stayed bare, and a sheet of writing paper with a fold in it stayed on the table under a weight for four days with nobody in the room willing to be the one who opened it, and on the second of a month in a fair hand a county four hundred miles off said four things that were all true, and not one of them was an answer to anything, because there had never been a question.
