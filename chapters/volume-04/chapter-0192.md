# Chapter 0192

**The face moved overnight, and nobody was standing on it, and about forty people came back to it at the fourth hour of the second morning of the four days and a man of thirty-four was already at the top of it with a bar in his hand, and he had been there since the middle of the night, and there is no hour in this valley that the middle of the night is.**

The frozen foot of the bank had let go of the face at the top and gone over into it, and it had gone over the width of a hand, and the face had come in with it and sat at the bottom in a slope, and the step nobody had cut was the slope, and a man of thirty-four had spent the middle of the night deciding whether that was a disaster or a day's work.

“It is not a disaster,” he said. “It is what frozen ground does when you take four feet out from under it in a cold and then leave it. I have seen it four times and two of them were my fault and one of them was not and I could not tell which one at the time, and the fourth one was a byre in the low country that came down in a wet week and nobody was hurt and it was still a byre the week after.

“**We are not going to move the face up. We are going to move it back and take the top of it off in bars and get the step in, and the step is what makes it four days instead of six, and I worked that out at the second hour of the night with a lamp and I am telling you at the fourth hour so that you can all be cross about it in front of each other.**”

Nobody was cross about it. About nine people were, later, in kitchens, and one of them said out loud that a man who works out at the second hour of the night what a thing is going to cost should be asked what it costs before he does it, and the man of thirty-four said the answer to that in a yard two days later and it was four sentences long and none of them was about the night.

---

**The ninth generation asked about the water for the second morning, and it is the ninth time in nine generations that generation has asked an older hand a question about the trade and the answer was given four times in one morning, and it was the same answer three times and a different answer once.**

They asked where the water under the ford comes from. They had asked it in the second month of this valley and got the same answer and had been told that getting the same answer twice was a thing to be told about. They asked again because the man of thirty-four had put his hand on the frozen foot where the colour changes and had come back up without saying anything about it, and eleven of them noticed that and nine did not.

“Burn above and Burn below and a beck that comes out under the wall at the Low Holm four hundred yards up this bank, and that is the three of them and none of them is coming out of this face, and I have put a stick in four places in this bank over two winters and got water in one of them in the ninth week of the first cold and I did not know then what it was and I know now and it is not this.”

“Then what is under it.”

“I do not know,” said Wystan Roole, and the ninth generation wrote that down, because nine of them had brought something to write on and four of them had a slate.

He said it in eleven words and he did not improve on it and he has not improved on it since.

The fourth answer was a different one, and it was given to a man of about twenty-six who asked whether a barrow should go up the made ground or round the low end of it, and the man of thirty-four said the barrow should go round the low end, because made ground in a cold is a thing that has not been under anything yet, and as many again of them had been on those four hundred yards with barrows every day for nine weeks and knew that and had never heard anybody say it.

---

**And about the fifth hour he stopped the work for an hour, and it was not because anybody was tired and it was not because of the eleven, and nine people in that bank were cross about it and eleven were not.**

He stopped it because the barrow run went over the top of the face and the top of the face was going over into the face, and a barrow at the top of that in a cold is a barrow with four hundred pounds of ground behind it, and a man of twenty-two of the ninth generation was on the run with a barrow at the time and came down the bank nine feet faster than he had come up it.

“Everybody off the face,” he said, and about forty people came off a face in four seconds, and it was the most frightening thing that has happened in this valley in nine generations and nobody was hurt and four of them have told it four different ways since.

Then he put sixteen of them on the top of the bank with bars for an hour and had the barrow run moved round the low end, which cost eleven yards a load, and the making good of those eleven yards is a thing the sixteen of them did in the afternoon of the third day and not before.

“**The face is four feet and it is nine feet across and it is a hole in a bank with a barrow run on top of it, and there is no way in this world to have both of those, and I have had both of those for one hour and I am not having them for a second one.**”

Nobody asked him about the eleven in that hour. A man of about twenty-six asked about them later, at the ford, in the plainest words anybody in this valley has used about it in a month.
“If one of the eleven comes back in the warm week and says no, what happens.”

“Nothing happens,” said Wystan Roole, “and I want to say it in the plainest words I have got. **The four days are the four days. In the warm week there is no face in this bank, because a face cannot be made in a warm week, and a man of thirty-four can stand on a bank in the warm week and shout at a woman with a yoke about a hole he is not going to be able to dig. So it is four days or it is not done at all, and I have known that since the second day of the first winter and I have not said it to forty people before because nobody has asked me.**”

“And if the seven write to somebody.”

“Then a man off a road will read it in about a season and a county will read it in about a year, and there is not one thing anybody in this valley can do about either, and I have not been to that man and asked him to come and I am not going to, because the moment I ask him to come then a thing in this valley has been asked to go and fetch a man, and I have spent nine days taking a stranger's mark out of a public stone for exactly that.”

The man of about twenty-six said, “That is an answer and it is not a good one,” and the man of thirty-four said, “It is the one there is,” and four people heard both sentences and four heard one.

**And the girl with the barrow was on the run on the first morning and was not on it on the second, and eleven people noticed and four of them said something about it, and the man of thirty-four said one thing about it and did not say it to anybody who had not asked.**

She is nine. She has a barrow she most days does not go and get out at the ford, and she counted four hundred and six stones in a wall in two goes round it and nine through-stones in the same wall, and she counted the nine coping stones going down a bank in the fourth week of the fourth cold in the order they came off, and she has never once asked anybody what any of the numbers were for, because then she would have to be told.

“Her father had her on the run at the fourth hour and she was at the top of the face at the sixth, not at the edge of it, and I saw her from nine feet,” said a man of thirty-one of the ninth generation who farms four steadings and sits third on a bench that is not a bench. “I sent her home. I will say that in a room and I am saying it in a yard. I would put her back on it tomorrow and I am not going to, and I would like the man of thirty-four to tell me in front of these people whether I am right, and then I would like everybody to go back to work.”

“She is nine.”

“She is nine and she is the fastest thing on that run and I know the second of that better than you do.”

“Then you know the second half of it as well,” said Wystan Roole, “and you have not said it, and I am going to say it, and I am not saying it to you. **A child on the top of a face that is going over is a child on the top of a thing that has not found its angle yet, and a man of thirty-four stood on the top of one of those nine weeks ago and came off it four feet on his back in front of a woman with a yoke, and the woman with a yoke has not forgiven him and is not going to and it is not about the wall. Put her on the low end. Put her on the low end tomorrow, and in the warm week, and in every warm week after it, and when she is fourteen she can have the top of a face and not before, and I will be dead or I will not be able to get up a bank.**”

Four people said something about it and as many again did not, and the man of about thirty-one said, “That is not the reason,” and the man of thirty-four said, “It is the reason and it is all of it, and there is a second reason and it is that I do not know what is at the bottom of that face and neither do you, and a man who has not got the bottom of a face has not got the top of it either.”

She was on the low end of the run on the second afternoon and on the third day and on the fourth, and nobody asked her why she was not on the top of it and she did not say.

---

**And at about the seventh hour of the second day he said the thing he has known for nine years and had never once said in a room, and he said it on a bank in front of about forty people because the four days are four days and there is not going to be a fifth one for him to say it in.**

“This ford moves.”

Four people said something at once and nine said nothing.

“It moves about a foot a year and it has moved about a foot a year since I was twenty-five, and I have known it every day of nine years, and I have never said it out loud in a room, and I have said it to myself about four hundred times, and I have never once said it to a man.

“**The reason I have never said it is that a man who says a thing like that in this valley is a man who is asked to be right about it, and there is nobody in nine miles who can tell me whether I am right, and I would rather be a man who has said nothing for the whole time than a man who has said a thing for the whole time and cannot say where it is going.**”

He put the bar down on the step that was in the face by then and stood on it.

“There is one question about this valley I have never been asked and have never had the answer to, and the man of forty-one who farms three steadings asked it of me on the fourth day of the fourth cold in a room with a hundred and forty cut into the end of the bench he was sitting on, and I told him the truth, which is that I do not know. Nine good stones are in the footing of the second stand under four feet of water in the order they came off, and that footing is in the bed of this ford, and this ford goes down about a foot a year, and I have laid a footing twice in nine years and laid the second one wrong and there is a man in this valley who knows which of them it was and he is not going to tell you.

“**And I am not going to work it out in four days. I have got nine years ahead of me and I have not got the end of the ninth, and a man who works a thing out in that time and writes it down has made a survey, and I am not going to make a survey in a valley with no writ and no bell and no office and a hundred and forty cut into a bench.**”

Nobody said anything. A man of fifty-eight was at the top of the bank and had come up in a cold to stand and had not come to argue and did not.

And then the man of thirty-four went down into the face about two feet and put his hand flat on the step and stayed on it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp came down after him with a rope round a stone about the size of a melon that had come out of the face on the first day, and neither of them said anything and nobody within thirty feet of it heard anything at all, which is the reason the rest of this valley has nine accounts of the four days and none of them is checkable against the others.

---

**And by the ninth hour of the second day there was a step in the face and there was a barrow route round the low end of it, and the second of those had cost eleven yards a load, and eleven people worked that out in their heads inside a minute, and four of them said it out loud.**

The step is the second thing in nine generations of this valley's craft that is not on a drawing, because there is no drawing and there has never been a drawing and there is not a piece of paper in this valley that anybody has ever made a thing on.

“A step wants a footing,” said a man of fifty-one who had lost an argument about plates in the second month of this valley and got it back four minutes later, who had been at the second stand and at the mill floor and in the cutting for four hours on the fourth day of the fourth cold, and who had come to a bank on a cold morning because somebody told him there was a hole in it.

“Yes,” said Wystan Roole. “And the footing wants a bed, and the bed wants the bottom of the bank, and the bottom of the bank is what we have not got to yet, and that is tomorrow, and if you want to know what is at the bottom of it you have to be here tomorrow and not tonight.”

He was there tomorrow.

**And the sixteen of them made good the eleven yards on the third afternoon, and it took nine hours, and it is the only ground in this valley that has ever been made twice.**

The making good is not a trade anybody outside this valley would recognise. It is not a wall and it is not a road. It is a bank about four feet high and eleven yards longer than it was, put up out of what came out of the face, in a course of one height, with nothing cut, and the reason nothing was cut is the reason a man of thirty-four said in front of forty people in the fourth week of the fourth cold with a bar in his hand, which is that a stone squared on two faces wants a course of one height all the way along because that is the only bed it will go into.

It took nine hours and about twenty-six men and eleven barrows and the sand out of the same place the barley comes out of in the warm week, because there is nowhere else in this valley to put sand. The barrow run went over it at the fourth hour on the fourth day, loaded, with the man of about thirty-one on it, and it did not go down.

Nobody said anything about that either, and a man of about fifty-one who had been in the bank all morning said afterwards that he had been waiting two days for somebody to put a loaded barrow over the eleven yards and that he had said nothing about it when it did not move, and the man of thirty-four said that the silence was worth more than the sentence and that he had waited a day and a half for it.
