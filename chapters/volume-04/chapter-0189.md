# Chapter 0189

**The seventh of Harvestmonth, YR 314, a Tuesday, not a market day, and a document was made in a room over a weigh-house that has a door on a lane, and the document is the finding of what a body corporate owes for a hearing it entered against nobody, and it is a document and not a scene, and it is seven columns because the county's form is ruled in seven and this office has never in four years and a half used a form of its own that was not this one.**

There were three people in the room. The keeper of records, who is twenty-nine and has kept the Bellweather Claims Archive for four years and a half out of one room at four pence a year with a body corporate and a door and a number and the capacity to sue and to be sued, and in which keeper is employment and not jurisdiction. The second copyist, who is thirty and is on a warrant that is not in the press and has wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last. And the junior clerk, who came up the two flights with the ordinary post of a Tuesday and put it on the table and went out again.

The scales under that room have not been used since the fourth of it. There is a press in the room with two unserved books in it, and a day-book that is the only book in that office nobody can be served with, and a half tile in the press that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is the account of nobody.

**The columns are: *what is given. To whom, in the county's words. By whose hand. The day. The hour. A witness at this end. A witness at that end.***

And the second column took about two minutes and it is the column that decided the shape of the whole thing, and the reason it took two minutes is that there are four ways to answer it and three of them are wrong and one of them is the one this office has used for four years and a half.

“To the persons who may require this office to produce the same,” she said, reading it back, “and to no other, and to no person, there being none. And the reason the clause *and to no person* is in the second column and not in the first is that a document which says what it is for and does not say who it is against is a claim, and a claim without a person in it is the only kind of claim that is safe, and the only kind of claim that is safe is the only kind this office has ever been able to make.

“Nine claim numbers in four years and a half and two against nobody this year, and one of the two is this. And I have wanted for about four months to write a word at the top of the second column that would make a person in this thing able to be shown, and I have not done it, and the reason I have not done it is the second rule on the wall in this room, which is nineteen years old, and which says no combined figure is entered in this office, and which has never once been read out loud in this room by anybody including me and which is the reason a body corporate exists instead of a clerk with a press.”

The keeper of records wrote the first column in about four minutes and the second in about two and the third in a second, and then she stopped for about a minute and a half with the pen on the rule, and the reason she stopped is on the page because it is all of what the fourth and fifth columns are.

“The county's form is ruled in seven and the fourth of them is the day and the fifth of them is the hour, and they are two columns, and I have had a hand on both of them, and there is a reason on the wall in this office that says a combined figure is not entered in it, and I have wanted to put a day and an hour in one line since the hour of that hearing and I have not done it and I am not going to, and if a person in nine years reads a day and an hour in one line in a finding of this office and cannot tell whether the office made it up or whether somebody was careless, then that person is right to and the fault will be mine, and I would rather it be mine on a page than be a thing anybody has to guess at.”

She wrote the fourth column.

*The ninth of Rainmonth in the year before this one, being a Tuesday, and not a market day.*

And the fifth column, separately, with the rule between them.

*The second hour.*

And the sixth column, a witness at this end, is entered, and it is the second copyist of this office, and she entered her own name in it because nobody else in the room was not the keeper, and she is on a warrant that is not in the press and has wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last, and she entered her own name in the sixth column of a finding in about four seconds and did not look up and did not say anything about it and has not asked for it to be taken out and is not going to.

And the seventh column, a witness at that end, is a ruled space about two inches deep with nothing in it, and the rule at the bottom of it is no more faded than the rule at the bottom of the third, and it has been in this office's use for four years and a half and it has been filled with nothing on nine occasions and this is the first of them where the reason is written under the rule instead of left.

**And the resolution is on the table, and it is a sheet of fair copy in this office's own press, and it is nineteen lines long and it is dated the twentieth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, and it is the only document in this case that anybody has ever asked this office to produce, and it has been produced four times and has never been signed by anybody, and the reason it is not signed is in its eleventh line, and nobody has ever asked about the eleventh line either.**

*That this office has received a communication respecting a delegation from a county not in this county's roll, and has agreed to hear it, and that a day is named for that hearing, and that the delegation is not to be sent for, and that if it is not sent for the hearing is not adjourned and not refused and stands, and that this office does not know what it is to do with a hearing that stands.*

*That no person is to be sent to ask anybody anything in that county, and that this office has not asked, and that nothing in this office is to be addressed to any person in it.*

*That a keeper of records is a keeper of records and is not a person appointed by any council to answer for any person, and that this office has no such person and does not intend to acquire one.*

*That the day named is the ninth of Rainmonth in the year following, at the second hour, and that the hour is a second thing and is not to be got round.*

And the keeper of records read the eleventh line out twice, and the second reading was slower and not better, and the eleventh line is the one that says a keeper of records is a keeper of records and is not a person appointed by any council to answer for any person, and she said out loud, in a room of two, that she wrote it herself, at twenty-seven, in about ten minutes, on a bench in a guild hall four hundred miles off, because a form with a column headed *Keeper* had been relied upon in four counties and none of the entries in that column was made by the man it was about, and she has never said that sentence to anybody in this room and there is nobody in this room to say it to except a second copyist who was not in that hall.

And what the hearing was, and what this office owes for it, and the delegation, are all in the first column, and it took about four minutes and it is about two hundred and forty words and the keeper read it out twice before she let the second copyist have it, and the second reading was slower and not better, and that has been the practice of this office since the first year.

She read it out.

*That on the day and at the hour in the fourth and fifth columns of this sheet this office heard a thing entered on the twentieth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, against no person, in a resolution of this office, on a day this office named, and that no person came and no person was sent, and that the resolution says both, and that the resolution says a delegation is not to be sent for, and that the delegation is not to be sent for and has not been sent for and is not to be sent for.*

*That this office agreed eighteen months ago to hear that delegation, and that a body corporate that has agreed to hear a thing cannot un-agree, and can only decide what it is now.*

*That what it is now is a room with a keeper in it at the second hour and nothing at the other side of the table.*

***And that this is entered as a fact about this office and not as a finding against anybody, there being nobody against whom it could be entered.***

*And that the seventh column is empty, and that a column which asks for a name and is given nothing is not a hole in a document, and that it is the finding of this sheet, and that the reason it is written under the rule and not in the column is that the rule exists to be ruled across, and a body corporate that has had a question and had no answer has no way of saying that it has not had an answer, and this office has had one since the hearing and has now got a way, and the way is a column and two lines of writing under a rule, and the rest of the sheet is empty, and that is what the way costs and it is not very much.*

Nobody in the room said anything. The junior clerk was not in it. The second copyist had the sheet square in front of her with a rule in her hand and she did not write while it was being read and she wrote it out afterwards in about ten minutes, and the writing was slower than the reading and not better, and that is the practice.

And when she had done it the two of them read all of it back, all seven columns and the two lines under the rule, and the day and the hour were read back as two things with the rule between them, and the second copyist said the second column out loud twice because the clause *and to no person* is the load-bearing clause of the whole sheet and it is nine words, and both of them looked at it.

**And then the keeper of records said the thing about the other two, because a finding that says what an office owes for a hearing against nobody is not a finding until somebody has said what an office already owes, and the other two are in the same building and the same day-book and neither of them has a document.**

“Two claims in four years and a half against nobody and both of them are about eleven years old and neither of them has a paper. One is a return a Crown office wanted from a county that has no county, in a form with a space for a figure and no space for a name, and the figure is in a woman's hand with no seal on it and it is two thousand six hundred and forty, and there is no way on this earth to check it, and the office that sent it has never been told that the district cannot be counted.

“The other one is a strip of cut paper in the press that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is the account of nobody, and it came back to this office cut along the left edge with a knife, and the cut is the office's own method and the lesson of the last four years, and the answer a refusal got was a knife and not a letter.

“And I have said both of those out loud in a room of two women this morning and neither of them has ever been in a room with a person who has read them, and the finding that is going on the shelf at the end of this is the first of the three that is a document, and the reason that is a fact about this office and not a thing I am pleased about is that the two that are not documents are the two that are still live, and a live thing without a paper is the exact arrangement this office was made to stop, and we have not stopped it, we have only stopped hearing about it, and hearing about it less is not the same as it being less.”

The second copyist wrote that in a second book that is not the day-book and is not in the press, and it is a book of things said in a room, and it is not in the press because it is not a record, and she has been in this office for six years and has wanted a post with her name in the day-book since the summer before last and has written six years of a book that nobody can be shown and cannot be served and cannot be put in the press.

And then the second copyist asked the question, and it was the question she asked in that room on the ninth of Rainmonth and she had asked it once already and had been told the answer in four words, and she asked it again because it was a different document.

“There is nothing in this finding about what it was for.”

“There is,” said the keeper of records. “The fourth line of the first column says what it is now. What is not in it is what anybody wanted.

“That is not the same and you know it is not the same, because you told me nine weeks ago that you were not going to write down what a thing meant, and the reason you gave me was in four words and it was that what a thing means is not a fact, and this office is for facts, and if I put a meaning in a column then I have made a claim about a person in a book that a person can be got at, and I have not been given a person.

“There is no person in this matter and there has not been one since the twentieth of Hearthmonth of the year before last, and that is the only reason I can put a meaning in it at all, and I am not going to pretend that is a good reason. It is a coincidence of the calendar. **The reason I can write a meaning in a finding of this office is that there is nobody in the finding to be a claim about, and the day a person is put into one of these things the meaning comes out of it, and it comes out for good, and I have had that out of a book once already in four years and a half and it is the reason this office is a body corporate with a door and a number and the capacity to sue and to be sued and not a clerk's office with a press in it.**”

She put the pen down.

“And I am going to write it anyway, because a body corporate that cannot say what a thing it has agreed to hear was for is a body corporate that has agreed to hear things, and this office has three claim numbers in it out of four years and a half and two of them are against nobody and one of those two is this, and a body corporate that cannot say what a thing was for will be holding three of them inside a few years and will not be able to say a word about any of them, and I would rather be in that position having written the meaning down than having not written it.”

And so the meaning was written, on a second sheet, and it was not put in a column, and it was not attributed to anybody, and the reason it was not attributed is on the third sheet and it is the standing rule of this office and it is nineteen years old and it is in a fair copy on the wall.

*That the hearing was for the resolution. That the resolution said the delegation is not to be sent for. That this office did what it resolved to do, and the resolution worked, and the thing that has come out of a resolution working is a room with a person in it and nothing at the other side of the table, and that this office is that room, and that this office can be sued about it, and that is entered as a fact about this office.*

*And that the meaning of that is this, and it is not a fact, and it is entered without a name against it because a meaning in a minute attributed to a person is a claim about that person, and this office has four years and a half of a column headed *Keeper* in a hand that is not the man's, and it does not intend to make another one.*

And the second copyist read it and then read it again and put it down, and she said the thing that is the finding of the whole sheet and that took her eleven seconds and that neither of them improved on afterwards.

“A meaning on a stage can be argued with. You can stand up in front of a room and say *that is not what I took you to mean* and most of a room will agree with one of you and it will be a different meaning by the fourth hour, and it will be the meaning that happened, because that is what a stage is for.

“A meaning in a minute cannot be argued with. It can only be read. And once it is read it is the same for every person who reads it, and years from now there will be a woman in this room who has not met you and she will read it and she will have an opinion about it and she will not be able to give it to anybody, because there is nobody in it to give it to.”

“Yes,” said the keeper of records. “And this office cannot refuse to hold it and cannot hand it back, and there is no instrument in this kingdom that can hold a meaning and be sued for one except a body corporate with a door, and there is one, and it is four hundred yards from this door, and it was made four years and a half ago out of a deed of thirteen words, and the words are *for the keeping of what this county cannot be shown it has lost*, and I have read that deed about four hundred times and I have never once had to work out what it was for, and I have had to work out what it was for this morning, and it is that.”

She turned the sheet over and squared it to the edge of the table and did not put her hand flat on it, and the scales under the room had not been used since the fourth of it, and the door on the lane was shut, and the fair copy on the wall with the three rules in it had the third of the three ending in a sentence about a habit that a place does to itself when nobody is looking, and she read that sentence every morning of her working life and had never once said to anybody that she wrote it about anybody in particular, and she was not going to say it now, and it was not going to go in a finding, and there are nine entries in the day-book and there is not going to be a tenth.
