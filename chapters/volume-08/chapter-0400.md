# Chapter 0400

**The twenty-fifth day of Mudmonth, YR 318, a Sunday, and not a market day, and the first hour and the second hour and the sixth hour, at the head of a made road four miles west of Marl, a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a bar of nine feet of oak has been going down at the second hour and coming up at the sixth on four days in seven since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and a man of about nineteen has been standing at one end of it for a hundred and fifty-one days, and the county does not trade on that Sunday, and about two hundred people out of two thousand were out of their houses and one of the two hundred was on a bar four miles off.**

The rope was on its post and the bar was up. **In a town of about two thousand people about two hundred go out of their houses on a day the county does not trade, and on that Sunday about a hundred and forty of the two hundred went to a chapel about nine miles off and about forty of them went nowhere in particular and stood about, and one of the two hundred was four miles west of that market at the end of a made road with a lever in his hand, and he was the only person on that road that Sunday who was working.**

**Nine feet of oak, an iron strap, a chain, and two sockets in a kerb, and a man of about thirty with a mallet cut those two sockets in about two hours on the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth and nobody has touched them since.** Up to that month it was up on three days in seven the whole year. In that month it became four days in seven and it has not gone back. It comes up in about four seconds on a lever and a fulcrum, and it went down at the second hour, and about nine carts have been turned back off it since then and it will come up at the sixth.

**The shed is a yard's and it is nine feet by seven with a stove in it and boards on the top of it, and it stands against the bank at the head of that made road with the bank side of it to the salt, and there is a bench inside it, and the bench is there because a man of about nineteen cannot stand for four hours and then walk four hundred yards and do it again the next morning, and nobody thought about that and it is the only reason the bench is there.**

He is about nineteen years old and he came off a cart four hundred miles off on the morning of the twenty-fourth of that month in Wolfmonth and nobody sent him and nobody appointed him and he is not on a roll in four counties and is not in a yard's book and is not on the Crown's own series.

**A Sunday is the busiest day on a made road in that district and it is busiest because a Sunday is the day the parish goes to the next parish, and a man who has a cart on a Sunday is going about nine miles to a chapel that is shut and nine miles back with a load on the way, and a bar that is up on a Monday and a Wednesday and a Friday and a Sunday is not up on a day anybody is going anywhere, and four generations of that county have put the bar where it is and nobody has ever said so out loud, and nobody in that county is paid to know.**

## One

**The four months came out on the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth and it is the twenty-fifth of a month in Mudmonth, and that is a month, and nobody has said it to him.** He is not a fool. He has counted the days off the front of a shed about four times in about five months and about two hundred people in a town of about two thousand have counted them too in one way or another, and about eight of them have worked out that a man of about nineteen who came off a cart on the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth ought to have gone home on the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth, and about four of the eight are wrong by a day and about four of them are right and none of the eight of them has ever said one word to him about it, because there is no form on which to say it and because a man who says it to a man of about nineteen who is standing at a bar is a man who has come four miles to be a fool in front of a bar.

He did not go home on the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth and he did not go home on the twenty-fifth and he is not going to go home on the twenty-sixth, and nobody has said anything to him about any of it, and nobody is going to.

**He has a sister in the county he came off the cart from and she is twenty-two and he has not spoken to her in about a year, and not one of the nine men on that yard four hundred miles up that county's own road has ever asked him about that, and there is no form on which to ask it and there is no column on any of the Crown's own series that either of them can be entered in, and the fourth column of that series is headed Keeper and takes a person by that person's trade, and a sister of twenty-two is not a trade and neither is a man of about nineteen.** He has a bundle. It has been in that shed since about the fortieth day and it is not a bundle that a man packs for four hundred miles and it is not a bundle that a man packs for one mile. **It is a bundle, and it is in the way of the stove in the winter, and he has not opened it in about a hundred days, and about two hundred people in a town of about two thousand people have known for about a month that the four months are out and about two hundred people in that town have not said one word to him about it.**

**He has a rate for the three days in seven that the bar is up. It is nine pence a day, and he asked for the three days in seven out loud in a yard in the second week of a month in Wolfmonth where nine men were setting terms, and nobody agreed to them and nobody disagreed with them, and a man of about nineteen who asked out loud in a room is a man who has a trade of his own at the third hour on those three days and it is nine pence a day and it is four hours a day lower than the one he came off, and it is a yard of nine men standing at eight while it is holding a span over a beck.**

**Nobody is paid for the four hours. He is not paid for the four hours and he is not paid for the four months of the year when nobody comes, and a person who is not paid for the four months and is not paid the four hours is a person nobody in this kingdom has a rate for at all, and he is the only person within nine miles of that town who is working and is not on anything.**

## Two

About two hundred times in four hours a person comes up that made road and goes past. **Nine times in four hours a cart comes up it, and each of the nine is somebody else's afternoon, and a man of about nineteen has about a second and a half to say whether it goes round the bank or back down the road again, and on that Sunday two of the nine could not and turned in about four hundred yards and stood at a yard gate until the sixth hour.** The driver of the second one was a man of about thirty-three with a barrow, and he said nothing at the time and has said nothing since. He came up that road once in the third week of a month in Frostmonth and did not come again, and before that he had come once and had not come again either, and the reason a man of about thirty-three with a barrow stops coming up a made road is not a thing anybody on that road has asked him and not a thing anybody in that town has said out loud, and a town of about two thousand people losing one barrow on a made road is not an event and is not nothing.

**There is a ditch four hundred yards west of that bar that has been in the way of carts since the middle of that year, and a boy of about eleven with a pail has been on its shoulder every hour of the daylight, and nine yards of soft shoulder on the salt side takes a loaded cart down about two inches and holds it there.** Nobody has ruled that ditch and nobody was going to. About two hundred people in that town went round that quarter of a mile on that Sunday and have gone round it every day since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and the going round is now a thing they do and not a thing they complain about, and there is nowhere in this kingdom to enter a complaint about a quarter of a mile of a made road, and a boy of about eleven with a pail has not been asked why he is on the shoulder and has not said.

**And a woman of about fifty who has kept a list of nine of them for nineteen years did not come out of her door that day, and has not come out of her door on a day like it in nineteen years, because a list can only be checked on a morning the list can be checked on and that is not a Sunday.** She has been asked about that list four times in about five months by three different people and has answered all four and has shown it to none of them, and about nine hundred set stones in that market do not know that there is a list of nine of anything within four miles of where they are standing.

## Three

At about the fifth hour the weather came in off the water and the man of about nineteen put the lever down and stood in the mouth of the shed and let it come past him, which is what a man of about nineteen does with about nine minutes of weather.

**And the bar came up at about the sixth hour, and it comes up in about four seconds, and it is a thing that about two hundred people on that made road have watched a great many times and that not one of them has ever written a line about, and a bar that is up and down on four days in seven for four months of the year and up on three days in seven for the other eight is not a toll and is not a rate and is not on a sheet, and there is no line in this kingdom that takes the hour it comes up at and the hour it goes down at and the name of the man and the number of carts in between, because that is four different things and not one figure.**

**And the four months of the year when nobody comes are four months of the year in which about nine hundred people on a coast about two days west along that salt road cannot run a boat, and a yard about eleven days east of that coast has agreed one load a week down a road for those four months at its own day rate in coin on a Friday, and it is one week in three and not one week in one, and nobody is paid for the four months at either end of it and nobody has ever made a column with the two of them in it, and about two hundred people in a town of about two thousand people on the salt do not know any of that and a man of about nineteen four miles west of that town does not know any of it either and is not going to be told.**

**And about nine hundred people four hundred miles off, on a flat country of nine miles by four with a river across the middle of it, have a bar of nine feet of oak in a kerb at the top of a rise on their only road out, and it has been up about fifty-seven days, and about four hundred people live on the far side of it and about nine people were on the far side of it on the morning it went up and have been there ever since, and nobody on either side of that bar has said when it comes down and stays down, and there is no room on that river in which a person could enter why, and there has not been one for about nineteen years.**

He is going to be at that bar at the second hour tomorrow and on the three days after that and on the four days after that, and that is the whole of what anybody on that made road knows about the four months of the year when nobody comes.

**A road is a line and two hands and a date, and a sheet is a list, and the bar went down at the second hour and came up at the sixth and the man of about nineteen put the lever against the wall of the shed and is not paid for the four months of the year when nobody comes, and nobody has ever told him when they end, and he has not asked, and he is going to be there in the morning.**
