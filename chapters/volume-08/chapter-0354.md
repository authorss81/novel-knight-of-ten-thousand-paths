# Chapter 0354

**The fourteenth day of Rainmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, in a long room over a wharf, where a bench of nine sat at about the seventh hour because it sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a sheet of the Crown's own printed series was read out in that room at about the fifth hour, and that nobody in that room was asked a single question about it.**

A Saturday in that county is a market morning. About two thousand people came into four hundred yards of stone, and forty men were on a reach four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of it with the road open over it, and a yard four hundred yards up the other way had nine men in it and the press in the room at the end of it stands open and has stood open for eighteen weeks and nobody has shut it. **A bell of nine stone with a band of forty-one rivets in it stands a hundred yards up that county's own road and went at the fourth hour and a half, on a Saturday, the way it goes, and it is rung by a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is still not good at it and by a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper, and neither of them is paid by the guild and neither of them is a member of anything.**

---

**And a long room over a wharf in a town of about four thousand people has a bench of nine at the long side of it and a table about twenty feet along and a chest under the table with two marks in it that have not moved since the ninth of a month in the year before last, and a minute kept nineteen years by a clerk of about sixty, and a subscription book with four names in it of which two are the same man written twice, and about four feet of bare floor between the long table and the wall that nobody has ever stood in except a man of forty-seven on a Saturday.**

**The clerk of about sixty read back the minute of that sitting once, at about the seventh hour, which is the house practice, and a clerk reading a minute back once is the house practice and is a different thing from a man reading a sheet out to nine men who have not been asked, and about two people in that county have worked out since that the only difference between the two acts is that one of them is a thing a clerk does about a thing the clerk wrote and the other one is a thing a stranger does to nine men who have not asked him.**

**A man came into that long room at about the fifth hour with a sheet of about nine hundred sheets' worth of printing on it, and he did not put it to the bench, and that is the whole of what happened in that room on that Saturday, and about two people in that county have said since that the single most important thing that happened in that room was a man declining to do the one thing a man with a sheet always does.**

“**It is a schedule of nine crossings,**” he said, standing at the long table, “**and a price at each, and the price is two shillings a cart, and it is for the season, and it takes effect in about three weeks, and the season is the four months the crossings run.**”

The bench of nine had nine members on it. Four of them were there for one reason and three of them were there for another and two of them were there because the other seven are in the room. **A clerk of about sixty who has kept that room's minute for nineteen years wrote down that a sheet had been read, and he wrote down the five things that were on it, and he did not write down anybody's opinion of the five things, because there was not one, because nobody was asked.**

“**Nine crossings,**” a man at the end said. “**I have read the nine.**”

“**The nine are on the sheet.**”

“**Then I have read them,**” the man said, “**and I have not said they are the nine and I have not said they are not the nine.**”

**And nobody in that room put the sheet to the bench. Not one question was asked of nine men about a schedule of nine crossings and a price, and there is a page about nine inches by six under the fourth step of that outside stair with a figure at the head of it and nine marks under that figure, and it said nine before that Saturday and it said nine after it, and nobody in that hall has ever put that figure on a form.**

---

**And four of the nine had come for one reason and three had come for another and two of them had come because the other seven are in the room, and the reason four of them came for was a length of the market's edge-stone on the far side of that street that had begun to let water at the fourth joint after about nine years, and the reason three of them came for was a rate for the coming year, and neither of those two things was put, and a man of about forty-seven who is on the roll with a vote and not an office had been in that room five times in about a year and had been asked nothing at any of them, and the sixth thing he was going to be asked about was a price and not the edge-stone.**

**And a man of forty-seven stood in four feet of bare floor between the long table and the wall for about two hours that morning, and he is on the roll with a vote and not an office, and he was asked nothing, and that is the fifth time in about a year that he has been in that room and been asked nothing, and about two people in that county have said since that a man who stands in four feet of floor for two hours at a market on a Saturday and is asked nothing has begun, in about a year, to be a kind of furniture, and that a kind of furniture does not object to a price.**

He said nothing about the sheet. He had said four things, five times in a year, to nobody, and nobody in that county has ever wanted to hear them, and nobody asked him that Saturday, and the four things are not written down anywhere and are not going to be.

**And a bench of nine was asked nothing at a market morning on a Saturday for the second time in about two years, and the first time was the one where the ninth asking was refused in eleven words with a reason in it, and the count under the fourth step is nine and is not a count of persons and is not a count of sheets and is not added to anything.**

---

**And the man with the sheet went out of that room at about the seventh hour and did not take the sheet, and the sheet stayed on the long table, and about thirty people came in after him and looked at it and two of them turned it over and about nine of the nine hundred sheets a month that go out of an office into nine counties in two rounds are about the size of the one on that table and none of the thirty people in that room knew that.**

The sheet is the office's own printed series. Six columns and five heads, *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper* and *How often*, and a sixth column with no head on it and the same width as the other five, and about four of the six are ruled for a figure. **And nowhere on the whole of it, on any sheet of that series, in any of the four hundred and nine parish rolls behind it, in any month for nineteen years, is there a place for the name of a person. The person column is the third, and it takes a person by that person's trade and not by that person's name, and the fourth is headed *Keeper*, and it takes a person the same way the third does, and that head has been the same head for two years.**

**Nobody in that room read those five heads. Two of the thirty people turned the sheet over and about nine of them said, afterwards and in the street and not to each other, that a Crown sheet with a price on it is a Crown sheet like any other, and that a Crown sheet with a price on it is a thing a man can be angry at in a way a Crown sheet with a keeper in it is not, because you can be angry at a price and there is nothing on that sheet to be angry at that anybody in that room can put a name to.**

**And a man of about thirty-eight came into that room at about the fifth hour and asked a question, and it was the ninth asking of that bench in nineteen years, and it was not on a Saturday in a market, and about two people in that county have said since that a Crown officer of thirty-eight who has asked a court for nine years that a record show what was asked came into a guild hall on a market Saturday and asked a bench of nine for a thing nobody had thought of asking, and that she had prepared it, and that the bench heard it and did not answer it, and that the clerk wrote down that the bench did not answer it, and that this is a different act from a refusal with a reason in it and the bench has done that once, and that nobody in that hall thanked her for asking and nobody told her she was right, and that she is not redeemed and is not converted and is not going to be by any part of this.**

“**I have a sheet in my hand that I did not come in here with,**” she said, “**and I am going to read it out in a room that has a minute kept nineteen years, and the minute will have a line in it, and the line will say that a woman stood in a hall on a market Saturday and asked a bench whether the second column of a Crown schedule is a place or a person. And then the minute will have a line saying the bench did not answer, and there will be no other line, and the sheet will be in the room and the price will be in the room and the head over the fourth column will be in the room, and in about three weeks somebody will be able to produce the minute.**”

Nobody answered her. A clerk of about sixty wrote it down and read it back once, which is the house practice, and about two people in that county have worked out the difference between that minute and a sheet is that the minute has a person in it and a sheet has a trade in it, and that a person in a minute can be asked to read it again and a trade in a sheet can only be copied out.

---

**And about nine people went to the bar that Saturday for a drink the way about nine people go to a bar in a market town on a Saturday, and four of those nine were in that room at about the fifth hour, and two of the four said out loud in the street that a Crown sheet with a price on it is a thing you can be angry at, and about two people in that county have worked out since that the entire difference between a price and a keeper is that a price is a number you can be angry at in a doorway and a keeper is a trade you can be angry at in a sheet two hundred miles off, and that a man has to be in the room to be angry at a keeper and nobody had put him in the room.**

**And about four people in that county have worked out since that a bench of nine was read a price and did nothing about it, and that the reason it did nothing about it is that the sheet had no head on it that a bench can be angry at, and that a man who is not on a bench can be angry at a price in a way he has not worked out, and that in about three weeks the price will be two shillings a cart on nine crossings and the anger will have had a fortnight to find a form and will not have found one.**

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was not in that room, and does not know that a sheet was read out in it, and was two hundred miles off at about the seventh hour of that Saturday in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, at a table, with a document in front of him, and about four thousand people in that town do not know either of those two rooms and about two thousand people on the salt do not know about either of them, and a bell a hundred yards up a road went at the fourth hour and a half and went on going.**
