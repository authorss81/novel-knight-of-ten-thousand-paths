# Chapter 0363

**The eighth day of Fallowmonth, YR 317, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a town of about two thousand people on the salt and in nine places inside forty miles of it, where a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag set a bar of oak and iron in two brackets beside a made road and did not ask the council of that town anything, and the council of that town did not know until the ninth day, and about two thousand people in that town knew before the council did.**

The nine of them are not the Crown's nine. **The Crown's nine are crossings and they are on a printed schedule with a price on it that goes into effect in about four days, and the man of thirty-one had that schedule in his bag and looked at it twice that week and it says nothing whatever about any of the nine places he had been to.** The nine of his are anchors, and an anchor on that flat country is a fixed thing a road bears on. A post in a sill at a footbridge. A stone with shoe-iron in it at a ford. A kerb at the head of a towpath. A sill at a wicket. A step down to a landing. A bar-socket in a lane. A hard point where a salt pan track leaves good ground. A pier at a weir. And the head of a cart track that runs out of a field.

**And two people in that town have worked out since that a man who is given nine places to go to is given nine places and not nine names, and that nine places is a thing a man can carry in his head for nine days and nine names is a thing that would have to be written down, and that nobody in that district has ever asked him which of the nine is which.**

---

The third of the nine is the kerb at the head of a towpath that runs for about a mile beside a made road and then goes onto hard ground and joins a lane. **The towpath is two yards wide and four inches above the made road and it is the only hard ground for two miles, and about nine hundred people in four villages walk on it, and a woman with a load cannot get off it and onto the made road and onto another path without putting the load down, and about nine hundred of them walk it to Marl on the market days and two hundred of them walk it on the days that are not market days.**

A seal is a bar. **Four pence the oak, five pence the strap and the chain and the two brackets, and three pence the man who sets it, and four pence and five pence is nine pence, and nine pence and three pence is a shilling, and that is the whole of what one of the nine cost.** A bar of nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain laid in brackets either side of a two-yard path is not a wound. It does not cut and it does not burn and it does not take anybody, and a person who has decided to get past it can get past it in about four minutes with a lever off a wall and a stone off the ground, and about two hundred people who had decided to get past it had got past it by the eighth hour of the day the third of the nine was done.

“**It is a bar,**” the man of about thirty-one said, to a woman of about thirty-eight who had walked up from a lane with a basket and had come back down again to ask. “**It is not a gate and I have not got a key and I am not going to be standing here, and you can get under it with a stick if you want to and about two hundred of you have.**”

“**Then what is it for.**”

“**It is for a cart,**” he said, “**and there is a place two miles off where a cart can go round, and it is a quarter of a mile, and I know that because I have walked it.**”

**And the man of about thirty-one was right, and had a second sheet for it, and did not ask the council of that town anything.**

He had a second sheet. It had a number on it and a date on it and a list of nine places on it in the order he was going to them, and the third of the nine on that list was the kerb at the head of the towpath, and the sheet said he need not ask anybody, and it did not say why it did not say that, and he had read the sheet and agreed with it.

“**Do you want me to go and tell the council,**” he said.

“**No,**” a man of about thirty-two with a limp said, from about nine feet below the path, where he had been for a quarter of an hour and had not asked to be there.

“**You are not the council.**”

“**I am not the council,**” the man of thirty-two said, “**and the council is not going to lift it and if you tell the council then the council will write to somebody and somebody will write back, and the two of those will take about a month, and I have seen the month.**”

**And the man of about thirty-one put the second sheet back in his bag, and about four people in that district have said since that a man with a limp told a man with a paper not to tell the council, and that the reason he gave was a month. About two hundred people in that town carried a basket under a bar that day and said afterwards that they would have gone round it, and going round is a quarter of a mile and nine minutes, and nine minutes there and nine minutes back is half a day of a woman's working day, twice a week, for as long as the bar is there.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-two with a limp was at the bottom of that towpath for a quarter of an hour before he said anything, and about two people in that town have said since that he was not looking at the bar and was looking at the made road, and at the place where the made road has a soft shoulder on the salt side of it about nine feet down, and at the ditch that takes the water off that shoulder in a wet week and has not taken any since Mudmonth.**

He did not put a hand on it. **He stood at the bottom of a two-yard path with a stick he cannot lean on and watched about two hundred people of a county who had not come to watch him, and he counted nothing, and the thing he was doing is a thing a man does at a footbridge when a load is on it.** A man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag asked him once whether he was there about the bar, and he said that he was there about the road, and the man of about thirty-one said that the road was nine weeks of a season away and this was a Thursday in the second week of it, and the man of thirty-two said that it was, and that he had walked nine miles that morning to look at four hundred yards of made road on a flat country and that the four hundred yards had a bar on it now and had not had one when he started, and that he would go and look at the rest of it tomorrow.

**And about four people in that district have worked out since that a man with a limp came nine miles to stand at the bottom of a towpath for a quarter of an hour and did not touch the bar and did not say a word against it, and that the only thing he asked anybody was whether the road was all right, and that a person who asks whether a road is all right and does not go and fix it is doing a thing about nine men in a yard four hundred miles off do every morning, and that nobody in that district has ever met the sort.**

**And the council of that town is nine men who meet when the town has business, and it had not had business since before the sickness started, and what happened at that towpath is that nobody told anybody and a bar came anyway, and that a bar is a thing that arrives before a conversation and stays after it, and that the nine men would have lifted it in about a quarter of an hour on any ordinary afternoon and would not have needed to be told a reason, and that a reason is what nobody had.**

**And a woman of about thirty-eight who had a basket and had gone round in a quarter of a mile came past the bar again at about the sixth hour on her way back and looked at it for about a minute, and about four people in that district have been saying since that a woman looked at a bar for a minute the way a person looks at a thing they have decided to remember, and that what she decided to remember was not the bar and was the quarter of a mile, and that there is no column on any sheet in that country for a quarter of a mile a woman has decided to remember.**

---

**And an anchor on that flat country is an ordinary word and it means a fixed thing a road has leaned on since before anybody in that town was born, and there are more of them in forty miles than anybody has counted, and the nine of them are nine of about four hundred, and the four hundred are not a list and are not on a sheet and are known to about nine people in that district the way a bar at a crossing is known, by having fallen down.**

“**My grandfather's grandfather put a shoe in it,**” a man of about thirty-three who was standing at the head of the path with a barrow said, when the man of about thirty-one asked him what the place was. “**And the shoe is not the road. The road is the ground and the shoe is what the ground was told to do it on. I have heard the word used for about nine places in this country since I was a boy and I have never once heard anybody use it for anything else, and I would not be surprised if there are nine hundred of them, and I have heard of nine.**”

“**That is nine places you have heard of.**”

“**That is nine places I have heard of,**” the man of about thirty-three said, “**and I have heard of a tenth at the head of a cart track that goes up off the flats, and somebody has been up that track in the last fortnight, and there is a bar across it, and I did not think to say so because I did not think anybody was asking.**”

---

**And it was not cruelty and it was not a plan either, and a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag said so himself at the seventh hour, standing at a bar with a chain on it, to nobody in particular and to about two people who were there.**

“**I have set four of these,**” he said. “**Four in nine days, and this is the third and the fourth was on the Tuesday, and I am paid by the bar and not by the day, and there are nine of them, and the weather is going. And I will tell you the thing about them that nobody has told you. A bar is a bar. I am putting nine of them on nine places and the nine places are where the ground is bad and where a road has ever been moved, and nobody in this district has ever asked me to do it and nobody has ever told me not to, and I have not asked anybody which nine they are.**”

“**Which nine are they,**” the man of about thirty-one said, before anybody else could. “**Which nine places out of a county.**”

“**That is what I have been asking since the first of Fallowmonth,**” he said, “**and there is no answer, and I have got a list of nine places in a bag and about four hundred places in this district, and somebody chose the nine out of the four hundred and did not write down how.**”

---

**And a bar of nine feet of oak lay across a two-yard towpath four inches above a made road at about the eighth hour of the eighth of Fallowmonth, and a quarter of a mile of hard ground went round it, and a woman with a basket had got under it in about four minutes with a stick off a wall, and about two hundred people in four villages who were not there that day will go round it, and the council of that town did not know, and the man who set it had a paper that said he need not ask, and none of that is anybody's fault and all of it is what came out of a piece of paper with a number on it.**
