# Chapter 0382

**The twenty-seventh day of Frostmonth, YR 317, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, on a made road and a lane and a cart track in forty miles of flat country around a town of about two thousand people on the salt, called Marl, where a man of about thirty-two with a limp was at four bars in one day, and nobody in that town knows that there are four of them.**

The market was shut and the rope was on its post and the bar was up. **A bar at the north end of that market is up on a Monday and up on every day that market does not trade, and it has been up on those days for longer than anybody in that town has been asked about it, and a woman of about fifty who has kept one list of nine of them for nineteen years came out of a door at about the second hour and looked at him and did not ask him anything, and four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman who keeps a list for nineteen years can tell in one look at a man walking a made road which of the nine he is going to look at next, and that she has never once said so.**

She said one thing and it was about her own work and not about his. **She said that the bar at that end goes up on five days in seven because the market trades on two, and that it has been on her list for nineteen years, and that nobody has ever asked her whether it ought to be on it, and that nobody has ever asked her because a woman with a book is not a person anybody asks.**

“**You have got a list of nine,**” she said, “**and you have not asked me for it and I have not offered it, and I have been in this town thirty-one years and there is a page in your coat and there is a book in my house and neither of us is going to find out what the other one has got.**”

---

**Forty miles of flat country around that town is not a bad road and it is not a good one. It is a place where the ground is hard on the made side and soft on the salt side, and the whole of what there is to know about it is in the first nine yards off the kerb, and every decision a person makes in that district is made in those nine yards or not at all.** A bar in that country is not a wall and it is not a gate. It is nine feet of oak with a strap and a chain, and it is put in two sockets or on two trestles, and it goes down and it comes up, and the coming up is done by hand in about four seconds by somebody who is not paid for the four months a year it is down.

---

What the cooperation had been, from this end of it, was four days and a bar and a shed and a road. **It had begun as four drawings a hundred and forty years old and it had gone on being a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag who came when he said he would come, and it had ended at about the fourth hour the day before in a room over a shop without one word of an argument, and nobody in that town has heard of any of it and never will.**

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp got the sum of what it had cost in about four seconds standing at the north end of that market, and the four things are a bar at this end that is up five days in seven and a bar four miles south that is down and up four times a day and a bar in a kerb four miles west with a man of about nineteen on it and a bar on a cart track nine miles west with a man of about thirty-one's handiwork on it, and nobody in that town has ever been shown the four of them together and there is no place in that town to show them.**

The second of the four was four miles south and it was down and up four times a day. **A bar of oak and iron, a penny a person and four pence a cart, and a woman of about forty-one on her own side of it who has been at it for about eleven years, and she is at it on the fourth and on the eighth as well and is paid for four months of the year, and nobody in that town knows the second of those two facts and four hundred yards of the four know both of them.**

He stopped at it because he had stopped at it before. **She said nothing about the room over the shop and she said nothing about a tray of returns and she said one thing about a bar.**

“**You have got four of them in your head,**” she said, “**and if you put the three that take money together in one line you will have a figure by the fourth hour, and there is no line anywhere in this kingdom for three prices of three different things, and I have watched a man try it once and I am not going to be the second.**”

“**I was not going to write it.**”

“**I know you were not,**” she said, “**and that is the whole of it. I do not mind a man doing it. I mind a man putting it down.**”

---

The third of the four was in a kerb at the head of a made road four miles west, and it was down, because it is down from the second hour to the sixth on four days in seven and the twenty-seventh of that month was the first of those four days.

**Nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain in two brackets, and the two brackets are sockets a man of about thirty cut in a kerb in about two hours on the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth with a mallet and a plank and nine nails, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow held the bar while he cut them, and a man of about nineteen has been standing at one end of it since and a man of about nineteen was standing at the end of it on the twenty-seventh of that month with his back to a kerb where the ground is hard.**

**Nine yards of soft shoulder on the salt side of that made road takes a loaded cart down about two inches in nine yards, and a ditch four hundred yards west of that bar had two inches of brown water in the bottom of it that week for the first time since a month two hundred miles back, and a boy of about eleven with a pail was on the shoulder every hour of the day, and nobody has ruled that ditch and nobody was going to.**

**At the far end of that made road there is a thing nine feet by seven with a stove in it and boards across the top of it and a bench inside it, and the bench is there because four hours standing and four months of them is a thing a body will not do twice in a week. Nobody in that town can describe that bench from a market four miles off without walking out and looking at it, and nobody in that town can describe the bar at all.**

He stood on the other side of that road from it for about two hours and did not touch it. **Thirty-three days. A man of about nineteen with an empty hod has been on the end of that bar since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and nobody in that district is paying him for the four hours, and what he is paid for the three days in seven comes out of a yard's rate that is four hours a day lower than the one he walked off with, and that is not a rate anybody has a name for because it is not one rate. There is no figure anywhere within nine miles of that town for what a man does on that bar, and he is the only person inside nine miles who is working and is not on anything at all.**

**Three days in seven he is on his own trade at the third hour and that trade is nine pence a day, and nine people in that district have worked out since that a yard of nine men gave those three days to the man who was going to need them, that the yard neither agreed to them nor disagreed with them, and that a yard which argues about a thing for a quarter of an hour loses the thing. He asked for them. That is the whole of how he has them.** There is a sister of his in a county four hundred miles off who is twenty-two and that he has not spoken to in about a year, and not one of the nine on that yard has asked him about that, and nobody in that district knows about the sister and nobody is going to be told.

**And about two hundred people in four villages went round that quarter of a mile on the twenty-seventh of that month as they have gone round it every one of those thirty-three days, and nobody in either of the two of them has been told why, and there is nowhere in this kingdom to enter a why, and nobody in that town knows that two of their four villages are not getting through a road.**

---

The fourth of the four was nine miles west and he did not go up it.

**It is a bar of nine feet of oak on two trestles across the head of a cart track, and it went there in the third week of Fallowmonth and it has been there about a month, and the man who put it there is the man of about thirty-one with the canvas bag who is nine days off and is not coming back, and four people in that district have worked out since that nine men on a hurdle at the head of a track two days north and a bar on a track nine miles west were put there by the same hand in the same three weeks for the same reason, and neither of them has a why on it and a bar is not a why.**

He stopped at the mouth of that track at about the eighth hour with the stick he cannot lean on and did not go up it. **He had been asked in a room two hundred miles off on the twenty-sixth of that month whether a field was going to be told and he had got as far as putting his hand flat on a table, and four people in that district have worked out since that a man who does not go up a cart track in the eighth hour is a man who has learned something and is not going to say what.**

---

**And the cost of the twenty-seventh of that month is a figure he got in about four seconds and has not got anywhere.** Three of the four take a price and one of the four does not. **The three prices are on three sheets in three rooms, and a penny a person and four pence a cart at one of them and two shillings a cart at nine of them, and the two shillings is a price on a Crown's own sheet and the other is a price a woman of about forty-one set for herself out of what the four months pay her, and a toll is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and a bar is not a rate and a return is not a toll, and there is not one column in this kingdom that takes two of those at once and there is not one person in forty miles who would know what to do with the total if he had it.**

He had it anyway. **He stood at the mouth of a cart track nine miles west at the eighth hour of a Monday and had the sum of three prices in about four seconds, and four people in that district have worked out since that a man who can do that in four seconds has no place on this side of the sea to put any of it, and that a yard of nine men four hundred miles up a county's own road has been putting the same kind of thing in a book that cannot be served with anything for four years and has not been asked about it, and that the two of those are the same sentence and nobody has joined them.**

That was the fourth one in about a month. **The register is one. The two villages are one. The field four miles off that town is one. And the four bars are one, and he has not written any of the four down, and there is a page in his coat with nine lines on it and no heads and none of the four of these is on it, and a page with nine crossings on it is a page for showing somebody where to go and there has been nobody to show it to since he made it in about nine minutes in a yard with a stick against a bench-board.**

A man of about thirty-three with a barrow came up the made road behind him at about the eighth hour and asked him what he was doing standing at the mouth of a track.

“**Counting bars,**” the man of about thirty-two said.

“**What did you get.**”

“**Four,**” the man of about thirty-two said.

“**That is not a number anybody has,**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said, “**and it is the first one you have said out loud since you came down this road in Harvestmonth that I have not been able to write down either, and I have wanted to ask you about that for about four months and I am not going to now.**”

**And four people in that district have said since that two men stood at the mouth of a cart track nine miles west at the eighth hour of a Monday and one of them said four and the other of them said that four is not a number anybody has, and that neither of them said what else they had, and that a man of about thirty-three with a barrow has not asked a question since the twenty-seventh of that month and nobody in that town knows that.**
