# Chapter 0397

**The twenty-fourth day of Thawmonth, YR 318, a Thursday and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in one room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, in a provincial town about two hundred miles off a fen county, and in the other rooms of nine counties where a book is open on a table, where a roll has carried the name of four villages for nineteen years and the figure on it has not moved once, and about eleven hundred people are on nothing at all and are not on a list of anybody.**

## One

A roll is a place and a figure and about four heads. **There are about four hundred and nine of them in this county's own book and about four hundred and nine in each of eight others, and they go out of the building behind that door on a fixed day and come back a fortnight later, and a roll that is out of the building is a roll that is out of the world, and about forty people in a building two floors up work in three shifts on the days the round is loading and there are seven presses and about four hundred forms a month come in and about nine hundred a month go out.**

The four villages that emptied into a field above a ditch are four of the places on those rolls. **They have been there since before anybody in that building was born. They are in the same four lines and they are in the same four lines they were in nineteen years ago, and the figure against each of the four is the figure those four places had when the last count was taken, and the last count was taken before the woman of about forty-two who counts faster than a man with a stick was born.**

Nobody has amended them. **A clerk of about sixty has been in that room for thirty-one years and keeps two kinds of minute and is not going to keep a third, and he has been asked twice in about four months whether a place with no people in it should have its figure moved and he has said twice that there is no column for it, and the second time he said it he also said the other half, which is that a figure on a roll is not a count of the people who are there and is a count of the people a place was accounted for at the last time anybody came and counted.**

## Two

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet came up that lane at about the fourth hour on the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth with his own round in a bag and one errand on the back of a page and about eleven days to the coast in his legs if he went on.

**His errand was not about a register and it was not about a roll and it was about himself.** He is the loading end of a cart that goes out on a fixed day, and about five weeks of it are left to him, and the round that had gone out in the third week of a month in Hearthmonth had come back in the fourth week of a month in Hearthmonth with about forty sheets of a round of returns in it that were not the round he had loaded, and nobody had told him and he had found it himself on a fixed day when the bag was lighter than it should have been.

“**I want to know who swapped them,**” he said, “**and I have been at it for about a month and I have got about nine things and not one of them is a person.**”

“**Then you have got nine things,**” the keeper of records said, “**and I am not going to help you with them, because if you take a sheet of that round out of this room with a wrong round's figure on it then a place is going to be held open or let go on the strength of a clerk's arithmetic and I do not know which of the two and neither do you.**”

“**That is fair,**” he said. “**I did not expect it and it is fair.**”

He had been in that room about nine minutes and he was about to go, and the clerk of about sixty asked him the question that a clerk of about sixty asks.

“**What does a fortnight of the round cost you,**” he said. “**Not the wages. The other part. The cart and the feeding and the two of you.**”

And the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said that the cart was four pence a day and a man was five pence a day and there were two of him and one cart and the round was out a fortnight.

**A cart at four pence a day for fourteen days is fifty-six pence. Two men at five pence a day for fourteen days each is seventy pence apiece, and seventy pence apiece is a hundred and forty. Fifty-six and a hundred and forty is one hundred and ninety-six pence, and one hundred and ninety-six pence is sixteen shillings and fourpence, and the clerk of about sixty wrote it on the back of a form and added it in pence on the boards before he said the total and not after, and about forty people in a building two floors up work in that figure every day and none of them is paid at it.**

“**That is a fortnight,**” the clerk said. “**It goes out in a month twice. So that is about three hundred and ninety-two pence a month for one cart and two men, and the cost of the sheet of paper it carries is a quarter of a penny, and there is no line in this kingdom for what a round is worth and there never has been and I have asked twice and been told there is not one to ask.**”

## Three

**And that is how the figure of about eleven hundred came out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane on the twenty-fourth of a month in Thawmonth, and it came out sideways and it came out by accident, and it has not been on a printed thing since the day it was written and it never will be.**

The clerk of about sixty was talking about a round and about how much a round costs and about the fact that a place is held open or let go on a figure on a sheet, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said the thing that had been sitting in him since Fallowmonth and that he had never once said to anybody.

“**Then what is a place,**” he said. “**I have been round nine counties for eleven years and I have never had to be told what a place is. A place is a place on a roll. How many people.**”

“**A place is a place on a roll,**” the clerk of about sixty said, “**and how many people it was accounted for at the last time anybody came and counted, and those are not the same and they have not been the same for about forty years and there is no column in this room or in this building or in this kingdom that can move one into the other, and the man who wrote the roll knows it and the man who reads it does not, and the man who reads it is the one who decides.**”

“**Then how many people are in those four places now.**”

“**Nothing,**” said the clerk of about sixty. “**Nothing is not a figure anybody may write on a roll. A place that is empty keeps the figure it had, and if the figure were moved down to nothing then every other place on that roll would have to be moved too, because they were all counted at the same time by the same man, and there is one man left in this county who was taught that count and he is in a house four miles off and he is eighty-one.**”

---

The keeper of records said one thing in that room on that day and she said it to a man with a bag and not to the clerk.

“**I have got a figure of about eleven hundred,**” she said, “**and I did not get it off a roll and I cannot tell you where I got it and I am not going to put it on anything, and I am telling you it is about eleven hundred because there are about eight hundred people in a field four miles off a town on the salt and there are four places on a roll in nine counties that have not moved in nineteen years and the difference between those two is not mine to work out.**”

“**You have just worked it out,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**I have said two numbers in a room and I have not done the sum and neither are you going to,**” she said, “**and I am not writing it, and if it is in your bag in nine days it has got there out of that building and I will know and so will the clerk, and there is nothing in this room you can do with about eleven hundred.**”

“**Then who can.**”

“**Nobody in this room,**” she said. “**And I have wanted a thing for about four years and a half that this room cannot do, and it is not a figure and it is not a sum and it is the whole of what I have been asked for in a year by about nine people and it is the one thing in that building I have never been able to put in a column, and I have stopped asking for it, and I am not going to explain it to a man with a bag at the fourth hour on a market Thursday because you would write it down and take it eleven days to a coast.**”

---

## Four

**A clerk of about sixty has been striking a line out of a roll for thirty-one years and he has done it about nine thousand times and he has never once struck a place.** He can strike a person, because a person is a line with a name and a date against it and a place is a line with a figure against it and a figure means nothing at all without the four lines round it that were counted on the same day by the same man with the same tally. **He has struck a great many names in thirty-one years and he could tell you the name of every one of them and he is not going to and nobody has ever asked him for one.**

The four villages are four lines about nine lines from one another on the same page of the same roll and they have been on that page since before the room was this room.

“**I will tell you the one thing I can do,**” he said, to nobody in particular, and the keeper of records let him, which she does about twice a year. “**I can write the four of them again. Same words, same figure, same page, in a hand that is mine and not the hand of the man who counted them, and put the date of today against it, and that is called a second entry and it is the ordinaryest thing in this trade and it means that somebody looked, and it is the only mark in this building that says that anybody has ever wondered about a place.**”

“**Would that help.**”

“**It would tell a man two hundred miles off that a clerk in this county has not forgotten those four villages,**” he said, “**and it would not tell him a single thing about the people, and he would have a date and a hand and no figure he can move, and a date and a hand on a line is worth a great deal to a man who has to decide and worth nothing at all to a person who is not there.**”

“**Then do it,**” said the man of about thirty-four with a sheet.

And the clerk of about sixty did it, in about four minutes, in a second hand, on the same page, with the same four figures, and did not tell anybody, and it is the only thing that has been done in nine counties that month about those four villages and it changes nothing and it is not nothing, and the people who live in the places those four lines name are not in that room and were never going to be.

## Five

**And the four villages are still on about four hundred and nine rolls, in four lines, with the figure they had before the woman of about forty-two was born, and about eleven hundred people are on no roll and on no sheet and on no list, and the register that has a place and a figure in it in nine hundred sheets two hundred miles off is not destroyed and is not withdrawn and is in a tray with a strap over it and goes round again in about a month, and the woman of about thirty-three on the third floor found the drawer in the dark in about four seconds that afternoon and put a bundle of about forty sheets in it and shut it, and the press in that room stands open where it has stood open since before the weather turned and nobody shuts it.**

**And in nine counties, in the other rooms of them, a book was open on a table that evening, and the people who live in the places those four lines name are in four hundred and nine different rooms and none of them is in the same room as anybody else and not one of the four lines has been struck, and not one figure has been moved, and about eleven hundred people are somewhere that no book in this kingdom has a line for, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet went out into a street of about nine hundred yards with a bag that had about forty sheets of the wrong round in it and a figure of about eleven hundred that was not in the bag, and he had it in his head and took it with him, and about two hundred people in that street saw a man with a bag go down it and one of the two hundred was the woman behind a counter in another street who has not asked anybody anything in a year and did not ask him.**
