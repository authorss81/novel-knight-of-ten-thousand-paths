# Chapter 0369

**The twenty-second day of Embermonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a town of about two thousand people on the salt and at a place two days north of it past the end of the made road, where about four hundred people live in about nine dwellings and a hurdle is across the track, and about two thousand people in that town know there is a place two days north and nine hundred people in a field four miles off know there is a place two days north and about four hundred people in it know that two things.**

A Saturday on the salt is a market day, and about nine hundred people came into that town and stood about four hundred yards of it, and a rope at one end of it took stock and a bar at the other took carts, and a woman of about forty-one has been on her own side of that bar for about eleven years and is paid for the four months of the year it is down and is at it on the other eight, and Embermonth is the second of those four, and the whole of what a month is.

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet went to the place two days north on the sixteenth of Fallowmonth and came back on the eighteenth, and what he did in between was walk two days of a cart track with a sheet and a spare shirt and a bar of bread and come back two days later with the sheet, and two people in that town have worked out since that a man who goes two days to a place and comes back in two days has spent the whole of that time walking, and that a man on foot arrives at a place a day before a cart does and is the only kind of anybody who ever has.**

He had a form. **The form asked for a figure against a place. He had a figure. He could not get past a hurdle with it, and there is no column on the form for a figure he could not get past a hurdle with, and he wrote nothing in the second column and carried the sheet back himself and put it in a tray in that room.** He told it in four sentences in a room over a chandler's shop on the twentieth of Fallowmonth and wrote none of the four down.

There is a hurdle across the track. **There are about nine men on the hurdle. They would not come out. They would not let him in past the hurdle. He stood at it for half an hour and they stood at it for half an hour and nobody said anything at all for half an hour, and at the end of it one of the nine said that there was nothing to say, and that was the whole of it.**

**And a man of about thirty-four who has been a Crown officer for nine years said the rest of it in four sentences, and the rest of it was that the sickness is on the salt and not two days north, and that a round is a round and not a visit, and that a man who is turned back at a hurdle has not been told why and has not asked, and that he had a spare shirt and did not need it.**

---

**And nobody went again.** The office did not send a second man. The man of about thirty-four with the sheet did not offer to go and would not have been asked to. A man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag and three bars still to do did not go up that track and did not say he was going to. **And a man of about thirty-two with a limp, who had a page in his coat with nine lines on it and no heads on it and had been in that district for seven weeks, did not go, and why nobody went is that a man who has been refused a place cannot be sent to that place again, because going again is not finding out whether the answer has changed, it is telling about four hundred people that the answer does not matter.**

And that is not a rule anybody made. **There is no form on which a refusal is entered and there is no sheet on which a place that has refused is ruled as a place that has refused, and the man of about thirty-four wrote nothing down about the hurdle, and nobody wrote anything down about the hurdle because about four hundred people did not say anything, and there is nothing in this kingdom that enters a thing nobody said.**

A man of about fifty-four who burns lime at a kiln two miles off that town asked the man of about thirty-four, at the seventh hour of the twentieth, whether the place had anybody ill in it. **The man of about thirty-four said he did not know, and that that was the answer, and that the four sentences he had brought back contained no figures at all and one of them was a number of men on a hurdle and that the number was not the thing anybody had gone to find.**

“**I have got a place in a column,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and I could not get a figure against it, and I have brought the form back with the column empty, and the column is empty because nine men were standing on a track. That is the whole of what the second column has ever been for and it has never been that before.**”

---

**And about eight hundred people were in a field four miles off that town on that Saturday, and the sickness in that field had been two a day for a month, and nine hundred of them have been in that field for nine weeks, and the sheet that shut the place runs to the last day of Wolfmonth, and nine people went out of it a week ago and are in a barn with a stove in it, and two thousand people in that town know there is a place two days north and a field four miles off and a bar four miles south, and none of the three is on the same paper and all three are in the same county.**

**And the place two days north is nine dwellings and a track and a hurdle and about four hundred people, and four hundred people in nine dwellings is a place a person can be reached by in two days of a cart and has been reached by in two days of a cart four times in about nineteen years, and the four times were a man with a dog and a boy with a fever and a man looking for a man who had gone ahead of him and a man with a sheet, and the last of the four is the one everybody in that town knows about, and two people in that town have worked out since that a place that is reached four times in nineteen years is not a place anybody has stopped going to, and that about four hundred people who have been reached four times in nineteen years are not a people anybody has stopped reaching, and that the two of those are the same thought said twice and only one of them is in a form.**

---

**And the man of about thirty-four is at his own trade, and his trade is a form, and the tray in that room has about nine hundred sheets in it a month and one of the nine hundred has an empty second column and the rest of them have a figure in it, and he did not mark the empty one, and marking it would have been a fifth thing on the form and there is no fifth thing on the form, and two people in that lane have said since that a man who marks the sheet he cannot fill is a man who has decided the sheet is about him, and that nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room and a man cannot mark every one of them and has not tried.**

**And nobody in that room asked him to go again, and he was not asked by anybody, and if he had been asked he would have said the four sentences again, and the four sentences do not have a request in them and do not have a reason in them and do not have anything in them that a person can be persuaded out of by being asked twice.**

---

**And the round went round again and came back, and a sheet went out for the place two days north with a place in the first column and a figure in the second, and the figure in the second column of that one is nothing, and about nine hundred sheets went into a handcart and one of the nine hundred has a second column with nothing in it and the rest of them have a figure in it.**

A figure of nothing is not a figure of no people. **It is a figure of a person who could not get to a place, and the two are different, and the form does not know that they are different because the second column takes a figure and a figure of nothing is a figure.** A man of about thirty-three with a barrow in that town put it straight, to nine people at the kiln, and what he said was that a form cannot tell the difference between an empty place and a shut one, and that every other form in four counties has the same trouble and that this one has it in a worse place because the other ones are about yards.

And the four columns that the return has no head on are a person, a day, a hand, and a way of telling anybody anything at all. **One of those four would have said refused. Nobody ruled it. Nobody asked for it. And the one that would have said refused is one of the four with no head on it, and a column with no head on it cannot be filled in by anybody because there is nothing to fill it in against, and the whole of what the four unheaded columns are is the four things that would have to be asked about a person, and a person is the one of the four nobody asks about, and that has not been an oversight for nineteen years and is not one now.**

**And a form is a machine for making an office out of a word, and this one was ruled in a building two hundred miles off by a person nobody has ever met, and about nine hundred of them go out of a room in a lane every month, and the second column of the one in the tray is empty and the second column of the one that goes out next month will be empty again, and the same man will put it in the tray, and about two thousand people in that town will not be told, and four hundred people two days north of it will not be told either, and what is happening in that room is a piece of paper going round a county with a column on it that nobody has ever filled and never will.**

**And the nine men on that hurdle have about four hundred people behind them and what a hurdle is, is that it is a thing a person can be got over in about nine seconds, and these nine were on it for half an hour, and about two people in that town have said since that the reason a hurdle took half an hour is that a hurdle is not for carts, and that about four hundred people do not use carts, and that a man with a sheet was on a hurdle that was not put there for him and could not tell by looking at it that it was not.**

---

**And about four hundred people are two days north of a town of about two thousand people on the salt, past the end of the made road, in about nine dwellings, and they were there on the sixteenth of Fallowmonth and they are there now, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet has told four sentences in a room over a chandler's shop and written none of them down, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls in a building two hundred miles off carry the name of that place and a figure that has been carried every month for nineteen years out of a roll that did not burn with a building, and the figure on the roll is a figure of the number that was on the roll, and the roll does not know and has no column that knows.**

**And nobody goes again, and a refusal that is not written down anywhere is the only kind there is that nobody can overrule, and a tray in a room in a lane has a form in it with an empty second column, and the man is at his own trade, and about nine men are on a hurdle, and about four hundred people are two days north.**
