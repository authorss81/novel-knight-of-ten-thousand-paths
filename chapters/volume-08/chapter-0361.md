# Chapter 0361

**The twenty-sixth day of Harvestmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a field above a ditch four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where about eight hundred people were living who had been living somewhere else nine days before, and where nine days of those eight hundred had been ill for four days and had not been told what it was, and two thousand people in that town know a price is coming in about three weeks and eight hundred people in that field know nothing about it and would not go back for it.**

A Saturday on the salt is the one day in the week that a person who keeps a bar four miles south of that town is at a bar, and about nine hundred people came into that town and about two thousand of its people are not there, and the market has about four hundred yards of stone in it and a rope at one end and a bar at the other and a cart at the bar and a woman of about forty-one on her own side of it, and about four people in that town have worked out since that the woman is the only person on either end of the bar who is not a customer, and that this has been the case for about eleven years. **And the sickness in that field is in the low arc and the low arc is the village whose wheel went and the low arc is eleven families and the low arc is on the soft ground at the shoulder of the ditch, and nine days of the eight hundred are in it and nine hundred of the eight hundred are not.**

**And a man of about forty with a stick who counted that field on the first morning and gave up on the second morning came back to it on the fourth morning, and he had not come back to count and everybody in that field thought he had come back to count, and he did not say what he had come back for.**

---

**And the ordinary labour of a field of about eight hundred people is digging, carrying water, loading carts, mending, cooking, and the work of about four hundred people who are not speaking to the two hundred, and on the fourth morning of a sickness every one of those six things was being done by somebody who could not do it properly, and nobody in that field had anybody to hand it to.**

A cart came off the track into the high arc on the fourth morning and was unloaded by four men and took about an hour and a half, and on the ninth day it had taken twenty minutes, and the reason was that the four men were the four men of that household and one of them had it. **A woman of about forty-four in the high arc was doing the cooking for eleven households on two pots before the second hour and did it again at the fourth hour and did it a third time at the sixth, and the third time she did it she sat down between the second pot and the third pot for about a minute and a half.**

A tilt cart out of the low village has a wheel out of true. **A man of about thirty put it right with a piece of board off another cart and one nail on the second morning, and he took a day at it, and about nine people in that field know that what he did to it will not carry a load through Wolfmonth, and he is one of the nine and has not been told and has not asked.** **On the fourth morning the mend was the thing that was tested, because a cart with a bad wheel and a plank in it came off the soft ground at the shoulder of the ditch and went up onto the track, and the plank held and the nail held and a man of about nineteen in a fen county four hundred miles off was not in that field and does not know any of it.**

**And two thousand people in that town on that Saturday were buying salt and peat and a load of glass that had come up the salt road behind nine waggons since the spring, and about nine hundred of them were in the market and about four hundred people in a field four miles off were digging, and the two of those facts are about the same day and about the same weather and about nine hundred yards of hard ground apart at the nearest point, and what a bar is for is that a cart can go round it and a person can go under it, and the two of those are what a Saturday in that district is made of.**

---

**And a boy carried what they drink in a pail with no handle from one end of the high arc to the other, four times between the second hour and the sixth hour, and he has been doing it since the first morning, and about four hundred people in that town know there is a field and cannot name a person in it. It comes out of a ditch that has been dry since Mudmonth, and the ditch is four feet wide and has about two inches of brown water in the bottom of it, and it is not anybody's and has never been anybody's and is not on any sheet, and there is no form on which a field's water can be entered and there has never been one.**

The four days had gone like this. **A man of about thirty-eight in the low arc could not get up on the second morning. A woman of about sixty-four in the low arc was on her feet on the third morning and was on the ground on the fourth. Two children in the low arc had it on the third morning and had it worse on the fourth. And nobody in the high arc had anything on the fourth morning, and the high arc is the same eight hundred people drinking out of the same ditch.** Two people in that field have worked out since that the high arc and the low arc drink out of the same two inches of brown water and that the two arcs are nine hundred yards apart and that the difference between them is the ground the water sits in and not the water.

**And a woman of about forty-two who was a schoolmistress in one of those four villages did the counting of the ill on the fourth morning, and she did it in about eleven minutes, and she did it from the high arc with a hand against her mouth, and she did not call it a count because a count is a figure and a figure is a thing anybody can be shown.**

“**I have got nine days,**” she said, “**and the nine days are all of them in the low arc, and I counted the high arc at the same time from where I was standing and I did not find one, and I have counted the high arc three mornings running and I am not going to count it a fourth because a woman who counts four mornings running is a woman whose figure is wanted for something.**”

“**You counted it three times and I counted it once and gave up,**” the man with the stick said.

“**You counted a line of carts,**” she said. “**There is no line of carts in that field that anybody can walk, and the carts are in two arcs because the ground is soft, and the low arc is where the ground is soft, and the low arc is where the water is worst, and the water is the same water in both arcs and the water is worse in one of them.**”

“**The water is the same water.**”

“**The water is the same water,**” she said, “**and I am not going to say the next thing, because if I say the next thing then eight hundred people in this field will know that the sickness is the water they have been drinking for nine days, and that is a thing a person can be told and cannot be un-told, and there is a great many of the eight hundred who would rather have the sickness and not know that.**”

---

**And two people in that field have said since that a woman of forty-two stood at the top of a bank nine feet high with a hand against her mouth and did not say a sentence that four hundred people in the low arc had been waiting nine days to hear, and that the reason is that the ones who have drunk the most of it are the ones who will go looking for the ditch, and about four hundred people went looking for the ditch between the fourth hour and the seventh hour of that Saturday and nine hundred of them came back and two hundred of them did not stop at the fence.**

So it happened in about an hour and it was not an order. **A man of about thirty with a mallet and nine nails and a plank said a thing about a fire and nine men began to dig three feet down at the low end of the field where the ground is hardest, and four more went eleven yards off to find a place the diggers could not crowd, and a woman of about thirty-three in the low arc brought a pot, and a woman of about twenty-nine brought a second pot, and the two pots were on a fire of sticks by the fifth hour and the fire was out of the ground and the smoke was going nine feet up in still air.**

---

**And the fire needed more wood than that field had, and the man of about thirty said so at the sixth hour without being asked, and said that a cart of wood at Marl was six pence and that there were three carts of it in the field and that four days was about half of what eight hundred people drink at the rate they drink it, and he added the three up in the dirt, because a man who has been in a field four days does not trust himself to say a figure out loud twice.**

Six pence a cart. Three carts. **Six pence and six pence is twopence over, and twopence over is not how a man says it, and the way a man says it is that six pence and six pence and six pence is eighteen pence, and eighteen pence is a shilling and sixpence, and eighteen pence is what about eight hundred people have in the way of boiled water for about four days.**

“**Three carts is about a week,**” the woman of forty-two said.

“**Three carts is about a week,**” the man of about thirty said, “**and the wood at Marl is six pence a cart, and the road to Marl goes out of this field at the head of the track, and the head of that track has about nine stone of oak across it with a chain on it, and it is not mine and I am not going to be the man who took it off, and a cart that cannot get out of this field is a cart that cannot get back into it.**”

**And a bar left standing across a made road is a quarter of a mile of made road in front of every cart that comes to it, and there is a woman four miles south of that town who puts a bar down and takes it up four times a day and who is not paid for the eight months, and the bar at the head of that track is not that bar and is nine stone of oak and it went there nine days ago, and two people in that field have worked out since that about four hundred of the eight hundred have been saying since the second morning that it went there because of the sickness, and nobody in that field has asked anybody whether it went there because of the sickness, and nobody knows.**

---

**And about two hundred people in that field are not speaking to about four hundred, and that is the ninth day of it and nobody in either two hundred has said why, and nine people in the field have worked out since that the two hundred and the four hundred are not the same two hundred and the same four hundred they were on the ninth day, and that a field of about eight hundred people with nine days of it ill is two fields standing next to each other with a ditch between them, and that a ditch with about two inches of brown water in it is the only thing between them that does not move.**

The man with the stick was still in that field at the seventh hour and was told nothing and told nobody anything, and four people in that field have said since that the man who counted it once and gave up stood in a field of about eight hundred people on the fourth day of the worst four days of it and did not count it, and that being the man who did not count it is the closest that man has come to a thing anybody wanted from him in about eleven years, and that he did not know that and has still not been told.

**And two pots stood on two fires at the low end of that field at the seventh hour, and nine hundred people in that field drank boiled water for the first time in nine days, and four hundred of the nine hundred drank it because a woman of forty-two said a thing about a fire, and the rest of the nine hundred drank it because their feet took them to a pot, and nobody in that field has a figure for either of those two numbers and the two are not added to each other and the second one is the bigger one and the smaller one is the one anybody would be told about.**
