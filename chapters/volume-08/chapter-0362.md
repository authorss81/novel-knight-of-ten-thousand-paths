# Chapter 0362

**The first day of Fallowmonth, YR 317, a Thursday, and a market day, and the third hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, in a town of about two thousand people on the salt and in a field four miles off it above a ditch, where a price is in about a fortnight on nine crossings nobody in that field has been shown, and where a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag came off the Marl road at about the fourth hour with a sheet folded once across the middle, and about eight hundred people in that field had not heard of a sheet and about two hundred of them had been awake for four nights.**

Fallowmonth is the first month of a season that nine days of cart off this field is called bridge season, and a yard of nine men in that other county is seven weeks off having a bridge rate or not having one, and about four thousand people in a town on a river four hundred miles off know nothing about either of those two facts and neither does anybody in this field. **A Thursday in that town is a market day and about nine hundred people came into it and the bar at the south end of it went down four times and came up four times for a woman of about forty-one who is paid for four months of the year and is at it on the other eight, and Fallowmonth is the first of those four, and that is what a month is.**

**And about nine hundred forms a month go out of a building two hundred miles off into nine counties in two rounds, in the ordinary way, on a series with six columns and five heads. About four hundred and nine parish rolls go with them, behind them, and have gone for nineteen years, and not one of the two has anything to say about a field four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt.**

**And the market in that town on that Thursday had salt in it and peat in it and a load of glass that had come up the salt road behind nine waggons since the spring, and about nine hundred people came into it and went out of it again, and the price that is coming in a fortnight was talked about in it by four men and by nobody else, and the four men were talking about carts and the rest of that market was talking about a boy of eleven at a school nine pence a week, and neither of those two subjects had a column on the same page and neither of them was on the page the man with the canvas bag was carrying.**

---

**And four nights is not a long time to a person who sleeps, and it is a long time to eight hundred people in a field who cannot, and the fourth night of it in that field went like this. The two hundred and the four hundred did not stop not speaking to one another, and the not speaking was easier on the fourth night than on the first because by the fourth night everybody in both halves had understood that the other half was frightened about the same thing and was saying nothing about it. Two pots stood on two fires at the low end of the field and the fires had gone through nine baskets of sticks in four days and the man who had the mallet said so out loud at the sixth hour on the second day and nobody answered him. A boy went the length of the high arc four times a day and came back with about two inches of brown water in a pail that had no handle and had never had one. A woman of about sixty-four in the low arc was on the ground on the fourth morning and was not dead on the fourth morning and nobody in that field knew the difference between the two and neither did the woman.**

**And about four people in that field have worked out since that the hardest part of a field of about eight hundred people with nine days of it ill is that nobody in it can be told anything, and that a thing told to the two hundred is not a thing told to the four hundred, and that a thing told by a stranger from off the field is not a thing told at all.**

---

**And the sheet that came off the Marl road at about the fourth hour was folded once across the middle and was not waxed and was not a mark, and it was about nine inches by six, and it was the instrument of the Chancellor of the Lantern Council, and it carried a number and a date and a place and a term of ninety days and a ruled space at the foot for a signature, and the place on it was four miles off that town, and the term began on the day the sheet was dated, which was the day it was dated.**

A man of about thirty-one read it standing up at the top of the bank the way a man reads a thing he has not written, and then he put it down on a cart flap and did not hand it to anybody.

“**It says the place is shut,**” he said. “**That is all it says. Ninety days from the day it is dated, and it is dated today, and the ninety days is the first of Fallowmonth to the last day of Wolfmonth, and after that somebody has to look at it again.**”

“**And after that.**”

“**I do not know what happens after that,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**and I have not been told and I have not asked, because I have carried four of these in six years. Two of them were about a price. One of them was about a road. One of them was about a thing in a town I did not go to. And I have never once been asked what happens after the day a sheet is dated, and I have never once wanted to know until this week.**”

---

**And the sheet had nine things on it and there were seven things a person would want to know about a place that is shut, and the seven were not on it, and the nine that were on it were all of a kind that only an office can want.**

It did not say who was inside. **It did not say who brought food to them, or how often, or at whose cost.** It did not say what a person inside it might do and might not do, and it did not say whether a person inside it might walk out to the low end of a field and come back. It did not say what a cart inside it might do, and it did not say what a person outside it might be charged for going in, and it did not say who lifted the thing at the end of ninety days, or whether the person who lifted it had to be the person who put it there.

“**There is no column for any of those on it,**” a man of about forty with a stick said, from nine feet lower down the bank.

“**There is no column for any of those on it,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**because there is no office in this part of the world that has to know any of those seven things. The sheet is a good sheet. The seven is not on it because the seven has never been anybody's job, and I have written to tell them that once, in Fallowmonth two years back, and the answer was a form.**”

And that was the whole of what he said about it, and he said it once. **Two people in that field have worked out since that a man of thirty-one read out nine things off a good sheet and did not read out the seven things that are not on it, and that he read the nine in the order they are on the page and did not put the word shut first, and that a man who has carried four of these in six years has learned to read the bottom line first and to let the top line come on its own.**

---

**And a woman of about forty-two who was a schoolmistress in one of those four villages said the thing that about eight hundred people in that field were going to say about nine hours later, and she said it standing up, and the man of about thirty-one wrote none of it down because he had no column for it.**

“**Then we are not going in,**” she said, “**and we are not going out, and neither of those two is the same as being shut, and eight hundred people in this field will spend the next four days working out which of the three they are, and the answer all of them will come to is the first one.**”

“**What is the first one.**”

“**We are not going in,**” she said. “**Nobody has told us to go in. Nobody has come and said come in. A sheet is not a bar, and a term of ninety days is not ninety days of your life, and a place that is shut by a piece of paper is a place that eight hundred people will sit down in and wait for a man to come, and nine hundred of them have been waiting nine days for somebody to come already and know what the water is.**”

---

**And a man of about thirty-two came up that bank at about the fifth hour, which takes him four seconds longer than sitting down, and he was not asked to come and he had a stick he cannot lean on and a road-marked left palm that was warm at the fourth hour and had been warm since the fourth hour, and a book in his coat that stayed in his coat, and a page in the same coat with nine lines on it and no heads and no columns, and he took the page out at the fifth hour and looked at it and put it back.**

**And about four people in that field have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp came up a bank and took a page out of his coat and looked at nine lines and put it back, and that the nine lines are the same shape as a thing lying on a cart flap nine feet below him, and that neither of the two can hold a person.**

The sheet on the cart flap has a place and a figure and a term. The page in his coat has nine crossings and a price he made up himself in about nine minutes at a bench-board four hundred miles off. **And a man who is good at seeing a load path looked at both of them in about four seconds and understood that the second is a thing he made and the first is a thing somebody made for him, and that the whole of the difference between the two is that nobody has been asked to act on the second one yet.**

He did not say that. **He said one other thing, and he said it standing up, and about two hundred people in that field heard it and about six hundred did not.**

“**Read it out,**” he said.

“**It is a sheet. You have had it in your hand.**”

“**You have had it in your hand and eight hundred people in this field have not,**” he said, “**and there is nobody here who can read it, and nine hundred of them have been awake for four nights, and the first person in this kingdom who says out loud what is on that page is either going to be you or it is going to be somebody in that town at the eighth hour tomorrow. If it is you then eight hundred people hear it from a man who is stood in the field with them. If it is them then eight hundred people hear it at the end of four days, having spent the four days on somebody else's version of it.**”

**And the man of thirty-one did not want to, and read it out.**

He read all of it. He read the number and the date and the place and the term of ninety days, and the seven and twenty days, and he read it in the order it was on the page, and he did not read out what is not on it, because there is nothing to read out.

“**The place is shut,**” the woman of forty-two said.

“**The place is shut,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**from the first of Fallowmonth to the last day of Wolfmonth.**”

**And about four people in that field have said since that a man of thirty-one read a Crown instrument out loud at the top of a bank in the first week of a shut season, and that he read it in the order it was on the page and not in the order it hurts, and that a sheet read out loud to the people it is about is a different thing from the same sheet on a table, and that the whole of the difference is about two hundred people, and that nobody in that field has been told which of the two hundred they are.**

**And nine hundred of the eight hundred were still in that field at the seventh hour, and the field was shut, and nothing else about it had changed, and the two hundred who were not speaking to the four hundred went on not speaking, and the man of about thirty-one put his sheet into his canvas bag and said that he had eight more of them to do over nine miles of road and that the weather was going, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went down that bank in about four seconds and stood at the bottom of it for a while and did not go back to Marl.**
