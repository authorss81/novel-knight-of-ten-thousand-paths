# Chapter 0390

**The twenty-fifth day of Goatmonth, YR 317, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the sixth hour, at the head of a made road four miles west of a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a bar of nine feet of oak goes down at the second hour and comes up at the sixth on four days in seven, and a man of about nineteen is standing at one end of it, and the county does not trade on that Wednesday, and nobody in that town can be shown the man who took a chain off a lane four hundred miles up a road that does not come this way.**

The market was shut and the rope was on its post and the bar at the north end of that market was up. **A Wednesday in that county is a day nobody has ever put anything on, and a market four hundred yards long in a town of about two thousand people is a street on five days in seven and on the other two it is a street with a rope in it and a bar across one end of it, and about two hundred people out of two thousand were out of their houses and one of the two hundred was on a bar four miles off.**

He is about nineteen and has been standing at one end of that bar for ninety-one days. **The bar is nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain in two brackets, and the two brackets are sockets a man of about thirty cut in a kerb in two hours on the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and the man of about nineteen came off a cart four hundred miles off on that morning and nobody sent him and nobody appointed him and he has been there for ninety-one days and he is not on a roll in four counties and is not in a yard's book and is not on the Crown's own series.**

**At the far end of that made road there is a thing nine feet by seven with a stove in it and boards across the top of it and a bench inside it, and the bench is there because four hours standing and four months of them is a thing a body will not do twice in a week, and a man of about nineteen sits on it for about a minute at a time between the carts and stands up again, and two hundred people in that town can describe that bench from a market four miles off without walking out and looking at it, and not one of them can describe the man who sits on it.**

**A ditch four hundred yards west of that bar had two inches of brown water in the bottom of it for a week, and a boy of about eleven with a pail was on the shoulder every hour of the day, and nine yards of soft shoulder on the salt side of that made road takes a loaded cart down about two inches in nine yards, and nobody has ruled that ditch and nobody was going to, and two hundred people went round that quarter of a mile on that Wednesday as they have gone round it every day for ninety-one days, and nobody in that town has ever been told why and there is nowhere in this kingdom to enter a why.**

**A woman of about fifty who has kept one list of nine of them for nineteen years did not come out of her door that morning, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who keeps a list comes out on a day the list can be checked and that a Wednesday has never been that day, and that she has been asked about the list four times in about four months by three different people and has answered all four and has shown it to none of them.**

Nobody is paid for the four hours. **He has a rate for the three days in seven when he is not on that bar and it is four hours a day lower than the one he came off, because the yard is nine men and stands at eight while it is holding that crossing. A man who is not paid for the four months a year that crossing runs and is not paid the four hours either is a man with no rate at all, and he is the only person within nine miles of that town who is working and is not on anything.**

**And the four months that crossing runs are the four months the boats on a coast two days west along that salt road cannot run, and two people in that district have worked out since that a man on a bar for four hours on four days in seven and a man on a slip in the other four months are the same job at two ends of a road, and that neither end of it has anybody in it who is paid for it.**

**The salt on that coast comes out of a place nine miles west of the slip and is raked into a heap by four men and a woman in about a day and a half, and the heap is covered with boards and a weight, and about half of what comes out of a heap in a year is sold and the other half is used by the eleven households that keep the eleven boats, and a bushel is a bushel on that coast and is not a bushel anywhere else and the Crown's price for a bushel is fourpence and the price four houses in that place have paid for nine years is six pence, and those are two prices of one thing and they are not in a column with each other in any book in this kingdom, and nine hundred people on that coast have known both of them since the third week of that month and the four hundred people in the place they come from have known both of them for a year.**

**A market of four hundred yards in that town trades on a Saturday and on a Thursday and on nothing else in the week, and a rope goes up at one end on five days and a bar goes across the other, and the bar is there because a market is a street that is bought for a morning, and four people in that town have said since that the bar at that end has been up more days than the market has been open and that nobody has ever counted which and that a man who counts that is a man with nothing to do.**

**The bar is up on three days in seven all the year and down on four days in seven for the four months, and the man on it is on it at the second hour and off it at the sixth, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow put the numbers of a yard's day in his head in Wolfmonth and got them right, and nobody in that town knows that the four hours on that bar are the same four hours that a yard of nine men four hundred miles off calls the first four hours of a working day.**

**And the salt road is nine yards wide where it is a road and it is a made road for about a mile and then it is a track, and a cart on it makes two decisions a mile and neither of them is about a bar, and four people in that district have said since that a person who has driven nine miles of that road in a month can tell you which of the four hundred yards of that market is the worst to cross in wet and which is the best to load at, and cannot tell you what a person standing at the end of a bar four miles off it is paid and neither can anybody else in that town.**

A man of about thirty-three with a barrow had been coming up that road since the twenty-seventh of a month in Frostmonth. **He came on the twenty-seventh and he did not come again, and nobody on that road has asked him why and nobody in that town has said a word about one barrow fewer on a made road, and about four people in that district have said since that a man who has asked a man with a stick three questions in about a week has stopped coming, and that is the only answer anybody on that road will get.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp came down that made road at about the fifth hour of the twenty-fifth of that month and stood about nine feet from the other end of the bar with his stick he cannot lean on, and the two of them stood nine feet apart for an hour, and neither of them said one word to the other, and what passed between them was nothing at all, and both of them stayed.**

**The word for what they are to each other is brother, and neither of the two of them has said it out loud in two years. A yard of nine men four hundred miles up that county's own road has the man of twenty-five in it and the man of about nineteen is the other one out of the same nine, and the two of them have not said a word to each other since a Sunday morning in Wolfmonth when nine words were said in that yard and both of them stopped there, and nobody in that town knows that a man standing at one end of a bar four miles off a market is standing there because of a yard four hundred miles off, and about two hundred people in that town saw a man with a stick walk past a bar on a Wednesday and did not stop.**

He did not ask him anything about the bar. **He did not ask him how long he had been on it and he did not ask him what he was being paid and he did not ask him whether anybody had told the other end that he was there, and nobody in that town knows that those three questions were available to a man who has been four hundred miles off and back and who asked none of them, and about nine hundred people four hundred miles off a river do not know that a man has been standing at one end of their offer for ninety-one days.**

---

At about the sixth hour the bar came up. **It comes up in about four seconds and it is lifted out of two sockets with a lever and a fulcrum, and about two hundred times in four hours a person comes up that made road and goes past, and nine times in four hours a cart comes up it and a man of about nineteen has to decide in about a second and a half whether the cart can get round the bank or whether it has to go back, and about two hundred people in that town know that a man is standing at the end of a bar four miles off and none of them has ever asked him a question.**

“**You are not going to be here in the spring,**” the man of thirty-two said, at about the sixth hour, to a man of about nineteen.

Nobody had asked him to say it. **He had not said it to anybody in four hundred miles and he said it once and got no answer and walked down the made road, and two people in that district have worked out since that a man with a limp said a true thing about another man's four months at a bar from about nine feet away and was not answered, and that a thing said once is worth more in a district than a thing said twice, and that the two of them were about nine miles apart inside an hour and nobody in that town knows it.**

**And the man of about nineteen said nothing at all, and he has not said one word to anybody since the twenty-fourth of a month in Wolfmonth, and he is about nineteen years old and came four hundred miles off his own trade and there is a sister of his in a county four hundred miles off who is twenty-two and that he has not spoken to her in about a year, and not one of the nine men on that yard has ever asked him about that, and he is going to go home at the end of the fourth month and nobody has told him when the end of it is and nobody is going to.**

**And it was an ordinary week on a day the county does not trade.** A man at one end of a bar was not paid for the four months of the year when nobody crosses, and had not been told when the four months end. **A man with a stick went down a made road at about the sixth hour and did not say one word about any of it, and about two thousand people in that town went home at about the seventh hour and about two hundred of them had been out of their houses and not one of them knew where the man with the stick had gone or what he had been doing in that district for thirteen weeks, and a road is a road and a schedule is a sheet, and the man on that bar is on neither of them, and neither of them has him, and he is going to be on that made road at the second hour tomorrow and on Friday and on Saturday and on the Sunday, and that is the whole of what anybody on that road knows about the four months of the year when nobody crosses it.**
