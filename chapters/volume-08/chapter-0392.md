# Chapter 0392

**The twenty-eighth day of Goatmonth, YR 317, a Saturday and a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a place of about nine dwellings on a river four hundred miles off, and in a yard four hundred yards up the county's own road in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a bar went up on a river four hundred miles off on the same morning, and that a man standing in that yard at the fourth hour was told about it and said nothing.**

## One

The steward came out of his own house at the first hour and there were about nine men with a bar. **It is nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain, and the sockets had been cut in a kerb at the top of the rise in about two hours on the day before by a man of about thirty with a mallet, and the sockets are four inches deep and the road is nine feet wide and the two of them together are the width of the road.** The bar went in at the first hour and it took four men to carry it and four men to lift it and the man of about thirty with the mallet cut the sockets on the morning before and had nothing to do on the morning itself except stand about nine feet off and watch four men of his own neighbours put a thing in a kerb in front of him.

The road out of that place starts on the far bank of the river. **That has been true for longer than anybody there can remember and it is the whole reason a place of about nine hundred people on a flat country of nine miles by four has ever been able to send anything at all to anybody: the road goes where the far bank is, and the crossing is on the river, and the crossing runs four months of the year, and about four hundred of the nine hundred live on the far bank and the rest of us are on this one.**

The steward was fifty-eight years old or near it. He said four things and he said them in the order he had decided on, and he had decided on them in a room in a fortnight.

“**I am refusing the person,**” he said. “**I am refusing him and I am refusing her, and I would refuse a man I liked.**”

Nobody in that place said anything to that, and the bar went up while he was saying it, and about nine hundred people heard him say it because about nine hundred people on that river were within earshot of a road at the first hour on a morning when a bar was going up.

“**A man from a town on the salt wrote our name and our figure on a sheet,**” he said, “**in a field, on the second morning of four days, and nobody told us, and there is a tray two hundred miles off with our sheet in it and somebody in a building is deciding which places they hold open and which ones they let go and they have not told us which we are.**”

“**That is a wrong they have done to us,**” said a man of about nineteen.

“**It is,**” said the steward. “**And I want you to hear the rest of it before you say anything to the others, because you will be one of the ones who says something to the others, and I want you to say the right thing.**”

He turned round and put his hand flat on the bar.

“**A road that will carry a load in those four months is the one thing about nine hundred people on this river have that nobody four hundred miles off has. Not a better road. The only road. Everything we have ever sent out of here has gone on it and everything anybody has ever brought us has come on it, and it is shut for eight months and it is the best thing we have, and in the four months it is not a road at all, it is the only road in the world.**”

“**So the road out of here is shut,**” said the man of about thirty-four, who was not the steward and had walked in from the far bank and had not been in that room in a fortnight.

“**So the road out of here is shut,**” the steward said, “**and it is shut at the top of the rise and not at the water, because at the water a man with a boat can do something and at the top of the rise he cannot. And I want it understood that this is not a threat and I am not the Crown and there is no order in it and I have not been asked for one and I will not take one. It is a bar. It is nine feet of oak and it is up and it stays up and it comes down when we say.**”

“**There was a paper,**” said the woman of about forty.

“**There was a paper,**” the steward said, “**and there are nine lines on it and the eighth of them says a steward may refuse the person, and I am the steward, and the road does not open, and it does not open for the four months after that either, and I have not said that and I have not needed to, because it was on the paper before I read it.**”

And a woman of about thirty, who was standing about nine feet off with a basket on her hip and had no part in it, said one thing and it went round that place inside two days.

“**Then you have shut us off,**” she said. “**From the far side.**”

And the steward said: “**Yes.**”

And that was the end of the first hour and he went back into his own house and the bar stayed up and about nine men of nine hundred went and did their own work at about the second hour. The crossing on the river went on being what it is for the other eight months of the year, which is a river.

## Two

In the fen county the market was full from the first hour and about two thousand people were in four hundred yards of stone, and the road was open over four timber bays four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of it with about forty men on it, and up the road above that street there was a hut beside three bars with a man of about fifty-five inside it, and a yard four hundred yards up the other way had nine men in it and a press standing open in the room at the end of that yard with four sheets in it that have nothing at all on the wrapper.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet came up that road on foot at about the fourth hour with a bag and he was the loading end of a cart that goes out on a fixed day and comes back a fortnight later, and he had nine days of his own round in that bag and about two days of something else, and about two days of something else is a thing that gets put at the end of a bag.

**He had come up that road to ask one question and it was a question about a tide, and it had nothing to do with the sheet.** There is a stretch of a salt road two days west of a town of about two thousand people on the salt where the ground is below the level of the sea and goes under at a state of tide that a cart does not want, and the round that man carried went into that district in cartloads twice a year, and in the last five weeks four of the nine had been shut in forty miles of flat country and two counties of his round had been a fortnight late and one of them had been a month.

“**I am not asking the yard to do anything,**” he said. “**I am asking whether a man can walk it. Not a cart. A man, with a bag, at the turn of the tide, and how long he is stood in the water at the turn.**”

And the man of about fifty-five in the hut beside three bars came down the road for it, because a man who keeps a hut beside three bars is the man anybody in that county asks, and he said that a man can walk it and that the turn gives about twenty minutes and that a man who is not off the flats by the second hour after the turn is standing in nine inches of moving water in a place where a man of about fifty who went in once in Harvestmonth is still not right in his legs. That took about four minutes and it was the whole of what he came for.

Then he did not put the bag down.

“**There is one more thing,**” he said, “**and I have thought about it for two days and I am going to say it because I have said the other one first and I would rather the other one was in the room.**”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“**A place four hundred miles off has put a bar up on its own road. I did not write that down anywhere. I have it because a woman of about forty told a room on the salt a fortnight ago that there was a paper of nine lines on it, and because the parish roll of that place has been carried on a figure since before I was born and the figure did not move, and I asked her why a figure does not move and she said because nobody has a column that could move it. That is the whole of what I have and it is nine lines long and I could not draw you a map of it.**”

“**When,**” the man of twenty-five said.

“**Two days ago at the first hour,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**Bar up. Nine feet of oak. Their own men put it in.**”

“**And there is no order,**” said the man of about sixty, from the heap, without looking up.

“**There is no order,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and there is nobody in this room who could put one there, and I have wanted for about nine days to ask somebody to and I have not asked, and I am telling you that I have not asked, because a man who says he has not asked has told you something and a man who has not said it has not.**”

**And then he said the thing, in front of nine men, in the ordinary way, and the room heard it.**

“**There is a man in this county who could have stood on that rise two days after the bar went up, and he did not go, and I have not asked him about it and I am not going to.**”

---

Nobody said a word, and the boy of fifteen's pen did not stop, and the man of about sixty kept his hand on a lid, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was at the low end of a bench-board about nine feet long with a stick he cannot lean on in his right hand and his left hand flat on the wood about four inches from the end of it. **A bell of nine stone with a band of forty-one rivets in it went at the fourth hour and a half a hundred yards up that county's own road, and it is rung by a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for two years and three months and is still not good at it and by a carpenter who is a hand on that yard and not a keeper of anything, and neither of them is paid by the guild and neither of them is a member of anything, and about four thousand people in that town have never once put the question of who keeps it to anybody.**

The man of about thirty-four waited about as long as a man waits when he has said a thing in a room and is finding out whether it was a thing. Then he said:

“**Is that true.**”

And the man of thirty-two with a limp did not answer that, and he was not asked it a second time, and the man of about thirty-four turned round and put his hand on the board beside the boy of fifteen's and read the column upside down for a moment and then stopped, and the man of twenty-five let him.

“**Then I will go and be useful,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and you can tell me in a year if I was.**”

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp took the page out of his coat at that point and it was the second time in about four months that anybody in that yard had seen it, and he put it on the bench-board and put his finger on the top line and said six things and none of them was about a bar four hundred miles off.**

“**These are nine places and there are no heads on them and I made them in about nine minutes out of a sheet somebody else had ruled. Six of the nine are up for the same four months every year. Two of the nine are up a fortnight either side of those four months and are down for the other ten. The ninth is up all the year and about four people go through it in a week. That is all I have got and I have never written it down and I am not going to now.**”

“**Can we use it,**” said the man of about thirty-four.

“**You can read it,**” the man of thirty-two said, and put it back in his coat.

**And he put it back in his coat in the second place it has been in about four months, and nobody in that yard asked him to write one thing on it then or has asked him since, and about two thousand people in that town went home at about the seventh hour, and the bell went on going a hundred yards up the road and nobody heard it and it did not stop.**
