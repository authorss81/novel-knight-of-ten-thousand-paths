# Chapter 0366

**The fourth day of Embermonth, YR 317, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the eighth hour, in a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a man of about thirty-one who is called Ottmar Vell and has been setting bars on nine places for a month came into a room over a shop in a lane and put a canvas bag on a table and took a page out of it, and about two thousand people in that town do not know his name and about nine hundred people in a field four miles off do not know it either, and neither of those two facts is in any register and there is no form on which a man's name goes against a bar.**

Nothing trades on a Tuesday. **The bar four miles south of that town is up, as it is on four days in seven, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow and a woman of about thirty-eight with a basket go round a quarter of a mile of made road to get past a bar that a man with a canvas bag set on the eighth of Fallowmonth, and about two hundred people in four villages go round it, and the woman of about forty-one at the toll bar down there does not know any of that and would not want to.**

He put the bag down and took out a second thing, which was four sheets about nine inches by six folded once, and he put them on the table and squared the edges with two fingers.

“**I am Ottmar Vell,**” he said. “**I have been putting bars on nine places since the sixth of a month in Fallowmonth and I have six of the nine done and three to go and the weather is going. I have never been in a company of anything and I am not on a list of anything and I have never been asked to be.**”

---

**And a man of about thirty-one said his want out loud, once, to a man with a limp who had not asked him for it, and about two people in that lane have worked out since that a man of thirty-one told a man with a limp the whole of his want in nineteen words that foot at nineteen, and that not one of the nineteen words was about the Crown or about a sheet or about an order, and that the only noun in the nineteen is a list.**

“**I have nine before the weather turns and there is no list that says which are the right ones.**”

**And he said it in that order because that is the order it was in, and a man who has been in a lane for a month and has not been asked anything by anybody has a want with no explanation attached to it and no grievance in it and no speech in it.**

**And the money of it is three pence a bar. Three pence and three pence is six pence, and six pence and three pence is nine pence, and nine pence and three pence is a shilling, and a shilling is the whole of six bars, and nine of them is twenty-seven pence, and he said the twenty-seven without being asked, and about two hundred people in four counties have been told that figure and about nine have paid it.** **A man who has said his wages out loud in a room where nobody had asked has a bar count of nine and a day count of none, and the six days it took him to do six are not on any sheet and are not in the twenty-seven and are not anywhere.**

“**And what is the day for,**” the man of about thirty-two said.

“**There is no day,**” Ottmar Vell said. “**There is a bar. A man who does four in a week and a man who does four in a day are the same four to the office that sent the sheet, and I would like to be a man who does four in a day and there is nothing in the three pence that says which of the two I am.**”

---

**And a man of about thirty-one was in that lane at the fifth hour on a Tuesday in the fifth week of a shut season and he had been in it for a month, and about two people in that lane have worked out since that he sleeps at a lodging over a cooper's shop at the north end of the market and eats at the same table at the same hour and has done since the sixth of a month in Fallowmonth, and that he was a carpenter's mate for about six years before that and was asked to do this in a doorway in about forty seconds by a man he has not seen since, and that he took it because it is work and it is the fourth month of a shut season and there is no bridge work in that district until Embermonth is out, and that nobody has ever asked him whether he wanted to and that is the correct way round.**

He is not a member of anything. **He has no company, no guild, no master, and no sheet, and he is not on a roll in a building about two hundred miles off, and the only record of him in any of the four counties is four small crosses in a pocket book that he wrote himself.** He did not explain the office he works for and he was not asked to and he would not have.

**And the field four miles off has heard of eight of the nine, and about nine hundred of the eight hundred people in it have heard of two, and the two are the one at the head of a towpath that about two hundred people in four villages go round in a quarter of a mile and the one at the head of a cart track that nobody in that field has asked about.**

**And the lane did not trade on a Tuesday either, and the two of them were in a room over a shop that sold nothing that day, and what was in the room was a table, four sheets, a page, a bag, and a man of about thirty-one who had not eaten since the fourth hour, and about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man who has been walking nine miles of road for a month eats at the same table at the same hour and that the reason he had not eaten was that a man with a limp had put a finger on a sheet and had not said anything for four seconds, and that four seconds is a long time to be hungry in.**

**And the four sheets came out of the bag in the order they came and he squared the edges and a man of thirty-two with a limp did not square them and did not need to, and the difference between the two men and four sheets of drawing is that one of them is paid three pence a bar and the other one is not paid anything at all for looking, and that neither of them is paid for the looking in the same way, and that two people in that lane have said since that a man who looks at a drawing for four seconds and a man who looks at a drawing for a quarter of an hour are in the same room and are doing the same work and one of them is being paid by the bar.**

---

**And then he put a page on the table, and it was a page out of a pocket book and it had nine lines on it and no heads on it and no columns and no ruling, and about two people in that lane have said since that a man of thirty-one in a lane on the fourth of a month in Embermonth made a page with nine lines on it and no heads, and that the Crown has been sending sheets with six columns and five heads into nine counties for nineteen years, and that the nine lines and the no heads are the first schedule anybody has made in that country in about nineteen years, and that it was made with a pencil, in a pocket book, on the back of nothing.**

Beside four of the nine lines he had put a mark. **A mark is a small cross and a cross is not a figure and there is no column for it.**

“**I made that on the second of Fallowmonth in about nine minutes at a table in a room over a byre in a village nine miles off,**” he said, “**and I made it out of the second sheet, which is the one that says a place and a number and a term and a signature at the foot, and I put the nine places on it in the order I was given them, and I did not know where the nine came from, and then I found out, and it took me a week.**”

“**You found out where the nine came from.**”

“**I found out that four of the nine came off a price sheet,**” Ottmar Vell said, “**and the other five did not, and I have marked the four, and I would like to have told you that on the second of Fallowmonth and I had nobody to tell.**”

---

**And about four people in that lane have said since that a man of thirty-one marked four of nine places on a page of his own and did not know where the nine came from until a week later, and that the four he marked are the four that are on the Crown's schedule of nine crossings and a price, and that the other five are not on that schedule and have never been on it.**

**And nobody in that lane has ever seen that schedule, and nobody in that lane has ever heard of a list of nine that a woman about nine days off has kept for nineteen years, and the woman is not in the schedule and the schedule is not in her book, and no character in this block has said one word to the other.**

Ottmar Vell spread the four old sheets flat on the table and put his own page on top of them, and he did it in about four seconds and he had clearly done it before.

“**Look at the second lines,**” he said. “**There is one at every one of the nine. At the four I have marked, the second line is a quarter of a mile. It is a mile at two of the other five. It is four miles at one of the other five. It is not on the sheet at all at the last of the five.**”

“**And which is the last of the five.**”

“**The head of a cart track that runs up off the flats,**” Ottmar Vell said, “**about nine miles west of here, and the second line at that one is nine miles, and there is a bar across the head of that track and it has been there about a month and it is not mine, and a man with a barrow told me in a lane on the eighth of Fallowmonth that somebody had gone up that track in the fortnight before and he did not think it was worth saying, and I wrote it on the back of a sheet in my pocket book that night, and I have been carrying that in a bag with a canvas cover since the second of Fallowmonth.**”

---

**And about four people in that lane have worked out since that a man of thirty-one who has been putting bars on nine places for a month has worked out in about nine minutes with four drawings a hundred and forty years old that the four places somebody chose out of about four hundred were chosen off a list of places where a person pays, and that every one of the four has a way round it in a quarter of a mile, and that the five that were not chosen are the five where a person cannot get round them, and that nobody sent him to any of the nine and that he had to stand at all nine of them to find it out, and that the standing is not paid and is the whole of what he has.**

“**So the four are the ones where it costs a quarter of a mile,**” the man of about thirty-two said.

“**The four are the ones where it costs a quarter of a mile,**” Ottmar Vell said, “**and about two hundred people a week go round them and nobody has ever complained because a quarter of a mile is a thing you do not complain about. And I have three left to do and three of my three are on that side of it, and I am going to do my three, and the weather is going, and I am not asking you about the fourth because the fourth is not one of my three.**”

“**There is a field at the end of that track.**”

“**There is a field at the end of that track,**” Ottmar Vell said, “**and there is a bar across the head of it, and I have not been up it, and it is not mine, and nobody in this lane has told me whose it is and I have not asked and I am not going to ask, and I have been carrying it in a pocket book since the second of Fallowmonth, and the second line at that one on the old sheet is nine miles round, and I have not got a cross against it because I have not been there.**”

**And about four people in that lane have said since that a man of thirty-one finished his want in that lane on a Tuesday in the fifth week of a shut season, and that he did not ask a man with a limp for anything, and that a man with a limp did not give him anything, and that the only thing that passed between them in about half an hour was four drawings a hundred and forty years old and a page of nine lines with four crosses on it, and that the two of them are now the only two people in that country who know about the second lines, and that about two thousand people in that town know about a price that is in effect, and that none of those three facts is on anybody's paper.**
