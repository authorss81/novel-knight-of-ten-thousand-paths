# Chapter 0365

**The twenty-fifth day of Fallowmonth, YR 317, a Sunday, and not a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the eighth hour, in a field above a ditch four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a man of about fifty-four died in the night in the low arc and about two hundred people in that field came out of the four hundred to look at him and two hundred stayed where they were, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp was in that field and had come down that bank at the fourth hour and did not know anybody in it and was known by about nine of the two hundred.**

Nothing trades on a Sunday. **The bar four miles south of that town is up and has been up for four days of Fallowmonth, which is the first of the four months it is down in, and the woman of about forty-one who puts it down has not put it down since the first of the month because a bar that a cart goes round is a quarter of a mile of made road that a cart goes round, and there have been no carts.** Two thousand people in that town are in their houses and a yard of nine men in a town on a river four hundred miles off is at a bench-board with a press in a room at the end of it and a lime heap against a wall, and about eight hundred people in a field are in it.

**And the sickness in that field went from about nine days of the eight hundred in the second week of Harvestmonth to two a day in the third week of Fallowmonth, and nobody in that field has said the word for that and nobody in that field will be the first, and the word is not on any sheet and is not in any of nine hundred returns and is not going to be.**

---

**And the wood in that field ran out on about the seventh day of it and there have been two fires since and no third, and the two fires are at the low end and the smoke of them goes nine feet up in still air and comes down again over the same nine yards, and about two hundred people in the two hundred drink out of the ditch because the pots are on the fire for the four hundred and not for them, and that is not anybody's doing and is what a pot is.**

A Sunday in that field is a Sunday. **The carts stand in two arcs and nobody is on a cart. A boy went the length of the high arc with a pail and came back with about two inches of brown water in it. The schoolmistress had a list of the ill on the back of a cart flap in pencil and rubbed two names out of it with her thumb and did not put anything in the place where the two names were.** Two hundred people and four hundred people sat in two fields nine hundred yards apart on a Sunday, and the nine hundred yards between them had a ditch in it with about two inches of brown water in it, and the ditch was dry in Harvestmonth and was not dry now.

**And a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag had been at five of the nine places by the twenty-fifth of Fallowmonth, and had four left.**

---

**And a woman of about thirty-four of the four hundred, who is the woman a man with a limp later carried a dead man's coat across a field to, had been in the low arc for five weeks and had a cart with a handcart's handle on it, and a woman who has not spoken to a man of the two hundred for twenty-nine days carried a dead man's coat nine hundred yards across a field at the fourth hour on a Sunday and did not stop in front of him, and a thing a person does not do on purpose is a thing they have been doing for twenty-nine days.**

**And the two hundred watched from the top of the bank and the four hundred watched from the low arc, and the nine hundred yards between them had a ditch in it with about two inches of brown water in it, and the coat went across the ditch and not round it, and nobody in that field has said why and about four people in it have worked out since that a ditch is two hundred steps in the dark and about nine hundred in the light, and that the woman did not have to cross it at all.**

**And a man of about fifty-four died in the low arc between the third hour and the sixth hour on a Sunday, and the two hundred and the four hundred have not been speaking for twenty-nine days, and the four hundred came out and the two hundred did not.**

A woman of about fifty-nine was on the ground beside him at the fourth hour with her hand on his face, and a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag was in that field and came over.

**And the man on the ground had a good coat on him, and the good coat was the only good coat in that field, and it was a fustian coat of the kind a man wears to a market four hundred miles off, and it had been on him for three weeks and everybody in the low arc knew it was not his and nobody had said so and everybody in the high arc had noticed and nobody had said so either.**

“**He has had that coat since the cart,**” a woman of about thirty-one said, in the four hundred, not to anybody.

And a man of about thirty-four of the four hundred came across the field at the fifth hour to a man of thirty-two with a limp standing at the bottom of the bank, and he did not say the man's name and he did not say what the coat was and he said five words.

“**Take that for the body.**”

“**I have nothing to put a body in,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and the two hundred have carts and the four hundred have got nothing, and you are the only person in this field that came off it, so take it, and I am not going to stand here and be thanked for it.**”

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp took a dead man's coat in four seconds because somebody put it in his hand, and a man who has been in this trade for eleven years and has been on a political errand for a year does not stop a stranger in a field to ask where a coat came from, and he took it, and he gave it to two men of the two hundred to lay the body out on, and all of that took nine seconds and two hundred people watched it.**

**And a man with a limp gave a dead man's coat to a woman of about fifty-nine to lay her husband out on, and at the fourth hour he did not know whose it was and at the sixth hour he did, and about two hundred people in that field watched nine seconds of it and two of them worked out what it was and neither of the two said so.**

---

**And a woman of about forty-two who was a schoolmistress in one of those four villages told him at about the sixth hour, standing at the bottom of that bank, and she was not asking him for anything and she was not telling him anything about the water or the sickness or the sheet or the bars or the schedule.**

“**That coat is not his,**” she said.

“**I know.**”

“**You do not know,**” she said. “**You know it is not his. You do not know whose it is, and I am going to tell you whose it is, and I am telling you because there is a man in this field who has been going about for two days without it and he has not stopped, and I am not asking you for anything and I am not going to say anything else about it.**”

“**All right.**”

“**It belongs to a man in the high arc who is alive,**” the woman of forty-two said, “**and he is about thirty-eight and he lent it on the ninth day on the cart because it was cold on a cart and he has not had it back, and he has been looking for it for two days and he thinks it went with the carts, and he does not know that it came off a man who died in the low arc and that about two hundred people in the four hundred have known for three weeks whose it was.**”

---

**And he did not ask. He did not ask her who the man was, and he did not ask her how a coat that was a man's coat in the high arc came to be on a man in the low arc for three weeks, and he did not ask her what a man of thirty-eight ought to be told and by whom and in what words, and he did not ask her anything, and two people in that field have worked out since that a man who has been the one in a room with a question in his mouth for eleven years stood at the bottom of a bank on a Sunday afternoon in Fallowmonth and had four questions in his mouth and did not ask one of them.**

**And about four people in that field have said since that he did not ask because a man on a political errand does not have the standing, and that the standing is not courage and is not a rule anybody made, and that it is the ordinary shape of a thing, and that it is the shape of this one, which is that a woman who has been in a field of eight hundred people for a month is not a person he can put a question to about a coat without becoming, for four seconds, the second thing that has come into that field in three weeks and told her about her own business, and that he knew that, and that knowing it is what stopped him.**

“**Why are you telling me,**” he said, which was not one of the four.

“**Because somebody has to,**” the woman of forty-two said, “**and I have no stake in your errand and no stake in that sheet and no stake in those bars and none in whatever it is you have written on a page in your coat, and if there is anybody in this field with no stake at all it is me, and that is why.**”

**And she went back up the bank, and about two hundred people in that field have worked out since that a woman of forty-two who will not be the one who counts went up a bank and left a man standing at the bottom of it, and that is what she did about him, and she has not done anything else, and that he did not thank her and she did not want him to.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp walked about nine hundred yards across that field at the sixth hour, which is a quarter of a mile and takes him eleven minutes, and found a man of about thirty-eight in the high arc who had been going about for two days, and put a fustian coat into his hands, and did not ask him a single question, and about two hundred people in the low arc watched him do it from about the middle of the field.**

“**Where did you get it.**”

“**Off him,**” the man of about thirty-two said, and did not say who he was, or off whom, or in which half, or how long he had had it.

And the man of about thirty-eight did not say thank you. **And about four people in that field have said since that a man of thirty-eight did not say thank you to a man with a limp who had just walked a quarter of a mile across a field of ill people in a Sunday to give him back a coat, and that the reason is that the man of thirty-eight did not know there was anything to be thanked for, and that he had been given back a coat, and that a coat is a coat and not a kindness.**

**And what a man of thirty-two with a limp got out of it was that he did not know, and could not ask, and had been corrected anyway, and that about two people in that field have said since that being corrected is not the same as being forgiven and that there is nobody in that field who is going to keep a count of which of the two he is owed, and that a person who has been wrong in a room and cannot put it right is not a person who has learned anything and is a person who has been shown the shape of the thing he cannot do, and that the shape of it is about four questions long and every one of them was about somebody else's coat.**
