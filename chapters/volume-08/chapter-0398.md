# Chapter 0398

**The third day of Mudmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, at a beck about two miles up the county's own road off the north end of that market, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where two rotted bays of a cart span were out and about forty men were on it and this was the nineteenth morning of that work and the last morning of it on which a man can stand on a staging, and the press in the room at the end of the yard four hundred yards back up that road stands open at about forty-nine weeks with four sheets in it that have nothing at all on the wrapper, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that about forty men are on that span.**

## One

**Mudmonth is the worst month for roads in that county and the year it is worst is the year the ground is soft from about the fourth week of the second month, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar who has done four jobs in that county said the two figures out loud on the first morning of that work, and they were the whole job at about forty days and about nineteen of them days that a man can stand on a staging in, and this was the nineteenth of them and there is no weather left in this month for the twentieth.** Nobody argued with him and nobody in that yard has ever argued with that man about a number.

The span is two bays of oak over a beck and the rotted thing was not the middle and it was never the middle. **It was the two ends, where the oak is let into a masonry abutment at a level of about nine inches above the beck, and the ends take the water and the ends take the frost, and the last man who built it put the tenons in dry and the man who pointed it put lime in the joints at both ends because there was lime there, and two bays have gone soft at the foot and a cart that went over one of them in the first week of this month went into about nine inches of water and a boy of about eleven was standing on the shoulder with a pail for about two hours and the cart came out of it on its own and nobody was in it.**

The work is a sill and a course and two bays and a bar. **There is no drain under that beck worth the name and there is nobody's claim on the beck and the beck is in a cutting under a mill road and the mill has been a store for about nine years and the man who owned the mill is in another county and has not been written to.**

**And the other twenty-one days out of the forty were not nothing either, and they are all of them days that come after this one.** A man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar worked out what a man does on a day he cannot stand on staging in the fourth month, and it is not nothing, and it is the whole reason the forty is forty: the timber is cut and squared in a shed nine feet by seven at the near end, the iron is drawn out and the straps are forged and a hole is drilled in each tenon by hand because there is no power at that beck, the gravel is barrowed and the sand is dug out, the joints are pointed when there is a day to point them in and the pointing waits for a day like this one, and about four men cut kerb for the sockets on the days when cutting kerb is indoors work. **A man who has done about nine bridges in that county said on the first morning that the twenty-one days are the job and the nineteen are the bridge, and nobody in that yard has ever had a better sentence about a month of the year and nobody has ever written it down.**

**The money is the timber and the lime and the iron, and nobody went outside that county for any of it, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet who was in that yard for about nine minutes in the second week of that month asked what the oak was going to cost and was not told, and wrote nothing down, and said on his way out of that gate that he had not expected to be told.**

Two pieces of oak at twenty-three pence and nineteen, a piece of fir at eleven, and a pound of lime at four. Twenty-three and nineteen is forty-two, forty-two and eleven is fifty-three, fifty-three and four is fifty-seven, and fifty-seven pence is four shillings and ninepence. **That is the whole of the timber and the lime and the iron on that span and it is not one hour of the forty days and not the days of weather and not the cart that went into the beck and it was said out loud on the staging before the total and not after, and about forty men on that span had the parts before they had the whole of it, and that is a yard and not a rule.**

## Two

**Nine men out of that yard are not all on that span and two of the nine are on it every day that it can be worked, and one of the two is the man of twenty-five and the other is a man of thirty-two with a limp and a stick he cannot lean on, and neither of the two of them was put on it by anybody, and the nine have not said one word about which two, and about two thousand people in that town on a market Saturday have not noticed which two either.**

**The man of thirty-two with a limp was the man of twenty-five's brother, and neither of the two of them had said so in that yard in about two years, and neither of them said it on a span, and there are about forty men on a span and a span is the worst possible place in this county to say a thing like that.**

He cannot get up a bank. **He has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight and he is not going to, and getting up is about four seconds longer than sitting down, and he is not going to do it in this month in this mud in front of forty men.** So the far abutment is his and the far abutment is at the level of the ground, and the far abutment is where the tenons go in, and the far abutment is nine feet by four and has a stone in it that has to be out and a footing under it that has to be looked at before anybody decides anything.

**And the footing under it was sand, and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said so on the second morning in about nine seconds and then said the other half of it, which is that a bay that goes on sand goes again, and that a mason would want about nine days and a stone, and that nobody had said a word to a mason and that there is no mason in that county in the fourth month and the nearest one is about eleven days off.** They dug the sand out in about two days and the far abutment came down four inches onto a gravel the colour of a wet road, and the gravel was about two feet deep and it was the only good thing on that beck and nobody on that span had known about it. **The man of about thirty-two with a limp got the gravel out with a barrow from the near side, because he could not get up the far bank, and the barrow came down four hundred yards of about nine degrees and a man of about nineteen came and took the other end of it, and neither of the two of them said one word while they did that, and about four men took the empty barrows back up.**

**The man of twenty-five set the line from the near bank and he set it every morning at about the second hour before anybody else was on the span, and he set it the nineteen mornings there were to set it on, and he took a sight off the near end of the span to the far one and wrote a figure in a book and the figure was a number of inches and the book took it because a figure is a thing set.**

On the nineteenth morning of work, at about the fourth hour, a bay was coming home on four men and a barrow and a line and it was a hand's width out at the far end, and everybody at the near end could see that it was out and nobody at the far end could.

**And a man stood at the far end with his left hand behind his back and his right hand on a stick he cannot lean on and he did not do the thing, and about four men on a barrow waited for about eleven seconds for a man to tell them it was right, and then the man of twenty-five said from the near bank that it would do, and it did do, and the bay went in a hand's width out at the far end, and the far end of that span is a hand's width out and will be a hand's width out for about two hundred years, and nobody has ever been told that and nobody ever will be.**

**Nobody said a word about those eleven seconds. There were about forty men on that span and not one of them saw anything happen in them, and the man of about thirty with a mallet was on the far abutment the whole of it and he did not see it either because he was holding a tenon, and that is the last that anybody on that span ever had to say about those eleven seconds and they did not say it to each other and they did not say it to anybody.**

## Three

The bar went in the same afternoon at about the sixth hour. **It is nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain, and there are two of them, one at each end of the span, and each one sits in two sockets in a kerb and each pair of sockets took about two hours and a half to cut in the stone and the two of them are four hundred yards apart with a beck in between and a bay of oak on each side of the water.**

The near one was cut by the man of about thirty with a mallet and the far one was cut by the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar, who is the only man within four days of that county who can cut a socket true in a kerb, and they are not the same man and about four thousand people in that town have never been told which is which.

**And here is the thing about a bar that is two bars, and it took two men who did not speak about it to find it and neither of them said it out loud.** A man at the near end cannot see whether the far one is true. There is a bay and a beck and about four hundred yards and a rise at the far end, and at four hundred yards in Mudmonth with a beck running, a man at the near end sees a shape and not a line. So a man has to stand at both, and one of them has to take the other's word for it, and there is no way round that and there never has been and it is not a fault in a bar and it is not a fault in a yard.

**At about the sixth hour the near bar went down and the far bar went down inside about two minutes of each other, and a made road came open over a beck in a market county in the worst month for roads in the year, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar stood at the far one and the man of twenty-five stood at the near one and neither of them could see the other, and each of them put his hand on his own bar and waited, and about eleven seconds later a cart went over and it did not move, and that is the whole of the discovery and nobody in that county has written it down.**

## Four

**And what the two of them had in nineteen mornings was this: they never arrived at the same hour and neither of them said why, and the nine men had worked it out between themselves in about four days without a word being said to either of them about it, and the man of about nineteen was on a bar four miles off a town on the salt on four days in seven in this month and was in that yard on the other three, and he did not say which three, and nobody asked him, and about nine men on that yard had decided in about four days not to ask him.**

**And they did not follow one another.** He was in that yard in Mudmonth and he had asked, in the third week of a month in Goatmonth, whether the yard would put a span back over a beck two miles up that road, and the man of twenty-five's want is unchanged and has not changed once in four months and nobody has improved on it. **He was at the far end of that span at about the fourth hour and about the sixth hour and nowhere else in nineteen mornings, and the man of twenty-five was at the near end of it and nowhere else, and there is no part of those nineteen mornings that was the one of them and there is no part that was not the other,** and about four thousand people in that town do not know that either of those two men was on it and about two hundred of them were stopped at three bars up that county's own road that morning by a man of about fifty-five who said the same thing he says every morning, which is that it is a road and a bar and a man.**
