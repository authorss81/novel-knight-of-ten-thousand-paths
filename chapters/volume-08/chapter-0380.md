# Chapter 0380

**The twenty-fifth day of Frostmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, in a market street of four hundred yards and nine hundred set stones in a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where about nine hundred people were standing in it with a rope at one end of it and a bar at the other, and four miles west of that market at the head of a made road a man was standing at one end of a bar with his back to a kerb, and about two thousand people in that town know about the first of those two things and none of them know about the second.**

That bar is still up, on four days in seven, the way it has been all through. **The woman who puts it down and takes it up has been at it for about eleven years and is at it on the four and on the eight, and about two hundred people in four villages went round that quarter of a mile that morning as they have gone round it since the eighth of a month in Fallowmonth, and there has never been a second bar on that road and there is not one now.**

The market shut at about the seventh hour. **Nine hundred people came into it and about four hundred yards of clay and stone and nine hundred set stones in it and a rope and a bar and about four hundred people who were somebody else's business, and nobody stood in the middle of it, and a market four hundred yards long in a county that trades on a Saturday and on a Thursday is a street on every other day of the week and this was a Saturday.**

**And the man of about nineteen has a bench inside a shed nine feet by seven and a stove in it and boards across the top of it, and the bench is there because a man cannot stand for four hours for four months, and he sits on it for about a minute at a time between the carts and stands up again, and two people in that district have said since that a bench is the whole of the second thing on that road and that a bench cost nothing and that a bench is the only part of that crossing anybody could describe from a market four miles off without going and looking.**

**And the seventh of the nine lines of a paper that went four hundred miles off in a bag with the woman of about thirty-five says what happens if the person at this end is taken off, and it says the road shuts on the day the person goes and does not open again for that season, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that there is a condition on a road four hundred miles off which is a person, and that a man has been standing at one end of that condition for a month, and that the person has not been told by anybody that he is the condition.**

**And the road is open. It has been open since the twenty-fourth of Wolfmonth, and it is open in the ordinary way, and a cart can go up it from the Marl road and a cart can come down it, and a man stands at one end of it from the second hour to the sixth on four days in seven, and the road is not carrying very much and it is carrying some.**

---

**And nobody set a rate. A road can be open for a month and not have a price on it, and this is the first one anybody in that district has ever been able to point at, and there is a price on a Crown's own sheet for a cart at nine crossings and there is no price on this one anywhere, and the two are not in a column with each other and are not added to each other, and a toll is not a wage.**

The man on that bar is about nineteen years old and came off a long road in a handcart and he has been on it for a month. **He is not paid for the four hours and he is paid at the yard's rate for the three days in seven that he is not on the bar, and that rate is four hours a day lower than it was in the month before he left, because the yard is nine men and is now eight for four hours on four days, and a person who is not paid for the four months of the year the crossing runs and is not paid the four hours either is a person nobody in this kingdom has a rate for, and he is the only person within nine miles who is working and is not on anybody's paper.**

**And a man of about thirty-three with a barrow stopped at the bar on the nineteenth of that month and asked him what a cart cost to go over, and the man of about nineteen said that he did not know, and the man with the barrow said that he had asked because he had a figure in his head and wanted to see whether the two of them were the same, and the man of about nineteen said that he did not have a figure and had never been given one and did not want one.**

“**Then what are you doing here.**”

“**Standing at the end of a bar,**” the man of about nineteen said, “**four hours a day, four days in seven, and I have not got a figure for it and nobody has offered me one and I have not asked, and you can go up the road and tell that to whoever you like and it will not change anything.**”

---

**And a woman of about forty was in that market on the twenty-fifth of that month, at the fourth hour, at the north end of it, and she had been in that town for about nine weeks and had not gone back, and about nine hundred people on a river four hundred miles off had sent one person out once and had not sent anybody out again and had not sent for her, and she had not asked to be sent for.**

A man of about thirty-four with a sheet came across that market to her at about the fifth hour. **He has a form and it is a good form and it has six columns and five heads, and two thousand people in that town do not know that there is a room two hundred miles off in which a sheet with the back of it written on has been in a tray for nine days with a strap over it, and nobody in that room has turned it over, and it does not know what it is.**

“**The offer is still standing,**” she said, before he had said anything.

“**I know it is.**”

“**Then you have nine weeks to have answered it,**” she said, “**and you have not, and the road at the far end of it has been open for a month, and the road is open and the offer is not answered, and those are two different things and a bar is a much easier thing to look at.**”

**And the bar a woman of about forty was talking about is four miles west and is not the one at the south end, and about two thousand people in that town have two bars in it in their heads, and one of the two of them has a person on it, and neither of the two is on anybody's sheet.**

---

**And then the question came, and it came from a man of about fifty-four who burns lime at a kiln two miles off, and it was the ordinary question and it was asked in a market and not in a room, and about two thousand people within nine feet of it heard it.**

“**What is the man on that bar being paid,**” he said.

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp was standing nine feet off with a stick he cannot lean on, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet was nine feet off with a form, and a woman of about forty was between them, and about nine hundred people in a market of four hundred yards heard a man of about thirty-four answer it and about eight hundred of them did not know who he was.**

He did not give a figure. **He said that he had not got it, and that he was not going to work one out in a market on a Saturday, and that a rate for a man on a bar at the end of a road four hundred miles off is not a thing anybody in this town is going to be told about, and he said that in the order of a man saying that a thing is not his to say and not in the order of a man keeping a thing back.**

“**You are the one who sent him.**”

“**I am the one who sent nine men to a yard four hundred miles off and was refused by nine of them and did not send the order again,**” the man of thirty-two said, “**and I have not got a figure for a man on a bar and I am not going to make one up, and a figure I make up is a rate and a rate is a thing a man can be paid in, and I have watched what a rate does to a thing and I am not doing it to a person.**”

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp said no to a figure in a market of nine hundred people, about a man of nineteen standing at a bar four miles west, and he did not say why, and the woman of about forty did not say why either, and the two of them had known each other for about nine weeks.**

---

**And he still has not answered it. The offer is a road, theirs, and four months, and a person at this end, and the person at this end has been on that bar for a month without anybody having said to the other end that he is there, and two people in that town have said since that the road is open and the offer is unanswered and that both of those are the same fact, and that a road without a person at this end does not carry, and a person at this end without a road does not either, and about nine hundred people four hundred miles off do not know that a man has been standing at one end of their offer for a month.**

---

**And two people in that market have said since that a man of about nineteen on a bar four miles west of that market is not paid for four months of a year, is paid at a rate four hours a day lower than the one he left, and is on no roll in four counties, and that there is a fourth thing beside those three and not one of the four concerns him, which is that a sheet two hundred miles off has a place on a river four hundred miles off written on the back of it in the hand of a woman he has never met, and that about two thousand people in that town could not be shown any one of the three that concern him and would not know which room to ask in.**

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet said that the man on that bar is not on any roll in four counties and is not on the Crown's own series and is not in the yard's book, and that the yard's book takes a thing set and a term is not a thing set, and that there are four sheets in a press in a room at the end of that yard that have nothing at all on the wrapper and that nobody in that county has ever read the wrapper.**

---

**And about nine hundred returns were written in four days in a field four miles off that town in the third week of Fallowmonth, by people in a field, with a place in the first column and a figure in the second and four columns with no head on them, and those sheets are somewhere between a lane in that town and a building with four floors and seven presses in it two hundred miles off, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls behind that building carry the same four village names out of that field and have been carried every month for nineteen years and have never once been amended by anybody for anything, and not one person in the eight hundred of that field has been told what is done with the second column of a sheet.**

**And the round comes round again in a month whether anybody is ready or not and nobody rings, and about two thousand people in that town went home at about the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth of that month, and about nine hundred of them had been in four hundred yards of stone, and a man of about nineteen was still at the end of a bar four miles west and was not on anything and was not paid for any of it.**
