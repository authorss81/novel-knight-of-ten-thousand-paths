# Chapter 0396

**The nineteenth day of Thawmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, in a field four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt, and in eight other rooms between four miles and nine miles of that field, where about four hundred people have been told what a sheet is and about eleven hundred people have not been told and are not on any of the sheets and are not on any list of the four hundred.**

## One

The field is above a ditch and it has been above that ditch since the third week of a month in Fallowmonth. **There are about eight hundred people in it and about two hundred carts and about eleven tents that are not tents and a tilt for boiling water that is mended with a plank and a nail, and there is a boil in it every week of the third month and it does not kill anybody now and it killed nobody then either, and about four hundred yards off the middle of it there is a hole that a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag put a post in on the second morning of four and that post is the only thing in that field with a Crown office's number on it, and he put it in because a woman of about forty-two who counts faster than a man with a stick asked him to.**

Nobody in that field is a refugee in anybody's mouth. **They are four villages' worth of people who are living in a field above a ditch because there is nowhere else to be, and about four hundred yards of the field is wet to the knee after rain and the rest of it is cart track and about two hundred carts is not two hundred carts' worth of ground, and about eight hundred people and about two hundred carts and a field of about eleven acres is the arithmetical fact and nobody has ever said it out loud in that field because nobody there is a person who says arithmetical facts out loud.**

The sheets went into a tray with a strap over it on the sixteenth day of a month in Frostmonth, went two hundred miles off the salt, and were read against a list of places that somebody keeps open.

## Two

**About nine hundred returns, written in four days, by about four hundred people, on a form with a place in the first column and a figure in the second and four columns with no head on them.** A figure of nothing is not a figure of no people. One of the sheets has an empty second column because about nine men stood on a hurdle for half an hour two days north, and nobody marked it, and marking it would have been a fifth thing on a form with four columns.

**And on the nineteenth day of a month in Thawmonth, about four hundred people were told what a sheet is, in nine rooms, in nine places, by nine people who were not the ones who read it, and the nine people were a woman of about forty-one who takes a toll at one of nine places on the flats, a woman of about fifty who has kept a list of nine of those places for nineteen years, a man of about thirty-three with a barrow, the woman of about forty-two who counts faster than a man with a stick, a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar, and four more that the field knew, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was in one of the nine rooms and told none of it.**

**Nobody read anything out. Each of the nine said it in their own trade and then sat down and let about nine or about forty people ask what they wanted, and there are nine different sets of questions because there are nine rooms, and the ninth room had a question in it that four of the others had not.**

## Three

**And the register was corrected in nine places in nine rooms, and what was corrected is different in every one of the nine, because the nine problems are different, and the correction is a second hand on the sheet in a margin, and the sheets are not destroyed and are not withdrawn, and they go back into the round, and the round comes back in about a month.**

Here are the nine, and they are as different as they are.

**One, in the second room.** A place in the first column and nothing at all in the second, and the fix was the nine men written in the margin in a second hand with a note that they stood on a hurdle for half an hour, and the second column left empty, **and nobody in that room has ever seen a form with a place in it for a man who is standing somewhere and not going anywhere.**

**Two, in the fifth.** The same place on two sheets with two different figures, off two different mornings of the four, and the fix was both figures kept with the morning written beside each of them, **and neither one struck out, because a person who strikes out a figure has decided which morning was true and the woman of about forty-two who counts faster than a man with a stick was not in that room and she is the only person in four counties who could have.**

**Three, in the seventh, and it is the one that was done in full and the rest of this account is it.** A place spelled two ways.

**Four, in the fourth.** A figure in the second column that is a count of carts, because the person who put it there was asked for people and did the arithmetic with what was in front of her, and the fix was the word carts written underneath it — **a fifth thing on a form with four columns, and there is nobody two hundred miles off who has to accept it and nobody here who has to refuse it.**

**Five, in the third.** A place and a figure on one line with two other places and two other figures on the same line, because there was not enough paper and about nine hundred sheets go out in a month, and the fix was none, and the reason the fix was none is that the paper ran out at about the fourth hour of the second morning and four women went and got more out of a house two miles off and did not tell anybody and got the credit for it in nine days instead.

**Six, in the sixth.** A figure in the second column that is smaller than the figure in the column next to it, and there is no column next to it, and there are no heads on any of the four, and the fix was none and the room got up and looked at the sheets and sat down again.

**Seven, in the first.** One place on four sheets in four rooms, because the round gave a sheet to every household that asked and one place is a great many households of one place, and the fix was one of the four marked as the sheet and the other three marked as the same place, in the same second hand, **and every household in that place is now on it once and three times over, which is not a thing anybody can undo in a margin.**

**Eight, in the eighth.** A figure in the second column that is a figure of households and not a figure of people, and nobody in that room knows which it is, and the woman who wrote it is nine miles off and two days into a month that is wet underfoot. The fix was the word household written under it in a hand that was not sure.

**Nine, in the ninth.** A sheet with nothing at all in the first column and a figure in the second, and not one person in that room knows whose figure it is, because the person who put it there is one of about nine hundred and was not in that room and has not been in it since the second morning of four. The fix was none, and the room wrote the figure on the back of a piece of paper and put the paper in a woman's apron pocket and said out loud that she was to hand it back when the person came, and she is not going to remember.

## Four

The seventh room was a kitchen with a table in it and about forty people in it and it was the third room the woman of about forty-one had been in that day, and a sheet was on the table with a place in the first column written two ways, one of them in a hand that was copying and one of them in a hand that was not.

**The woman whose place it was is about thirty-four and she had been in that kitchen for the whole of the two hours and she had not said anything for the first hour and a half, and then she said, in about nine minutes, that it is not written the way it is said where she is from, and that it is not written the way it is said two valleys over either, and that the way it is written on both of those sheets is the way it is said in the town where she bought a churn in the spring.**

“**Then we do not know it,**” said the man of about thirty-three with the barrow.

“**We know four ways,**” she said, “**and I have heard about four ways and I have watched four people read it four ways this morning, and I am not going to sit here and pick one out of a room because somebody wants a column to be right.**”

And a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar, who was in that kitchen because he had walked nine miles to be in it, said that a name is the only part of that sheet that a person can be wrong about, and that a figure cannot be wrong about a name, and that if the four ways go back in the round then in a month somebody two hundred miles off is going to read four ways and pick one, and that there is no version of this in which somebody there does not pick one.

Nobody argued with that. **The four ways went on the sheet together in four hands in about nine minutes, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was asked, by a woman of about thirty-four in that kitchen, what the people two hundred miles off were going to do with it, and he said the only true thing he had, which is that they were going to do whatever they were going to do and that there was nothing anybody in that room could do about it and that he had been in the room where it was said and that he had not been in the room where it was done.**

“**Then why are you here,**” somebody said.

“**Because I have been in nine rooms in four months and I am in this one,**” he said, and nobody said anything about that, and about forty people in that kitchen went on asking about the four ways and about the eight other rooms and about the eleven hundred.

---

And the tilt that boils the water in that field was mended again at about the fourth hour of that day, and it had been mended six times since the third week of a month in Fallowmonth and the mending is a plank and nails and a pound of lime to close the holes where the nails go in. **A plank at eighteen pence, four nails at a penny, and a pound of lime at six. Eighteen and four is twenty-two, twenty-two and six is twenty-eight, and twenty-eight pence is two shillings and fourpence, and that is the whole of what mending a tilt costs and it is not one sheet of paper and not one mile of cart road and not the two weeks the four villages spent walking here, and it was said out loud on the boards before the total and not after, and about eight hundred people in that field have a boil in it and about four hundred of them are on nine hundred sheets two hundred miles off and there is no figure in this kingdom for any of the others.**

## Five

And then somebody asked the question, and it was asked by the man of about thirty-three with the barrow and he asked it standing up, and he had asked a question standing up once before in about five months and it had a person in it.

“**There are about four hundred of us on those sheets,**” he said. “**How many of us are there that are not on them.**”

And the room went quiet in the way about forty people in a kitchen go quiet.

“**I have never had a figure for that,**” the woman of about forty-two said. “**I have a figure for how many of us wrote them and a figure for how many of us were in the field on the second morning and a figure for how many carts came and a figure for how many did not come and there is no figure for anybody who is not in this field, because a figure is a count of people who are somewhere you have been.**”

“**Then somebody has to go and find the rest,**” the man of about thirty-three said.

**And nobody in that kitchen answered him, and nobody in any of the other eight rooms answered him either, and nobody in that field has a list of the ones who are not on the sheets, and the eleven hundred are not on any list of the four hundred and are not a figure of persons in this kingdom and have not been put on anything, and the register is not destroyed and is not withdrawn and goes back into the round in about a month, and about four hundred people have been told what a sheet is and about eleven hundred have not and the eleven hundred are the ones nobody is going after, and the decision that was made in that kitchen on that afternoon was made by about forty people in about a quarter of a minute and it was this: the nine rooms keep the second hands, the sheets go back in the round, the four ways go back in the round, the four figures with the mornings beside them go back in the round, and nobody burns a sheet, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow was told in a short sentence by the woman of about forty-two that there was no list and would not be one, and he said all right, and he went out into a field with about eight hundred people in it and about two hundred carts and a tilt with four nails in it and nobody on that field can tell him where the other ones are.**
