# Chapter 0399

**The fifteenth day of Mudmonth, YR 318, a Thursday and a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour and the eighth hour, on about thirty miles of a salt road that runs out of a coast about two days west of a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where about nine hundred yards of the road is below the level of the sea and goes under at a state of tide that a cart does not want, and there are nine feet of oak in a kerb at the near end of the nine hundred yards, and about nine hundred people on that coast did not know until the second hour of that morning that it was shut.**

## One

**The road goes under between about the second hour and about the sixth at a neap and between the first and the seventh at a spring, and about four days in ten it is under, and a man who has been about thirty miles of that road for eleven years does not need to be told what the tide is doing and the four days in ten are the days the nine hundred yards is shut and the nine feet of oak is up.** The man at the second stile who poles the boat is the man who lives nearest the nine feet of oak and he is about twenty-nine and he has a day of about nine hours of walking hired out on that road at a figure he names himself and nobody has ever written down.

**The rule has been kept three times since the third week of a month in Hearthmonth and the man at the second stile kept it once, on the eleventh of a month in Thawmonth, and it took him about nine hours and four crossings with a flat-bottomed boat twenty-two feet long and a boy of about fourteen, and nobody paid him and nobody has asked him about it, and on the twenty-second of that month he told a woman of about thirty-four in a room at the back of a salt warehouse that he was not going to be the nearest any more.**

“**I said it once and I said it plain,**” he said, on the twenty-second, **and nobody argued with him and that was the end of it, and about four households within nine miles of that nine feet of oak heard him say it and most of that coast did not.**

## Two

It shut at the first hour on the fifteenth of Mudmonth and it was a spring tide and the ninth day after the last one, and the ninth day after a spring is a neap and the neap shut it for about six hours instead of four, and nobody on thirty miles of that coast knows that in advance and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet found that out in about nine seconds by looking at a tide table that a boy of about nineteen keeps in a hut and has never been in a return.

**There was a cart on the far side of the nine hundred yards at the first hour with eleven bushels on it that had been loaded the night before, and the man who had loaded it was at the near end and could not get to it, and the nine feet of oak is not a thing a man walks round because the two sides of that nine hundred yards are a soft salt bank and a made road and about nine feet of water between them at that hour.**

The third nearest person to the nine feet of oak is nine miles off. **He is a man of about fifty-four who has a holding and a great many acres of salt pan and about nine men in a season, and he is nine miles off because nine miles is what nine miles, and about four people in that district had worked out before the second hour of that morning that the third nearest is the man with the pans and that the pans do not run in the fourth month.**

**The nearest is four miles off and he is not coming, and everybody on thirty miles of that coast knew he was not coming inside about nine minutes, and about forty people in four households within nine miles of that nine feet of oak knew it and had no idea what to do about it and did the same thing, which was to wait.** The man of about thirty-eight with a barrow is the second nearest and he is four miles off on a made road and he was at a house four miles off on the morning of the fifteenth of Mudmonth and he has not been nearest once in his life and nobody had ever thought about what a man who has not been nearest would do about it.

## Three

At about the fourth hour a man of about thirty-four with a sheet came to the near end with a bag and did the thing that man does, which is to find out what has come in and what has not.

“**Four times,**” he said. “**That is three since the third week of Hearthmonth and this is the fourth, and I have a number for the hours as well, because a man who counts what goes out counts what does not. The first was a woman of about thirty-four at the third landing and it was four hours. The second was a man of about sixty-three at the fifth and it was four hours. The third was a man of about twenty-nine at the second stile on the eleventh of Thawmonth and it was six hours, and he has not done one since, and he said on the twenty-second of that month that he would not, and I wrote that down because I write everything down that anybody says about a day.**”

“**That is a bad rule,**” said the man of thirty-two with a limp, who had walked the nine hundred yards over at about the third hour with a stick he cannot lean on and who had not been asked anything by anybody.

**Everybody on that piece of made road turned round, including a man of about thirty-four with a bag who had known him for about four months.**

“**You have said that once,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**In a room on a coast about nine days from here, in the first week of a month in Thawmonth, and you said it and then you said the other thing, and I have thought about the other thing for a month.**”

“**I remember what I said.**”

“**Then say it again and then say what has happened since,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**because between the twenty-second of that month and this morning the road was shut twice and nobody opened it either time. Once for about five hours on a day in the ninth month and once for about four hours on a day in the second month, and on both of those days the man at the second stile was at home and was about four hundred yards away and did not come, and I have both of those days written down too and neither of them is a figure anybody on that road is going to enjoy.**”

## Four

**And a man of about thirty-four with a bag said the thing that every person in every room says about a rule like that, which is that somebody ought to be paid, and he said it well, and he had thought about it for nine miles of salt road, and the woman of about thirty-four asked him one question and he answered it and she did not answer him back.**

“**If I pay the man at the second stile,**” she said, “**then I have to know which nine men are nearest on which day. And to know that I have to ask nine people, and to ask nine people I have to keep the answers, and to keep the answers I have to keep a list of nine men and the day each of them was nearest, and that is a list of people and there is nothing in this kingdom that a person can keep a list of people about, and I am not keeping one, and I would rather a road be shut for six hours than have a list.**”

“**You could pay a man for the hour without knowing which hour.**”

“**Then I pay whoever is nearest and the man nearest becomes a man who is paid to be nearest, and in a year there are about nine of those, and this year there are two, and the man who is nearest in the fourth month is a man who has worked that out and is telling me he is not going to, and he is right and I would not send him and that is the same as sending him.**”

**Nobody in that room improved on it. There was a man of about thirty-four with a bag who had an idea at about the fifth hour and did not say it and a woman of about thirty-four who had three and gave none of them away and a man of about fifty-four with four hundred acres of salt pan nine miles off who came at about the sixth hour and was told the whole of it in about a minute and who said a figure and went back, and the figure was not a rule.**

## Five

The boat came at about the eighth hour and it was the woman of about thirty-four's own boat and the man of about fifty was on it and he had not been asked and he had not offered and he was out on the making of the tide because that is when a man of about fifty who has run a boat for twenty-two years is out.

**She did not send for it. She did not pay for it. It came because about four hundred yards up that road a man of about twenty-nine who poled a flat-bottomed boat for a living had sent a boy of about fourteen on a bicycle with a note, and the note is not in anybody's hand and nobody has asked the boy what it said and the boy is fourteen and does not remember.**

And that is the cost of the fourth time, and it has to be set down because it is what happened, and nobody improved on it. **Whoever is nearest opens a shut road and the person nearest is not paid for it, and it has been kept four times in about two months, and the three before this one were a household at the third landing, a man of about sixty-three with no household at the fifth, and a man of about twenty-nine at the second stile with a trade of his own — and the fourth was the coast, and the coast runs eleven boats and about nine hundred people eat off them, and the coast has a rule about nine hundred yards of salt road and the coast did not make the rule and cannot unmake it, and about nine hundred people on that coast had no way of knowing until the fifth hour of the fifteenth of Mudmonth that the nearest had become the largest thing on the road, and about nine hundred people on that coast will find out about the next one at the same time as everybody else.**

**And it is not only the fourth that is the coast. A man of about thirty-four with a sheet has six days on it and it is six days of about nine hours each and a man of about twenty-nine has three days on it of about nine hours each and there is no way of putting those two in one column because one of them is a man with a trade and the other one is a boat and the two of them are the only two people in four counties who can get eleven bushels over nine hundred yards of a salt road at that state of the tide, and about nine hundred people on that coast have just worked out that a rule about who is nearest is a rule about which two people in the whole of this coast they cannot do without, and there is no version of that in which anybody gets better off, and the woman of about thirty-four said so in about nine seconds when it was put to her and she was right and it did not help and nobody improved on it.**

**A man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote the four times and the hours down on the back of a page and did not put it in the bag, and the man of about fifty rowed back to the slip and went to bed, and the cart with eleven bushels on it went home at about the ninth hour and the nine bushels went into a store on that coast and the two went into a store four miles off, and nobody anywhere on thirty miles of that salt road has improved on a rule that costs about six hours and about four times in two months, and about forty people in four households within nine miles of that nine feet of oak had spent a day doing nothing that anybody has written down, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp walked back up that salt road in the dark with a stick he cannot lean on and got in at about the eleventh hour and did not tell anybody what he had seen there and nobody asked him.**
