# Chapter 0359

**The seventeenth day of Harvestmonth, YR 317, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, in a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road, where nine men were at a bench-board and two figures were still folded on it from five weeks before, and a man of thirty-two with a limp came up that road at about the fourth hour with a different figure, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a man had come to that yard five weeks before with a different figure and had been told no by a man of twenty-five, and that he was told no again that Thursday.**

A Thursday in that county is a market day. About two thousand people came into four hundred yards of stone and forty men were on a reach four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of it, and up the road above that street there was a hut beside three bars with a man of fifty-five inside it, and nobody that morning was stopped at the bars who is not stopped there every other morning, and a bench of nine did not sit, because it sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week. **A bell of nine stone with a band of forty-one rivets in it went at the fourth hour and a half a hundred yards up that county's own road and went on going, and nobody in that county can be asked who keeps it and nobody has ever been asked.**

---

**And a yard of nine men in a market week on a Thursday is a different yard from the same nine men on a Tuesday in Longlight, because on a Thursday about two of them are out of the yard at the second hour standing at the end of a market four hundred yards off, and about two of them are waiting for a cart, and about five of them are doing something, and the yard is a building with a board in it and a press in a room at the end of it, and a man of twenty-five in a yard on a Thursday is a man who has already said the thing he came to say by the fourth hour and has about three hours of the day left to be wrong in.**

**And the figure the man of thirty-two brought up that road at about the fourth hour was ten pence a man for a day, and it was the same figure from both sides, and it was not six pence, and he had it because a yard of nine men on Weir Street is not the only yard within nine days of the flats and a man will tell you what nine other men take if you ask him in a doorway.**

“**Ten,**” the man of twenty-five said, before the man of thirty-two had got the stick off his hip.

“**Ten.**”

“**Then we are four pence above what that boy wrote in the book,**” the man of twenty-five said, and did not look at the bench-board, “**and the boy wrote six because six was the figure that was said out loud in this yard on a Tuesday in Longlight, and a figure that has been in the book stays in the book until somebody takes it out, and nobody is going to take it out because of a different figure on a different day.**”

“**I am not here about the figure. I am here about one crossing.**”

And the man of twenty-five put down the board he had been planing on, which he had not been planing on, and stood up, and about four people in that county have worked out since that a man of twenty-five in a yard of nine men put down a tool he was not using in order to have both hands free, and that he has never done that in that yard in about four years, and that the reason a man of twenty-five needs both hands free is that he has decided he is going to say no, and that there is no way of working out whether a man is going to say no except watching what he does with his hands.

**And he said his want out loud in his own yard, once, and nobody improved on it and nobody has asked him for it since.**

“**I have nine men and a yard and I am not putting either of them on a line.**”

“**It is not a line. It is a bar at a crossing for four months.**”

“**It is a line the moment it is on a sheet and there is a price on it,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**and the price is the Crown's and the sheet is the Crown's and the man who collects it is a keeper in a column, and about nine days ago a man came up this road with a sheet and told me that the fourth column takes a person by their trade. I have thought about it for five weeks. I have not got anybody to think about it with.**”

---

**And a man of about nineteen with an empty hod was in that yard at about the fourth hour and heard all of it, and about two people in that county have said since that a man of nineteen who has held an empty hod for four months and cut out one lynch pin was standing in a yard when two men refused each other over a bar, and that he went out to the gate at about the fifth hour and came back in at about the sixth and neither of them had noticed either time, and that a man who is not noticed when two people disagree about a thing is a man who will be the one who is asked to do it, and that nobody has asked him.**

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp said the thing he had come up that road to say, and it was an order, and about two people in that county have said since that a man who is good at seeing a load path gave an order in a yard in a market week and that it was the plainest order he has given anybody in about two years.**

“**I want one of the nine held,**” he said. “**Not manned, not repaired, not held against anybody. Held. A man in a yard on a Thursday and a man in a yard on a Saturday and a man there when the four months start, for as long as the four months last.**”

“**No.**”

“**It is one man.**”

“**It is one man out of nine in a yard that has no work in Fallowmonth, and Fallowmonth is the first month of bridge season and the first month of bridge season is the month a yard either has the season or does not have the season,**” the man of twenty-five said. “**You have a thing that is a position. I have a thing that is a season. If I put one man on a bar for four months I have eight men for a bridge season, and a bridge season is fourteen weeks, and eight men is not a crew, and a yard with a bridge rate and eight men either borrows a crew or loses the rate.**”

“**The bar would be up.**”

“**The bar would be up,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**and the eight months it is up in are the eight months that nobody in this yard is paid for either, and you are asking me to spend a season on an eight-month problem, and I have not got a season and neither has the Crown.**”

**Nobody in that yard said the word for what they are, and nobody on that yard has ever said it, and it is not a word that yard uses, and about two people in that county have said since that two men in a yard on a Thursday morning in a market week said everything there was to say about a bar and a season and about eleven other things, and neither of them called the other anything at all.**

---

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp stood in that yard for about two minutes and then did the thing that about four people in that county have worked out since was the first time anybody in that county had seen him do it, and that is that he did not say the next thing.**

He had it. It was in his mouth, and about two people in that yard have worked out a man who has not in about eleven years been told no by anybody he could not overrule stood in a yard with the next thing in his mouth and did not say it, and that the next thing was not a threat and would have sounded like one, and that a man who can be angry for two minutes and then not be angry is a thing this county has only ever heard about.

“**All right,**” he said, and put his stick down and picked it up again, which is the whole of what his body will let him do about a feeling. “**Tell me what a bar costs to hold.**”

“**Nothing,**” the man of twenty-five said. “**That is the answer and you did not believe it, and a bar costs nothing to hold if the person holding it is not paid to hold it, and a person not paid to hold it holds it for four months and is not there for the eight.**”

“**That is a woman four miles off a town of two thousand people,**” the man of thirty-two said.

“**It is a woman four miles off a town of two thousand people who took twenty-five pence on a Sunday and has said one true thing about a bar in about nine years,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**and I have not met her and I have thought about her for five weeks and I would not put one of my nine on that bar for what that Crown sheet is offering, and you can take that back up that road and say I said it twice.**”

---

**And about two people in that county have worked out since that a man of twenty-five said no to a man who is good at seeing a load path, and that a man who is good at seeing a load path has not been told no by a person in a yard in about eleven years, and that the no was not about the nine pence and was not about the flats and was about fourteen weeks of a season, and that a man who has been refused on the right grounds and knows it has nothing to do next time but say no again on the same grounds, and that the same grounds are in a press in a room at the end of that yard in four sheets with nothing on the wrapper.**

**And the crossing went untended for nine days after that Thursday, and about nine people crossed it in the first week and about four in the second, and a bar that a person has to lift four seconds out of its sockets with a lever and a fulcrum was lifted four times a day by a woman of about forty-one who was not paid for the lifting, and the price on the Crown's sheet for a cart was two shillings and about two shillings' worth of carts did not come, and about two people in that town have worked out since that the whole of what one refusal in a yard cost was about nine people and two shillings in nine days, and that the man who refused it was right about the season and the man who was refused was right about the bar, and that two men being right about two different things in the same yard on a Thursday is the ordinary condition of a yard and is not a story and has no form.**

**And about four thousand people in that town do not know that a man of thirty-two came up a road that day with a figure and was told no, and about two thousand people on the salt do not know it either, and a press in a room at the end of that yard stands open and has stood open for about twenty weeks, and there is a bundle of four sheets in it with nothing written on the wrapper, and it is on the yard's book and it is never going to leave that county, and the yard's rule is that only a thing set goes in that book, and a refusal is a thing set, and there are four of them in a wrapper in a press in a room anybody can walk into, and nobody in that county has ever read the wrapper.**
