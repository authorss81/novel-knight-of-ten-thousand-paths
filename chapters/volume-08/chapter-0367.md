# Chapter 0367

**The eighth day of Embermonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the seventh hour, in a field above a ditch four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where about nine hundred sheets came in a handcart on a Thursday in the third week of Fallowmonth and were filled in over four days by people in a field, and about four hundred people in that field would not fill one in at all and about two hundred would, and the round is over and the sheets are gone, and two thousand people in that town know there is a round and two hundred of them have been sent one.**

The round is one sheet for one place. **It is ruled for six columns and two of the six have a head on them, and the two are a place and a figure, and two hundred of the sheets that came in that handcart have a name of a place in the first column and a figure in the second, and the other four columns are empty and have no head, and the four of them are a person, a day, a hand, and a way of telling anybody anything at all.**

**And about two people in that town have worked out since that a sheet with a place and a figure on it and four empty columns that have no head on them is a form for counting where people are, and that a form for counting where people are is the oldest form there is, and that the four empty columns are not an oversight and were not left off by a mistake, and that whoever ruled it ruled six and headed two because the other four have never been anybody's job.**

---

**And a handcart came off the Marl road on the Thursday of the third week of Fallowmonth with about nine hundred sheets in it and a strap over them, and it stood in the low arc of that field for about four days with a board leaning against it, and the strap came off on the first morning and went back on on the fourth morning, and the round is a thing that happens whether the strap is on or off, and the four days in that field were not the round and were the only part of it that anybody in that field has ever seen.**

**And about nine hundred sheets is not nine hundred people and nobody in that field ever said it was.** A sheet is a place. A field is a place. A yard is a place. A kiln is a place. A lime heap is a place, and there are more places in a county of two thousand people wide than anybody in that district has ever gone round and counted, and the round is one sheet for each of them and the round does not care how many there are.

**And the four days were like this. A sheet came for a place and a person was found to put a figure in it, and the person was found by asking somebody, and asking somebody took a minute, and there were more places on the salt than there were people willing to stand in the rain and ask. On the first morning nine sheets were filled in. On the second morning about two hundred. On the third morning about four hundred. On the fourth morning the round closed at the seventh hour with a figure against every place on that salt that anybody had been able to reach, and a figure of nothing against one place two days north that nobody had been able to reach, and no column on any of it for the difference.**

A woman of about forty-two who was a schoolmistress in one of those four villages filled in nine of them herself, and about four people in that field have worked out since that a woman who will not be the one who counts the field wrote nine figures for nine places off the top of her head in an hour, and that every one of the nine was about a place she had taught in, and that not one of the nine was about the field.

“**I will not be the one who counts that,**” she said, when the sheet for the field came round the second morning, “**and I have said that three mornings running and I am not going to be asked a fourth time, because the day I put a figure in that column is the day the figure is a figure that a woman counted, and it will be in a room two hundred miles off inside a month and there is nowhere on it that says whose hand it was.**”

---

**And a man of about fifty-four who burns lime at a kiln two miles off that town filled in the sheet for the kiln himself, on the third morning, with a figure that is wrong by four, and he wrote it wrong on purpose in about nine minutes.**

**And the market at Marl was on that Saturday, because Marl's market is a Saturday and the eighth of Embermonth is a Saturday, and about nine hundred people came into four hundred yards of stone and a rope at one end and a bar at the other, and about two thousand people in that town know there is a round, and about two hundred of them have been sent one, and about eleven hundred of them have not and would not know where to send it, and the sheet for a field four miles off was on a cart flap in that field and not in that market, and what the round is for is that it comes round again in a month whether anybody is ready or not.**

**And a boy of about eleven carried the handcart out of the field on the fourth morning with a strap over the sheets and a board leaning against it, and he carried it to the Marl road and a man took it there, and what a boy of eleven did with nine hundred sheets on a handcart is walk four miles with a strap across his chest, and about four people in that field have worked out since that the boy asked twice whether he was to bring the board back and was told to bring the board back, and that the board is a cart flap off a tilt cart and is going back to a field and a cart that is not going anywhere in particular.**

**And the field did not know what had happened to the sheets, and a woman of about thirty-three asked a man of about thirty-three with a barrow on the fifth morning whether a round came round twice, and he said it did, and she said that she had not known that, and what a round is to a person who has been in it once is a thing that comes back in a month and nobody rings, and two people in that field have said since that a person in a field is the only person in four counties who does not know whether a round comes round twice, and that this is not a thing anybody has thought of doing on purpose.**

**And a man of about thirty-one with a canvas bag stood on a cart flap at the fifth hour and read nine of them out loud, and about two hundred people in that field heard their own number said out loud by a man who was reading it off a sheet.**

He read nine. **A sheet for a yard with nine in it, and a sheet for a yard with eleven in it, and a sheet for a yard with four in it, and six others, and a sheet for a place that is a kiln and a sheet for a place that is a lime heap, and he read them in the order they were clipped to a board, and he read the number last and the place first.**

“**That one is nine,**” a woman of about thirty-three said, from thirty feet off. “**That is our nine. It is eleven and it has been eleven since the ninth day.**”

“**It is a headcount,**” Ottmar Vell said, “**and a headcount is a headcount, and there is no column on this sheet that a person can be in, and if I put eleven in it then I have put in it a thing that is not the second column, and the second column is a number of people and eleven is not the number of people in your yard.**”

“**Then put eleven in it.**”

“**I cannot put eleven in it,**” Ottmar Vell said, “**and you have said it twice and I have heard it twice and I am not going to write it, and I am not going to write anything else either, and if there were a place on that sheet where a person could say a thing like that I would put it in, and there is not, and about two hundred people in this field have now heard that from a man with a bag and about two hundred people in this field know that this is what a form is.**”

**And she went back to her yard and counted it again at the sixth hour and it was eleven, and eleven is the number, and eleven is in a handcart by the seventh hour, and about four people in that field have said since that a woman of about thirty-three went back nine hundred yards to count her own yard and got the right number and told a man with a bag and the man with a bag could not put it anywhere, and that this is the whole of what a form with a place and a figure on it is for, and that it is not a lie and it is not a mistake, and that a yard is a yard and a figure is a figure and the connection between the two of them was made by a woman nine hundred yards away in the rain.**

---

**And the paper of it. Four pence a quire at a shop in a lane in that town, and about nine hundred sheets of a ruled six with two heads on them is about forty quires, and four pence and four pence is eight pence, and eight pence and eight pence is sixteen pence, and sixteen pence and sixteen pence is thirty-two pence, and thirty-two pence and eight pence is forty pence, and that is ten quires, and forty pence and forty pence is eighty pence, and that is twenty quires, and eighty pence and eighty pence is a hundred and sixty pence, and a hundred and sixty pence is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and that is the whole of what about nine hundred returns cost in money.**

A hundred and sixty pence is the paper. **A hundred and sixty pence is not the four days.** The four days was about four hundred people on the salt standing in the rain asking other people for a figure, and about two hundred people in a field going at a place each and asking somebody what was in it, and one woman of about forty-two writing nine figures for nine places off the top of her head in about an hour, and a woman of about forty-two refusing three mornings running to write one, and a man with a barrow asking for one to be read twice, and none of that is on the sheet and none of it has a column and the second column is a figure and the figure is a number of people in a place.

---

**And about two hundred people in that field have worked out since that there are now two places in that district where the name of a village exists on paper, and that both of the two are official, and that neither of the two is the village.**

A village's name is in the first column of a sheet that went into a handcart on the fourth morning and is somewhere between a room in a lane and a building with four floors and seven presses two hundred miles off. **And about four hundred and nine parish rolls behind that building still carry the same four village names in a column of their own, on a roll that has been carried every month for nineteen years, and about four hundred of those rolls behind the same building have never once been amended, and the roll says the number that is on the roll, and the roll has not been amended, and the county that owns the roll has not been told that any of the four has been emptied, and the roll does not know and has no column that knows.**

A person from one of those four villages is therefore in two places at once. **The roll has them where they were. The sheet has them where they are. Both figures are correct. Neither figure is the person, and the two are not added.**

---

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp was in that field at about the seventh hour and was asked by a man of about thirty-three with a barrow how many people were in the field, and he did not know, and he said so, and two people in that field have said since that a man who has been in that field for seven weeks and has stood at the bottom of a bank and walked a quarter of a mile with a dead man's coat could not say a number, and that he did not ask the woman of forty-two, and that not asking her is the first thing in about a month he has done in that field that cost him something he wanted.**

**And the sheet for that field is somewhere between a lane and a building two hundred miles off with a place in the first column and a figure in the second, and the four columns after those two are empty and have no head on them, and one of the four of them was a person, and nobody will ever know which hand wrote the second column of that one, and that is not a mystery and is a form.**
