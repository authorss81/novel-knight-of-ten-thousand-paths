# Chapter 0368

**The fifteenth day of Embermonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the third hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a field above a ditch four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt, and on a made road four miles south of that field and at a bar across a cut in it, where nine people went out of a field that has been shut for six weeks, and two thousand people in that town know there is a bar four miles off and about eight hundred people in that field know there are nine people going out of it and neither of those two facts is in the same head.**

The nine were the last of the low arc. **A woman of about thirty-eight and a woman of about forty-one and seven others, of whom four could walk and five could not, and a man of about thirty-four of the four hundred, who is the man a man with a limp carried a dead man's coat across a field to on the twenty-fifth of Fallowmonth, arranged it in about nine minutes on the second morning with a man of about thirty-three who had a barrow and two men who had a handcart.** None of the four of them had been asked by anybody. **The sickness in that field had been two a day for a fortnight, and eight hundred people in that field had been there for eight weeks, and the sheet that shut the place ran to the last day of Wolfmonth, and a woman of about thirty-nine said that she was not going to sit in a field with a bar on it until Wolfmonth with five people who cannot walk, and that she was going to move five people to a barn four miles off that has a stove in it and a woman in it who has agreed, and that the woman who has agreed is not an office.**

A man of about thirty-two with a limp was told it on the second morning by a boy of about eleven.

---

**And the road out is a mile and a half of towpath beside a made road, and a towpath is two yards wide and four inches above the made road and is the only hard ground for two miles, and a barrow and a handcart will go along it, and nine hundred people in four villages walk on it and two hundred of them went round a bar at the head of it in a quarter of a mile on the eighth of Fallowmonth, and did not go round it on the fifteenth of Embermonth, because a handcart with a sick man in it does not go round a quarter of a mile, and a bar with a chain on it at the head of a two-yard path can be got past in about four minutes with a stick off a wall, and about nine hundred people in four villages have known that for a month.**

It took nine minutes with a stick off a wall. **A man of about thirty-three with a barrow held the cart while a man of about thirty, who had a mallet and a plank and nine nails and a tilt wheel out of true, put a stick under a nine-foot bar of oak and lifted it, and the two of them did it without a word to each other and without anybody having said anything to either of them.**

**And the made road from that field to the Marl road is about four miles, and at four miles south of the field there is a cut in a made road with a bar of oak and iron across it that is nine feet long and weighs more than a man can lift alone out of its two sockets, and a woman of about forty-one is on her own side of it, and a bar that is left down in a made road is a quarter of a mile of road that a cart goes round, and about two shillings a cart is the price on a sheet that went into effect on the twelfth of a month in Fallowmonth and that nobody in that field has been shown.**

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp stood at the end of that made road at the fifth hour, at a stile with a hurdle in it nine hundred yards north of the bar, and he did not go the last nine hundred yards, and four people in that district have said since that a man who has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight stood at a stile nine hundred yards off a bar and watched the rest of it, and that getting up a bank is four seconds longer than sitting down and that a man who has been told that by his own body for about two years does not test it in front of nine people.**

**And he watched a handcart and a tilt cart and a barrow come up nine hundred yards of made road, and at the bar a woman of about forty-one put a lever under the strap at the middle and a stone in the cut and had the bar up in four seconds, and the three carts came through, and she put it up behind them, and that is what she did.**

“**Nine,**” she said.

“**Nine,**” a man of about thirty-four said, from the road. “**And not one of the nine is on a sheet and not one of the nine is paid, and you have had that bar up in four seconds and you have not asked anybody for anything.**”

“**I know that,**” the woman of about forty-one said, and did not take anything.

---

**And a man of about thirty-four of the four hundred said one more thing at the second hour, to the man of about thirty-three with the barrow and not to anybody else, and what she said was that a house with a stove in it is a different thing from a barn with a stove in it, and the difference is a door that shuts, and that there is a woman in that house who has agreed and that a woman who has agreed is a person and not an office and can un-agree at any hour of the day or night without telling anybody why, and four people in that field have worked out since that a woman of thirty-four chose a person over an office for nine people who cannot walk, and that the whole of her reasoning was a door and a stove, and that nobody in that field has asked her for the reasoning and she did not give it twice.**

**And the nine went out at about the fifth hour in a handcart, a tilt cart and a barrow, and the two arcs of carts in that field had a lane cut between them that afternoon that had not been there on the morning, because four carts went out of the low arc and the low arc's carts are on the soft ground and a lane was cut across it in about an hour by about nine men with planks, and nobody has written down that lane and it is not on any sheet, and what a lane cut across soft ground in an hour by nine men with planks is worth is a lane, and two people in that field have worked out since that a field is a different shape at the seventh hour than it was at the second and that the difference is a lane and that the figure against the field has not moved and the field is not the same field.**

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp did not go with them, and did not walk beside them, and was not at the head of that lane and not at the back of it, and about four people in that district have said since that a man who has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight stood at a stile and watched nine people leave a field, and that what he was doing there was being the last person in that district who saw all nine of them at once, and that he told nobody that.**

---

**And the field was watched from the top of the bank by four hundred people who are not speaking to two hundred people, and neither of them came down.**

**And a woman of about thirty-eight went in a barrow, and a woman of about forty-one went in a handcart, and two of the four who could walk went at the front and at the back on their own feet, and four of the five who could not walk went in a tilt cart with a plank in it and a wheel out of true, and the fifth went in the handcart with the woman of about forty-one because the handcart was the only one of the three that had a board across it.**

---

**And the two people who opened that road were the woman with the lever and a man of about thirty with a mallet and a tilt cart with a plank in it, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp did not ask either of them for anything and neither of them knew who he was, and about two people in that district have worked out since that a man with a stick stood nine hundred yards off and watched nine people and three carts go past a bar that he could not have lifted and had not been asked to lift, and that what he did on the fifteenth of Embermonth was stand at a stile.**

“**Who sent you,**” the man of about thirty-four said, to the man with the stick, at the seventh hour, on the made road north of the bar, coming back.

“**Nobody,**” the man of thirty-two said.

“**Then who has been standing at that stile.**”

“**I have,**” the man of thirty-two said, and two people in that district have worked out since that a man of thirty-two with a limp said the word *nobody* about five carts and nine people and then said *I have* of himself, and that the two sentences together are the whole of what he did that day and that the second of them is the one that took the longest.

---

**And the four miles between that bar and the barn is a made road with a kerb on the salt side of it about every nine yards, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp came up the four miles in about an hour and ten minutes, and he went on the salt side of the kerb the whole way because the other side is soft and about nine yards of a kerb is a thing a man with a right leg cannot get over twice in a mile.**

**And a woman of about forty-two was in that barn and had come four miles in a handcart that she had arranged herself on the second morning, and she did not ask him anything, and he did not ask her anything.**

**And it was not a thing he did. A thing a person does is the only kind of thing anybody has ever been able to pay him for, and he has been paid for doing things since he was seventeen years old, and the fifteenth of Embermonth is the first day in about two years on which the whole of what he was for was that he stood at the end of a road.**

He walked the four miles to the barn, which is about an hour and ten minutes on a made road with a stick, and a woman of about thirty-nine was there and a woman of about forty-two was there and about nine people were there, and he stood in the doorway of a barn with a stove in it and asked how many.

**And then he made a decision, and it is the only decision he made in about a month, and about two people in that barn have worked out since that a man who has a page in his coat with nine lines on it and no heads on it stood in a doorway and worked out that he now knew two figures about the same eight hundred people and that one of the two was a field and the other was a field with nine people taken out of it, and that the two figures are not added and that he could not enter either of them anywhere in this kingdom, and that a man who holds two figures about the same people and can enter neither of them is a man who has to be asked, and that being asked is a thing that happens to him.**

He did not write it down. **There is a page in his coat with nine crossings on it and no heads, and there is no page in his coat with eight hundred on it, and there is no page in his coat with nine hundred and ninety on it, and the reason is not that he is careful and it is that there is nowhere, and a thing that is nowhere is not kept and is also not lost, and two people in that barn have said since that a man who has nowhere to put a figure does not put it anywhere, and that this is not a virtue and is the ordinary condition of a man without an office in a country where every figure anybody has matters goes into a column.**
