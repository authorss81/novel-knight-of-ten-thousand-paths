# Chapter 0375

**The seventh day of Wolfmonth, YR 317, a Sunday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in that yard, four hundred yards up that county's own road above a market that was shut, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where four of nine men were mending a sill and five were not there, and a man of thirty-two with a limp came up that road at about the second hour and was told no in nine words, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a man who has been in this district since Longlight has been refused a road by a yard of nine men.**

Nothing trades on a Sunday. **A market of four hundred yards long in a town of four thousand people is a street with a rope at one end of it that is not there and a bar at the other that is not there, and about two hundred of four thousand people were out of their houses and three thousand eight hundred were in them, and a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road is four men and a bench-board and a press standing open in a room at the end of it.**

**And the four men in that yard were mending a sill, and mending a sill in Wolfmonth is bridge work, and bridge work is the thing a yard of nine men exists for, and the fourth month of a shut season is the month that a yard either has or has not, and a yard which spends a Sunday mending a sill has already answered a question nobody put to it, and it answered it with a mallet and not with a sentence.**

He had taken a bed at a lodging house at the north end of that street and had come up the road at the second hour. **Coming up that road is about four hundred yards and it takes him about a quarter of an hour, and he came up it on the north side and stopped at the gate, and a yard's gate has a sill in it, and getting over a sill is about four seconds longer than getting over nothing, and he has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight and is not going to.**

**And the four men in that yard were mending a sill and they did not stop, and a boy of fifteen was at the bench-board on a Sunday copying the space under a number for the ninth day running, and a man of about sixty was packing lime against a heap that has a lid on it, and a yard of nine men with four of them in it on a Sunday does not stop for a man who has come up a road, and a yard which stopped would be a guild, and this is not a guild.**

He gave the order again because he had come to give it again. **It was the same order and the same four miles and the same four hundred miles, and about two people in that yard worked out that a man of thirty-two with a limp had ridden four hundred miles to give an order on a market Saturday and had come up the road on a Sunday morning to give it a second time, and that there was nobody in that county who had ever seen anybody do that.**

The man of twenty-five was at the bench-board and he listened to all of it.

“**No. I have not got that season to give.**”

---

**And he said it in nine words, unhedged, and the nine words were a season, and the yard carried on mending a sill, and a man who has not in about eleven years been told no by anybody he could not overrule was told no in nine words by a man he could not overrule either, and nobody in that yard heard anything else and nobody went out of the yard.**

He had it. **It was in his mouth, and it was the next thing, and about two people in that yard worked out that a man who has been in this trade for eleven years stood in a yard on a Sunday morning and did not say the next thing, and that the next thing was not a threat and would have sounded like one.**

**And the standing for not saying it was not a rule anybody made and it was not courage. It is that an order is a claim and a claim that is refused is a claim, and a man whose claim has been refused once in a yard has a claim that can be refused again, and a man who turns a refused claim into an argument about his own standing has turned a rate into a position, and there is no form on which that can be entered and about four thousand people in that town would find out about it in about nine days if there were.**

“**You can say the next thing,**” the man of twenty-five said, without looking up. “**I have not got the season and I have said it in the order I would say it to anybody, and I say it that way because I have said it in a doorway to a man with a sheet and it came out of my mouth the same way, and I have not practised it and I am not going to.**”

---

**And then the man of twenty-five put his hand flat on the bench-board and left it there, and he said the rest of it, and what he said was that he would not do it on that order, and that he would not do it for a sheet, and that if it was done at all it would be done on what the nine of them said, and that they had not said it.**

“**I will not do it for you and I will not do it for a sheet, and if it is done at all it will be done on what the nine of us say and we have not said it, and I am not going to say it this morning because four of the nine are in this yard and five of the nine are not, and the five who are not would want to be here, and I will not take a thing like this off a man who is not in the room.**”

“**You are the owner of the yard.**”

“**I am one of the nine,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**and there are eight others and about four of them are on this county's own road every day of a working season, and I have been thinking about that season since before the first of this month, and I have been thinking about the one before it for about a year, and the thinking has not been about a bar.**”

**And about two people in that yard have said since that a man of twenty-five gave an order away in a yard, and that a man of twenty-five giving an order away is not defiance and is not theatre and is the only thing in that yard that is not a rate, and that a man who has been thinking about a season for about a year does not do it to be looked at and does not do it for a man from another county, and that he said it at about the fourth hour on a Sunday with a sill being mended nine feet off.**

**And the boy of fifteen copied the space under a number through the whole of it and did not look up once, and the number he was copying is the figure that a bar of nine feet of oak and an iron strap has been carried at in that yard's book since Longlight, and nobody has told him what the figure is for, and a boy of fifteen will copy a number for a bar or a number for a bread oven with the same face, and that is worth more to a yard than any of the nine.**

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp had a page in his coat with nine crossings on it and no heads on it and he did not take it out, and about two people in that yard have worked out since that a man who has made a list of nine things himself and has never shown it to anybody did not show it to a yard of nine men on the morning he was refused, and that he did not take it out because there is nothing on it that would have been any use to nine men and he knew that before he was asked.**

---

**And a man of about nineteen with an empty hod was in that yard at the fourth hour and had heard all of it, and he was the only one of the nine who had not said one word, and a man of nineteen who has held an empty hod for four months was standing in a yard when two men refused each other over a road four hundred miles off, and the only one of nine who says nothing while two men are saying everything is the one somebody is going to ask.**

The man of about sixty said nothing about any of it. **He had a lid on his heap and he kept his hand on the lid through the whole of it, and a lid is what a man puts his hand on when a thing is being decided in a yard and he is not one of the deciding, and a man of about sixty is not paid out of that yard, and nobody in that yard has ever asked him how he knew to do that.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp went down that county's own road at about the fourth hour and did not come up it again for two days, and a man who had come up a road four hundred yards twice in two days and had been refused once went down it and stayed down it, and the two days were not a sulk and were not a plan, and a man with no office and no office building and no place to put a figure has got two days of nothing in him and about four thousand people in that town never found out what he did with them.**

---

**And about four people in that county have said since that a sill cut and fitted in Wolfmonth is about nine days of a man's work and about nine days of a yard's rate, and that the four men in that yard had been at it since the second hour and would be at it at the seventh, and that a refusal given in the middle of nine days of that is a refusal a man has to carry through eight more days of it, and that is a thing no man in that yard had thought about and the man of twenty-five had thought about.**

**And two people in that county have said since that a boy of fifteen has copied the space under a number about nine hundred times since the first of Longlight, and that the space has been empty every one of those nine hundred times, and that a boy who has copied a thing nine hundred times without being told anything about it will not put anything in it on a Sunday either, and that this is the single most reliable thing in that yard.**

He had the whole of the order in his head and the whole of the nine words in it, and there was nothing in either of them that he was entitled to do anything with.

**And he did not send the order again. He could have, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a man who has been refused in a yard can send the same order up the same road on the same piece of paper and nobody would send him away for it, and that a second copy of an order is not a second claim, and that he did not send it, and that nobody in that yard knew that he had not sent it, and that he did not tell them, and that a man not telling a yard that he is not going to overrule it is the ordinary end of a thing and is not a story.**

**And his decision, which he made on a bed at a lodging house at the north end of that street at about the sixth hour and did not say to anybody and has not said to anybody since, was to leave it with the nine of them, and to let whatever they said be what was said about it, and to come back up that road on the third day and not before, and to ask them for it and not ask them for anything else.**

---

**And two people in that town have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp was not in that county for two days and about four thousand people in that town did not know that he had been, and that a man with no office is the only kind of man anybody in this kingdom cannot account for on any day of the week, and that on the days when nothing happens nothing is written down about it.**

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was refused a road by a yard of nine men and did not send the order again and did not say one word about it to anybody, and a man who has not done that before in about eleven years did it in about four seconds and then walked four hundred yards down a road, and what he gave away by giving it away was a thing he had never had a name for and could not have used.**
