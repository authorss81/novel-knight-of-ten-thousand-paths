# Chapter 0356

**The twenty-seventh day of Rainmonth, YR 317, a Friday, and not a market day, and the fourth hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, in a county that has four market towns in a district and about nine hundred people, in a room with a table in it and a press in it, where a woman of forty-four who holds one of nine sets of forty sheets came in out of a wet morning and was asked a question she could answer, and nine hundred people in that county know that she holds that set and four thousand people in a town of four thousand on a river know nothing about it and nine days of cart away there is a bar on a made road where a woman takes a penny a person and four pence a cart and knows nothing about this room either.**

A wagon that goes out of that county on the Tuesday comes back on the Thursday, and it came back on the Thursday in the wet twice this month and on the Friday once, and about two hundred of nine hundred people were out of their houses on that Friday and about half of them were in it because of the rain. **The mill at the low end of that water went on turning and the miller has been at that wheel nine years and does not get down for anybody. Nine men were inside a yard four hundred yards off and did not go out into it. A man of fifty-five was at three bars on a road above that street, and anybody at all can go round at either end in nine seconds, and a bench of nine on the far side of that street did not sit, because a bench in that county sits on a Saturday and on nothing else in the week.**

---

**And a woman of about forty-four was in that room from about the sixth hour the evening before, because the room is the room she works in and she was in it, and about nine people in that county know that she holds a set of forty sheets and about four hundred people know that a building burned on the twenty-first of Mudmonth and about two hundred people have been told by her, in person, that a set of forty sheets is about nine sets of about forty sheets and none of them is complete and each of them has one person by name, and that nine people in four counties have said since that the whole of the protection in those nine sets is that everybody in four counties knows which person, and that a rule nobody made has been the only thing that has held for four months.**

**And a man with a sheet came into that room at about the fourth hour on a Friday, and there was a woman of forty-four in it with a set of forty sheets on a table that were not in a press and had not been in a press since the twenty-first of Mudmonth, and she took them off the table when he came in, because nine days of a person's life is a thing you do not leave on a table.**

“**You keep a set,**” the man said.

“**I keep a set of forty sheets and about nine of us do and there is no form for it and there is no list of the nine and I have never been asked for a list of the nine and neither have you.**”

“**I am not asking for a list of the nine. What is your trade.**”

And about two people in that county have worked out a woman of about forty-four was asked her trade on a Friday in Rainmonth by a man with a sheet, and she answered it in two words without sitting down, and that the answer was a trade and not a name and that a name would have been the wrong answer and would have cost her the set, and that she has never known how close it was.

“**A wicket-keeper,**” she said. “**The landing at the low end of the cut. Fourteen years. It is not a guild.**”

“**A keeper,**” the man said, and wrote something on the back of the sheet with a pencil, and two people in that county have said since that the whole of what a sheet does to a person happens in four seconds with a pencil and not in a room, and that if the man had come back in a week she would not have known, and that nobody in that county would have told her.

“**Nowhere on the sheet,**” the woman said, looking at it. “**There is nowhere on that to put a name.**”

“**No.**”

“**And you are going to put a landing on it.**”

“**I am going to put a trade on it,**” the man said, “**and a trade is what a wicket is, and a wicket is a crossing with a keeper in it, and the price on a crossing with a keeper in it is two shillings a cart for the season, and the season is four months, and the four months are the ones where a wicket is up.**”

---

**And two people in that county have said since that a man with a sheet asked a woman a question at the fourth hour on a Friday and had his answer at the fourth hour and eleven minutes and that the eleven minutes in between were him writing four things on the back of his own sheet, and that a man who is going to spend eleven minutes deciding to write nothing has already decided, and that the woman did not know any of that and was doing arithmetic about a tide.**

**And the woman worked out the price at the bar, and she worked it out in four seconds standing up, and two people in that county have worked out a woman of forty-four who has not been asked about a price in nine years worked out a Crown price against a landing bar in four seconds and that she had it right and that a woman who has kept a wicket for fourteen years knows a wicket.**

Four pence a cart is the price at a bar on a made road where the bar goes down and comes up. Two shillings a cart is the price the Crown has put on nine crossings for the season. **Four pence and twenty-four pence, and the whole of the difference between them is twenty pence, and there is no rate anywhere in this kingdom at which a woman can find out what twenty pence a cart for four months is worth, and nobody asked her and nobody has ever asked her.**

“**Twenty pence a cart,**” she said.

“**For the season.**”

“**For the season on a landing where the wicket is up four months and down eight,**” she said, “**and a landing takes four carts a week in the four months and none in the eight, and there is nobody on that landing but me between the second hour and the fourth, and the eight months are mine and the four months are nobody's because the tide is in.**”

**And four people in that county have worked out since that a woman of forty-four said the whole of what a Crown price is worth at a landing in sixty-six words standing up, and that it was true, and that the man with the sheet wrote none of it down, and that he had a sheet in his hand with about nine lines free on the back of it, and that the four lines he did write on the back of it were the word *keeper*, a landing, a season, and a figure, and that none of the sixty-six words is on the sheet and all of them are in that room.**

“**What does a man get for two shillings a cart,**” she said.

“**Nine pence in the four months, at a landing, by your own count.**”

“**And in the eight.**”

“**Nothing.**”

“**Then you have offered me nine pence a week for a year of being at a landing,**” the woman said, “**and you have offered it at two shillings a cart so that nobody has to work out the nine pence, and that is the cleverest thing anybody has ever done with a number and I am going to say yes to it and I want you to know that I know it.**”

---

**And a man of about thirty-one with a satchel came into that room at about the fifth hour and put a sheet down on the table and the woman of forty-four read it standing up in nine seconds, and two people in that county have worked out since that the ninth set of forty sheets left that county in a bag on a Friday afternoon in Rainmonth and two people have also worked out since that a man with a satchel has carried two thousand sheets in six years and has never once known what was in them, and that on that Friday he knew what was in one of them and did not look at it, because the man who put it down said not to.**

“**That is nine of them,**” the woman said.

“**That is nine of them,**” the man with the satchel said, and put his hand flat on it and did not open it, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a man who has carried about two thousand sheets in about six years without knowing what was in any of them put his hand flat on the two thousand and first and stopped a woman reading it, and that he did it because he was asked to, and that being asked to is the only thing in six years that has ever changed his mind about his own work.

---

**And she said yes, and two people in that county have worked out since that the whole of what happened in that room between the fourth hour and the fifth hour of that Friday was a fair question and a fair answer and a sheet, and that nothing was seized and nothing was lied to and nobody was compelled and no hand was raised and no door was shut.**

“**I have a boy of eleven,**” she said, when the man had put the sheet away, which is the only reason in the world and is a good one. “**He is not at a landing. He is at a school in Marl and he is at a school in Marl because Marl has a school and this district has a school in about four days of a wet road, and that is nine pence a week and it is the nine pence and not the twenty.**”

“**I did not know there was a boy,**” the man said.

“**There is a column for a keeper and there is a column for how often and there is nowhere on that sheet for a boy of eleven,**” the woman said, “**and a man who offers nine pence a week for a year to a woman at a landing has to be told about the boy or he is a thief, and I am not going to let you be one, and I am also not going to let you be somebody who is not, because that would be worse.**”

---

**And the forty sheets went into a press in a building with four floors and seven presses, about two hundred miles off, on a Friday, in the ordinary way, entered under a head, and nine people in four counties know that the ninth set left a county that day and four hundred people know that it came and nobody in either place has written a word about it, and there is no form on which the journey of a set of forty sheets can be entered and there never was.**

**And a woman of forty-four who had kept the ninth set since the twenty-first of Mudmonth went out of that room at the seventh hour in the wet with nothing in her arms, and a man of fifty-five walked two miles down from three bars and did not know she had been in that room, and nine hundred people in that county did not know and nine hundred in a town four hundred miles off knew her name and knew what she kept and would not have said yes.**
