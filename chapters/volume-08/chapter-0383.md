# Chapter 0383

**The sixth day of Hearthmonth, YR 317, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, at the end of a lane off the west end of that market, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, about a mile off that town, where a cart crossing over a drain had been shut for nineteen days and a man of about thirty-two with a limp came up that cart road from the salt at about the second hour and walked out onto the shut surface, and nobody in that town knows that the crossing is shut.**

It went on the seventeenth of the month before. **A sill is a line of stone laid across a cart crossing so that a wheel runs along it and not over it, and this one was six feet of it, cut and fitted, with a course on each side of it and the joints pointed, and it had been in that lane about eleven years and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar and a shovel has been in this district since the eighth of a month in Fallowmonth and has never once been asked about it.**

The crossing was shut at the second hour on that morning by three men with a length of chain and has been shut every day since. **About a hundred crossings a week that were going over it have gone round, and two loaded carts have gone down the soft shoulder on the west side in nineteen days. The second of them went in on the ninth day, and the man who lost four hours did not get them back and did not ask anybody to.**
**That cart was loaded to about eleven hundredweight and it took the west side where the shoulder is softest, and the near wheel went under the sill and stopped and the cart did not go on. Nine men with a chain got the off wheel up in about four hours and the near wheel came out of the hole it had made on its own in about nine minutes, and the sill did not move and the drain underneath it did not move either, and the hole in the soft ground on the west side of that lane is still there and a cart will go into it again.**

A cart crossing is not a bridge. **It is a raised surface about nine feet wide laid across a drain with a kerb on each side, and the stone in the middle of it is the sill and the rest of it is hard core, and the whole of it stands about nine inches proud of the lane, and a crossing is shut with a chain rather than with timber because the surface is still there and a man would go over it in the dark and put a wheel through.**

**A lane off the west end of that market is nine feet wide for the first two hundred yards and then it is a made road about eleven feet wide with a kerb on each side, and it runs a mile out to where it stops at a set of gates into a field. About two hundred crossings a week are made on it and about eleven of those are a loaded lime cart or a coal cart or a timber cart, and a crossing that is shut takes all of them at once.**

Nobody has said why to anybody. **A man of about fifty-five who keeps a hut beside three bars up that county's own road has been asked three times in nineteen days and has said three times that a crossing is shut, and that is a true sentence and is the whole of what he has, and nobody in that town has ever come up that lane and asked him a second question because a hut with three bars in front of it is not a place anybody goes twice.**

---

**And what the thirty-eight with a crowbar said at about the fifth hour, when he had set the crowbar against the kerb and looked down the drain, was that the crossing is not the thing that has to be rebuilt.**

“**I can put a new sill in that lane in nine days,**” he said, “**and I have put nine of them and I will put the tenth, and the tenth one will be under water in two winters, because the drain under it has not been out in about four years and there is silt in it up to the crown of the pipe and the water that used to go down the shoulder and off down that lane has not been going anywhere, it has been standing in nine yards of soft ground two hundred yards west of here since before I came.**”

**And a rebuild cannot come before the thing that has to be rebuilt, and the thing that has to be rebuilt on that lane is a drain under a made road, and the drain is not anybody's, and a man who is not anybody's is a man there is no one to send.**

---

**And four people in that town have worked out since that the whole of what a shut crossing is, in nineteen days, in one lane, is a length of chain and three men and about a hundred crossings a week going round, and that a person who has never heard of a sill in his life can tell in about nine seconds that the second of those things is the cost and the first of them is the bill.**

The cost in materials, which the man of about thirty-eight said out loud at about the fifth hour because a man of about thirty-two with a limp had asked him and he had a crowbar in his hand and no reason not to, is this. **A stone sill at thirty-four pence, and a course on each side at eleven pence a course, and lime at three pence a bushel and two bushels of it.** Thirty-four and twenty-two is fifty-six pence, and fifty-six and six is sixty-two pence, and sixty-two pence is five shillings and twopence, **and that is the whole of what the stone is and not one hour of the nine days and not the drain and not the chain and not the cart that was in to the axle for four hours, and a toll is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and a bar is not a rate and none of those is in a column with the cost of a stone.**

---

**The man of about thirty-eight came into that county on the eighth of a month in Fallowmonth with a cart and a crowbar and a shovel and has been here six weeks and has done four jobs and has a bed in a lodging house at the north end of that street. What he wants is a fifth and a sixth. He has said so to nobody and he said it in that lane by accident on the sixth day of that month, and four people in that town have worked out since that a man who says what he wants by accident has wanted it for some time, and that nobody in that town has ever asked a man with a crowbar what he wants before or since.**

**And the drain is the ordinary end of the ordinary thing.** It goes under the made road in a pipe of about two feet and it has been in that lane longer than the sill has and it is not on any map anybody in that town has, and the silt came out of it in a wet autumn four years back and went into a field and the field is lower than the road. **A man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar says that a drain is a two-day job for two men and that a two-day job for two men in a lane a mile off a market on made ground belonging to nobody is a job that does not get done, and that it is not done because there is no one to be told that it wants doing, and that he has been in this district since the eighth of a month in Fallowmonth and has not been told.**

He said the second part to the man of about thirty-two and not to anybody else. **And a man of about thirty-two with a limp said nothing about that and nobody in that town ever heard it, and a man who has been given a piece of his own trade by a stranger in a lane is not a man who can do anything with it, and the one thing he can do with it is put it to a yard of nine men four hundred miles up that county's own road, and he came up that road twice about two months back and was refused on the second morning and did not send the order again.**

---

**The pointing is lime and sand and water put into the joints of the stone after the stone is laid, and it is the last half-day of the work, and it is the half-day that decides whether the next winter goes under the sill or over it. A man who has done nine of them can tell from across a lane whether the man who did it knew what he was doing, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar has not done nine of them and has done four and knows the difference between the two and says so about once a season.**

**And a bar goes on a rebuilt crossing afterwards, and that bar is not the chain. The chain is three men and a length of iron and it is there because a crossing is shut. The bar that goes on a crossing that has been rebuilt is nine feet of oak with a strap and a chain, and it is there because a load must not go over new pointing until the pointing has cured, and a yard that lifts it out in about half an hour has done a morning's work, and a man who has been kept out of a lane for nineteen days is not the man who decides when it comes off.**

**And a yard of nine men four hundred miles up that county's own road is the yard he has to go to, and the man of twenty-five in it is the one who refused him on a Sunday morning in Wolfmonth in nine words, and neither of the two of them has said a word to the other since that morning, and four people in that county have worked out since that a man of thirty-two with a limp has not used a word for what a man of twenty-five is to him in about two years, and that a man who stops using a word is not a man who has stopped being the thing, and that neither of the two men in that yard has ever been asked about it by the other eight, and that a man who comes nine days on a cart road to stand at a crossing a mile off a market has not worked out on the way that the yard he is going to is the yard he was refused in, and a man who goes to a yard for a drain is not asking that yard for a drain.**

**And two thousand people on the salt four hundred miles off are not in that lane and do not know that it is shut.** A man of about nineteen is standing at one end of a bar four miles off that town and has been standing there for forty-two days. **The two of those are about nine days of cart road apart and nobody in that lane has put them together and nobody is going to.**

**That county trades on a Saturday and on a Thursday and on nothing else in the week, and the Wednesday of that week was an ordinary Wednesday, and the market was shut and two hundred of four thousand people were out of their houses, and a bench of nine sits on Saturdays and did not sit that day. Forty men were on a reach in that county with the fourth bay pointed in the week before, and not one of the forty would put down a tool for a lane a mile off that market.**

---

At about the eighth hour the man of about thirty-eight took the crowbar up and put it in the cart and stood on the soft shoulder for about a minute, which is not a thing a man does on a shoulder that has taken two carts down in nineteen days.

“**Two days for the drain,**” he said. “**Nine days for the sill. Nine days of curing before a load goes over it. Twenty days and a chain, and a chain is not a man, and by the time a man is standing here it will be about half past the fifth hour in the evening in Hearthmonth.**”

“**Can you not do the drain.**”

“**I can do the drain,**” the man of about thirty-eight said. “**I have a barrow and a crowbar and a shovel and I have done about four of them in this county in about six weeks and I get paid by the job and there is no job, and I am not going to be the man who did a drain for nothing in a lane a mile off a market in a month when the man who owns that lane does not know he owns it.**”

**And that is the cost of the sixth day of that month in one lane, and it is a man with a barrow and a crowbar and a shovel who will not do a two-day job for nothing, and nobody in that town has heard one word of it, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp heard all of it and could not pay for it and did not offer, and a man with a limp and a stick who has been in this district since Longlight did not offer a man with a crowbar money and has not offered anybody anything in about four months.**
