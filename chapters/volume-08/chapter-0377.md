# Chapter 0377

**The thirteenth day of Wolfmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where about two thousand people were in a market of four hundred yards and nine hundred stones two hundred yards below that yard, and a man of thirty-two with a limp worked out in that yard at about the fourth hour which two villages are going without for four months, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that anybody has worked it out.**

About two of the nine were in the market at the second hour and about two were waiting for a cart and five were doing something. **A boy of fifteen was at a bench-board copying a figure and the space under it, and a man of about nineteen was planing a board he had been planing for about four months, and a man of about sixty was packing lime against a heap with a lid on it, and a press in a room at the end of the yard was standing open at about thirty-two weeks and was not going to be shut.**

He did the arithmetic out loud, because it was in him and because nobody was going to do it for him and he had been in this district for about ten weeks and had walked the ground himself.

**The four hours are the first four hours of a working day. There are about nine hundred people in four villages within nine miles of a town of about two thousand people on the salt, and about two of the four villages load a cart at the first hour and are on the made road before the second hour, and there is no other way in for a loaded cart out of either of them except nine miles west, and nine miles west is a day there and back.**

“**So a bar is down across the head of that road from the second hour to the sixth hour on four days in seven for the length of four months,**” he said, “**and about two of those four villages lose their cart day in those four days, and they lose it in the four hours they had, and they are going to lose it for four months, and nobody in either of them is going to be told anything about it.**”

**There is a week in a village that has two carts in it and a week that has one, and about two of those four villages have their one cart day on the days the bar is going to be down, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow who had come up that road with him in a cart in the fifth week of that district and had said nothing for five weeks said that those two villages are not nine miles from a market and are eighteen from one on the days the road is shut, and that he has done it, once, in the summer, and it took the whole of a day and a night and he was not paid for the night.**

Nine men in that yard stopped what they were doing for about four seconds and then carried on.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp walked about nine miles of that ground himself in the tenth week of being in that district and worked out in a yard in about four minutes which two villages a bar at four hundred miles would cost, and he did the arithmetic the way a man does it who has been the one who walked it, and not one of the nine in that yard could have done it, and the man of twenty-five could have done it and did not want to.**

---

**And the man of twenty-five said the thing that about four people in that yard had been waiting nine days for somebody to say, and it was not what any of them expected, and it was that it goes in a book.**

“**Then it goes in a book,**” he said. “**Somewhere. Any book. I do not care whose and I do not care what is on the head of it. Those two villages are going to lose four months of a year because nine men in this yard said so, and they are entitled to be told that, and they cannot be told that unless it is written down, and if it is not written down then in nine years one of them is going to say the year we did not come and the year we did not come is the year somebody stole the road off us.**”

“**And it goes in a book,**” the man of twenty-five said again, “**and the men who stand on it go in a book with it, and a man in a book is a man that can be found, and I have not got anything against a man being found.**”

**And then he stopped, four words into the third clause of a sentence, and nine men in a yard heard a man of twenty-five stop, and the thing he stopped on was not the finding and was the book.**

And a man of thirty-two with a limp said no to him, in a yard, in front of nine men, at about the fourth hour of a market Saturday, and he said it in fifty-seven words and the speech as printed is fifty-seven words with the attribution excluded.

“**I have no standing in this yard. I came up that road with an order and you said no, and that is the one thing I have said in this room, and I am not going to put a condition on a refusal. A condition on a refusal is a second refusal with a price on it.**”

---

**And about two people in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp refused a man of twenty-five in that yard in front of nine men, and that it is the first time anybody in that county had seen the two of them refuse each other about a thing, and that neither of them said one word about what it was, and that the man of about sixty kept his hand on a lid through the whole of it, and that the man of about nineteen went out to the gate and came back in and neither of them noticed either time.**

**And the man of about nineteen went out to the gate for about a quarter of an hour and came back in and about four people in that county have said since that a man of nineteen went out of a yard at about the fourth hour on a market Saturday because two men were refusing each other and came back in, and that the yard had been his for four months and it was not his for a quarter of an hour, and that a man of nineteen with an empty hod has a sister of twenty-two in that county he has not spoken to in about a year, and that none of the nine asked him where he had gone, and that one of the nine could have and did not, and that not one of the nine has ever said a word to him about anything except work.**

He could have sent the order up that road a second time. **He had sent it once and been refused and could have sent it again the same week in the same hand on the same paper, and nobody would have sent him away for it, and a second copy of an order is not a second claim, and two people in that county have worked out since that the order was still his to send and he did not send it, and that nobody in that yard had any idea that it happened, and that a man of about nineteen went out to the gate for a quarter of an hour and does not know what he went out for either.**

---

**And before any of that, at about the fourth hour and a half, the man of twenty-five asked him one question that two people in that yard had not expected from a man who had been refused twice in nine days, and the question was what the road is for, and the answer was given in about four seconds and it was the whole of it.**

“**A road four hundred miles off that carries a load in four months of the year when nothing on this side carries,**” the man of thirty-two said, “**and I have never been to it, and I have not been told who is at the other end of it, and the four months are the four months that bar is up in.**”

“**And the four months are a season,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**and a season is not a favour and I have worked that out in about nine days without being told it, and a yard that takes a season on a road it has never walked on is a yard that has bought something and has not had a look at it, and I am not going to pretend to you that I like it.**”

**And a man of twenty-five said that he did not like it and went on setting the terms anyway, and a man in a yard who does not like a thing and does it because nine of them have said it is the ordinary condition of nine men and is not a story and has no form.**

**And two people in that county have said since that a man of about nineteen went out to the gate for about a quarter of an hour and came back in and did not say where he had been, and that a gate is four hundred yards down a road with a market at the bottom of it and a lodging house at the top, and that a man of nineteen can get to either of them and be back in a quarter of an hour, and that nobody in that yard has asked him which of the two he went to and that nobody ever will.**

**And then they looked for somewhere to put it, all of them, in a yard, in about nine minutes, and there was nowhere. Nine men and a man with a limp went through every piece of paper anybody in that county knows of, and there was no column on any of them for why two villages at four hundred miles would not get through a road, and they were not looking for a reason and they were looking for a place to write one down.**

---

**And they did it standing up in a yard on a market Saturday with about two thousand people in the street two hundred yards off, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow said the only thing anybody said in nine minutes that was any use, and what he said was that a why is a thing a person tells a person, and that about two of those four villages will be told a why by somebody in about four months, and that the somebody will be a woman standing in a doorway who does not know that there is a bar.**

A return takes a place and a figure and has four columns with no head on them. A parish roll takes a place and a figure and has been carried every month for nineteen years and has never once been amended by anybody for anything. A bench minute takes what a bench was told, and that bench is asked nothing, and the count on the page under the fourth step of an outside stair is nine and has been nine and nobody in that county is going to go and tell it anything. **The yard's book takes a thing set, and a refusal is a thing set, and there are four of them in a wrapper in a press in a room that anybody can walk into, and the fifth one would be a reason and a reason is not a thing set.**

A man of about thirty-four with a sheet was not in that yard and has not been in that county for a month. **A press in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane two hundred miles off is standing open and has been standing open for about thirty-two weeks, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room into nine counties in two rounds, and not one of the two series that goes out of that room has a column that would take why two villages at four hundred miles would not get through a road.**

**And there is nowhere in this kingdom to enter a why, and it is not a rule and nobody made it, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that nine men spent nine minutes looking for one, and about two thousand people on the salt four hundred miles off are going to find out in about four months that two of their four villages are not getting through a road, and nobody is going to have told them anything, and there is no room anywhere for a person to put the reason down.**

---

**And his decision, which he made in that yard at about the seventh hour of a market Saturday and did not say out loud and has not said out loud, was that the two villages would go without and that he would not put the yard's five terms into a book to stop it and would not put the order up that road a second time to overrule the nine of them, and that both of those were the same decision and that he had made it in about four seconds, and neither of the two things is in anybody's book, and one of the two costs something four hundred miles off and the other one does not, and nobody is going to be told which of the two is which.**
