# Chapter 0360

**The twenty-third day of Harvestmonth, YR 317, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the fourth hour and a half and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where a market four hundred yards long is shut and the county trades on a Saturday and on a Thursday and on nothing else in the week, and nine days off, in forty miles of flat country around a town of two thousand people on the salt, there are nine crossings, and nine lists.**

A Wednesday in that county is a Wednesday. About two hundred of four thousand people were out of their houses and three thousand eight hundred were in them. **Nine men were in a yard four hundred yards up the road, and forty men were on a reach four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of that market, and a man of fifty-five was inside a hut beside three bars on a road above that street, and a boy of fifteen was at a bench-board copying a figure that has been in that book since Longlight and copying the space under it, which is empty, and nobody on that yard has ever told him what goes in the space and nobody is going to.**

**A hundred yards up that county's own road there is a bell of nine stone with a band of forty-one rivets in it, and on that Wednesday it went at the fourth hour and a half, the way it goes on every day of the week, and the two who go are a steward's apprentice of two years and three months who is still not good at it and a carpenter who is a hand on that yard and not a keeper of anything, and the guild pays neither of the pair and neither of the pair is in any company, and four thousand people in that town have never once put the question of who keeps it to anybody, and it does not stop, and nothing in this year or the next has anything to do with it.**

---

**And a market four hundred yards long in a town of four thousand people has a rope at one end of it and a bar at the other, and the bar is for carts and the rope is for stock, and nine hundred stones are set in it in clay and nobody has ever tried to name them, and four feet of the width of that market is a bar of nine feet, and a market in a county that trades on a Saturday and a Thursday and on nothing else in the week is a street on every other day of the week, and four thousand people in that town live on three streets and know nine of them.**

**And nine people crossed four hundred yards of that market between the second hour and the fourth, and four of the nine were somebody else's business, and nobody stood in it, and two thousand people in that town who were not in it do not know that a sheet with a price on it has been read into a hall in that county and a bar on the salt has been told what a keeper is and a set of forty sheets has gone two hundred miles off on a Friday, and two people in that town have worked out since that the three counties that matter to anybody here are not counties anybody can put on one sheet, and that four thousand people in that town and two thousand people on the salt and nine hundred in a county of four market towns are the whole of what anybody has touched so far, and that a woman of thirty-three on the third floor of a building in a provincial town is a fourth place and that she is in no county of her own.**

**And the whole of what stood in that market on that Wednesday was a street, and a bar at the end of a street is a thing a market has got and not a thing anybody stands at, and about nine hundred stones in it are ones a person could name and about nine hundred stones in it are ones nobody ever has.**

**And nine days off, in about forty miles of flat country, there were nine crossings, and a schedule had been read into a guild hall in a fen county in a market on a Saturday with nine crossings and a price on it, and four hundred and nine parish rolls in a building two hundred miles off had carried nine hundred sheets a month into nine counties in two rounds for nineteen years, and not one of those sheets has a place on it for the name of a person.**

Nine crossings and nine lists. One of the nine lists is nineteen years old and has one line folded across the top and about a half inch. One of the nine lists is a page a month in a hand that goes back eleven years. One of the nine lists is a day-book in a hut that cannot be served with anything. One of the nine lists is a set of about forty sheets in a press two hundred miles off that arrived on a Friday under a head. **And four of the nine have nobody at them at all, and one has a bar and a lever and a fulcrum and a woman who is paid for the four months the crossing runs and is at it on the eight as well, and one has a bar and a woman of about forty-four who is not there on the eight months because the tide is in, and two have a bar and a child on a horse and a cart goes round both of them, and one has a bar and a man who is not paid by the guild, which brings it to nine.**

**And the schedule says nine, and the week says nine, and the two nines are not the same nine, and two people in that town have said since that a sheet can say nine and a week can say nine and a man can put them in the same sentence and the sentence is true and means nothing at all, and that this is the only arithmetic anybody in four counties and in forty miles of salt has that nobody has been able to do anything about.**

---

**And a hundred yards off that bell a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in the wet at about the fourth hour of that Wednesday for about twenty minutes and went back down the road without going up, and about two people in that town have been saying since that a man who has stood under that bell four times has never once been asked about it and has never once asked, and that those two are the same thing and are not the same thing, and that four thousand people in that town do not know either.**

**And at the back of a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road a man of about sixty packed lime against a heap that has a lid on it, and he is the only man in that yard who is not paid out of it, and he said nothing on that Wednesday, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a man who said one true thing about a lid out loud in a year said nothing on a Wednesday when a yard was in the fourth month of a season it did not have, and that a man who says nothing is not a man who has stopped noticing, and that a lid is a lid in any month.**

**And a woman of twenty-nine in a provincial town two hundred miles off was at a table at about the fourth hour of that same Wednesday with a register of seven questions in front of her, all of them questions and not one of them a finding, and a day-book on a shelf behind her at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and a press in that room standing open at twenty weeks, and a woman of thirty-three at a column on the floor above her at the same hour who did not look up, and neither of them entered an eighth question and neither of them was thanked.**

**And a woman of fifty in a town of two thousand people on the salt folded the ninth line of a list of nine crossings across the top and about a half inch, and a woman of about forty-one put a bar down and took it up four times between the second hour and the fourth hour at a crossing four miles off that town, and eleven people went through it and four carts, and a bar that is up for eight months of the year is a quarter of a mile that a cart goes round, and a quarter of a mile is not on anybody's sheet and cannot be and there is no form on which a county can enter a quarter of a mile.**

**And about two people in that town have worked out a sheet can say nine and a week can say nine, and that the two nines are not the same nine, and that the reason a week is a worse record than a sheet is that a week counts what came and a sheet counts what was written down, and that in about four hundred years of that county there has never once been a week that anybody could produce, and that nine people in four counties have been keeping something for four months that is closer to a week than anything in this kingdom has ever been, and that nobody made the rule and there is no form for it.**

**And eight hundred people were in a field above a ditch, drinking out of a four-foot ditch with two inches of brown water in the bottom of it, and not one of those figures can be added to another of them, and there is nobody anywhere in four counties or in forty miles of salt who can be shown all of them at once, and nobody took that decision, and it is not a rule, and it is not a charter, and there is no form for it in this kingdom.**

**And three hundred yards above the market there is a hut beside three bars on a road, and a man of about fifty-five has been inside that hut nineteen years putting the bars down and up for whoever goes through, and a bar is a bar and not a gate, and there is no form on that barrier and no book at that hut, and a hut on a road in that county holds a stool, a lamp and nine tools and nothing else anybody has ever put in it, and two people in that county have worked out since that nineteen years of putting a bar down and lifting it is a longer record than any of the nine sets of about forty sheets, and that the hut has a lamp in it and a lamp in a hut on a barrier is not a record of anything and is the only thing in that county that has happened every day without interruption since before a boy of fifteen was born.**

**And a road is a line and two hands and a date and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it, and a crossing on the salt has two hands and a date and a witness, and what is on the Crown's sheet is a place and a figure and a head over a column, and a place and a figure are not two ends and there is no column for a witness, and four thousand people in that town do not know that there is a line of nine crossings on a sheet nine days away, and two thousand people on the salt do not know that the sheet has a column headed *Keeper* on it, and a bell a hundred yards up a road went at the fourth hour and a half and went on going, and a street four hundred yards long is still a street on a Wednesday, and a bar is still a bar on the eight months of the year when nobody crosses.**
