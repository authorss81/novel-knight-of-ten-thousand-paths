# Chapter 0376

**The ninth day of Wolfmonth, YR 317, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the eighth hour, in that yard, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, four hundred yards up the county's own road and a quarter of a mile above its market, where nine men were at a bench-board about nine feet long and a press stood open in a room at the end of it, and the terms of a road four hundred miles off were read out at that bench-board by a man of twenty-five who said before anybody had asked him that they were not his, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a yard of nine men has set terms on anything.**

He came up that road on the third day as he had decided to, and he came up it once. **The market was shut on a Tuesday and the reach four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of it had forty men on it, and a man of twenty-five was at the bench-board and had said the whole of what he had to say on a Sunday and had said nothing since, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a man who has refused an order in nine words and has said nothing for two days afterwards is a man who has spent the two days finding out what the nine of them will agree to.**

“**It is not mine,**” the man of twenty-five said, before anybody had asked him anything.

“**I have not asked you anything.**”

“**You came up that road a second time on a Sunday and a third time today,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**and about four of the nine have been in this yard since and about four have not, and the four who have not have been told and have said nothing, and the four who said nothing did it because the other four had said nothing first, and that is how a yard of nine men says a thing, and I have not said a word to you about it and I am not going to.**”

**And a man of twenty-five told a man of thirty-two with a limp that the terms were not his before the man had spoken, and a man who gives away an order has to keep giving it away or he has only borrowed it, and that is what a yard is, and it has no form, and about four thousand people in that town could not tell you how it works.**

---

**And the terms are these, and they are five, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow who was in that lane for the third day running asked for them in writing and was told there was nothing to write them on.**

The first is the yard's own and it is four years old. **Nothing that belongs to a man goes into that yard's book and only a thing set goes into it. So there is no sheet, and the crossing is not on a sheet, and the men who stand on it are not on a roll, and nobody in four counties can be shown a figure against that road, and the man of twenty-five said that this was the first thing he said and that he said it first because everything else on the list depends on it and because he was tired of hearing it asked about.**

**And the way the nine of them said a thing, in a yard with a bench-board about nine feet along, is this. Four of them were in and four were out. The four who were in said nothing first, and the four who were out were told in a doorway and said nothing, and the two fours then had nothing to argue about because there was nothing in the room, and two people in that county have worked out since that this is a slower instrument than a meeting and is a better one, and that it has been used in that yard for about four years, and that about four thousand people in that town could not describe it and would not believe it if it were described to them.**

---

The second is a bar and a man. **The bar is nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain in two brackets, and it goes down at the second hour and comes up at the sixth, and that is four hours, and a man is on it while it is down, and the four hours are the first four hours of that county's working day, and the three other days in seven the bar is up and the road is a road and a cart goes round.**

---

**And about four people in that yard have said since that a yard of nine men with a man on a bar four hundred miles off for four hours on four days in seven has worked out, in a yard, in about nine days, that the man of nineteen keeps his own three days and the yard is four hours a day lighter for the four, and that nobody in that yard has worked out yet what the yard does about the fourteen weeks, and that about two people in that county have.**

The third is that a man at a bar is not a wage. **It is a man of the nine taken out of a season for four hours on four days in seven for the length of four months of somebody else's year, and the yard does not get it back, and the yard is not going to be paid for it out of anything, and a man of twenty-five said the third of his five terms in the past tense, and a man who says a thing in the past tense about a thing that has not happened has already made up his mind and is telling the room about the decision he has already made.**

The fourth is the two things that get put up. **A bar, and a thing nine feet by seven with a stove in it and boards across the top of it, and a bench inside it, and the bench is there because a man cannot stand for four hours for four months on a made road.**

**Fifteen pence the oak, and twelve pence the strap and the chain and the two brackets, and eight pence the man who fits it, and fifteen and twelve is twenty-seven pence, and twenty-seven pence and eight pence is thirty-five pence, and thirty-five pence is two shillings and elevenpence, and that is the whole of what a crossing costs to make and none of what it costs to hold.**

The price on the Crown's own sheet for a cart at the far end of that road is two shillings. **A toll is not a wage and that is not a wage and the two are not in a column with each other and are not added to each other anywhere in this kingdom, and both figures are correct, and the difference between them is the whole of what has happened in forty miles of flat country in a year.**

---

**And two people in that county have said since that the five terms were said in about a quarter of an hour in a yard in ordinary words with no figure in any of them except two, and that the two were about a bar and not about a man, and that a man of twenty-five who has been thinking about a season for about a year put no figure on himself anywhere in a quarter of an hour and has not been asked to.**

**The fifth is the only one of the five that anybody asked for, and it is not about a bar at all.**

---

**He had held an empty hod for four months and had been in that yard every day of it, and he said one thing, and it was the only sentence said in that yard in a quarter of an hour that was about a person's own day, and nobody asked him for it and nobody thanked him for it, and it was the only one of the five that had a man in it who was going to be affected by it, and about four months of a year is going to be decided in a yard by nine men, and the only one of the nine who is going to stand on the bar is the one who said the least about it.**

“**Three days in seven the bar is up,**” he said. “**I have a trade of my own on those three days at the third hour and it is nine pence a day, and nine pence on three days in seven is not a wage for a man of nineteen and it is not nothing, and I have not had it said out loud in a yard in about four months and I am saying it out loud in a yard now because we are setting terms and that is what terms are for.**”

Nobody in that yard said anything about it. **The man of twenty-five wrote nothing down, because it is a term and a term is a claim and a claim is not a thing set, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a man of nineteen got his three days in seven by asking for them out loud in a yard where nine men were setting terms, and that nobody agreed to them and nobody disagreed with them, and that a term nobody has agreed to is the only kind there is that cannot be taken off a man afterwards.**

**And there is a sister of his in that county of twenty-two and he has not spoken to her in about a year, and not one of the nine in that yard has asked him about that, and a man who asks a man of nineteen about a sister is a man who has been told nothing about how long the not-speaking has been going on, and nobody in that yard has been told and nobody in that yard was going to be.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp asked one question at the fourth hour and the answer was given in about nine seconds by a man of twenty-five, and the question was whether the four hours could be any four hours, and the answer was that they were the first four hours of a working day because that is when a man in that yard eats, and a man who has not eaten is not standing at a bar, and a man of twenty-five has thought about a bar four hundred miles off for about nine days and has come out of it with a table of a man's day and not with a rate, and he has not said that and would not.**

**And the man of about sixty did a sixth thing at about the fourth hour, and it was not a term and it was not in the list, and a man of about sixty who is the only man in that yard not paid out of it gave two bushels of lime out of a heap of his own for the boards across the top of a thing nine feet by seven that is going to stand on made ground four hundred miles off, and the lid stayed on the heap while he did it.**

“**That is not out of the yard,**” he said, when the man of twenty-five looked at him.

“**It is not in the book,**” the man of twenty-five said.

“**It is not in the book and it is not out of the yard,**” the man of about sixty said, “**and I have a heap with a lid on it and the lid is for weather and not for a shed, and the two are about four hundred miles apart and the weather in between is the same weather.**”

**And a man of about sixty who has said one true thing about a lid out loud in a year said a second one at about the fourth hour on a Tuesday in a yard in front of nine men, and neither of the two men who said a true thing that day said it to the man who came up the road, and both of them said it to lids and to heaps and to a board about nine feet long, and that is the only way a yard of nine men has ever given anything away.**

---

**And at the eighth hour the five terms had been said and none of them had been written down, and about two people in that yard have worked out since that a yard of nine men had agreed to hold a bar and a man and a shed at the end of a made road four hundred miles off for four months of a year that the yard will not see the end of, and that about four thousand people in that town do not know it, and that the press in the room at the end of that yard is standing open with four sheets in it that have nothing on the wrapper, and that not one of the five terms is in the book and not one of the five terms is anywhere else.**

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a heap of lime and a man of nineteen's three days in seven and the fourth month of a shut season that a yard of nine men is now spending on somebody else's four months, and about two people in that county have said since that a yard of nine men has just bought itself a season with a road four hundred miles off and does not know yet which of the two seasons it has bought, and that a yard with a bridge rate and eight men either borrows a crew or loses the rate, and that nobody on that yard has worked that out yet, and that the man of about sixty worked it out eleven years ago about a different thing and did not say it.**
