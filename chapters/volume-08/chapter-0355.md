# Chapter 0355

**The twenty-first day of Rainmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, at a crossing four miles south of a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a bar of oak and iron goes down and comes up, and a woman of about forty-one was on her own side of it, and a man of about thirty came up the made road with a sheet of about nine hundred sheets' worth of printing on it, and about two thousand people in that town do not know he came, and about four thousand people nine days off do not know there is a bar.**

A market morning on the salt is a morning when the carts come in from the flats and go out again, and the crossing runs four months of the year and this is the fourth month. About two hundred of about two thousand people were out of their houses. **And the man who came up that made road at about the fourth hour had been in nine counties in about nineteen years and had been to about nine hundred sheets in that time, and about four people in that county have worked out since that the whole of that man is a person who goes from a person to a person with a sheet, and that he is not paid by the day and is not paid by the sheet and is paid out of a box by somebody who has never been in this county, and that a person who goes from a person to a person with a sheet for nineteen years is a person about whom nobody has ever asked a single question, and that this is the ordinary condition of an office and is not a conspiracy and is not anybody's fault.**

**And he had a want of his own on that Saturday morning and it had nothing to do with anybody's paper.** He wanted to be at the fourth house in that lane by the third hour because a woman at the fourth house had a boy with a bad wrist and he had a trade that took wrists in about nine minutes and he wanted to be there and get it done and not be behind. He said so, to the woman of about forty-one, without being asked and in one breath, when she told him she was busy.

“**I have to be at the fourth house by the third hour,**” he said. “**There is a boy at the fourth house with a wrist and it is nine minutes and I would rather do it before I do anything else.**”

“**Then go and do it,**” she said.

“**I will go and do it,**” he said, and went, and came back at about the fifth hour and finished the conversation about the bar, and about two people in that county have said since that a man who puts a Crown price on nine crossings went to a fourth house in a lane first and set a boy's wrist in nine minutes, and that both of those things are the ordinary work of an ordinary morning, and that the sheet was in his bag the whole time.

**And the man who came up that made road at about the fourth hour did not have a warrant, and about two people in that county have said since that in eleven years not one man with a sheet has come up that road with a warrant, and that this is because a warrant is a thing a person can refuse and a sheet is a thing a person can be shown, and that the office that sends the sheets knows which of the two is cheaper.**

---

“**You keep the bar,**” the man said.

“**I put it down and I take it up.**”

“**That is not what I asked.**”

“**It is what I do,**” the woman said. “**Ask me the next one.**”

“**What is your trade.**”

And about two people in that county have said since that a woman of about forty-one was asked her trade at a bar by a stranger in the ordinary way, on an ordinary morning, with a sheet, and that she answered it, and that the answer was two words and that they were the correct two and that the sheet did not need a third word from her or from anybody else in that room.

“**A toll-keeper,**” she said. “**Eleven years. My mother before me. It is not a guild and it is not on any roll.**”

The man put the sheet up on the flat of the parapet, uncreased, with one hand, the way a man does who has carried a sheet nine days. **And the woman looked at it, and about two people in that county have worked out a woman read all of it in about nine seconds including the six columns and the five heads and the sixth column with no head on it, and that a person who takes a toll for a living is the sort of person who reads a sheet the way other people read a bar, which is to say to find out what it is going to cost her.**

“**Two shillings a cart,**” she said.

“**Two shillings a cart for the season.**”

“**That is six times what I take.**”

“**It is a price and it is a price on a crossing and I am not the office and I do not set it.**”

“**Then you are a man with a sheet,**” the woman said, “**and I am a woman with a bar, and one of those two is the whole of what you came out here for, and I would like to know which one of us you came to see.**”

---

**And he said it, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has said in about nine counties in nineteen years and has never once had to explain.**

“**A keeper,**” he said. “**The office keeps a roll of who keeps what in nine counties. A toll-keeper is a keeper. A ferryman is a keeper. A wicket-keeper, a bar-keeper, a lock-keeper, a man who keeps a sluice, a woman who keeps a landing, a man who keeps a pound.**”

“**And it is on the sheet.**”

“**It is the fourth column and it has been the same column for two years and the head over it says *Keeper*,**” the man said, “**and it has a person in it and a person in it is a trade, and the trade is written down in the fourth column every month out of a roll that goes back nineteen years, and I did not write any of it and I have never seen a trade in that column and I do not need to.**”

**And about four people in that county have worked out since that a man with a sheet stood at a bar and said out loud that he did not need to see what was in the column, and that the whole of the power of a column is that nobody has to be in the room, and that a man who fills in a column for nineteen years from a roll has never once met a person whose trade he wrote down and has never once been asked whether the person would have written it down if there had been somewhere to write it.**

“**It is not names,**” the woman said.

“**It is trades,**” the man said. “**A name is a name and I could not get nine hundred names in a month in any county in this kingdom and neither could anybody. A trade is the same on every sheet and it is the same in a parish roll and it is the same in the roll at the office and it goes into the third column and into the fourth and the third is a person having knowledge and the fourth is a keeper, and the two of them take it the same way.**”

“**And the third one is a man who knows something.**”

“**The third one is a man who knows something,**” the man said, “**and I do not know your name and I do not want it and there is no column for it.**”

**And about two people in that county have worked out since that a man said the whole of how a price is put on a crossing out loud at a bar, and that he said it the way a man says a thing he has said in nine counties, and that he did not once say the office, and that he did not once say a name, and that the two things a man does not say in a conversation like that are the two things that would have made it possible to be angry at anybody in particular, and that about four people in that county have worked out since that being angry at a column is a thing you have to do on your own and that nobody will help you and that about half of what is on the sheet is not anybody's fault.**

---

**And the woman of about forty-one put her hands on the parapet of her own crossing and said the thing that is the whole of this volume, and she said it once that day, and she had said it nine years before to a man who was complaining about the price of salt, and nobody improved on it and nobody is going to.**

“**A crossing is not a line. A line has two ends and two hands and a date, and the moment a crossing is a line on somebody's paper it is a toll, and a toll is the only kind of paper anybody obeys.**”

The man of about thirty did not write it down. He had a sheet in his hand and there were about nine lines of free space on the back of it and about two people in that county have worked out since that a man standing at a bar with a sheet on his arm was the single most convenient person in that county to write a thing on and did not, and that the reason he did not is not a rule and is not a kindness and is that a toll-keeper who has just told you that a line becomes a toll the moment it is on paper is a person you would not put a pen in the hand of.

“**I am not going to argue with you,**” he said, and squared the sheet against the parapet, and put it away.

“**I did not ask you to,**” the woman said. “**I asked you which one of us you came to see, and you have not told me, and it is the fourth hour and there is a cart coming.**”

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was four miles north of that bar at about the sixth hour of that same Saturday at a table in a room with a door on a lane, and he had a list in front of him that he had made himself, and it was a list of nine crossings and it was on one side of a page and it had no heads on it and no columns and it was a list, and it was the first schedule anybody had made in this kingdom in about nineteen years and he had made it in about nine minutes in a yard with a stick against a bench-board, and he had not told anybody he had made it, and there is nowhere in this kingdom to enter it.**

And about two people in that county have worked out since that a man of thirty-two who has crossed at least four times in nine years and who has spent nine days on a cart came back from the flats with a list he made himself, and that a list of nine crossings with no heads on it and no columns is exactly as capable of being a schedule as a sheet with six columns and five heads is, and that the only difference between the two is that one of them has never been printed and the other one goes out about nine hundred times a month into nine counties in two rounds.

**And the ninth crossing of the nine, which is a place about four miles north where the water is up to a cart's axle in Rainmonth, and which is a good crossing, and which about four hundred people could use and about nine do, was not on that man's list and was not asked about that day, and about two people in that town have worked out since that a man with a sheet went up a made road on a Saturday morning and talked to about four people, and there are nine crossings in that list and about five of them are on his and he did not know that, and that a schedule of nine which is short by four is a schedule of nine and not a schedule of the nine, and that nobody in that county has a second list to check it against, and that the woman who has one has refused to look at his.**

**And the woman of about forty-one put her bar down at about the sixth hour of that Saturday for a cart with salt in it, and took her penny, and put it up, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that the whole price of her crossing had been explained to her by a man who did not want to be told that he was explaining it, and about four thousand people nine days off do not know that anybody has asked her what she keeps.**
