# Chapter 0393

**The first day of Thawmonth, YR 318, a Tuesday and not a market day, four hundred miles from here, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, on a flat country of nine miles by four with a river across the middle of it, about nine dwellings on the near bank and about four hundred people on the far one, and a bar of nine feet of oak in the kerb at the top of a rise on the only road out, and nobody on either side of that bar has said a date and no month and no weekday has been said on that road.**

## One

The bar went up at the first hour three mornings ago. **It is nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain, and a man of about thirty with a mallet cut the sockets in that kerb on the morning before in about two hours, and the sockets are four inches deep, and the road is nine feet wide, and the two of them together are the width of the road and there is nothing else in the kerb for about two miles either way.** The road out runs about four hundred yards from the water to the top of the rise. The ground is soft on the north of it. To the south of it the ground drops four feet to the river over about nine yards of a bank that a man can get down in a wet week and that no cart has ever been down.

**About four hundred people are on the far side of that bar and they have been on the far side of it their whole lives, and none of them has ever been the one who could not get up the road, and that is the first thing that has to be understood about what happened three mornings ago and it is the reason nobody in that place is standing at the bar.**

There were nine of them on the wrong side of it. **Nine feet of oak went into a kerb at the first hour and they had gone over at first light, in the twenty minutes that the crossing gives at that state of the water, and they were back at the top of the rise by the time the light was on the water. The crossing was running and it runs four months of the year and it was in one of the four. Not one of the nine saw it go up, because they were behind a rise and nine feet of oak makes no sound that carries three hundred yards in still air.**

A woman of about thirty was the oldest of the nine and she had a basket on her hip and she was the one who had said the thing that went round that place inside two days, which was that you have shut us off from the far side, and the steward had said yes.

## Two

She did not go up to the bar. **That was the thing about her that the people on that river worked out afterwards and had not noticed at the time: in three days she did not go and stand at it once, and she did not go and look at it, and she did not go within a hundred yards of it, and she got on with about four hundred people who had a week of work in them.**

The far side has a lane in it and about nine holdings on the lane and a lime bed four hundred yards up the lane, and a woman of about thirty-four whose holding is the second one has about two hundred bushels of stone in a kiln that she has been filling since before the water turned. **A kiln takes about a week to fill and about two days to empty, and a woman who fills one has to be at it four times in a day whatever else is happening in the world, and the three of them who went over that morning were over it because of the kiln and the two hundred bushels, and one of them is the woman of about thirty-four at the second holding and she has been at it four times in a day for three days and a kiln is not a thing you can be away from for a week.**

The rest of them are on the far side for reasons that have nothing to do with a bar. A man of about forty-four is there because his load is on the near side of the rise and has been for nine days. A boy of about thirteen is there because a boy of about thirteen goes where his mother is. **A woman of about nineteen is there because there is a wedding at the third holding on the far side and she was in it and she has a sister on the near side whom she has not spoken to in about two years, and that is the one the steward was asked about first and the one nobody argued with, and on the second morning she asked the steward to her face whether the bar came down for one person and he said that it did not come down for one person and she said that she had thought it might and he said that he understood why and that he could not do it, and that was the last conversation anybody in that place had with the steward about the bar and there was not another one.**

The four hundred went to work. They have a lime bed and a lane and nine holdings and a river and a week, and the lane runs four hundred yards from the bar to the kiln and four hundred yards the other way to the top of the rise, and every one of those four hundred yards is on the far side of a bar that was not there a week ago, and about four hundred people went up and down it four times a day and did not stop. **A bar that is up does not put anybody out of work. That is the thing about a bar and it is the reason the four hundred are not waiting with the nine: a lime bed does not care whose name is on a sheet, and a kiln does not, and a lane does not, and about four hundred people on that side of nine feet of oak went to work on the first morning and have gone to work on the three mornings since.**

## Three

The man of about thirty-four came down that lane at about the second hour on the fourth morning, on foot, from the far side of the rise. He had a coat on that was made for a person who is cold and he was not that kind of cold. He came down about two thousand yards of lane and stopped about nine feet off her and did not say anything for a moment, and the kiln was going and there was about nine feet of lane between him and the kiln wall and she had to turn her head to see him at all.

“**Has anybody come,**” he said.

“**No,**” she said.

“**Is there anything you want me to take up.**”

And she thought about it, and it is worth saying that she thought about it, because a person who does not think about it has not been asked a fair question.

“**Salt,**” she said. “**We have about a week of it. And a sack of nails, and the man at the third holding has a bad tooth and there is a man on the other side of that rise who pulls them.**”

“**I will take it up tomorrow.**”

And then she asked him the only question she was going to ask him, and she asked it standing still with her hand on the sill of the kiln door, and it is worth giving it as it was given.

“**Is it coming down,**” she said.

“**I do not know,**” he said. “**It is not mine and nobody has said and I have been up there four times this morning asking three people and one of them was the man who cut the sockets and he said he had been told nothing either. That is all I have got. It is not coming down on a day that anybody has said out loud and I would tell you if I had one.**”

“**All right,**” she said.

“**You are not going to ask me why,**” he said, and she said that she was not, and he stood in the lane with the answer to that in his hand and it was more than he had come down for.

“**You can take it up every day for the rest of the year,**” she said, “**and it will not be different, and I am telling you that so that you can decide what to do with your day. I am not asking you for anything and I am not thanking you and I have not got a thing here that you would want.**”

**And she went back to the kiln and he stood in the lane for a while and then went back up it, and about four hundred people on that side of a bar went on with a week, and about nine people on that side of a bar went on with the same nine days they have been having, which is the whole of what nine people do when they cannot get up a road and nobody has told them a date.**

## Four

At about the fifth hour the woman of about thirty-four at the second holding came out of the kiln and stood in the lane and said one thing to a man about thirty-four, and she said it to him and not to the woman of about thirty, and it was about nine feet of lane and it was not unkind.

“**You are going to come down here every morning,**” she said, “**and in a place of four hundred people a man walking two thousand yards of lane every morning is a thing four hundred people talk about, and there is nothing else in it for them to talk about, and they are not cruel and they will not stop.**”

“**I know.**”

“**Then do not come,**” she said, “**and do not send anybody, and do not stand up there in the morning and look at it, because they can see the top of the rise from a kiln.**”

And the man of about thirty-four said that he was sorry and she said that he was not to be, because there was nothing to be sorry for, and that was the end of it, and he went back up the lane.

## Five

And here is the thing that is true and that nobody in that place said and that has to be written down because it happened: **the crossing on the river was running.** It was in one of its four months and it was giving about twenty minutes a day at that state of the water and about four hundred yards of the lane came down to it in about nine minutes. The woman of about thirty could see the water from where she stood at the kiln door. She had been down the bank twice in her life. **The difficulty was not the river. The difficulty was three hundred yards of soft ground and a bar in a kerb at the top of a rise, and a bar of nine feet of oak is not a river and cannot be swum and is nine feet from one end of a road to the other and there is no going over it and no going round it and no going under it, and three hundred yards of soft ground on the north of a made road will not carry a barrow either and a handcart goes into it up to the axle by the second push.**

**And nobody on either side of that bar has said when it will come down and stay down.** It is not a sentence with a date at the end of it. It is a bar and a chain and about nine hundred people who are the only people on that flat country who can put it up and take it down, and about four hundred of them are on the far side of it and are not asked and have not asked, and there is no room anywhere on that river in which a person could enter why, and that is not an oversight. It is what about nine hundred people did in a morning, and then they went to work.

**About nine people are on the wrong side of a shut road in a place of about four hundred people, and the four hundred are at their week, and the nine are at the end of theirs. The crossing is running four months out of the year and is four hundred yards away and is not the difficulty, and nobody on either side of that kerb has been told a day and nobody is going to be.**
