# Chapter 0378

**The twenty-seventh day of Wolfmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, on a made road four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt, at the head of a made road that runs west, where a bar of nine feet of oak went into two brackets in a kerb on the twenty-fourth of that month at about the second hour and a man has been standing at one end of it since, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that anything has been put in the ground that week.**

He came off the long road with a change of horses at two inns and had been in that district nine days. **A made road runs west off the main road for about a mile and then stops at a bank, and there is nine yards of soft shoulder on the salt side of it and a ditch that takes the water off that shoulder in a wet week, and the bar is in the kerb on the made side where the ground is hard, and that is a decision about nine yards that took a man of about thirty with a mallet about two hours.**

The bar went in on the twenty-fourth, which was a Wednesday, and not a market day. **A man of about thirty with a mallet and a plank and nine nails cut two sockets in a kerb and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow stood and held the bar while he cut them, and a woman of about forty-one with a lever and a stone, and a boy of about eleven with a pail, and a man of about nineteen from a yard four hundred miles off who had not been appointed to do any of it and was not asked to do any of it and was standing at the far end of the made road with a handcart.**

He came off the long road on the eighteenth of that month and was in that lane on the nineteenth and did not go to the head of that made road until the twenty-seventh, and a man who can be got to a thing on the second day goes to it on the third day, and nobody in that town has ever known him to be late to anything and nobody has ever known him to go early either.

**And he stood at the bank at the end of that made road and did not go down the soft shoulder and did not put a hand on the bar and did not put a hand on the kerb, and two people in that town have worked out since that a man who has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight stood at the top of a bank nine feet high for about two hours in Wolfmonth and watched four people put a bar in, and that getting up a bank is about four seconds longer than sitting down at it and that a man who has been told that by his own body for about two years does not test it in front of people.**

---

**And there are two things built on that road, and the first of the two is not what anybody would call a building, which is nine feet of oak with an iron strap and a chain across a made road with a man standing at one end of it, and the second is a shed nine feet by seven with a stove in it and boards across the top of it and a bench inside it, and the bench is there because a man cannot stand for four hours for four months.**

**The materials of the second one came off a handcart that arrived on the twenty-fourth with a bar in it and a bundle of boards and two bushels of lime in a barrow, and a hundred nails, and a sheet of iron about three feet by two, and a man of about thirty-one who was not on that yard came with the cart.**

---

**And two people in that district have worked out since that a socket in a kerb is not taught and is not in any book and is done by a man who has watched a man do it nine times and then done it once, and that the man of about thirty had watched it nine times in a county four hundred miles off, and that two thousand people in that town do not know that the thing holding a bar in the ground is the work of one man nobody sent.**

---

A hundred nails at four pence, and a sheet of iron at nine pence, and two bushels of lime at three pence a bushel. **Four pence and nine pence is thirteen pence, and thirteen pence and six pence is nineteen pence, and nineteen pence is a shilling and sevenpence, and that is the whole of what a shed of nine feet by seven costs in materials, and the stove came out of a kiln two miles off Marl that has been out of use since a kiln is a thing a man does not shut.**

**And the fifteen pence and the twelve pence and the eight pence that the bar itself came to four hundred miles off were said in a yard on a Tuesday in that month by nine men and were not said here, and a bar and a shed four miles from a town of two thousand people cost thirty-five pence and nineteen pence, and neither of the two figures is on anything, and neither of the two was told to anybody on that road, and both of them were got right.**

**And a bar is not a shed and a shed is not a bar, and the two of them are not added to each other anywhere and are not in a column with each other, and a rate is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and none of the three is in a column with either of the other two, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that a crossing has been made about half a mile from the end of a made road, and that what it is worth is not a figure anybody has.**

---

**And what a man does for four hours at the end of a bar on a made road is this. He stands with his back to a kerb where the ground is hard, on the side that the carts come from, and he watches the made side and he watches the shoulder, and about two hundred times in four hours a person comes up the made road and goes past him and about nine times in four hours a cart comes up the made road and he has to decide in about a second and a half whether a cart can get round the bank or whether it has to go back, and about four people in that district have said since that the whole of that man job is nine decisions in four hours and everything else is standing.**

**And the soft shoulder is nine yards wide and it takes a loaded cart down it about two inches in nine yards, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow walked it on the twenty-seventh and said that a loaded cart down that shoulder once is a cart that wants three hours, and that a boy of about eleven with a pail was on the shoulder every hour of that day because a ditch four hundred yards west of that bar had two inches of brown water in the bottom of it in Wolfmonth for the first time since Mudmonth, and nobody had ruled that ditch and nobody was going to.**

**And the man who put it in was not the yard either. A yard of nine men four hundred miles up a county's own road set five terms on a Tuesday and did not write any of them down, and what came off that yard in the week after the terms were set was a bar and two brackets and a chain and a handcart, and a yard which sets terms and does not write them down still sends the iron, and the iron arrived on time and in good order, and nobody on either end of that road knew what it was for until the second hour of the twenty-fourth.**

**And the man of about thirty-one who was not on that yard made sure of the iron and the lime and counted the nails, and he was not appointed by anybody, and the only person who was in the whole of it who had been asked to do anything was the man of about thirty with a mallet, and he had been asked by a man of about thirty-three with a barrow on the morning of the twenty-fourth and not by anybody at all before that.**

**And the man standing at the end of the bar at about the fifth hour on the twenty-seventh of that month was about nineteen years old and came from a yard four hundred miles up that fen county's own road and had been in that yard for about four years, and two thousand people in that town had walked past him four times that day and not one of them had asked him his name and he had not offered it to anybody.**

---

**And two people in that district have said since that a stove out of a kiln two miles off Marl that has been out of use since a kiln is a thing a man does not shut is worth nothing and is the reason a man can stand at a bar in the second month of a winter, and that about nine hundred yards of that district has a bar and a shed and a man on them in the middle of a night, and that none of the three is in a book.**

---

He had a trade of his own at the third hour on three days in seven and it is nine pence a day. **He was at that bar on the other four days from the second hour to the sixth hour, and about nine people in that district have worked out since that a man of nineteen who asked a yard for three days in seven in a quarter of an hour on the morning the terms were set has got them, and that nobody agreed to them and nobody disagreed with them, and that the only way three days in seven happens in a yard of nine men is that the man who is going to stand on the bar is the man who asked.**

**And he had come four hundred miles off his own trade and nobody had sent him and nobody had appointed him and his own yard's book has nothing in it about him, and there is no sheet anywhere in four counties with his name on it, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane two hundred miles off into nine counties in two rounds and not one of them is him and not one of them has a place in it that would take him.**

**And a yard of nine men set five terms in Wolfmonth and one of the nine did not write them down because a term is a claim and a claim is not a thing set, and the man who is standing at that bar four hundred miles off is the one of the nine about whom nothing was written down, and two people in that county have said since that a yard which cannot hold a promise in a book got a man four hundred miles off by not writing anything, and that the arrangement fits inside a page with nothing on the wrapper and that nobody in four counties has ever read the wrapper.**

---

**And about two villages go without that head of road for four hours on four days in seven for the length of four months, and nobody in either of them has been told why, and there is nowhere in this kingdom to enter a why, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that two villages nine miles off have been given a bar and a man in the first four hours of a working day.**

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp watched a bar go down and a man walk to the far end of it and put his back against the kerb, and a man who had been there four hours stood where he had been put and did not move, and the two of them were about nine feet apart for about an hour and neither of them said one word to the other, and what passed between them was nothing at all, and both of them stayed.**

**And a woman of about forty-one with a lever came by at about the eighth hour on her way to her own bar four miles south and stopped for about a minute and said the bar looked well and said nothing else about it, and the man of about thirty-two with a limp said that the man who put it in had cut two sockets in a kerb in about two hours and that a socket in a kerb is not a thing anybody is taught and that somebody had to have done it before.**

**And that was the whole of what he said about it, and the man of about nineteen at the end of the bar did not turn round, and the woman of about forty-one went on to her own four miles, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that the first of the two things on that road is a crossing and that a person is on it.**
