# Chapter 0394

**The fifth day of Thawmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a market street of four hundred yards on that coast, about two days west along a salt road from Marl, and in a room at the back of a salt warehouse off that street, where about nine hundred people eat off eleven boats and about four hundred people nine miles up that road have been between two prices since the third week of a month in Hearthmonth, and about nine hundred people on a coast do not know what the four of them are going to do this year.**

## One

The market had been full since the first hour and the tide was making. **A market four hundred yards on that coast is not a market of four hundred yards of stone two days east, because everything in it is either wet or in a net, and about two hundred people were in it out of a good many more than that, and that is a coast.** Four of the eleven went out and came back on the turn of the tide and one of those four is a boat a man of about fifty has run for twenty-two years and is not on any roll in any county.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet came into that market at the fourth hour on foot with a bag and eleven days of walking in him, and he had walked it because he had been told there was a road there that was shut and a woman on that coast who had not said yes and a yard eleven days east that had said one sentence in a room, and a man who counts what goes out is a man who walks to find out what has come in.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp came into that market behind him at the fourth hour and a half, and he had walked the same eleven days, and nobody had sent him, and nobody had asked him to, and there was no errand in that county for him and he was not on the round and he is not on any roll in any county he has been in.**

Nobody said anything about the two of them coming up the same road.

## Two

In the room at the back of the salt warehouse there were four people, and three of them had said something in it before, and the fourth had not.

**The man of about fifty asked his question for the second time and it was a question about a season and not about a boat.** He had asked it in the third week of a month in Hearthmonth, standing at about the eighth hour in a market street, and he had said then that he wanted to know before the season started whether the four of the eleven were going out, and that a man who has run a boat for twenty-two years can take a season or leave it and cannot do it in the middle of it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it down at the time because it was about a season and a date and not about a boat.

He asked it again at about the fifth hour in that room and he asked it of the woman of about thirty-four, and he said that he had left a mate of nineteen in charge with the tide tables in the middle of a night about two months ago, and that he had walked eleven days up that road and nobody had paid him for the eleven, and that he was not going to do it again in a season he had not been told about.

“**I have not said yes,**” she said, “**and I have not said no, and that is the same answer as the one I gave you in Hearthmonth and it is going to be the same answer in a fortnight.**”

“**That is not an answer about the season.**”

“**It is the only answer there is until four more of the eleven of us have been in a room,**” she said, “**and those four are on a slip two days east and one of them is my mother and two of them are about sixty and one of them does not speak in a room and I have not asked her to.**”

And the man of about fifty said: “**Then tell me when.**”

“**When the tide allows it,**” she said, “**and I will not say that it is soon, because soon is a word for a person who has nothing to lose in the four months and I have eleven boats and a slip and a wall.**”

**And that is the whole of what a man with twenty-two years of a boat got out of that market day, and he had asked twice about it in about two months and got the same shape of answer twice, and he said nothing more about it in that room, and about two hundred people in that market do not know that either of those two things happened.**

## Three

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet put his bag on the table and said the thing he had walked eleven days for, and he said it to the room and not to anybody in it, which is a thing a man does when he has been in nine rooms in four months and has learnt which of the nine is a room.

“**A place of about four hundred people nine miles up this road salts its own and sells its own,**” he said, “**and it cannot do both of them at the price the four houses have stopped paying, and one of the four houses stopped in the third week of Hearthmonth and has stopped for good and did not say why, and the two prices are not in a column together anywhere and are never going to be.**”

“**That is a trade war,**” said a woman of about thirty who had come down that road in the morning. “**That is the whole of a trade war and I have said it once and I am not going to say it again.**”

“**Then tell me what you want,**” he said.

“**I want about nine hundred people to eat off eleven boats and I want about four hundred people nine miles up this road to be able to sell what they have salted at a price they can live on, and there are two ways and one of them is a Crown sheet that goes out about nine hundred times a month and the other one is a road.**”

“**What road.**”

**And she told him, and it took about four minutes, and it is a road that everybody on that coast can see the reason for and nobody on that coast can get anybody to build.**

**Salt goes out of that place by cart. The lane is nine miles and it is made for about four of the nine and soft for the other five, and a loaded cart cannot do the nine in the four months because the ground on the salt side takes the near wheel down in about four hundred yards, and in the eight months it can. There is no other way out of that place except the nine miles of lane and about nine miles of sea, and a boat of that size does not carry a load of salt up a river, and about four hundred people know all of this and have known it for a season and have nobody to say it to.**

---

“**Then somebody has to make a road,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

And that is when the man of about thirty-four worked it out, and it took him about four seconds, and he worked it out in front of four people and did not work it out quietly, because a man who counts what goes out does not do arithmetic in a corner.

“**There was one,**” he said. “**Four hundred miles off, a crossing that runs four months of the year and can carry a load in them, and it was offered and it was not taken up, and the condition of it was a person at this end and the person was never named and I asked for the name twice in about two months and was not given one. It has been shut for seven days and there is a bar on it.**”

He turned round and looked at the man of thirty-two with a limp.

“**You were in that room in Embermonth,**” he said. “**And in the autumn. And you are the only person that offer was ever going to be open to, and you never put yourself in it, and I did not ask you to and I am not asking you now.**”

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp was standing at about nine feet off with his stick in his right hand and he did not answer that and he was not asked it again, and this is not going to say what he was doing while it was being said, and he did not say whether he had ever thought about it, and nobody in that room ever asked him and nobody is going to.**

“**It is shut,**” he said.

“**I know it is shut,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**I walked here to find out how a man who is not going anywhere spends a Tuesday.**”

**And that is the second time in a week that a man who has been on an errand for eleven days has said the same shape of sentence at the end of it, and the man of thirty-two with a limp did not answer that one either, and the man of about thirty-four put his hand on the table and said: “**Then I will be useful, and you can tell me in a year.**”**

---

## Four

And what was useful was about four minutes long and it was a question and it was not the one he had been asked. **The man of thirty-two with a limp asked the woman of about thirty-four how many of the eleven could be on the slip at a state of tide that a cart road would not take, and she said all eleven of them could, and that being able is not the same as being willing, and he asked how long a boat takes to load a cart's worth and she said about two hours and a half on a neap and he asked whether the two hours and a half was the same in the four months and she said it was forty minutes longer in the four months and that the man who knew that was the man of about fifty and not her.**

And the man of about fifty said: “**Forty minutes. All right.**”

**And that was the whole of what that market day produced for anybody: all eleven of them could be loaded with a cart's worth at a state of tide a cart road would not take, and that is the whole of what a tide does for a place nine miles up a salt road, and the salt road's nine miles of lane is soft for the other five of the nine and a loaded cart cannot do it, and about four hundred people nine miles up that road are not going to be helped by two boats, and a place of about four hundred people on that salt road has been between two prices since the third week of a month in Hearthmonth and one of the two prices is a Crown price and it arrived first and is not going away, and the other thing that used to be there was a road four hundred miles off that could carry a load in these four months and there is a bar on it, and that road was never anybody's and the man in this room was never anybody's and neither of those facts did one thing to it.**

---

And then the woman of about thirty said her figure, because a place of about four hundred people that is not going to sell anything wants somebody to have said a number out loud in a room where there are three other people, and she said it in about nine seconds and then said it again a second time for the man of about thirty-four to write down, and the number was what it costs her place to salt its own and not sell any of it for one firing.

**Coal for a pan at nineteen pence, and a man raking it at nine, and a sieve at four. Nineteen and nine is twenty-eight, and twenty-eight and four is thirty-two, and thirty-two pence is two shillings and eightpence, and that is one firing of one pan and there are about nine pans in that place and none of them has been lit for a month, and the number was checked out loud before she said the total and not after, and the four pence a bushel that a Crown sheet puts on that road and the six pence four houses have paid for nine years are two prices of one thing and are in no column with these three and never will be.**

“**Write it,**” she said. “**Write the pan and not the bushel. If you put a bushel in there I will take it back out and you can make a sheet of it at the far end of that street.**”

And the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote the pan, and did not write the bushel, and put the sheet in his bag, and about four hundred people nine miles up that salt road do not know that their own cost of one firing is the only figure of theirs in anybody's hand, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was in the room for all of it and said one more thing, which was that a man who rakes at nine pence a day is being paid a quarter over what a yard of nine men up that road pays its lowest man at seven, and the woman of about thirty said that she knew and had known for a year and that was why she had said the number, and nobody added the two figures together and there is no column in this kingdom that takes them both.

**And the tide made at about the seventh hour and about nine hundred people on that coast went out of their houses to look at it and about two hundred of them had been out already, and four of the eleven boats went out on the making and the man of about fifty was on one of them and the four of them that run the other four months were not discussed again that day by anybody, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet walked out of that market with a sheet in a bag and a pan on it and eleven days in his legs, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went out the other way with nothing, and about two hundred people in that market saw a man with a stick go out of it and one of the two hundred was the woman of about thirty-four and she watched him go and she did not say anything and she has a year of not saying things to get through.**
