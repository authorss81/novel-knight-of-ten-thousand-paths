# Chapter 0379

**The eighteenth day of Frostmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in an upper room over a chandler's shop in a lane that runs off that market, in a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where about nine hundred people had come into four hundred yards of stone that morning and a man of about thirty-eight from a county two hundred miles off asked a fair question in the ordinary way and got a fair answer, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that a Crown officer of thirty-eight has ever stood in that room.**

The man of about thirty-eight came into that district on the eighth of that month and has his own errand and it is not this one. **He is a Crown officer and he has been one for eleven years, and he is not on a roll in any office he does not work for, and he is not a member of anything, and nobody in that room asked him a single question about himself and nobody has any reason to.**

**He had been in that county ten days on an errand of his own, which is a different errand, and it is about four hundred and nine parish rolls that go out of a building two hundred miles off on a cart every month and come back a fortnight later, and the whole of what is wrong with a cart that goes out every month and comes back a fortnight later is that the two of them are not in the same county and the men who load it do not unload it, and a man of thirty-eight is a man who counts things that go out and does not know what comes back in, and that is his own business and has nothing to do with anybody's book, and he would be the first man in that building to say so.**

He had a form. **It is a good form and it is six columns and five heads and a sixth with no head on it, and the second column takes a figure and the third takes a person by that person's trade and the fourth is headed *Keeper* and takes a person the same way the third does, and that head has been the same head for two years, and nowhere on the whole of it is there a place for the name of a person.**

A woman of about forty-four had been asked in this county nine months ago, in a room of this kind, whether she would keep a crossing for a fixed price, and had said yes, and nothing was taken from her and nothing was said to her that was not true, and the officer of thirty-eight is the same man who asked her, and he has not once been asked about the ninth set that went out of this county in Rainmonth, and he does not know that anybody in four counties knows where it went.

The woman of about thirty-five went back on the fourth of Wolfmonth with the paper. **The woman of about forty did not go with her and has been in a lodging house in another street since the twenty-third of Embermonth, and about nine hundred people on a river four hundred miles off have sent one person out once and are not going to send anybody out again inside four months, and a person who has been sent out is a person who waits.**

He asked the question in the ordinary way, and he asked it in twenty-seven words, unhedged, and the speech as printed is twenty-seven words with the attribution excluded, and he did not put a hand on the form while he said it.

“**Will the crossing at the head of your road be kept for a fixed price, and will whoever keeps it be a person we have written down.**”

And the whole of what was wrong with the question is that the person it was asked of is four hundred miles from the crossing it was about, and nobody in that room said so, and the man knew it, and he asked it anyway, because that is the way the question is asked.

---

“**No,**” the woman of about forty said, and she did not say it the way a person says a thing they have not thought about.

She said what she was, first, because a room that has been told a winter in four pages wants to know. “**I am not the steward. The steward is four hundred miles off and I do not know this week whether he is in his own house. I keep the account of the worst winter in eighteen years and I wrote it and I read it out in that room in Embermonth and nobody else read it out. I am not a keeper of anything and there is no office in this kingdom that would put me in a column and I would not want to be in one.**”

“**Then I have to put it to somebody who can answer it.**”

“**You have to put it to a steward,**” she said, “**and the answer is the same, and I will say the same thing to him, and you can go four hundred miles and ask him and it will cost you four hundred miles and you will get one word out of it, and I am giving you the whole sentence for nothing because the sentence is not mine and it is theirs, and I am only the one who came.**”

**And about two people in that lane have said since that a woman of about forty told a Crown officer of thirty-eight that he could go four hundred miles and get one word and that she would give him the whole sentence for nothing, and the whole sentence is longer than one word, and the officer of thirty-eight wrote it down, and a man who writes down a sentence that is longer than the word he came for is a man who is at the end of his own errand and not at the beginning of another.**

---

**And he did it in about nine minutes, and the nine minutes were the nine minutes of a man who has a sheet and knows what the front of it is for, and about two people in that room have said since that a Crown officer of thirty-eight who has been at it for eleven years did not turn a sheet over for about four of the nine minutes, and that the four minutes were the four minutes of a man deciding whether a thing he is about to do is a thing he is allowed to do, and that he did it and has not decided since whether he was allowed.**

---

**And about four people in that lane have said since that about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane and about nine hundred come back, and that a sheet which comes back with the back of it written on is a sheet somebody will turn over, and that whoever turns it over will be a person about forty in a building with four floors who has about nine hundred to get through in a month and has never once been told what the back of a sheet is for.**

**And two people in that lane have said since that a Crown officer of thirty-eight has asked that question, in that ordinary way, about nine hundred times, and that on about nine hundred of those occasions a person has said yes, and that a thing nine hundred people have said yes to is not a thing anybody stops to ask about, and that a woman of about forty is the first one in this county to say no, and that nobody in that room understood that she was the first.**

**And then he had to write it, and the form is a form, and there is no column on it for a place that has said no, and the fourth column of that series is headed *Keeper*, and there is nothing on the front of that sheet which would take what she has just said, and that has been the case for two years, and nobody in that room said a word about it.**

He put the place in the first column. **He could not put a figure in the second and he did not write a figure of nothing in it, and he said that he was not going to write a figure of nothing because a figure of nothing is a figure of a person who could not get to a place, and that he had one of those in a tray two hundred miles off already and that he was not going to make a second one on purpose.**

---

And she took the form and turned it over and wrote on the back of it.

**Four lines. A place, in her own hand, spelled the way that place has always spelled itself. A date. A sentence. And her name at the foot of the back, and her name is the first name of a person from that community that has been on any paper out of this county in about nineteen years, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that.**

“**The back of it is not part of it,**” the man of about thirty-eight said.

“**The back of it is the only part of it there is,**” the woman of about forty said, “**and I have put a place on it and a date and a sentence and my name, and there is no column on the front of that sheet for any of the four, and you are the only person in this kingdom who is going to see the back of it.**”

**And a woman of about forty wrote a refusal on the back of a Crown instrument because there was nowhere else to write it, and the reason there was nowhere else to write it is that the front of the instrument has six columns and five heads and a sixth with no head and not one of the six is for this, and a thing written on the back of a sheet is not part of the sheet, and the officer of thirty-eight folded it and put it in a bag.**

---

**And two people in that lane have said since that a man of about thirty-four with a sheet was in that room for the whole of it and said nothing for the whole of it, and that he had a form and a column and about nine years of other people's forms, and that the only thing he did at the seventh hour was look at a man of thirty-two with a limp, and that about two thousand people in that town would not have understood the look.**

**And what the errand cost him, and he said so himself, because a man who has been a Crown officer for eleven years has been told to account for what a day costs and has never once had a day he could account for.**

A sheet at a penny, and a candle at twopence, and a lodging at fivepence a night for four nights is twenty pence. **Twenty pence and twopence is twenty-two pence, and twenty-two pence and one penny is twenty-three pence, and twenty-three pence is a penny under two shillings, and that is what it cost the Crown to be told no in a room over a chandler's shop, and the four hundred miles is not in it and never has been in anything.**

A man of thirty-two with a limp was in that room at about the fourth hour and had come in off the long road on the eighteenth of Wolfmonth and had not been in the lane before that. **He did not say a word for the whole of the asking and the answering, and the woman of about forty looked at him once at the seventh hour for about four seconds, and he looked at her, and about two people in that lane have said since that the two of them had worked out the same thing in that room and neither of them said it, and what the thing was is that a place's name in a woman's own hand on the back of a Crown instrument, in a building two hundred miles off with four floors and seven presses, is a place that can be found, and that about nine hundred people on a river have never once been findable by anything, and that she has done that on purpose and she would do it again.**

And she said so, and she said it once. “**I know what I have put on that sheet. I have been in this town for about eight weeks and I have not been in a room with a form in it until this morning and I have not asked for one, and I have put my name and my place on the back of a piece of paper that is going two hundred miles, and I would put it on the front if they would let me.**”

**And the man of about thirty-eight put the sheet in a bag and the bag in a case and the case under his bed at a lodging house in another street, and about two hundred miles off there is a building with four floors and seven presses in it and about forty people in it and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane into nine counties in two rounds, and one sheet with the back of it written on is going into that room in about nine days in a tray with a strap over it, and nobody in that room is going to turn it over, and about two thousand people in that town on the salt do not know that a place four hundred miles off has just become a thing that can be found.**
