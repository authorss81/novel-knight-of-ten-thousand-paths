# Chapter 0391

**The twenty-sixth day of Goatmonth, YR 317, a Thursday and a market day four hundred miles from here, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a room that holds nine, in a place of about nine hundred people on a river and a flat country of nine miles by four, where a woman of about forty came back on the fourteenth hour of a walk of eleven days and put a roll on a table, and no month and no weekday was said in that room, and about nine hundred people on that river do not know yet what she has come back to say.**

She had been off this place for five months and eleven days of them walking. The room holds nine and there were eleven in it, and the woman of about twenty-six had a child of about four asleep against her shoulder in a way that was going to end before the fourth hour, and nobody moved for her, and the man of about thirty-four said the first thing.

“**You have been eleven days on a road and you have not washed,**” he said. “**Say it plain and then somebody will get something on this table.**”

“**Our name is in the first column of a sheet,**” she said, “**and our figure is in the second column, and the sheet is in a tray in a building two hundred miles off the place it was written about, and it has been read, and nobody told us it was going to be read.**”

Nobody said anything for a moment. Then the man of about thirty-four said: “**Read by whom.**”

“**By an office two hundred miles off the place it was written about, and it is deciding which places it keeps open and which it lets go, and it has not told us which we are, and the round comes round again in a month and nobody rings.**”

“**Then somebody will come,**” said the woman of about twenty-six.

“**Nobody comes,**” the woman of about forty said. “**A round is a cart. The cart goes out on a fixed day into nine counties and comes back a fortnight later, and it goes to about four hundred and nine places, and this is not one of the four hundred and nine, and there is nobody in that building who has ever set foot in this place or in any place on this river, and there never has been. They do not need to come. That is what a tray is.**”

That was a short answer and she had given it standing up with her bag still in her hand, and then she sat down, and the man of about thirty-four said the thing that had been in his face since she came in the door.

“**Then what do they want it for.**”

And she did not answer it, and that is the whole of the first hour of that day, and about nine hundred people on that river did not know it had happened.

---

What she could not answer was not a thing she had been asked to carry. **She had been asked four questions in a town on the salt and had written the four answers on the back of nothing, in her head, walking, and three of the four had answers and the fourth had not, and the fourth was the one the man of about thirty-four had just asked.**

“**I asked a man with a sheet that question myself,**” she said, “**in a room over a shop in a lane in a town on the salt, on a day that county does not trade, and he answered it plainly, and then he said that the answer was four lines long and none of the four lines was a reason.**”

“**What are the four lines.**”

“**That they are keeping a list of places. That a place with a figure in the second column is being held. That a place without one is not. That there is nothing on any of it that says which.**”

“**That is not a reason,**” said the man of about thirty-four. “**That is a machine doing a thing and not telling us which part of the machine it is.**”

“**No,**” she said. “**And he could not tell me which part and I did not ask him twice and I am not going to stand here and guess at it in front of you.**”

And a man of about fifty who keeps the only list of the nine places in this community that any person keeps, and who has kept it for longer than anybody in the room has been alive, put his hand flat on the table.

“**Then what are you for,**” he said. “**You have come eleven days to tell us we are on a piece of paper. Everybody in this room is on a piece of paper. What are we supposed to do with the knowing.**”

And she could not tell him that either, and it took the rest of the hour, and the reason it took the rest of the hour is that eleven people in a room of nine spent it on that.

---

Here is what the room did with the hour, and nobody in it was wrong. **A man of about nineteen asked whether it was a Crown sheet and was told it was not, that it was nine hundred sheets of one round and the round was a cart that went out on a fixed day, and that a Crown sheet is a different shape and has a head on it and this has four columns and no heads on any of them.** A woman of about thirty said that a column with no head on it is a column nobody has admitted to, and the man of about thirty-four said that was a fair thing to say and that it did not help.

**A man of about sixty said the thing that was going to cost them the most and he said it by accident.** He said that a figure of about nine hundred had never been anybody's business, that his own father had been one of the nine hundred and had not been asked for a figure in his life, and that the second column had a figure in it, and he stopped in the middle of the sentence and put his hand over his mouth like a man who has said a thing at a funeral.

And the woman of about forty said: “**That is it exactly, and that is why I could not answer you. Somebody wrote our figure down. Not one of us. Somebody who was asked for it in a field, by a woman who counts faster than a man with a stick, on the second morning of four, and he wrote what she said and he did not know what it was for either.**”

---

**And then somebody asked about the roll, and it was the woman of about twenty-six that asked, and the room changed.**

The roll was in her bag and she had carried it four hundred miles and back and had not intended to bring it and had brought it. **It is the parish roll of this place and it has a figure on it and it has had a figure on it since before the man of about sixty was born, and the figure has not moved in nineteen years, and the name on it is this place, spelled the way four hundred people here spell it, which is not the way it is spelled on the sheet.** Two sheets. Same name. Two spellings. One of them made by a person who was copying.

The man of about thirty-four looked at both for a long time.

“**Two documents,**” he said. “**Nineteen years apart. One of them we asked for and one of them we were not told about. Has anybody ever written that down in one place.**”

“**No,**” said the man of about fifty. “**And there is nowhere here to write it in one place. I keep a list of nine and it is nine lines and there is no line on it for a sheet.**”

“**There is nowhere in this kingdom to write it in one place,**” she said, “**and that is not the sad part. The sad part is that we have known our name was written down for nineteen years and we have never once wondered where, and one of the two of us would have been asked.**”

The woman of about twenty-six shifted the child on her shoulder and said the thing that the two men in that room had not thought of, and she said it to the table and not to anybody in it.

“**I have been to a town on the salt. Once, four years ago, with a man who is not here today, and I stood in a street of four hundred yards of stone and a man asked me what I did and I said I had eleven at home, and he wrote it down in a little book, and he was pleasant about it and I was pleased that somebody wanted to know. That is the whole of it. I have thought about it every day since and the whole of it is a man being pleasant with a little book.**”

“**That is not the same,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**It is exactly the same,**” she said, “**except that I noticed this time.**”

And the man of about fifty put his thumb on the roll and said: “**Then this is not new. It is the second one.**”

“**It is the second one I have seen,**” she said, “**and I have been off this place five months and I have not seen the first one and I am not going to pretend to.**”

---

**And the fourth thing that happened in that room was about a paper, and nobody in it joined the two things together afterwards, and the woman of about forty was glad of that and said so to nobody.**

The paper was nine lines long and it had been on that table in a town on the salt five months ago and it was the reason she had been away. It was a road that was theirs, and a season, and a condition, and the condition was a person. **Nobody in that room had seen it. The man of about thirty-four knew that a paper had come and he knew that somebody had walked out with an answer and he did not know what the answer was, because the answer had been written on a sheet in a press two hundred miles off and he had not asked for it.**

“**There was a road in it,**” he said.

“**There was a road in it,**” she said, “**and there is a person in it, and the person is not a rate, and I have not been asked to bring either of those back and I have not.**”

“**Then we will hear about it,**” said the man of about fifty.

“**You will hear about it when the steward is here,**” she said, “**and I am not the one who will tell you, because I am not the one who can be refused, and if I say it in four places on a landing it will be said four times by a woman who is not entitled to say it once.**”

And the man of about fifty said: “**That is true and I had not thought of it.**” And then nobody said anything for about a minute, and it was the first minute of that day in which the eleven people in that room were not arguing.

---

She had it in four places and not in one room, and that was the thing she had worked out on the road and not before it. **A room here holds nine and there are about nine hundred of us, and there are four places where people stand in the open waiting for a boat, and if she said it in a room it would be a thing said nine times over nine days by nine different people, each of them with a different version of it, and by the ninth telling the ninth telling would be the one that was believed.**

“**Four places,**” said the man of about fifty.

“**Four places,**” she said. “**And it is me that says it. All four. Because I was in the room where it was said, and because if nine people say it then it is a story that nine people told, and if one woman says it four times it is a thing a woman saw, and one is worth more here than nine and I know that is not a thing I can argue for in a room.**”

“**It is not arguable,**” the man of about fifty said. “**It is just what happens.**”

She put the roll back in her bag and tied it, and it took her about nine seconds, and the child of about four woke up and was put down on the floor and slept again inside a minute the way a child does when it has decided.

“**And I have four days,**” she said, “**because the four places are four days apart on foot and after the fourth day the first of them will have told it nine times already.**”

“**What are we to do.**” The man of about thirty-four said it standing up, and he had not sat down since she came in. “**That is the whole of it. You have given us a document and there are eleven of us in a room that holds nine and there are about nine hundred of us and I am asking you what we are to do.**”

And she said: “**I do not know. I have not been told and I did not ask, because I was asked to be there about a letter and not about this, and I would have liked to have asked and there was no room to ask it in.**”

**And about four hundred people on the far side of that river heard none of it that day, because the four places she had chosen were four of the nine and the far side is one of the five she had not chosen, and she had chosen the four for the reason that a person standing on a landing is a person who cannot walk off.** She did not say that. Nobody asked her why and nobody worked it out, and the child of about four slept on the floor of a room that holds nine, and the roll went back into a bag, and about nine hundred people on that river went on doing what about nine hundred people do with a day.
