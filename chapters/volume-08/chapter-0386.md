# Chapter 0386

**The thirtieth day of Hearthmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, on a coast about two days west along a salt road from a town of about two thousand people on the salt, in a yard where eleven boats are kept and a woman of about thirty-four has run the keeping of them for about nine years, and nobody on that coast knows that four of the eleven have been asked for.**

The boat came at the second hour and he had been on it since the fifth hour of the day before. **A passage across that water with a fair wind is six hours and against it is eleven, and the man of about thirty-two with a limp paid his way in coin and not in a form and there is no column in this kingdom for a man buying a passage, and nobody on that coast knows that anybody came off that boat and four hundred of the nine hundred would not have cared.**

The yard is not a yard in the sense a fen county means it. **It is a stone slip on the side of a headland with eleven boats in it and a wall about five feet high along the landward side of the slip, and the wall is the whole of what a boat yard is on that coast, and eleven boats are kept in it and eleven boats are run out of it and about nine hundred people on that coast eat off one or another of them.**

She was on the wall when he came up it. **She is about thirty-four and has run the keeping of the eleven for about nine years and her name is Nella Vearn and about four hundred people on that coast know her name and the other five hundred do not, and a woman with eleven boats is not a person anybody has to be introduced to anybody.**

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet came up the slip an hour behind him. **He has a form and it is a good form and it has six columns and five heads and a sixth with no head on it, and the second column takes a figure and the third takes a person by that person's trade and the fourth is headed *Keeper* and takes a person the same way the third does, and that head has been the same head for two years, and he came two days along a salt road to ask a question that had been asked in nine counties already this year, and he was not the man who can decide anything on that coast and everybody on the slip knew it before he got to the wall.**

---

**That coast is two days west along a salt road from the town the man with the limp came off that came from, and the salt road runs through a place of about four hundred people nine miles on from the coast who salt their own and who are not on that coast and have not been there since before the eleven boats were run by one woman. The water there is a bay with a bar of sand in the mouth of it, and the bar moves forty yards a year, and eleven boats have crossed that mouth in nine years and two of them have been lost on it and neither was in a storm.**

**The tide in that bay does about fourteen feet twice a day and it is the only clock anybody on that coast keeps. A boat goes out on the making and comes back on the making, and a boat that misses the making waits twelve hours, and a man who has waited twelve hours has not lost a day, he has lost a night, and he has to eat and to sleep somewhere that is not his own house. Four hundred crossings a day across that water is a number of crossings and not a number of people, and nobody keeps it, and it is not in any return, and the eleven households that agree the fare have never written it down anywhere.**

**Eleven boats is not a fleet and it is not a company. Four of them go out with fish in them and come back with fish in them, and the fish is divided on the slip in an hour and nobody weighs anything. Three of them are flat-bottomed and go up a river that is four miles wide at the mouth and dry in four months of the year, and they carry salt up and lime down. Two of them are the kind of boat a person gets into with a basket and a basket of what a household has grown. And two of them are the two that have been lost, bought back and mended, and the two that were lost are the two nobody on that coast will go out in in a fresh breeze.**

**And a woman of thirty-four who keeps the eleven of them has a wall to keep them against and a slip to put them in and nine years of a record in her own head of which of them will take a weight on a spring tide, and about four hundred people on that coast have worked out since that a record in a woman's head about her own boats is a thing that cannot be copied and has never been asked to be, and that it is the only record on that coast of anything at all.**

**And the asking had been made in the third week of that month by a person with no stake in it, and the four had not been named, and the eleven had not been counted out loud, and the answer had not been given, and nobody on that coast knows that an asking is outstanding on their slip.**

**She said her want once, at the fourth hour, to a man in that yard who is not the man who can decide it, and the sixteen words foot at sixteen and the speech as printed is sixteen words with the attribution excluded, and the word *about* does not stand in front of the figure.**

“**I have eleven and you have asked me for four and I have not said yes.**”

Nobody on that slip answered it. **About four people on that coast have worked out since that a woman of about thirty-four said sixteen words to a man with a form on a stone slip and then sat down on the wall, and that she said them before anybody had asked her a second question, and that the second question is the one everybody asks, which is why she said them first.**

“**You have not said no either,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**No,**” she said. “**And that is the whole of what I have said and I am not going to say the other one either, and the two of them are the same shape on that wall and you know they are, and you are a man who counts things that go out and you are going to go back with eleven and not with four, and eleven is a true number and four is a true number, and the difference between them is seven boats and a woman on a wall and neither of those two is a thing that goes on a form.**”

---

**The asking came in on a salt road with the ordinary round, in the third week of that month, in the same way about nine hundred sheets a month go into nine counties, and it was not a form and it was not a letter and it was a sentence with a number in it on the back of a page, and the sentence was that four of the eleven boats would be lent to somebody else's use for the length of a season, and the number on the back of the page was the number of the season and not the number of the boats.**

**And nobody on that coast was asked whether the eleven should be lent, and about nine hundred people on that coast found out in about a fortnight that an asking was outstanding, and the finding out was done by a woman on a wall telling eleven households one at a time, which is eleven days, and eleven households on a coast is a thing you can do in eleven days and cannot do in one.**

**And a boat on that coast is not a boat and it is not a rate.** Eleven boats go out on a tide and eleven boats come back on a tide and about nine hundred people on that coast have been fed off eleven of them for about nine years, and the tide does not care what is asked of it.

**A boat out and back is a day and a night, and eleven of them on a tide is about four hundred crossings a day across that water and not one of them is on a Crown's own sheet, and the price of a cart at nine crossings a week of cart road east of here is two shillings, and the fare on a boat on that coast is a figure eleven households agreed in about nine years and it is not a toll and is not a wage and is not in a column with the two shillings, and a toll is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and none of those is in a column with any of the others.**

The cost of setting a boat yard up to carry somebody else's loads for a season is this, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it down and did not foot it on the page, and about four people on that coast have said since that a Crown officer who counts things that go out came two days to a coast and wrote down a column of figures and did not add them, and that the not adding was the whole of what he came for, and that he did it on the second day and stayed seven more.

**Tar at three pence a pot and three pots is nine pence. Rope at four pence a fathom and eleven fathoms is forty-four pence. Oilcloth at seven pence. Nine and forty-four is fifty-three pence, and fifty-three and seven is sixty pence, and sixty pence is five shillings, and that is the whole of what the standing gear of eleven boats costs to renew in one season, and four of those eleven boats is not in the figure and is not in a column with it, and a rate is not a toll and a toll is not a rate and a boat is not a bar.**

“**You are not the one who decides it,**” she said, at about the seventh hour, to the man of about thirty-four with a sheet.

“**I am not,**” he said. “**I am the man who has written down that you have not said no, and that is the whole of what I have on that form, and I am going to take it back nine days along that road and put it in a tray, and somebody two hundred miles off is going to read it, and one number in it is eleven and not one of them is four.**”

“**And nobody will ring,**” she said.

“**Nobody rings,**” he said.

---

**A man of about fifty runs one of the four that go out with fish and has run it for twenty-two years, and he is not on any roll in any county and about nine hundred people on that coast have eaten off that boat and four hundred of them have not known his name. What he wants is to be told in the spring whether the four are going, and to be told before the season starts and not in the middle of it. He said that once, to the man of about thirty-four with a sheet, on the slip, at the fourth hour, and it was not the want of the woman on the wall and it was nobody's want but his own.**

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote that down because it was about a season and a date and not about a boat, and about four people on that coast have said since that a Crown officer can be got to write a thing down by making the thing about a date, and that nobody on that slip has worked out why, and that the man with the limp did work it out and did not say.**

**And a woman of about thirty-four who has not said no and has not said yes has been asked the same question in the same words about nine hundred times a month in nine counties for about four months, and a thing nine hundred people have said yes to is not a thing anybody stops to ask about, and the first person in about a year to make a question take longer than the asking is a woman on a wall who has not answered it.**

And the man of thirty-two with a limp said nothing about any of it and walked the slip and looked at eleven boats and could not add them to anything. **A page in his coat has nine lines on it and no heads and none of the nine is a boat, and he has been carrying that page since a yard with a stick against a bench-board, and nobody on that coast knows that anybody has a page.**

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet got back on the boat at about the seventh hour with a form he had not filled in and a woman of about thirty-four sitting on a wall with eleven boats behind her and not one of them promised, and about nine hundred people on that coast went to bed that night with no more idea than they had at the second hour that four of the eleven had been asked for and that a woman had said sixteen words about it, and that is the whole of what a question costs when the person it is asked of is the one who has to be asked.**
