# Chapter 0387

**The twelfth day of Goatmonth, YR 317, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the seventh hour, in a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road and in a room at the end of that yard, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where nine men had been on a lane a mile off the west end of that market for about twenty days and a man of about fifty who had walked eleven days on a salt road came into that room at about the second hour and said one thing, and nobody in that town knows that a yard, a coast and a place four hundred miles off have started answering each other.**

**A Thursday in that county is a market day, and about two thousand people went into four hundred yards of stone that morning, and a hut with three bars in front of it stood up that county's own road with the man of about fifty-five in it, and forty men were on a reach four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of that market, and not one person was stopped at those bars that is stopped there every other morning of the week.**

About two of the nine were in the market at the second hour and about two were waiting for a cart and five were in the yard. **A boy of fifteen was at a bench-board about nine feet long copying a figure and the space under it, and a man of about sixty was packing lime against a heap that has a lid on it, and the press in the room at the end of that yard was standing open at about forty-two weeks with four sheets in it that have nothing at all on the wrapper, and it cannot be served with anything and nobody shuts it.**

The man of about fifty came in at about the second hour and he was dirty and he was thin and he had walked nine days. **He runs one of four boats that go out with fish on a coast about two days west along a salt road and has run it for twenty-two years, and he is not on any roll in any county, and nobody in that yard had ever seen him, and four people in that county have said since that a man who has run a boat for twenty-two years walked eleven days on a salt road to say a thing about a season and a date and that nobody sent him and nobody paid him for the eleven days.**

**And the woman who told him to come is about thirty-four and keeps eleven boats and was not in that room and was not in that county, and he was in it on her word, and four people in that county have said since that a man from a coast eleven days off went to a yard four hundred yards up a road he had never heard of because a woman on a wall said go, and that is the whole of the reason there is.**

---

**A yard of nine men in that county keeps a book that has been kept the same way for four years, and the book takes a thing set, and a thing set is a bar and a course and a sill and a figure and a date. A yard is a yard because of what it can be asked for and not because of what it has done. That yard is on a reach forty men wide four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of that market in a working season, and it is on lanes and drains and sills the rest of the year, and it is on nothing at all for six weeks in the middle of a year, and it has been on nothing at all for those six weeks four years running.**

He said one thing and it took him about nine seconds and it was about a season and not about a boat.

“**My four boats cannot run from the first of the second month to the end of the fifth, and the eleven of us eat off them and we have eaten off them for nine years, and there is a yard eleven days east of me that has nine men and no work in the middle of a year, and I am not asking you for anything. I am telling you that there is a thing on the salt road that is shut and there is a yard that is shut and the two of them are eleven days apart and there is a town on the road in between them.**”

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds. **And the boy of fifteen's pen stopped and the space under the number was still empty when it started, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a man who walks eleven days to say one thing and gets four seconds of silence has not been in nine rooms in his life and has never been in a room that was going to decide anything in nine minutes.**

“**What is shut on the salt road,**” the man of twenty-five said.

“**The tide,**” the man of about fifty said. “**And four boats and about nine hundred people and a slip and a wall.**”

“**What is shut in this yard.**”

“**Six weeks of it, if you are honest, and about ten if you are not, and the rest of the year you are on a reach and a lane and a man of thirty-eight with a crowbar.**”

---

**He had a boat of his own on that slip and a mate of nineteen who could not be spared off it for nine days and who had not been asked, and the man of about fifty had left him the tide tables and a word about the bar of sand in the mouth of the bay, and had walked off at the making of the tide. Four people on that coast have said since that a man who runs a boat for twenty-two years and walks eleven days to say one thing has left a nineteen-year-old in charge of a business with a bar of sand in the mouth of it and has not asked anybody to help.**

**The tide tables are a sheet of paper with two columns and no heads on them, and they are kept by a man of nineteen on that coast and they have never been in a return and are not in any roll, and about nine hundred people on that coast read them every morning and about a third of them cannot read them and copy the numbers out of somebody else's hand.**

**And the boy of fifteen was in that room for the whole of the quarter of an hour with the book open on the bench-board in front of him and nine columns ruled in it and one of the nine empty. He put a figure in the fourth column at the second hour and a figure in the fifth at the fourth hour and a figure in the sixth at the sixth hour, and he ruled the seventh column with his own rule and left it empty, and four people in that county have said since that a boy of fifteen who rules a column himself has decided what goes in it, and that what went in it on the twelfth day of Goatmonth was nothing, and that he is going to go on deciding.**

**And the decision that yard made on the twelfth day of Goatmonth was made in about a quarter of an hour, and the man of twenty-five said before anybody had asked him that it was not his, and four of the nine were in that room and five were not and the five were told in a doorway, and nobody in that town knows any of it.**

The first of it is a load a week. **The yard will take one load a week down that salt road for the four months the boats cannot run, for anybody who asks and for nobody who does not, and the load goes at the yard's own day rate and the day rate is a thing a man can be paid in and the money is in coin on a Friday and not on a sheet.**

The second of it is which man. **It is never the man on a bar, and it is a man the nine of them name on a Friday, and a man at a bar is not a wage and a man on a salt road is not a wage, and four people in that county have worked out since that a yard of nine men in a quarter of an hour made a rule that looks like a rule and is really one sentence said twice, and that a man who says a sentence twice in a room is a man who has been saying it in his own head since the ninth of Wolfmonth.**

The third of it is the rate and the third of it is the whole of the trouble. **A load a week down a salt road that is eleven days each way is a man out of that yard for twenty-two days plus the loading, and a yard of nine men cannot lose a man for twenty-two days in a working season, and the man of twenty-five said that out loud and then said the other half of it, which is that a yard of nine men that goes eleven days down a road every third week is a yard that is not in its own county and cannot be sent for.**

“**Then that is the answer,**” the man of about fifty said, and started to stand up.

“**That is half of it,**” the man of twenty-five said. “**The other half is that we do it one week in three and not one week in one, and three weeks on and one week here is a thing nine men can hold, and the man who goes knows before he goes which of the three weeks it is, and the coast knows it before he starts, and a thing that a man knows before he starts is the whole difference between a yard and a committee.**”

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp was at the end of that room through the whole of the quarter of an hour and did not say a word in it, and nobody in that room asked him a single question about it, and no man in that room said one word to him about a bar four hundred miles off, and he did not say one word about it, and nobody in that town knows that the man standing at the end of that room came up that road with an order about a bar four hundred miles off, was refused it, and has not sent the order again.**

---

**And the three of them have not met. A yard eleven days east of that coast has said one sentence in a room at about the fourth hour. A woman of about forty who keeps the only account of the worst winter in eighteen years for about nine hundred people is in a town on that salt road and has said nothing all month. And about nine hundred people on a river four hundred miles off have not been told that a yard and a coast have started answering each other and are not going to be told by anybody in that county.**

And at about the seventh hour he went out of that room and down that road to the market and did not write any of it down. **A yard's book takes a thing set, and a load a week down a salt road is not a thing set, and a rate is a figure and a figure is a thing set, and a man who names a man on a Friday is a term and a term is a claim and a claim is not a thing set, and four people in that county have said since that a yard of nine men has just agreed in a quarter of an hour to run a road eleven days off for four months and that the whole of the arrangement will fit inside a wrapper with nothing on it.**

**And a page in his coat has nine lines on it and no heads and none of the nine is a salt road and none of the nine is a boat and he has not taken it out in about three weeks, and about four people in that county have said since that a man who made nine crossings on a page in about nine minutes in a yard with a stick against a bench-board has been asked this month to put a thing in that page and has not done it, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him why and nobody is going to.**

**And the press in the room at the end of that yard is standing open at about forty-two weeks with four sheets in it that have nothing on the wrapper, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went out of that room with nothing on him that was not in his coat when he came in, and a yard of nine men, a coast with eleven boats, and a place four hundred miles off where about nine hundred people have never been findable by anything have each said one sentence in about a month, and the three sentences have not met in a room, and the man who is standing at the end of one of the three rooms writes none of it down, and nobody in that town knows that any of the three of them has been said.**
