# Chapter 0385

**The twenty-third day of Hearthmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road and at a lane a mile off the west end of that market, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where nine men had been on a lane a mile off that market for fourteen days and had not been paid for any of them, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that.**

**The market took four hundred yards of clay and stone and about two thousand people that morning. Forty men were on a reach four hundred yards down a lane off the north end and its fourth bay had been pointed in Harvestmonth and had not been touched since, and the man of about fifty-five was in his hut with three bars standing in front of it, and nobody went past him all morning that does not go past him every other morning of the week, and a bench of nine sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week and did not sit.**

The drain was open on both sides of the made road and the pipe was laid and the ends were walled. **That is a fortnight and it is the fortnight nobody puts in a figure, and a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar and a shovel had done the digging with four men hired in from off that county's own road and had been on that lane every day since the ninth of that month, and a yard of nine men four hundred yards up that road had put the stone money in and had not been asked to.**

The sill went in on the nineteenth of that month. **Six feet of it, cut and fitted dry with a shim of lead at each end, set on a bed of lime, and the two courses on either side laid on lime and not on earth, and a man who cuts stone for a living cut it and four of the nine of that yard fitted it with a wooden mallet and a line, and about four hours a day of the fitting is waiting for the line, and nobody in that town knows that four hours a day of a bridge is a man standing still.**

---

**A wooden mallet and a line is the whole of the trade and neither of those is a thing anybody teaches. A man sets the line at both ends of a course and stretches it along the top and works the stone up to it, and when the stone is right the line is a foot off the ground and when it is wrong the stone is a foot off the line, and the difference is not visible from the lane and is visible from four inches. Two of the nine of that yard were on the sill all day and neither of them is the man who cut it, and about four people in that county have worked out since that the man who fitted it is not the man who cut it and that a yard which keeps both is a yard that has not had to learn to do without one.**

**And the bed of lime is three bushels for six feet of stone and it is mixed in a trough by a man with a shovel and stood overnight, and lime that has stood overnight is not the same as lime that has not, and about two people in that county have said since that the curing of the lime is the first of the two nine-day cures on that lane and that nobody puts a figure on either of them.**

**A yard of nine men that is not paid on a Friday does four things and does them in that order. It takes two men off the job on the Monday. It takes the rest of the money out of the stone and puts it into the men. And it says so in the yard in nine seconds to the four who are in and to the five who are not, in a doorway, and about four people in that county have said since that a yard which does the third of those four things is a yard that has never lied to itself, and that a yard which has done the first two of them for a fortnight has a third fortnight in it and not a fourth.**

**And the yard is not being paid for it, and the whole of the not being paid is in one sentence said by the man of twenty-five in that yard at about the fourth hour of a market Saturday, and he said it once and nobody improved on it.**

“**I have nine men and a yard and I am not putting either of them on a line,**” the man of twenty-five said, and the boy of fifteen's pen did not stop, and a man of about sixty kept his hand on a lid through the whole of it, and that is the whole of what was said in that yard about the job and about the money.

**The eighteen words foot at eighteen and the count is the speech as printed with the speaker's attribution left out, and the word *about* is not standing in front of the figure. And a man of thirty-two with a limp was in that yard and heard the whole of it and had heard the same eighteen words in that yard in Harvestmonth, and neither of the two men said one word about the hearing of it and nobody in that yard said a word about it at all, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a man has said the same eighteen words twice in about four months in the same room.**

The want had not changed. **It is the want it was in Harvestmonth, word for word, and about four people in that county have worked out since that a man whose want does not change when the price of what he is being asked to do changes is a man who has a want, and that a man who is a position has a price and changes it.**

---

**And what the yard is out by is this, and the woman of about thirty-four who has a holding at the end of that lane is the one who has the figures and she gave them to him in about nine minutes at the fourth hour, and a toll is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and a subscription is neither and none of the three is in a column with either of the other two.**

The stone is paid for. **A sill at thirty-four pence and a course on each side at eleven pence a course and lime at three pence a bushel, and thirty-four and twenty-two is fifty-six pence, and fifty-six and six is sixty-two pence, and sixty-two pence is five shillings and twopence, and that money is in and it is the only money that is in.**

The labour is not. **Eleven households on that lane and the two ends of it and a mill at the far end have between them put in what they could in fourteen days and it came to less than the drain cost, and a yard of nine men has been fourteen days out of pocket at the ordinary rate of a yard that pays its men on a Friday, and nobody has asked that yard to take less and nobody has offered it more, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp did not offer it more and could not and did not say that he could not.**

What nine men eat in a day is the only figure about the job that anybody keeps, and the man of twenty-five keeps it and it is this.

**Bread, nine loaves at twopence is eighteen pence. Cheese, four at threepence is twelve pence. Drink, nine measures at a penny is nine pence. A candle at twopence is two pence. Eighteen and twelve is thirty pence, and thirty and nine is thirty-nine pence, and thirty-nine and two is forty-one pence, and forty-one pence is three shillings and fivepence.**

**And about four people in that county have said since that a yard of nine men working a lane a mile off a market on money that has not arrived is eating for about three shillings and fivepence a day and getting about a shilling under the going day rate from the work, and that a yard can carry about a month of that and not two, and that the part of the year they are carrying it in is the part with no bridge work in it and it has not run out.**

---

**A man of thirty-two with a limp came up that road at about the second hour, went down to the lane with the nine, walked about a hundred yards of it and came back, and did not go down the soft shoulder and did not put a hand on the stone.** **Coming up into that yard takes him four seconds longer than going down out of it, and a bank has been out of his reach since the seventh day of a month in Longlight, and four of the nine on that yard have watched him do it nine times in as many visits and have never once said a word about it.**

**Eleven households on that lane put in what they could between the ninth of that month and the twenty-third of it, and a subscription on a lane is not a price and is not a rate and is a thing eleven households decide in a doorway over a fortnight, and none of the eleven has ever sat in a room and none of the eleven has ever been asked whether a crossing should be shut, and the one household that has been asked is a woman of about thirty-four and she has not been asked by anybody in an office.**

**She has two carts and one of the two went into that drain to the axle on the ninth of that month and was out again in four hours, and the second cart has gone round for nineteen days, and a woman with two carts and one of them in a drain is the only person on that lane keeping a figure of what the shut is costing, and nobody has asked her for it and she has not offered it and she keeps it on the back of a bill.**

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp did not go down to that lane again that day, and about four people in that county have worked out since that a man who has been in that yard three times in about two months and has not asked for a thing in it three times is the only person in that county nobody has had to answer anything.**

A woman of about thirty-four at the end of that lane said the thing about the money that about four people in that county have said since is the only thing anybody has said about it that is true.

“**You have got the stone and you have not got the days,**” she said. “**And I cannot pay for the days. I can pay for the stone out of what a sill is and I have paid it, and the days are not a thing I can price because I do not know what a day is on that lane and neither do you, and there are two men in that yard on that lane this week who are not from your county and one of them has a wife and two children nine days off and he has not said a word about it and I have.**”

“**He does not have to say it,**” the man of twenty-five said.

“**He does not have to say it,**” she said, “**and I am saying it to you and not to him, and I have said it once and I am not going to say it again, and you have agreed to forty days in the part of the year that has no bridge work in it, and a yard of nine men that is four hours a day light on four days in seven is not going to get the days out of me and it is not going to get them out of anybody on that lane.**”

**And about four people in that county have said since that a woman with a holding at the end of a shut lane said in about nine minutes that she cannot price a day, and that a man of twenty-five did not tell her she was wrong, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in the same yard for the whole of it and was not asked one question about a bar four hundred miles off and did not ask one, and that a man of about nineteen has been standing at one end of that bar for fifty-nine days and neither of the two men in that yard has said his name out loud since the ninth of Wolfmonth and nobody in that yard has asked either of them why.**

---

At about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar came off that lane and said the one thing he had said once in nineteen days, which was that a bridge is not a thing you can be paid for in the middle of it.

“**Forty days,**” he said. “**Nine of them are curing and a man standing still on a lane for nine days is a man a woman with a holding does not have to pay for and thinks she ought to and will say so in a year. Nobody has ever got paid for the curing and I have never known anybody who thought they would be.**”

**And nobody in that yard said anything about the curing, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a yard of nine men four hundred yards up their own road is nine days past a lane a mile off their own market with the stone paid for and the days not, and that a man of thirty-two with a limp has been in that yard three times in about two months and has not given an order in it and has been asked nothing, and that the press in the room at the end of that yard is standing open at about thirty-nine weeks.**
