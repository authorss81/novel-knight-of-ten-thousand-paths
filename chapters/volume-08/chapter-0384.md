# Chapter 0384

**The fourteenth day of Hearthmonth, YR 317, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where nine men were at a bench-board and a man of thirty-two with a limp came up that road at about the fourth hour for the third time in about two months, and nobody in that town knows that nine men in that yard have just agreed to put a crossing back a mile off their market.**

A Thursday in that county is a market day, and the market is a Saturday thing that happens on Thursdays as well, and four hundred yards of that one took about two thousand people that morning. The hut with three bars in front of it was up that county's own road with the man of about fifty-five in it, and the bars did not stop one person all morning that they do not stop every other morning, and a bench of nine sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week and did not sit.

Five of the nine were in that yard on that Thursday and four were not, and one of the four was standing at the end of that market with a cart and one was at a kiln two miles off. **A boy of fifteen was at a bench-board about nine feet long copying a figure and the space under it, and the space was empty and had been empty about nine hundred times, and a man of about sixty was packing lime against a heap that has a lid on it, and the press in a room at the end of that yard was standing open at about thirty-eight weeks and was not going to be shut, and four sheets with nothing at all on the wrapper were in it, and nobody in that county has ever read the wrapper.**

---

The person who came up that road was not him. **A woman of about thirty-four has a holding at the end of a lane a mile off that market and two carts and twenty-seven days of going round, and she had been told on the sixth of that month by a man with a crowbar that a yard of nine men four hundred yards up that county's own road is the only thing in the county that can lay a sill, and she had not believed him for four days and then she had gone and looked at the yard from the top of the road and had not gone in, and on the twelfth of that month she had gone in.**

She said it once at about the fourth hour and the speech as printed is forty-four words with the attribution excluded, and the forty-four were a lane and a man and a crossing and a season, and the word *about* does not stand in front of the figure, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp was in that yard and did not know any of it.

“**A mile off your market there is a crossing with a chain on it and a drain under it, and I have two carts and twenty-seven days of going round, and there is no other man in this county who can put it back.**”

**And the man of twenty-five put the board he had been planing on down on the bench-board and took his hands off it, and nobody in that yard had seen him do that since a Sunday morning in Wolfmonth, and about four thousand people in that town do not know it, and the board he had been planing on was not the board he had been planing on the last time.**

He asked her two questions and they were both fair. **He asked what she had and she said the stone is five shillings and twopence and has been on that lane eleven years and is written in a book in a room at the end of that market, and he asked who does the drain and she said a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar has said he will not do it for nothing, and the man of twenty-five said the drain is half the job, and nobody in that yard had said so and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar had said it four times.**

---

**And the decision that yard made on the fourteenth day of that month was made in about a quarter of an hour and is not in the book, and the whole of it is that the job is nine men and about forty days and that about five of the men on it every day will not be from that county.**

The man of twenty-five said the first part of it and the boy of fifteen copied it.

“**The drain is a fortnight and the sill is a fortnight and the pointing is a day and the stone has to cure nine days before a load goes over it, and the course on each side is in the fortnight. That is thirty-eight days, and there are two days in the forty that are weather and not work, and the man with the crowbar said twenty and the twenty was him and the forty is the lane. The forty days are the part of the year this yard has no bridge work in, and a lane a mile off a market is not a bridge and a drain is not a bridge, and I am not putting a bridge rate on either of them for a man I have never worked for.**”

Nobody in that yard said anything. **A yard of nine men said nothing for about four seconds and a boy of fifteen's pen stopped and started again and the space under the number was still empty when it started, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a yard of nine men takes about four seconds to agree to a thing and that four seconds has been the same in that yard for about four years.**

“**Where are the nine,**” the woman of about thirty-four said.

“**Four of them are in that yard and five of them are not and will not be in this season, and one of the four is on a bar four hundred miles off for four hours on four days in seven, and I have not got nine, and about four of the nine of us can be on that lane in any one day and I am going to take six more on from two other counties and pay them the going rate and not the rate in that book, and the going rate is a day rate and a day rate is a thing a man can be paid in.**”

**And about four people in that county have said since that about half of the men on that job every day would be from other counties, and that a yard of nine men that has to hire half its own work in is a yard that has said yes to a thing it does not have, and that the man of twenty-five said it in a quarter of an hour in a tense he had no business using, because a man who describes a decision in the tense of a fact has made the decision already and is only telling the room about it.**

“**You have said yes.**”

“**We have said yes,**” the man of twenty-five said. “**I have said it for the nine. Four of them were in this yard this morning and five were told in a doorway, and the four who were in said nothing first, and the four who were out said nothing, and the two fours then had nothing to argue about because there was nothing in the room.**”

---

**The six men are not from that county. Two of them are from a yard four days off in one direction and four of them are from a yard nine days off in the other, and the rate they are coming in at is a day rate and is not the figure in that book's book, and about four people in that county put it the way a yard would: a yard which pays two rates for the same nine days of work has understood something about rates that a yard which pays one rate has not.**

**And a yard of nine men taking two rates for the same forty days is a yard that has said yes to being a yard that borrows, and it said it in a quarter of an hour on a Thursday in a market week, and the whole of what a yard borrows is somebody else's season, and a season does not come back.**

**And the ordinary labour of a bridge is this, and it is not a story and it has no form and it is not anybody's trade but a tradesman's.** A drain is opened and cleared and the silt goes into a barrow and the barrow goes somewhere. **A hole is dug under the made road on both sides and a pipe of about two feet is laid and the ends are walled in dressed stone with a plank on top of the wall, and that is a fortnight and it is the fortnight nobody puts in a figure.**

A sill is cut and fitted. **Six feet of stone, cut by a man who cuts stone for a living, and fitted dry with a shim of lead at each end and set on a bed of lime, and the two courses on either side of it are the same stone and they are laid on lime and not on earth, and the whole of the fitting is done by hand with a wooden mallet and a line and about four hours a day of it is waiting for the line.**

The pointing is lime and sand and water and about a day's work for two men. **The curing is nine days of doing nothing at all on that lane, which is the hardest part of bridge work and is not anybody's idea of work, and a man with a crowbar and a shovel who has done four jobs in six weeks has said twice in that county that the nine days of curing is where a job is lost.**

“**I have never lost one to it,**” the man of about thirty-eight said, when he was told. “**I have lost four to the four months in the year when a man cannot get out on a lane.**”

---

**The man of about sixty packed lime against a heap that has a lid on it and said nothing about any of it, and a lid on a heap of lime in Hearthmonth is not a thing a man does for a yard that has just agreed to forty days. A man of about sixty who is the only man in that yard not paid out of it took three bushels of lime out of his own heap at about the fifth hour and put the lid back on it while he did it, and nobody in that yard asked him where it had gone and nobody has asked him in about four years.**

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was in that yard from the fourth hour to the sixth hour and did not give an order and was not asked for one.** He came up that road twice before this in about two months and both of the two were about a road four hundred miles off, and this one is about a lane a mile off a market, and **he had no standing in that yard on any of the three and everybody in that yard knew it and nobody said it, and the only thing anybody in that yard said to him on the fourteenth day of that month was the price of the stone.**

“**What is the yard out by,**” he said. “**You are four hours a day light on four days in seven. That is four men out of nine for four months and you have just agreed to a forty-day job with about five of you on it.**”

“**The whole nine,**” the man of twenty-five said. “**And the fourth hour of a day when that bar is down is not four hours, it is four hours and a walk of nine days at both ends of a season. I have had four months to work that out and I have not got a better answer than the one I have.**”

**And about four people in that yard have said since that a man of thirty-two with a limp asked a yard of nine men a fair question about their own book in public work and got a fair answer, and that neither of the two men said one word about a bar four hundred miles off, and that a bar four hundred miles off was the reason the yard was four hours a day light and neither of the two of them said so, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that.**

---

**And the bar cost thirty-five pence and a shed nine feet by seven cost nineteen pence, and neither figure is on anything and neither was told to anybody on that road, and a stone sill at thirty-four pence and a course on each side at eleven pence a course and lime at three pence a bushel and two bushels is sixty-two pence, and that figure was said out loud on the sixth of that month at about the fifth hour in a lane a mile off that market by a man with a crowbar who had a reason not to, and a drain is sixty-two pence of nothing, and a bar and a sill and a drain and a shed are four different costs of four different things and are not added to each other anywhere in this kingdom, and a toll is not a wage and a wage is not a toll and a bar is not a rate and none of the four of them is in a column with the other three.**

At about the sixth hour the woman of about thirty-four went back down that road with the price of the stone in her head and a date. **A yard of nine men had agreed in a quarter of an hour to put a crossing back after twenty-seven days, on terms the yard set and did not write down, and about four thousand people in that town do not know it, and the press in the room at the end of that yard is standing open with four sheets in it that have nothing on the wrapper, and not one of the forty days is in the book, and a boy of fifteen has been copying a figure and a space since the first of a month in Longlight and the space is empty and nobody in that county has ever told him what it is for.**
