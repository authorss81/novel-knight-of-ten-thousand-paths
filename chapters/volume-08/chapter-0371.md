# Chapter 0371

**The twenty-sixth day of Embermonth, YR 317, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in an upper room over a chandler's shop in a lane off that market, in a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a paper that had been folded once across the middle for eleven days was opened out flat on a table, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp read all nine lines of it with a finger and put it down and asked what one person would be paid, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that a paper is open on that table.**

Nothing trades on a Wednesday. **A bar four miles south of that town is up, as it is on four days in seven, and the woman who puts it down and takes it up is at it on the four days and is at it on the eight as well, and about nine hundred people came into that town on the last Saturday and about two hundred of them are out of their houses this morning, and nobody in that lane is doing anything that anybody can be shown.**

A woman of about thirty-five had carried that paper eleven days and had not opened it since the second of them. **She opened it. She squared the two halves with her thumbs the way a person squares a thing they have carried a long way, and she did not look for the fold, and two people in that lane noticed that she did not.**

The paper is nine inches by six. **It has no head on it and no columns and nothing ruled on it, and at the foot of the ninth line there is no name and no mark of any kind, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet asked about that at the second hour and was told that a name at the foot would make the paper a person's paper, and that their council is a room and not a bench, and a room does not sign and a bench does.**

Five of the nine lines are about the place. **The first of the five is about nine hundred. The second is that the crossing runs four months of the year and does not say which four. The third is that the road holds a load in a season when no other road on that side holds a load. The fourth is that the account of the worst winter in eighteen years is theirs, and that one woman wrote it, and that she reads it out herself. The fifth is that they have been two winters without a road and are still there.**

The other four are the offer and the writing in them is the writing of somebody who has been thinking about it for two winters. **The road is theirs and is not lent and is not given and is not for sale. For the four months it will carry a load for anybody who comes to it from this side. They will not send it out and will not widen it and will not put a price on it. And there has to be a person at this end, one person, for the four months, and not a crew and not a rate and not a man who collects.**

**And then the seventh of the nine lines, which is the one that about four people in that room had not expected, is what happens if the person is taken off. It is that the road shuts on the day the person goes and does not open again for that season, and that no other road on that side carries a load until the four months come round again, and it is written the way a thing is written when the people who wrote it have already thought about a person walking away with a load on a road they made.**

---

**And two people in that lane have said since that the whole of what the nine lines add up to is four months and a person and a road that is already there, and that a piece of paper with three things on it that two winters of a community of about nine hundred people have agreed to is a more careful thing than a Crown instrument with nine things on it and a ruled space for a signature, and that neither of the two was in that room.**

Nobody in that room said a word about the seventh line for about a minute.

“**That is the line I would have written second,**” the man of thirty-two said.

“**That is the line we wrote second,**” the woman of about thirty-five said, “**and we wrote it first and we took it out and put it back, and I want you to understand that there are about nine hundred of us and that all of us agreed to the order of those lines.**”

---

**And the eighth line is the steward and the ninth is the end of the four months. The eighth says that a steward may refuse the person, and that if a steward does not want the person then the road does not open, and does not open for the four months after that either, and that this is not a threat. The ninth says that at the end of the four months the person goes home, and that the road is theirs again, and that what it has carried stays where it has been carried to, and that they do not ask for it back and will not take it back.**

**Nobody in that room had expected a line about giving things back. A man of about thirty-three with a barrow asked about it twice and the woman of about thirty-five answered it twice and gave the same answer both times, which was that a road that is used for four months will be different at the end of them and that a community of about nine hundred people who have been two winters without a road cannot un-use it and has not pretended that it can, and that a place that asks for a road back and its loads back is a place that has not thought about what a road is.**

---

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote none of the nine lines down. **He has a form and it is a good form and there is no column on it for a line that says a road shuts on the day a person goes, and there is no column for a line that says we will not take it back, and he said so himself at the fourth hour and then did not say anything else about either of them for the rest of that day.**

“**There is nothing on this to write,**” he said, “**and there is nothing on my sheet that is not on it, and that is the first time in nine years that has happened to me, and I have not found a way of making it stop happening.**”

And a man of about fifty-four who burns lime at a kiln two miles off that town worked out what the paper had cost to come here while he was saying that, because a man who has been at his own trade for thirty years works out a figure in his head and cannot stop doing it.

**Eleven nights at a lodging at threepence, and eleven days of bread at twopence, and eleven threepences is thirty-three pence, and eleven twopences is twenty-two pence, and thirty-three pence and twenty-two pence is fifty-five pence, and fifty-five pence is four shillings and sevenpence, and that is the whole of what it cost a person to walk here with nine lines on a piece of paper.**

“**Fifty-five pence,**” he said. “**I have had a kiln go nine days on that in Embermonth two years back and I know what a fifty-five pence winter is.**”

The woman of about thirty-five said that he had it right and had it right because he had been walking about four hundred miles of that road himself in his own life and had never once counted it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said that the figure was not going on anything, and the woman of about thirty-five said that she had not expected it to go on anything and had not asked for it to.

---

**And a man of about thirty-three with a barrow asked the one question in that room that had a person in it, and he asked it standing up and he asked it once, and what he asked was who would be the person, and the woman of about thirty-five said that the steward at that end would say no to a name and no to a man he had not met and would say yes to a person who had stood in the room they keep, and that a name on a paper four hundred miles off is worth nothing at all to a man who can say no.**

“**I have written the name of a keeper in a column,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and I do not need to see what is in the column, and that head has been the same head for two years.**”

“**Then you know what a name in a column is,**” the woman of about thirty-five said, “**and I am not going to say the rest of it in this lane, because a man who has said that in a room has said it in a room and I have eleven days of road behind me and I am not going to have it said about me in a town of two thousand people.**”

And about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of about thirty-five stopped a man in the middle of a sentence she was owed the end of, and that what stopped him was not the road and was not the eleven days, and was that about nine hundred people had agreed to the order of nine lines and she was the one carrying them.

---

**And then a man of about thirty-two with a limp did the thing that about four people in that lane worked out afterwards was the whole of what he came for, and it took him about four seconds, and it was that he worked out that the offer can be answered and cannot be answered with a figure.**

There is no form for a person standing at the end of a road for four months. **There is no column for it, and a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane two hundred miles off has a press standing open and seven presses behind it and about four hundred and nine parish rolls that have been carried every month for nineteen years, and not one of the twenty-odd instruments in that building has a place in it for a thing that is not a place or a figure or a person by that person's trade.**

**And there is a page in his coat with nine lines on it and no heads and no columns and no ruling, and he put his hand on it twice that afternoon and did not take it out, and two people in that lane have worked out since that a man with a page in his coat that he has not looked at has found out that the page is not the thing he came for.**

The offer wants one of those three. **The offer wants a person, and a person is the only one of the three that cannot be written, and about two thousand people in that town do not know that a road four hundred miles off has been offered to them against a thing that this kingdom has no box for.**

“**Then it is answerable,**” the man of about thirty-two said.

“**It is answerable,**” the woman of about thirty-five said, “**and it is not answerable here, and it is not answerable by anybody in this room, and the only person who can answer it is a person who has to stand in a room four hundred miles off and be refused or not, and I have been in this lane for eleven days and I have not seen anybody get on a horse.**”

**And the paper went back into the bag, and a man who had come up that road with something to say had spent the whole of an hour finding out that the thing he had come to say was a person, and a person is the one of the three that cannot be written, and he found that out in about four seconds and then asked a question about a horse.**

“**The four months are the same four months,**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said, at the seventh hour, on his way to the door, “**and you have known that since the first day and neither of you has said it out loud in nine days, and it is not a favour.**”

“**No,**” the woman of about thirty-five said. “**It is not a favour. It is the other side of a season, and I have said so, and I will say it again in nine days, and in nine days I will still be here, because we have been two winters without a road and we can wait nine days.**”

**And his left palm was warm at the fourth hour and had been warm since the fourth hour, and he put it flat on the table for a minute at the seventh hour with the paper folded in front of him, and nobody in that room asked him about his hand, and a man who puts his hand flat on a table in front of a folded paper is a man waiting for a season and not for a reply.**
