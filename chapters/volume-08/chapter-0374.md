# Chapter 0374

**The sixth day of Wolfmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a yard on Weir Street, four hundred yards up that county's own road above a market of four hundred yards and nine hundred set stones, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where nine men were at a bench-board about nine feet along and a press stood open in a room at the end of the yard, and a man of thirty-two with a limp came up that road at about the fourth hour with an order, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that anybody came up that road with one.**

A Saturday in that county is a market day and about two thousand people came into four hundred yards of stone. **Nine hundred of those stones are ones a person could name and nine hundred of them are ones nobody ever has, and forty men were on a reach four hundred yards down a lane off the north end of that market, and a bench of nine sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week, and about four thousand people in that town have never once had to think about which day the county trades on.**

The yard was not empty on a Saturday and it was not full. **Two of the nine were in the market at the second hour standing at the end of it and two were waiting for a cart, and five were doing something, and a boy of fifteen was at a bench-board copying a figure that has been in that yard's book since Longlight and copying the space under it, which is empty, and nobody on that yard has ever told him what goes in the space and nobody is going to.**

At the back of the yard a man of about sixty was packing lime against a heap that has a lid on it. **He is the only man in that yard who is not paid out of it, and he said nothing at the second hour and said nothing at the fourth, and a man who has said one true thing about a lid out loud in a year said nothing on a Saturday morning, and a man who says nothing is not a man who has stopped noticing, and a lid is a lid in any month.**

---

**Forty men have been on that reach since the first of the month, and the reach has four timber bays in it, and the fourth bay was finished and pointed in the week before, and about four people in that county have said since that a yard four hundred yards up a road and forty men on a reach four hundred yards down a lane are the two halves of the same thing and that on a market Saturday in the fourth month of a shut season both of them are working, and that one of the two halves has been told about a road four hundred miles off and the other one has not.**

**And the fourth bay of that reach was pointed in the week before, and a pointed bay is a bay with stone laid into the joints of it, and about nine feet of that stone, and the stone came off a cart from a quarry two days north that nobody in that town has ever been to. Forty men have been putting pointing into timber on that reach since the first of that month, the road is open over the top of it, and rather more than two hundred carts have gone across it in a week, and not one of the forty has heard the name of a road four hundred miles off and would not be interested in it if he had.**

A man of about nineteen with an empty hod was planing a board and had been planing a board for about four months.

**He went out to the gate at about the fifth hour and came back in at about the sixth and neither of the two men in the middle of that yard noticed either time, and a man who is not noticed when two people are disagreeing about a thing is a man who will be the one who is asked to do it, and nobody has asked him, and there is a sister of his in this county of twenty-two that he has not spoken to in a year and that not one of the nine on that yard has asked him about that either.**

The man of twenty-five came out of the room at the end of the yard at about the fourth hour and a man of thirty-two with a limp was standing at the gate with a stick he cannot lean on. **Getting up to a gate takes him about four seconds longer than sitting down at one, and he has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight and is not going to, and about four people in that yard have watched him do it about nine times in as many visits and have never once said anything about it.**

**And the press in the room at the end of that yard was standing open and had been standing open for about twenty-eight weeks, and it cannot be served with anything, and four sheets with nothing at all on the wrapper were in it, and they are on that yard's book and they are never going to leave that county, and a refusal is a thing set, and there are four of them in a wrapper in a room that anybody can walk into, and nobody in that county has ever read the wrapper.**

---

**And he gave the order, and it was the plainest order he has given anybody in about two years, and he gave it standing in the middle of a yard on a market Saturday with six of the nine within about nine feet of him, and two people in that county have worked out since that a man who is good at seeing a load path does not soften an order to make it easier to refuse.**

“**I want nine men,**” he said, “**or as many of the nine as will stand a bar. A bar down and a bar up at the head of a made road that runs west, four miles off a town of about two thousand people on the salt, for the four months of the year the crossing there runs, and a man on it while it is down.**”

Nobody in that yard said anything for about four seconds. **A boy of fifteen's pen stopped and started again and the space under the number was still empty when it started, and two people in that county have worked out since that a boy of fifteen who copies a figure and the space under it watched a man give an order in that yard without knowing that the order was not a figure and was never going to be entered.**

The man of twenty-five did not say no. **He put the board he had been planing on down on the bench-board and took his hands off it, and four people in that county have worked out since that a man of twenty-five in a yard of nine men puts a tool down in order to have both hands free, and that he had not done it in that yard in about four years, and that nobody in that yard had ever seen him do it and nobody was going to be told what it meant.**

“**Where is it,**” he said.

“**At the head of a made road, on made ground, with nine yards of soft shoulder on the salt side of it and a ditch that takes the water off that shoulder in a wet week.**”

“**I have never seen it.**”

“**No.**”

“**How far.**”

“**Four hundred miles.**”

---

**And about four people in that yard worked out at the same moment what a man of twenty-five had worked out about two seconds after he heard the answer, which is that a yard puts a man on a bar at the end of its own road and not at the end of a road four hundred miles off, and that a man at the end of a road four hundred miles off is a man who is not in his own county and cannot be sent for by anybody in it, and that is a different thing from a man at a bar in a lane off this county's own market.**

A yard of nine men can put a man on a bar nine miles off. **It can put him there in a cart in an hour with a bar and a lever and a stone, and this is the ordinary condition of a yard and is not a story and has no form, and the thing that makes four hundred miles a different kind of number is not the four hundred and it is the nine days.**

“**Nine days,**” the man of twenty-five said. “**That is a cart road and not a made one, and I have got a reach to finish in this market week and a sill to be fitted on Tuesday, and if I put a man on that road I have eight men on a fourteen-week season instead of nine, and eight is not a crew, and a yard with eight men either borrows a crew or loses a bridge rate.**”

“**It is a bar and a man and four hours a day,**” the man of thirty-two said.

“**It is a bar and a man and four hours a day nine days of a week for the length of a season,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**and you have not told me what the four hours are, and I have not asked, and I am going to keep not asking until you tell me or until I have worked it out, and I will work it out.**”

“**You may ask.**”

“**I will not,**” the man of twenty-five said, “**because the moment I ask you about the four hours then the four hours are the thing you came up this road for, and they are not, and I have been at this trade since I was sixteen and I know what a man is for and I know what he has actually come for, and he has not come for four hours.**”

**And a man of twenty-five told a man of thirty-two with a limp that he knew what the man had come for, and he was right about it, and being right about it in a yard is worth more than being right about a figure, and the man of thirty-two did not tell him and did not say that he had.**

**And the four hundred miles sat in the middle of that yard for about a quarter of an hour with nobody doing anything about it, and a man of about nineteen with an empty hod said one thing about it in about nine seconds and then went back to planing, and the thing he said was about what a bar weighs and what a lever weighs and how many men a bar takes to lift out of two sockets.**

“**Two men and a lever and a stone,**” he said. “**And you have not said who the two men are, and that is the only part of it I have been in a position to have an opinion about, and I have had one and I am not going to give it to you on a Saturday in a market week like this.**”

**And a man of nineteen who has held an empty hod for four months said the only sentence in a quarter of an hour that was about a load and not about a rate, and he was right, and he was not asked, and a man who is right about a load and is not asked is a man who will be the one who is asked.**

---

**A yard on a market Saturday is also nine men eating at three different hours. One of them had bread and a piece of cheese standing at the bench-board with the book shut in front of him, one had gone home at the sixth hour and would be back at the first, and one had brought a wife and two children to the gate and had put them on the cart and gone to the market. None of that is in anything. A yard of nine men has nine days in it and about nine hundred hours and a great many of them are spent in ways that would not survive being put in a column, and the four thousand people in that town have never known that and two people in that county had worked it out.**

**And the order went into the yard and nothing went into the yard's book. That is the rule of that yard and it has been the rule for about four years, and it is that nothing that belongs to a man goes in that yard's book and only a thing set, and a rate offered to a yard is a claim and a claim is not a thing set, and an order from a man who came up a road is a claim.**

**The boy of fifteen had a book open on that bench-board and nine columns ruled in it and one of the nine was empty, and a boy of fifteen who has never been told what goes in an empty column will spend the rest of that year copying a figure and a space and will not put anything in the space, and that is the only thing a yard has ever taught him and it is worth more than a figure.**

---

**And at the seventh hour the man of thirty-two with a limp was still in that yard and had not been asked anything else, and a man who has come up a road four hundred yards with an order and has been asked two fair questions and has answered both of them is in a worse position than he was at the second hour, and nobody in that yard has told him that and nobody was going to.**

**A yard of nine men in a market week on a Saturday is a different yard from the same nine men on a Tuesday in Longlight, and about two of them were still in the market at the seventh hour and about two of them had gone home and five were still at the bench-board, and none of the nine had stopped, and not one of them had said that they would think about it, and two people in that county have worked out since that a yard which has been given an order it does not want does not discuss the order in front of the man who gave it, and that a yard which stopped to discuss it would be a guild and this is not a guild.**

**And the man of about sixty with the lime heap packed the last of it and put the lid back on the heap, and a lid on a heap of lime in Wolfmonth is not a thing anybody in that yard would do for a man with a stick who has been in their yard four hours, and a man of about sixty who is not paid out of that yard did the one thing in it that is a kindness and did it to a lid and not to a man, and neither of the two of them said anything about it.**
