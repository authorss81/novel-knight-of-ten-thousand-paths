# Chapter 0370

**The twenty-fifth day of Embermonth, YR 317, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a room over a chandler's shop in a lane in a town of about two thousand people on the salt, where a woman of about thirty-five who had walked eleven days put a folded paper on a table and four people read it, and no month and no weekday was said in that room, and two thousand people in that town do not know that anybody came eleven days on foot into that room, and nine hundred people in a place four hundred miles off know exactly what was in that paper because they wrote it.**

A person is the way a thing is sent four hundred miles. **There is no other way in this part of the world and there has not been one in nineteen years, and what a courier is on that side of the flats is a person with a bag and a road and enough to eat, and the road is four hundred miles and is not a made road for the first two hundred and thirty of them.** She came into that lane at the second hour on a Tuesday of the second week she was in the town, and she had been in it nine days before that, and she had slept at a lodging and eaten at a table at the same hour each day, and what she carried was the paper and a change of shirt.

**And the writing is good.** Four people in that room have said since that a hand that can do that has been taught it and has been taught it by somebody who was taught, and that the hand writes a *t* the way a person writes a *t* who has been taught twice, and that the paper is not a form and has no head and no columns and nothing ruled on it, and that the whole of the paper is nine lines long and every one of the nine lines is a sentence.

The four people were a man of about thirty-four with a sheet, a man of about fifty-four who burns lime at a kiln two miles off, a man of about thirty-three with a barrow, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp who came in at the fourth hour and had a stick he cannot lean on. **Nobody in that room had any business in that room except the man of about thirty-four with a sheet, and the other three came because a man of about thirty-four with a sheet told a man of about fifty-four who burns lime that there was a paper, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet had told nine people and nine of the nine came, and four of the nine had a reason and five of the nine did not.**

---

**And the first thing the woman said when anybody asked her anything was that they would want to know what the crossing is, and she said the crossing runs four months of the year, and she said the four months without saying which four, and a person who keeps a count in crossings open and weeks shut does not have a month to say, and what a month is on that side is a thing a person on this side has to work out from a river.**

The second thing she said was about the people. **About nine hundred of them. About nine hundred people in a place of about nine dwellings and a river and a flat country of nine miles by four, and about nine hundred people who keep a list of their own that nobody else keeps, and about nine hundred people whose steward can refuse a road on their behalf and has refused two this winter already.**

The third thing she said was about the winter. **They have had two bad winters and the second one is now, and the account of the first of them was kept by one woman and is the one everybody in that place believes and nobody outside that place has ever read, and the account is a winter and a river and a road that is not a road for four months of the year, and the woman of about thirty-five said that their road holds a load in a season when no other road in that part of the flats holds a load, and that this is not a boast, it is a thing nine people would have to be asked about and eight of them would say it.**

“**Our road carries when nothing else on that side carries,**” she said. “**That is all of it. It is not a better road. It is a road that is there in the season when the others are not.**”

---

**And then she gave them the offer, and the offer is four lines of the nine, and two people in that lane have said since that a woman walked eleven days with an offer in four lines and that the offer was in four lines because she had been told that a thing that takes nine lines is a thing being wanted more than being offered, and that the whole of what a person eleven days on a road can carry in her head is four lines.**

The offer is a road. **It is theirs and it is not lent and it is not given and it is not for sale, and it runs four months of the year, and for those four months they will put a load on it for anybody from this side who comes to it, and they will not send it out, and they will not widen it, and they will not put a price on it, and two thousand people in that town do not know that any of that is on the table in a lane.**

The condition is a person.

“**We will do it,**” the woman of about thirty-five said, “**and there is one thing we cannot do it without, and it is not money, and we have thought about it for two winters. We cannot send a road out to somebody else's season with nobody at our end of it. There has to be a person at this end. One person. For the four months. Not a crew and not a rate and not a man who collects. A person who is here, and who is answerable while they are here, and who goes home when the four months are out, and who can be refused by a steward if a steward does not want them.**”

**And a woman said the word steward twice, and what a steward is in that room is a person who can say no, and about nine hundred people have given that power to one person or a few, and the giving and the taking is a thing that happened two winters ago on a river and is not in anybody's writing.**

“**If you send somebody and our steward does not want them,**” she said, “**then we do not do it, and we do not do it for the four months after that either, and that is not a threat. It is what we are. We have been two winters without a road and we are still here, and we would rather be two winters without a road than be one winter with a road that was sent to us.**”

---

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet asked her what a person at that end would be paid, and she said she did not know and that it was not hers to decide and that the steward would decide it and that the steward had not been asked, and a man with a sheet asked a woman about a wage, and the answer was that a person four hundred miles off had not been asked what a person from this side should be paid, and the answer is also a refusal of four hundred miles of questions.**

“**Will they take a person off us,**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said. “**Off you.**”

“**Nobody takes anybody off us,**” the woman of about thirty-five said, “**and we have nobody here to hand over, and the one person is one person and is not a levy, and if that is what you are thinking then the answer is no and you can stop asking.**”

---

**And nobody answered it.**

A man of about fifty-four who burns lime at a kiln two miles off that town said that a road that holds a load in a bad season was worth having and then stopped, and did not finish the sentence, and a man who burns lime said the useful half of a thing in nine words and did not say the other half, and the other half was about who would be standing at the other end of it in a bad season, and he did not say it because he is a man of about fifty-four who has been at his own trade for thirty years and knows exactly what is on the other end of a road.

**A man of about thirty-four with a sheet said that a road is a road and that a price is a price and that the two of those are not the same thing and did not say any more than that.** A man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that about four hundred people two days north of that town had been on a hurdle for half an hour, and that a person who will not come out past a hurdle is not a person anybody is going to send a road to, and a man with a barrow said a true thing about a hurdle in the same room as an offer of a road, and the two of those do not fit each other and nobody in that room fitted them.

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp did not answer it either, and he has a page in his coat with nine lines on it and no heads on it, and what he did was look at the paper for about four seconds, which is how long it takes, and then look at the woman, and the rest of the hour was not the paper and not the woman and was nine people in a room who wanted to say something and had each found out inside about four seconds that the thing they wanted to say was a person and not a figure, and a figure can be agreed in a minute.**

**And the woman of about thirty-five put the paper back in her bag and said that she would be in the town for nine more days and that there was no hurry, because they had been two winters without a road and they could wait nine days, and that nobody was to walk eleven days for an answer, and that if there was going to be no answer she would rather be told in the town than find out on a road. And the whole of what she said about the four months is that they run four months of the year, and the whole of what she said about the people is nine hundred, and the whole of what she said about the account of the worst winter in eighteen years is that it is theirs and that the woman who wrote it reads it out herself and that nobody else reads it out for her, and the last of those three things is the whole of what four hundred miles came for and the first two of them are the price.**

**And the woman of about thirty-five did not sit down for the whole of that hour, and about two hundred miles of road were under her feet, and a man of about fifty-four who burns lime offered her a stool out of the kiln and she took it, and a woman who had walked eleven days took a stool out of a kiln, and did not take it out of her own pack, and a person four hundred miles off who had come to be refused did not have a pack with a stool in it.**

**And at about the seventh hour the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said that he had a question and it was whether their four months and the salt season came at the same time, and she said that they did, and that they are the same four months, and two people in that room have worked out since that this is the only question anybody in that room asked that could have been asked in four seconds and that it was the only one anybody asked that had a figure in it, and the answer means that the offer is not a favour and is the other side of a season, and two thousand people in that town do not know that the two of those are the same thing.**

**And the paper went back into the bag, and nobody in that room answered it, and the offer is real, and the condition is a person and not a price, and two thousand people in that town do not know that a road four hundred miles off is on a table in a lane, and nine hundred people in a field four miles off do not know either, and about four hundred people two days north of that town do not know that there is anything at all.**
