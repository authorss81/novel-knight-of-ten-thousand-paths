# Chapter 0601

**Embermonth the twenty-sixth, YR 320, a Friday and not a market day, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house that is shut on Fridays and is not shut, four hands of writing, a name at the top of it, and a bundle a hundred and four in a band.**

The room over the weigh-house is shut on every day that is not a market morning, and on the twenty-sixth of Embermonth it was open at the second hour, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps it gave no reason for that on the stair and gave none afterwards, and the key came out of her apron and went into the lock and the door stood open for about five hours on a day it has stood open for about four and a half years on market mornings and on nothing else.

The reason was a carrier and a ford. The week's returns come in on the Thursday as they come in on the Thursday, and that Thursday the carrier turned back twice and came in on the Friday morning instead with the band, about a hundred and four in one bundle, which is a series of its own and does not wait on a day because it is bound in a cord and the cord is what keeps it. The copyist of about twenty-six said the cord had been tied twice and showed the knot to nobody, and then she said nothing more about it, because a knot that has been tied twice is a carrier's knot and not a matter for a room that keeps records about roads.

She went through the band the way she goes through a band, two to the minute and no talking. The register lay on the table with seven entries in it and every one of them a question and none of them a finding. The day-book lay open at nine. The press stood with its mouth open and no date anybody will give it. The drawer about eleven inches deep held about four hundred returns and she did not count them, because counting them was the office's business and not hers. The Crown's form was on the side table with three heads filled and the fourth ruled and empty, and it had been about seven months that way, and nobody had filled it and nobody had said out loud that anybody anywhere had filled one.

At the fourth sheet she stopped.

She read it. She put it down flat on the table with the writing side up. She picked it up and read it again, and the second time she went along it with one finger, which she does not do.

Four hands of writing. No seal. Nothing on the back of it. Nothing folded into it. At the top of the first page, in the place where a name goes on a thing that is going to be acted on, **Aren Kest**.

The woman of about twenty-nine came and read it standing at her shoulder, and then she said that the copyist was to read it out, which is a thing that happens in that room about four times a year and each of the four times it is because the copyist has found something she cannot make out and not because anybody in nine counties has ever doubted that she can read.

“**It is not a return,**” the copyist said, and that was the first thing said about it.

“**Read it,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

The copyist read it. It is four hands long and it is written in the flat hand that nine counties of clerks write in when they are writing something they intend to be kept. It directs that the keeper of a certain road be stood down for nine days, and that the road be shut for the same nine days, and that the matter be under question. The road is named by its two ends and by nothing else: the ferry stone at the low end, the ninth milestone at the top, and a ford between them that is dry in most summers.

“**Who sent it,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

The copyist turned the sheet over, looked at the back, which was blank, and put it down again.

“**Whose question,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

Nothing on the page said. It said the matter was under question, and it did not say by whom or about what, and that is the ordinary way of writing, and the woman of about twenty-nine said so herself in the same breath, because she is a woman who has spent four and a half years knowing exactly what a document does not say.

“**Then it has a name at the top and nothing at the bottom,**” she said, “**and a name at the top of a thing is the whole of what makes it a direction, and a direction with a name at the top of it does not need a sender, because a sender can be asked questions and a name cannot.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet was in the lane, because the key is in the lane while the door is open and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps that key stands in the lane while it is, and the man with the sheet had been standing at the board outside the weigh-house for an hour with nothing to do and had come up when the door opened. He read the sheet on the table in about a minute and a half and then asked three questions, which are the three a man in his position asks, and got no answer to any of them, and said so, and then asked a fourth one that nobody had asked.

“**It names a district,**” he said. “**It does not name a road. A road is two ends and a length and somebody who walks it every week. This names where it happens to be.**”

“**Then what is it naming?**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

“**It is naming about a hundred paces of somebody's work,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**That is the whole of what a district is, once you have stood in this room long enough.**”

“**Then it has a name at the top and nothing at the bottom,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, and went back to the sheet, and read the first line of it again. “**A road may be described only by its own answer. That has been the standing of this room for four and a half years and I have never once had cause to write it on a bare leaf, because it has never come up here.**”

“**It is coming up here,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**It is a piece of paper with a name at the top,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**And the fourth head of the Crown's form has been empty on that side table for about seven months and no man in nine counties has put anything under it, and those are two objects about two feet apart and neither of them is a return.**”

Nobody said what to do with either of them, and the copyist wrote nothing at all on the bare leaf she keeps for things with no head over them, which is not the same thing as writing nothing on it and is a smaller thing than a clerk thinks.

Aren Kest was not in that room and did not know the sheet was in it. He was four days and about nine off, on a made road, with his stick, going towards a place where a cart was not coming back, and a woman of about thirty-eight had said four days ago that she would not send for him and had sent anyway, which is a thing that can be done with a hired boy and eleven miles and is done often enough in nine counties that nobody has ever thought to write a rule against it.

The copyist said the second thing of that day about half an hour later, to the window, to nobody, and there were four people in that room and all four heard it.

“**Then it is a direction,**” she said, “**and a direction is obeyed.**”

“**Nobody has to obey it,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**Nobody has to,**” the copyist said, “**and everybody will, and the reason they will is that a world in which nothing with a name at the top of it is obeyed is a worse world, and that is true, and it is the reason, and I have sat in rooms where it was true.**”

The woman of about twenty-nine did not answer that, and the copyist did not press it, and the sheet lay on the table face up for the rest of that day with nothing on top of it and nothing under it.

At the seventh hour the room was shut, which is the hour it shuts on a market morning, and the key went back down the outside stair into the lane, and the band was finished and the about four hundred returns went into the drawer about eleven inches deep, and the carrier was paid at the door for the two days he had lost at the ford and said that the cord had been tied twice by a woman at the second bridge who was not his wife and was not going to be asked about it again.

**And the cost of that Friday is a column and it is the copyist's, and a sheet that arrived two days late and a name that is on it and not from it are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a bundle cord at eight, a sheet of foolscap at ten, an inkhorn at twelve, a pen at sixteen, a quill knife at seventeen, a cake of ink at nineteen, a rushlight holder at twenty, a tallow candle at thirty-one, a wipe at forty, and a sheet of thin paper at forty-one.** Eight, ten, twelve, sixteen, seventeen, nineteen, twenty, thirty-one, forty and forty-one is two hundred and fourteen pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and fourteen pence is four marks and twenty-two over**, and nine counties, four hands of writing, seven rooms of clerks, about eleven inches of drawer, about four hundred returns, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, a fourth head ruled and empty, a half leaf face down, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries and a press standing open are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the second hour, the fourth hour, the seventh hour, a Friday, four and a half years, about seven months, a market morning and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and Aren Kest and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and about four people who came up the stair and a carrier who lost two days at a ford and a woman at the second bridge who tied a cord and about four hundred people in a market town who hold one true thing about him and about four hundred who hold another are ten figures of persons, and this column is the first of them and does not speak for the other nine.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked.
