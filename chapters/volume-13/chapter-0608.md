# Chapter 0608

**Wolfmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 320, a Sunday, the second hour at the counter under the weigh-house, a printed handbill in a district hand, a woman of about fifty who keeps a road for nineteen years, and nine days that cost nobody anything anybody can point at.**

The room was shut because it was a Sunday and the room is shut on every day that is not a market morning, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet was at the counter under it anyway, because the district post rides on Sundays, the made road to the district is sound on a Sunday and is churned on a Thursday, and a direction has to be carried and the room cannot carry it.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key saw him at the counter from the lane with a sheet on the board in front of him and a handbill under his hand, and she did not say anything to him, and she went and found the man of about fifty-eight with a badge on his back steps at the inn and told him in eleven words what was on the board, and the man of about fifty-eight came down to the weigh-house on a Sunday, which he has not done in about four years, and nobody said that out loud.

The sheet he read was the one from the twenty-sixth of Embermonth, four hands of writing, a name at the top, no seal, nothing on the back. The handbill was new. It was the direction cut down to about nine lines and printed in the district's own hand, with the same name at the top of it in the same place, and it added three things the sheet had not: where the road was, what the keeper was to be told, and from what day.

**The man of about fifty-eight with a badge read it twice and then said the only thing about it that was new.**

“**It does not need to be believed,**” he said. “**It needs to be delivered. That is the whole of what happened in eleven days and there is not one person in this county who has done a dishonest thing.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet said that he had written the copy out in his own hand at the counter on the Thursday and had carried it to the district on the Friday, and that he had asked the woman of about thirty-four whether he should send it, and that she had said that was not her office, and that he had sent it.

“**Why did you send it?**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**Because it is a direction with a name at the top of it and no name at the bottom,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and I am a keeper of sheets, and a sheet that comes to me with a name at the top of it is a sheet I carry, and the not carrying of it is the only thing I could have done and nobody asked me to do that and nobody would have believed me if I had.**”

“**You would have been right,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**I would have been a man in a market town who had read a sheet and done nothing with it,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and I would have had to say that out loud, in a lane, to about four hundred people, and about four hundred of them hold one true thing about that man and about four hundred hold another and the two of them do not overlap.**”

Nobody in that lane said out loud that the two sets were talking about Aren Kest, and everybody in that lane knew, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet knew best of them and said nothing about it then or afterwards.

What happened next took about a minute and a half and it happened four days off, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key had it from a carter on the Thursday.

A gatekeeper at the district end of the ferry stone read the handbill at the gate. He is a man who has stood at that gate for about eleven years and whose job is to read out loud what a paper says to anybody who is going along the road. **He did not check the name. He said out loud, and the page says he said it out loud, that checking the name meant asking who the man was, and the man was not in the district, and there was nobody in the district who could say, and a gatekeeper who stops a road to ask a question he has no way of answering is a gatekeeper who has stopped the road.**

The woman of about fifty who keeps that road for nineteen years was told at the gate on the twenty-eighth of a month that she was stood down for nine days and the road was shut for the same nine days, the matter being under question.

She was not asked anything. Nobody asked her anything. She was not shown the page. She was told by a man at a gate, in the ordinary way, and she said that she would do as she was told, and she went home for nine days, and she came back on the ninth day and the road was open and there was nothing on it and nobody had been harmed by anything at all.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said the second part of that out loud at the counter, and said it as a fact, and did not soften it, and the copyist wrote it on the bare leaf with the other bare leaf and read none of it back.

“**Nine days,**” he said. “**A road shut nine days in a dry month on a road with about nine households at the top end and about nine at the bottom, and the slack in that month is nine days, and no wages went anywhere, because she is paid in kind and the kind is a season's slack. Nothing rotted. Nobody was hurt. Nobody was even late. The gatekeeper did his job. The man with the sheet did his job. The man at the top of the page has not been in this county in four years and did not write it, and there is no law in nine counties that says a man may not have his name put at the top of a page, and there is no law in nine counties that says a gatekeeper may not act on one.**”

He put both hands flat on the counter.

“**So I am going to say the thing this room has to say and I am going to say it in the lane afterwards so that anybody going past can hear it, and the copyist will write it on a bare leaf because there is nothing else in this room with a head over it. On the second of a month I stood in a room in this town and I said that a thing with a name at the top of it is not false because the man in the room thinks it false, and I said that in thirty-one years of this work it had never been done, and I said I wanted that on a leaf with my name on it as the man who said it. It has now been done. It took eleven days and it cost about nine hundred pence of a county's paper and nobody's livelihood at all. I said it had never been done, and I said it out loud, and the leaf has no head over it, and that is the whole of my part in it.**”

“**Your name is not on the leaf,**” the copyist said.

“**Then write it at the top,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and read it back to me.**”

She did not write it at the top. She said that a leaf with a name at the top of it is a direction, and that she had said that in that room in the first week of a month, and that she was not going to be the second person in nine counties to prove her own point, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge stood at a counter under a weigh-house and said that she was right and that he had lost an argument he had started.

The copyist wrote the seven words *the man with the badge said it* on the bare leaf, which is not a name and is not an office and is not in any book, and read none of it back, and it went into the drawer about eleven inches deep with about four hundred returns and a leaf with a figure on it and a leaf from the first of a month with two true things on it.

**And the cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the man with the badge's, and nine days on a road and eleven days in a county and a gate at the low end of a made road are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a printed handbill at sixteen, a bundle cord at twenty-one, a seal at thirty-four, a sheet of thin paper at thirty-five, an inkhorn at thirty-seven, a cake of ink at thirty-eight, a quill at thirty-nine, and a wax at forty.** Sixteen, twenty-one, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-seven, thirty-eight, thirty-nine and forty is two hundred and sixty pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and sixty pence is five marks and twenty over**, and two ends of a road, a ninth milestone, a ferry stone, about a hundred paces of a district, nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries and about four hundred returns are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Sunday, the second hour, the sixth hour, the seventh hour, four and a half years, about seven months, about three weeks and a market morning are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and Aren Kest and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a gatekeeper who has stood at a gate for about eleven years and about nine households at the top end and about nine at the bottom are ten figures of persons, and the eighth of them has not been asked one question in four and a half years and the ninth of them did the only thing he could do with a paper and the tenth of them has not been paid for nine days and is not owed anything for them.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the seven words went into a drawer.
