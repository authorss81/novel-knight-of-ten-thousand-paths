# Chapter 0644

**Mudmonth the twenty-seventh, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, about the fifth hour at the far end of about four hundred paces of lane, a wicket gate and a wall, and two people who had been walking the lane telling people about a meeting in the open.**

They had been walking the lane since the second hour because there is no notice to leave and there is nobody to leave it with, and the lane is about four hundred paces long and it has nine gates in it and about nine houses, and they had been to eight of them and had told about nine people at each one that there would be a meeting in a street on a Saturday morning with nothing at the top of it, and that the way to find out if it was on was that it was on.

Aren Kest stopped at the ninth gate and put his stick against the gatepost and sat down on the bank on the wall side, and getting up again took him about four seconds longer than sitting down had, and he did not pretend it had not.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room stood on the other side of the lane with her back to the hedge and waited until he had got his breath, and she did not say anything about the four seconds, and neither of them had ever said anything about the four seconds.

Then she said the thing, in a working voice, the way she said everything that was a fact.

“**Then from tonight we do not keep one place between us. I am not asking you about it and you are not going to answer me about it. I have had about a week and I have been walking it and I would rather said it in a lane at the end of the walk than in a room, and this is the end of the walk.**”

He said that he had thought so too, and that he had thought it on Saturday about half an hour after he said no, and that he had not said it then because she had been in front of about nine people and it was not the place.

“**Then this is the place,**” she said. “**And I am going to say three things and then we are going to do the ninth gate.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**The room is the room. It has a door on a lane and a number and a deed of thirteen words and it is fourpence a year and it is a body corporate with a door, and it is a place for records and not a place for two people to sleep in, and it has been that for longer than either of us has been asking each other anything. The two rooms over the saddler's are the inquiry's and they are empty above a shop that shuts at the seventh hour, and they are four hundred paces from here, and they are yours. I am not giving them to you. They are the inquiry's and the inquiry's has a floor somebody washes.**”

He said that he would take them on Saturday and that he was not going to say anything else about it, and that if she thanked him he would walk back down the lane.

She said that she was not going to thank him, and he said that this was the correct arrangement.

Then she said the second of the three things, and it took longer, and she said it facing down the lane and not at him.

“**There is not going to be any part of this where you are in the middle and I am in the middle. On Saturday you said no, and I was standing about nine paces from you when you said it, and I want you to know that I heard it and that I am not sorry and that I am not going to argue with you about it ever, including with somebody else on your behalf. In a lane in front of about nine people the middle of a thing is a place a person stands, and I am not going to stand in it, and I am not going to have you stand in it either while I am doing the work. And if we were both in it then the two of us would be the arrangement, and the arrangement is a room with a door, and I have had a room with a door for about four years and a half and I know exactly what it is for.**”

Nobody said anything for about as long as a barrow takes to go round a turning.

“**Say the third one,**” he said, “**because I have not got one and I am not going to let you put mine in for me.**”

“**I have got about four people and you are going to have about four people and the two sets are not going to be the same people. The copyist is mine and the woman of about fifty is mine, and the man with the satchel is yours and the badge-man is yours, and either of us may argue with the other's people in the open for as long as either of us likes, and neither of us is going to call it anything. And if one of mine comes to you with something you do not like you are to hear it out and not send them back down the lane to me, and if one of yours comes to me I will do the same.**”

“**That is the right arrangement,**” he said, “**and I am not going to improve on it, and I want to say one thing that is mine and not yours.**”

“**Say it.**”

“**I said no on Saturday because I could not stand in the middle of a thing about roads, and I meant it, and it made the last thing we were doing impossible, and I am not sorry for the no and I am not going to be asked about it by either of us.**”

She said that she had not been going to ask him about it, and that she had said the second of the three things because she had said it before he had his no and that she was not withdrawing it.

Then the third of the three things, and she said it quickly and she said it to the gate.

“**There is no document.**”

“**No,**” he said.

“**Not in the room, not in the inquiry's, not in nine hands, not on anything. It is said in a lane at the end of a walk and it is not written down anywhere and if it were written down anywhere it would be a thing with a place and I have watched a place swallow four and a half years of that room's life.**”

“**No document,**” he said, and he said it as though he had been expecting to be asked and had his answer ready and was glad not to have to use it.

A carter came up the lane with a load of nothing much and the wheels went through the one puddle that lane has and neither of them moved out of the way because there was room, and the carter said good day to neither of them in particular, and went on.

Then they did the ninth gate.

She knocked and he stood with his stick against the wall about four paces back, because a man with a limp at a gate is a man somebody is going to help and neither of them wanted that this morning. A woman of about sixty came to the gate and was told about a meeting in a street on a Saturday with nothing at the top of it, and said, “**How would I know?**” and was told that the way she would know was that it was on.

“**I have been to market four days this month,**” the woman of about sixty said, “**and I have not heard of it, and I am not complaining. I am saying that if it is on then somebody will have to tell me, and I am too old to walk a street and stand at a wall. Who is going to come to my gate?**”

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said that this was the correct objection and that it was the one that had been made to her in about four different lanes over two days, and that there was no answer to it, and that the answer that existed was that somebody who went to market would hear about four people talking about it and about four people would not.

Then Aren Kest said one thing to the woman of about sixty, and he said it to her and not to the woman of about twenty-nine.

“**I will be at the low end of the boards at the second hour on Saturday and I will stand there until the seventh hour and if anybody asks me whether it is on I will say that it is on. That is not a notice and it is not a promise and I am not asking you to walk anywhere. I am telling you that there will be a man at the boards.**”

The woman of about sixty said that was as good as anything anybody had said to her about anything in about a year, and shut the gate, and neither of them said anything about that.

Then they walked back down about four hundred paces of lane, and she went ahead by about the length of a cart, and he let her, and neither of them said another word about the three things, and neither of them said the word that neither of them was going to say, and both of them meant it, and there is no part of this in any document and there is not going to be one.

At the boards she said that she would be there on Saturday at the second hour and would be doing her own part of it, and he said that he would be at the low end of the boards and would be there until the seventh hour, and that he had said that to about nine people in a lane and would say it to about nine more in a market street and that a thing said to nine people is not a rule and is worth about nine times what a notice is worth.

Nobody thanked anybody. The register stood at nine questions and no finding went under any of them and the work went on, and both of them were in it, and the two of them were in one room together with a third person in it in none of it, and the road that is here and unknown sent nothing, and nobody had been to it, and nobody was going to be sent.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is nobody's, and an arrangement made in a lane and not written down anywhere and two sets of about four people who are not the same people are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a tallow candle at 1, a wicket latch at 10, a boot brush at 12, a string of twine at 18, a hoop of iron at 24, a sack of lime at 34, a barrow wheel at 37, a bundle of straw at 39, a gatepost at 40, and a padlock at 41.** One, ten, twelve, eighteen, twenty-four, thirty-four, thirty-seven, thirty-nine, forty and forty-one is two hundred and fifty-six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and fifty-six pence is five marks and sixteen over**, and a lane, nine gates, about four hundred paces, one puddle, a hedge, a wall, a wicket, two rooms over a saddler's, a shop that shuts at the seventh hour, a deed of thirteen words, a room with a door, three things, about four people of hers, about four people of his, a fourth head ruled and empty, and four hundred paces are sixteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Thursday, the second hour, the fifth hour, the seventh hour, about four seconds, about a week, tonight, Saturday, the fourth of the walk, a day, about four years and a half, four days, a month, about a year, the first of the month, and a year are sixteen figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **a carter with a load of nothing much and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a woman of about sixty at a gate and a woman of about thirty-four who came four days for a sentence and the copyist and Aren Kest and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road nineteen years and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned are ten figures of persons, and the tenth of them is not in this lane and the fourth of them said she was too old to walk a street and stand at a wall.** Nobody thanked anybody and nothing was written down about the three things and the two of them keep the work and keep the right to disagree.
