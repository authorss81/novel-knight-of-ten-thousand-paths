# Chapter 0619

**Hearthmonth the eighteenth, YR 320, a Wednesday and not a market morning, the second hour at the gate at the low end of a made road, a page read out a second time, nineteen columns, and a woman who asks what her own line is for.**

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge came four days down the made road to a gate at the low end of it and nobody sent him, and he said so to about four people at the gate before anybody had said anything to him, which is what he does.

“**A man with a sheet told me on a market morning that a road I keep is on a page going round nine counties,**” he said, “**and I am four days early and I am here because I would rather be here four days early than one day late, and there is no hearing in this county that can make a page false, and I have said that in a room and on a bare leaf with no head over it, and I am not going to say it again at a gate.**”

The gate at the low end of that road is the ferry stone, and a man has stood at it for about eleven years and his job is to read out loud what a paper says to anybody going along the road, and he read it out again at about the second hour on the eighteenth of a month to about nine people who had not heard it read the first time, in about six weeks, and he read it the whole way through including the parts that were only instructions about where the road was and what the keeper was to be told, because that is what a gatekeeper does and because he had been asked to by nobody in particular and he does it anyway.

The first time, at the end of the first week of a month, he did not check the name, and he said out loud why, and the reason was the same today and nobody in the lane disagreed with it then and nobody disagreed with it now.

The woman of about fifty was at the gate. She keeps the road from the ferry stone at the low end to the ninth milestone at the top, and she has kept it for nineteen years, and there is a ford between the two ends that is dry in most summers, and about nine households are at the top end and about nine at the bottom, and she had not been asked anything when a man at that gate told her in the first week of a month that she was stood down for nine days, and she had gone home for nine days, and about eleven days later she had come four days of carting to a market town to say a number out loud to a room, and the number was on a slate in her apron and the slate was hers and it went back into the apron.

The page came on the district cart at about the second hour and was put on the flat stone by the gate because there is nowhere else to put a page, and the woman of about fifty read it standing, and it took her about two minutes, and about nine people stood at the gate and a carter waited with his horse yoked because a carter who waits is a carter who is paid for waiting.

Then she asked the question.

“**What is my line for,**” she said.

The man from the district who had brought the page said what a man says when he is asked, and he said it carefully, because he is a man who carries paper and does not know what is on it.

“**Your line is the sixth,**” he said. “**Six lines are marked and six of them are to be held and the seventh is the one to be shut, and the page asks the six of you to put a mark in the space against your own line and by putting it in you are saying you will not stand in the way.**”

“**Then the page says nothing about my road,**” the woman of about fifty said, “**because my road is the sixth and the sixth is assumed. And the sixth is the only line on that page that is assumed, and I have found out this morning that about nine people at a gate can be asked to agree to shut a road on a page which has never once said in nine counties that the road they are being asked to keep exists.**”

Nobody said anything, and she went on, and she was not angry and there was no weight in her voice at all, and this is the third time in about six weeks that a person in this manuscript has said a true thing in a working voice.

“**I will put a mark in the seventh space,**” she said, “**and I will say why I will and I will say it now so that nobody has to guess at it afterwards. I am putting a mark in a space because the seventh space is empty and a road with nothing on it goes first, and I have watched this road go quiet for nine days in the first week of a month for a reason that was on a piece of paper and not on anybody's work. That is enough. Nobody has to weigh me and nobody has to weigh the seventh one. I am going to put a mark in a space because I have a figure and I am going to give it to you now, in the open, at a gate, in front of about nine people, and if I am wrong about it then every one of you will be able to say so to my face for the rest of my life.**”

Then she said the figure, and she said it in the way a person reads out a thing they have kept on a slate in an apron for nineteen years and have never been asked for, which is without any performance in it at all.

“**It is nineteen columns,**” she said. “**One column for each year, and in each column the days that this road was passable out of the days in that year, and the last column is not finished because the year is not finished, and there is a nine taken out of the last one in a hand that is not mine, and I do not know whose hand it is and I have never asked, because the nine was in it before I ever saw it and I have not rubbed it out and I am not going to.**”

“**Who took nine days out of it,**” a man near the gate said.

“**I have told you I do not know,**” she said, “**and I have told you that I have never asked, and both of those are true and I am not going to be asked a second time about it in a lane.**”

Then she said the thing she had come four days of carting for the second time in six weeks, and she said it to the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and not to the gate.

“**I would like somebody in this county to tell me which way my count runs,**” she said, “**and I asked a room in a market town in the first week of a month and nobody in that room knew, and nobody has told me since, and I am not angry about that, and I am not going to stop keeping it, and I am going to keep it exactly as it is until somebody who knows says otherwise, and that is the whole of what I have.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said nothing at all.

He stood at that gate for about half an hour while a woman read out nineteen columns of her own keeping out loud to about nine people and a carter and a man from the district who carries paper, and he did not say one word, and the reason he did not say one word is on the page and it is not modesty. **There is a page four days off in a market town with a man's name at the top of it in a hand that is not the man's, and that man's name is the name of nobody at that gate, and the man of about fifty-eight has been the only man in nine counties to say out loud that such a page is not false until somebody shows it false, and he has said it in a room and in a lane and on a bare leaf with no head over it, and standing at that gate on that morning he understood that the woman was about to make the one thing he has never been able to do happen by accident, which is to be argued with.** She was going to be argued with, in the open, about a figure, by about nine people at a gate, and he was not going to be the man who interrupted it to talk about a page.

Then she put a mark in the seventh space, in her own hand, and the gatekeeper watched her do it because a gatekeeper watches, and then she stepped back and looked at it.

A carter who had been waiting with his horse yoked asked the question that about four people at that gate were also thinking and nobody else had said.

“**Does this mean a road can be shut on a count now,**” he said.

“**No,**” the woman of about fifty said, and she said it at once and without any doubt at all. “**A count does not shut anything. A count is the only thing anybody can be argued with. I have given mine because the seventh space was empty and an empty space is a road nobody is carrying, and I would rather be argued with than be left alone on a page. I have said it once, at a gate, about my own road, on a day when a page came round, and that is one time, and it is mine, and if you want it you will have to come and get it and it will cost you four days of carting and I will not be pleasant about it.**”

“**Then it is not a practice,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said, and it is the first thing he had said in half an hour and it was the correct thing and he said it in the voice he uses for the hearing standing.

“**It is not a practice,**” she said, “**and nobody may cite it, and a person who has offered one thing once, for a reason, about her own road, has not started anything that anybody else may continue.**”

Then a woman of about forty-three who keeps a road that is not one of the six was at that gate as well, and she said that she had a thing to say and would say it where the other one had said hers, and nobody at the gate stopped her.

“**My line is the fifth,**” she said. “**I have had the page read at my end twice and I am not going to argue with it. The man whose name is at the top of it is not in my county and I have never met him. My road will stand aside and that is the whole of what I came to say.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge asked her the question about the cost, and he asked it flatly and not as an argument, because it is the thing in front of him.

“**It costs you the road,**” he said.

“**It costs me the road,**” the woman of about forty-three said, “**and it is not nine days, it is the rest of it, and my people knew that before I got here because they walked it with me on Tuesday. So I am not going to be argued out of it here and I am not going to be praised for it either, and if my name goes in that space I want the count taken first.**”

“**Whose count,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, and he said it fast, because he is a man who has spent a month watching one figure get made out of another figure.

“**Mine,**” the woman of about forty-three said. “**Ours. What we keep is the crossings that are open and the weeks the road is shut, and that has never once been a number of days in anybody's mouth and it is not going to be one this morning. I am not asking anybody to look at it. I am asking for it to be taken by the people who walk it, on the ground, before my line is settled, and if it comes out at nothing then my line is nothing, and I will have said so in front of about nine people, and my people will have heard it from me and not off a page.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said that he could not grant it.

“**You can say it,**” she said. “**I am not asking you to grant it. There is no head in nine counties that can grant it and I have known that longer than you have. I am asking the road. The people who walk it are not in this county, and they will be asked by me and not by a page, and if they take it then the count is ours and it is dated by whoever walked it and not by a gate, and if they will not take it then my line stays blank, and a blank is a thing a page cannot read.**”

Nobody wrote it down, and the gatekeeper read nothing out about it, because it was not on the paper and a gatekeeper reads the paper.

Then the woman of about fifty, who had not been asked for anything by anybody, said the only thing she said about it, and she said it to the carter and not to the road.

“**That is not a practice,**” she said, “**and it is not the second half of what I said. A count is the only thing anybody can be argued with, and I said that once about my own road, and this is a woman asking for hers to be taken, and it does not touch mine. And here is the other half, and she is not doing anybody a favour by asking. The road did not agree to be shut. The road agreed to be counted. Those are not the same and the page cannot tell them apart, which is the whole trouble with the page. And if anybody in nine counties writes down this morning that a road agreed to be shut on a count, they will be believed, because that is what a sentence in a hand is for.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote one line on his own sheet with no head over it, and the line said that a road on the fifth line had asked for its own count to be taken by its own people, on the ground and not at a gate, before its line was settled, and he put the day on it, and then he did not show it to anybody, and it went into the satchel and down the lane in his coat, which is the fourth time a figure has gone down that lane in a coat in about six weeks, and the first three were a number of barrows, a note on the back of a copy, and a sheet that said which line came back with what in it.

Nobody at that gate was thanked, and the woman of about forty-three said before she went that she would not be thanked and was not going to stop keeping the count, and that if anybody wanted to say that this morning had become a way of doing things then they could come and stand on her road on a Tuesday and say it to her face, and about four of the about nine people at that gate said afterwards that they would not, and one of them said that he would, and she said that that was all right too.

The page went on round the district that afternoon with a mark in the sixth space and a mark in the second and a mark in the fifth and three spaces with nothing at all in them, and about four of those three are refusals and one of them has not been reached, and there is no way anybody will ever have of telling which. The road was open at the seventh hour and nobody was stood down and nothing rotted and no wages went anywhere, and the gatekeeper read nothing out a second time, and the slate went into the apron and up the made road.
