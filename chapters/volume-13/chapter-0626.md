# Chapter 0626

**Hearthmonth the thirtieth, YR 320, a Monday and not a market day, the third hour at the counter under the weigh-house, a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and nowhere to put a name, and the finding that there was never an arrangement.**

The room over the weigh-house was shut on a Monday and the counter under it was where about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month arrive off the roads, and on a Monday the copyist of about twenty-six had the counter with a scraper and a bundle cord and a roll book and nothing else to do, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet came down the outside stair at the third hour with the satchel and put it on the boards and did not open it.

He had been up since the first hour and he had been to the district and he had been back, and both halves of that had taken about a mile of lane each way and about an hour in a cold room with a clerk.

“**I want to put a name on it,**” he said.

“**Then what is the address?**” he said, and he said it to the copyist and not to the door, and he had said it twice in a district office that morning and been told twice that there was not one.

“**There is not one,**” the copyist said, without looking up.

“**I have not got one,**” he said, “**and I have been to the district about it and I am going to tell you what happened there, because I think it is the most important thing anybody has said in this lane since the ninth day of a month, and it was said by a clerk who does not remember saying it.**”

Nobody asked him to go on and he went on.

“**I said there was a road on the seventh line of a page and that it had come off a set of works and that a person wanted to be told. And the clerk asked me which road, and I described it. And he asked me who keeps it, and I said I did not know, and he asked me what it is called, and I said it has not got a name on anything anybody holds, and he said, then I cannot enter it, and he said it the way you say it when it is a thing about the form and not about you. And I said the page calls it the seventh line. And he said that is not a road, that is a number on a piece of paper, and a number on a piece of paper is not a thing I can send a rider to.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six put the scraper down.

“**He was right,**” she said. “**And I want the whole of this lane to hear that a clerk in a cold room with no reason to help anybody was right, and that the reason he was right is a standing of this room that I have kept for four years and a half, and the standing is that a road may be described only by its own answer, and the consequence of the standing, which I have never said out loud in four and a half years and which I am saying now, is that there is no address.**”

“**Say that again,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said, coming down the lane, because a man saying a true thing in a lane is a thing he comes down for.

“**There is no address,**” the copyist said. “**Not for her road, and not for that one either, and not for any of the seven. There is no list of keepers in nine counties, because a list of keepers is a list of roads and a list of roads is a description of roads and a road may not be described here until it has answered. That is the correct standing. It is why two contradictory warnings about the same four miles of channel sat in two books for three weeks. It is why the two in three of about nine hundred drying cloths was never counted. It is why nobody has ever been able to say out loud which of the seven is the gap. And it is why a man of about thirty-four with a sheet cannot write a name at the top of a piece of paper about a woman four days of carting away, and he is not going to be able to write one tomorrow, and there is nobody he can go and be angry at.**”

Nobody said she was wrong. The man of about fifty-eight with a badge stood in the lane for a while and then said the thing that has been said to him by other people for about four and a half years and had never had to say himself.

“**Thirty-one years,**” he said. “**I have spent thirty-one years saying to rooms that there is no head in nine counties and that a rule needs one, and I have said it about a great many things and I have never once had to say it about a woman. I am saying it now and I am not going to pretend it is a surprise to me and I am not going to pretend it is a comfort to anybody.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet said the half of it that was his and the half of it that was not, and he said both.

“**I have carried a thing with a name on it or a gate to go to about four and a half years and I have never once had to decide who to write to,**” he said. “**I have had to decide when to be late and I have never once had to be late. And I have been in four counties this month and I have asked about nine keepers and I can put about four of them on a piece of paper, and the other five I could not put anywhere, and I have never once thought about the five before, and I was not thinking about her this morning at seven o'clock when I set out for the district. I was thinking about the sheet.**”

“**Then it is nobody's fault,**” a man near the boards said, and he was about forty and he had a bar and a bucket in the corner of the lane and he had come up the made road four days and had not been in the room on Sunday night and had heard about it from a carter.

“**It is nobody's fault,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet agreed, “**and that is going to be the sentence in about four months when somebody is praised for it, and I would like it written down somewhere with no head over it that the whole of it was nobody's fault and that the woman it happened to has not been told.**”

Then the man of about thirty-one from the road office came down the lane, and he had not been asked to come, and he had a schedule under his arm, and he said before anybody spoke that he was not going to use it and that nobody was to think he had come to use it.

“**I offered on Sunday night to put a line under a heading at the first hour and have it carried,**” he said, “**and I was told not to, and I am not going to, and I want it on record in the lane and not in a room that a road office had the machinery ready at seven in the morning and a man told it not to at two in the morning, and that the two of those happened about nine hours apart and neither of them knew the other was going to happen.**”

“**It was the right call,**” the copyist said.

“**It was the right call and it is going to cost that road everything,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**and I have been in this work about nine years and I have never once been right in a room by accident, and I have never once been in a room where the right thing to do and the thing that saves a road came apart, and I did not know they could, and I am going to go back and write a letter about a schedule with nothing in it.**”

“**Do not put anything at the top of it,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said, from the door. She had come down at the third hour because the counter is hers on a Monday and it always has been, and she had not come into the lane and she did not come into the lane now.

“**I will not,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**and I have been told by two people in two days not to, and the second of them said why, and the reason is that the second one is right.**”

Nobody thanked anybody.

Then Aren Kest came down the lane about a quarter of a mile away at about the fourth hour, on foot, from the made road, with his stick and about a day's food in a bag, and he was going towards the coast four days down, and about four people at the counter saw him and about five did not, and nobody said a word to him about any of it.

He did not come to the counter. He went past the weigh-house at about four hundred paces short of it, on the other side of the lane, and he did not slow down, and that is the whole of what he did, and the reason is on the page and it is not cowardice and it is not care.

**He could have gone four days and stood on a slope and told her himself, and he has been four days away twice in about two months and got there, and if he had gone this time he would have arrived on the fourth day and she would have known on the fourth day instead of in a market, and there is no arrangement in nine counties by which a man four days off is fetched for anything, and there is nobody who would have fetched him, and he is not a keeper of that road and not a man with a badge and not a man anybody in nine counties has to answer to. He went past the counter because if he had stopped he would have had to say the sentence out loud, and he would have said it, and then it would have been a thing a man with a limp said to a woman about her own road on a day when nobody else in nine counties had been told, and the woman of about forty would have had a person to be angry with, and she has spent two months making sure that a road with no figure on it is not a road anybody can do a favour to or a favour against. He did not want to be the person on that road. He wanted the road to have no person on it, and he wanted the sixth line to be settled on the seventh, and both of those are the same wish and he has never said so to anybody and he is not going to.**

At about the fifth hour the counter shut, which is the hour the room shuts on a market morning and is not a rule about counters, and the roll book went upstairs, and the satchel went down the lane, and a page with six ruled spaces stood at three with a mark and three with nothing in them, and two of those three are refusals and one of them has not been reached, and the page cannot tell which is which, and nobody in the lane that Monday knew that any of that had changed at all.

**And the cost of that Monday is a column and it is the copyist's, and four and a half years of correctly kept series and a standing with no address at the end of it are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

nail at thirty-five.** Thirty-seven, twenty-seven, twenty, forty-one, thirty-two, fourteen, thirty-nine, twenty-three and thirty-five is two hundred and sixty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight woman's address in four and a half years are fifteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Monday, the first hour, the third hour, the fourth hour, the fifth hour, a Sunday, the second hour of the night, a month, two months, four years and a half, and about nine hours are eleven figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A copyist of about twenty-six and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-one from a road office and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a man of about forty carrying a bar, a bucket, who has not been in a room on a Sunday night and a man of about forty-seven with a page who was not in the lane either and Aren Kest and a working life, and the tenth of them has not been told anything and is four days of carting from a lane and does not know that anybody in this lane knows she exists.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the sheet went down the lane in a coat and it was not a number.
