# Chapter 0640

**Mudmonth the first, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning and the first of a month, the second hour in the market street and then in a room over a weigh-house, nine pieces of paper in nine hands with no second copy, and a road that is not got back.**

The first of a month came round again and the hearing stood in the street, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said it in the lane at about the second hour in the words he uses, and about nine people stood on the boards and four of them had been there on the first of a month in the winter and five had not.

Then the room over a weigh-house was open, because it is a market morning and the door stays open until the seventh hour, and nobody came up the outside stair to sit down.

That is the first fact of that morning and it was said out loud by the copyist of about twenty-six to about four people at the top of the stair and nobody repeated it after her.

“**Eight days,**” she said. “**Nine days and then eight days, and the room was open this morning at the second hour on the first of a month and nobody came up it but the four of us who keep it. That is not a complaint. I am saying it because it is the shape of the thing and I have watched nine counties keep books for four years and a half and I know what the shape of a thing looks like when it stops.**”

Nobody thanked her. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said that she had not expected a crowd and that a crowd would have been worse, because a crowd in a room over a weigh-house on a market morning is nine people who came to look at a thing and not nine people who keep a road, and that there is no arrangement in nine counties by which the second sort turns up, and that this was not going to be fixed by a rule or by a clause or by anybody being angry about it in a lane.

Berta Lomax read the three clauses out on the boards, in the open, to whoever wanted to hear them, and about nine people listened and four of them had a hand with the clauses in it and five did not, and she read all three twice.

A man at the back asked her, after the second reading, whether the rule covered a road that nobody in nine counties could write to.

She said that it did not, and she said it plainly and at once and did not look at anybody while she said it.

“**It covers roads that can be written to,**” she said. “**That is what it says. It says a notice at the end of a road and a witness named by the people who work it and a man who can be found afterwards. If there is nobody at the end of the road and nobody working it and no man to be found, then there is nothing to comply with, and nothing that has not been complied with, and the rule has no opinion.**”

Nobody argued with her and nobody thanked her and about four people in that market said nothing and about five went on to their business.

Then a man at the boards asked the copyist of about twenty-six one question out loud in a market street in front of about nine people, and she heard it and she answered it, and she said that she did not know, and she said it in the plainest voice she has, and about four people heard her and about five did not.

“**I do not know,**” she said. “**And nobody in this market knows, and that is not a way of putting it, it is the answer. Not whether it is shut and not whether it is open. I have kept the three series correctly for four years and a half and not one line in any of them carries a word about that road, and I made three of them and I have never once seen anything come back that would let me put a line in about a road that nobody has asked.**”

“**Is the road shut?**” the man said, and the copyist had not asked him to keep his voice down and he did not.

“**Write one about what?**” she said, and she did not raise her voice and she did not soften it and she looked at him until he had understood that she was not asking.

The badge-man did not try to make it better and said why in one sentence.

“**It is the correct answer and it is the worst sentence anybody has said in this lane in about a month,**” he said, “**and I am not going to stand here and improve on it, because a man who improves on it in a market is a man who has decided that the rule is bigger than the thing it was for.**”

Then the woman of about fifty, who keeps a road nineteen years, said the last thing anybody said that morning, and she said it to the boards and not to the room.

“**There was a letter in this room on the tenth of a month from a road that will not be sending anybody, and it said that in about four and a half years nobody had ever asked it a question it could answer, and that the first number anybody says out loud about a place is the number that place has for the rest of its life. And nine people spent nine days arguing about what may be done to roads and they wrote three clauses and every one of the three clauses is an answer to that letter. That is not nothing. I want that said. And I want the other thing said in the same breath, which is that one road went quiet in the middle of those nine days and has not been heard from since, and nobody in this market knows which road it is, and no clause we wrote will reach it, and we are not going to find out in a month.**”

Nobody thanked her.

“**The man of about fifty-two put his chair back under the table himself on the seventeenth of a month and said he would be back on the ninth of nine days,**” the copyist said. “**He is not here. Nobody sent anything. There is nothing in nine counties that can be sent, and the reason there is nothing is the reason I did not write his road down, and the reason I did not write his road down is that it is the correct standing and it has been the correct standing for four years and a half and it cost a road its address about three years before he came into this lane.**”

“**Then say it plain,**” the man of about forty-seven said, from the boards, where he had been standing for about an hour and had not gone up.

“**Then here it is plain,**” she said. “**A road is here and nobody knows anything about it. Not whether it is shut and not whether it is open. Nine counties and about nine hundred sheets a month and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month and not one of them carries a line about it, because a road may only be described by its own answer, and its own answer has gone quiet, and there is no arrangement by which anybody in this market could go and ask. I did not put that there. I have kept it and I would not take it back and I am not going to be the one who says it is somebody else's.**”

Nobody told her she was wrong and nobody thanked her and the register stood at nine questions and no finding went under any of them and the fourth head of the Crown's form stood ruled and empty and nobody filled it.

Then the badge-man said the thing about the hearing, because it was the first of a month and he says it every month.

“**The hearing stands,**” he said. “**On the first of every month, in the street, and not in the room, and there is no bench in it and there never has been. And anybody who wants to argue with any of this can come up a stair on a market morning, and I would like about nine counties to understand that a rule in nine hands in a market town of about two thousand people is not finished. It is not a book. It is nine people who can be argued with, and in a month there ought to be more than nine of them, and the next one ought to be harder.**”

Nobody thanked him. The man of about forty-seven with a page went down the lane on foot at about the fourth hour with the page in his coat and did not give his page to anybody and did not take anybody's, and the satchel went down the lane in a coat with the man of about thirty-four with a sheet, and neither of them said the word closure once in about four hours.

Aren Kest was at the low end of the counter boards for about an hour with his stick and nobody told him any of it and nobody asked him anything, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came out of the door about twenty minutes after the stair and went past him and neither of them said anything, and that is the whole of what happened between them in a month, and nothing between them is described in any document.

And nothing has come from that road since the middle of the month before, and the volume does not tidy it up, and no chapter after this one is going to say which road it was.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is nobody's, and nine clauses in nine hands with no second copy and eight days of an empty stair and a road that is here and unknown are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sack of coal at 20, a bench at 22, a hearth brush at 28, a lamp at 30, a stool at 31, a water butt at 33, a barrow at 34, a pail at 35, a candle at 37, a bollard of oak at 38, and a handcart at 40.** Twenty, twenty-two, twenty-eight, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-three, thirty-four, thirty-five, thirty-seven, thirty-eight and forty is three hundred and forty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and three hundred and forty-eight pence is seven marks and twelve over**, and a room over a weigh-house with its door open, a deed of thirteen words, three separate series, a register of nine questions with no finding under any of them, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, a press standing open with no date, a page with six ruled spaces with three in them marked, a satchel with a sheet in it that has no head over it, about four hundred paces of lane, a market town of about two thousand people, a page of seven lines, nine pieces of paper with no second copy of anything, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, and nine counties are eighteen figures of distance and of count and middle of the month before, about a month, four and a half years, about three years, thirty-one years, about four and a half months, and in a month are sixteen figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man at the back who asked a question in a market street and got an answer at once and four people on the boards who had a hand with the clauses in it and a man of about fifty-two who keeps a road he will not name and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road nineteen years and Berta Lomax of the fruit valley and five people on the boards who had no hand with the clauses at all and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room and a copyist of about twenty-six and about nine people who came up the stair and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about forty-seven with a page and Aren Kest are fourteen figures of persons, and the eighth of them would not write a name down and the sixth of them read three clauses out twice and the fourth of them is the one thing in nine counties that this rule cannot reach.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and nothing went into the register.
