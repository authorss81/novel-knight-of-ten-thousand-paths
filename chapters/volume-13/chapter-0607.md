# Chapter 0607

**Wolfmonth the twenty-third, YR 320, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, a man of about forty-seven with a page of seven lines, six of them marked, and the sixth true thing anybody has said about the seven.**

The man came up the lane on foot with a page folded in four in the inside of his coat and he came at the second hour on a market morning because the room is open at the second hour on market mornings and he had asked a man at the weigh-house board the week before which days the room was shut and had been told Fridays and no others, and had believed the answer, and had come on a Thursday.

He gave his age as forty-seven when somebody asked and gave no trade, and he said he had come about the seven and not about anything else, and he said the word *seven* before anybody in that room had said it out loud since a market morning a week before, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet looked up at him when he said it.

The page had seven lines on it. Six of the six had a mark against them. The seventh had nothing against it at all, and there was no line through it either, and the man of about forty-seven who brought it said, before anybody asked, that the seventh had not been struck out, because a struck-out line can be read as an opinion and a line with nothing against it is a line that has not been weighed.

“**Garvin Slade,**” he said, when he was asked, and the copyist of about twenty-six wrote it on a bare leaf with no head over it, and nobody in the room had ever heard the name and nobody asked where he had come from, because that is not a question a room asks a man who has put a page on a table.

Then he told them why, and he told it in the flat voice of a man who has done an addition twice and is not arguing about the method.

“**Seven roads, and one set of works under an abbey four days off, and the works will take seven and it has taken seven since a year before any of you were in this room, and I do not say that as a threat, I say it because it is the only reason the seven are seven. In the winter of two years ago the fourth of the seven went cold for six days and four of the other six went with it inside forty hours, and in the winter before that the second went cold and three went with it, and both times it was a road on a high flat that nobody was watching, and both times it was a road with a number on it and a name against the number and somebody whose job it was.**”

Nobody in that room said that he was wrong, and nobody was going to, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge asked him the only question that mattered, which was whether he was asking them to shut a road.

“**I am asking six of you to agree to let me shut one,**” Garvin Slade said.

“**That is not the same question,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**It is the same question with the words in the order a person can accept,**” Garvin Slade said, and he said that without any edge, and then he said the thing that made the room go quiet, and he said it to the page and not to anybody.

“**And here is the part I have not been able to improve. I have weighed six of the seven. Four of them weigh more than anything else I have put on a scale in my life and two of them weigh about what a door weighs. The seventh does not weigh anything. It is not a light thing. That is the mistake people make about it. A light thing can be carried by two men. A thing with no weight in it is a gap, and a gap is not carried by anybody, and a gap goes first, and I have never once in my life had to be cruel to close a gap.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote that down. He wrote it down because he writes things down, and he did not ask whether it was the room's business, and about four people in that room thought afterwards that it should not have been in his book and about five thought that it was the truest sentence said in that room in about four and a half years, and the two sets did not overlap and nobody asked which of them anybody was in.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room then did the thing that is her whole trade, which is to ask what a document does not say, and she asked four questions and got three answers.

“**Who is to be shut?**”

“**I do not know,**” Garvin Slade said. “**That is the honest answer and it is why I am here. I have six weights and a gap and I cannot put them in order without a number for the gap.**”

“**Then what is the plan?**”

“**The plan is that six of you put a mark against the seventh and I shut it and the six hold.**”

“**And if the six do not hold?**”

“**Then I have shut one road and lost six,**” Garvin Slade said, “**and I have done that arithmetic in front of nine people and I have not hidden it and I am not going to be talked out of it by anybody who has not weighed six of them.**”

That was the first true thing. The second true thing was said about two hours later and by somebody else.

The second true thing was the copyist's, and she said it standing up from the table with her hand flat on the drawer about eleven inches deep, and she said it to Garvin Slade and not to the man with the badge.

“**I could tell you which one it is,**” she said, “**inside an hour. There is a band of about a hundred and four in a drawer in this room that I have had for four years and I could put my hand on the answer in it, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month come in off the roads and I could put my hand on the other half of it.**”

“**Then do,**” Garvin Slade said.

“**No,**” the copyist said, “**because one of the three is not made out of the other two and that is the rule of this room and I have kept it for four years, and if I put the band and the rolls together to name a road for you then every sheet in this drawer becomes part of one series, and then the first man who comes in here with a page of seven lines and no name at the bottom of it can be answered, and answered properly, and you would be standing in a room where the answer is a fact about a drawer.**”

“**That is exactly what it would be,**” Garvin Slade said.

“**Yes,**” the copyist said, “**and I have not said that it would be a good thing. I have said that it is not a practice.**”

He looked at her for a while and then he put his hand flat on the page, on the seventh line, where there was nothing against it.

“**Then you are telling me that you have six weights and a gap in a drawer and you will not weigh the gap,**” he said.

“**I am telling you that I keep three series and that none of them is made out of another, and that the moment I make one out of another for you I have given nine counties a single number they can all be checked against,**” she said, “**and a single number can be checked by anybody, including a man with a page of seven lines and no name at the bottom of it.**”

Nobody said out loud what the obvious thing was, and the obvious thing was that the room had a drawer with an answer in it and was not allowed to open it, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge looked at the drawer and then at the man of about thirty-four with a sheet and did not say it.

Aren Kest, who had been standing at the back of the room for the whole of it with his stick against the wall, said one thing, and he said it at the seventh hour to about four people and not to the man of about forty-seven.

“**Six of those seven have somebody in them who can be asked,**” he said. “**Four of them I can write to and they will answer, and one of them I cannot write to and I have never been there, and the sixth is the fruit valley, where there is a person standing in the open who can be refused by her own valley and who has said that her valley's right to refuse stays in writing wherever it goes.**”

“**And the seventh,**” Garvin Slade said.

“**The seventh has a woman on it who runs a lime kiln and a boy of about eleven who can say its whole count out loud, and I was on it four days ago, and there is no number for it, and the woman has told me why, and the reason is that she is the one who walks it.**”

“**So the seventh is the gap,**” Garvin Slade said.

“**The seventh is the seventh,**” Aren Kest said, and did not say the other thing, and the other thing was in the room and about four people had it and about five had not.

He asked for six marks. He got none. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said that the room could not mark anything and had never been able to mark anything and had no head on anything to mark it under, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said that the hearing stands and has no bench in it and cannot rule a head filled or empty, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said that he had written the sentence about a gap and would take the page away, and would not leave it on that table in a room with a drawer in it, and would come back on the first of a month.

Garvin Slade put the page inside his coat. He said that he had expected nothing and that he would come back on the first of a month, and he said it to about nine people, and nobody thanked him for the afternoon and nobody thanked him for the arithmetic, and he went down the lane and out past the weigh-house board.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is Garvin Slade's, and a page of seven lines and a fourth of the year and two winters of a road going cold are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a folio at six, a sheet of thin paper at eight, a bundle cord at ten, a printed handbill at twelve, an inkhorn at thirteen, a cake of ink at fifteen, a quill at twenty-one, a taper at thirty-one, a rule at thirty-two, a book cover at thirty-four, and a folding case at thirty-five.** Six, eight, ten, twelve, thirteen, fifteen, twenty-one, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-four and thirty-five is two hundred and seventeen pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and seventeen pence is four marks and twenty-five over**, and seven lines on a page, nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a drawer about eleven inches deep, about four hundred returns, a half leaf face down and about nine households at the top end are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Thursday, the second hour, the fifth hour, the seventh hour, a market morning, about seven months, four and a half years and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about forty-seven who brought a page and Aren Kest and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and about nine people who came up the stair and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a miller four days off are nine figures of persons, and the last of them lost a day of grinding in a fortnight on a report nobody in this room is permitted to set beside another report and the column does not set it beside anything either.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the page went back inside a coat.
