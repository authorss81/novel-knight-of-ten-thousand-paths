# Chapter 0611

**Hearthmonth the first, YR 320, a Sunday and not a market day, the first hour and the second hour at the counter under the weigh-house, the first of a month, a hearing that stands in the street, and a page of seven lines come back with six ruled spaces on it.**

The room over the weigh-house is shut on a Sunday and the counter under it is where the parish rolls come in, because about four hundred and nine of them arrive in a month off the roads on whatever day the carts arrive and the carts do not consult a market. The copyist of about twenty-six had the counter from the first hour with the roll book open and a scraper and a bundle cord and nothing else to do, which is a morning she gets about four times a year. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room stood at the far end of it with her hands in her sleeves and did not open the roll book, because the roll book is the copyist's on a morning and the copyist knows it.

The man of about fifty-eight with a chain of office that is a badge came down the lane at the first hour and stopped in the street and said what he says on the first of every month.

“**The hearing stands,**” he said. “**It has no bench in it and it sentences nobody to anything and it is not a court. There is no stair open this morning. About nine of you have heard me say that from this gate and none of you has come up, and I am saying it at a counter so that the counter knows it was said this morning and not last.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody wrote it down, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key was in the lane with the key in her hand because the key is in the lane while the room is open, and the room was shut, so she had brought it in and was holding it, and she did not say anything about that either.

The man of about forty-seven came at the second hour, on foot, the way he came before, and he said before he had said anything else that he did not have to be here on the first of a month and would have come on any first of a month, and that he had walked it because the cart does not run on a Sunday and he does not trust a cart with a page.

The page was not the page.

That was the first thing the copyist said, out loud, to the counter, before she had touched it.

“**That is not the page he left here,**” she said. “**That is a bigger sheet, folded in two, and there is a space ruled against six lines and nothing at all against the seventh, and this has been cut and printed, and it has been printed twice, because I can see where the first one came out wrong on the line about the third of the seven.**”

She unfolded it on the counter with the weights she uses for a roll book, because a roll book is heavy and a printed sheet is not, and the sheet went flat and stayed flat.

Six ruled spaces. Six lines marked. The seventh line bare and not struck out, because a man of about forty-seven named Garvin Slade had said so out loud in that lane a month ago and had been right, that a struck-out line can be read as an opinion.

“**Read it,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

He said what was on it, and it was nine sentences, and eight of them were sentences he had said in that room before, and one of them was new, and the new one was not about the water.

“**Six of you put a mark in the space against your own line,**” he said, “**and by putting it in the space you are not shutting anything. You are telling me that you will not stand in the way of my shutting the seventh. I want that understood before anybody puts anything anywhere, because a man with a sheet has written down in a book somewhere that a mark on this page is a vote, and it is not a vote, and if six of you put a mark in six spaces the seventh goes quiet for a season and the other six hold. That is the whole of what I am asking and it is more than I have ever asked anybody for.**”

Nobody said that it was a small thing to ask, and it was not a small thing. It is one thing for a man to shut a road and it is a different thing for six people to agree that he may, and the difference between those two is the whole of what anybody in nine counties has ever been able to do about a road, and about four of the people at that counter understood it and about five did not, and the two sets did not overlap.

Then the man of about fifty-eight with a badge asked the question that decides everything, and he asked it in the street, and he asked it once.

“**Does the page name anybody.**”

“**It does not name a sender,**” the man of about forty-seven said. “**It names seven places and it has one of me at the bottom, in my own hand, and the bottom of it is the only part of it I have ever written. I will show you.**”

He did show it, and there was a name at the bottom in a hand that belonged to a man of about forty-seven who gave his age when he was asked and gave no trade, and the woman of about twenty-nine read the name and the man of about fifty-eight read the name and the copyist read the name, and the name was the only thing on the sheet that anybody could put a finger on.

“**Then I cannot show it false,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, to the street and to about nine people, “**for the reason I gave in a room a month ago and have put on a bare leaf, and the reason does not improve because the paper is better. A page that names six places and one man and cannot be shown false is going round nine counties, and every county is going to do what this county did, which was carry it.**”

“**It is going to be carried by you,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, from the end of the counter, flatly. He had not been in the lane and nobody had sent for him, and he came up because a sheet going round nine counties is a thing a keeper of sheets would want to see once.

“**It is going to be carried by me,**” the man of about forty-seven said, “**on a cart, on a district post, on a walking man, and I am not going to leave it lying on this counter, and I am not going to leave it upstairs in that room either, and I am telling you both that so that neither of you has to ask me.**”

Then the copyist said the thing that made the morning turn over, and she said it standing up, and she said it to the page and not to the man.

“**That is not a form,**” she said. “**A form has a head over it, and the head of a form says what the thing is for, and this one says who sent it, and a man's name at the top of a thing is a direction. Not one of the three that come through this counter in a month is a page with six spaces in it. This is the first thing that has come into this lane in four and a half years that I cannot tell you what it is, and I am not being dramatic about it. I genuinely do not know.**”

“**Then tell us anyway,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**It is a page that wants six marks and has no head,**” the copyist said, “**and it is going round nine counties, and a mark in a space is a man saying he will not stand in the way, and nobody can be shown to have done a dishonest thing by putting one in, and nobody can be shown to have done a dishonest thing by leaving it empty, and that is the part I cannot get past this morning and I have been sitting with it since he opened it up.**”

The man of about forty-seven then asked the only question on the page that anybody in nine counties could have answered, and he asked it without any edge at all, which was the worst way to ask it.

“**The seventh space is empty because there is nothing to put in it,**” he said. “**I am not asking you for a name. A name I have. I am asking whether anybody in this lane can tell me what the seventh road is, in a figure, so that a mark against it can be a mark against a thing instead of a mark against a hole.**”

Nobody answered him for a while, and about four people at that counter knew half the answer and about five knew none of it, and the room that could have answered was upstairs and shut, and the one woman who could have answered anything was four days of carting off on a road not made for about a mile and a half of its length.

Then the woman of about fifty, who keeps a road, said the thing that ended it, from the end of the counter, in a voice nobody had heard in that lane before and which had come four days on a cart.

“**I have kept a road nineteen years,**” she said, “**and there is a figure for mine on a slate in my apron and I have never been asked for it, and I would give it to a man with a page this morning if he were asking me for my own road. He is not.**”

Aren Kest had been at the weigh-house board outside for about half an hour and came in at that, and he said one thing to about four people and not to the man with the page, and he said it standing, and it took him a while.

“**I can name it,**” he said. “**The seventh one is a road about four days of carting from this counter with a lime kiln at the near end and a woman at the top of the slope who has held the other end for about thirty-one years, and it is not made for about a mile and a half of its length, and it is a road on the days when two people turn up at the two ends of it. I have been on it twice. I am not going to give the man a figure for it and I have not got one, and if I had one I would not give it to him, and he can ask me why when he next comes to this counter and I will tell him the same thing again.**”

“**You have got a description,**” the man of about forty-seven said.

“**I have got a description,**” Aren Kest said, “**and a description is not a weight. I have been on it twice and I could not tell you how many people are on it, and neither could the woman who walks it, and that is not carelessness on her part and it is the whole of the reason there is no figure.**”

“**Then you have not helped me,**” the man of about forty-seven said, without any edge, and he put the page back in his coat as he said it.

“**No,**” Aren Kest said. “**And I have told nine people at a counter which road it is, which is about nine more than knew it this morning, and you may write that down and it will not be a figure.**”

Nobody thanked her for that either, and she had not said it to be thanked, and Garvin Slade took it the way a man takes a thing a stranger has told him, and the copyist wrote it in the roll book margin with a date against it, and it is the first thing in four and a half years to go into that book that is not a figure of a person, and she did not read it back.

He folded the page in two, put it inside his coat, and said what he said last time, and he said it to about nine people.

“**I have expected nothing and I am going to carry it myself, and I will bring it back to this counter, and I am not going to be able to tell you when, and that is not a fault of yours, it is the shape of the thing.**”

Then the copyist took a bare leaf out of the drawer under the counter, which is not the drawer about eleven inches deep upstairs and holds nothing, and wrote on it: **what a page with six ruled spaces and no head is.** And no answer under it.

“**Put it in the register,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**The register is upstairs and the room is shut,**” the copyist said, “**and I am not opening a room on a Sunday for a question I have not finished asking, and that leaf goes in this drawer and it comes upstairs on the next market morning, and I am telling you where it is going so that none of you has to trust me.**”

**And the cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the copyist's, and a page that wants six marks and a seventh space nobody can fill and a question on a leaf in a drawer are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sheet of foolscap at four, a bundle cord at seven, a sheet of thin paper at eight, a book cover at twelve, a quill at seventeen, an inkhorn at twenty-four, a cake of ink at twenty-seven, a pair of gloves at thirty-two, a roll of linen at thirty-four, and a candle holder at thirty-seven.** Four, seven, eight, twelve, seventeen, twenty-four, twenty-seven, thirty-two, thirty-four and thirty-seven is two hundred and two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and two pence is four marks and ten over**, and nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, about eleven inches of drawer, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, a half leaf face down, a press standing open and four and a half years are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Sunday, the first hour, the second hour, a market morning, the first of a month, about seven months, four and a half years and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about forty-seven who brought a page and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road nineteen years and about nine people at a counter and a man who keeps a coast road who has not written to this lane in four years are nine figures of persons, and the seventh of them asked for a figure once in four and a half years and the ninth of them has a figure in an apron and is not the woman the page is asking about.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the leaf went into the drawer under the counter.
