# Chapter 0613

**Hearthmonth the seventh, YR 320, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, a man of about thirty-one from the road office who has been sent to ask what this room's practice is, and a correct reading of the rule of this room by a man who is not in it.**

The man of about thirty-one came up the outside stair at the second hour with a letter and no coat and the dust of four days of made road on him, and he said his business before he sat down, which is what a clerk does.

“**I have been sent to ask what this room's practice is,**” he said, “**and not to tell you anything, and I have no paper from my office that says I am entitled to an answer. My office has a page going round nine counties with a line on it for a road we cannot find, and we wrote to ask how a room of records holds a thing it has never been told the name of, and the answer to that letter is that there is a room.**”

Nobody thanked him for saying the last four words the way it is said, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said that the room was four and a half years old and had a deed of thirteen words and a number and a door on a lane, and that the door was a door and not a favour, and the man of about thirty-one wrote that down because it is the kind of thing a man from a road office writes down.

Then he asked his questions, and he asked them well, and he asked them in the order of a man who has been taught to ask them.

“**Is the band made out of the outgoing sheets,**” he said.

“**No,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said.

“**Are the rolls checked against the band.**”

“**No.**”

“**Then how is anybody to know that a thing has been said twice?**”

“**They are not,**” the copyist said, “**and that is the practice, and it has been the practice for four years, and I have not altered it and I am not going to be asked to.**”

“**You are not going to be asked,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**you are going to be told. I have written a correct reading of what you have just said and I am going to read it to you before I put it in my letter, because my office will act on it and I would rather you heard it from a person than from a clerk.**”

The room went quiet in a way that rooms do, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge stopped moving, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room put both hands flat on the table, which she does.

“**Say it,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

He said it standing, with the letter in his hand, and he said it slowly, and it is worth setting down whole because it is correct.

“**A rule that keeps three series apart is a rule that says no two returns may be read together. Not this return against that return of the same series. Any two. And a page that wants six marks is a thing that cannot be read any other way, because there is nothing else in nine counties to read it against. There is no register of roads. There is no list with a name at the top of it. The only place in nine counties where a number about a road could be found is a drawer about eleven inches deep, and the three series are the drawer, and the practice is a wall in front of the only door there is, and the wall is doing the work of the man who wrote the page.**”

Nobody said he was wrong.

The copyist of about twenty-six stood with her hand on the drawer about eleven inches deep, and about four of the people in that room went back about four years in their heads, because she had said a version of this herself at a counter in the first week of a month, standing up, in the voice of a woman reading out a practice, and she had said that a wall is what you put a road behind, and that she had been standing with her back to this one for four years and two of the sheets were on it.

What she said then, and she said it without any pleasure in saying it, was the sentence that made the morning cost something.

“**That is a correct reading of the rule and I have had four years to make it and I have not made it,**” she said. “**I made the rule for a different reason and the reason still holds and I am not going to change anything this morning. I am going to say the reason out loud in front of nine people and I am going to say the other thing as well, and then I am going to go on keeping them separately, and if anybody in this room wants to argue with me about it they may, and I am not going to answer them.**”

“**Say the reason,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**A pattern is a thing a district can be shown,**” the copyist said. “**If one bad return is set beside one good return and the two of them make a line, then a district can be shown a line, and a district that has been shown a line will act on it, and the line will be wrong about half of the time, and the road will not care which half. That is why the three are separate. It is not to hide anything. It is so that nobody anywhere can be shown a line made out of two things that happened to be true in the same month.**”

“**And the other thing,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

“**The other thing is that you are right,**” the copyist said, “**and that I have known for a month and have not said it to this room, and that two sheets about the same four miles of channel sat in two books for three weeks and one of them is a lie, and that I did not know which, and that nobody in this room is permitted to put them side by side, and that I laid them side by side once, on a counter, on a Monday, and then put them back. And there is no second practice in this room to put in the place of the one I have just said is a blind spot. I have looked. I have been looking for a month. There is not one, and that is a thing nine people now know and I would rather nine people knew it than one.**”

The man of about thirty-one wrote all of it down, or a version of it, and he did not soften the version, and he said that he was not going to argue with her about it in a room because he was not the person who made the practice and had no standing at all.

“**You have standing,**” the copyist said. “**You have a letter.**”

“**I have a letter asking a question,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**and the question has been answered and the answer is worse for my office than an empty drawer would have been, and I am going to write that down in the plainest words I have.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge asked him the only question that mattered about a thing like that.

“**Are you going to fill the fourth head.**”

“**I have been instructed to send a form,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**and I am going to send it, and you are not going to fill the fourth head, and I have been in this work for about nine years and I have never once seen anybody fill a fourth head and I am not going to start by writing a letter that assumes you have.**”

“**Nobody is going to fill it,**” the copyist said, “**and nobody may rule a fifth, and I have said that on a bare leaf with nothing over it and the leaf is in that drawer.**”

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet opened the satchel, because the page was in the satchel, and the page had been out in the counties for two more days since it came back the first time, and two more spaces had come back empty.

“**Two more,**” he said, “**and that is three out of six now, and none of them has anything in it, and I want somebody in this room to tell me what I have.**”

“**You have three refusals,**” the man of about thirty-one said at once, because he was a man from a road office and it is what a road office does with an empty space.

“**I have three blanks,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and I cannot tell the difference, and neither can you, and that is the point and you have just walked into it. A man who read that page and said no comes back as a blank. A page that has not reached a man comes back as a blank. In nine days those will be the same blank, and there is nobody in nine counties who will be able to say which of the three of those roads a keeper has stood on and which of them nobody has had time to think about, and by the second time round there will be six blanks and one mark, and one mark will be the whole of what nine counties have to say about seven roads.**”

The room did not answer that, and nobody could have, and the copyist of about twenty-six wrote one thing on a bare leaf and read none of it back, and the leaf said that a page with six ruled spaces records agreement by default and that this is not the same as agreement, and that the difference is going to be impossible to show anybody in about nine days.

Aren Kest was four days off on a made road with his stick, going towards a culvert at the gate of this town that a man of about fifty who lays stone had said he would look at after the frost, and the frost had not been after yet, and the man of about thirty-one from the road office said so out loud at the fourth hour for no reason anybody could give, which was that he had been four days on a made road as well and had noticed the same gate.

At the seventh hour the room shut, and the key went down the outside stair, and the man of about thirty-one from the road office went down it with the letter in his coat, and the page went back into the satchel with three blanks in it and one mark, and nobody knew which was which, and that was the position at the seventh hour on the seventh of a month and it did not improve before the seventh hour again.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the copyist's, and four years of correctly kept series and a blind spot nobody has been able to replace are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a sheet of thin paper at nine, a book cover at thirteen, a quill at nineteen, a printed handbill at twenty-two, a bundle cord at twenty-three, an inkhorn at thirty-one, a cake of ink at thirty-four, a seal at thirty-eight, and a waxed string at forty.** Nine, thirteen, nineteen, twenty-two, twenty-three, thirty-one, thirty-four, thirty-eight and forty is two hundred and twenty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and twenty-nine pence is four marks and thirty-seven over**, and nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, about eleven inches of drawer, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, about four miles of channel, six ruled spaces and three series are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Saturday, the second hour, the fourth hour, the seventh hour, a market morning, four years, about seven months and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about thirty-one from the road office and a copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a woman who keeps a channel by hand and a man who keeps a coast road and about nine people who came up the stair and Aren Kest are ten figures of persons, and the first of them came four days on a made road to be told that a rule he did not make is a wall in front of a door, and the third of them has not looked at a fourth head in about seven months and the last of them is four days off the same gate.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the letter went into a coat and the three blanks went into a satchel.
