# Chapter 0602

**Wolfmonth the second, YR 320, a Thursday and a market morning, the fifth hour in a room over a weigh-house, a man of about fifty-eight with a badge, a sheet nobody has covered since Friday, and the two true things he says about it.**

The hearing stood on market mornings at the second hour and was shut at the seventh, and the man of about fifty-eight with a chain of office that is a badge came up the lane on foot and said what he says, in the street where anybody going past could hear it, before he went up the outside stair.

“**The hearing stands,**” he said. “**There is no bench in the room. Nobody is sentenced to anything. Nobody in nine counties has been asked to sign anything and nobody is going to be asked.**”

He had said the last four words on the first of a month and on market mornings and on about nine other occasions that anybody could put a date to, and nobody in that lane had ever thanked him for any of it, and he had stopped expecting it in a way that had begun, about two years back, to look like a habit rather than a judgment.

Upstairs the sheet was on the table where it had been put on Friday, face up, with nothing on top of it and nothing under it. The register was at seven entries and all of them questions. The day-book was at nine. The press stood open with no date. The Crown's form was on the side table with three heads filled and the fourth ruled and empty, and it had been about seven months, and the two objects were about two feet apart and had been about two feet apart since Friday.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge read the sheet twice. He is a slow reader and is not embarrassed about it and does not let anybody hurry him, and about four of the people who come to that room have learned not to try.

Then he put it down and said the first true thing.

“**I am not going to call this false,**” he said. “**A thing with a name at the top of it is not false because the man in the room thinks it is false. It is false when somebody can show it to be false, and this room has no way of showing anything false, and I have been in thirty-one years of work and I have never been able to show anything false in a room like this one.**”

“**It was not written by him,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

“**Then say who wrote it,**” the man of about fifty-eight said. “**Not to me. To the county. Put a name at the bottom of it and take the name at the top off, and it is an anonymous slander and I can act on it today. Leave the name at the top and no name at the bottom, and it is a direction, and I cannot act on it today, and neither can the county, and by Friday neither can nine counties.**”

Aren Kest was at the back of that room with his stick against the wall and he had understood it at the first word and had not said a word, and about four of the nine people who had come understood it too and about five had not, and the two sets did not overlap and no one asked which of the two anybody was in, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote down that about four of them understood it without being asked and wrote down nothing about the other five.

“**That is the first true thing,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and I am going to say the second one, and I want it written down, and the copyist will do it on a bare leaf because there is nothing else in this room with a head over it that it will go on.**”

He said the second thing standing up, with his hand flat on the edge of the table, and he said it once.

“**A direction that is obeyed because of a name and not because of an office can be written by any person in nine counties with a pen and a bad afternoon, and I have been in this work thirty-one years and I have never once seen that done and I have never once heard of it being done, and I want it on a leaf in this room that I said it, and I want the leaf to say that I am the one who said it and not whoever is holding the pen when this is read out in a year.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six wrote it on a bare leaf with no head over it, in her own hand, and she read none of it back, and she did not look at him while she wrote it and he did not look at her.

A man near the door, who was about forty and had come about a sum of money that was not the hearing's, said that it was a good thing to have somebody stand in a room and say a thing out loud. The man of about fifty-eight with a badge did not turn round and did not answer him, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not write it down either, and the man near the door said that was fair enough and went back to his sum of money.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet was then asked the only question anybody in that room could usefully have asked, which was whether the room could check the road.

“**Two ends,**” he said. “**The ferry stone at the low end and the ninth milestone at the top. Between them about a league and a bit of made road and a ford that is dry in most summers and wet in the bad ones. It is kept by a woman of about fifty who has kept it nineteen years and has never sent this room anything, because nobody has ever asked her for anything, and she keeps a list of about nine households at the top end and about nine at the bottom and no figure at all for what is between the two, and that is not carelessness, that is a woman who was asked for two lists and gave two lists.**”

“**Then it is the one road in nine counties this room cannot walk,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

“**It is the one road in nine counties this room cannot walk,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**And it is on a page with a name at the top of it, and a name at the top is the whole of what makes it a direction, and I said that on Friday and I am saying it again because nothing has happened since Friday except that a man with a badge has said two true things.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge went down the outside stair at the sixth hour and said in the lane that the hearing stands, which he had already said once that morning, and nobody thanked him for saying it twice.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet was asked at the seventh hour to copy the sheet out, in his own hand, for the district keeper, because a district that has a direction sitting in a room in a market town has to be told what it is, and a district that is not told has to find out from the road.

He asked the one question that was worth asking, which is the one nobody asks.

“**Am I to send it?**” he said.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge was already in the lane and came back up four steps to answer it and did not go all the way up.

“**That is not my office,**” he said. “**And that is exactly why it will be done.**”

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man with the sheet's, and a direction that is true about itself and false about the man at the top of it is not in it and has no price.**

**a sheet of thin paper at eight, a sealing wax at eleven, a cake of ink at twelve, a quill at thirteen, a pen at nineteen, a bundle cord at twenty-one, a sheet of foolscap at twenty-two, an inkhorn at twenty-five, a knife at thirty-two, a taper at thirty-four, and a printed handbill at thirty-five.** Eight, eleven, twelve, thirteen, nineteen, twenty-one, twenty-two, twenty-five, thirty-two, thirty-four and thirty-five is two hundred and thirty-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and thirty-two pence is four marks and forty over**, and two ends of a road, about a hundred paces of a district, nine counties, a ford dry in most summers, a ninth milestone, a ferry stone, about four hundred returns, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, a fourth head ruled and empty, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries and four hands of writing are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the second hour, the fifth hour, the sixth hour, the seventh hour, a Thursday, a market morning, about seven months and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road nineteen years and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and about nine people who came and about four of the nine and about five of the nine and a man of about forty near the door are ten figures of persons, and the sixth of them has not been asked for anything in nineteen years and the column does not ask her either.** Nobody thanked anybody and the man near the door was not thanked again.
