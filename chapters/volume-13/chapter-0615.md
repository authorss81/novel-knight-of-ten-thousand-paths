# Chapter 0615

**Hearthmonth the twelfth, YR 320, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, a man of about thirty-six who has walked four days with a paper in his coat, and an answer that is correct and is nothing at all.**

The man of about thirty-six came up the outside stair at the second hour on a market morning with a paper in his coat that he took out and put on the table and did not open, and he said that he had come about the seventh line and not about anything else, and that he was four days from a town on a made road and had come on foot because a cart would have cost him more than the walk.

The hearing stood. The register was on the table at seven entries, all of them questions. The day-book was open at nine. The press stood with its mouth open. The drawer about eleven inches deep held about four hundred returns. The Crown's form was on the side table with three heads filled and the fourth ruled and empty, and it was on the side table in a room with the door open, which it was about twice a month and never on any purpose.

“**Say what you want,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said, which is what she says to everybody.

“**I want a figure,**” the man of about thirty-six said. “**For the road on the seventh line. I have read in a town four days off what that page is doing and I have read that a road with a figure on it is a weight and a road with nothing on it is a gap, and I have understood that, and I have not been able to do anything about it for about nine days, and I would like a number so that I could do something.**”

Nobody in that room said the sentence back to him and nobody asked him where he had read it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote down that he had come four days on foot and did not write down the rest.

Then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said the whole of her answer, and she said it standing up, and she said it once, and about nine people heard it and about four of them understood all of it before the end.

“**Then I am going to tell you three things and you may write them down and I will not repeat any of them,**” she said. “**First. The figure is not this room's to give and it is not this room's to hold. I have a drawer about eleven inches deep in that corner with about four hundred returns in it and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month come in off the roads, and I could not put a figure for that road in it this morning if I sat down and tried until the room shut, and that is not modesty and it is not tidiness. There is no figure in here. There is not a figure in here for most of the roads people ask me about.**”

“**Then it is in one of the other two,**” the man of about thirty-six said, “**and you do not put one beside the other.**”

“**No,**” she said, “**and that is the second thing. A road may be described only by its own answer. That has been the standing of this room for four and a half years and I have never had cause to put it on a bare leaf until now, and I am putting it on one this morning, and I am not going to tell you why the standing exists, because the reason is that if this room makes one series out of another for one man with a paper, then every number this room has ever held becomes a number that man is entitled to, and then the room is a shop and not a room.**”

“**And the third,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said, from the table, without looking up. “**The third is hers and not mine and I am not going to say it for her.**”

“**The third is this,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**A figure about that road has to be said out loud by a person who walks it, in the place it is, in front of people who will remember it, and it has to be said by somebody who can be moved off it. There is nobody in this room who can be moved off that road. There is one person who can, and she is four days of carting from here on a stretch that is not made for about a mile and a half of its length, and this room has not asked her for anything, and this room is not going to ask her for anything, and the reason is not politeness. The reason is that the moment a room asks a person for a figure, the room becomes the party that wants one, and every figure that person says afterwards belongs to the room and not to her, and she will be right and the room will be able to use her being right, and that is the whole of what I have to say.**”

The man of about thirty-six stood at the table and did not sit down at any point in the next ten minutes.

“**Then the page goes round twice while I walk four days,**” he said.

“**It does,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**And I am not going to pretend that is an answer to you, because it is not one. It is the correct position and it is not a comfortable one, and a road that a man in a market town four days off cannot put a figure on is going to be shut inside a fortnight, and there is nothing that anybody in this room can do about it, and I have thought about that for about nine days and I have come to the same place four times.**”

“**Then what do I take back,**” the man of about thirty-six said, and he took his paper out of his coat and put it flat on the table beside the other one.

“**That you asked,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**That the room would not give, and that the room was right, and that the room has a standing of four and a half years that you are welcome to look at in about four hundred returns in about eleven inches of drawer. Write those three. Do not write a figure and do not write a number of people and do not write a distance, because you have not got one of those either and you are four days on foot and a man four days on foot has about the same knowledge of a road as a man in a market town, which is none.**”

The man of about thirty-six wrote those three things on the paper he had brought, and he wrote them in the plain hand of a man who keeps something, and he did not put a name at the top of it, and when the copyist said that she had noticed that he had not, he said that he had looked at a page with a name at the top of it and had not enjoyed it and was not going to do it himself with a smaller thing.

The copyist of about twenty-six said the sentence that is the standing cost of that morning, and she said it to the drawer and not to the man.

“**I have said before that I could name which of the seven is the gap inside an hour and would not, and I am not going to say it again this morning, and I am not going to defend having said it the first time, and nobody in this room is going to defend me and I would rather nobody did. The three series are correct. They are going on being correct this afternoon and tomorrow and the day after, and the thing that is wrong with this room is not a practice, it is that there is no second practice, and I have had a month to find one and I have not found one.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said the part that made it a fact rather than an opinion.

“**There is no form in nine counties for asking a person for a figure,**” he said, “**and there is no form in nine counties for refusing to give one, and the only place either of those has ever been written in the whole of my thirty-one years is a bare leaf with no head over it in a drawer about eleven inches deep, and that is where this is going, and I am not going to stand in a room and pretend that a drawer is a way of doing things.**”

“**It is not a way of doing things,**” the copyist said. “**It is a place to put one thing so that it is not lost. That is a different claim and I would like the room to hear the difference and I am not going to argue with anybody about it.**”

She wrote it on a bare leaf in her own hand, in the woman of about twenty-nine's words and not in her own, and read none of it back, and it went into the drawer about eleven inches deep with about four hundred returns and a leaf with a figure on it and a leaf from the first week of a month with a question on it and a leaf from the first day of a month with another question on it.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key came up at the seventh hour with the key in her hand and said that she had not been asked which roads were open and was not asking anything, and she said it for the second time, and nobody in the room told her she was wrong.

Aren Kest was not in that market town. He was on the made road four days down with his stick, going towards a gate at the low end of a road that had been shut for nine days in a month past, and nobody had sent him, and the woman who keeps that road had not been asked whether he was coming.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the woman of about twenty-nine's, and a correct answer that is nothing at all to a man who has walked four days is not in it and has no price.**

**a sheet of foolscap at nine, a quill at thirteen, an inkhorn at twenty-four, a cake of ink at twenty-eight, a bundle cord at thirty, a book cover at thirty-one, a seal at thirty-four, a roll of linen at thirty-five, and a sheet of thin paper at thirty-nine.** Nine, thirteen, twenty-four, twenty-eight, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-four, thirty-five and thirty-nine is two hundred and forty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and forty-three pence is five marks and three over**, and nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, about eleven inches of drawer, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, a half leaf face down, a press standing open and four and a half years are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Thursday, the second hour, the fourth hour, the seventh hour, a market morning, four days, about seven months and about nine days are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about thirty-six who walked four days on foot and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and about nine people who came up the stair and a man of about forty-seven with a page who is not in this county and a woman of about forty who runs a lime kiln and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road are ten figures of persons, and the second of them has been asked for a figure about a road she cannot be moved off and the ninth of them has not been asked for a figure about her own road in about nine days.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the leaf went into the drawer.
