# Chapter 0643

**Mudmonth the twenty-second, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards, about nine people and about five more standing with nothing at the top of anything, and a man who is asked whether he will be the middle of it and says no.**

The meeting was in the open because there was nowhere else for it to be, and there was no bench in it and no chair at the head of it and nobody had brought a chair, and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge stood at the low end of the boards with his back to the weigh-house wall so that anybody in the street could hear him and not only the people on the platform.

The sun came over the far side of the street at about the third hour and put a bar of light across the stones, and it did that in the middle of what the badge-man was saying, and about four people moved out of it and made a small hole in the crowd and stood in the hole, because a man in the light is a man you can see the face of and the whole street understood that within about ten seconds and made a place for it without anybody deciding to. The carts went round the far end. A dog went through the whole of it and out the other side. Somebody's boy came down the middle of the street selling nothing at all, and went back up it.

About nine people had the three clauses in nine hands, and about five more had come who did not have a clause and had not been at the nine days, and one of the five was a woman of about thirty-four who had walked four days for one sentence.

He let the five speak first, because he had said he would, and the woman of about thirty-four said her sentence and it took about nine words and it was the sentence she had come four days for.

“**I want this meeting to find out which road went quiet in the middle of the nine days, and to say the name of it out loud in this street, and to write the name on something.**”

About nine people said something at once.

The badge-man let them finish and then he said no, and he said it before anybody had made an argument, and he gave the reason he had arrived at over about three weeks and not on the spot.

“**I could put a rider on it inside four days if I had a name to put him on, and I have not got a name, and if I had done it then a road that has been quiet for about three weeks would have had a stranger walking down it inside a week. And that stranger would have been sent out of a market town by a man with a badge who has no head. I have had about three weeks to think about it and that is what I have come to, and I would like whoever wrote the sentence written down that I said it this morning and not that anybody stopped him.**”

Nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him, and the copyist said that she was not going to write the name of that road in anything, ever, and she gave the reason in a working voice and four people heard all of it.

“**If I put the name of a road in a book because a man stood on wet boards and said a word to me, then the only thing anybody in nine counties has about that road is a line. And a line is a thing you can put beside another line, and the next thing anybody does with a line is put a number at the end of it, and then it is on a list, and then it is the cheapest thing on the list. That is not a thing I am going to do about eleven days before the anniversary of the last time anybody did it.**”

The woman of about thirty-four said that she would say the sentence again on the first of every month for as long as there was a market, and nobody asked her to stop and nobody asked her to wait.

Then the man with the page stood up with the page in his hand and did not open it, and said the thing he had come four hours to say.

“**Does it bind anybody who has not got a clause? I have seven lines in nine counties and six of them are marked to be kept and one is not, and I have been carrying that page for about four months and I have not shown it in this street and I am not going to show it in this street. I am not asking the meeting to adopt the page. I am asking that one question. Because if a rule is in nine hands and it binds nine hands, then what I am carrying is in one hand, and a thing in one hand is advice, and I have been right five times and I would rather have a rule that is wrong than no rule.**”

The badge-man said that the three clauses bound nobody at all, because a clause is not an order, and that this was the whole of the argument he had been making for nine days and that he would make it again, and that a man who has been right five times in about four months and who is asking to be bound by a thing he is not carrying is doing the only honest thing anybody in nine counties has done with a page in about a year.

Nobody thanked either of them.

Hester Vane, of the fifth line, said two sentences and both of them were the same sentence she had said twice before.

“**My road's count is my road's and it is not in this. And if any of you use me as a thing that has been done, you are using me and I will know and I will say so in this street.**”

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned waited until the man of about forty-seven had finished, which took about three quarters of an hour, and then she asked the question that she had asked in a room on the fourth of the nine days and that nobody had answered then either.

“**Can a road refuse to name a witness?**”

And then she answered it herself, out loud, in the market street, the way she had answered it before.

“**If a road can refuse then nothing happens. That is what refusing means, it is the only thing it has ever meant, and if nothing happens then the second clause works, and it works because a road was allowed to say no and not because a road was frightened. And there is not one word in either of those clauses about the right to say no. That is the part I would want somebody to read out loud one day in a room with about nine people in it.**”

Four people in that market were still moving and five stopped, and none of the five said anything, and the badge-man did not answer her.

“**No,**” he said. “**Not today, and not on the first morning. A rule that has already been asked whether it might be softened has been softened, and I have watched it happen to about four things in thirty-one years and I am not going to be the fifth. I would rather you found out at the end that you had not thought about it than found out at the beginning that I had thought about it for you. That is the whole of my reason and it is a bad one and I know it is a bad one and I am giving it to you in a street at the second hour because there is nowhere else to give it.**”

Nobody agreed with her and nobody disagreed with her.

Berta Lomax of the fruit valley was at the back of the boards with a sheet of her own that had nothing on it, and she read nothing out that morning, and she said one sentence to the woman of about thirty-six in a low voice that four people heard.

“**If it gets said out loud in a room with about nine people in it, then it is not a favor anybody does you. It is a thing a person can be asked about.**”

Then the badge-man turned round and asked Aren Kest, who had been at the low end of the boards with his stick against the edge of the platform and his good leg on the stones for the whole of the morning and who had said nothing at all, whether he would be the middle of it.

He asked it in those words and not in any other words, and afterwards he said that he had chosen the words, and that there was another question in nine counties that he had not asked that morning on purpose, and that about nine people in the street knew which one it was, and that nobody would ever hear him say it, and that it had not been an accident.

Aren Kest had known the shape of the morning before the sun came over the far side of the street. He had known it the way he knows a load before anybody has put it on him, and he had known it standing at the low end of the boards with his weight on his good leg and his stick against the platform edge, and he had been standing there for about two hours doing the arithmetic of it, and the arithmetic had come out the same way four mornings running.

If he said yes then the questions that had no answers would stop at him. That was all. Every one of them. The question about a road that could not be written to would stop at him, and the question about whether a road had a right to say no would stop at him, and both of those would stop at a man in a street who could not get to either of them, and a question that stops at a man is a road shut with better manners than the last one. He had shut a road with better manners than the last one in about nine minutes in a room with nine people in it, and the road had gone quiet, and the people on it were still on it, and he had not been able to put it down since and had tried twice, once in writing and once by walking about four hundred paces and standing at a wall, and both times it had come back on him by the middle of the afternoon.

There was no part of him that wanted to be the middle of it. He wanted to be at the boards. That was the whole of the wanting, and it was not a large want, and he had never in his life said it out loud and he was not going to start.

Aren Kest said no, and he said it standing up, and he took a while about it.

“**No. I cannot answer for a road I cannot write to, and if I stand in the middle of this then every question that cannot be answered stops at me, and a question that stops at a man is a road that is shut with better manners than the last one. I have done that once. About four months ago I shut a road in about nine minutes in a room with about nine people in it and I have not been able to put it down since, and I am not going to be the middle of a thing about roads, and if that makes me a man who wants the work to be harder on him then that is what I am.**”

Four people in the market said nothing at all and five looked at the ground.

The man of about forty-seven said that it was the worst argument he had heard in about four months for a man not being put in charge of something, and said that he had made better ones himself, and the badge-man said that it was the first argument of that kind anybody had made in nine counties in about thirty-one years and that he was not going to let anybody make a thing of it.

Nobody thanked anybody.

Then the meeting came to the only thing on the ground that was actually to be settled, and it took about four minutes.

The badge-man asked whether anybody wished to write down what the meeting had said, and four people said yes, and then about nine people said no at once, and the copyist gave the reason and it was the reason the three clauses had gone into nine hands in the first place.

“**No paper. Not a sheet, not a leaf, not a line in the book, and not a second copy of anything anybody brought. Nine people each hold a piece and can be asked about it by anybody at any time, including the ninth of us, including me, and I am not going to be the one who holds it. If you write it down it has a head and a place, and a place is where a decision goes, and a decision that lands here has nowhere to be challenged.**”

Nobody argued with her, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet put his sheet away and did not write one word, and the satchel went down the lane in his coat, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key said that she had walked four days to give them something to argue with and that she had got it and that she was going home.

At about the fourth hour the badge-man named the two things that were left, out loud, in the street, where anybody could hear them, and he said that he was naming them because a thing that is left over in a room is a thing that gets forgotten, and because a street is the only place in nine counties where a thing gets said twice without anybody deciding to say it twice.

“**Two things. One, the road none of us can write to, which is what she has been saying for about a month and which I am not going to answer, and I am going to say here in public that I am not going to answer it, so that nobody in this market can say afterwards that the meeting forgot about it. And two, whether a road has a right to say no, and whether that right is dangerous, which is her question, and I am not going to answer that one either yet. Both of them get answered or neither of them does, and about nine people are going to have to think about them out loud in front of other people, and that is the whole of what is left of about nine days of arguing and I would not have it any other way.**”

Nine people were still standing in the market street at about the fifth hour and five of them had been at the nine days and four had not, and the man of about fifty-two was not there and had not been there and nobody had sent for him, and the road he would not name had sent nothing since the middle of the month before, and nobody in that street said a word about either fact.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is nobody's, and about fourteen people standing in a street with no bench in it and a paper nobody wrote and a rider nobody sent and a question nobody answered are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a handbell at 11, a coil of rope at 12, a lantern at 14, a post at 24, a trestle at 25, a board at 30, a peg at 31, a link of chain at 32, a hammer at 33, and a tarpaulin at 35.** Eleven, twelve, fourteen, twenty-four, twenty-five, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-three and thirty-five is two hundred and forty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and forty-seven pence is five marks and seven over.** The crowd went down the lane in ones and twos after that and the bar of sun came off the stones and went up the wall behind the weigh-house, and the boards were still there in the middle of the street with nothing on them but grit, and the man who swept them on market mornings did not come for that one and swept them on the Saturday after instead, which is what a market does with a morning that was not a market morning by nine o'clock.
