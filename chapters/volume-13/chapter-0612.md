# Chapter 0612

**Hearthmonth the fifth, YR 320, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, a page that has been four days in a county and has come back, and a copyist who says out loud that the keeper of the room did not come down.**

The hearing stood at the second hour and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said so in the lane first, which he does, and about nine people came up the outside stair, which is a good number for a room with no bench in it. The register was on the table with seven entries in it, all of them questions, and none of them findings. The day-book was open at nine. The press stood with its mouth open and no date anybody will give it. The drawer about eleven inches deep held about four hundred returns and nobody counted them.

The page came back at about the third hour with the man of about thirty-four with a sheet, who had walked four days for it and had it in a satchel and not in his coat, which is a thing he did and did not explain.

It came back with one space marked.

That was the first fact and about four of the nine understood what it meant and about five did not, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said it out loud so that nobody would have to pretend.

“**One mark, in the space against the waterworks country, put in by a woman who keeps a channel and has kept it by hand for about twenty-two years,**” he said, “**and her hand is in it, and I have seen it on about four hundred and nine rolls a month for four years, and I am telling you who put it there because a mark with a hand in it is the only kind of mark on that page that anybody can check.**”

“**Then she has a figure,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**She has a figure,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**And that is why she could put a mark in a space and the rest of you cannot. A mark in a space is a person saying they will not stand in the way. A person can only say that about a thing they can name in a figure, because a figure is the only way anybody in nine counties has ever been able to be held to anything.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six wrote nothing down, which is what she does, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room was not in the room, and that was the second fact of that morning, and the copyist brought it out at about the fourth hour in front of about nine people, and she brought it out flat, and nobody in that room agreed with her.

“**The keeper of this room did not come down to the lane this morning,**” she said, “**and she has not missed a market morning in four and a half years, and I know that because the key is in the lane and the key is in the lane when she is on the stair, and I have not had the key in the lane in the lane's hand on a market morning since I came here.**”

Nobody said anything for a moment and it was not quiet, because about four of the nine looked at the window and about five looked at the table, and the two sets did not overlap.

“**I am saying it because nobody else is going to,**” the copyist said. “**And I am not asking anybody anything, and I am not saying that anything is wrong, and I am not going to stand here and tell nine people what a person is doing with her own morning. I am saying that a room is standing open with nobody in it who keeps it, and that the room is still working, and that I would like somebody else in this room to have noticed that before I said it.**”

“**A person is not a thing that arrives on time,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, and he said it kindly, and it was the kindest sentence said in that room that morning and it was also, the copyist thought and did not say, the reason.

“**Then say what is missing,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said. “**Not what is wrong with her. What is missing.**”

The copyist stood for a moment and then said it, and she said it as a person and not as a clerk.

“**Nobody is missing,**” she said. “**That is what I have just found out and I have not found it out by looking for it. The register is here and it is at seven and I could go and get it. The day-book is here and it is at nine and I could go and get it. Three series go out of this room on three different days and none of them is made out of another, and I know every one of them the way I know my own hands, and if the keeper of this room were to walk into that lane tomorrow and stay out for a month, this room would go on keeping three series correctly and would not know it had lost anything, because nothing in it depends on her being in it. That is not a fault. I have wanted a room like that since I was about nineteen. And I am telling nine people about it in front of her, which is the part of it I would like somebody to notice.**”

Nobody noticed it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote one line on his sheet and it was not that, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said nothing, and about four of the nine had understood her and about five had not, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key came up at the seventh hour with the key in her hand and said the one thing she had come up to say, which was that she had not been asked and was not asking.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said the other thing of that morning, and he said it to the page and not to anybody, and what he said was a fact about a sheet and not a complaint.

“**Four days,**” he said, “**and one mark, and five spaces that have not been reached, or have been reached and come back empty, and this page cannot tell me which, and neither can anybody else in nine counties, and that is not a small thing about a piece of paper, it is the whole of what a piece of paper is for.**”

“**Say the rest of it,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said, and she was in the room, and she had come up at the seventh hour with the key in her hand and the copyist had not seen her come in, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not stop and did not look round, which was the second kindest thing that happened in that room that morning and which nobody in the room noticed at the time.

“**A man who will not put a mark in a space is a man who has refused,**” he said, “**and the page has no way to hold a refusal, so the refusal comes back as nothing, and nothing in a ruled space is the same thing as a space nobody has got to yet. And by the time the page has been round nine counties twice, the spaces that have come back empty and the spaces that were never reached are going to be the same spaces, and there will be no way anybody will ever have of telling a man who read it and said no from a man who has never seen it, and that is going to be true of a page that shuts a road.**”

“**Then it must not be allowed to go round twice,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, and the copyist said, at the same moment, that the page was not hers and was not the room's and had never been in the room, and neither of those was a correction of the other.

Aren Kest was in the room from the second hour until about the fifth. He had come up because the man with the badge had sent a boy to the inn again and had given the same reason, which was that a man who is asked nothing in a room learns to be a man who is told nothing in a room, and that the reason was the only one there was and was not a good one.

He said one thing and he said it at about the fifth hour to about four people and not to the nine.

“**A page that cannot be disproved is not a page that is wrong,**” he said. “**I have not seen this one and I am not going to say a word about it, because I have a woman four days off whose road it shut for nine days and she has never been asked a question about it and she came here once to say a number and went home again. What I will say is the thing about the blank spaces, because the blank spaces are the whole of it. If a refusal is a blank and a blank is a page nobody has got to yet, then a road that everybody refuses has been shut by a printing press.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge looked at him for a while.

“**Then the page is only dangerous while it is going round,**” he said.

“**It is only dangerous for about nine days,**” Aren Kest said, “**and then it comes back with six spaces and nobody in nine counties can show that the six of them meant anything at all, and after that it is a piece of paper in a coat. That is the whole of its life and it is about nine days long, and the man carrying it does not know it is the ninth day.**”

Then he went down the outside stair at the fifth hour, which is two hours before the room shuts, and stood in the lane with his stick, and nobody thanked him and nobody wrote any of it down, because it was not a figure and the copyist had not finished asking her question.

The leaf came up out of the drawer under the counter in a fold of the roll book margin and went into the drawer about eleven inches deep with the other bare leaves, and the register stayed at seven entries, all of them questions, and none of them a finding, and the day-book stayed at nine.

At the seventh hour the room shut the way it shuts, and the key went back down the outside stair into the lane, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came up the outside stair behind the copyist, who had not seen her come in either, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not stop and did not look round, which was the second kindest thing that happened in that room that morning and which nobody noticed at the time.

Aren Kest was about nine paces off in the lane with his stick and she came down past him and neither of them said anything, and about four people going past heard nothing at all and about five heard two people not saying something, and the two sets did not overlap.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the copyist's, and four and a half years of a room that runs without the woman who keeps it and a question nobody noticed before she said it are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a sheet of thin paper at one, a bundle cord at four, an inkhorn at fourteen, a quill at twenty, a book cover at twenty-four, a cake of ink at twenty-five, a scraper at twenty-seven, a roll book ribbon at twenty-nine, a printed handbill at thirty-three, and a tallow candle at thirty-eight.** One, four, fourteen, twenty, twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-seven, twenty-nine, thirty-three and thirty-eight is two hundred and fifteen pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and fifteen pence is four marks and twenty-three over**, and nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, about eleven inches of drawer, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, a half leaf face down, six ruled spaces and a press standing open are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Thursday, the second hour, the third hour, the fifth hour, the seventh hour, a market morning, four days and about seven months are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and Aren Kest and a woman who keeps a channel by hand for about twenty-two years and about nine people who came up the stair and about five of the nine and about four of the nine are ten figures of persons, and the sixth of them was not in the lane and the seventh of them has a road four days off that was shut nine days and the ninth of them has not been asked which roads are open in four and a half years.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the five blank spaces went back into a satchel.
