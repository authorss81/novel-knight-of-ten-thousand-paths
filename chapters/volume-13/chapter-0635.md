# Chapter 0635

**Thawmonth the seventeenth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the third hour in a room over a weigh-house, a man of about thirty-one who wants a clause in that will cost him the most, and a man of about fifty-two who goes home.**

The burden clause was the shortest of the three and it took the third day of the nine to get because everybody wanted it and nobody would pay for it, and Aren Kest was at the low end of the boards outside for the whole of it with his stick and had not come up that stair since the third of a month and nobody sent for him, and the man of about thirty-one from a road office paid for it out loud in about nine sentences before anybody had finished asking for it.

“**My office proposes closures,**” he said. “**Not often. Two in a season and one of them was last spring. But it is my office and it will be my office in about four months, and the burden clause says the burden of proof sits with the man who proposes. That is me. That is my clerk, my wagon, my wage, and about four men in my county who would have to walk to about nine ends of roads in a season to stand on them and be found. There is no money for it and I am not asking for any and I would like it written down here today that I did not ask.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said that it was the correct position and that thirty-one years had taught him that the offices which object most loudly to a burden are the offices that most need it, and that the objection had been made four times in his life and had been correct four times.

Then he said the other thing, and he said it before anybody else had got to it, which is what he does.

“**I am going to say the whole of it in about four sentences and then I am going to let it stand. This clause would not have stopped what happened on the night of the twenty-ninth of a month. Not one man in that room would have been caught by it, because he would have said the word and then he would have gone out into a lane about four hundred paces long at about the third hour of the night and nobody would have stopped him and nobody would have thought to ask. It is written for the next one and not for the last one, and if I sit here for nine days and let anybody believe it is written for the last one then I have sold about nine people a thing that does not exist.**”

Nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and he had not asked to be thanked.

“**I want it,**” the man of about thirty-one from a road office said. “**That is the difficulty and it is why I am here. I want it and I can tell you exactly how it will be obeyed, and I would like somebody to write down how I am going to be able to tell.**”

He said it in a working voice, standing at the end of the table the way a clerk stands.

“**A man in my office has a schedule. He has a column. He has a rider and a clerk who does not know her name and will have to find it. If the burden sits with him then to shut a road he must produce a notice in that road's words at that road's end, and a witness named by the people who work that road, and he must produce them before the shut counts. And he will produce them. He will write to the keeper and the keeper is the witness and the notice is read to the keeper by a gatekeeper, and every box is full, and the whole of the burden has been discharged on paper by a man who did not go.**”

“**That is four sentences and every one of them is right,**” the man of about forty-seven said.

“**And I cannot be got at for it,**” the man of about thirty-one from a road office said. “**There is no head over the clause. Nobody can make him go. So you have written a thing that will be satisfied by a letter to a keeper, and the letter will satisfy it in about six days, and the road will go off in about the same six days, and it will be lawful, and I will have signed nothing.**”

Nobody thanked him. The copyist of about twenty-six said that he had just described the whole of the last four and a half years in a room and had described it better than the room had ever described it, and that this was the second time in about a month that a man had walked into that room and told it something true about itself, and that in both cases the man had come to ask for something.

He then described the rider, because the badge-man asked him to and because he said he would rather have it written down while it was in his mouth.

“**The rider is a man of about twenty with a good horse and a bad coat and he does not know the road and he is given a district and not a name. The clerk at the far end is a woman of about forty and she does not know her own name is on the book and she has to find it in a list of about nine counties before she can send anything at all, and that takes a day. So a letter to a road is about two days there and about two days back and about a day in a book, and I have watched it be right in about four cases out of five and I have watched it be wrong in the fifth in a way nobody found out about, and the fifth is the one that matters.**”

“**You want the notice in the road's own words,**” the badge-man said, “**and the rider takes it in a bag with about nine other things in it.**”

“**The rider takes it in a bag,**” the man of about thirty-one from a road office agreed, “**and I have been the man who writes the line on the top of the bag and I have never once written the name of the road underneath it. I have always thought that was a thing about the bag. It is not a thing about the bag. It is a thing about nine counties and about what happens when somebody writes the name of a road on a piece of paper for a bag.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody agreed with him and he sat down.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said the thing that turned the clause round, and he said it from the doorway with his satchel in his hand because he was leaving.

“**Then the burden cannot sit with an office,**” he said. “**It has to sit with a man, and it has to be a man who can be named afterwards. So it is not the road office that carries the burden. It is the person who stands up in a room and says the word shut, and if he cannot put a name to himself afterwards then the clause does not apply to him at all, and the fact that it does not apply to him is the only part of it that will ever bite.**”

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned said that this was the second time in nine days a man had made the rule about a person instead of about a road, and that she did not think either of them had noticed that they had both done it.

The clause as it went out of that room, and this is the whole of it:

The burden of showing that a road may be shut lies with the man who says it may be shut, and if that man cannot be named and found afterwards then nothing has been shown and the road is open.

Berta Lomax read it out to the people at the back and when she had finished she said that it was the only one of the three that a person could keep in their head without writing it down, and that a man who wanted to do a thing and could not remember the sentence would still have to do the walking, and that she thought that was the whole of why it was the right one.

Nobody thanked her.

At about the fourth hour the man of about fifty-two stood up and put his chair back under the table where it had been at the second hour, and said that he had four days of road in front of him and that a man his age who keeps a road cannot leave four days of it to nine people arguing, and that he would be back on the ninth day.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came down to the boards for it and asked him one question, which was whether he wanted the three clauses sent after him, and he said that he would take them if she brought them herself and that he would not take them from a boy.

She said that she would bring them herself, and he said that he would be at the low end of the boards on the ninth day at about the fourth hour, and she said that the room was shut on the ninth day and that they would be standing in the open, and he said that he knew that and that it was the correct arrangement, and that was the whole of what the two of them said to each other in nine days.

“**Which road?**” the man of about thirty-one from a road office asked, out of the habit of an office.

“**No,**” the man of about fifty-two said, and he said it without heat, and the man of about thirty-one apologised and nobody said anything else about it.

He went down the lane at about the pace of a man with four days of road in him. The copyist of about twenty-six did not write down his name or his road or the fact that he had gone, and she was asked about that afterward by three separate people in about a month and gave the same answer to each of them, and the answer was that she would not put a name in a book because a man had said a word in a lane, and that if she did, then the only thing anybody would have about him was a line.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked.
