# Chapter 0624

**Hearthmonth the twenty-eighth, YR 320, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour at the counter under the weigh-house, a woman of about forty who has come four days of carting with a load of lime and has not been asked anything, and a question asked a second time.**

She came up the lane behind a cart of quicklime with two of her own hands on the shafts and nobody sent her, and the carting was four days each way and she had a sale for it and that was the whole of her reason and she gave the whole of her reason to about four people at the counter before anybody asked her a single thing about it.

The counter under the weigh-house is where about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month come in off the roads, and on a market morning it is also where about nine people stand, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room was upstairs in the room and did not come down, and nobody said why and nobody asked, and the copyist of about twenty-six was at the table and had said once already in a month that she was not going to say a thing a third time.

Nell Craddock put her hand on the cart and looked at the board outside the weigh-house, which has the weights on it and nothing else anybody reads, and then she said the thing she had come up to say, and she said it to the lane and not to a person.

“**There is a page going round nine counties,**” she said, “**and I have had it read at my end twice by a man with a sheet, and I am not going to say that I have read it because I have not had it in my hands, and I am not going to say I do not know what it says because I do know what it says, everybody knows what it says. What I want to know is whether the seventh line is going to be shut.**”

“**Nothing is shut,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said, from the door, which is the answer he gives and gave in a room in the first week of a month with the door open.

“**I did not ask you,**” she said, and she said it without any edge, and the badge-man took it, and about four of the nine people at that counter noticed that a woman had just declined an answer from the only man in nine counties who gives answers.

Then she said the other half of it.

“**I have not been asked anything by anybody in nine counties, and I am not complaining, because I would not answer it. I have said that out loud on a slope and in a market town before and I am not going to say it a third time in a lane, and I am saying it now because a man came past my end on Tuesday and said the third road had water on it and that a road had gone cold at a crossing, and he said it the way a man says a thing when he has been told it and is not the one it is about, and I want to say that I heard it, and that I did not go and look, and that I am telling you I heard it and not that I have come to be told.**”

Aren Kest was in the lane. He had come down the outside stair at the second hour because it was a market morning and he had been in that lane every market morning for about two months, and he was at the low end of the counter boards with his stick against the wall and his hands in the sleeves of a coat he did not need in that weather, and he had said nothing to anybody for a week.

She turned round and saw him and did not startle, which she has never done, and he had last seen her at the bottom of a slope about two months before and had had her name from a woman of about sixty who had carried it up there and put it nowhere.

“**You,**” Nell Craddock said, and she had not said her own name to anybody in this market town in about two years and she said it now because a page had been read at her end twice and a man had come four days and asked her for nothing.

“**I have not asked you anything,**” he said, which was true and was not an answer to anything she had not yet asked.

“**No,**” she said. “**And I have had four days of carting to decide whether I would be glad or sorry if you had, and I have decided I would be sorry, and I have decided that I am going to ask you anyway, because I have come four days and I am not going to go four days back without having asked.**”

“**Then ask,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said, and he said it from the door and he should not have, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet looked up from the counter and did not stop writing and did not write this down.

She put both hands on the cart shafts and looked at him, and it was the second time in about two months and she said it exactly the same way, in a working voice, with the cart still moving a little under her hands.

“**Do you want it?**”

Nobody at that counter said anything, and the market went on behind her, and about four people were within earshot and about five were not, and a woman with a basket went round the outside of the cart the way you go round a cart.

He did not answer.

She waited for about the time it takes a cart to settle, and she did not repeat it, and she was not asking twice in the way people ask twice, and when she spoke again she was not asking him anything at all and that is on the page and it is the reason what he did on Sunday night cost what it cost.

“**Right,**” she said. “**Then I will tell you what I have, because you have not asked and it is mine and I am not giving it to anybody and I am not refusing it to you either, and the two of those are not the same and I have been at pains to keep them apart since a Sunday in the autumn.**”

“**You do not have to,**” he started, and he got as far as the comma.

“**I know I do not,**” she said. “**That is the first time anybody has said that to me about a figure and I am going to remember it and it changes nothing. Nine crossings open and the weeks the road is shut, and that is the whole of it, and the boy has it and the boy is eleven and he will have it until he is a man of about twenty and then it will be in nobody at all. I have not said how long any of the shut weeks run and I am not going to, and it has never once been a number of days in anybody's mouth and it is not going to be this morning, and that is not modesty. Days are a thing you count a road with when somebody is going to be responsible for it, and nobody is, and I would rather it went on being a thing two people say out loud to each other at two ends of a hill.**”

“**You have said it out loud to me in a lane four days from this counter,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, from under the board, and he said it in the voice of a man who is asking a question he already knows the answer to.

“**Yes,**” she said, and she turned round and looked at him, and it was the first time she had looked at him since she came up the lane. “**And that is because you are the only person I have ever met who keeps something with no head over it, and I noticed that about a month ago and I have thought about it a great deal since, and I am telling you it does not make you a person I would give it to, because a man who keeps a sheet in a satchel is a man who will hand it up. It does make you a man who will notice. Those are different and the second one is worth a walk.**”

“**I have not written it down,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**I can see that you have not,**” she said.

The lane went on. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not write down nine crossings open or the weeks the road was shut, and nobody in nine counties had any way of finding out that he had been told, and she had given it in a lane beside a cart of quicklime for a sale she had come to make, and she had not been asked, and she was not refusing.

At about the third hour the man of about forty-seven with a page came through the lane on his way to the weigh-house board and stopped when he saw the cart, and she looked at him for a while and then said one sentence to him, which was that she hoped his road was all right, and he said that it was, and she said that she had heard the third had gone cold and that she was sorry about the third, and he said that the third had a keeper and a number, and she said that she knew, and that was all.

At about the fourth hour the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said the hearing stands in the street, and the hearing has no bench in it and sentences nobody to anything and about nine people heard him say so, and one of them wrote it on the back of a handbill and two of them did not write it down and one of them wrote down something else.

At about the fifth hour Aren Kest went up the lane towards the Crown's road office and then turned off it at the first turning, because the road office shuts at the fourth hour and because there is a hired room over a saddler's in that lane where the inquiry sits when it sits, and it is not the room over the weigh-house and it is not shut on any day of the week, and about four people saw him turn off at that turning and about five did not.

He did not go to be alone. He went because on Sunday night at about the second hour there are going to be about nine people in that room, and there is going to be a question in it that nobody can ask, and he is the only man in nine counties who has seen the bed of the crossing and the bed of a culvert at a gate and the middle of a new sill laid in three courses on a Thursday morning by a man who would not lay a fourth, and nobody had said that out loud at a counter, and about four people had heard it, and it was going to come up in that room on Sunday night whether anybody wanted it to or not.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is his, and a question asked a second time at a counter boards and not answered is not in it and has no price.**

at thirty-one, and a bucket at thirty-seven.** Thirty-five, twenty-four, six, twenty-eight, twenty-two, eighteen, forty, thirty-one and thirty-seven is two hundred and forty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and forty-one pence is five marks and one over**, and four days of carting each way, a cart of quicklime, a counter under a weigh-house, about nine people at a counter, a page with six ruled spaces, three of them with a mark and three of them with nothing in them, nine crossings open, the weeks a road is shut, a boy of about eleven, a sill in three courses, them goes in at either end of this column. **Aren Kest and a woman of about forty who runs a lime kiln and a copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room and a man of about forty-seven with a page and a man of about fifty who the cart went down the lane with quicklime in it and nothing went down the lane in a coat.
