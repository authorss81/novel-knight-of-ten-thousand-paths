# Chapter 0632

**Thawmonth the sixth, YR 321, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the fourth hour at the counter boards under a weigh-house with the room shut behind them, and a man of about forty-seven who is right and knows it.**

The room over a weigh-house was shut and stayed shut, and the reason was said out loud on the boards at about the fourth hour by the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps it, in a working voice, to about nine people in a lane about four hundred paces long.

“**It is shut on every day but a market morning and I will not have it opened for this. You may stand here in the open as long as you like and you may hear one another at the boards and everybody on the market can hear you, and on a Saturday I will put the door open and read out whatever has been written and sent to this room. That is the arrangement and I am not going to improve on it for anybody.**”

The boards were wet and about nine people were on them and it was a Tuesday and there was no market, so the lane was empty except for them and a carter who put a load down at the low end and went away without saying anything.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge had a sheet with the three sentences on the back of it in his own hand and he read them out twice.

“**No closure without local notice in the road's own words. A human witness who can be named and found. The burden of proof on the person who proposes it. That is what nine people said out loud on Saturday and nothing else, and I am going to put the first thing before anybody starts and then I am not going to put it again. Nobody in this lane is going to say what happened on the night of the twenty-ninth of a month. Not one of you. And the reason is not kindness to anybody in a cold room. The reason is that a group which goes back over what it did has already decided what it is for, and a group that has decided what it is for cannot be argued with about anything else, including this.**”

Nobody said what happened on the night of the twenty-ninth of a month.

Garvin Slade took the three sentences apart, and he took them apart politely, and Aren Kest was at the low end of the counter boards for about half an hour of it with his stick and did not come up on to them and was not asked anything by anybody in nine counties on that Tuesday, which is the ordinary condition of him in a lane about four hundred paces long, and he was the hardest person on those boards to argue with because he was right and knew it and had done the arithmetic that other people had been arguing about since the autumn.

“**Notice,**” he said. “**Local notice, in the road's own words, given where the road's own people are. That is a good clause. It is the best of the three and I did not write it and I would not have thought of it. And it is going to cost you the thing you cannot buy back, which is time.**”

“**Say what that means and do not make it sound better than it is,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said.

“**It means that on the day the arithmetic runs again, in about four months, there will be a man in a room who has to shut a road, and the clause will say that before he does it he must go to the end of that road and speak to somebody in words that road uses, and that somebody must then be found again before the shut can be counted as done. And the man in the room will not be cruel. He will be the best man in nine counties. And he will do it about a week later than he should have, because there is no week in this. I have watched the whole of it. Six roads that are marked to be held are being carried by six roads instead of seven and there is not a great deal of slack in that and in about four months the same arithmetic runs again with no gap on it at all and something with a number and a name and a keeper on it has to come off, and the notice clause will be the reason it comes off about nine days late, and the six will feel it.**”

Nobody argued with him.

“**I am right,**” he said, “**and I would rather have a rule that is wrong than no rule, and I want that on the page in about four seconds so that nobody in this lane spends the next nine days believing they have found a man who has finally changed his mind. I have not changed my mind. I have said for about four and a half months that a road with a figure on it is a weight and a road with nothing on it is a gap, and a gap goes first because nobody is carrying it, and everything I have said since has been arithmetic.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said that he agreed with him and that agreeing was the correct position and that the room was not going to be able to argue him out of it in nine days or nine years.

“**Then what is the point of us standing here?**” the man of about fifty-two said.

“**That is the second time somebody has asked me that,**” the badge-man said, “**and the answer is the same both times and I have never liked it. The point is that in about four months one man will have to shut a road on his own word in a room with no head over it, and this time there will be about nine people in nine counties who have already said out loud in the open that a thing like that may not happen without notice and a witness, and he will know it, and it will be in him, and there is no head in nine counties that can make it bind him. It will be worth exactly what a thing that cannot be obeyed is worth. It is not nothing. That is all I have ever been able to get.**”

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said the thing that the room argued with for three of the nine days, and he said it standing at the boards with his satchel against his leg.

“**Then the notice is not for the road,**” he said. “**I have been carrying it about as though it were, because that is the way round it feels. It is not for the road. A road cannot be told anything. A road is a thing that a person is put on and a gatekeeper reads out and a keeper hears about nine days afterwards from somebody buying a basket. The notice is for the man who proposes to shut it, because the man who proposes it is the only person in the whole of this who can be found afterwards, and he is the only one of us who can be made to answer for it. And that is not the burden clause, because the burden clause is about proof and this is about the order of the words.**”

“**Go on,**” he said, and he had not said go on to anybody in about four months.

“**So it does not read *no road is shut without notice*. It reads *no road is shut until the man proposing it has been to the end of it and said it out loud in words that road uses, and has somebody standing on that road at the time who can say afterwards that he said it*. That is a different clause and it is a clause about a man and not about a road, and I cannot make anybody obey it either, and it will be easier to break, because breaking it does not require anybody to be cruel, it only requires a man to be in a hurry.**”

Nobody thanked him for it. He wrote two lines on his own sheet with no head over it and put the sheet back in the satchel.

The man of about fifty-one who keeps a road in the glass country on the high flats asked then the only question anybody asked all morning that was not about a rule, and he asked it of the man of about forty-seven.

“**You said on Saturday that the thing that goes next will have a number and a name and a keeper on it,**” he said. “**Have you got one in your head?**”

“**No,**” he said.

The man of about forty-seven did not answer at once, and the man of about fifty-one filled the silence himself.

“**Then you have got a shape and not a road.**”

“**I have got a shape and not a road,**” the man of about forty-seven said, “**and I am not going to convert it, and if anybody in this lane offers to convert it for me I will go home, because a name in a head is not a name on a page and the moment one of you writes it down the next page has it at the top before it goes out of the room. I have watched what that does. I am not going to be the man who hands nine counties a list.**”

Nobody argued with him about that either, and the woman of about fifty, who keeps a road nineteen years, said the only thing she said that morning, and she said it to the boards and not to anybody.

“**Then on the day it happens,**” she said, “**somebody is going to be asked, and it will not be you, because you have said out loud that you will not name one, and that is the correct thing to have said and it will not save anybody. And when it is asked, the man who is asked will be one of about nine people in this lane and he will have a number against his road and a name against the number and about nineteen years of it behind him, and there is nobody in nine counties who has ever asked any of us what our road is before they do it to it. That is all I have. I am not making it into a rule.**”

The man of about thirty-one from a road office said that the whole of that described his own office and that he had come to say so before anybody worked it out, and that if a clause like that existed then about four men in his county would have to walk to about nine ends of roads in a season to satisfy it, and that there was no money for it and that he was not asking for any.

“**Then we will not have it,**” he said. “**It does not matter. Write it anyway and let them break it. A clause that has been broken is worth more than a clause that has not been tried, because the first one puts a man in front of a road and the second one puts nothing in front of anything.**”

A woman came up the lane from the low end while he was saying it, and she was about forty-three and she had walked four days, and she said her name out loud to the boards before she said anything else, and it was the second time in nine counties.

“**Hester Vane,**” she said. “**Fifth line. And I have come about the witness, and I am going to say one thing and then I am going to stop, and nobody is to write down that my road did anything first, because it did not, and I have said that in a room once and I am saying it in the open this time because a lane is better than a room for it.**”

Nobody wrote it down.
