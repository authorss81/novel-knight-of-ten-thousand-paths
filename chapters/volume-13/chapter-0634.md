# Chapter 0634

**Thawmonth the fifteenth, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, nine days given out loud, and the clause about a person who can be found.**

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge gave them nine days on the fifteenth of a month and gave them the reason for the figure, which nobody had expected and which everybody in the room accepted.

“**Nine because I have been in this work thirty-one years and the longest thing anybody in nine counties has ever agreed to was about nine days and it did not hold, and the shortest thing anybody ever agreed to was half a day and nobody remembers agreeing to it. Nine. And on the ninth whatever you have is what you have, and I am not going to open a tenth, and if you have not got it by the ninth then you did not have it and the only honest thing anybody can do is go home and be embarrassed.**”

“**What was the nine days?**” the man of about fifty-two said.

“**No,**” the badge-man said. “**I am not going to tell that story in a room that is about to write three clauses. It has nothing to do with you and it would make nine people think about the wrong thing for a week. Ask me in a month.**”

Nobody asked him in a month, and that is the only thing that anybody in that room asked for and did not get.

Nobody thanked him. The copyist of about twenty-six wrote the figure nine on the corner of a sheet and did not put it in a book, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it on his own sheet with no head over it and did not make a tally of it either, on the grounds that a count of days was not a measurement of anything anybody had done.

The witness clause took four of the nine days and it took them because Hester Vane would not let it go, and she argued it the way a person argues about a thing that has already cost somebody something once.

“**A witness is not a signature,**” she said. “**I want that said first because everybody in this room is thinking of a signature and a signature is a thing you can write. A witness is a person who was standing on the road at the time. My road's own people took the count of it on the ground and the count is theirs and dated by whoever walked it and not by a gate, and that was once, and I am not asking anybody to cite it and I have said so twice. What I am asking for is that whoever writes a thing down about a road has to put a person against it, and the person has to be one the road's own people know, because a man from a county office is a man a county office sent.**”

“**And that is the whole of the objection to it,**” the man of about forty-seven said. “**Say the rest of it. You have been building to it for four days.**”

“**I have,**” she said. “**A witness who can be named and found is a person, and a person can be put out of the way, and this room cannot protect one, and if it cannot protect one then the clause is a clause that makes a man choose between his road and a man he knows, and every road keeper in nine counties will choose the road, and they will choose it correctly, and they will choose it the first time anybody asks them to.**”

Nobody argued with that either. It had been true for about two days and it was still true on the fourth hour of the fourth day and the room wrote it anyway, which is the thing the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said he had watched happen twice in thirty-one years and had never been able to explain.

The clause as it went out of that room, and there were about four versions of it and the third is the one nine people carried:

No road is shut without a witness standing on that road at the time, and the witness is named by the people who work that road and not by the man proposing to shut it, and if that witness cannot be found afterwards then the road is open.

That last five words were Hester Vane's and the man of about thirty-one from a road office said out loud, in the room, that they were the clause that would cost his office the most and that he wanted them in.

Nobody thanked him for wanting them in.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned then asked the question that took the rest of the afternoon and that nobody in that room answered, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it out in full because he said a question nobody has answered is worth more in a hand than in a memory.

“**Can a road refuse to name a witness?**”

She had come to it from a place that had burned a list, and she said so in one sentence and did not develop it, and what she said was that the men who burned the list in her county did not hate anybody and were not cruel and had a sheet, and that the whole of what was required of them was that a thing should be handed in.

“**If a road can refuse to name a witness,**” she said, “**then nothing happens. That is what refusing means. And if nothing happens, then the clause works, and it works because a road was allowed to say no, and not because a road was frightened. And there is not one word in either of those two clauses about the right to say no and that is the part I would want somebody to read out loud one day in a room with about nine people in it. I am not asking for it today. I am asking whether anybody here thinks it is dangerous.**”

Nobody in that room thought it was dangerous and about four of them thought it was the whole point and nobody said so, and the badge-man said that the question was going to stay open until the last day and that he would not have it answered early, because a rule that had already been asked whether it might be refused was a rule that had already been softened, and he would rather they found out at the end that they had not thought about it.

Then the man of about fifty-two said the thing that tied his own refusal to the clause, and he said it standing up, which he had not done all afternoon.

“**I sat here on Saturday and would not say which road I keep,**” he said, “**and every one of you let me, and about four of you thought I was being clever. I want to say what I was doing. I was holding out against being the witness. If I say a road, I am a man who knows a road, and then any man who wants a witness for that road has only to come and ask me, and I have said no once and I will have to keep saying no, and I will get tired of it, and in about four months somebody will come at me at the wrong hour of the night and ask me where I sleep. I would rather be nobody's witness. That is all I have and it is not a principle.**”

“**It is a clause,**” Hester Vane said. “**That is the whole of what you have done. You have said a road's own person can decline, and about four people in this room did not want to hear it and I did want to hear it, and neither of us is going to be thanked for the next six days.**”

Berta Lomax read the two clauses out loud to the people at the back who could not follow them in a hand, and she read them slowly and she read the word found three times in the first reading and once in the second, and she said at the end that if she had been asked to write it she would have written found and not looked for, and that she did not know which of the two she meant and that neither of them was wrong and that both of them were in her mouth.

Then the room went out into the lane because it was the fourth hour and four of them had a cart to catch, and Aren Kest was one of the nine and he went down the lane with his stick at about the pace of a man going up a bank, four seconds slower than he came down one.

He went as far as the low end of the boards and then he went four hundred paces further down the made road to a crossing that two people had put in three weeks earlier with a sill in three courses and a bar of oak and a length of chain, and it was one of about nine crossings that were open and he had not been asked about it and he was not going to be asked about it.

He stood at the boards on the near side of it at about the seventh hour in the light and he did not go over.

If anybody had asked him about that, and nobody did, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key was the only person within about forty paces of him and she did not ask, he would have said that the crossing had been put in by two people who had counted it on the ground before anybody laid a stone in it, and that there was nothing on the far side of it that needed him, and that both of those were true and neither of them was the reason.

He turned round and went back up the lane at about the pace of a man going up a bank and he did not stop on the way and he did not write anything in the small book he carries and he did not look at his left hand.

the road he keeps and two people who put a crossing in that nobody asked about and four of the nine people who went out into a lane with a cart to catch and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a man of about forty-seven with a page and about nine people who went out into a lane and Berta Lomax of the fruit valley and a man of about of persons, and the tenth of them stood at the boards on the near side of a crossing and did not go over and was not asked and the eleventh of them argued four days for one clause.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and nothing went into the register.
