# Chapter 0617

**Hearthmonth the fifteenth, YR 320, a Sunday and not a market day, the third hour at the counter under the weigh-house, a man of about forty-seven who is right for the second time in about six weeks, and the price of being right.**

The room over the weigh-house is shut on a Sunday and the counter under it is where the parish rolls come in, and about four of the nine people who came up the stair on a market morning were in the market on that Sunday, and the man of about forty-seven with a page was one of the four, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet was one of the four, and nobody had sent for anybody.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge came down at the third hour because a Sunday counter with four men on it and a page in a coat is a thing he comes down for, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key had the key in her hand because the room was shut and she brings it in, and nobody said anything about that.

Garvin Slade put the page on the counter and put his hand flat on it and said that he had been right about the water in a market in the first week of a month and that he was going to be right about a second thing, and that the second thing was smaller and cheaper than the first and would cost somebody their work.

“**A direction that leaves a room on a Friday and is read out at a gate on the Friday has no check in it,**” he said. “**There is nothing between the two. A man reads it at a gate and a road is shut on the same day, and the gatekeeper cannot ask anybody anything, because the man at the top of it is not in the county and nobody in the county can say who he is, and the keeper has not been told and there is nobody to tell her. That is not carelessness. That is arithmetic.**”

“**Then put a day in,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said. “**You have said the answer twice. What is the answer.**”

“**One day,**” the man of about forty-seven said. “**A direction must not be readable at a gate on the day it leaves a room. One day. That is the whole of the fix and it costs about a sheet of paper and it is the cheapest thing anybody in nine counties will be offered this winter.**”

Nobody thanked him and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not move and did not say anything for about a minute, and then he said the thing that made the morning cost something, and he said it to the counter and not to the man of about forty-seven.

“**Then I am the man who has not got a job in the morning,**” he said. “**That is all that happens. Nothing about a road changes. No road is shut, no keeper is stood down, no gatekeeper does anything differently, and the works under the abbey still take seven. What changes is that a sheet does not leave this county on the day it is written. My office is the day. I am paid to carry a direction out of this room on the day it is written and I have been doing it for about four and a half years and I have never once been late, and I am not going to tell anybody in this lane that I would not be, because I do not know that and neither do you.**”

“**You have never been late,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said.

“**I have never been late,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and I have been carrying directions on the day they were written since I was about twenty-six, and there have been four that mattered and I have been thinking about them for about a month, and two of them were right and I have never once been asked whether I minded.**”

He said that last part to nobody and then he said the second part of it, which was the true part, and it was the reason a man of about forty-seven was right for the second time in six weeks and it was not going to be thanked for.

“**A delayed thing has nobody's name on the delay,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said. “**If this page sits over a day, then I am the man who sat on it, and about nine people in four counties will never know I sat on it, and the only person in nine counties anybody could ask about the day is me, and if they did ask I would say yes, and that is the whole of what a day does to a man who carries sheets. It moves the blame off the gate and onto the room, and the room has a door and a number, and I have a face.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge then said the thing he said in a lane in the first week of a month and had put on a bare leaf, except that this time it was about a different kind of nothing.

“**It is a rule,**” he said, “**and a rule needs a head, and this room has no head to rule it on, and the district has no head to rule it on either, and neither has nine counties, and I have been in this work thirty-one years and I have never once had to ask a man to be late because there has never been anything to be late with. A rule that is right and has nowhere to go is worth exactly nothing. I have spent thirty-one years putting things that are worth nothing into rooms. This is the first time I have watched one of them be right, and I am telling nine people at a counter that this is what right looks like when there is no place to put it, and I am not going to pretend it is a comfort.**”

“**I knew that when I said it,**” the man of about forty-seven said. “**I said it in this lane six weeks ago and I said it again and I am going to say it a third time before I am forty-eight, and nobody in nine counties has ever asked me what it costs, and I have begun to think that is not an accident.**”

“**It is not an accident,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**it is thirty-one years.**”

Then Aren Kest said the argument, and he said it in a working voice, to about four people at a counter, with his hands on the boards, and it is the whole of what the block finds and he gave it away in about nine seconds and then nothing became true.

“**A mark in a space is not a vote,**” he said. “**It is a person saying they will not stand in the way, and a person can only say that about a thing they can name in a figure, because a figure is the only thing anybody in nine counties has ever been able to be argued with. So this page is not asking six people whether the seventh road is worth keeping. It is asking six people whether they can be held to anything at all, and it is going to find four blanks and two marks and a whole lot of paper, and in about nine days nobody will be able to tell a man who said no from a page nobody got to, and the road will have been shut by a printing press and it will be shut by a printing press again in about four months.**”

“**Then what would you do,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, and he asked it the way a man asks a question he wants the answer to.

Aren Kest did not answer it.

He had the answer in his hand and it had been in his hand since the ninth day of a month in a room over a weigh-house, when a woman who runs a lime kiln had told him that a figure about a road is a thing a person says out loud in their own place and is wrong in if they are wrong, and he had carried it up a bad mile and a half and down it again twice. **He had it, and he had it in front of about four people at a counter on a Sunday, and he did not use it to make a thing true, and the reason he did not use it is on the page and it is not modesty. A thing said in a lane by a man who can see the load-bearing problem in a place is a direction, and a direction is a page with a name at the top of it, and he has spent six weeks watching what a page with a name at the top of it does to a road four days off. He knows exactly what it does. It shuts nine days. And the only person who can say a number for that road is a woman on a slope with a barrow in her hands who has told him to his face that he is not going to be asked by anybody on anybody's behalf, including his own, and he has not asked her, and he is not going to.**

“**You have gone quiet,**” the man of about forty-seven said, after a while.

“**I have,**” Aren Kest said. “**You have got a page and a day in it and neither of those things needs me, and that is the best morning anybody in this lane has had in a month, and I am going to go and stand at a gate and clear a grating, because there is nine barrows of silt on that street and nobody has taken them.**”

At the seventh hour the counter shut, which is the hour the room shuts and is not a rule about counters, and the four men went four ways, and the page went inside a coat with a man's name at the top of it in a hand that is not the man's, and the satchel went down the lane with a note on the back of a copy, and a delay that is right existed in about four counties in the mouths of about nine people and in no book in nine counties.

**And the cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the man of about thirty-four with a sheet's, and an office that is the day and a delay that is right are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a sheet of foolscap at eleven, a bundle cord at sixteen, an inkhorn at twenty-six, a cake of ink at twenty-seven, a quill at twenty-eight, a book cover at thirty-four, a printed handbill at thirty-nine, a seal at forty, and a sheet of thin paper at forty-one.** Eleven, sixteen, twenty-six, twenty-seven, twenty-eight, thirty-four, thirty-nine, forty and forty-one is two hundred and sixty-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and sixty-two pence is five marks and twenty-two over**, and nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, about eleven inches of drawer, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, a half leaf face down, six ruled spaces and two ends of a road are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Sunday, the third hour, the fifth hour, the seventh hour, nine days, four and a half years, about seven months and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of about forty-seven with a page and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and Aren Kest and a copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road with a slate in her apron and a gatekeeper who has stood at a gate for about eleven years and about nine households at the top end and about nine at the bottom and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room are ten figures of persons, and the second of them has never once been asked whether he minded and the sixth of them has never once been asked which way her count runs and the seventh of them did the only thing he could do with a paper.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and nobody blamed the man of about thirty-four with a sheet either.
