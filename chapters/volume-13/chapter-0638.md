# Chapter 0638

**Thawmonth the twenty-second, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house with the door open, three clauses written out nine times in nine hands, and a woman of about thirty-six who says what the rule does not cover.**

On the eighth of the nine days the room did the thing it had spent four and a half years refusing to do, which was hold a rule, and it did it by not holding it at all, and Aren Kest was on the boards outside the whole of that morning at about forty paces from the low end of the counter and read nothing and heard the name of the hole in the rule said out loud in a market and did not go up the stair.

Nobody wrote a copy. That was settled in about four minutes and it was the copyist of about twenty-six who settled it and who gave the reason.

“**Nine of you are going to write out the three clauses in your own hand and you are going to keep them and there is not going to be a second copy of anything, and I am not going to be the one who holds it. A room with a door on a lane and a number and a deed of thirteen words has just spent four and a half years proving that it cannot hold a decision, and the reason is that a decision that lands here has nowhere to be challenged. A rule is not a decision. A rule that lands here would be a thing that nine people had each got to themselves and that nobody could bring in here and hold up. So it goes in nine hands. And if you want to say I have refused to do my office, come and say it on the boards.**”

Nobody said it on the boards. Nine people wrote three clauses out in nine hands and folded them and put them inside their coats, and four of them read their own back out loud afterwards to check that they had not softened a word, which two of them had.

One of the two had written must and had gone back over it and put may, and said so out loud before anybody else found it, and nobody thanked him for finding it himself, and one man asked him why he had done it at all.

“**Because I am going to be the man who has to live in a county with a keeper in it,**” he said, “**and if I write must then in about four months somebody will come to me with a road and I will have to go and stand at the end of it, and if I write may then in about four months somebody will come to me with a road and I will be able to go home. I have written may. I would like the other eight of you to write must. And I would like it written down that one of the nine said may and said why.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it down and it went down the lane in a coat, and the man who had written may did not ask for it to be left out and nobody left it out.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said what she had said on the first of a month about a drawer, and she said it standing with her hand on the frame of the door, and she said it once.

“**A room can hold a question. A room can hold a thing somebody has refused to sign. It cannot hold a rule either, and the answer to where a rule goes is the same as the answer to where a decision goes. Nine hands. And I want it noticed that the ninth question in that book has been standing unanswered for about a month and that it is still standing there and that nothing that happened in this room this morning answered it, and I am not going to pretend to anybody that it did.**”

The register stood at nine and no finding went under any of them and nothing was written in it that morning.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned said the thing about the rule that the man of about fifty-eight with a badge had told the room would be found at the end and not before, and nobody had to be asked a question to bring it out, and it took her about four sentences.

“**Then here it is and I am not going to be gentle about it because nobody in this room has been gentle about anything for nine days. This rule covers roads that can be written to.**”

“**Say what you mean,**” the badge-man said.

“**I mean that every one of those three clauses requires somebody to be found. The notice requires somebody to be reached at the end of a road. The witness requires somebody to be named by the people who work that road. The burden requires a man who can be named afterwards. And a road that cannot be written to has nobody at the end of it, and nobody working it, and no man in nine counties who could be found. And a rule about finding people is not a rule about roads. It is a rule about roads that have people on them at the end.**”

Nobody argued with her and about four people in that room agreed with her and about five did not and none of them said so.

“**And I will tell you why I am the one saying it,**” she said. “**In my county there was a list of names and it was burned, and I have never in my life been able to work out whether the men who burned it hated anybody. I have gone at it for about twenty years with about four other people and we have never got past the question of whether the sheet did it or the men did. And what I have come to, and I may be wrong, is that they did not hate anybody and it was a sheet. A sheet with good manners on it is still a sheet. So the three clauses you have written are three good manners, and about nine counties will obey them beautifully, and any road nobody can write to will not be touched by any of it, and that is not a small hole. That is the whole of the thing this rule is for.**”

“**Write it down,**” Garvin Slade said, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it down, and it went down the lane in a coat in a satchel in about nine words, and Garvin Slade said afterwards that he wanted it in the world and not in a book and that he was not going to be thanked for asking.

Nobody thanked him.

At about the fourth hour the room went out onto the boards and Berta Lomax read all three clauses out loud in the open for anybody in the market who wanted to hear them, and about nine people stood on the boards and about four of them had heard the clauses before and five had not, and the five heard the second one for the first time.

Then a carter put a load down at the low end of the boards, the way a carter does, and said it to the middle distance in about nine words.

“**Nothing has come from the end of it,**” he said. “**Nothing in, nothing out, not a boy, not a note, not a basket of anything, since the middle of last week. And I have been up and down that lane every day of this week with a load and I have been the only thing going up and down it.**”

Nobody said anything and then about nine people said something at once.

“**Which road?**” the badge-man said, and he said it once, and he did not say it a second time, and he said afterwards that he had not said it a second time on purpose and gave the reason, which was that a man who asks twice is a man who has begun to want an answer.

“**And I have wanted an answer since about the second hour this morning,**” he said, “**and I have not been able to do one single thing with it, and the wanting is the part I have to be careful of and not the asking. I have been careful of it for thirty-one years and I would like somebody in this lane to believe that I have not been careful of it.**”

The woman of about fifty, who keeps a road nineteen years, said one sentence about that and did not expand it.

“**You want to know which road it is because you would like to be able to send somebody,**” she said. “**That is not the badge talking. I have watched a man want that for thirty-one years and I have watched what it does to him.**”

The man of about thirty-one from a road office said that a road office could send a rider to any of seven roads in nine counties inside four days, and the copyist of about twenty-six told him not to, in front of about nine people, with the reason given in a working voice.

“**You do not know which one,**” she said. “**You know seven and you know one that has gone quiet and there is nothing in nine counties that joins those two facts. And there is not going to be one. And I am not saying it to be cold. I am saying it because I am the person who writes things down in this room and if anybody in this lane wants to know what I am going to write down today, it is nine clauses on nine pieces of paper and one carter, and that is all.**”

Nobody thanked her. The nine clauses went into nine coats.
