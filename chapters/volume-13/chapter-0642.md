# Chapter 0642

**Mudmonth the twentieth, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house with the door open, about fourteen people up the stair, and five of them who were not at the nine days.**

The room was open, because it is a market morning and the door stays open until the seventh hour, and about fourteen people had gone up the outside stair, and five of the fourteen had not been in the lane at the nine days.

The copyist of about twenty-six counted them from the doorway and said the number out loud so that everybody in the room would know it, and she said it the way she said everything, in a working voice.

“**Fourteen, and five of them I have not had in this room before, and I am going to say the five out loud so that nobody in here is surprised later by a face. The badge-man. The woman who came four days for a sentence. Two I have never seen. And a woman of about thirty-six who came on the eighth of the nine days and has not been in a room since.**”

Nobody thanked her for counting.

The room held fourteen and it was built for about nine, and the extra five had to stand, and the standing people were at the back with their hands behind them, and the woman who keeps the room did not ask any of them to go out and did not offer anybody a stool.

The badge-man said the shape of the morning out loud before anybody had asked for it, and he said it standing, not sitting, and he said that the meeting was on the Saturday and that this was not the meeting.

“**This is the day people find out whether the Saturday is worth walking to. That is all this morning is. I am going to hear the five of you say what you have come for, and I am going to say it out loud so that the nine who were here do not have to wonder about you at the back. And I am going to say before we start that the terms are the same for all fourteen and that if you are here for a figure you will be told in the open and not in a corner, and I am not going to pretend that is fair, because it is not fair, and it is the only arrangement I know how to make.**”

The woman of about thirty-four who had come four days for a sentence stood up before she was asked, which nobody had expected, and she said it in about nine sentences without stopping.

“**I have a road. It is not a small one and it is not a light one and it has about nine houses on it that I could count in an afternoon if anybody wanted to stand still long enough. I have never said a figure for it out loud to anybody, not once, in about four years, and the reason is that a number said first about a place is the number that place carries afterwards, and nobody gets to choose it afterwards, and the person who says it first is the person who has said it for the rest of its life. And I have come four days to say it in a room where about nine people keep the three series, because if it is not said in this room then it is said in a doorway by a carter in about four months and it is said wrong.**”

About four people in that room said nothing at all, which is not the same as agreeing.

The copyist gave her the reason that a room gives, and gave it in a working voice, and she gave it to the room and not to the woman.

“**About nine hundred sheets go out of this room a month and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come in and I make both and I derive neither out of the other, and none of the three is the same as the other two. A figure that gets into one of them gets into nine counties inside a week and there is no arrangement by which a room un-says a thing. So the question is not whether a figure may be said. The question is who says it first, and the honest answer is that it will not be this room, because this room has never been to the road and I have never met it and I have kept it correctly for about four years and a half by not asking it anything.**”

Nobody thanked her.

The man of about fifty-two's chair was still under the table from the ninth of the nine days and the woman who keeps the room had not once looked at it, and about four people looked at it and nobody said anything about it.

Then a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned stood up at the back and said the thing she had come for, and she said it in the room and not in the lane, and she said that she was going to say it again on the Saturday where about nine people could hear it and that she was not going to be asked to be quiet by anybody.

“**A rule that covers roads that can be written to is not a rule. It is three good manners and any road nobody can write to will not be touched by any of it, and in about twenty years the only thing I have got out of the fact that a sheet was burned in my county is that a sheet with good manners on it is still a sheet. So I am on the side of the road with the bad manners, and there is a hole where you people are standing and the hole is the size of a road and none of you can reach it, and I am not going to help you paper it.**”

About four people agreed with her and about five did not and none of the five said so, and the copyist said out loud that four agreed and five did not, because she said things like that out loud.

Hester Vane, of the fifth line, said four sentences and they were about her own road and not about anybody else's.

“**I said on the ninth of the nine days that the letter from my road is the argument against the rule you are writing, and I am not going to take it back and I am not going to be softened by fourteen people in a room. My road's own count is my road's. I am not asking anybody for it. I have said that twice now in front of people and I am saying it a third time because there are five of you at the back who have not heard it, and if any of you cites it after the Saturday as a thing that has been done before, then you are using me and I will know.**”

Nobody thanked her and about four of the five at the back wrote nothing down.

Berta Lomax of the fruit valley read the three clauses out loud once to the fourteen, and she read them slowly, and she stopped at the end of the second one and put her hand flat on her own sheet and said the thing she had said before about the word at the end of the first one.

“**The word that is doing more work in the first clause than any other word in it is the one about the far end of a road, and I do not know what it means on a road that has a lot of crossings open, and I have not been asked, and I am not asking, and I am telling you that the sentence is in the mouth of a person and not in a dictionary.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote nine words and put it in the satchel, and the satchel went down the lane in his coat at about the fifth hour and went nowhere else.

The man of about thirty-one from a road office put his hand on the table and asked whether the form would be kept, and the copyist said that the press stood open and had stood open for longer than he had been a man, and that anything put under it was in a room with nobody in charge of what came out of it, and that if he wanted his sheet back he could have it now or in about two years and not in between.

He took it back.

Then the badge-man heard the two he had not seen, and they were a man of about forty-four and a boy of about seventeen, and the man had come to ask whether the meeting would be in writing and the boy had come because his master was mending a bridge and could not come and had sent him to sit in the room and hear it, and the badge-man said that the boy could sit and hear it and would not be asked anything, and the boy said, “**Am I going to be asked anything?**” and the badge-man said that he was not, and the boy said that he would rather be, and about four people in the room said nothing at all and about five looked at the floor, and nobody asked him anything.

Then the badge-man said the last thing of the morning and he said it to the five at the back and not to the nine.

“**You came here for things you want and about two of you are here for a thing you want that I have already decided you will not get, and I am telling you that now so that nobody has wasted a day of walking. On the Saturday there is no list. There is no column with your road's name at the top of it. There is no notice with a head on it, because there is no head, and I have not got one and I am not making one. What there is, is nine people and about five more of you, in a street, in the open, and anybody may say a thing and anybody may say the opposite thing about four minutes later and both of them go into nine counties in nine hands. That is what you have come to. If it is less than what you came for, you may say so on the Saturday, and I will hear it first and before anything else, and nobody will be allowed to answer you until everybody has heard you.**”

About four of the five at the back said they would come, and the fifth said that she was going to come and say a thing that nobody in the room would like, and nobody asked her what the thing was and she said that she would say it on the Saturday in the open where it could be heard, which is where things get said in this market and always have been.

Aren Kest was on the boards outside the whole of the morning with his stick against the edge of the platform and came up the outside stair about nine steps, once, and stood in the doorway and did not go past it, and the woman who keeps the room did not look at him and he did not look at her, and about four people in that room noticed and about five did not.

Nobody thanked anybody and nothing went into the register and the standing notice over the stair still said what it had said.
