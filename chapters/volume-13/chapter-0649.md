# Chapter 0649

**Greenmonth the thirteenth, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards, about nine people each agreeing to hold a thing that has not been written, and one woman who has had the words since a Saturday in a lane without being given them.**

The badge-man put the last of the three things to the boards on the last working morning before the end of the month, and he put it in about nine words, and then he stood against the weigh-house wall and let it sit there.

“**Who will hold it.**”

About nine people looked at about nine other people, and nobody answered for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and the copyist of about twenty-six said the thing that everybody in that market had been waiting for her to say, and she said it flatly and without any pleasure in it at all.

“**Not me. I said in Thawmonth that I would not be the one who holds a rule and I have not changed my mind in a month and I am not going to change it because about nine people are tired. A room with a door on a lane and a number and a deed of thirteen words and a press standing open is a drawer, and a thing that goes in a drawer in about four years is a thing nobody can be asked about, and I have watched about four things go into drawers in my own time.**”

Then nobody would do it, for about half an hour, and the badge-man did not help anybody, and the man of about thirty-one from a road office said that a road office could hold it and would be glad to, and the copyist said that a road office holding it would make it a decision, and he said that a road office is a public thing and she said that a drawer is a public thing too if you wait long enough, and about four people laughed and about five did not.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned said something that stopped the whole of it, and she said it to the boards and not to any one person.

“**You have all been asking who will hold it as though the answer is a person. It is not a person. What is held is a sentence, and a sentence is not kept by the person who says it out loud, it is kept by every person who heard it and understood it and could say it back. If nine of us can say it in a month, then nine of us hold it, and if one of us forgets it, then eight of us hold it, and that is not a loss. That is the whole of what a rule is and there is no more of it than that.**”

About four people said nothing at once and about five said nothing at a different time, and then about nine people said, one after another and not all together, that they would hold it, and nobody counted and the copyist said out loud that nobody was to count and that a count of hands on a thing is a figure that will be quoted in about four months by a man who is not in this market.

Then the badge-man asked Aren Kest whether he would hold one of them, and he asked it in those words, and he asked it once, and he did not ask a second time, and he gave the reason afterwards to about four people who had noticed, which was that a man who asks twice is a man who has begun to want an answer and that he had wanted an answer to this one since about the second hour on the previous Saturday and had not been able to do one single thing with it.

Aren Kest said no, and he did not give a reason, and about four people looked at the ground and about five let it go, and the badge-man said that was the correct arrangement and that he had asked once on purpose.

Then the woman of about thirty-four who came four days for a sentence said her sentence for the third time in a market street, in the same words, and nobody answered her, and the badge-man said that he was not going to answer it and had said why on the seventh of Mudmonth and would say why again in about a month if she asked him in a street.

“**I am not going to ask you in a street,**” she said.

“**Then I will not be asked,**” he said.

Hester Vane, of the fifth line, said her two sentences for the last time in that street, and about nine people heard them and about five had heard them before.

“**My road's count is my road's and it is not in this. And if any of you cite me I will say so in this street and you will not be thanked for it by me.**”

Nobody thanked her and nobody cited her and the man with the page said that his page was still inside his coat and that it was going to stay inside his coat until somebody with a head asked him for it, and that he did not expect to wait long.

Then the badge-man said the two things he had said in Thawmonth that he had not got anybody to look at, and he said them in about a minute, and the market went on around them.

“**A man of about fifty-two is not here and has not been here since the ninth of the nine days, and nobody has been sent to him, and I have not sent anybody and I will not, and the reason has not changed since I gave it in this street. And nothing has come from the end of that road since the middle of Mudmonth, and about nine people have now said that out loud in about nine different rooms and lanes and streets, and not one of them has said which road it is, and that is not a failure of the saying. That is the whole of what nine people knowing a thing amounts to.**”

Then Berta Lomax of the fruit valley said the last thing anybody said that morning, and she said it standing in the sun with her own blank sheet in her hand, and nobody had given her anything and nobody had sent her anything, and she said where she had got it.

“**I have had the words since the seventh of Mudmonth, in a lane, at about the fourth hour, in a market street with about nine people standing in it. Nobody wrote them down and nobody sent them to me and nobody gave them to me. I read them out of the air because I read things out, and the woman who said them is standing about nine paces from me and will say so herself, and I am not the keeper of this and I have not been asked to be. On a market morning, in the room over the weigh-house, with the door open, and about nine people standing in it, I will read them out loud. And that is a reading and it is not a keeping and it is not a copy, and if you want to argue with the words then come and stand in the room and say so to the woman who said them.**”

About four people said they would be there and about five said nothing, and the badge-man said that the next market morning was a Thursday and that the room opened at the second hour and shut at the seventh, and that anybody who wanted to argue with the words could come up the outside stair on a Thursday, and that he was not going to say anything else about it and that he would not be thanked for the arrangement.

Then the copyist said the last thing of the morning and she said it to the boards while she was folding the day's returns into her apron, and she said it in the flat voice of a person who has been counting things for about four years and a half and has just been told what one of her counts is for.

“**And what would you put in its place?**” the badge-man said, and she said that he knew the answer to that as well as anybody did, and that the answer was nothing, and that a nothing put in the place of a series is a series that has stopped being a series.

“**In about four years there is going to be a Saturday morning in this street where about nine people are standing at these boards waiting for a man to be asked whether a road refused, and the road will not have been refused, because about nine people will have been busy. That is what I have got and I have not got a second thing to put in place of it. I am going to go on keeping the three series correctly on Monday and I am not going to be thanked for that and I am not going to be asked to stop.**”

Nobody thanked her and the badge-man said that she was the only person in nine counties who had ever said a thing like that about her own work in a street, and that he was not going to improve on it.

Nobody thanked anybody. The register stood at nine questions and no finding went under any of them, and the meeting had not finished, and nobody in nine counties had a head with which to call the next one, and that was a fact about the arrangement and not about the men in it, and nobody in that market said so out loud except the badge-man, and he said it once.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is nobody's, and about nine people holding a sentence that was never written and a sentence nobody was given and a man who was asked once and not twice and a page that is still inside a coat are not in it and none of them has a price.**
