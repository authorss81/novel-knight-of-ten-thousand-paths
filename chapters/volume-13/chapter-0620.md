# Chapter 0620

**Hearthmonth the nineteenth, YR 320, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, a page that has been round nine counties, a register that has stood at seven entries for four and a half years, and a copyist who says what she did on a Tuesday.**

The hearing stood at the second hour and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said so in the lane first, and about nine people came up the outside stair, and the press stood with its mouth open and no date anybody will give it, and the drawer about eleven inches deep held about four hundred returns, and the Crown's form was on the side table with three heads filled and the fourth ruled and empty, and the half leaf lay face down where it has lain since the sixth of a month in a year nobody in that room would name.

The keeper of the room did not come down to the lane for the second market morning running, and the copyist of about twenty-six said so out loud at the fourth hour in front of about nine people, and nobody in that room agreed with her, and she had expected that and had gone on keeping three series correctly for about four and a half years and was going to go on doing it for as long as anybody would let her.

“**I have said it twice now and I am not going to say it a third time,**” she said, “**and I am not saying anything against anybody and I am not asking. I am saying that a room with nobody in it who keeps it went on working this morning for the fifth hour, and that I am the only person who has noticed, and that about four of you understood what I said last time and about five did not, and that the five of you are the reason I have to keep saying it.**”

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet put the page on the table, and it had been round nine counties and back, and about nine people looked at it, and it is worth saying exactly what was on it, because it is the whole of what nine counties have to say about seven roads.

Three of the six spaces had nothing at all in them. Two of them had a mark in them in a hand. **The sixth space had a mark in it, and under the sixth line, ruled long afterwards in a different hand and in a small neat column, there were nineteen columns of figures, and the last one had a nine taken out of it in a third hand.** The seventh line was bare and was not struck out.

“**Three blanks and three marks,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and about four of those three blanks are men who read it and would not, and one of them is a line the page has not reached, and the page cannot tell me which is which, and neither can anybody else, and I have been in four counties with it and I have seen about nine keepers and about four of them asked me what a blank was supposed to mean, and I said it meant they had not put a mark in, and that is a true answer and it is not a complete one.**”

“**And your figure,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said, from the doorway, because she had come in at the seventh hour and had not been in the room earlier and nobody had seen her come.

“**I have got a sheet,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, and he took it out of the satchel, and it was his own writing with no head over it, and it said which line came back with what in it and in what order and on what day, and it was the only record in nine counties of what a page with six spaces actually did. There was no head in that room for it and there never would be, and it went back into the satchel and down the lane in his coat at the seventh hour, which is the third time a number has gone down that lane in a coat in about six weeks, and the first two were a number of barrows and a note on the back of a copy.

Nobody thanked him for the sheet and he did not say he had made it for anybody.

Then the copyist asked the question that put the eighth entry into a register that had stood at seven for four and a half years, and she asked it plainly, and it is her practice to ask a thing in front of the people it concerns.

“**What goes in,**” she said.

Nobody answered for about half a minute, and then the woman of about twenty-nine said the whole of it in about nine words, and the copyist wrote it, and the register is at eight.

“**A woman of about fifty came four days of carting to this room in the first week of a month and asked to be told before a page about her road was read at a gate,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, “**and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge told her in a room with the door open that there is no way to agree it, and she went home, and nobody has asked her again, and it is a question and not an answer and it goes in the register and no finding goes under it.**”

“**You cannot put a question that has been answered no into a register of questions,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**It has not been answered no,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**It has been answered there is no way to agree it, and that is a different answer, and it is one that can be taken back up in about four months by anybody who feels like it, and this register is for questions and it has been at seven since the day it was started and there is nobody in nine counties who will ever fill a head, and so a question it stays.**”

So the register stands at eight entries, all of them questions, and none of them a finding. The day-book stands at nine, because the copyist wrote the eighth in the register and not in the day-book and would not say why and was not asked twice. The press stands open with no date. The fourth head of the Crown's form is ruled and empty and nobody has filled it and nobody may rule a fifth.

And then the copyist of about twenty-six said the thing that is the cost of that Thursday, and she said it standing up, and she said it to the room and not to the drawer.

“**I put the nine in,**” she said.

Nobody said anything.

“**On the Tuesday in the first week of a month, at this counter, a woman of about fifty put a slate down on it and did not open it, and she told me she had taken nine days out of the last column in a hand that was not hers, and I wrote nine into the last column of that slate with this pen, and I did it at the counter about ten minutes after she told me about it, and she did not see me do it and I did not tell her.**”

“**Why,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said.

“**Because I did not know which way her count runs,**” the copyist said, “**and she did not know either, and there was nobody in this room who knew, and I am a person who keeps figures in columns and when I do not know which way a count runs I put the figure at the end, because the end is where a figure you do not understand goes, and that is not a reason, that is a habit, and I have had it since I was about nineteen.**”

“**And you have not told her in about six weeks,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, and it was not a question and it was not an accusation, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge has not made an accusation in a room in thirty-one years and is not going to start on a Thursday.

“**No,**” the copyist said, “**and I am telling her now and I am telling her now because her count was read out loud at the ferry stone on the eighteenth by a man with a page, in the open, in front of about nine people, and it is on a page that is going round nine counties, and a person who has put a figure into another person's count ought to say so before anybody uses the figure, and I am the person, and nobody asked me and I am not going to let it be found out by somebody else.**”

“**Then it stands,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said, “**where it is, in the wrong place, in a hand that is not hers, until somebody who knows which way her count runs says otherwise. I am not going to have it rubbed out and I am not going to have a second nine put in beside it. That is the correct answer and it is a bad answer and it is the only one.**”

Nobody thanked the copyist and nobody told her she was wrong, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote one line on his sheet and it was not about the nine, and about four people in that room understood what it had cost and about five did not.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet asked the last question of that Thursday morning, and he asked it to Aren Kest, and he asked it because he had asked him everything else in about six weeks and had never asked him that.

“**Is there anything on that page that is wrong.**”

Aren Kest had been in the room from the second hour to about the fifth. He stood up off the wall, which he does, and he said two things and the second of them was the whole of the block.

“**Yes,**” he said, “**and I am not going to tell this room what it is, and I am going to tell you why I am not, and the why is the true thing and not a way of being dignified.**”

“**Then you are standing in a room with a page on it and you know what is wrong with it,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**I am,**” Aren Kest said. “**And if I say it in this room then the next man who comes up that stair with a page and a name at the top of it will be able to say that a man with a limp has told this room that a road is saved by somebody standing on it and saying a number. That is a page. I would have made it. I have watched one of them shut a road for nine days in six weeks and about nine houses at one end and about nine at the other did not know whether the woman who keeps that road was stood down or had gone home for a bad week, and she has a slate in her apron that nobody was ever supposed to see, and the page that shut that road has a name at the top of it that is not hers and is four days off and is in a hand that is not his.**”

Nobody in that room said anything at all, and about four of the nine had understood the whole of it and about five had understood a part of it, and the two sets did not overlap, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not write any of it down.

“**Then what do we do with the eighth question,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said, and she asked it flatly and she was not asking him and she has never once in four and a half years asked him a question she had not already answered herself.

“**You keep it,**” Aren Kest said. “**And the woman of about fifty keeps her nineteen columns, and a man of about fifty on a made road four days down has put a mark in a space because he can be found and be told he is wrong, and a man cutting a ditch four days down has cut about nine hundred paces of it because the water was coming over his road and nobody asked him to. None of them was sent. I did not send any of them and I am not going to claim any of them and I would like this room to notice that I have not stood up here and said one word about any of those people, and that they are the only people in nine counties who have done anything about that page this month.**”

He went down the outside stair at the fifth hour and the lane had about nine people going out of the market by then and about four of them were within earshot, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came out of the door about twenty minutes later and went past him and neither of them said anything, and that is the whole of what happened between them in about six weeks, and neither of them said anything about it, and there is no finding under the eighth question and there is not going to be one from him.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the copyist's, and a nine put in a wrong place by a person who did not know which way a count runs and about six weeks of not saying so are not in it and neither of them has a price.**
