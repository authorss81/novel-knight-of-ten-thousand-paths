# Chapter 0616

**Hearthmonth the fourteenth, YR 320, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house and about the second hour in the lane, a copy of a sheet in a man's own hand, and a name at the top of it.**

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet came down the outside stair at about the second hour on a market morning and did not go into the lane until he had looked up and down it, and then he came the length of it and said a thing that nobody in that lane had said to him before, which was that he wanted to show Aren Kest something and wanted to do it outside.

The room was open behind them and about nine people were in it and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room was at the table with the register, and neither of the two men went up.

He had a sheet in his hand. It was not the sheet. It was a copy, and it was in his own writing from end to end, one hand, and he had made it at the counter under the weigh-house on a Thursday a month ago because a district that has a direction sitting in a room in a market town has to be told what it is, and a district that is not told has to find out from the road.

“**I was not going to keep this,**” he said. “**It is not my office to keep it and there is a drawer about eleven inches deep upstairs that is the place for it, and I am not going to put it in that drawer, and I have not done anything clever with it. I have had it in a satchel for about a month. I am telling you that before you look at it so that you know what it is.**”

“**Show me,**” Aren Kest said.

“**Read the top of the first page,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

Aren Kest read the top of the first page. There was a name at the top of it, in the place where a name goes on a thing that is going to be acted on, and the name was his own, and it was not in the hand of the man who had written any of the other three hands on the original, and it was in the hand of the man standing in the lane holding it out.

He read the whole of it, which takes about a minute and a half, and then he read the top of the first page again, which is what anybody does.

Then he said the first thing.

“**What is written at the bottom of it.**”

“**Nothing,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**What is written on the back of the original.**”

“**Nothing. I turned it over at the counter and I have never seen a mark on it and I have looked at it about four times since.**”

“**Then it is not mine,**” Aren Kest said, “**and it is also not anybody's, and both of those are true, and the second one is about a month old.**”

“**You may say it,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, and he said it in the plain voice of a man who has been waiting about a month to say something and has not been asked to, “**and it will not do you any good at all, and I am telling you that because I have watched a page do this and I have watched a man say it and then stand in a market afterwards, and the two of those facts together are worth nothing to anybody in nine counties.**”

“**Say the rest of it,**” Aren Kest said.

“**I have been in a room four and a half years,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and there is no form in this room for a man to say that a page is not his, because the room has no head to put such a thing under, and because a thing a man says about a page is a claim and a claim has to be believed, and being believed is a thing you are given and not a thing you do. There is no form for that. I have looked.**”

“**And a man in this town has about four hundred people who hold one true thing about me and about four hundred who hold another,**” Aren Kest said, “**and the two sets do not overlap, and I have been asked whether I was going to put the two of them straight and I said no, and I said no in front of about nine people, and there is a leaf in a drawer upstairs with no head over it that says so.**”

“**Then that is the third thing,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and I am not going to say it because you have just said it, and you said it better than I would have.**”

They stood in the lane for a while and nothing happened, and about four people went past and heard nothing at all and about five went past and heard two men not saying something, and the two sets did not overlap.

Then Aren Kest said the thing that the whole of that month had been leaning on, and he said it to the boards of the weigh-house and not to the man.

“**There is one thing I could do about it and I am not going to do it,**” he said, “**and I am telling you what it is so that you can stop waiting for me to do it. I could stand in that lane on a market morning and say out loud, to about nine people, that the page is not mine. It is a page with a name at the top of it and nobody in nine counties can show it false, and I am the man at the top of it, and if I say the page is not mine then a man at a gate four days off who was told at a gate about a keeper being stood down for nine days has been told a false thing, and about nine people will have heard it said out loud in a market by a man with a limp, and it will be in about four hundred mouths inside a week, and about four hundred of them already hold one true thing about me and about four hundred hold another, and a man who has stood in a lane and picked one of them has told nine strangers which one he is, and he has not asked either of them anything.**”

“**So it is not that you do not believe me,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**It is not that I do not believe anybody,**” Aren Kest said. “**It is that there is about nine days of road between me and a gate where a woman keeps a road that was shut for nine days because of a name, and about four hundred people in this town, and about nine days is not very long but it is long enough to be the whole of the distance between a thing that is true and a thing that is useful.**”

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet turned the copy over and wrote on the back of it, in his own hand, in about the ordinary size of a man's writing, and he did it without being asked and he did it on the back of a copy of a page that had nothing on the back.

He wrote: **the man named at the top of this copy was shown this copy on the fourteenth of Hearthmonth YR 320 at about the second hour, and did not say the writing was his.**

“**Why are you writing that,**” Aren Kest said.

“**Because it is a thing that happened,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and because in about a month somebody will hold up that page in about four counties and say a man with a limp has denied it, and I will be the only person in nine counties who can say that he did not deny it and that he was shown it and read it and said nothing, and I have got a hand that four people would know and I have got the date on it, and that is the whole of my use.**”

“**It is not going in the room,**” Aren Kest said.

“**There is no head for it,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, and put the copy back in the satchel, and that was the end of it, and nobody thanked him for it.

The other thing that happened on that market morning happened at about the fifth hour in the room and came out of the satchel with the page, which had been out in the counties for six more days and had come back with a second mark in a space.

“**The coast,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said. “**A man who keeps a road on the coast and has kept it about nineteen years. He keeps his count in tides, which none of the three of us can use and which is a real count, and he put a mark in the space against his own line, and he did it because he wants the arithmetic to be honest.**”

“**Did he say why,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge asked.

“**He said that a gap is a gap,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and that he has never in nineteen years had anybody in a market town four days off use one of his numbers, and that if a gap is going to be shut then somebody had better be able to say what the gap is, and that he is not the somebody but that he is the only person within about four days of it and that a mark in a space from him is worth a mark and that a mark in a space from anybody else is worth nothing at all.**”

Nobody said out loud what the two marks were worth, so it is worth saying. **A mark in a space is not a vote for the arithmetic. It is a statement by a person who can be named and found and asked what their figure is, and there are two of those in six spaces, and five spaces have nothing in them, and about four of those five are refusals and one of them has not been reached, and nobody in nine counties will ever be able to say which is which.**

At the seventh hour the room shut and the key went down the outside stair into the lane, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came out of the door and went past the two of them, and about nine people were going up the made road out of the market by then and about four of them were within earshot, and the three of them said nothing, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet went up the lane the other way with the satchel, and Aren Kest stood in the lane with his stick for about as long as it takes a cart to get out of a market and did not go up to the inn and did not go into the room either.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is Aren Kest's, and his own name in a clerk's hand in nine counties and about nine days of road between him and a gate are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a sheet of thin paper at eleven, a book cover at thirteen, a bundle cord at fourteen, a quill at seventeen, an inkhorn at eighteen, a cake of ink at twenty, a seal at twenty-two, a scraper at thirty, a printed handbill at thirty-three, a sheet of foolscap at thirty-four, and a candle holder at thirty-nine.** Eleven, thirteen, fourteen, seventeen, eighteen, twenty, twenty-two, thirty, thirty-three, thirty-four and thirty-nine is two hundred and fifty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and fifty-one pence is five marks and eleven over**, and nine counties, four hands of writing, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, about eleven inches of drawer, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, a half leaf face down, six ruled spaces and two ends of a road are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Saturday, the second hour, the fifth hour, the seventh hour, a market morning, about seven months, four and a half years and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **Aren Kest and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man who keeps a coast road and counts in tides and about four hundred people who hold one true thing about him and about four hundred who hold another and a woman of about fifty who keeps a road with a slate in her apron and a woman of about forty who runs a lime kiln are ten figures of persons, and the first of them has not said whether a page is his in a market and the seventh of them has said it and the eighth of them has said the other and the ninth and the tenth of them have not been asked.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the copy went back into a satchel.
