# Chapter 0605

**Wolfmonth the sixteenth, YR 320, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, the hearing standing, a man asked in the room what roads are open, and the answer is seven and the room has no head for a list.**

Aren Kest was in the room on the market morning because the man of about fifty-eight with a badge had sent a boy up to the inn at the first hour to ask him to come, and had said, when the boy said that the man had sent no reason, that the reason was that a man who is asked nothing in a room learns to be a man who is told nothing in a room, and that this was the only reason and it was not a good one.

The hearing stood at the second hour and the man with the badge said so in the lane first, and about nine people came up the outside stair, and the register was on the table with seven entries in it, all of them questions, and none of them findings, and the day-book was open at nine, and the press stood with its mouth open and no date anybody will give it.

At about the fourth hour, with nine people in the room and nobody seated because there is no bench in it, the man of about fifty-eight with a badge asked the question himself, in the plainest words he had, and he asked it once.

“**What roads are open.**”

Nobody in that room answered for about a minute, and the minute was not silence. It was the sound of about nine people deciding whether the question was for somebody in particular, and about four of them looking at the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room, and about five of them looking at the window, and the two sets not overlapping.

Then Aren Kest stood up off the wall he had been standing against and said it, and he said it in the flat voice of a man reading a list off a post, and he said it slowly, and he did not say why.

“**The ferry city, where the crossing is worked by about nine families and the water is on somebody's account every week. The county where a list of names was burned and the people on it are still there under other names. The waterworks country, where the channels are kept by hand and a boundary stone is moved once in a generation. The coast of returning roads, where the count is kept in tides and the piers are shared. The fruit valley, where a person answers for it and may be refused by her own valley, and where nobody has said a month or a day out loud in about twenty-two years. The glass country on the high flats, where the workshops make the glass that goes into an anchor and they do it in about nine huts and they are all in the same valley. And a small one.**”

He stopped there and the room waited for the seventh and he gave it to them.

“**And a small one,**” he said, “**about which I am going to say only that it is small, and I have not been able to say the rest of it in a room of this size, and I have not been asked to, and I would like it noted that nobody asked me to.**”

“**You have been asked,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said. “**I asked you in front of nine people.**”

“**You asked me for the open roads,**” Aren Kest said, “**and the small one is open, and I have given you seven, and there is no list in this room and there is no head on the register for a list, and the register is at seven entries and every one of them is a question.**”

“**Then write one,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said. “**On a bare leaf. There is one.**”

“**And who is to be written at the top of it,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said, from the table, without looking up.

That stopped the room for longer than the question about the roads had.

“**Put nobody,**” Aren Kest said. “**Put nothing at the top.**”

“**Then it is a list that any person in nine counties can add to,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and any person in nine counties can take from, and there are about four hundred people in this town who would like to add and about four hundred who would like to take, and the two of them do not overlap, and there is no way on that leaf of telling which is which.**”

“**Then it is better than a certificate,**” Aren Kest said. “**A certificate has one man's name on it and no way of being argued with. That has no name on it and no way of being hid behind.**”

Nobody wrote the list. The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said out loud that there was nothing else in the room with a head over it that a list could go on, and the copyist of about twenty-six wrote the figure on a bare leaf, and the figure was seven, and she read none of it back, and the leaf went into the drawer about eleven inches deep with the returns, and the register stood at seven entries and all of them questions.

Then the man of about fifty-eight with a badge asked the second question, and this is the one the room did not get an answer to.

“**Who answers for the seven?**”

“**I do,**” Aren Kest said.

“**Alone?**”

“**Alone.**”

“**Say what that means, because a man of about thirty-four with a sheet has written a thing on a leaf and I have seen it, and what he has written is not a name and it is not an office, and I want to know whether it is a man.**”

Aren Kest said the whole of it in about nine sentences and he said it standing up, and he did not make it easier for himself by standing away from the wall.

“**It means that seven things come into nine counties and I am the only person they all come to, and I answer for the ferry city to a woman there who can refuse me, and I answer for the fruit valley to a woman there who can be refused by her own valley, and I can do those two because those two have a person in them who has told me what they want and who can stop me on an ordinary Tuesday. The other five do not have that. Three of them have a person I can write to and a person I cannot write to, and one of them is a country that has never had a list of itself in anything, and the fifth is the small one and I cannot say one fact about the small one in this room without somebody in it writing it down and it being true for a week.**”

“**Then you have answered for two,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said, “**and there are five you have not answered for, and you have told this room which two and not one word about why those two and not the other five.**”

“**I answered for the two I have been in,**” Aren Kest said. “**In the last year. That is the whole of the reason and I am not going to dress it up as anything else in front of nine people.**”

“**Then you have not chosen,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**you have been the last one there, and that is the same shape of thing as choosing and it is worse, because a man who chooses can be argued with about his reasons and a man who has been last somewhere cannot be argued with at all.**”

Nobody answered that, and about nine people stood in a room with no bench in it and did not answer it, and the copyist did not write it down, and it was not in the register.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge then asked the last question of that morning, and he asked it in a way that made the room go quiet, because he had come prepared to ask it and nobody in the room had known he was going to.

“**How many?**”

“**Seven,**” Aren Kest said. “**And there is no eighth, and I have not tried for one, and I am not going to find out this month whether there is.**”

“**Why not?**”

“**Because I have run out of a thing I cannot name,**” he said, “**and the thing does not ask my permission and it does not tell me when it is finished, and I have said that out loud in a room and I would rather it were in the record than in a man's head, and I cannot put it in the record, and that is the whole of the answer.**”

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key was in the lane, because the key is in the lane, and she came up at the seventh hour with the key in her hand and said the thing that was the true thing about that morning, and she said it once, to the woman of about twenty-nine and not to the man.

“**I have kept this lane four and a half years,**” she said, “**and the room upstairs has a drawer about eleven inches deep with about four hundred returns in it and a register with seven questions in it, and in four and a half years nobody in this lane has ever asked me which roads are open, and I have never said that out loud, and I am saying it now, and I am not asking anybody anything.**”

Nobody answered her either, and she went back down with the key, and the woman of about twenty-nine did not tell her she was wrong, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote nothing on his sheet and looked at the window and said nothing about the four hundred people and the other four hundred people for about an hour afterwards and then went home.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-four's, and four and a half years of not being asked is not in it and has no price.**

**a bar of soap at thirteen, a cake of tallow at sixteen, a rushlight at nineteen, a bootlace at twenty, a padlock at twenty-three, a jar of oil at twenty-five, a bundle cord at twenty-six, a waxed string at twenty-eight, a sheet of foolscap at thirty-nine, and a broom at forty.** Thirteen, sixteen, nineteen, twenty, twenty-three, twenty-five, twenty-six, twenty-eight, thirty-nine and forty is two hundred and forty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and forty-nine pence is five marks and nine over**, and about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, a drawer about eleven inches deep, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, nine counties, two ends of a road, about four miles of channel and a half leaf face down are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Thursday, the second hour, the fourth hour, the seventh hour, a market morning, about seven months, four and a half years and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and Aren Kest and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and about nine people who came up the stair and a man who keeps a channel and a man who keeps a coast road and about nine huts in a high valley are nine figures of persons, and the first of them has not been asked one question in four and a half years and the last of them has never sent this room anything and neither of them is in this column.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the leaf with a figure on it went into a drawer with a figure's worth of depth left in it.
