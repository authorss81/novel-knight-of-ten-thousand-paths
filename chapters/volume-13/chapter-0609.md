# Chapter 0609

**Frostmonth the fifth, YR 320, a Tuesday and not a market day, the second hour at the counter under the weigh-house, nine days gone, a slate in an apron, and a woman of about fifty who asks for one thing and is told there is no way to agree it.**

She came the nine days because the nine days were up, and the road between the ferry stone and the ninth milestone was open again on the morning of the ninth day, and she was on it before the light, because there were about nine households at the top end and about nine at the bottom and a road that has been shut for nine days has grass coming up in the middle of it by the time anybody thinks to look.

She had not been asked anything and nobody had shown her the page. She came to the market town because it is four days of carting and because a man at a gate had told her, and because a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a key in a lane had sent a carter with a message, which was not *come* and was not *you are wanted*, and was *there is a room and there are people in it and you may say whatever you like and nobody will ask you anything you do not want to answer*.

The woman of about fifty put her slate on the counter and did not open it.

“**I am not here to complain,**” she said. “**I want that said first, because I know what a room does with a person who has come to complain, and I have watched two of them in my life and one of them was me. I am not here to complain. I am here to say a number.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge came down at the third hour and stood on the other side of the counter and did not sit, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came up the outside stair with the door open behind her and stayed in the doorway, and the copyist of about twenty-six kept her hands in her sleeves, and about four people who had come for their own reasons stood at the far end and did not come closer.

The woman of about fifty said the number.

She kept it the way anybody who has kept a road for nineteen years keeps it, which is on a slate in her apron, in a hand that had got a great deal better in nineteen years, and it was not a number about the road at all. It was the number of days in a year that the road had been passable, out of the days in that year, and it was on the slate in a column, and there was a column for every year of the nineteen, and the last column had nine days taken out of it in a hand that was not hers.

“**Nine,**” she said. “**Nine days off, and the year is not finished, and I have put the nine in the wrong place because I did not know which way the count runs, and I have not rubbed it out, and I would like somebody in this room to tell me which way the count runs, and that is the whole of what I came for.**”

Nobody told her, because nobody in that room knew how her count ran, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said so, and she said she had not expected it and had come anyway, and that was the end of that.

Then she said the other thing, and she said it flatly, and she was not angry and nothing in her voice was asking for anything.

“**The nine days cost me nothing,**” she said. “**That is what I have been trying to say to the four people who have asked me about it since Tuesday. Nothing. The month is slack. Nobody sent for me. Nothing was late. I went home for nine days and I came back and the road was there.**”

“**Then what is the matter,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**The matter is that for nine days, nobody in about nine houses at the top end and about nine at the bottom knew whether I was stood down or gone home for a bad week,**” she said. “**That is the whole of it. I have kept that road nineteen years. I have never once not been on it. And in nine days last month, if the road had gone under, or if a cart had gone into the ford in a way that wanted two men and a rope, there was no arrangement at all by which about nine people who owe me for a season could have found out in a morning whether to go and look for me or to wait.**”

She put her thumb on the slate without opening it.

“**And the reason there was no arrangement is that the standing down came off a gate,**” she said, “**and the gate said it to me, and the gate did not write to me, and nobody knew I was to be told because the paper does not have my name on it. The paper has a man's name on it four days off who has not been in this county in four years. It does not have mine on it anywhere, and it does not have nine households' worth of anybody's on it, and I have been on that road every day of nineteen years and there is not one line on that page that a person could use to find me.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge said that he would put it in the room, on a bare leaf, in her words, and that he would say it out loud in the lane, and she said that she would rather he did not, because a thing said out loud in a lane is said out loud in a lane and she has four houses that owe her for a season and about five that do not and she does not want the five deciding that the road is a thing that is argued about in a market town four days off.

“**Then I will not say it in the lane,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

Then he said the true thing, and it was the last true thing anybody said in that room that day.

“**There is no way to agree what you have asked for,**” he said. “**You have asked to be told before a page about your road is read at a gate. I would agree to it in this room in about four seconds and it would be worth nothing, because the page goes to the district on a Friday on a cart and I am in a room in a market town on a Friday and there is no rider, and by the time anybody in this room heard about it the gate would have read it, and the gatekeeper would have done his job, and you would be at home.**”

Nobody said anything.

“**I have been in this work thirty-one years,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and I have watched a road get a name put at the top of a page and I have watched it cost nothing, and the nothing is the part I cannot get past, and I am telling nine people in a room that there is no arrangement, and I am not going to dress it up as one.**”

The woman of about fifty said that she had not expected an arrangement, and that she had come four days of carting to say one number out loud to a room, and that she had said it, and that she would go home now, and she said it without any weight at all, and she picked up the slate and did not open it.

“**What is on it?**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said.

“**Nineteen years,**” the woman of about fifty said. “**That is all. Nineteen columns and a nine in the wrong place in the last one. It is mine and it is not for you and I would not want it in a drawer with four hundred returns in it, and I have said so, and if somebody takes it out of my apron I will take it back.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six said that nobody was going to take it, and the woman of about fifty said that she knew, and went out, and the carter was at the weigh-house door with the horse already yoked because a carter who waits is a carter who is paid for waiting.

Aren Kest was not in that room and came into the lane at about the fifth hour and stood about nine paces off while the carter yoked up, and the woman of about fifty saw him from the step and looked at him for the length of a count and then got in.

“**I have heard about the page,**” she said.

“**Have you.**”

“**I have heard there is a man's name at the top of it,**” she said, “**and that is all I have heard, and I have not come about it, and I am telling you that I have not come about it so that you cannot think I have.**”

Then the carter went, and the lane was clear, and he was nine paces off with his stick and he had not come closer, and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge came out of the door and stood beside him and said the two sentences that the whole of that month had left him with.

“**Nine days,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and a woman has come four days of carting to tell nine people that nobody could find her, and the nine days are gone and the road is open and the slate is in her apron, and there is no arrangement and I have said so in a room with the door open.**”

“**Then what do you want me to do?**” Aren Kest said.

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge looked at him for a while, and about four people in that lane were within earshot and about five were not, and the two sets did not overlap, and nobody asked which of them anybody was in.

“**Nothing,**” he said. “**I want you to do nothing, and I have never in thirty-one years wanted a man to do nothing, and I am going to a room and put it in a book with the date on it, and that is the whole of what I have.**”

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a column and it is the woman of about fifty's, and nine days on a road and nineteen years on the same road and four days of carting each way are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a nail bag at six, a bushel measure at ten, a tallow candle at twelve, a whetstone at thirteen, a canvas apron at fourteen, a rake tine at fifteen, a gate hinge at seventeen, a hurdle wire at twenty-two, a sheep hurdle at twenty-four, a stone hammer at twenty-five, a drench horn at twenty-nine, and a file at thirty-eight.** Six, ten, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, seventeen, twenty-two, twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-nine and thirty-eight is two hundred and twenty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and twenty-five pence is four marks and thirty-three over**, and two ends of a road, a ninth milestone, a ferry stone, about a hundred paces of a district, nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries and about four hundred returns are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Tuesday, the second hour, the third hour, the fifth hour, nine days, four and a half years, about seven months and about three weeks are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A woman of about fifty who keeps a road and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and Aren Kest and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a carter who was paid for not waiting and about nine households at the top end and about nine at the bottom are ten figures of persons, and the first of them keeps a slate in her apron and the last of them have never in nineteen years been told that anybody was stood down and neither of those facts is in this column.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the slate went into an apron and up a made road.
