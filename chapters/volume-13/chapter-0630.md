# Chapter 0630

**Thawmonth the first, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning and the first of a month, the second hour in a room over a weigh-house, a register that has stood at eight entries for about a month, and the answer a man gives to a question nobody asked.**

The year turned on the first of a month and nobody in that lane turned with it, and the first thing anybody wrote down with the new year against it was a date in the corner of a roll book, and the copyist of about twenty-six wrote it in the same hand she has written every date in for four and a half years and did not look at it twice.

The hearing stood at the second hour and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said it in the lane first, as he does on the first of every month, and about nine people came up the outside stair, and the room over the weigh-house was open because it is a market morning.

Aren Kest was not in the room. He was in the lane, at the low end of the counter boards, with his stick, and about four people saw him and about five did not, and nobody went and told him there was a stair open.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet had the page out of the satchel and put it on the table, and it had been round nine counties a second time, and about four people looked at it before anybody said anything, and the state of it was this: three spaces with a mark in them, and three spaces with nothing at all in them, and two of those three are refusals and one of them is a line the page has not reached, and the page cannot tell which is which and cannot be made to and no one in that room has ever worked out which.

“**It will go round a third time,**” he said, “**and the third time there will be a fourth blank and it will be the first space on the sheet, and the fourth blank will be a man who has read it and would not, and there will be nothing whatever on that page to tell anybody so, and I have got a piece of paper with no head over it in a satchel that says which one it was and it goes down this lane in my coat at the seventh hour, and I am not going to be asked to prove it.**”

Nobody asked him to prove it.

Then the copyist of about twenty-six asked the question, and it is the ninth entry, and the register is at nine, and all of them are questions, and none of them is a finding, and the eighth was asked by a woman of about fifty in the first week of a month and none of the nine has an answer under it.

“**What goes in?**” she said.

“**Say it and do not soften it,**” the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said, which is not what she said in a month ago, and it is what she said now.

The copyist said it standing, and she said it to the room.

“**Whether a room with a door on a lane and a number and a deed of thirteen words may hold a thing that was decided in a room with no head over it, and if it may not, where that thing goes.**”

Nobody answered for a while.

“**It may not,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**And I know the answer to the second half as well as I know the answer to the first, and it is a drawer about eleven inches deep, and it is not a place. It is a place so that a thing is not lost. And I have said that once in this room and I am going to say it once more and then I am going to let it stand. A room can hold a question. A room can hold a thing somebody has refused to sign. What it cannot hold is a decision, because a decision that lands here has nowhere to be challenged, and a decision that cannot be challenged is not a decision, it is a thing that happened to nine people in a cold room at the second hour of a Sunday night. It goes in as a question and it stays a question and it is the ninth, and no finding goes under the ninth, and I would like the man who wrote it and the man who decided it both to be in the room when somebody tries to make it a finding.**”

“**Then I will be in the room,**” the man of about fifty-eight with a badge said, and nobody thanked him, and he is a man who has wanted a room to hold a thing for thirty-one years and has just been told that the one thing he has been asking it to hold is the one thing it must not hold, and he said so himself and said it was correct.

The man of about forty-seven with a page came up the lane at the third hour on foot and said the thing he has come to say four times now, and he says it now with something new in it and the new thing is worse.

“**Six held,**” he said. “**For a season. The works has six on it and it will take seven and the six that are marked to be held are holding and the third is dry. That is my arithmetic paid and I have said that I will not enjoy it and I have not and I have not stopped.**”

“**Then say the new thing,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**The new thing is this,**” Garvin Slade said. “**I am right four times and every time I have been right the room has found a reason not to let me do anything, and the fifth time there will not be a gap. There is one gap. I have closed it. In about four months the same arithmetic runs again and there will be no line on any page in nine counties with nothing against it, and the thing that goes first will be a road with a number on it and a name against the number and a keeper, and I have not got a name for which one and I have said I will not guess and I am not going to.**”

“**You could guess,**” the man of about thirty-one from a road office said, from the end of the table.

“**I could guess,**” Garvin Slade said, “**and I have not, and I would like the room to understand that the not-guessing is not modesty. If I name a road then the next page has a name at the top of it before it goes out, and I have watched what that does, and I am not going to be the man who hands nine counties a list.**”

Nobody thanked him. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote two lines and put the pen down.

Then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room said the thing that closed the morning, and she said it standing with her hand flat on the table the way she does.

“**Then I am going to say the last thing in this room and it is about a road and it is not about anybody in it,**” she said. “**The year turned this morning. I have been in this lane about four and a half years and I have watched two years turn and neither of them changed anything that this room holds. The seventh is still off the works and the page is still going round and the register is at nine questions and the fourth head of the Crown's form is ruled and empty and nobody has filled it and nobody may rule a fifth, and the drawer is about eleven inches deep and about four hundred returns, and the presses stand open with no date, and a leaf lies face down on a table in a room over a saddler's that nobody has turned over and that nobody in that room is minded to turn over, and a road four days of carting from this counter is being carried on the days when two people turn up at the two ends of it, and no date on any paper in this room makes that road better or worse, and I am not going to pretend to anybody that it does.**”

Nobody thanked her and the year went on being a new one.

At the seventh hour the room shut and the key went down the outside stair and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key went down it with the key in her hand, and about nine people went out into the market, and the man of about forty-seven with a page went down the lane on foot, and the satchel went with the man of about thirty-four with a sheet.

The lane was about four hundred paces long and there were about nine people still in it and about four of them within earshot of the low end of the counter boards, and Aren Kest was at the low end of the counter boards with his stick, and he had been there since the second hour, and nobody in that lane had asked him a single question all morning.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came out of the door about twenty minutes after the stair and went past him, and neither of them said anything, and that is the whole of what happened between them in about a month, and nothing between them is described in a document and there is not going to be a finding about it from anybody in nine counties.

And then he said it, to the lane, to about four people, and to nobody in particular, and no one had asked it.

“**I wanted it,**” he said.

Nobody said anything.

“**Nobody has asked me that in two volumes and about a hundred and thirty days,**” he said, “**and I said it out loud in a room in this county a month past, that a man who has been the only person who can see the load-bearing problem in a place has never once been asked whether he wanted it, and I did not say anything when a woman of about forty asked me it in a lane at a counter, and I have not said anything tonight, and I am saying it now because nobody is going to ask and because it is a Thursday and there is a market on and about four of you can hear me. I wanted it. That is the whole of it. I wanted it and I am not going to be told that wanting it is a defence, because it is not one, and I would rather be a man who wanted a thing and knew that about nine households on a road are carrying what six were carrying, than a man who was asked whether he wanted it and said no and did it anyway.**”

“**It is not in the register,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said, from the doorway, and she had come down and she was not going to come any further than the doorway.

“**No,**” he said. “**And I would not want it to be, and I want that said too, because if it went in there it would be a finding about a man and this register is for questions, and a man who has been asked afterwards and has answered afterwards has not been asked. A thing I say in a lane on a Thursday to four people who can hear me is not being asked. It is the other thing. And I have wanted it and I have said so and it is in no book in nine counties and it does not make anything true, and the sixth road will hold until it does not, and I will be asked again in about four months, and there will be no head in nine counties then either.**”

Nobody thanked him. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not write it down, and the copyist of about twenty-six went back up the stair and shut a door that had been shut for about a minute already, and about four of the people in that lane went home and about five did not, and the two sets did not overlap and no one has ever said which of them anybody was in.

Aren Kest went out of the market town at the eighth hour on the made road towards the coast, and he had not been told anything by anybody, and he had not asked anybody for anything, and there was nothing in the lane behind him that would ever be written down, and the year was a new one.
