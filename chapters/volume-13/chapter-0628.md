# Chapter 0628

**Goatmonth the third, YR 320, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street of a town of about two thousand people, a woman of about forty who has been four days of carting and has not been asked anything, and a man from a road office buying a basket.**

She was in the market before the room over the weigh-house was fully open, because a market at this hour belongs to the people who sell in it and not to the people who keep records about it, and she had come up on the Thursday with an empty cart and a debt to collect and about four hours of work ahead of her that nobody had given her.

She found out at about the second hour, from a man of about thirty-one from a road office, in about nine words, while he was putting down a basket.

He was not doing it to her. He had come into the market to buy a basket, which he does, because the office sends him out for a basket whenever the office decides it needs a basket, and he had been in the market about four minutes, and he said it to the middle distance, the way a man says a thing that has happened to him and is not addressed to anybody.

“**Two days and a bit,**” he said. “**Took a road off the works under the abbey on Sunday night. Seventh line. Nobody had to tell me, I read it in a schedule that came back with a blank on it. There was a note, it said the road had been taken off on a man's word in a room, which is not a thing a schedule has a space for.**”

And the woman of about forty was standing two paces off with her hand on her cart, and she said:

“**Say the name of the road?**”

He turned round. He was about thirty-one and he had a basket in his hand and he had not looked at her, and he said the thing a man says when a stranger in a market asks him a question.

“**I do not have it,**” he said. “**The schedule does not carry names. It carries a line and a number and a district. Seventh line, taken off Sunday night, on a man's word, no address.**”

“**I keep the seventh line,**” she said.

He looked at her then properly for the first time, and he had a basket in his hand and about four hours of work in front of him, and what went across his face was not guilt. It was the ordinary inconvenience of a man who has said a thing in a market and has found out that a person standing in the market cares about it.

“**Then I am sorry,**” he said. “**I did not know. There is nobody in the office I could have told and I did not know there was anybody to tell.**”

“**No,**” she said, “**you did not, and that is the whole of what I came to say and I have been four days saying it to myself on a slope and I would rather say it in a street.**”

Then she said it, and she said it in a working voice, standing at her cart in a market street, and about nine people heard it and about four of them are the only people in nine counties who will remember the words.

“**Nobody told me,**” she said. “**I have been four days of carting since Friday. Nobody came up the slope. Nobody left anything at the kiln. Nobody said anything at the top end to the woman who has held that end for about thirty-one years, and I have been twice up there since Friday and both times she asked me whether I had heard anything and both times I said no, and I said it about a thing I had not been told and I have not slept properly about it since. So I am not going to stand here and tell anybody you are cruel, because you have not been cruel, and I want that said first, before anything else, because I know what is coming and I would like it on the record that nobody has been cruel to me.**”

The market went on around her and nobody said anything, and a man selling nothing came through with a barrow and went round.

“**Nobody sent anybody,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said. He had been standing at the edge of the boards and he came over, and he said it flatly, and he did not apologise, and nobody in the market thought he should have. “**I have carried a sheet with a line on it for four days. I have been to a district office. I have been told by a man with a limp not to put it in a schedule, and I did not, and I have not put it in a schedule, and I would not have known how to deliver it if I had, because a line is not a road and a road here has no keeper against its name. That is not a thing I did to you. It is a thing that was in front of me and I did not do anything about it, and you are entitled to that sentence and I am not entitled to a better one.**”

“**I know what happened,**” she said. “**I have known since Friday, and I have not been told, and those are two different facts and I have had four days to hold both of them and I have.**”

Then the badge-man said the true thing, from about nine paces off, because the badge-man is the man who says true things where anybody can hear them.

“**There was no arrangement,**” he said. “**I have been in this work thirty-one years and I have never in my life had to tell a woman that nobody was going to tell her, and I have told her. I do not know how it is that a thing can be true in nine counties, written down nowhere, acted on by everybody, and there is no one to blame and no one to ask, and I have had a month to look for the arrangement and there is not one. There is not a bad arrangement. There is not any.**”

“**There is a reason,**” the copyist of about twenty-six said, and she said it from the boards, and she was not going to be allowed to have said it anywhere else. “**I have kept a standing in that room for four and a half years and the standing is right and I have said so in front of nine people about a month ago. A road may be described only by its own answer. The consequence of that standing is that there is no list of keepers in nine counties and no address for anybody, and I did not put that consequence there on purpose and I would not have put it there if I had thought about it, and I have thought about it since Friday and I am not going to take the standing back, because if I take it back then the two warnings about four miles of channel go into one book and somebody in nine counties can be shown a line made out of two true things.**”

The woman of about forty looked at her for a while.

“**Then it is you,**” she said.

“**It is the standing and not me,**” the copyist said, “**and I have been called a great many things at that counter and I have never once been called correct, and I would like to be allowed to be correct about this without it being a comfort to either of us.**”

Then the woman of about forty asked the only question she had come to ask, and she asked it to the market and not to a person, and it was not a question about rights and it was not a question about the road.

“**Is the count to be kept?**” she said. “**Because I am going to keep it either way and I would like to know whether I am keeping it.**”

Nobody in that market said anything at all for a moment, and the badge-man looked at a man of about thirty-four with a sheet, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet looked at the boards.

“**Keep it,**” he said. “**There is nowhere to put it. That is not a reason to stop. I have been in four counties this month and I have asked about nine keepers and I could put about four of them on a piece of paper and the other five I could not put anywhere, and I have never once thought about the five before, and I am thinking about one of them now and I am not going to be able to do anything about it, and you should keep the count and the boy should go on having it and neither of those things is mine to tell you to do and I have just told you to.**”

“**Then I will keep it,**” she said, “**and I will tell the woman at the top end on my next trip, which will be Friday, and she will not be pleased and she will not say so, and the two of us have been doing this for two years and we have never once asked anybody to do anything for us and we are not going to start on a Sunday.**”

Then she said the last of it, and it is the sentence this block is built on, and she said it without any performance at all, to a man of about thirty-four with a sheet, because he was the nearest person who had said anything to her.

“**You have all been very careful,**” she said. “**I want you to hear the rest of that sentence, because you are going to go away and hear the first half. You have all been very careful, and there was nobody in nine counties who could have been uncareful, and that is not the same as anybody being careless and it is not a defence either and I am not offering anybody one. I am not saying you were cruel. I am saying it happened. It happened on a Sunday night in a room and it happened to a road and it happened to a boy of about eleven who has a count in his head that nobody has ever written down. And I have not got a word for the other thing, and I have been looking for one since Friday, and I have not found it, and I am not going to make one up, and I would like it understood that I have not found one because there is not one and not because I am slow.**”

Nobody thanked her and nobody was thanked and nobody told her she was right, and the badge-man said that nobody would and that he had tried to prevent about four people in nine counties from being thanked for it in a month and had not managed it, and that being right is not the same as being thanked for and that he would like somebody in the market to write the second one down somewhere with no head over it.

Aren Kest was about forty paces off in the market street with his stick, and he had been about forty paces off since the second hour, and she had known where he was the whole time and had not looked at him and had not come over, and about nine people in that market did not know that and about four did, and nobody said anything about it then or afterwards.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is hers, and four days of carting and a lie told twice to a woman of about sixty who asked her twice and a count nobody will ever hold are not in it and none of them has a price.**

and nothing went down the lane in a coat.
