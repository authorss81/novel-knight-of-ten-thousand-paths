# Chapter 0650

**Greenmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street and then a room over a saddler's, a hearing with no bench in it, about nine people, and a woman of the fruit valley reading a sentence out loud that was never written down.**

The badge-man said the standing notice in the street at about the second hour, and then he said the one thing about it that was not standing, and about nine people heard both.

“**The hearing stands in the street on the first of every month. Today is the twenty-fifth of Greenmonth and today is not the first of a month, and I am saying it anyway, and I am saying it because this is the last morning of a thing that began in this street in Thawmonth and I am not going to let it end on a day nobody comes. There is no bench in it. There has never been a bench in it. That is not a complaint about the furniture.**”

Then he said where it was going, and the woman who keeps the room said it with him and then said the part he had left out.

“**Not the room over the weigh-house,**” she said. “**It is up thirteen stairs and there is a woman of about sixty at the low end of the boards who was told on the first of a month that anybody could come and she is not going up thirteen stairs to be told it again. The room over the saddler's is on this lane and it has no stairs and it has about nine people' worth of floor in it. I have kept the room over a weigh-house for about four years and a half and I am telling nine counties that on this one morning it is not the right room, and nobody is to write that down as a thing about the room.**”

Nobody wrote it down and nobody thanked her.

The hearing went up the outside stair of the saddler's and stood in the inquiry's rooms with about nine people in them, and there was no bench, and there was a floor, and the floor had been washed at some point in about four and a half years by somebody nobody in that room knew and nobody asked about and nobody was thanked for.

A half leaf lay face down on a table at the far end and nobody turned it over and nobody said what was on the other side of it, and nobody in that room could have said how long it had been face down and nobody asked, and there was no arrangement about it in nine counties and there never had been one.

Aren Kest was in the room for about a quarter of an hour with his stick against the frame of the door and his good leg on the boards, and the copyist of about twenty-six brought the register up in her apron and put it on the table beside the leaf, and the register stood at nine questions with no finding under any of them and nothing had gone under any of them in about four years and a half.

Then the woman who keeps the room read the standing of the hearing out loud to about nine people in a room with a door on a lane, and she read it as a person reads a thing she has kept, and not as a person reading somebody else's words.

“**The hearing stands in the street on the first of every month and it has no bench in it and it cannot say that a sheet is false. The book on this table has nine questions in it and no finding under any of them and nothing has gone under any of them in about four years and a half. The fourth line of the outgoing sheet is ruled and it is empty and it is not going to be filled and I am the person who keeps the sheet and I have said that out loud about four times in about a year. The three clauses are in nine hands and there is no copy of anything, and I am not the tenth and I am not going to be. There is no head in nine counties with which to call a meeting, and the man who says that most often has a badge and no head, and that is not a fault in him. Two roads sent writing instead of a person and both letters are on a file in the room over a weigh-house and neither of those roads has been visited. One road went quiet in the middle of nine days in Thawmonth and nothing has come from the end of it since the middle of Mudmonth, and nobody knows which road it is, and nobody has been sent, and nobody is going to be sent. That is all of it. That is what about nine counties have.**”

Nobody thanked her.

Then the badge-man said the thing nobody had said, which was that the meeting was not finished, and he said it in about nine words and then stopped and let the silence be the end of it.

“**It is not finished. There is no rule about who calls the next one and there is no head to call it with, and I am not going to make one, and about four months from now somebody is going to want a thing out of this and there will be nobody to ask. I have thought about that for about a month and I have not got an answer and I am not going to give nine counties a bad one this morning because it is the last morning.**”

Nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and about four people in that room said nothing and about five looked at the floor.

Then he named the reader, and he said where the words had come from and he said it in about nine words and nobody contradicted him.

“**Berta Lomax of the fruit valley has had these words since the seventh of Mudmonth at about the fourth hour in a market street, and nobody gave them to her and nobody sent them to her and nobody wrote them down, and the woman who said them is standing in this room and will say so herself. About nine people hold them. Nobody holds them for anybody else.**”

Then the badge-man gave Aren Kest the standing notice to carry, and did it in the plainest words anybody used all morning, and what he asked for was not a post and he said so.

“**What is it, then,**” the carter said, from the low end of the boards, not meaning it as a question and getting an answer anyway. “**Not a post. I have no post and there is no post. I want you to say the words in nine lanes, one each, in the next two days, standing at a gate, in the open, to whoever opens it. And when somebody asks whether the hearing is on you say that it is on, and when somebody asks you what it is you say that it is a hearing with no bench in it that cannot say a sheet is false, and you say it about nine times and not nine hundred.**”

“**I will do that,**” Aren Kest said.

“**And you are not being given a post by a man with a badge,**” the badge-man said, “**and I will not hear that it is one, and if anybody in nine counties says that the man who can be sent for has been given a post to be sent for, I will stand in this street and say that it is not one, and I have not got a head and a badge is not a head.**”

Nobody thanked either of them.

Then Aren Kest went out of the room and down to the lane and stood at the low end of the counter boards with his stick, and the woman who keeps the room did not look at him and he did not look at her, and about four people in that room noticed and about five did not, and that is the whole of what happened between them in a month and nobody in nine counties has it in a document and nobody in nine counties is going to be given one.

Nobody thanked anybody.

Berta Lomax of the fruit valley stood in the middle of a room with about nine people in it and read the clause about the right to refuse out loud, and she read it slowly, and she read it in the words it was said in, and nobody stopped her and nobody helped her and nobody thanked her.

“**A road may refuse to name a witness. If a road refuses, then nothing happens to that road. The man who said the road might be shut has not shown that it may be shut, and the road is open. No road has to say out loud that it has refused, and nobody is going to be able to get inside it afterwards and find out.**”

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is nobody's, and a hearing with no bench in it and about nine people standing in a room and a sentence that was never written down and a half leaf face down on a table and a floor washed by nobody anybody knows are not in it and none of them has a price.**
