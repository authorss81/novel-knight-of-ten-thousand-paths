# Chapter 0621

**Hearthmonth the twentieth, YR 320, a Friday and not a market morning, the second hour of the night, four days off on a made road, a set of works under an abbey that will take seven, and the third of six going cold on a night.**

The set of works is under an abbey four days off and it is older than the abbey, and it is made of three things and no more: a course of stone laid across a channel, a beam of oak with an iron strap on it, and a length of channel cut by somebody who has been dead a long time and whose name is on nothing.

A man of about thirty-one from a road office had said in a market on the seventh of a month that all seven of the roads go through that one set of works, and Aren Kest had walked out on the eighteenth and looked at it, and nobody sent him and nobody was waiting for him and there is no form in nine counties on which a man can be sent to look at a bed.

He had slept two nights in a barn four days down a made road and gone back up on the nineteenth to the third of the seven, which is the waterworks country, and the keeper of it is a man with a number against his road and a name against the number and it is his job, and the number is old enough that the man who wrote it is dead.

He was there on the twentieth at about the ninth hour of the day and the crossing looked the way it had looked on the eighteenth, and he went on up the made road at midday with a bar of iron in his hand and came back down at dusk because the cart had gone and there was no other reason to be there and no better one.

The third went cold at about the second hour of the night.

It did not go cold the way a road goes cold in an ordinary winter. It went cold in about the time it takes to say a thing twice. He was awake because he was forty yards off and because the sound of the channel changed, and he came back down to the crossing with the lantern and stood on the low side of it for about a minute and a half and then he knew.

The oak beam had not broken. That was the thing he went and looked at first, because that is what a man looks at, and it was sound, and the iron strap on it was sound, and the course of stone under it was where he had left it on the eighteenth with a bar of iron in his hand and a lantern on the stones.

The water was coming over the road instead of under it.

A man of about forty who keeps the third had come out with a lamp and had a boy of about fourteen with him and a dog that did not want to be there. He had been out of his bed and had been out of his house and had put his boots on, and he said the thing first that a keeper says.

“**It is not me,**” he said. “**I have not had a bucket out and I have not had a barrow out and I have not touched it. I was asleep at the first hour and I heard it and I came out.**”

“**I know you have not,**” Aren Kest said, and he said it because the man had said it first and had said it to be believed, and a keeper who has been blamed once for the water in his own channel will say that before he says what the water is doing.

The water was not much. It was a hand's depth at the near edge of the made road and it was moving and it was cold in the way that water from under a sill is always colder than water off a hill, and it was coming out from under the course of stone and going across, and where it went across it began, within about a hundred paces, to find the made road's own camber, and the camber is what a road is.

“**Where is it coming out?**” the man of about forty said, and he did not say *what is wrong* and he did not say *what do we do*, and Aren Kest noticed that and did not use it.

“**Three places,**” Aren Kest said. “**And I can show you two of them in a minute and the third one I cannot show you because it is under the road and the road is made.**”

“**Then show me the two.**”

He showed him the first. It was at the joint between the course of stone and the left abutment, and it was a scour about a foot and a half across and a foot and a half deep at the low side, and the stone at the bottom of it was clean, and the lime above it was clean, and there was nothing in the gap.

“**That is not water wearing stone,**” Aren Kest said. “**Water wears stone round. That is a stone gone from under another stone, and the water found the hole and has been going through it since some time before either of us was asleep.**”

“**Then somebody took it out.**”

“**Somebody took it out,**” Aren Kest said, “**or it went out on its own, and a stone does not go out on its own between two clean joints, and I have said that in a lane at the gate of a market town about two thousand people and a boy of about seventeen counted nine barrows of silt for me there is nobody in nine counties who has ever asked me about a middle course.**”

“**That was a different set of works,**” the man of about forty said, and he was right, and he said it to be helpful, and Aren Kest let it stand because it was true and because a man who corrects you about a culvert two hundred miles off is being kind.

The second was at the far end of the course, where the channel turns, and it was smaller and it was the newer of the two, and it had a different look about it. The first scour had been in it a long time. The second had stones in it that were not from the course and were not from the channel, and they were broken, and one of them had a face on it that had been cut.

“**That one is recent,**” Aren Kest said. “**Within a month, and somebody with a bar and a wedge has been in there, and I am not going to say who and I am not going to say why, and the reason I am not going to is that I do not know and a man who does not know should keep his mouth shut about a thing that can be used against somebody.**”

The man of about forty said that he had a road to look after and that he had four hours of it in front of him, and Aren Kest said that he did, and that he was not going to keep him, and that the man should go and put the water off his road with what he had, and that what he had was enough for the night and not for the morning.

The man of about forty went to put the water off his road. The boy of about fourteen went with him. The dog stayed.

Then Aren Kest sat down on the low parapet of the crossing and did the thing he had come four days to do, which was look at a bed, and he did it properly, on his own, at the second hour of the night, by the light of a hand lantern, and nobody asked him to.

The arithmetic took him about two minutes and it is worth setting down whole because everything that happens in the next nine days is in it.

**The works will take seven. It has taken seven since a year before anybody in the market town was in that room. Seven roads go through it. Seven are on it. Six of them are marked on a page to be held and the seventh is on the page with nothing against it, and the seventh is not on the page at all as a road anybody can weigh, because there is no figure for it, and the man who said that was right and the man who said the seventh is a light thing was wrong, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of what has happened at this crossing tonight.**

The third is the one that has gone cold. The third is not a high flat and it is not a road nobody was watching. It has a number and a name against the number and a man whose job it is and that man is standing four hundred paces up this road with a bar and a bucket doing the thing he can do about it at the third hour of the night. It went because the works is carrying more than it will take, and it went because it was carrying it, and it will go again.

**And the gap did not go first.** That is the part that costs the man of about forty-seven something four days off in a market town and has not cost him anything yet, because nobody has told him. The gap has not gone first. The gap is a road with no figure on it and there is nothing on the page that anybody can be shown to have weighed, and so the gap has sat on the works at no cost to anybody at all, and the marked roads have been carrying the difference, and one of them went tonight, and the ones that did not go tonight will go, and the man of about forty-seven's arithmetic is the reason they went, and it was good arithmetic, and it will be vindicated in about four days in the worst possible way anybody in nine counties has been able to be right.

He had written none of it down. He had not been asked for it and there is nowhere in nine counties to put it, and a room over a weigh-house has three series and none of them is made out of another, and a man of about forty-seven with a page has six weights and a gap, and none of the three of them can take a thing said at the second hour of the night at a crossing four days off.

At about the fourth hour the water came down about two inches and the man of about forty had it off the road and the road was drying and the boy of about fourteen had gone home. The man came back down to the crossing with his lamp and stood on the high side and looked at the water going under again and said that the works had done this before.

“**It has,**” Aren Kest said.

“**It is not the first time,**” the man said. “**A man came through in the autumn and stood about where you are standing and said it would do this, and he said it would do it because of the number of roads on it, and I asked him how many and he said seven, and I said we have always had seven, and he said that was the point and he went off up the road on foot and did not say his name and I did not ask him and I have thought about it since.**”

“**What did you think about it?**”

“**I thought he was counting the seven that go over the high ground and the seven that come through here,**” the man said, “**and I have never been able to make those two sevens be the same seven, and I have had a bar and a bucket and a wet road ever since.**”

“**They are not the same seven,**” Aren Kest said, and he said it because it was the truest thing he had said in two days, and then he stopped, because there is a point past which a man who is on a road alone at the fourth hour of the night becomes a man who is doing a second thing.

The man went up. The dog went with him. The crossing went on taking seven.

**And the cost of that Friday is a column and it is the night's, and a scour about a foot and a half across and a stone that is not from the course and seven roads on one set of works are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a hand lantern at seven, a wick at thirty-three, a tinderbox at twenty-nine, a coil of tarred rope at thirty-seven, a hand axe at two, a wedge at five, a bar of iron at thirty-nine, a chalk line at twenty-one, a bucket of lime water at thirteen, and a sack of sand at twenty-three.** Seven, thirty-three, twenty-nine, thirty-seven, two, five, thirty-nine, twenty-one, thirteen and twenty-three is two hundred and nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and nine pence is four marks and seventeen over**, and one set of works under an abbey, four days off, a made road four days down, seven roads on the works, six lines marked on a page, a seventh line bare, three courses of stone at the crossing, a scour about a foot and a half deep, a beam of oak, an iron strap, a channel cut by somebody dead, about four hundred paces of road above the crossing, two clean joints and a gap between them, and nine counties are fourteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Friday, the second hour of the night, the fourth hour, the ninth hour, midday, dusk, the eighteenth, the nineteenth, the twentieth, two winters ago and a winter before that are eleven figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **Aren Kest and a man of about forty who keeps the third road and a dog and a man of about forty-seven who came through in the autumn, saying seven, giving no name and a man of about thirty-one from a road office who said all seven of the roads go through it and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room and about nine households above the crossing and a man dead a long time whose name is on nothing and a boy of about fourteen are eleven figures of persons, and the second of them said *it is not me* before he said what the water was doing, and the fourth of them is four days off in a market town and does not know yet and will not be told in time, and the first of them is the only person who was ever going to be able to say who put a wedge into a channel and has still not said it.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked and the crossing went on taking seven.
