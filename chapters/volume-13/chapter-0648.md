# Chapter 0648

**Greenmonth the eleventh, YR 321, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards, a question asked on the fourth of nine days and left open on purpose, and the answer given out loud in a street and not softened.**

The badge-man had refused to have that question answered for about a month, and he said in the first minute of that morning that he was answering it now and that he would not be helped, and nine people at the boards heard him say it and five more heard it and went on.

He did not answer it straight away. He had the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned repeat it out loud first, in the words she had used on the fourth of the nine days, and he made her say all of it, and four people heard the whole of it and five heard the first part of it. She said it without any temper in it at all, which was worse than temper, because she had said the same four sentences four times in four different rooms and streets and had not improved them.

Then he answered, and he answered in about four minutes, and he did not write anything down and he did not ask anybody's opinion first.

“**Yes. It is dangerous, and I am not going to say that it is dangerous in a small way, and I have thought about it since the fourth of a month and I have not slept much of it. Here is the danger, and it is not the one everybody in this street is thinking of. The danger is not that a road says no. A road saying no is the only thing in those three clauses that has ever been a choice, and a choice that is written down is worth something to about nine people on a road and to nobody else on it. Here is the danger. In about four months a man is going to do something he is not allowed to do. And if a road can refuse, then that man will be able to stand in front of nine counties and say the road refused. And then nobody will be able to reach inside the refusal to find out whether that road refused, or whether that road was asked in a way that it could not say no to, and I know the difference between those two things and nobody in nine counties is going to be able to, and I have watched about four men in thirty-one years get away with exactly that.**”

Nobody in that market said anything.

The market went on saying it, though. A carter put a load down about nine paces off and made more noise than the four minutes had. Somebody laughed about a quarter of the way off, once, at nothing, and the woman of about thirty-six turned her head about a quarter of the way and the laughing stopped, and afterwards nobody could say whether it had been at nothing. The sun was on the boards. A barrow-load of onions went past at the low end and the man who had it put a hand up to the badge-man without stopping and the badge-man put a hand back, and that was the whole of the acknowledgment and it went on being the whole of it for the rest of the morning.

Aren Kest heard all of it, and he heard the man in the sentence, and he was the man in the sentence. It had taken him four minutes to get there and it had taken him about four months to be somebody who could be described that way at all. He had not slept much of it either, and not for the reason the badge-man gave, and he was not going to say so in a market street, and he understood while standing there with his stick against the edge of the platform that not saying so was not innocence, and that a man who lets nine counties hear a true thing about the shape of a crime and does not put himself in it has not been let out of anything at all.

What the badge-man had just given away, and had given away on purpose and in the open, was the only thing Aren Kest had ever wanted: somebody in nine counties to say out loud that there was a shape a man could hide inside, before a man got inside it. He had wanted that since Thawmonth. He had not known until about the second minute that morning that it was going to arrive, and it had arrived, and it had arrived without anybody naming him in it, which meant that the shape was available to about four hundred people and he was still nobody. He found that he was glad of it. He found, about four minutes later, walking back down the lane to fetch his coat, that he was not glad of it at all, and that both of those were true and that a street like this would hold both of them without picking.

“**And I have wanted to put something in a rule about that since the fourth of a month, and I am not going to, and I am telling this street that I wanted to and did not, because I would like nine counties to be able to say afterwards that the man who had thirty-one years and a badge sat here and had an answer available and left it out on purpose. That is the first thing I have wanted to put in a rule in thirty-one years that I have not put in. You may write that down. I am saying it out loud so that about nine of you are the ones who have it and not one clerk with a column.**”

Nobody wrote it down and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet had his hands in his coat.

Then the badge-man answered the other question, the one about the road that cannot be written to, and he answered it in about a minute and he did not pretend it was an answer.

“**The second thing. A road went quiet in the middle of the nine days and nothing has come from the end of it since, and nine counties have spent about a month finding out that they cannot find it and have no way to find it and have no list that would help them find it. That is the whole of what has happened. Nine people in nine counties now know a road went quiet. That is not a rule and it is not a fix and it is not the road and the road has not come back and nobody in this street is going to go and see. I have said that here before and I am saying it here again on a market morning at the second hour, and the woman of about thirty-six is standing about nine paces from me and she has been saying the same thing for a month and she is right, and it is not a small hole, and I will not have it called one in this street.**”

Nobody said it was a small one and nobody told her she was right and nobody thanked him.

The man of about forty-seven with a page stood at the back of the boards with his hands behind him and said, out loud, that the answer was the worst thing he had heard in about a year and that he had wanted a rule that was wrong, and that this was not that either, and that a man who wanted a rule that was wrong had not understood what a rule is, and that he was glad of it in a way he had not expected to be.

He had come to that market morning with a page in his coat that would have settled the whole of it, and about four people in that street knew that, and nobody in that street knew it as well as he did. He stood at the back and put his hand flat on the front of the coat, about as high as the pocket, and left it there for a while, and then took it away, and said one more thing to about nine people and to the boards.

“**I have been right five times and I have been wrong once and the once was a road. I came here this morning with a thing in this coat that would let anybody in nine counties put a number against a road and call it a load, and it is a good piece of arithmetic and I have never had a moment's doubt in it. And I have taken it out twice today and put it back twice, and the second time I did it I was not deciding anything. I was finding out whether a rule could stand up in a street without a page in the room, and it cannot, and I have known that for about four months and I did not want it.**”

Then he said the thing he had said every time, which was that a rule that could not be reached inside could not be checked, and that this was a hole and the hole was the size of a road, and that he was not going to be the man who said out loud in nine counties that the finding of that Thursday was a failure, because the finding of that Thursday was that nine people knew more than they had known in Thawmonth, and that is a thing you cannot pay for and cannot do twice.

Nobody thanked him.

Then the woman of about thirty-six said the thing that the last four minutes had been for, and she said it standing in the sun with her hands at her sides and she said it to about nine people and to the street and to nobody in particular.

“**I asked whether a road can refuse to name a witness, and I answered my own question and I was not answered, and I have been not answered for about a month and I want to say one more thing about it and then I want to stop. If a road can refuse and nothing happens to it, then the right to refuse is not a favor you do to a road. It is the only thing in those three clauses that is the road's own. And the three clauses do not say so. They get there by a road being unusable, and a road being unusable is not the same as a road saying no, and I have been the one saying that for about a month and about nine people have heard me.**”

“**What would you have put in it?**” she said.

The badge-man said that he was not going to answer that in a street, and that she did not want an answer in a street either, and that both of them had had about a month on it and had come to the same place from two directions, and that this was not a thing anybody was going to be thanked for.

Then he said that her four sentences were the answer, and that it was a better answer than the one he had, and that he was not going to improve on it, and that he was not going to put it in a clause, because if it went in a clause then it went in nine hands and a person could be asked whether a road said no, and the whole of what he had just said in this street for about four minutes was that nobody was ever going to be able to reach inside one.

Four people in that market said nothing and five went on to their business, and the woman of about thirty-six said that then somebody ought to read it out loud, and not in a clause, and she said where.

“**In a room,**” she said. “**Not in a street and not in a lane. In a room with a door on it, with about nine people standing in it, and a person reading it out loud so that it is a thing that has been said and not a thing that has been written. And the room is shut on every day but a market morning, so it will have to be a market morning, and I am not asking for a market morning. I am telling you that a thing said in a street is heard by whoever is in the street and a thing said in a room is heard by whoever is in the room, and that about nine people in a room is a thing that nine counties can hold, and that this is the only favour I am going to ask anybody for in about twenty years.**”

Nobody answered her at once and then the badge-man said that the room was shut on any day but a market morning and that he was not going to have it opened, and that if it was going to be said in a room then it would be said in a room on a market morning, and that there was one of those coming.

Aren Kest stood at the low end of the boards through all four minutes of it with his stick against the edge of the platform and his good leg on the stones, and about nine people in that market looked at him at some point between the second minute and the fourth, and nobody asked him anything, and he did not say anything, and that was the whole of what happened to him in that street, and he has still not been asked and he did not answer.

Then the badge-man said the finding of that Thursday out loud so that about nine people had it and not one clerk, and it was about nine words and it was the opposite of what anybody expected.

“**We have thought about it and it is worse and not better. That is what four months of thinking has bought. Nothing is fixed. About nine people know more and about nine people are less comfortable, and one of them is going to be asked in about four months whether a road refused, and none of the three clauses can help him, and I would not put it in one. I would rather nine counties found out at the end that we had not thought about it than that I had thought about it for them and got it wrong in a document.**”

Nobody thanked him and the meeting was not finished and the register stood at nine questions with no finding under any of them, and the road that is here and unknown sent nothing, and nobody had been to it and nobody was going to be sent.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is nobody's, and four minutes in a market street and a refusal to put an answer in a rule and a request for a room with about nine people in it are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a barrow of broken stones at 9, a coil of chalk line at 23, a bundle of hazel rods at 30, a pair of shears at 31, a sheet of oiled paper at 35, a rushlight holder at 37, a bag of nails at 38, a water can at 39, a trestle at 40, and a hanglock at 41.** Nine, twenty-three, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-five, thirty-seven, thirty-eight, thirty-nine, forty and forty-one is three hundred and twenty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and three hundred and twenty-three pence is six marks and thirty-five over.** The finding went out of the street in about nine mouths and into nine counties inside a week, with nothing on a piece of paper about it anywhere, and about a week later a man in a town nine counties off was asked by a person who had been in that street what had been decided, and said, we were told it is worse, and the person who had been in that street said yes, and neither of them could have said why, and that is what a rule in fragments is, on the day it is first said.
