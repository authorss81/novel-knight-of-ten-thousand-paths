# Chapter 0603

**Wolfmonth the sixth, YR 320, a Monday and not a market day, the second hour at the counter under the weigh-house where the parish rolls come in, two sheets about the same four miles of channel, three weeks apart, and two books.**

The room over the weigh-house is shut on a Monday and the copyist of about twenty-six was at the counter under it, because the parish rolls come in off the roads on whatever day the carts come and the carts do not consult a market. About four hundred and nine of them came in that month, and they are a series of their own, and they go into the roll book and nowhere else, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room stood at the other end of the counter with the band because the band has to be reconciled with the rolls and the band came in on a Friday and has been sitting in a drawer for two days waiting for somebody with the authority to open a drawer.

The copyist found the first of the two at about the second hour, in the roll book, at a leaf between two returns about a boundary stone.

**The top four miles of a channel had gone cold.** The waterworks country wrote it in the plain hand of a man who keeps a channel and does not write like a clerk. He said the water was going round and not through, that it had begun at the upper weir and was now a day and a half down, that a mill below the fourth mile had lost a day of grinding in a fortnight, and that anybody who said the same four miles was carrying should be asked for a figure and for a date. He gave a date. He gave no figure.

The second of the two was in the drawer with the returns, where a returned sheet goes when a district sends one back, and it had come back three weeks after the first, on the nineteenth of a month, and it was in the plain hand of a man who keeps a coast road and writes like a clerk because he has been taught to. **The same four miles of the same channel were carrying and had carried all year.** It said that anybody who said otherwise should be asked for a figure, and then it said the thing that made the copyist read it a second time, which was: *and we have none, and we have said so twice.*

The copyist read both and then put the roll book down and did not open the drawer again for about a quarter of an hour.

“**Those are the same four miles,**” she said.

The woman of about twenty-nine put down the band.

“**Read me the dates,**” she said.

“**The ninth and the nineteenth,**” the copyist said. “**Ten days apart in the writing and three weeks apart in the books, and they are both true in their own hands and neither one is a lie, and one of them has to be a lie, and I cannot tell you which.**”

Nobody in that room said which, and the woman of about twenty-four who keeps the key was not in that room, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet was standing at the far end of the counter with a sheet of his own and had heard the whole of it and had not come over.

Then the copyist said the thing that is the whole of that morning, and she said it to the counter and not to anybody, in the voice of a woman reading out a practice.

“**It is because we do not derive one from the other,**” she said. “**Three series. About nine hundred sheets a month out into nine counties, about a hundred and four in one band on a fixed day, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month in off the roads, and not one of the three is made out of the other two, and that is not an accident and it is not tidiness. It is so that one bad return cannot be set beside one good return and made to look like a pattern. That is the rule and I have kept it for four years and I will keep it on Monday morning or any other.**”

“**Then you have just told me the rule is the problem,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

“**I have told you the rule is a wall,**” the copyist said, “**and a wall is what you put a road behind, and I have been standing with my back to this one for four years and two of the sheets are on it.**”

The woman of about twenty-nine did not argue, and there was nothing to argue with, and the copyist was right and the practice was right and the two of those facts sat about two feet apart on a counter under a weigh-house and neither of them moved.

“**Say what follows,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

“**That a road can be two things for three weeks,**” the copyist said, “**and everybody in this room is forbidden from noticing.**”

“**And the mill?**”

“**The mill has lost a day of grinding in a fortnight if the first sheet is true, and the mill is four days off and has not written to us, and the road office has not asked it, and the road office will ask it if somebody puts the two sheets in one hand, and nobody in this room is permitted to put the two sheets in one hand.**”

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key was not in the room and came out to the counter at about the fifth hour with the key in her hand because the counter is under the same door. She said one thing, and she said it to the copyist and not to the woman of about twenty-nine, and she said it once.

“**Aren Kest and the keeper of this room were in the lane together on Saturday morning,**” she said, “**and they were not in the lane together on Thursday morning, and the hearing stood on both, and nine people came on Thursday and about nine came on Saturday, and nobody has said why, and I am not asking.**”

Nobody in that lane answered her, and she went back in with the key, and the copyist did not write it down, and the woman of about twenty-nine did not say that the woman of about thirty-four was wrong.

The rest of that morning went as mornings go. The band was reconciled. The about four hundred and nine rolls were put away. The register stood at seven entries, all of them questions and none of them findings, and the day-book stood at nine, and the press stood open with no date anybody would give it, and the fourth head of the Crown's form was ruled and empty on a side table in a shut room, and the half leaf lay face down where it has lain since the sixth of Mudmonth, and nobody turned it over and nobody had booked anybody to turn it over.

At the seventh hour the copyist took the first sheet out of the roll book and the second sheet out of the drawer and laid them on the counter side by side, writing up, which is not a practice.

“**This is not a practice,**” she said.

“**No,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said.

“**I have done it once,**” the copyist said, “**and I am not going to do it again on a Monday, and they go back in the drawer tomorrow, and neither of us is going to say what tomorrow is.**”

The two sheets lay on that counter until the light went off the boards, and nobody thanked the copyist for laying them down, and nobody asked the woman of about twenty-nine what tomorrow was, and the sheets went back into two different books at the seventh hour because there was nowhere else in that room for them to go.

**And the cost of that Monday is a column and it is the woman of about twenty-nine's, and two sheets about the same four miles and a rule that has been kept for four years are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a roll book at five, a bundle cord at eight, an inkhorn at ten, a waxed string at twenty-five, a scraper at thirty-four, a file at thirty-seven, a cake of ink at thirty-nine, and a sheet of foolscap at forty.** Five, eight, ten, twenty-five, thirty-four, thirty-seven, thirty-nine and forty is one hundred and ninety-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and ninety-eight pence is four marks and six over**, and about four miles of channel, two ends of a channel, nine counties, about nine hundred sheets a month, about a hundred and four in one band, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month, a drawer about eleven inches deep, about four hundred returns, a register of seven questions, a day-book of nine entries, a fourth head ruled and empty, four hands of writing and about nine households at one end and about nine at the other are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and a Monday, the second hour, the fourth hour, the seventh hour, about seven months, four and a half years, about three weeks and two days are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A copyist of about twenty-six and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man who keeps a channel and does not write like a clerk and a man who keeps a coast road and does and a mill four days off and about four hundred people who hold one true thing about a man with a limp are nine figures of persons, and the eighth of them has not been asked for anything and the ninth of them has never written to this room and neither of them is in this column.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was thanked.
