# Chapter 0631

**Thawmonth the third, YR 321, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street and the third hour in a room over a weigh-house, and nine people who had not met before.**

The man of about fifty-eight with a badge had no way to call a meeting and said so out loud in the market street at the second hour, to nobody in particular, in the middle of a market doing what a market does.

He said it plainly, the way he says things. He said that nine counties had had a road taken off a set of works in a room with no head over it, and that no head in nine counties had ordered it, and that the room over the weigh-house was the only body in those nine counties that a thing could be brought to, and that he was going to ask whether the room would hold a rule instead of a decision, and that he had about four hours before the market packed up.

About nine people heard him. About four of them cared, and one of those four was a man of about fifty-two who keeps a road and had come into the market for a sack and stayed for the rest of it, and about four people in nine counties would have been enough if the four were the right four and he did not know yet whether they were.

The room over a weigh-house was open that morning because it is open at the second hour on a market morning and shut at the seventh and shut on every other day of the week. The copyist of about twenty-six had the returns out on the table in the ordinary way, about nine hundred sheets a month going out into nine counties and about a hundred and four in one band on a fixed day and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month coming in off the roads, and none of the three is made out of another and she has never once let anybody suggest it.

The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room came up the outside stair at about a quarter past the second hour and stood in the doorway with her hand flat on the frame.

“**It is a records room,**” she said. “**And you have said a thing in a street. That is your own business and I am not going to tell a man of fifty-eight what he may say in a street, and I will tell you what this room is. It has a door on a lane and a number and a deed of thirteen words and it can be sued and it can sue, and there is not one other thing about it. It is shut on every day but a market morning and I am not going to have it opened for nine people on a Tuesday because nine people asked nicely.**”

“**I did not ask you to open it on a Tuesday,**” the badge-man said. “**I am asking you to sit down on a Saturday with the door open and let about nine people say a thing out loud where anybody on the boards can hear it. That is the whole of what I have come for and I have wanted it for thirty-one years and I have never asked before because I never had a reason that nine counties could check.**”

Then the copyist of about twenty-six put down the sheet she was sorting and asked him the question, and she asked it in front of about nine people because she does that.

“**Why a street?**” she said. “**You have been in this work thirty-one years and you have never once put a notice up anywhere. What has happened to you?**”

“**A head has, and it is the ordinary thing,**” he said. “**A head is the only thing in nine counties that can make a person come, and there is not one I am allowed, so a street is what is left. I said it out loud on a boards where anybody can hear me and I put no name at the top of it and I will not put a name at the top of anything, and if a thing has got my name on it and no one to be sent to, then it is not a notice, it is a road with a hole in it.**”

“**Then why did you not say it at a gate?**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**Because a gate is where a man reads a thing out and nine people hear him and the man is still standing there afterwards,**” the badge-man said. “**I have got no reason to be stood in front of. I have got a lane about four hundred paces long and a market that finishes at the ninth hour and about four of those people out of about nine would come up a stair. That is not enough either and it is what there is.**”

Nobody thanked him for the arithmetic and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote two lines on his own sheet and did not show them to anybody.

“**You have one now,**” she said, from the doorway, and she had been standing there through the whole of it. “**And I am going to say the other thing before anybody sits down, and I am going to say it once. The register is at nine. Nine questions and not one finding under any of them, and the ninth has been standing unanswered for about a month, and the ninth is whether a room with a door on a lane and a number may hold a thing decided in a room with no head over it, and I answered it and I answered it correctly. I am not going to be handed a second question before the first one has been dealt with by somebody.**”

Nobody said anything to that for a moment, and the copyist of about twenty-six said it instead.

“**And I will keep the three series,**” she said, “**and I will not merge them and I will not derive one out of another and I will not write a rule in a book of questions. I will read out in this room, with the door open, anything that is written and sent to this room, and it will go nowhere else, and if a man in nine counties wants to know what was said here he can come up a stair on a market morning and hear it said again.**”

That is what she said, and it is what she has said before, and the room had about nine people in it by the fourth hour.

They came up the stair in ones and twos over about two hours, and the reason they came was not the badge-man, who is not a man anybody trusts to call a thing, and it was not the room, which is a room, and it was a sentence one of them had heard in a street from a man with a badge and had carried four days.

The first was the man of about fifty-two, and he would not say which road he kept.

“**I keep a road,**” he said. “**I am not going to say which one, and I want that written down as a thing I said and not as a thing I was asked for, because you did not ask me and I am not going to be asked twice.**”

“**Why not?**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**Because the moment one of you has the name of it, you have a road with a name against it,**” the man of about fifty-two said, “**and everything that happens to that road from then on gets argued about on a piece of paper instead of on the ground where it happens. I have watched it happen to two other roads. I would rather be a person in a room than a name on a list.**”

“**That is the best argument anybody has made in this room in about four and a half years,**” the man of about forty-seven with a page said, and nobody thanked the man of about fifty-two for it and nobody thanked the man of about forty-seven for saying so.

The second was a man of about fifty-one who keeps a road in the glass country on the high flats, and he had come two days and had a map he did not put on the table, and the third was a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a road in the county where a list of names was burned, and she was the only one of the three who came with a paper of her own and the paper was nine lines long and it was not about her road.

Then the two letters.

They came up the outside stair in the same band as the returns, because a thing that is written and posted goes with the post and the post does not care what is in it, and the copyist took both off the top of the band and put them face up on the table without opening them, and then stood there with her hands flat on either side of them, and about nine people looked at two pieces of paper and did not touch them.

“**Those are from roads,**” she said, “**and there are seven roads and five of you have spoken and there are two that have not, and I have not opened either of these and I am not going to, because the moment I read one of them out this room becomes the place where two roads have been asked a question, and this room has asked nothing.**”

“**Then let them speak for themselves,**” the man of about fifty-two said.

“**They are writing,**” she said. “**That is them speaking for themselves and it is the only kind that has ever counted in this room. It will be the sixth morning, on a market morning, and the door will be open, and I will read them out, and about nine people can stand on the boards and hear.**”

The woman of about fifty, who keeps a road nineteen years and had come in without sitting down, said the thing that made the morning into a decision.

“**No closure without local notice,**” she said, “**in the road's own words, and a human witness who can be named and found, and the burden of proof on the person who proposes it. That is three things and I have said them out loud once. I am not saying it is right. I am saying that if nine people go out of that door with three sentences instead of none then something has been got that has not been got in about four and a half years, and the burden clause is the one I would not give up, because a burden on the man who proposes is the only clause anybody has ever managed to write that says a road is somebody's business.**”

Nobody thanked her. The badge-man wrote the three sentences on the back of a sheet he did not sign and read them out twice in the street afterwards to anybody who wanted them, and about four people wanted them and about five did not.

Aren Kest was at the low end of the counter boards for about an hour of that morning with his stick and went up no stair, and the copyist noted his name in her own head and not in a book, and he was told about the two letters on the sixth morning and about the three sentences on the fourth and about neither of those things until they were already in other people's mouths.
