# Chapter 0096

The twenty-second of Greenmonth was a Monday and it was not a market day, and the disclosure was read into a record at the second hour of the afternoon in a room over a weigh-house, and it took four minutes, and after it there were two things on the same table and they had not been on the same table in any room in this county before.

The disclosure is in Halven's own hand. It is four paragraphs and it is dated the fifth of Embermonth, YR 312, at the tenth hour, and it was made on his own motion and not on a fee, and both of those are in it in his own words because he asked for them to be in his own words.

*I am registrar of the fourth press in a provincial office two hundred miles from this county and I have been in it eleven years. On the fifth of Embermonth I sold a volume. The volume was entered among two hundred and nine other volumes in the fourth press and it was not on its shelf. It is the schedule book of the anchors, schedules and dispositions of a crossing in this kingdom, and its removal is dated the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, and there is no name against the removal and no office and no authority and no destination and no fee and no initial, and I have known it was gone for six years and I have said nothing for six years.*

*I sold it to an officer of that office for an endorsement. The endorsement discharges a writ of enquiry on the records of the reeve of this town, returnable the ninth of Rainmonth, and it is numbered and it is dated the fourth of Embermonth, and it is on the printed instruction that carries a form of a series, and I took it in the room in which I have worked for eleven years and I did not take a fee and I have asked that this sentence be the first one anybody reads of this.*

*What is in the volume is not a person's name. It is a schedule for three bells on a crossing in this kingdom, of which two are entered removed with no office against either, and the third is entered retained, and the retained one is on a bridge four hundred yards up a road in this county and the bell in it was cracked in the mouth before I was born. And it is a schedule of dispositions, and the county column of the dispositions runs past that valley to eleven other counties, and against one of the eleven counties the disposition word is a word this county does not use for anything else.*

*I am not going to say the word. I have said it in a room once, to two people, four days after I made this, and one of them wrote it in a book and one of them did not, and I have not been able to find out since which of them did.*

**And there is nothing in those four paragraphs about whether he should have taken it, and there is nothing in them about the year, and the date is his own and the hour is his own and the hand is his own, and he wrote all of that in a room in a provincial town before he knew what he was going to need.**

---

**The second thing on the table was the endorsement, and it was produced out of a wrapper and it has a number on it, and neither of those two things is a name, and that is why they fit on one page.**

*Memorandum of endorsement. Fourth of Embermonth, YR 312. Office: the marshal's emergency office. The writ of enquiry in the matter of the records of the reeve of Merefen, returnable the ninth of Rainmonth, is discharged. The officer named as receiving the volume has performed the condition, and the endorsement is not to be withdrawn, there being no procedure in this office for the withdrawal of an endorsement. Number: 4 of the season.*

Four of the season. It is a number in a series of numbers and the series is kept in a press in a provincial town and there is nothing in the world that says whose hand wrote the fourth, and there are about nine other numbers in the same series in the same press.

**Halven was in the room, and he had asked to be, and he came two hundred miles on the county post at his own expense on the eighteenth, and he was not a witness to anything, and he was there because he is a man who put four paragraphs in a book in August of last year and has not been asked about them since.**

He is sixty-one. He stood by the press for about an hour and a quarter and nobody asked him one question, and he counted, twice, in his own head, and Mara Vey saw him doing it.

"You are waiting for a question," she said.

"I am," said Halven.

"There is not going to be one."

"I know there is not going to be one," he said, "and I have known since the fifth of Embermonth and I have come two hundred miles anyway, and I would like that entered, because a man who has spent six months waiting to be asked about a thing and then is not asked has to be able to prove that he waited."

"Entered," said Mara Vey, and entered it, with the hour, against a person, "and I am entering the reason as well, because the reason is the document. You did it on your own motion and not on a fee, and you have wanted since the fifth of Embermonth that there should be one document in the world in which it says you did it. **That is not a want anybody in this county can give you. There is no instrument in this kingdom that can put a man in a book about his own act without something else in the book being about somebody else.** I can enter that you asked to be here. I cannot enter that you are the reason there is a page."

"Then enter that I asked to be here," said Halven, "and let the other part of it be the reason I asked."

And Sarn Oyelaran, who had not said anything for an hour and a quarter, looked at him for about a moment and then went back to her copy, and what she thought about it is not in this room and is not in any book, and is hers.

---


**The two documents together are the first thing in this case in which the Crown's shield and the Crown's payment for a book are in one paragraph, and that is not an accident of arrangement. It happened because two officers of the same office, in the same year, at two desks about nine miles apart, each did the ordinary thing their own desk did, and one of them did not know the other existed.**

---

Mara Vey entered it as a copy, with the hour, in the ordinary form, and she entered his reason, and she entered the fact that the office has no power either to compel a man to disclose or to protect him when he has, and then she stopped, and the pen stayed about a quarter of an inch above the paper for about four seconds, and then she put it down and wrote the time again against the last line, and nothing else.

"You have stopped," said Sarn Oyelaran.

"I have stopped because I have got to the end of what I can enter as a fact and I have reached the part where a keeper of records is going to have an opinion about a man, and I am not going to have it in this room."

"You have an opinion."

"I have four," said Mara Vey. "One of them is that he is a coward and one of them is that he is the only person in eleven years who has done anything about a thing, and they are not the same size, and that is exactly why neither of them is going in here. **The not writing is entered, and the not writing is the part that is a decision, and I have entered that four times this year and this is the fifth and it is the first one about a decision I would have made myself.**"

"Then what is the fifth decision," said Alish Renn.

"It is not entered," said Mara Vey, "and you will find out what it was in about four years, and so will he, and I have known since the eighth of Embermonth that I was not going to be the one who says it out loud."

---

**Alish Renn is thirty and she is a second copyist in that office and she has a warrant of her own, and the warrant is the reason she was in the room at all, and nobody wanted her in the room.**

The warrant was signed by Mara Vey in the ninth month of this year, in Wolfmonth, in her own hand, with the date on it, and it is a piece of paper that says that Alish Renn may make a copy of any record held in the Bellweather Claims Archive, and **it is not entered in the press.** It is in a day-book, against a person, with the hour on it, and it is the third warrant of her own she has signed in four years and it is the first one she has signed for a copyist rather than a keeper, and she entered that fact at the time and entered that she did not know why she had done it.

"A warrant is a permit," said Alish Renn, "and a permit that is not in the press is a thing a person has in a drawer, and in about four years one of us is going to be asked in a room whether a woman of thirty had authority to copy a page, and the answer is going to be that she had a piece of paper, and the piece of paper is going to be in a day-book in a room with a door on a lane over a weigh-house in a fen, and anybody can be got at that day-book, because anybody can come up the stairs."

"Nobody can serve the day-book."

"A man with a pair of shears and forty minutes could take a leaf out of it," said Alish Renn, "and he would not need to serve anything, and that is how half the things in this kingdom have gone missing. **A day-book is not a file, and a file is not a record, and a record is not a roll.**"

"Go on," said Mara Vey.

"That is what I have to say and I have been trying to say it for two months and I have not been in a room with a person who would let me finish a sentence. A file is a place where a fact about a person goes, and it can be produced, and a court can order it, and the person it is about is supposed to be able to see it. A record is a place where a fact goes, and it can be produced, and the person it is about is not supposed to be able to see it and often is not told. **A roll is a place where a fact about a person goes and nobody can take it out and everybody can add to it.**"

"Which of the three is this office."

"**That is the question I have been trying to ask for two months,**" said Alish Renn, "and I have been a copyist in it for two years and I have copied about nine hundred leaves out of the press and I could not tell you which of the three it is, and I am not going to be able to tell you by looking, and the only way anybody in this county is ever going to know is if somebody with a claim comes in and finds out which one we are. And I am a woman of thirty copying a register because the register is the only work in this county that I can be got into, and I have got a bar on it, and I want to be a keeper with a post and my name in the day-book, and I am telling you that in a room with a keeper in it because there is no other room."

Sarn Oyelaran said, quietly, "I have a key."

"You have a key," said Mara Vey, "and a warrant for a third post that has been in the day-book since the sixteenth of Greenmonth, YR 312, and I have not signed it and I have been asked about it four times by people who wanted it signed."

"You did not sign it because of me."

"I did not sign it because there is one room and two rooms' work and a third post makes three rooms' work in one room, and a woman of twenty-six with a key who is doing three rooms' work in one room is a woman who will leave, and this office has had four people in four years and a half and it cannot have five." She squared the two documents. "And the other reason, which is the one that is actually the reason, and which I have entered in a day-book and not here: **a person with a post is a person who can be asked whether she should have taken an endorsement, and I am not going to be the person who puts that question to a woman of twenty-six in her second year.**"

Alish Renn looked at her for about three seconds.

"That is a kind thing to say and it is not a kind thing to do," she said.

"No," said Mara Vey. "It is both. Go and copy the second leaf and do not improve my spelling of the county's name."

---

**Nobody is charged.**

There is no instrument that charges him, and there was never going to be, and the reason is a sentence Halven said out loud in this room in August to three people and did not have minuted, and which Sarn Oyelaran was in the room for and wrote down in her own hand on the back of a sheet and has never shown to anybody:

*There is nothing to charge. He did a thing that this office wanted done. A man may be sorry and may also have committed no offence, and the two of those are the ordinary condition of everybody in this building, and if we begin to charge the ones who are sorry then in about two years we will have charged the ones who are not sorry as well, because the ones who are not sorry are easier to find and they are cheaper to try.*

**The endorsement stands. It is not withdrawn, and it is not going to be, because the office that gave it has no procedure for withdrawing an endorsement, and it will not build one inside a quarter, and the clerk who would have to build it has four desks.**

The register still has a gap in it on the shelf at the foot, and the gap is on the roll, and the gap is dated the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, with no name against it, and a copyist of twenty-eight with a grandmother's habit of copying a whole leaf once put that in a fair copy in a bag out of a provincial office two hundred miles off, unasked, along with a leaf nobody had requested, and neither document has been produced anywhere and both of them have been in a press in Merefen since the eighth of Embermonth.

And in the day-book, that evening, at about the eighth hour, the last line of that day, and it is entered against no person and it is the sixth time this year that the not writing has been entered, and the reason is not going to be written out again:

*Twenty-second of Greenmonth, YR 313, a Monday, the eighth hour. Halven's disclosure entered as a copy, with the hour, and his reason entered, and the fact entered that this office has no power to compel and none to protect. No opinion entered. The endorsement is not withdrawn and this office has no procedure for requiring that it be. Nobody is charged. Entered against no person.*

*I have written the fifth decision down nowhere. I have had it since the eighth of Embermonth. It is the best decision I have ever made and it is going to cost this office something and I am not going to pretend in a day-book that the not writing is a thing I did for the good of anybody.*
