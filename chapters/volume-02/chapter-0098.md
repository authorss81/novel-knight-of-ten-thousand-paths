# Chapter 0098

The twenty-sixth of Greenmonth was a Friday and it was not a market day, and a fair copy of a minute came down a line into a room over a weigh-house, and it is eleven lines long and it is in the hand of a clerk of an assizes in a town that is not on any map in this kingdom, and it is the answer to the second answer, and the list was not burned.

**The list is not burned. It is superseded.**

That is the whole of the twenty-sixth of Greenmonth and it is the only thing anybody wanted out of eleven months and four hundred miles and the fourth of a month in a year nobody here keeps.

The instrument is withdrawn by the county's own bench, in a room, with the physician's four-year-old finding read out and the three hands named, and the superseding is entered as a fact and not as a defeat, and the reason it is not a defeat is that the magistrate says so in the room, in his own words, and there is a fair copy of his own words in the hand of a clerk of that assizes, and it is four sentences long and it is the only part of the minute anybody in Merefen will read in nine years.

*The list was the only instrument I had. I have had it eleven months and I have had a physician's four pages in the next room since the ninth month of the year before last and I have not acted on them, and I am glad to be shown a second instrument and I am sorry that it took four years and a stranger, and I want the county to hear the word stranger from me and not from him, because he is a man off a road and I am the magistrate of this county and it is my county and it is not his.*

And then, in the same four sentences, the rest of it, and it is the part that is on the page and the part that is going to be argued about for nine years:

*I am not sorry about the fever. The physician is right about the fever and he was right four years ago and I have had both of those in the same room and only one of them was true in the way I needed. A list that is not read cannot carry the softening and I was not going to lose this county in the spring to a thing I did not understand. I was wrong about the names and I have never once been wrong about the clay.*

---

**The three hands are named in the minute, and the third is still a letter, and the bench has entered that it cannot say whether the third is a hand and has resolved not to find out this year.**

*First: the rollkeeper's office at the assizes, which is one room in one building and does not move and has not moved in nineteen years.*

*Second: the hand of the Ninebridge, which is a tile-cutter and keeps the road and is a person the county can stand at the end of a line and has been asked about and has never been asked.*

*Third: a press belonging to a creditor's office in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, entered as a transaction.*

*A person is restored when two of the three agree, and a person is removed when two of the three agree otherwise, and the third is allowed to be wrong. The bench has entered that it is unable to say whether the third is a hand and has resolved not to find out this year.*

**Nobody in either country resolves the third hand and nobody in either country is going to.**

---

**And the rest of it, in the same fair copy, in the same clerk's flat hand, with the columns of a county that counts fires in them.**

*Two hundred and fifty-four fired tiles, broken by hand and the clay wedged back, over two years, at a cost of twelve marks one shilling and sevenpence, in the season this county calls the standing water, being the time between the second low water and the next first firing. Thirteen men, five days, at five pence the day. Nine cords of wood for the proof rolling at the kiln gate, two shillings and sixpence the cord.*

*The softening has slowed and has not stopped. Six boards in the last nine days against about forty in the nine before, at a rate nobody in this county would call a rate and the clerk of the roll has entered the six and the forty with the hour on them and no opinion.*

*Ash's dead stay Ash's. There is no claim upon them and there is going to be none and the minute says so in one line and the one line is the fourth shortest thing in the county's own book.*

That last sentence is a thing a man four hundred miles off wrote in a fair copy, and Mara Vey read it four times, and then she put the sheet down on a long table in a room over a weigh-house and put both hands flat on it, which is not a thing a keeper does, and which Sarn Oyelaran and Alish Renn and a man from the county town's clerk's office all saw and none of them mentioned afterwards.

"It is the only thing in this case that anybody has got right that nobody asked for," she said.

---

**The physician's four pages did not come with it, and nobody expected them to, and the minute says that they were read out, and a minute that says a thing was read out is a fact about a room four hundred miles off and not a document in this one.**

She had two documents about that county on the table and neither of them was the four pages.

The first was a fair copy of four lines in her own hand, copied exactly and in order, with the hour, entered against no person about six months ago at the eleventh hour, off a leaf that came in an outer room with a man who wanted to be recorded and was not recorded, and it is the reason the only copy of it exists in the world.

*1. There is a man here who fires at the kiln at Kilnholt and was struck off the roll of persons eleven months ago. 2. He has not gone anywhere. He is at his kiln. 3. He cannot be found because a man who is struck cannot be asked anything. 4. Tell us what a record owes a man who is in one and not in it.*

The second was a fair copy of a minute of a bench, of a rule with no office behind it, and a column of dispositions, and a sentence from a woman of about sixty.

She put the two sheets side by side and read them both, in the way a keeper reads two things, which is left to right and then right to left, and then she squared them, which is a thing a keeper does, and which she has done about nine times in four years and a half.

"There is a man in the first one," she said, "and there is nobody in the second one, and I have two documents in this room about a county that is not on any map in this kingdom and about a person in it that I have never seen, and either I join them or I do not, and if I join them then I have written down that the man in the first one is in the second one, and I cannot check it, and if I write down that I cannot check it then a person who reads this page in nine years will read it as a statement that he is in it."

"So you are not writing it."

"I am not writing it," said Mara Vey, "and I am entering the not writing, and it goes in because a keeper who leaves the worst of a decision out of the book has decided it twice, and I have written that sentence in this day-book seven times this month and I am going to keep writing it until somebody in this county stops me or until it is true that there is nothing to decide."

---

**And the thing nobody in that room said, and that Sarn Oyelaran wrote on the back of a sheet and put in the press face down, is that a supersession in a county that is not on any map in this kingdom cannot be served on anybody at all.**

*A county's own bench has withdrawn its own instrument, in its own room, on its own book, on a day in a month this kingdom does not use. There is no writ, no seal, no office, no Crown's man, and no county schedule that carries that county, and there is nothing in this kingdom that can be written to a court about it, and there is nobody to be summoned.*

*Which means it holds because about two thousand four hundred and fifty people believe in a bench, and a physician's four pages, and a rule with no office behind it, and a woman on three hearths who wrote down that she did not know — and it will go on holding for exactly as long as that is true, and it can be stopped by about eleven men in a room in about a morning, and no form in any office in this kingdom can stop it and no form in any office in this kingdom can stop the stopping of it.*

*That is the settlement. It is the first one in this case that anybody has got by asking, and it is not enforceable, and it is better than the two instruments it has replaced, and nobody in this county can prove that, and I am writing it down because a keeper of records who waits for a document to be enforceable before she enters it has waited four years and a half and has nothing.*


---

**And then she went and got out the served schedule of the eighth of Fallowmonth, and it is the oldest document in the case, and it is the one nobody has asked her about.**

It is a schedule, served, of seven villages by name, in the county's spelling, figures separately, `sum not stated` in the total line. Eight ruled lines. Seven of them have a village against them.

The eighth is ruled and it is empty, and there is a reason under it copied in the hand of a county boy of nineteen who asked whether it would still be on the sheet in a year.

The city is four thousand according to the Crown and has been for eleven years. **The seven villages are seven hundred and twenty by name, in the county's spelling, with the sum not stated on the sheet.** The two figures are never added together anywhere in this case, and they are not added together now, and nobody in that room has ever added them, and **the eighth line is the city's, and it is still empty, and the city is still not on the served schedule at the end of the whole business.**

She looked at it for about a minute and a half with no lamp, in a room she had been in for four and a half years, and it was the third time.

She did not fill it in.

And then she wrote in the day-book, at about the fifth hour, in the ordinary form, and this is what she wrote, and it is the eleventh entry in a book that has about four hundred of them, and there is no more in it than this:

*Twenty-sixth of Greenmonth, YR 313, the fifth hour. I have looked at the eighth ruled line in the served schedule of the eighth of Fallowmonth for the third time, in the eighth month after the first look and the seventh month after the second, and I have not written in the cell, and I enter that I wanted to, and I enter that the wanting is not the same wanting as the last two times and I am not going to explain the difference because I am the only person who can see it and a keeper who explains a difference to herself in a day-book has made a document about a person, and there is no person in this and that is the point.*

*And I enter, because the two sheets came down a line four hundred miles apart on the same morning and they are on one table, and because I have entered four things this year that I did not have to:*

*That the county of Ash has a rule in its own book with no office behind it, and the whole of it is that a person struck may answer and if a person answers the person is restored, and that in this county the fourth column of a form has a heading for a keeper and a man has to write in it, and that nobody in this county can be shown the line that would let a person answer, and that a person in this county who has been struck off a record cannot be put back on it by anybody.*

*That is all I am going to write. I am not going to join the two and a keeper who joins the two has made a roll of one thing out of two counties, and that is the thing this whole case is about, and I have written that reason down on these pages four times and I am not going to write it a fifth.*

---

**The brine weights are still cited and not filed, and the two day-book lines about them are five days apart in the same week, and they are read together, and the reason is on the page and the reason has changed.**

They are cited on the second of Fallowmonth, YR 312, at the second hour, and on the seventh of Fallowmonth, YR 312, at the fifth hour, four lines apart in the same book, and this month the two of them are about eight months old, and she read them together at about the sixth hour on the twenty-sixth with the fair copy of an assizes' minute lying face up on the same table.

The first line is a figure. The second line is a figure. Neither line has a name against it, and the roll of the dead that could be built out of them is not built and is not going to be built this year, and **the reason it was not built in Fallowmonth is on the page in that day-book in one line and it is the old reason and it is this: a roll built out of a man's ledger must be entered as a ledger, in its own order, with the weights standing beside the names, and never as a speech. That reason is about how to enter it. It was never about whether to.**

And the reason it is not being built now, written out in full because the reason has changed, and this is the whole of what four hundred miles bought this office:

*A ledger is a claim. This office has learned this year, from a county four hundred miles off, that a claim about people is not a record of them.*

A ledger of the dead built out of a man's brine weights would be the best document this office could ever produce and it would be a page on which two hundred names are struck out at once with a total against them, and it would be a claim, and a claim about people is a thing a person cannot answer, and **a county four hundred miles off spent nineteen years building exactly that, and it worked so well that about four hundred people went through nine rooms in nine days, and one of them does not know which kiln he fires, and there was no instrument in either country that would do one thing about it.**

She entered the two day-book lines and the changed reason and did not build the roll, and the not building is entered, for the seventh time this year, and there was nothing else on the line.

---

**And the last thing on the twenty-sixth of Greenmonth was a sentence that came down a line inside the fair copy, in a hand that is not a clerk's, and it is four lines long and it is the only part of the whole four hundred miles that is a person asking to be written down.**

*And a woman of this county, who is not a clerk and has not been asked to write anything in nineteen years, has written that she does not know whether re-grinding what was fired is a good idea, and that she is going to do it anyway, and that she would like somebody to write down that she said so, in a book that is not hers, and that she has asked for it to be sent north and has not been told whether it can be.*

There is no name against it. There is a hearth against it, and a trade, and the trade is *wife*, and the fourth column of a tile in that county has never once had to say which of four kilns, and a woman whose fourth column says *wife* is not a trade and the county has kept it for nineteen years and has read it.

**Aren Kest read it twice and then said one thing about it, in a room with three women in it, and he did not say it to Mara Vey.**

"She did not know," he said. "**That is the first sentence anybody in that county has produced in nine months that is not about what the county has got.** Four hundred and nine of them, and a bench of nine, and a magistrate, and a physician of sixty-three, and a man with a ledger who has been right about arithmetic for nineteen years, and every single one of them has been right about a thing they are going to do. And a woman of about sixty on three hearths has written down that she does not know, and asked for it to be put in a book that is not hers, and the clerk of that assizes has copied it out and sent it four hundred miles because she asked, and **that is the whole of what a person is, and it is in a fair copy, and it is four lines long, and it came down a line that does not open on a schedule.**"
