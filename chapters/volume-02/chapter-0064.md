# Chapter 0064

On the sixth day he went out to the bridge at the first light with a sett-cutter's bag he had not wanted to bring four hundred miles and had brought anyway, because a man who has nothing to do in a place he does not know will use a bag to look as though he has something to do.

She was at the fourth span with her hand flat on the third tile from the mooring ring, the same tile, in the same light, and she did not look up, and he said:

"Hand."

"Yes."

"I was going to say it properly and I have decided not to."

"Then it is the word," she said, "and there is a column for it, and it is empty, and you have used it in front of me, and I would like to know what you think you have said."

"I think I have said what you are," he said. "That is what the column is for. It is the word your trade has, the same way my trade has one and it is not *sett-cutter* in the field, it is *setting*, and it is a gerund."

"It is a noun," she said. "There is no other word in this county for the person who keeps the Ninebridge and I am not going to be the woman who improves on it at twenty to six in the morning, and you are welcome to call me the hand, and I am not going to correct you, and you should understand that I am not doing you a courtesy. **If I give you my name you have a person. If you have got the hand you have got a job, and a job can be asked to turn up, and I have to be able to say in nine years that nobody in this county was ever able to name the person who keeps this bridge, because the moment that is on paper the paper is the roadkeeper.**"

"That is the worst argument for keeping a thing open I have ever heard," said Aren Kest, "and I have heard a bench of nine give a better one about a bell."

"Then you have got the county wrong in about four days, which is faster than the clerk got it wrong, so you will do," she said. "Come and hold something."

---

The cutting shed at the north end of the Ninebridge belongs to the Ninebridge kiln, and it is a shed, and it is the place where every soft tile in that part of the county is kept between the pressing and the firing, on boards, under wet cloths, in a rack four deep, and the rack goes up to the roof and the whole of it smells of a thing that is not going to be a thing yet.

"There are about four hundred of them in this county at any one time," she said. "Every child in it has one and does not lose it until the seventh year, and a grown person has one that was fired, and between the two there are four hundred soft ones lying in sheds in racks being kept alive."

"A soft tile is a mark in clay that has not been through a kiln."

"A soft tile is a mark in clay that has not been through a kiln, and the mark is on the face, pressed in, dished, and the dish is what the clay is doing and the dish is what makes the mark readable, and I will tell you what nobody will tell you because you have not earned it yet, which is that a soft tile goes out of true. It lies in a wet board for seven years. It dries and it wets and it dries, and the face creeps, and the mark walks off the edge of the board a quarter of an inch in about four years, and after eight years the dish is a smear and a person cannot be found in it. And so every soft tile in this county is out of true at some point in its life, and re-pressing is the repair, and every single person in this county re-presses their own tile when it goes, and it is a Saturday job, and it takes a minute."

"Then where is the trade in it?"

"That is where the trade in it is," she said, "and you have not asked the right question, and the right question is *what is a person worth in this county*, and the answer is a tile, and a tile is the account of them that does not need them, and if the tile and the entry in the roll disagree then the law takes them off the roll without anybody doing anything, because a person is on the roll if two others witness them and their tile agrees with the entry, and if the two things do not agree then the law has said so and I have said nothing and no magistrate has said anything and there is no list anywhere in this county with a name on it."

She pulled a board out of the rack and set it on the bench, and on the board was a tile the size of a hand, and the face of it was damp and grey and had a mark in it about half an inch across that was nearly, but not quite, on the edge of the board.

"That is a woman's tile and it is out of true and she has not been in for nine days, and I am not going to do it for her, because the law says the person presses their own and I have never in nine years broken that and I am not going to start on a Tuesday," she said. "Re-press that."

---

He got it wrong four times, and the first time was not a mistake at all, and that was the thing he wrote down that night.

The first time he put his whole hand on it. The mark was half an inch across and his hand was not, and he came down over the whole face with the flat of the palm and the cloth between, and he pressed the dish out and set the tile down flat against the edge of the board, and it came out true, and it was perfect, and it had no mark in it at all.

"Twice," said the woman.

"That was the whole of it."

"That was the whole of it," she said, "and you have pressed out her dish and her mark is now a flat place with a wet thumbprint in it, and in about four years that flat place is going to be a soft grey nothing, and the clerk at the assizes is going to hold that tile up to the entry and the entry has a name and a hearth and a trade and a hand on it, and the tile has got a thumbprint, and they do not agree, and a woman who has done nothing whatever is off the roll of this county tonight because a stranger came in."

He stood there with a wet cloth in his hand.

"That is not a trade," he said. "That is a finger."

"That is a finger," she said, "and a finger is what most people in this county use, and it works, because a finger does not cover a mark. Now do it again, and do not press. **Do not press the tile. Press the dish.** The dish is what is out of true. The mark is what is in it. If you press on the mark you will take the mark out. If you press around the mark you will bring the dish up against the mark and the mark will stand up in it, and a mark that stands up in a dish is a mark that can be read at four feet on a wet morning, and that is the entire trade and it is a thing a child does."

---

The child did it in about a minute and a half, and the child was four, and her name was Bel, and she was at the low end of the rack on a stool with her feet not touching the ground and a cloth in her hand the way he had a cloth in his.

She was doing the tiles that come off the rack for a check, which is a child's job and has been a child's job in this county longer than the roll, and she was not showing anybody anything, and she got about nine right in a row.

He watched her for about a minute before he understood that she was not better at it than he was. She was smaller. Her hand went in one place and he was trying to get four inches of a body out of a hand that was built for a set three feet long, and the whole of the difficulty was that he had too much hand, and there is no such thing as too much hand in any trade he had ever been in.

"That's the whole of it," he said. "Four days, you said."

"You have four days," said the woman. "A child has a minute. You will not have four days because a man from a county that is not on any map is not going to be given a minute at a thing the county does on a Saturday, and I am not going to do it for you, and I am not going to teach you faster, and I will tell you why I am not going to teach you faster and then I am going to leave you with the rack and come back at the seventh hour."

"Because I am a stranger."

"Because you are a stranger," she said, "and because the county does not know what to do with a man who is good at a thing in an hour, and it has not had to find out in nineteen years, and if you are good at it on Thursday the man at the cutting bench will ask you how long you have been doing it, and you will have to answer, and the answer will be *four days*, and then he will tell the shed, and then in a week there will be a man in Kilnholt who has heard there is a man from the north who can do a child's job better than a woman of thirty, and that man will be asked to do two hundred re-presses in a week for a person who cannot be asked, and he will do them, and he will do them well, and there will be nothing on any tile in this county that says who pressed it."

She went out into the light with the roll of tools at her hip.

The man at the cutting bench put his head round the door at that, because a man at a cutting bench in this county has no door and is never announced, and he had a tile in each hand and a short steel in his belt and he had been cutting at that bench for nineteen years and is not named in anything.

"Four days," he said.

"He has four days," said the woman.

"Then you will be wanting the low rows before the high ones," said the man, "and the low rows are the children's, and the children's are not to be done by a man from off a road." And he went out again, and he had said it the way a man lays a tile down.

"He is right," said the woman.

"He is."

"He is right, and he is also a man who has cut tile for this bridge since before the roll was begun and who has not been asked to be careful about a thing in his own shed once in nineteen years, and I would like you to understand that those two facts are sitting side by side in him and that he does not know it. And I would like you to understand the other thing, which is that a kiln in this county is an employer and a kiln can shut, and a man from off a road who is good at a thing in a shed is a thing a man says in a market before the rolling ends. You have not said anything in a market."

"I have not said anything anywhere."

"You have not said anything anywhere," said the woman, "which is the only reason you are standing in my shed at all, and it is going to stop being the only reason at about the fourth day, and I would rather tell you now than then, because I do not lie to a man about what he is going to have to stand in a room with."

---

"And the other reason," she said, from the door, "which is the one that is actually the reason, and I am going to say it once, and then you can have the rack. **A road in this county is the only thing in it that is not an argument.** Every other thing in here is an argument. The roll is an argument with a man in it. The law is an argument four lines long. The list is an argument about two hundred names. The tiles are an argument everybody has already won, and they won it in the third hundred and eighth year, and it has cost them the only thing they had, which is a thing they can argue with. A road is a road and it is tiled and a tiling is a record and a record does not have an opinion, and I have kept four hundred yards of it for nine years, and in nine years it has been the only thing in this county I have been able to do a day's work on without somebody wanting me to change something. So when I say you are useful and not a visitor, that is the whole of what I am offering, and I am not going to soften it, and you can have the rack."
