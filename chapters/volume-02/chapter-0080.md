# Chapter 0080

The three hands were agreed in the twenty-second week and the agreement was not signed, and the two things about it that matter are both about the signing.

The rollkeeper's office at the assizes, which is Pernel Quist's office and has been for nineteen years, and which is in one room in one building and does not move. The hand of the Ninebridge, which is a woman, and which is not an office and is a shed at the north end of a bridge with a door on the side of it, and which holds about four hundred soft tiles on boards under wet cloths and four hundred yards of fired tile set in a kerb, one to a kerb stone, with a device impressed and the kiln's mark on the back and a date, so that a man can turn a stone over with a heel and read who cut it. And a third place, four hundred miles off, in a city that is not on any map in this kingdom: **the press of a creditor's counting house, at a price of a farthing of salt, on a shelf for which the price is eleven sheets that this county cannot go and get.**

The two hands this county has are the two hands a person can be got at for. The third is promised in a letter and is four hundred miles away and behind a price, and no one in ninety-one feet of drying floor pretended otherwise.

They met in the long room at the assizes on the twenty-second week, because the magistrate's room holds a magistrate and a clerk and a rollkeeper when there is a press to open, and it had held about nine people in nineteen years.

Magistrate Colm Arreld read Alis Merrow's four pages out loud. He did it himself, which nobody had expected, and he read them at the speed of a man reading a document he is going to be asked about, and he read the third page twice.

Then he put it down and said the thing that had been in the room since the seventeenth week and had not been said by anybody.

"I have had a physician's finding in a drawer for four years," he said, "and I have had a list with a law on it for nine years, and the reason I have not acted on either is that they are each one instrument, and every instrument I have ever made in this matter has had one good idea in it and the good idea has been a mark in the ground for the fever, and I have been right about the fever eleven times and wrong about the names every time, and I have known that for two years and I have sat here with it. **A stranger came off a road in the first week of a rolling and gave this county a second instrument, and I am glad of it, and I have been ashamed for about four years, and both of those are true and I am not going to tidy one of them up in front of forty people.**"

Somebody said *no*.

"You are not to say that," said Colm Arreld. "I have been the magistrate of this county for nine years and there is a thing I have learned and it is the only thing I have learned in nine years, which is that a magistrate in a county like this one is the man who gets blamed for the instrument that was available and the man who is never blamed for the one that was not, and so a magistrate who says *I am glad* in a room is a magistrate who has told the truth in a room, and it costs about nine minutes a year and it is the only honest thing I do."

---

The letter's argument was read out and minuted, and the minute is the whole of what happened in the long room, and the bench entered it in the county's own book, at the clerk's hand, with the date and the hour on it, and the date is in the county's frame and the county's frame is a rolling, and the rolling is a season of clay-digging and firing, and the rolling does not have weeks that anybody outside this county has ever heard of.

*The argument of A. Merrow, toll-house steward, of the counting house of the tolls of Bellweather-Living, read in this room and entered here whole at the officer's request:*

*That a creditor's press is the worst place in the world to keep a roll of persons and the only place in the world where nobody can quietly take it out of the book. That he has been arguing that for eleven years in a hall and that he is now the man who is proposing it to his own city. That he asks that the argument be minuted and that the minute be put where a Crown's man will find it in about nine years.*

And the bench tried to put the minute where a Crown's man would find it, and could not, and the reason it could not is the volume's whole subject and it took about four minutes in a long room to say out loud.

There is no Crown's man in this county. There is no office here, no schedule, no writ, no fee, no seal, no county in a column with a disposition word against it, and a county that is not on any map in the kingdom cannot be written to and cannot be asked and cannot be served, and the second of the five terms the county itself wrote says that no name is to be read out on the far side of the line by anybody from the north. There is nowhere in the County of Ash that a Crown's man will ever find a minute, and that is not because anybody here has hidden anything. It is because there is nobody here to look.

"So the request cannot be granted here," said the hand of the Ninebridge. "It can only be granted where he is. **He has to put it in his own city, in his own book, in his own hand, at his own cost, and then it will be in the world, and we cannot help him and we cannot stop him, and if he does not do it then the argument is a thing a stranger said in a shed in a country that is not on any map, and in about nine years that is worth nothing at all.**"

"Enter that," said the bench, and it was entered, and the ash-driver asked whether the bench had any power to require a man four hundred miles off to do a thing, and the answer was no, and it was entered that the answer was no, and the bench entered its own powerlessness in a book in a minute, which is the first time any body in this county has done that in nineteen years and the reason it did it is that the argument was about where a minute goes and the room could not do the thing and would not pretend.

---

Pernel Quist did not sign.

She agreed to the working. She said in about nine sentences that she would put two hundred and six people through the re-reading at the assizes, at the person, with the book in the room and the tile on the table and two witnesses whose names were in the book, and that she would do it in a room with a stove in it and not in a hall, and that she would do it at her own table and not at a bench's, because the bench is a room people vote in and she is not going to have a person's whole account of themselves entered in a room where a vote is taken.

And then she said why she would not sign it, and she said it to the magistrate and not to the bench, and she said it in about two minutes, and the clerk wrote all of it.

"I will keep the roll and I will read it to people, and I will not add a name to it and I will not take one off it, and I will not be one of the three hands that agrees. If I am one of the three, then every restoration in this county is a thing a woman in Kilnholt did — and I have known that since the sixteenth day of a rolling, when a stranger asked me a question in a room with a stove in it, and I said no in one word, and he wrote down the reason I said no, and the reason I said no was that I am the anchor. Nobody in this room is going to be the anchor in four years' time if I am the one holding the third hand."

She put the sheet down on the table and squared it, which is a thing a keeper does, and which in this room meant that she was about to say the part she had come to say.

"Second, and this is the part that matters and it is not a matter of principle. **The third hand is four hundred miles off and behind a price, and the price is a thing this county cannot go and fetch, and a roll that is signed by two hands and a promise is not a roll in three hands. It is a roll in two hands with a third hand written on the end of it in a fair copy, and in about four years a magistrate is going to ask which of the three it is and the answer will be that one of them is a letter.** I will not sign a thing that has a letter in it. I have spent nineteen years not signing things that have letters in them and I have been right about that every single time."

"So you are not signing."

"I am not signing on Thursday," said Pernel Quist. "I have not said no. **Do not put it in the book that I said no, because I have said no twice in nine years and both times I was wrong, and a book is a worse place to be wrong than a corridor and a better place to be right, and I have not decided which this is.**"

It is the fourth page of the minute and it is the longest thing in it and it is the only part of the whole of this business in nineteen years that anybody in this county will be able to quote in about nine years, and a clerk of the assizes wrote it down in a flat hand and did not improve it and read it back, and she corrected one word, and the word she corrected was *worse*.

---

He did not speak.

That is on the page too, because it is a strange thing to have in a minute, and the clerk asked him about it at the end and he answered, and what he said is in the minute, and it is eleven words long.

They had asked him three times in twenty-two weeks what he thought, twice by people who wanted to be helped by the answer and once by a man who did not, and the last of the three had been the sixteenth day of a rolling in a room with a stove in it, and he had refused, and the reason he had refused was in a book in a coat and nobody in the room had ever read it.

He sat at the end of the long table with his hands flat on it and he was the only person in that room who could have told them what a working at the scale of two hundred needs and what makes one fail, and there were three people in that room who had each asked him a question about it in the last four months, and he answered none of them, and at the end the clerk asked him why and he said:

"I have not got a trade for it. Eleven words, enter that, and it is the true reason and it is not modesty."

And then he walked out of the assizes and down a street in Kilnholt in the flat yellow light with the clay steps cut and wet to the height of a hand, and he went to the low end of the rack and sat down on the low stool and took out the notebook and wrote the following, in the trade's own words, in the back, and it is the entry that matters, and nobody in this county has read it, and nobody in this county will read it, and the reader has read the shape of it once already, in a shed, four months and about two hundred and fifty leagues back, in the correct vocabulary, in tons, and did not act on it then either.

---

*Twenty-second week. The three hands are agreed and one of them is four hundred miles off and behind a price, and the clerk of the roll has not signed, and the magistrate has said the true thing, and nobody in that room said* we are right *or* you are wrong, *and I have been in this county twenty-two weeks and I have noticed that a county which cannot pay for a thing does not argue about the thing. It goes quiet and then it finds the list. It took them four months and it took the kilns eleven weeks and the kilns did it faster than the magistrate ever could, and the magistrate knew it would, and he went four days away on purpose, and I want that written down because in nine years I think this county will say the stranger did it.*

*I am not going to write down what the rite is for. I have not worked it out. A man who writes down why a thing is good in the same book as what the thing is has written a sentence instead of a working, and I have watched a sentence get picked up in four hundred miles by a man who is quicker than me, and I am not going to do it again, and the only way not to do it again is to leave the room with the parts and not the purpose, which is what I have done every time for eleven years and it is the one habit I have got out of this trade that is worth anything.*

*Set out the parts. It is a working and it is a working at the scale of two hundred and I am going to write it in the words of the trade because I cannot write it in any other words and I cannot be trusted with the other words.*

*Anchor — the roll. One book, one room, one building. Nothing else in this county does not move.*

*Witness — the person answering, and two whose names are in the book. The rule in this trade is that the witness is not the man doing the working and is whoever would have refused. Here there is nobody who would have refused. I asked the keeper of the roll to her own face whether she would refuse and she said no, in one word, in front of me, and she is right that she would not refuse, and the reason she would not refuse is that she is the anchor. **A witness that is also the anchor is not a witness. It is a second reading of the same hand.** Same as the last time. I have written that sentence twice now in two counties and it has been right twice.*

*Counterweight — the county. About forty men and a day at five pence. Sixty marks one shilling eightpence for ninety cords and the cutting. Six men out of a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths whose names are six lines with nothing against them and no reason on them, and there is no column in this county for what happened to them, and there is not going to be one, and the reason there is not going to be one is the same reason there is not a column for a person.*

*Bound — the promise that these two hundred are the people. It goes all the way through. The roll is in one room and if the roll is right then one woman in Kilnholt is the only person in this county who can be wrong about two hundred human beings, and if she is wrong about two hundred then nothing in the world says she is not also wrong about the other two thousand two hundred and fifty, and a bound that goes all the way through a structure is not a bound. A bound has to be the narrowest thing you can make it. The width of the split. The length of the timber. This one is the whole of the county and I have made it and I know the size of it and I made it the first time I ever looked at it.*

*Figure. A working at that scale wants a figure and I have not got one. I do not know what a person weighs in a working. I have set a deck for forty tons on a hand's width of oak twice in my life and I could tell you to the pound. I am going to say two tons each, which is a number I had off a barrow of setts in a fen county in the last year and for which I have no reason of any kind, and two hundred of them is four hundred tons, and four hundred tons into a bed of river gravel at Ninebridge is a thing I can point at and say there, that is what this is, and it is on the right side of four hundred and it is the correct order of size for a thing I do not understand.*

*A document's load is not in tons. I know that. I have written it in tons anyway. I have now written the same number in tons in the same vocabulary in two counties, and the second time I have written it I have also written down that I noticed the first time, and noticing it did not stop me then and it has not stopped me now, and a man who has noticed a thing twice is not being careful, he is being consistent.*

*Four months, or thereabouts, which is the same thing at this rate, and I have not done the sum because the sum is in two calendars and I have not got both of them, and the one I have got does not have a week in it, and I wrote a line about that exact sum in this book the last time I was in this county and I have not read that line since and I know what it says without reading it.*

*I am going to build it anyway. That is written down as well, because I have written the mistake down before and read it and gone on, and the thing I have noticed this time, and the only new thing in this book today, is that **it is the same mistake both times and noticing it has never once stopped me, and a man who has noticed a thing twice is not being careful, he is being consistent.** That is not a trade sentence. I do not know what it is. I am putting it in here because it is in here and the book is on my knee.*

*Closed the book. There are about four hundred soft tiles in this county between here and Kilnholt and one pair of hands and mine, and I am going to do the bottom of the rack next, and it is the best work I have had in five months, and nobody in this county is going to hear a word about any of the above from me, ever, in a room, with a clerk in it, ever again.*
