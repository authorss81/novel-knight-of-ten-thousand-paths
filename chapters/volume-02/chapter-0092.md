# Chapter 0092

The fifth of Greenmonth was a Friday and it was not a market day, and the fever broke at about the sixth hour in a room over a weigh-house, and by the seventh hour a man who had been grey about the gills for three days was sitting up in a chair with his boots on, and about two hours after that he was in a room with a long table in it giving a keeper of records a date in the wrong calendar without noticing that he had done it.

He gave it to her in the ordinary way, in the middle of other things, in a voice that was flat and slightly hoarse and completely without weight.

"The clerk of the assizes there sent the schedule of the eight village readings down the line to the assizes here," he said, "and it went north on the eighteenth of the ninth month of that county's own year, and it is the reason I know there are eight places and not nine, because there are eight villages and the market town is the ninth and the town was not on the schedule, and the town was not on the schedule because the town has the room with the floor in it."

Mara Vey wrote it down.

She wrote the date as he gave it, in her own fair hand, in the day-book, against no person, in the ordinary form, at the seventh hour of the fifth of Greenmonth, and she wrote the month with it, and she wrote the word *county* beside the month so that anybody reading it in nine years would know which calendar the month belonged to.

And then, because she is a keeper of records and has been one for four years and a half, and because a keeper cannot help it, she went to the page and compared the entry against the day-book.

---

**It took her about eleven seconds and it took her the rest of the morning.**

He had given her two dates in the wrong frame that morning. Not one. Twice, in one morning, without noticing, the way a man who has been in a place for nine months says a thing that is true there and is not true here.

The first was the eighteenth of the ninth month, and there is no ninth month in this calendar, and the words *of that county's own year* are four words on a page and they are the only thing between a true date and a false one, and a clerk in nine years reading the day-book without those four words would read a date in a county that does not exist.

The second was eleven minutes later and she found it herself. He said, in the middle of a sentence about a floor: "— and the bench sat on the twenty-second and again on the twenty-fourth of the ninth month, which is two days apart, and the ninth of that month is the day the line was not there —"

"And it is the ninth of Greenmonth here," she said.

He stopped.

He stopped the way a man stops when he has put his hand on a hot thing and has not yet decided whether it is hot.

"Yes," he said. "It is."

"That is two dates you have given me this morning in the wrong calendar, and you did it twice, and you did not know you were doing it, and I want to say the rest of what I have to say about it and I am going to say it now and then I am going to go back to work, and you are not going to be able to stop me, and I am not going to be able to stop me either."

"Mara—"

"**A bridgewright would not have noticed.** That is what you are and I have watched you read a handrail in the dark from this window for four years and I know exactly what your trade does and does not do for you, and what it does not do for you is compare one thing with another thing in a different frame. **And a keeper of records cannot not.** That is what I am. It is not a virtue. It is the thing I do instead of eating."

She put the pen down.

"I have been not asking you a question since the twenty-second of Fallowmonth, YR 312, at about the tenth hour, in this room, and it is four lines in a day-book and the fourth line is the one the whole of this case is standing on without anybody knowing, and it says that this office is not going to ask him again. I have never written down anywhere else why. It is the reason I have not asked you about a letter in a room over a cooper since Longlight. It is the reason I have not asked you where you go. It is the reason I have had a line in a list in my own hand for four days that I cannot put down, and it is a number and not a name, and it is about a man at a kiln four hundred miles off, and I have not asked you one question about it and I am not going to."

"Then why are you telling me now."

"Because you have just handed me a date in the wrong calendar twice in one morning, and I did not find it by asking you anything, and **that is the difference between the two of us, and it is the whole of it, and it means I cannot do this by promising.**"

She turned the day-book round and pushed it across the table so that it was in front of him, which she had never done, and which is a thing a keeper of records does about four times in four years and not more.

"And I have noticed that the not asking has changed, and I wrote the day down when I noticed it, and I am going to put the day on this page and the hour with it, because I am not going to be a woman who has a feeling about a thing in her head. I noticed it on the twenty-ninth of Fallowmonth, which was the seventh day of the promise, and I wrote the day down on the fifth of Wolfmonth, the morning you came up out of that county, and I have said nothing to anybody about it since. The reason is on the page in two paragraphs and the reason is about this office and not about you, and I am not going to say that one in a room, and I am not going to write either of them again, because I wrote one once and writing it twice makes it a thing I have said out loud."

**"A wound she is choosing not to open is worse than a question,"** she said. "That is the whole of what has changed. In Fallowmonth the not asking was a thing I did to you. It is now a thing I am doing for myself, and the difference between those two is about a week and it has taken me seven months to do anything about it, and I have had two hundred and twenty-three days of that promise — from the twenty-second of Fallowmonth to this morning — and I noticed what it had become in the seventh day of it, and I have asked him nothing since. **That is the lateness, and it is not in the noticing. It is in the seven months.**"

"Write it down," he said.

She looked at him.

"You want me to put that on a page," she said, "in a day-book, in a book in a room, with the hour on it."

"Yes."

"That is your third time in eleven years," she said, "and the first was a letter and it cost me two nights' sleep and this town a bench-clerk's afternoon, and the second was a mark on a bridge four hundred miles off and it cost a man of sixty-four an answer, and if I write this down then in about nine years a man may come into this room and read a page in my hand about the day I noticed a thing about you, and there will be a date on it, and a date is the only instrument I have that I cannot stop using."

"Then it is the instrument," said Aren Kest, "and you have been carrying it for four years and a half and it is the only honest thing about you and it is going to be the thing that undoes you, and I would rather it did that than that you asked me about a morning in a yard and got a lie."

"I am not going to get a lie," said Mara Vey. "**I am not going to get anything.**"

---

**And then she did the thing that made it a breach instead of a scene, and she did it without hurrying, and he watched her do it and did not understand it until she told him what it was.**

She went and got out a clean sheet, not a day-book leaf, and ruled two columns on it, and wrote the twelve months of that year down the left-hand one in about nine seconds, and every one of the twelve was a word with no number in it.

"This is his year," she said, "and I have had it in my head since the fifth of Wolfmonth, when he came back out of a county four hundred miles off and told me the shape of it in about nine minutes while he was grey about the gills, and I wrote the twelve words down at the time because I thought a woman who reads for a living ought to have them, and I have not had them out of my head since, and there are two of them I could not swear to this morning and I have looked.

"They are not our months. Five of them here are not the same month. Every one of them is four weeks long, because a rolling has to be in before the water comes and four weeks is what a man can hold in his head, which is why a county that counts fires has a month of four weeks in it at all, and there is not one month of four weeks in this calendar, and there has not been one since the year before the writ."

"You could work it out," said Aren Kest. "You have the two ends. The fourth of Hearthmonth on the day I went and the second of Greenmonth on the day I came back, and that is nine months and six days in his frame and eighty-eight days in yours, and you could put a column on that sheet and fill it in and it would be a conversion."

"I could," said Mara Vey. "And it would not be a fact. It would be a number I made up, and every line under it would be a line I had invented, and the invention would be indistinguishable from the work, and in about nine years a man in a room would take a figure off it and use it, and he would be using me, and he would not know that is what he was doing."

"Three rollings and part of a fourth. Two low waters a year. The year counted from the first firing." She looked at the sheet. "There is not one of those in a year anybody in this kingdom keeps, and I have just written twelve words of it out on a piece of paper, and I am going to tear it up."

She tore it up.

"And I want that entered as a thing I did on purpose, with the hour on it. I have entered three things this year that I did not have to and two of them were about a person and this one is about my own office, and it is the first of the three that anybody could have called timidity, and it is not timidity, and I am not going to argue with anybody who wants to call it timidity. **A county four hundred miles off counts the year from its first firing and I am not going to write that county's year down on a page in this room. If the day ever comes when somebody in this kingdom has to know what day it is in Kilnholt, they can go and stand on a bridge there and ask.**"

"You could ask me."

"You could tell me," she said, "and I am not going to ask you, and I have just spent ten minutes showing you that I was right not to, and that is the difference between the two of us and it is the whole of it, and I would like it in a book, and there is no book in this county a thing like that goes in, and I have been looking for four years."

---

She wrote the entry at about the ninth hour and it is in the day-book over a weigh-house and it is one paragraph and it has a number against the office and nothing against anybody, and it is the ninth time in four years that she has entered a fact about a person in a book that is not a record, and the fourth time this year, and the second time this year that the thing entered is the not writing.

*Fifth of Greenmonth, YR 313, a Friday, the ninth hour. Entered on the statement of the road warden of this county's bridge guild, at the seventh hour, against no person, with no opinion: that two dates given by him this morning in the ordinary course of a conversation about a county four hundred miles off were dates of that county and not of this one, and that the same thing has been done twice in one morning, and that he did not know he was doing it.*

*And entered, because the not writing is the part that is a decision: that this office has not asked him a question about either of them, and that this office will not ask him, and that the reason this office wrote in Fallowmonth that it would not ask him again was a protection, and that the not asking is now a wound, and that this office noticed the change on the twenty-ninth of Fallowmonth, YR 312, which was the seventh day of the promise, and has entered nothing about it for seven months, and that the lateness is in the seven months and not in the noticing.*

*And entered, that the finding was not arrived at by asking him anything. It was arrived at by a comparison of two entries against a day-book, in the ordinary way, by the person whose trade cannot not do it. He has been told that a bridgewright would not have noticed. He has been told why. Neither of those is a reason to stop.*

---

**At about the eleventh hour she went down to the press with a creditor's fair copy in her hand, because a day cannot end with a paragraph in it and she has been ending days with a book in her hand since Fallowmonth.**

It is a fair copy of one leaf of the index of a register of outgoing writs, made in a counting house on a wharf in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, and it reached this county inside the fair copy of a fair copy of it on the first of Fallowmonth, and it has been in that press since the eighth of Embermonth and it has never been produced anywhere. Beside it in the same press is a fair copy of one leaf of a shelf register out of the fourth press of a provincial office two hundred miles off, which nobody asked for either.

She was not looking for anything. She goes to the press at the end of a day because that is where the day ends.

The leaf has nine lines. Eight of them are of a valley in this kingdom. The ninth is a tender in the three hundred and fifth year with a number on it and a date on it and the words *press four, die eleven, second impression of the season* at the foot of it, and a column headed *Returned* with nothing in it, and a column headed *Answered* with a date in it that is eleven days after the tender, and **a cell headed for a county, ruled, with nothing in it.**

She had looked at that cell on the eighth of Embermonth and had refused to fill it, and the refusal is in the day-book in three numbered parts and is one of the best pieces of work she has done in four years.

She looked at it now, in the ordinary way, with a finger on the edge of the leaf, in a room with a press in it and a table and a fire that was not her fire.

**It is still empty. There is a line above it with a number on it and a date on it, and the county cell on that line has nothing in it, and there is nothing on the leaf that says why, and there is no space on the leaf for a reason, and there never was.**

She could not have said when she started looking at it. She went to the press with a creditor's fair copy in her hand and she came away with an hour of the afternoon gone and no entry against anybody at all, and the only thing she wrote in the day-book about the hour is the hour.

**And she did not ask him about that either, and there is no reason on the page for that, and it is the same not asking.**

---

The brine weights are still cited and not filed. They were cited on the second of Fallowmonth, YR 312, at the second hour, and again on the seventh of Fallowmonth at the fifth hour, four lines apart in the same book, and the two of them are five days apart and seven months old, and they are read together every time she opens the day-book, which is four or five times a day, and she reads them together.

The roll of the dead that could be built out of them is not built. It is not built this month and it is not going to be built this year, and the two lines are five days apart and four lines apart on the page, and she reads them together every time she opens that book, which is four or five times a day, and there is no reason on the page for the not building and she is not going to write one.

She put the leaf back. She shut the press. She wrote the hour in the day-book against no person, in the ordinary form, with the claim number against the office and against nobody, and there was no opinion on the line, and there was not one word on the line about what she had been doing for an hour, and **the not writing is entered, for the fourth time this year, and it goes in for the reason the other three went in for, which is that the part of a decision a keeper leaves out of the book is the part she has decided on.**
