# Chapter 0081

Two hundred names, then four hundred, because the work goes faster than the argument and the argument is always slower than the work.

That is the whole of the sixth month of Aren Kest's time in the County of Ash, and he wrote it in the back of a notebook at about the eleventh hour of the last night of it, on a low stool at the low end of a rack, with a wet cloth gone cold in his hand, and what he wrote was not a boast and it was not a warning. It was a figure with nothing under it, and it is the first line of the last four months of this volume and the only one of the four that anybody in either country will ever read.

The three hands were working, or two of them were and the third was a letter.

The rollkeeper's office at the assizes was a room with a stove in it in the cold months and a box under the table and a woman in it who had not signed. The hand of the Ninebridge was a shed at the north end of a bridge with a door on the side of it and about four hundred soft tiles on boards under wet cloths and four hundred yards of fired tile set one to a kerb stone. The third place was four hundred miles off in a city that is not on any map in this kingdom, on a shelf in a creditor's press, at a farthing of salt, and the price of the shelf was eleven sheets that lay in a press in a room with a door on a lane in a county this man came from, and the county's own second term forbade the errand, and a promised hand is not a hand.

So two hands, and a fair copy, and about four hundred people going through a room in a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths.

---

It was proposed in a hall, because the assizes' long room is the only room in this county that will hold sixty people, and because the sixth of the five terms says a person who is struck may answer, and a term with nobody to answer it is a term that has not been put in force, and the argument for putting it in force had been finished in the fifth month and the argument against it had been finished in the fourth and there was nothing left to argue about.

A magistrate, a clerk, a rollkeeper, and about sixty people. Two of the kilns. The bench. The physician. The hand of the Ninebridge with a roll of tools at her hip and a board under her arm. About forty of the two hundred and six, who had been asked once each, by a cord-cutter and an ash-driver in their own sheds, in about a minute and a half a house, and who had answered, and who had come.

And a man from the north in a hat.

Nobody in that room had ever seen a hat in it. Colm Arreld looked at it for about two seconds and then looked away from it deliberately, in the way a magistrate looks away from a thing he has decided not to have an opinion about, and the clerk of the assizes looked at it for about four seconds and then wrote nothing, and the reason the clerk wrote nothing is that a hat is not a fact.

Aren Kest had put the hat on at the second hour for one reason and it was not modesty and it was not vanity, and he did not explain it to anybody and nobody asked him, and the reason is this. He had been asked three times in five months what he thought, by a magistrate, by a physician and by a bench, and he had answered the first and fought for the second and said nothing at all on the third, and each of those three had cost him something he could name. He was in this room as a man who had a trade and no office, and there is a difference in a county that has never refused anything between a man who comes to a hall and a man who comes down to it, and a hat is the only piece of clothing a bridgewright owns that anybody in this county would recognise as an outsider's, and it is not a lie, and it is not a claim, and it is the sort of thing a man wears when he has decided to be counted.

He was going to be counted. That was the whole of the sixth month.

---

"A person can check a rule," he said, "and a person who can check is a person who has been asked."

He said it at about the fourth hour, in the middle of a room, to about sixty people, and he had it in his mouth ready and he had had it ready for nine days, and he got it out in the flat voice a man uses when he is reading a line out of a book to people who are not going to be able to look it up.

Nobody wrote it down. That is worth saying, because in eleven years he has said a name of a thing five times in a room with a clerk in it, and the clerk has written it down four times, and the fifth time the clerk refused to. This time there was nothing to write down, because the sentence was a rule and a rule can be tested by anybody in a room, and a rule that can be tested is not a claim and does not need an office to hold it.

Here is the rite, as it was proposed, and as it was carried out, and it is four sentences long and it is the best idea anybody had in eleven months in either country and it killed a man who is alive.

A struck person re-presses their own soft tile, in the room, in front of two witnesses, and the soft tile is theirs and nobody else presses it and the room waits.

The two witnesses then press their own hands onto two other tiles, and the two other tiles are the witnesses' and not the person's, and the three go onto a board together and the board goes to a shelf.

And the office does not read the roll. That is the part that is the idea. The office watches people answer.

---

Nobody understood the whole of it in the first four days and nobody misunderstood it after the ninth, and the reason is that the fourth of the five terms has to be carried out in a room with a book in it, and there is only one book in this county, and a book in a room is a thing a person can be frightened of, and the whole of the proposal was that the office stops reading the book and starts watching the room.

That is what consent made physical looks like when it is done by a man who has never done it before and has watched other people do it badly. There is no form for it. There is no column headed *answered*, and there is not going to be one for ninety-four years' worth of reasons that are about a knife in a room in the assizes and not about consent. So the answer to the fourth term is a room with a book in it, and the person answering, and two people whose names are in the book, and the person's own tile on the table, and the clerk's pen not moving.

Which meant that the whole of the load went off the book and onto the room. Which nobody in that hall had thought about, and which Aren Kest had thought about, and which he had written down in two notebooks in two counties in the correct vocabulary and had not acted on and was not going to act on, because he was not looking at a structure. He was looking at a room full of people.

---

It went faster than he thought it would.

Two hundred in the first six weeks. A room with a stove in it and a long table and the roll on a shelf and about nine people in it at a time, from the second hour to the sixth, and a queue outside in the wet that behaved itself without anybody organising it because a county organises a queue better than a county organises anything.

Then four hundred. The number did not go up because more people came. The number went up because the six that nobody knew about came back, and the four that had been out of the county in the seventeenth week came back, and the people who had been struck two years and three years and a man who had been struck in the year before the list came to a room where a clerk read a list and said *are you on it* and he said yes.

**The work goes faster than the argument and the argument is always slower than the work**, and the reason is on the page in the county's own book, in a keeper's hand, in the ninth week, and it is that a county which has been asked for nothing for nineteen years will walk into a room and answer a question in about eleven seconds, and a county which has been arguing for four months about whether it ought to be asked will argue for four months about the answering.

---

He said it four words into a fifth sentence.

The fifth sentence was the reason he was the one saying it, and he had been warned about that sentence by a woman in a room with a stove in it in the eleventh month, and the warning was not about the content of it. The warning was: *you will want to say why it is you, and the why is a motive, and a motive is a thing a man can only repeat.*

"I have got no office in this county and I have had four asks put to me in five months and I have answered two of them and refused one and said nothing on the fourth, and I have been wrong about this twice," said Aren Kest, "and the second time I wrote the mistake down four months before I made it, in my own hand, in the trade's words, on a stool at this bridge, and read it, and went on, and there is nobody in this room who has read that and there is nobody in this room who ever will—"

And four hundred miles off, in a counting house in a city that is not on any map in this kingdom, a clerk of that office finished copying a fair copy of the minute of the long room at the assizes at Kilnholt, and stopped, and read the four words back, and then read the twenty that came after them, and put his pen in the inkstand.

The county had sent the fair copy because that is what the fourth hand is for. The third hand is a press on a shelf in a creditor's office, and a press holds what is entered on the shelf, and what had been entered on the shelf in the sixth month was a transaction, and a transaction has a paper attached to it, and the paper is a fair copy of the minute of the room in which the transaction was made. The county had written for it in the seventeenth week and the answer had come in eleven days to a shed at the north end of a bridge and not to an office in Kilnholt, because a shed at the north end of a bridge has a door on the side of it and an office has a door on the front and a clerk will answer a letter from a door on the side of it and will not answer a letter from a door on the front of anything.

The clerk's name is not entered in this volume. He is a clerk of a counting house of tolls in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, and he is fifty-eight, and he has been in that building thirty-one years, and he is not a Crown's man and is not in the Crown's service and has never in his life been asked to be anything, and the four sheets of conditions that came with the offer of a press say that a copy in that office is not produced in court and has not been asked for and will not be.

He entered it in eleven seconds and the eleven seconds are the whole of his reasoning and he gave them out loud to nobody, because there was nobody in the room with him, which is the ordinary condition of a man who is a clerk.

**A private book in another country is not a public book.** There is no officer of the Crown in this city. There is no register of arrests and no court that sits on one and no form on which a man may be named. Nothing in this kingdom can be got at this book except a man with a key, and the man with the key is the creditor, and the creditor is sixty-one and has a grandson of nine whose name is on the front of a four-hundred-leaf book and has not asked to see the press. And a book that cannot be got at is the only place in the world in which a sentence a man said in a room four hundred miles off is safe to keep.

So he wrote, in the fair copy, in his own hand, at the foot of the fourth sentence of the minute:

*The [day]. The fourth hour. A stranger. A motive, entered and not read out again. Entered against the name already on the leaf, being the name this office has used eleven months for that mouth.*

*Arrest.*

It is the second arrest this office has entered against that name in a year. The first is on the same leaf, eleven pages back, with a day and an hour on it and a room in it and a witness in it, and it is four words long and it was entered in a room by an old clerk who said out loud that he was entering it against the stranger and not against the marshal, because it was the stranger's mouth and not the marshal's, and because the stranger was the one who was going to be asked.

There are two arrests in this case. The sentence in the first was spoken in a hall in a city that is not on any map; the sentence in the second was spoken in a hall in a county that is not on any map; the name in both is the name of a man from a third place, which is on every map in this kingdom, and the book is in the first of the three.

Nobody in Merefen has a copy of either of them and will not for a long time. The counting house does not produce. The Archive holds a fair copy of a fair copy of an *index* leaf of the four-hundred-leaf register and does not hold this, and would not be able to tell from the index that the book it is a copy of had a second thing in it that was not on the index. That is what a press is. The man with the bar and the man with the ledger and the woman who came in and cut the seal off a letter with a knife have all said some version of that sentence in this volume and not one of them has said it about this.

And the page went back to the county, because a page always goes back, and it went to a shed at the north end of a bridge on a person who carried papers and did not stop in this county, and the hand of the Ninebridge read it, and read the four words, and did not know what an arrest was, because there is no word for it in this county either.

There is no word for *arrest* in a county that has never refused anything. She said so, out loud, to a man loading boards, in the sixth month, and he said that a thing that cannot be got at is worth a great deal in this county and that if a man four hundred miles off has written down that a sentence of his was taken, then the sentence is in a place that cannot be got at, and that is a thing this county understands and has never had cause to.

Nobody in the room where he said it understood what it was either, and the room went on, and the argument went on, and the queue outside in the wet went on behaving itself, and the man who had said the four words into a hall of sixty people in a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths had understood an arrest in a private book four hundred miles off in about two seconds.

He understood it the way he understands a load path. Not by thinking. By looking, in about a second and a half, before he had any words for it, the way he had known in a room with a stove in it that the magistrate was right about the fever, and the way he had known in a shed at the change of shift that a kiln-worker of fifty-eight had just said the answer to the question of the whole of his working life.

It was in his face for a second and a half and the physician saw it, because the physician watches a room and has watched rooms for nineteen years, and Hesper Lound looked at him and then looked at the floor.

Aren Kest did not say one word about it. Not to the magistrate, not to the clerk, not to the rollkeeper, not to the hand of the Ninebridge, and not to the notebook, and not to the man at the loading end, who had been in this county twenty-six years and had never once in his life seen a man come out of a sentence with a mark on it.

He had said *bell* out loud in a room with a clerk in it four times in eleven years and the word was in no document in either country, and a clerk in a provincial town had refused to write it down and had given him the reason, and the reason was: *if I keep it out of a book for you I am doing your work and not mine.* And here, four hundred miles from that room, in a book that cannot be got at, a clerk who had never met him and did not know his name and was copying a minute for a shelf, had put the word *arrest* against his name in his own fair hand with a day and an hour on it, in a city with no court in it, and had given eleven seconds' worth of reasoning that was exactly right and was made for the opposite reason to the clerk in the provincial town.

He understood that too, in about the same two seconds, and he did not say one word about that either.

What he did, at about the eleventh hour, in a shed, on a low stool, with a wet cloth gone cold in his hand, was this. He wrote the four words into the notebook, in the flat hand, in order, and wrote the day and the hour against them, and wrote *entered in a book I have never seen, in a city four hundred miles off, by a man in his trade, with my name on the leaf already and mine not his, and no copy will come of it and I will not get one for a long time.*

And then he wrote the thing he had actually been doing for nine days, which was the sentence about the work going faster than the argument, and the sentence about a room with a book in it, and the four sentences of the rite, and then four hundred words of the county's own figure — the tiles, the soft tiles, the boards, the four sittings a day, the queue in the wet — and he did not write the number of the people at the top of it, because he did not have it yet, and he was going to have it in five weeks, and it was going to be four hundred and something, and it was going to be the only figure in this volume that he was ever going to be proud of, and it was going to cost a man his name in a kiln he worked in every day of his life.

*Nobody in this county is going to hear a word about any of the above from me, ever, in a room, with a clerk in it, ever again.*

*And I am going to write down the one thing I have got, and it is this: a person can check a rule, and a person who can check is a person who has been asked. I have had that in my mouth for nine days and I have been afraid of it for nine days, and I have been afraid of it for a reason I could not get at until this afternoon, and the reason is that it is the best sentence I have said in eleven years and I said it in a hat in a room of sixty people, and there is a book four hundred miles off that has got four words of mine in it and an hour against them, and I have not the least idea in the world what those four words are going to cost and neither has anybody else, and a man who does not know what a thing costs is a man who has already agreed to it.*
