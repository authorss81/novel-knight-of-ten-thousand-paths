# Chapter 0053

The third of Embermonth was a Friday and the yard's count was closed at the sixth hour in the gateway with four men in it, which is how it is done, standing, because a man who sits down to count money is a man who finds some.

Tovan Kest had the week's sheet on his knee and a scrap of oak in his hand and he went down it in the order he always went down it, and the four men listened, and the man at the shave horse shaved, because that is a thing you do while somebody else is honest.

"Right," he said. "Five weeks."

"Five weeks," said the man at the shave horse.

"Five weeks, and it is spoken for, and a yard cannot be hired for a season it does not have. That is the first thing and it is not a complaint, it is the trade, and if anybody in this yard wants to be a man who works on the credit of a town that may be there in the spring, he can say so now and go home."

Nobody said so.

"The factor's stone went on Thursday in three carts and the last of it went at the fourth hour and there is a cart in the mud by the mill that I have been ignoring since Fallowmonth, and I am not going to ignore it in Wolfmonth either. The county town's wall is going and the man who owns that wall is an old fool and he has paid twice on time, which I will say for him, and that job is three weeks and it will be finished about a week before the water is at the low of the year, which is a sentence with two bad things in it. There is a culvert at Four Elms, four days, two of you, and it is a mucky job and the price is mucky and I have taken it anyway because a yard that waits for a good job is a yard that closes."

"And after the wall?" said the man at the shave horse.

"There is nothing after the wall," said Tovan Kest.

He turned the sheet over.

"Now the wages. Four men, twenty-five days, at the yard rate of seven pence the day, is seven hundred pence. Fourteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and I have added it twice and my wife has added it once and we agree, and I put it in the book in pence because it is smaller in pence and I do not want to look at it in marks on a Friday."

"And the book is at eleven marks," said Aren Kest.

"The book is at eleven marks and a bit over and it does not reach fourteen and the bit over is not a bit, it is three marks, two shillings and fourpence, and that is mine." He put the oak down. "And I have been eating a gap like that one since Longlight, and in Fallowmonth the trust's four marks and six a year would have closed it and the trust is in abeyance and the tolls are suspended, and there is a bench nine men over the wharf that is being asked to find four marks and six a year out of two marks in a chest, and so the four marks and six is coming out of the same pocket as the wall, which is my pocket, and I would like it noted that I have never once said a word about that in that hall and I am not going to."

Four men looked at the number on the sheet, which was a thing Tovan Kest had permitted, and one of them said, "Then it is a bad spring."

"It is a bad spring and I have known it since the eighth of Fallowmonth," said Tovan Kest, "and I have been rounding a figure about it for three weeks so that people would believe sooner, and I stopped on Saturday, and the stopping has cost me the good opinion of two men in this yard, and I would rather be liked than right, and there it is, and I have said it out loud because the book says a thing set and not a thing believed."

---

Then the carrier came, and the carrier had a leather bag, and there is only one kind of leather bag in this county and the four men in the gateway knew what it was before it came round the corner.

"Sixteen for the market," he said, and put it on the bench, and it was a Crown form of a series with R.C. at the head of it, and it was dated, and the date was the second of Embermonth, yesterday, and there were five ruled columns.

Tovan Kest read it standing up, which he did not need to do, and then read it again with his thumb on the third column.

"They have gone from writing to a person to writing to a number," he said.

"They have."

"Right." He read it out loud, in the gateway, with four men and a carrier in it, which is a thing he does and which the man at the shave horse has complained about. "*Object. Date. Person having knowledge. Keeper. How often.* And it is dated, and it is dated the second, and I want the date noted, and I want the fact noted that the form that came to this yard in Longlight was undated and that this one is dated and that the difference between those two things is the whole of what has happened to this county in five months."

"It is a return of a count," said the carrier. "A count of persons having knowledge of a declared object in this county. There is a space for a figure and no space for a name on the front. The instruction's on the back."

"I have read the back."

"You cannot have read the back."

"I have read the back," said Tovan Kest, "because it is the same back as the one that came in Longlight, word for word, and the woman from the office read it out in this gateway on Wednesday in front of all of us, and I have been turning it over in my mind for two days."

---

Aren Kest had been in the yard since the sixth hour and had been in it for the count, and he had not said anything at the count, which is a thing a man can be blamed for and which in this case he is not, because Toban's count is a count and there is a rule about it.

Now he took the form off the bench and read the front and the back and put it down flat, and there was a space in the last column and a ruled box under the fourth, and the box said *State the office, if any.*

"No," he said.

"Read it to me," said Tovan Kest.

"You do not want to refuse a dated form to a carrier in your own gateway," said Aren Kest. "You want to answer it. A refusal is a thing a man can be cross about. An answer goes in a book."

Tovan Kest looked at him for about two seconds, and then he took a stub of pencil and got the week's sheet up off his knee and turned it over on the back, which is the back of a sheet that has wages on the other side, and nobody in the yard said anything about that either, and it was the right thing to do.

"Right," he said. "Then here is the answer, and it is a fact and not a number, and I want that written down as what it is, and not as a figure, because a figure is what they asked for and a figure is what gets a man into a file."

He wrote, slowly, in a carpenter's hand that is not a clerk's hand, and read it back:

*Toban's yard, Weir Street, Merefen. This yard builds crossings in this county and does not inspect them and has never inspected one, and the reason is that a thing a man builds and a thing a man inspects cannot both be his, and I say that as a man who has left the bench of this guild at twenty-one years of age and has not voted in it since.*

*The number of the lines of the said form is eleven, and the hour at which I read it is the seventh, and the man who brought it is described as sixteen for the market and I have not asked his name and I have entered that I did not ask it and why.*

*And against the space for a figure I have nothing, because a count of persons is not a thing this yard can supply. I have four men and a boy and I could give you the number of the men and the number of the days and I could give you the number of the wages in pence, and every one of those is a fact about a yard. If the office wants persons, this yard has four, and I can name them, and I am not going to, because the moment a yard's men are numbered they stop being men and start being a return, and I have seen a return.*

He put the pencil down and looked at what he had written and then he put his thumb on the last line and did not take it off.

"And this," he said. "Read this out, because it is the load-bearing one and I want four men to hear it in my own voice and not in a fair copy."

"It is the second return I have answered in a year on the same grounds and the first one was in the same gateway and it is the reason the column on the front of that one says *Keeper* and the reason nobody in this county has an office to put in it, and the answer is the same answer as last year and it is not the same answer as last year."

"And what is the difference?" said the carrier, who was sixteen for the market and about thirty.

"Last year the answer was a fact and the fact was that this yard inspects nothing," said Tovan Kest. "This year the answer is a fact and the fact is the same, and the difference is that this year they have stopped asking who the person is and started asking how many the persons are, and a how many can be answered by a man with a good memory and a bad conscience, and a who can only be answered by the men themselves."

He turned the sheet round and pushed it across the bench.

"Take that to whoever sent it. And when you have taken it, I am going to say one more thing and you may write it down or not as you please, and I would like you to write it down."

Nobody in the gateway said anything, which is how a yard works.

"A yard is not an office," said Tovan Kest, "and a road is not a trade. That is my whole argument and I have been making it since I was twenty-one and I have never once been asked to make it in a room with a bench in it, and this year I have said the true thing three times in public and not voted once, and I would like whoever is counting this county to understand that the reason I do not vote is not modesty. It is that I have a brother who went off to another county on the first of Fallowmonth at the fourth hour of a Monday morning and did not tell me he was going, and I have not sent a word after him because I do not know the address, and if you wanted a man in this county who could be got at with a form you would not want me, and if you wanted a number I am one, and I would rather be the second."

---

The carrier went at about the eighth hour with the sheet, and the carrier said one thing at the gate that four men heard and that went into Toban's book that night with a date and an hour on it and no rule over it, which is the only kind of line in that book that is ever made without a rule.

"Two of the boys on the west channel were saying it in the yard at Two Elms last night," the carrier said. "They have heard the warden is abroad."

"Well they have not heard it from anybody who knows," said Tovan Kest.

"They have not," the carrier agreed. "They said a stranger came through on the first and stood on the bridge half an hour and went away again, and the boys have made up a story about it by now, and it is a very good story and it is about a man who is sent to places."

---

Aren Kest walked down to the water after that and stood at the end of Weir Street for about ten minutes in the flat cold light of a Friday in Embermonth, and the notebook came out, because there is a thing that has been standing in him for two days and it is not the form and it is not the count.

*Third of Embermonth. The road was opened on the first of Fallowmonth and has been on a named day since, and the day is written at the head of a page in a book four hundred miles off, in a guild roll here, in a day-book over a weigh-house, and in two slates, and the whole of the arrangement is four people and a rope.*

*And I went out on the first of Fallowmonth at the fourth hour and the far end of it was not the far end of it.*

He wrote that one line and then stopped, because he did not yet have the rest of it, and a man who writes down half of a thing and leaves the page is a man who will come back to it and add to it later and be able to tell himself the two halves were one thought.

So he wrote the second half as a question instead, which is a thing he taught himself in the yard in Greenmonth and has never once regretted.

*I have been four hundred miles off a road and a woman in another county has refused it, and I am not going to write down here what I think about that until I have been in a room with somebody in it, and the somebody is going to be the county, and the county is going to be the far side of a line I have agreed to and not a person I have been sent, and I have four days to make the day and one of them is gone.*

*What I will write down, because it is arithmetic and not a mood: a form came to this county in Longlight undated and one came on the third dated, and the difference between the two is the whole of what has happened in five months, and the reason it has taken five months is that an undated form cannot be tried. So a thing that cannot be tried and cannot be refused is not a document. It is a road with nobody at the far end. And a road with nobody at the far end of it is the thing I have been for eleven years, and the only reason this county has survived it is that four people stood at this end of it and said no when they were asked, and the number is eleven now, and eleven is not a constitution and I have said that out loud in a room with a clerk in it and I will say it again.*

He shut the book and put it away, and then he took it out again, because there was one more line and it was the line he would still be reading four months later in a room over a kiln, and he wanted it where he could find it.

*The woman at the far end asked me one question and I am going to be asked it again, and I know what the answer is going to be and the answer is going to be that I am not the man who sends anybody, and I want it entered here, tonight, in my own hand, while nobody has asked me: **I have sent nobody anywhere. I have been three times and I have never once sent a man, and if the day comes that I do, it will not be because somebody asked me to and it will be because there was nobody else in the room, and that is the day the road stops being a road and I do not think I will be able to tell which day it was.***
