# Chapter 0084

It took him four days, and he got it exactly right, and he was wrong about who did it.

That is the whole of the seventh month in four days at the low end of a rack in a shed at the north end of a bridge in a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths, four hundred yards from a kiln at which, nine days before, a man of fifty-eight had stood at the top of it in the dark and been asked which kiln he was at and had said *that is the one I am at*, and nobody has worked that out but him, and he did not work it out, he watched it happen.

Four days is a long time for a man who has been in a county for eight months and has spent every day of it carrying boards and pressing tiles, and four days is about two seconds to anybody who has not got a trade.

---

The first day was the boards.

He went down the racks at the low end and looked at about four hundred soft tiles under wet cloths and did not press any of them, and what he did instead was count them, which he had not done before and which the man with the ledger has been doing for nineteen years, and the count came to four hundred and nine, and he wrote that down because it was in front of him.

Then he sat on the low stool for about an hour and a half and did the thing he does, which is set out the parts, because a man who writes down why a thing is good in the same book as what the thing is has written a sentence instead of a working, and he has written that in the back of this notebook twice and it is the only rule he has brought into this county that has held for eight months.

Anchor. The roll — and it is not the roll any more, and that is the first thing that has changed since the twenty-second week. It is the room. One room, one stove, one table, about nine chairs and about nine people in it at a time, from the second hour to the sixth, with a queue outside in the wet. A working at the scale of four hundred and nine wants an anchor and what it has got is a room, and a room is not an anchor, and an anchor is the one thing in the working that does not move.

Witness. The person answering, and two whose names are in the book. **A witness that is also the anchor is not a witness, it is a second reading of the same hand.** He has written that sentence twice in two counties and it has been right three times now, and the third time it is right for a different reason, and the different reason is that the two men on the door are not the clerk, and they have never claimed to be, and the rule in the trade is that the witness is whoever would have refused, and nobody in that room would have refused, and the only person in that room who would have refused is in a different room with a box under the table and has not signed anything.

Counterweight. The county. Nine hundred and forty-one hearths. About forty men out and thirteen who did not go back and six that nobody knows about. Two kilns stopped of four. And now — and this is new, and this is the price of the sixth month and it was not on the board in the fourth month — the wages of the four weeks it took, which nobody costed, because the work was done by the county's own people in their own time and the county's own time is the one thing in this county that does not appear anywhere.

Bound. *These people are the people.*

He had written that bound in the nineteenth day and the twenty-second week, in two counties, in the correct vocabulary, and he had said then that a bound has to be the narrowest thing you can make it, the width of the split, the length of the timber, and that this one was the whole of the county and he had made it and knew the size of it.

He had been right. He had been exactly right, and he wrote that down on the second day, and it was the second day, and the second day is when he began to see what he had built, and the third day is when he found out it was not the bound that killed a man, and the fourth day is when he went and stopped a list.

---

The second day is the argument, and it is the whole of the argument, and he arrives at it by counting things in a shed.

A record that a person can contradict is not a record of that person. It is a draft of a record of that person, or it is a claim about that person, or it is nothing, but it is not a record of that person, and the difference between those three is the whole of what a person is owed.

A record that cannot be contradicted is a claim about a person. That is the other half and it took him about forty minutes on a stool at the low end of a rack and it came out of his own mouth in a shed and not out of a book, and he got it exactly right, and the words he used were these and he wrote them down in the flat hand and they are the first true thing he has put in this notebook in eight months that was not a working:

*If a thing in a book can be argued with by the man it is about, then it is a record of him. If it cannot, then it is a claim about him, and the only difference between the two is whether he has anything to say.*

And then the whole of this county, in nineteen years, in one paragraph, which he wrote at about the fourth hour of the second day and which is the shortest paragraph he has written in either book:

*The founding act of this county was fire everything. That was made in the fourth year after the register went and it took a county nine years to do and it is the single cleverest act any of them ever took. They built a record that could not be argued with. A fired tile cannot be re-pressed. A chip in it is permanent and a copy of it is a hole in it. A person's hand is in the back of it and it is in the world for ever and no man can put a word in it afterwards. They had lost a register and they were not going to lose the people, and they made a thing out of clay that a person could not take back off himself, and it protected this county for nineteen years.*

*It is the disease.*

*A county that cannot lose its people cannot hear them either. A thing that cannot be argued with is a thing that can only be obeyed, and a man can obey a thing about himself for nineteen years and at the end of the nineteenth year he has not said one word in the whole of it that anybody would recognise as his own, and the reason he has not is that there was nowhere in the county to say a word that was his.*

*And the cure is the one they have already built and have been building for four months without knowing what they were building, and it is the soft tile. A soft tile is the only record in this county that a person can change. It is in three hands now, in three places, none of them able to do anything alone, and a person is restored when two of the three agree and removed when two of the three agree otherwise, and the third is allowed to be wrong. **That is a record a person can contradict and it is the only one this county has ever had and they built it in four months by accident out of clay and a physician's four pages and a stranger they did not ask for.**

*It is being read too fast to survive.*

He sat with that for about a minute and a half, and then he wrote the sentence underneath it, and the sentence underneath it is the third entry in that book and it is the one that is different.

*It is not the bound. It is not the three hands. It is not the anchor, because there is no anchor and the room is not a thing that does not move.*

*It is the reading.*

---

The third day is when he found that out, and it took him the whole of it, and he did it by doing the sum backwards.

He wrote the parts down as they had been built, in the trade's own words, and he set the figure again, because a working wants a figure and he has never had one and has never pretended to.

Four hundred and nine. Two tons each, which is a number he had off a barrow of setts in a fen county in the last year and for which he has no reason of any kind. Eight hundred and eighteen tons, and eight hundred and eighteen tons into a bed of river gravel at the Ninebridge is a thing he can point at and say *there, that is what this is*, and it is on the right side of eight hundred, and it is the correct order of size for a thing he does not understand.

**A document's load is not in tons.** He wrote it again. He had now written it three times, in two counties, and the second time he had written down that he had noticed the first time, and this time he has written down that he noticed the second time as well, and he wrote the sentence underneath, and the sentence underneath is the third time he has written it and it has changed by one word:

*A man who has noticed a thing twice is not being careful, he is being consistent.*

*A man who has noticed a thing three times is not being careful either. He is being consistent faster.*

And then he wrote the part that is new, and it took him the rest of the third day, and it is the first paragraph in either notebook that is a sentence and not a working, and he wrote it deliberately, and he wrote in the flat hand, and it is the only sentence he has ever written in either book in which he is the object:

*I said on the nineteenth day and again in the twenty-second week that a bound is a promise, and a promise about four hundred people to one office is a shape I have had refused before me by a man in a room in another county, on a paper about a number of persons in a place that could not be asked a question. I have been sorry for that refusal for eleven weeks and I have not asked the reason twice. I am not asking it now. **I am going to tell this county the shape and the reason and neither of them is the bound, because the bound is the part I have been able to see from a stool, and the part I could not see was the one that mattered, and the part I could not see is that a promise is only as wide as the people who have agreed to it, and I had four hundred and nine of them and not one of them had agreed to anything, because I asked a hall at the fourth hour and a hall is not a person and I had the words for a room and I used the words for a county.**

*That is the mistake. That is all of it. I did not get the bound wrong. **I got the consent wrong, and I did it in a hat, in a room of sixty, and I told a woman with a stove that I would not do that again, and I did it four months later, and I have not been able to work out this afternoon whether I said it to be right or because a man who has been refused twice stops asking for a thing and starts building it.**

*A document's load is not in tons and I have written it in tons three times.*

---

The fourth day is the hall.

He went in at the first hour, before the first sitting, and Fennie Loam was already at the end of the long table with the sheet in her hand, because she was there at the first hour every day, because she was nineteen and the copy had to be in by the fourth and because she had got it into her head in eleven weeks that the list was read at the first sitting and the first sitting is the one that gets read properly.

He did not ask anybody. He did not ask the magistrate, who was four days away with a boundary. He did not ask the clerk of the assizes, who was at the end of the table with a pen. He did not ask the rollkeeper, who was in her own room with a box under the table and had not signed anything and had told him in the twenty-second week that a witness that is also the anchor is a second reading of the same hand, and who had been right about that in nine years.

He walked into a hall in a county four hundred miles from any room he knows anything about, and he put his hand flat on a sheet of paper in a girl's hand, and he said, "I am going to take this off you," and he took it off her, and he put it face down on the table, and he said:

"That is the end of the reading. There is no more reading out. Everything else in this room goes on exactly as it is."

And the room did what a room does when somebody has taken a thing off it, which is to stop for about two seconds and then look at about nine other people to find out whether the person who took it has any standing, and there was about one second in which the whole of the eleven weeks hung on Pernel Quist, who was not in the room, and a clerk of the assizes, who was, and who did not say anything, because a clerk of an assizes cannot stop a thing happening in a room and can only enter it and can only enter a thing he is asked to enter, and nobody asked him.

So it stopped.

It is the eleventh time. He has done it in a bench in a fen county and in a court in a fen county and in a hall with four hundred men in it, and the eleventh time was in a hall with nine chairs in it and a girl of nineteen at the end of the table, and it was the eleventh and it was the cheapest of the eleven and it was the one that worked.

She did not fight him for it. That is the part he cannot get round. She looked at the sheet face down on the table for about four seconds and then she said:

"Was I doing it wrong?"

And he said no.

"Then you can say what the right thing is," said Fennie Loam, "because I have been reading that list four times a day for eleven weeks and you have not told me one single time what it is for, and if there is a right thing I will read the right thing, and I have not said that I wanted to stop and I am not saying it now, I am asking what the alternative is."

And he did not have one.

That is what happened. He had come in with a diagnosis he had worked out over four days on a stool with a wet cloth in his hand, and he had got it exactly right, and he had come in at the first hour and taken a sheet of paper out of a girl's hand, and a girl of nineteen had asked him in about nine words what the alternative was, and he did not have one, and there was not one in the county, and there had never been one, and there was not going to be one.

Because the cure is a record a person can contradict, and the fourth term is a struck person may answer, and answering has to be *asked for*, and there is no column headed *answered*, and a clerk's list of restorations read four times a day is not a consent and is not an asking and is not a contradiction. It is a reading. It is the fastest mark in the county and it is the one mark in the county that nobody has ever answered.

He stood in a room in a clay county and found out that the disease and the cure are the same object at different speeds, and that the thing he had stopped was the only thing in the county that was not also killing somebody, and that stopping it meant telling about nine people in a room that the answer to a question was a silence, and that a silence in a room with a stove in it is a thing he is qualified to give.

---

The hand of the Ninebridge was on the second span with a roll of tools at her hip and he came up the steps from the hall at about the second hour and found her on the fourth span and she had seen him come out of that building about a quarter of an hour before, because she watches a building she has been near for nine months.

"That is the eleventh time," she said.

"I know how many it is."

"I do not think you do," said the hand of the Ninebridge, "and I am not going to tell you what the other ten were, because you have never once asked me a question and I am not going to hand you one now."

"Go on."

"I am going to say one thing and then I am going to go and do a kerb." She put the roll of tools down on the parapet, which she does not do. "**You have taken a piece of paper out of a clerk's hand. In this county a clerk's hand is a thing you go through, not around. I have spent nine years making a road that a man can turn a stone over on with a heel and read, and I did it with about eleven men and a kiln's worth of clay and a bench's consent, and it took nine years, and every one of those nine years the only thing standing between that road and a shovel was that everybody in this county could go and look at it.**

"And you have done the same thing in a morning, in a room, in a hat, on a girl of nineteen, and you did not ask her, and you are right."

She picked the roll of tools up again.

"It is right. **Being right is the whole of what has happened today.** You have been wrong about this county twice, or three times, and you wrote the wrong down both times in a book nobody reads, and I have read one page of it because I was standing next to you at a rack, and the wrong you wrote down is the best thing anybody in this county has produced in four years, and it took you a stool and a wet cloth and four days and a great deal of swearing, and I would have given a year's wages for it, and I have wanted to tell you so for about nine weeks and I have not, and I have been wrong not to."

"Soke—"

"**And I am a professional objection and not a civic one, and I want that in your own head and not in a book, because I do not write books.** A man who has spent eight months not asking anybody anything in this county is not being careful. He is being cheap. There is a difference and it is about nine years of wages and you have not been able to tell them apart because nobody in this county has ever asked you for anything, and I have not asked you for anything, and I told you in the eleventh month that I was the person who would, and you have spent eight months being useful, and being useful is a thing a man does *for* somebody, and this morning you were not useful to anybody in that room. You were right, and you were right at them, and about nine people in that room have not been right at anybody in nineteen years because there was no column."

Nobody said anything for about four seconds. The Kiln made almost no noise. It never does.

"That is the one thing I have got to say to you in eight months," said the hand of the Ninebridge, "and I have said it, and you did not ask me for it, and that is exactly right, and it is also the last day I am going to say it."

Then she went along the span and started on a kerb, and he carried boards for the rest of that day and did the bottom of the rack at about the seventh hour, and Bel, four years old, at the fourth row on a stool, said "again" without looking up, and he did it again.

---

It was in the notebook that night, at about the eleventh hour, on the low stool, in the flat hand, in the back, and it is the third entry and it is the last one of the four days and there is nothing after it in this county but what other people write.

*Seventh month. Four days. This is the third time I have set out a working in this book and the third time I have got it right and the third time it has cost somebody who is not me.*

*Being right was sufficient. That is the whole of what I have learned in four days on a stool, and I have been wrong for nineteen years about what being right is for, and I have been wrong in a way that nobody could have taught me out of, because being right is the only thing anybody has ever wanted me to be, and I have wanted it since I was nine.*

*In the fourth month of this year I was right in a room with a stove in it about what happens to a tile and it cost that magistrate a year of his own sleep and nothing else. In the eleventh month I was right about two hundred and six tiles and a day and a half of a wedging shed and the county lost the ninety cords and about forty men lost a day. In the sixth month I was right about the rite, in a hat, in a room of sixty, and four hundred and nine people went through a room in eleven weeks and a man of fifty-eight does not know which kiln he works in and is on the roll in three hands and correctly, and nobody in this county can do one single thing about it, and nothing whatever has happened that anybody can name.*

*Four times. And every one of the four times I was right, and three of the four times somebody else paid, and the fourth time I paid, and I did not know I was paying until a woman put a roll of tile-cutters' tools down on a parapet and told me the price in about nine seconds, and the price is that a man who has been refused twice stops asking for a thing and starts building it, and I have been building things for nineteen years and I have never once been the one who asked.*

*Fourth months and a market day back — by my own reckoning, in a county I have not been out of — I wrote this working down in this book at that rack, in the trade's words, in tons, and I wrote that the bound was the whole of the county, and I knew the size of it, and I went on.*

*I was right about the bound. I have been right about the bound four times now. **The bound is not what killed him and I have spent four days on a stool proving that I was right and the proving cost me the only thing I had left to be right about, which is the belief that a man who is right about a structure can therefore build one that is right about people, and there is no such man and there is not going to be one.**

*A document's load is not in tons. I have written it in tons three times in two counties, and I have now written down the fact that I noticed it three times, and the sentence I have written about noticing it is a sentence, and a man who writes a sentence instead of a working in the same book has written a thing that can be picked up in four hundred miles by a man who is quicker than me, and I did that in the fourth hour of a day in the sixth month in a room of sixty people, and there is a book four hundred miles off that has got four words of mine in it with an hour against them, and I do not know what they are going to cost and I have worked it out four days and I have not got it and I am going to stop.*

*Closed the book. The reading stopped today. Nobody in that hall thanked me and the girl is the only person in this county who could have stopped it and did not know for about a year that she was the reason. The rite goes on. Four hundred and nine people have been through a room and the roll has them and the tile has them and the account has them, and one of them has lost the order of his own day, and there is no instrument in this county or in any other that will do one thing about it, and I have stopped the only thing in the county that was doing harm and I did it in a hat, and a woman with a set of tile-cutters' tools has told me the price and I have paid it and it is not on any board in this county, and I am going to do the bottom of the rack now, and it is still the best work I have had in five months, and nobody in this county is going to hear a word about any of the above from me, ever, in a room, with a clerk in it, ever again.*
