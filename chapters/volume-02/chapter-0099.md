# Chapter 0099

The twenty-seventh of Greenmonth was a Saturday and it was a market day, and a fair copy came down the line in the afternoon, and it was four leaves again, and the first of them is the purchase of a county.

*Entered in the book of the counting house of the tolls of this city, in the counting house, on the twenty-seventh of Greenmonth, at the fourth hour, and made out at the steward's own cost on the steward's own paper, and entered by the steward in his own hand, and not on a fee.*

*Received: the roll of persons of a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths, in three hands, of which the third is this house.*

*Consideration: one farthing of salt.*

*The parties: this house, and a salt merchant of this city, being the brother of a salt merchant of a county in another kingdom, who has wanted a canal wharf for nine years, and whose name is entered in this house's book in this house's ordinary way, as a party to a transaction, and in no other way, and without any opinion.*

**The price is a farthing of salt because a farthing of salt is the smallest thing that office has ever bought anything with, and the office keeps a column for it, and the man who bought a roll of nine hundred and forty-one hearths has a name in a column and no want stated anywhere, and that is the whole of him.**

He is not a villain. He is a man who deals in salt on a coast and has wanted a canal wharf for nine years and has been told twice in writing and once in a market that a wharf costs money, and on the twenty-seventh of Greenmonth he was handed, by a counting house that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, a book about a county four hundred miles off with about nine hundred and forty-one hearths in it and two thousand four hundred and fifty people on a tile somewhere, **and he does not know what is in it, and he thinks it is a debt, and a creditor's book is the only place in the world where a thing like that is a perfectly ordinary purchase.**

And a creditor's press is the worst place in the world to keep a roll of persons, and the only place in the world where nobody can quietly take it out of the book, and a man in that city has spent eleven years arguing that and is this month the man who is doing it, and the argument is minuted in his own words and is on the second leaf of the four, in the same four lines as on the sixteenth, and it has not been improved on.

---

**The second leaf is the argument, and it is the reason a county four hundred miles off is being held in a city that is not on any map, and it was written by a creditor to be found in about nine years.**

*A creditor's press is the worst place in the world to keep a roll of persons and the only place in the world where nobody can quietly take it out of the book.*

*I have argued that for eleven years. I am now the man who is doing it to my own city, at my own cost, on my own paper, with my own name at the head of it, in a matter in which no court in that county has jurisdiction, which is the only kind of matter I have ever wanted.*

*I would like the argument minuted, and it is minuted, and I would like the minute put where a Crown's man will find it in about nine years. I do not know how. I have put it in the book of this house at the fourth hour on the twenty-seventh of Greenmonth, which is the ordinary place, and if that is not where a Crown's man finds it in nine years then we will all find out together and the minute will say that I said so first.*

*And I will say the rest of it, because nobody else in this room is going to say it and I have signed this. **There is no single office in that county that can add a name to this roll or take one off it. There are three, and a person restored is restored when two of the three agree, and a person removed is removed when two of the three agree otherwise, and the third is allowed to be wrong. I have been a creditor for twenty-two years and I have never in my life seen a claim that could not be split three ways, and this is the first one I have ever seen that can, and I have built it in four weeks out of my own book because nobody in that county could build it and because I was asked to and because a creditor who refuses a thing of this kind is a creditor who thinks he is a good man.**"

**It did not reach a court in that year. It reached a room over a weigh-house in a fen county on a Saturday afternoon in a market, in a fair copy, signed, and there is no form in this kingdom that a fair copy of this can be put into, and no county in this kingdom that can serve the man who made it, and it is the only document in this entire case that anybody is holding on purpose.**

---

**And the buyer of the roll is the brother of the man who owns the building the day-book is in, and Mara Vey has four pence a year, and she found that out a moment before the third leaf and sat down.**

"I know that name," she said. "I know the brother, not the man. He has wanted a canal wharf for nine years, which is longer than I have been in this room, and my landlord has said no to him twice in writing and once in a market, and there is a wharf at the north end of this town that has stood empty for six years, and I have looked at it every working day for four years and a half through the window I have watched a man stand on a slip from, and I have never once gone down to it."

"Then he has a reason," said Aren Kest.

"He has a reason and he has paid a farthing of salt for nine hundred and forty-one hearths, and he does not know what he has bought, and I am not going to ask him, and I would like it entered that I am not going to ask him, and why, and the reason is in four words and the four words are: *I work next door.*"

"That is not a reason."

"It is the only reason there is that belongs in a day-book," said Mara Vey, "and I have used it four times this year and I am using it a fifth, and if a woman in a fen who reads for a living is allowed to have one reason that is not about the case then I would like it to be the correct one. **A person who owns a room cannot be the person who finds out what is in it. That is not a rule anybody has ever printed. It is the rule this office runs on, and the reason it is the rule is that it is the rule.**"

---

**And Sarn Oyelaran asked the only question anybody in that room was entitled to ask, and it was a good one, and it was about her own post.**

"You have entered a name against a person," she said. "Out of a copy. In a day-book. With a claim number against nothing, because there is nothing to claim against."

"Yes."

"That has not been done in this office in four years and a half. I have the day-book open in front of me at the other end of this table every working day and I have never once seen it and I asked for it about a year ago for the third post and you said no and I did not know why and I have known why since the eighth of Embermonth and I would like you to say it out loud in a room."

"That a name in a day-book against a person is the only thing in this office that is not a record," said Mara Vey, "and that a third post is a post at a desk that keeps records, and that I was not going to give a woman of twenty-six the one job in this county that is about a person instead of about a document, on a year when a count of persons had gone out of this room with a *Keeper* column empty, and I was not going to do that to you and I was not going to explain it to you either, and I have not explained it to you for a year and you have worked it out in the time it takes to put a bag down, which is about a year faster than I did."

Sarn Oyelaran said, "It is nine seconds slower than a keeper of records and a copyist of twenty-six, in a building with one room in it, who has been doing the work of three for eleven months."

"Yes," said Mara Vey. "It is."


---

**The third leaf is a name, and it is the only new name in this business that has ever been read out in this county, and nobody asked for it.**

*Entered in the copy made of the index for the purpose of the said purchase, in this house's own hand, at the fourth hour on the twenty-seventh of Greenmonth, being a name in a column headed for a clerk of this house, against the fourth line of the register of this house for the season, and set out in the ordinary way: name, office, year of entry.*

And then, in the clerk's own fair hand and not in the counting house's, because a clerk of a copyist's trade is allowed to add an opinion to a copy and a steward is not:

*The said name is that of the clerk who signs this register in the hand he signs it in, and that hand is the hand his grandmother signed it in, and she signed it in the three hundred and first year, and the entry she made in that year is the only one in four hundred leaves that was ever frightened, and this is the second.*

*And the clerk has been asked whether he wishes the copy to carry the remark and he has said yes, and he has said that he does not know why he signed it frightened, and that his grandmother did not know either, and that a book that is four hundred leaves long and cannot be got at by anybody is the only place either of them has ever been frightened where it was safe to be, and that he would like that written down and would like nothing else, and the clerk of this house has written it down and nothing else.*

**Ansel Tarrant.** Twenty-one years in that house. The third hand in four hundred leaves, and the second frightened one, and the grandson of the first, and the name is in a copy of a column in a book in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, and it was read out in a fen county on the twenty-seventh of Greenmonth at about the fifth hour by a keeper of records who has four pence a year.

**No want is stated for him.**

There is nowhere in this case to state a want. There is no column for it in a creditor's book, there is no column for it in a county's roll of persons, and there is no column for it in a day-book over a weigh-house, and the four things this office has ever had about a person are a number, a figure, a mark on a tile four hundred miles off, and a line in a fair hand that cannot be put down.

Mara Vey read the third leaf twice, and then she entered it, in the ordinary form, with the date and the hour, **against a person**, and there was a claim number against nothing, because there is nothing to claim against and a county is not a party and a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists is not a party, and she wrote that in.

"It is the only name I have entered in a day-book out of a copy in four years and a half," she said, "and I did not ask for it and I was not asked for it and I am not going to pretend in the book that I could not have sent it back, and I am not going to pretend that I thought about sending it back, because I did not, and if I had thought about it I would have sent it back, and that is the honest order of those two things."

"You would have sent it back," said Sarn Oyelaran.

"I would have sent it back," said Mara Vey, "and there is a man of twenty-one years in that house who put a sentence about his grandmother into a copy on purpose, and the sentence is *I would like that written down and I would like nothing else*, and **nobody in the world can answer him except a book, and the book is in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, and it is the worst possible place for a sentence like that to be, and it is the only place in the world where it is safe.**"

---

**The fourth leaf is a schedule, and it is a page of a page, and it is the next question and it is on the page as a column and not as a revelation.**

It is the county column of a schedule of dispositions, and it is a copy of a copy of a copy, made for the purpose of the said purchase, and the county column runs to eleven other counties, and it is copied whole, and the counties are named.

**Eight of the eleven have a boundary oath on a stone.**

A long field. A boundary. A season. A stone, renewed in public, in a year anybody there can name, by men who are still alive and who can be asked what they swore and to what and in whose name.

Eight of eleven. And the other three have nothing in the leaf at all: no stone, no oath, no season, no year.

**Mara Vey looked at that column for about four minutes.** And she did the arithmetic, because she is the only person in this county who can do arithmetic on a column, and then she stopped, and she did not write the result anywhere.

"It is a pattern," she said, "and it is arithmetic and it is not a revelation, and I have got eight out of eleven and three out of eleven and a set of three that are not stones, and that is a figure, and a figure is what this whole case has been about for a year, and I am not going to be the person who notices one."

"Notice it," said Aren Kest. "You cannot help noticing it. You have been through that column four times since you sat down."

"I have been through it four times since I sat down and I have written nothing, and there are exactly two people in the world who can see it as a column and not as a story: **a registrar who sold the book, and a keeper who has a copy of a page of it.** And neither of them can say what an oath has to do with a severance. I have been a keeper for four years and a half and I have never in my life been able to say why a thing is in a column, and I am not going to sit down with a sheet of paper in front of me and eight stones on it this afternoon."

"Then it is a question," he said.

"Then it is a question," said Mara Vey, "and it is the only question in nine years that this case has produced that neither of us can spoil by answering it, and I am going to write it in a day-book as a question and not as a finding, in those words, with the hour on it, and I am going to leave the eight where they are."

*Twenty-seventh of Greenmonth, YR 313, the fifth hour. A column of eleven counties. Eight of them have a boundary oath renewed in public on a stone, in a year that can be named, by men alive. Three have nothing in the leaf. Entered as a question and not as a finding, with the hour, against nobody. No opinion. And entered, because there is a reason for the not writing and the reason is in this office and not in anybody's head: that this office cannot say what an oath has to do with a severance, and that a person who could is not going to be asked by me, and that I am the keeper of it and I have not asked, and that is all.*

---

**And there was nothing else on the four leaves, and Mara Vey went through all four of them a second time at about the seventh hour with a lamp, because that is what she does, and she came to the end of the fourth and put it down, and the thing she said when she put it down was about a bridge and not about a document.**

"There is a tile on a coping at the Ninebridge," she said, "that a man of sixty-four has swept round for forty-one years, and the county's own books do not mention it, and the county's own minute today does not mention it, and it has been in the fourth page of nothing at all in that county since before the roll was begun, and I have known about it for about ten months because a man off a road told me he did not know what it was."

"And did he know what it was."

"He said he did not know," said Mara Vey, "and I am going to be a keeper of records about a thing I have not got a record of, and I am going to write the hour against the fact that it is on the fourth leaf of a purchase in a city four hundred miles off and is in no column of it, and that is all I can do with it, and I have looked at a column of a copy of a purchase once in nine years and I am not going to look at another column of it again on a Saturday."

"You looked at the column four times in four minutes," said Sarn Oyelaran.

"That was arithmetic," said Mara Vey. "**A thing you do arithmetic on is not a thing you have looked at, and there is a tile on a bridge four hundred miles off that I have not done arithmetic on, and I am not going to start, and that is the correct decision and not the other one.**"
