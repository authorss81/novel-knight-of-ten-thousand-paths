# Chapter 0095

The sixteenth of Greenmonth was a Tuesday and it was not a market day, and a fair copy came down the line at the fourth hour, and it was signed, and it cost a man in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists about four days of his own trade, and it came into a room over a weigh-house in Merefen with a form on it that nobody in this county has any standing to fill in.

**They came down the channel and were walked in on foot, and there was no seal on them and no wrapper and no carriage, and a toll-taker on the Old Toll carried them eleven miles in a basket and put them down on a table in a room over a weigh-house and did not ask what they were, because that is a thing a toll-taker does not ask, and it is the fourth time in eleven years that a document has come into this county in a basket.**

There were four conditions on a wrapper in this case once, and they are on the page in a business hand in a city four hundred miles off, and they were given by the man whose counting house sent these four leaves.

*You will not copy it. Not a word, not a figure, not the press-mark, not the impression.* The copy is not produced in court and has not been asked for and will not be. The fact leaves this city and the line does not. And there is no motive, and a habit is a thing a man can check and a motive is a thing a man can only repeat.

**He has broken all four of them in four leaves in a fortnight and he has broken them on purpose and put it in the document, and the last of the third leaf is a clerk of that house entering his own opinion in his own hand because he expects to be asked about it in about nine years, and a man of sixty-one has permitted it, and the man in a basket in a fen county is not going to know any of that and neither is anybody else in this county.**

"The conditions are the most valuable thing in this case and they are four lines on a wrapper and they are broken," said Mara Vey. "**A condition is a promise about what a person will not do, and a promise about what a person will not do is the only kind that anybody ever breaks in order to keep one.**"

"Then what is on the desk," said Aren Kest.

"The same four leaves with a clerk's name at the foot of two of them," said Mara Vey, "and a reading on the first, and an entry in their own book on the second, and a minute of a hall on the third, and a schedule of nine county cells on the fourth, and **the conditions are in none of them, and I have looked, and the man who broke them did not write them down as broken, and that is how a person breaks a condition and gets away with it: not by denying it, but by not putting it next to the thing.**"


It is four leaves. It is headed with the name of an office of a creditor's own, in a counting house on a wharf, and it is entered in that office's own book as produced by that office, at that office's own cost, under that office's own law, **in a matter in which no court in this county has jurisdiction**, and those eight words are the reason it is worth anything at all and the reason it is worth nothing whatever.

The first leaf is a reading.

*Read out in the counting house on the sixteenth of Greenmonth, at the second hour, from the index of the register of outgoing writs, in the presence of the steward and of a clerk of this house, and entered at his request.*

*The ninth line. Tendered in the three hundred and fifth year. Press four, die eleven, second impression of the season. Returned, nothing. Answered, eleven days after the tender. The county cell is ruled and there is nothing in it.*

*The first line of this leaf. Press four, die eleven, first impression of the season. Returned, the twentieth. Withdrawn, the twenty-second.*

*And that is all there is on the leaf, and there is a name on the body line and there is no name on the index, and I can tell you the press and I can tell you the die and I can tell you which impression of the season and I have had both leaves in my two hands at the same time under a lamp, and I am not able to tell you the hand.*

**I can tell the die and I cannot tell the hand** is the whole of what came down the line on the sixteenth of Greenmonth, and it is one sentence, and the sentence is signed by a clerk of that house who is the grandson of the only hand in four hundred leaves that was ever frightened, and his grandmother signed that register in the three hundred and first year and had it in a book in a room in a city that was never built, and it has been in a drawer in a shed at the north end of a bridge four hundred miles off in a county that is not on any map in this kingdom ever since, and nobody there has read the back of anything.

The second leaf is an entry, in a business hand, with a date and an hour on it.

*Entered in the book of this house, in the counting house, on the sixteenth of Greenmonth, at the third hour, on the motion of the steward, and not on a fee, and not produced in court.*

*That the ninth line of the index of this book's register of outgoing writs and the first line of the said index carry the same press and the same die and two impressions of one season; and that the county cell of the said ninth line is ruled and empty; and that this house has this day entered the said ninth line and the whole of the said index in its own book, at its own cost, on its own paper, in its own law, and has not copied it out, and will not produce it, and has no intention of ever producing it.*

*And that no name is entered against the said ninth line in this house's book, because there is no name to enter.*

---

**Aren Kest read the first leaf nine times and the second leaf twice and had not read the third one at all, and the reason he read the first leaf nine times is that it is the only document in this case in which two facts about the same office are on the same paper.**

He has a fair copy of a fair copy of that leaf in a press in the room he was sitting in. He has had it since the eighth of Embermonth, and it gives him press four, die eleven, second impression of the season, and a county cell with nothing in it, and an *Answered* column with a date eleven days after the tender in it.

He has had a fact about the first line since the fourteenth of Mudmonth, YR 312, in a city, in a room, in a business voice, from a man with a bundle of string: that a creditor's register in that city has a line for the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, sealed with the press-mark of press four, die eleven.

"A die is a die," he said. "A press is a press. That is the whole of the thing and it is worth a paragraph."

"Go on," said Mara Vey.

"**A press-mark does not lie about a hand the way a name does.** A press-mark is a fact about an object. It is cut in the metal and it is the same shape every time the die is dressed and for as long as the iron lasts, and if two leaves of one book carry the same press and the same die then they came out of the same room, and there is nothing in that to argue with and nothing in that to feel.

"And a name is a fact about a person, and a person can be got at, and the getting at him is a thing you can refuse, and he can be alive or dead, and he can be in this room or four hundred miles off, and in eleven years he has been alive the entire time, which is the one fact about that man that nobody in either country has been able to use.

"So anybody holding both leaves can set the ninth against the first and know they came out of the same press in the same room. **And I cannot check the hand, and neither can anybody in this county, and that is not because the book is locked. It is because the index has no name in it, and a press is not a clerk, and a clerk is not a man, and a man is the only one of those three that can be asked what he did.**"

"You are telling me the die is proof and the hand is not."

"I am telling you the die is proof of the *book*," said Aren Kest, "and there are four hundred leaves of the book and one of them has a name in it, and I have that name, and I cannot show anybody in this county that the name was written by whoever wrote the ninth line, and I could not show anybody that it was not. **The ninth line and the first line are the same press. That is a fact. Everything else is a shape, and I have been burned by a shape once and it cost this town an afternoon and her two nights.**"

---

**The third leaf is the minute of the hall, and it is four lines, and Alis Merrow wrote it himself, and it is the reason he did the thing.**

*That a creditor's press is the worst place in the world to keep a roll of persons, and the only place in the world where nobody can quietly take it out of the book; that this office has argued that for eleven years and is this day the man who is doing it to this office's own city; that the argument is minuted at the steward's request and in the steward's own words and is not to be improved on; and that the minute is to be put where a Crown's man will find it in about nine years, and the steward does not know how and does not much care, and has put it in the book of this house at the third hour on the sixteenth of Greenmonth, which is the ordinary place, and which is a press in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists.*

*Entered, at the clerk's request, in the clerk's own words: that the steward asked whether the minute was to be put anywhere in particular, and that the steward said no, and that the clerk thinks the steward is a fool, and that the clerk has entered the clerk's opinion because a person who files an opinion is a person who expects to be asked about it in nine years, and the steward has not asked him to enter it, and he has entered it.*

Mara Vey read that four times.

"He is arguing against his own city," she said, "and he is a creditor, and he is right about both halves, and a creditor's office is the last place in this kingdom where a roll of persons ought to be, and he has just entered it in the only book in the world that no officer of anything can be served with."

"Yes."

"And in nine years a Crown's man will find it."

"In about nine years a Crown's man will find it, if there is a Crown's man left who is sent to look, and he will find it in the fourth press of a counting house on a wharf in a city that is not on any map in this kingdom, and there is no writ in this kingdom that gets at it, and no seal, and no Crown's man has ever been admitted to that building in seventy years." He put the four leaves down in order. "**That is the first document in this whole business that a person in another country is keeping, and he has made it out of his own book, at his own cost, on his own paper, with his own name at the head of it, and he has got it in exchange for nothing except a bad argument, and the bad argument is the reason it is worth anything.**"

"You cannot use it."

"I cannot use it. I could not use it in a court in this county to save my own life, and if I produced it in a court in this county the court would have to write to a city that is not on any map in order to find out whether the thing is genuine, and the writing would take about two years, and by the time the answer came the marshal would be dead or retired or appointed to something." He squared the four leaves, which is a thing a keeper does and he had picked it up from her without noticing. "And I am not going to ask him for a copy and I am not going to ask him for anything, and he has not offered, and that is the arrangement and it is the fourth arrangement of the four that have held this case up for eleven years."

---

**And the fourth leaf is a schedule, and it is the only one of the four that is a number, and it is a number a keeper can do something with, and she did it in one sitting with a finger, and it is the only sum in the four leaves.**

It is a copy of the nine lines of that index and of nothing else, which is the first condition on the wrapper gone, and the copy is in a fair hand and it is not signed. The nine lines are of the valley in this kingdom except one. The ninth is not, and it is dated in the three hundred and fifth year, and the county cell is empty.

*What is in the county cells of the other eight,* she said, going through it with a finger, *is the same valley, and it is the valley the Crown has been calling four thousand people for eleven years, and the eight dates are all inside about a week, and the eight lines are eight returns of a thing that happened once.*

"And the ninth one has nothing in it," said Aren Kest.

"**The ninth one has nothing in it and it is the only one of the nine with an *Answered* column filled in eleven days.** Every one of the other eight is marked *returned* and none of them is marked answered, and that means that whatever was sent to that county eleven days before somebody answered it, and it is the only line in nine that anybody ever wrote back to.

"And it is the only line with no county in it. And the eight that have a county in it were all refused, and the one with no county in it was answered, and I am not going to write a sentence joining those two things together, and I have wanted to for as long as it has taken me to read it twice."

"You have wanted to and you have not and that is the whole of your trade in one afternoon," said Aren Kest, "and I have wanted to about forty times in nine months and there is a notebook in a coat with none of it written down, and the reason neither of us is going to is the same reason and it is not a rule anybody taught us."

The four leaves went into the press, on a shelf, at the back, where a day-book is not and where nothing is that is a record of a person.

---

**And at about the seventh hour, on the same day, in the same room, two documents made in two places a week apart were on one table, and neither the woman who made one of them nor the man who made the other of them had any idea the other existed, and neither of them ever found out.**

"You have been a body corporate for seven days," said Aren Kest. "A door and a number and the capacity to sue and to be sued. I have read the minute. It is four hundred words and it says the word *office* in every line, and the founding deed is thirteen words long and it does not mention you at all."

"Yes," said Mara Vey. "And it means that from the ninth of this month there is a person in law in this county who can be written to, and can be written to by anybody with a claim, and who cannot say no to a summons, and who can be sued by a person she has never met about a thing that happened in a room."

"And a man in a city four hundred miles off has entered a roll of persons in the only book in the world that cannot be served at all."

"Yes."

"**So the two instruments made in this county in the last fortnight are exact opposites and neither of them is wrong, and I have been in a court and a hall and a yard about both of them in nine months, and I am not going to be the man who puts them together in a room, because the moment somebody does, one of the two has to be the wrong one, and neither of them is.**"

"I will not," said Mara Vey, "and I would like that entered, and I am entering it at the seventh hour, and the reason is not that it is dangerous. The reason is that I have been in this office four years and a half and everything in it has been kept on the principle that a thing which cannot be served is a thing that answers to the person it is about, and a thing which can be served is a thing that answers to whoever asks first, and both of those are true, and about nine years from now somebody is going to have to pick, and there is not going to be a minute."

---


And in the day-book, at about the sixth hour, in the ordinary form, with a claim number against the office and against no person, with no opinion on the line:

*Sixteenth of Greenmonth, YR 313. A Tuesday. Four leaves from the counting house of the tolls of a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, produced at that office's own cost, in a matter in which no court in this county has jurisdiction. Entered and not produced anywhere. The brine weights are still cited and not filed. Entered against no person. No opinion.*

*And entered, because the not writing is the part that is a decision: that this office has read all four leaves and has found one fact in them that can be checked by anybody in the world and two that cannot be checked by anybody, and that this office is not going to write down the two, and that the two are about a person.*
