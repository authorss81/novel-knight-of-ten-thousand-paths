# Chapter 0090

The hand of the Ninebridge had a sheet of paper in a drawer in her shed for about four months and she had never once read the small print on the back of it, and the reason she had not is the whole of the ninth month.

It is a shed at the north end of a bridge with a door on the side of it. It is a room with a board in it and racks and about four hundred soft tiles under wet cloths, and a drawer, and the drawer is not locked, because in this county a drawer with a lock in it is a thing somebody has to have a key to, and a key is a name, and a name is the thing this county has decided it will not do.

The paper came in the seventeenth week, in a bag, with a letter of four sheets and a fair copy of a shelf register from a registry in a province two hundred miles off. It was the wrapper. The wrapper of a form of a series of the Office of Provincial Registration, folded twice, and the outside of it said *Return of Contact* and had five ruled columns on it with the words *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper* and *How often*, and a woman of nineteen had read the whole of the front of it out loud in a shed four hundred yards away in about the seventh month and had not read the back of it, and there is not one person in this county who has ever read the back of a form, because in this county a form is a thing a person brings in with them and not a thing anybody has to answer.

**"There is nothing in this county that is going to tell me what to do with a form," said the hand of the Ninebridge, "and I have had it in a drawer since the seventeenth week, and I have carried boards past the drawer about nine times a day for four months, and I am not a person who is curious, and I want that entered."**

She read it on the ninth morning of the ninth month because a man off a road asked her for a room with a table in it, which is the first thing anybody in this county has ever asked her for in nine years.

There were five lines printed on the back. They were printed, not written, and they were in the office's hand, and the type was small and even and there was no seal on them and no signature and no office named except the name of the office in the head, which is the same office that keeps the press registers and is two hundred miles off in this county's terms and is not a mile from where a man from that county stands.

---

*TERMS OF A SECOND ROUTE. Printed at the foot of a sheet of a series of the Office of Provincial Registration, in the office's hand.*

*1. Two routes, and both held, and the holding of one is not the holding of the other.*
*2. Two carried over, and the carrier shall not be able to say which path gave him which.*
*3. A return, prepared before each crossing by two hands, and a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping the road; and if the witness is not there on the day, the route does not open, and the route does not fail.*
*4. One message and one object. The message shall be a list, and a list can only be checked. The object shall weigh under a pound, and the far side shall be able to use it, and the near side shall be able to do without it.*
*5. The second route does not answer when it is called. The far side decides. There is no procedure for a far side that has not decided.*

And below them, in a smaller type, as a standard of measurement, which is what it is and is not a term and is not about any of the five:

*a road held, three weeks*

---

He read it four times in about nine minutes, standing, in a shed, with a wet cloth in his hand, and the hand of the Ninebridge stood at the other end of the board and let him.

Then he went through the five in order, out loud, the way a man goes through a set of terms he has to build to, and he was quick about it and he did not editorialise once, which she noted at the time and has never mentioned to anybody.

**"One. Two routes, and both held, and the holding of one is not the holding of the other.** A road to a city four hundred miles off that calls itself a city and does not admit to the Crown that it exists, and a line to a county that is on no map in this kingdom. And he does not hold one and call the other a gate. I have a line and I have a stitch, and they are two ends of a piece of string and one of them does not open on a schedule.

**"Two. Two carried over, and I cannot say which path gave me which.**" He put his left hand flat on the board. "This. It reads a handrail in the dark, and it has been doing that since I was about twenty-one, and it is not a working, and I have never put it in a tool roll. And this—" and he stood up straight and put his weight a little back and his hands loose at his sides instead of in his pockets, which he has done since the ninth of the first month of his own time in this county and has not noticed doing, on a bridge in a market four hundred miles off from where he stands, and there is a woman in a fen who noticed it before he did and is not going to ask him about it. "And I cannot tell you which of those two came from the far end of the string and which came from the other end of the string, and it is written on this sheet that I will not be able to, and he is right, and I would not have been able to say which in about nine years and now I have it in print in an office's hand and I still cannot say which."

"Which is the one you would like to give me," said the hand of the Ninebridge.

"Yes."

"And you are not going to."

"No," he said. "I have four things I could say about it and all four of them are about me and none of them is about a road, and I said in a room with a stove in it in the eleventh month that I was not going to be a voice in this county, and I have been a voice in this county about four times since and I have paid for two of them and I am not going to do it a fifth time in a shed with the door on the side of it."

**"Three. A return prepared before each crossing by two hands, a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping the road, and if the witness is not there on the day the route does not open and the route does not fail.**

"That is not an office. There is no Crown's man in this kingdom who can be asked to fill that in and there is no schedule in this county that carries it. **It is a town four hundred miles off, working this out on a bench in a hall over a wharf, in about nineteen years, and working it out wrongly about nine times first, and putting it in a minute, and not being right until it was not about a bridge at all.** And I have had that on a page in a yard in a gateway in Weir Street since the ninth of the first month and I have read it about four times and I have never once said whose it was, and the sheet in your hand has got it printed at the foot of a form in an office that cannot say whose it is either, and the two of us are carrying the same sentence about four hundred miles apart in two countries and about four hundred years apart in the only sense that matters."

**"Five. The second route does not answer when it is called, and the far side decides, and there is no procedure for a far side that has not decided.**

"That is the one it will cost me. A route that answers on demand is a route somebody has to stand at a bridge for all hours, and the somebody is me, and I am the man the other end would have to ring up in the middle of the night, and I have written that down twice in two counties in a notebook and I have got it in front of me in an office's hand and neither of those is the reason I have not said this out loud in a room until this morning.

"I have not said it out loud in a room in eleven years and I am not going to say it out loud in a room in this county, and this shed is not a room. You did not ask me a question. **You took a sheet out of a drawer and read the back of it and then you stood at the other end of a board and let me, and that is a different thing from being asked and I have noticed the difference and I am not going to say thank you for it, because you would not know what to do with it.**"

---

"Four," said the hand of the Ninebridge. "The message and the object. I have read those three times and I have understood about two-thirds of it and I want to know what the county has got, and I have wanted to ask you for about a month and I have not, because nobody has asked me anything in nine years and I got used to it."

"Ask."

"What is the object."

"A tile," said Aren Kest.

---

Here is the object, and this county made it in about four minutes on the fourth day of the ninth month, in a shed at the back of the Kilnholt kiln, and the woman who fired that kiln was called in and asked and had never been asked a question in sixteen years and had answered about four of them.

A fired tile in this county is six inches by four and about two fingers thick and it carries a device and a name and a hearth and a trade, and on the back, the person's own right hand, pressed by them, in wet clay.

**A whole tile is the account of a person. Half a tile is the account of nothing.**

That is not a joke and it was not made as one. The man with the ledger put it, and then he took it back and put it properly, and the minute has it properly and it took him about nine minutes.

"A tile that has been fired once has one hand on it. A tile that has been fired twice has two, and the two of them are the same hand, and the county has two hundred and fifty-four of them and has had them for about a week. **A tile that has been broken has a chip on it, and a chip is the ordinary waste of a working cutting bench, and there are about four hundred chips off this county's benches in any month.**

"And a chip is too small to be anything and a whole tile is a record of a person, and this county has never in nineteen years had a record of nothing in it, and the county's whole difficulty is that it has nothing but records, and I am not going to carry a person to another country and I am not going to carry a person on the front of a message either, and the terms want an object under a pound, and a **half tile** is under a pound and is the account of nobody, and the county has got about nine hundred of them in the two hoppers behind the Kilnholt kiln and they are the only thing in the County of Ash that nobody in this county is entitled to want."

It was weighed at the kiln gate on a stone, in the county's own frame, on the fourth of the ninth month, and it came to a shade over ten ounces, and the weighing is the only figure in this whole business that anybody asked for, and it was asked for in the only two words anybody in this county has ever used about the north: *to the north*.

"**It is half a tile, and the half is the half that had nothing on it, because the half that had the hand on it is four hundred yards away in a box in a house in Kilnholt and it stays there.** And a keeper of records on the other end of that string can hold it, and can put it in a press, and can weigh it, and there is not one thing in it that anybody can take out of it, and she can do that for nine years and it will be the safest object that has ever been in her office, and the county has sent it four hundred miles to be safe in a press, and I have got no way at all of finding out whether it got there and I am not going to ask, because a man who asks whether an object arrived has told the other end that the object is worth arriving."

---

**The stage came on the ninth day of the ninth month in a shed at the north end of a bridge, and it did not come as a reward.**

There was no ceremony and there was nobody to give it. It is a word in a term and he had it in front of him in an office's hand on the back of a form in a drawer, and the only thing in this county that could be said about it was that four of the five lines were already true and had been true for between four and twenty-two weeks, and one of them was going to be attempted that afternoon, and nobody in that shed except the man who read them knew that a word existed for what had just happened to him.

A stage is not a thing anybody in the County of Ash has. It is not a trade and it is not a column and there is no word for it in this county and there could not be, because a county that counts fires has not got a way of counting a man.

So the only record of it is a page in the back of a notebook in a flat hand, and the only witness is the hand of the Ninebridge, who is the only other person in this county that can be asked a question about a road, and who has not been asked one in nine years until the seventh month.

She stood at the other end of the board with the roll of tools at her hip and she witnessed it because he asked her to, and she said yes, and the reason she said yes is that she is a hand and a hand answers for a road, and this was a road, and it was the only one anybody in that county had ever been asked to witness.

The boy with the slate was in the shed. He was sixteen, and he had been copying a price column and a roll of persons and four thousand boards' worth of lines since the fourth month, and he copied the page, and he copied the blanks under it, because that is what the boy does and it is the only thing anybody has ever said about him.

And when it was done the hand of the Ninebridge said:

"That was a stage."

"That was a stage," said Aren Kest.

"I do not know what it is and I have witnessed it," she said, "and I am going to put my mark on the page because the rule in this county is that a hand witnesses a thing and does not understand it, and there is not one word in this county for what I have just done and I have been the hand of a bridge for nine years and I have never once before been asked to be a person at anything, and I would like it entered that I said no to about four people who wanted me to be the hand of something else in nine years and I have said yes to this in about four seconds, and I would like whoever reads that in nine years to know the two of those are the same act and I did not notice at the time and I noticed at about the second minute."

And that is the whole of the stage and it is in a shed and it is not a reward and the room is a shed with a door on the side of it and the witness is the only other person in the county who can be asked.

---

**The message went on the fourth of the ninth month and it went north in a fair hand and it is four hundred words long and it is not a message.**

It is a bill. It is itemised. It is in the hand of the man with the ledger, because a bill is a thing a man with a ledger writes, and the county asked him and he said yes, and the boy with the slate copied the totals, and every line of it is a figure or a date or a trade, and a clerk four hundred miles off can check every single line of it, and that is the point and the point is not a courtesy.

*The first hundred words are the two hundred and six who were asked once, by a cord-cutter and an ash-driver, in their own sheds, in about a minute and a half a house, in the fifth month, the answers in the asker's own hand, no copy made, not in this county's book and not in any office.*

*The next hundred are the four hundred and nine who came through a room at the assizes in eleven weeks, in a method a stranger proposed at the fourth hour in the sixth month, and the four hundred and nine who came through nine places in nine days under this county's own fourth line, being the same four hundred and nine twice, and the second time is the one that counts and the first time is the one that will be in nine years.*

*The next hundred are the price: nine hundred cords over two years, and a cord at the kiln gate, and thirteen men, five days, five pence, and the cost of the un-firing, being twelve marks one shilling and seven pence, and two hundred and fifty-four of two thousand and something, and the man with the ledger's floor.*

*The last hundred are the road, and the witness, and the two hundred and fifty-four, and a line that is a number and not a name.*

**A list can only be checked. That is the reason and it is not a decoration, and a county that had been refused anything in nineteen years worked that out in about four minutes in a shed, and a man who has been putting lists into counties by arguing about them in front of witnesses for eleven years did not tell them a single thing about it and they got it right on their own in a shed.**

---

The crossing out was made up at the third hour in about nine minutes and the splice was out of a cooper's bin in a yard in a fen county and there is one splice in that yard and it is the only one he owns, and the toll-holder's clerk read the five terms out loud at the reeve's request from a fair copy in a gateway on Weir Street, and the second term was read twice at the boy's asking, and the second term is forty-one words long, and nobody in that yard said the word *name* out loud afterwards.

**And there was no witness at Ash's end and the county had eleven weeks to put one there and did not.**

The person the county chose in the first month was struck in the eighth month and did not come back through a room in time, because the county's own reading was nine days long and his place in the order was on the eighth day of them and the road's day was the ninth. Cobb Rennick, fifty-two, a tile-cutter at the Ninebridge kiln, was asked on the fourth day of the ninth month in a kiln shed at the change of shift, and said no in nine sentences, and the clerk wrote it down and read it back and left the word *mouth* in.

The bench does not hold a list of people who have been asked to stand somewhere. Nobody has ever thought of asking it to, because the fifth of the county's own terms says the witness at Ash's end is chosen by Ash and does not say a word about a list, and a bench in this county argues about a road and the price of a tile and has never once in nineteen years held a roll of anything that was not a roll of persons.

There were five people in the gateway of the assizes in Kilnholt at about the fourth hour of the ninth of the ninth month and the road was not there and nobody said the word *failed*, and a woman of about sixty who is on three hearths and whose fourth column says *wife*, and who is the second entry in nineteen years in that office against a person by name that is not a removal, and who had said out loud in a room in the ninth month **we did not lose our register, we lost our refusal, a register is what you have instead**, stepped forward off the dry side of the step without a word being said to her by anybody, and stood at the end of a line of tarred hemp in a county four hundred miles from any room anybody knows anything about, and looked at about nine people, and said:

"I do not know what I am standing in."

"You are standing in the fifth line," said the hand of the Ninebridge. "You are the witness at this end and you are not the hand and you are not from this road and you are on the roll with a tile of your own and it agrees with the book."

"And if I am not there on the day."

"Then it is not there," said the hand of the Ninebridge, "and it will not be anywhere near it, and nobody in this county is going to say the word *failed* about it, and I have said so first so that it is in somebody's mouth before anybody gets it out."

The woman of about sixty said, "Then I will be there on the day," and nobody thanked her, and the clerk of the assizes wrote the hour against it in a flat unhurried hand and did not improve it, and it is the only entry in that book in nineteen years that is a person standing somewhere and not a removal.

The line went out at the fourth hour and the far end of it went with it, and the road was not opened and nothing was opened, and the man at the other end of that string has been four hundred miles off for nine months and six days and there was no door in this county that day and there is not going to be one, and **the second route does not answer when it is called.**

Nobody called it. That is the part he has not been able to write down in any of three notebooks and has written down once, in one line, in a shed, on the last day, in the flat hand, and it is the whole of the ninth month:

*I did not call it. Nobody in this county called it. I have spent four months working out how to call a thing that does not answer when it is called, and a man at a cutting bench in this county had worked it out in about four minutes in the fourth month, and what he worked out was that you do not call a road, you stand at the end of it.*

---

**Four hundred miles off, on the morning before he came up out of it, at about the sixth hour, a list came down a line in a fair hand.**

It came on the fourth, and the fourth was a day the road was kept, and it came in the same channel and with the same terms and it was put in at the north end by a hand at that end with a witness at that end who is not the person keeping the road, and it was itemised, and it was four hundred words long, and it was the only thing that had come down that line in three months, and it came on a day when the road did not open at either end, because **a message is not a route**, and the county does not know that, and the county put the list in on the day it was going to be kept.

Mara Vey read every line of it.

She read it in a room over a weigh-house at the north end with a day-book open in front of her, and it took her about fifty minutes, and she wrote nothing for fifty minutes, and then she entered it in the day-book against no person with the hour on it, in the ordinary form, with a claim number against the office and against no person, and no opinion, because a figure is not a list and a list with the names taken off is a figure and this office has said that three times in four months to two different offices.

And then she read it a second time, at about the seventh hour, and she found nothing to argue with.

Not one line. There is no room in that list to argue with. Every line is a figure, and every figure has a date against it, and the dates are the county's own and she cannot check one of them, and she checked every one of them anyway, in the way a keeper checks, against everything she has, and the arithmetic is sound. The four hundred and nine twice. The nine hundred cords. Twelve marks one shilling and seven pence. The two hundred and fifty-four out of two thousand and something, with the man's own floor under it and his own sentence that he would not go past it.

She found one thing she could not put down.

It is line nine of the last hundred words, and it is a number and it is not a name, and it is this:

*Of the four hundred and nine, one has not been spoken to since the ninth week of the room at the assizes. The other four hundred and eight have been. He is at a kiln. His hearth has a fire in it on the nights this county can see it from a road. The clerk of the roll does not know which kiln and neither does he. It is entered here as a number and not as a name because the second of the county's own terms is forty-one words long and the county has kept it, and because this county has no column for it, and the county asks that the line be carried north as it stands.*

She read that line four times.

She has got a fair copy of four lines in her own hand on a desk in that room, copied exactly, in order, with the hour, entered about six months ago at the eleventh hour against no person, and she has had it on that desk ever since and looked at it about nine times, and it says: *There is a man here who fires at the kiln at Kilnholt and was struck off the roll of persons eleven months ago. He has not gone anywhere. He is at his kiln. He cannot be found because a man who is struck cannot be asked anything.*

That line on this list is a fact about the same man, dated four months after the four lines, from a second country, in a second hand, from a county that is not on any map in this kingdom, and it is the ninth time in four years that a record has arrived in this room about a person who cannot be found because he cannot be asked, and the difference this time is that the record is not asking her anything.

She cannot put it down.

Not because it is a fact about a person — she has entered a fact about a person four times in four years, in a day-book, against no person, in the ordinary form. Because of the four things, in this order, and she wrote all four of them down on the back of the list in pencil at about the seventh hour and then rubbed them out with her own hand because a keeper who writes four reasons on the back of a document has made a document about a person.

**One.** A keeper who enters a fact about a person begins, in about a year, to assemble facts about that person, and a keeper who assembles facts about a person is a person with an interest in a person.

**Two.** She cannot get a claim number for it. A claim is against a person or against a body. A county is not a party and cannot sue or be sued and has no officer in this kingdom that can take a return, and there is a man four hundred miles off who is the only person in the world who could tell her which county it is and he is not in this county and cannot be asked, and she wrote that reasoning herself into a day-book in the second month of this year in a room in this building, in her own hand, and it is correct, and it is the reason she sent it to nobody then and it is the reason she is not going to send it to anybody now.

**Three.** The office's own second rule is that no combined figure is entered in this office. There are two figures in this room about one man and neither of them has a name in it and she can prove neither of them against the other, and if she writes one of them under the other she has made a roll of one person out of two counties, and a roll of one person is the thing this whole case is about.

**Four.** She wrote in Fallowmonth, on a page that has nothing against it, at about the tenth hour, and it is one of four lines in a day-book and it is the line the whole of this case is resting on without anybody knowing: *that this office is not going to ask him again.*

And the reason she wrote that down, and she has never written it anywhere else and it is the last line of the case, and it is not a sentence about the man, and it is the reason the whole of the four months since has gone the way it has gone:

**"Because a keeper of records who asks a question has already decided that the answer is not in the records she keeps, and a keeper who has decided that has stopped being a keeper and started being a person who wants something."**

She wrote it at about the eighth hour, in the day-book, on the page, with the hour on it, and she did not enter the line from the list, and the page has got a number on it and not a name, and a claim number against the office and against no person, and no opinion, and the not writing the opinion entered, for the fourth time this year, because the not writing is the part that is a decision.

And in nine years a man may come into that room and read that page and say she had the fact and she sat on it, and the answer will be on the page, and it is: because I promised, and because I have noticed that the not asking has changed from a protection into a thing I am doing, and I have noticed that late, and the reason on the page is about this office and not about him, and the reason on the page is the only one I am going to give anybody, and I have never once in four years had a person in this room who wanted it more than I did.

**He is four hundred miles off. The road was not there on the ninth of the ninth month. The list came on the fourth, in a fair hand, itemised, and it can be checked line by line, and there is one line in it that is not checkable, and it is a number and not a name, and it is about a man at a kiln who does not know which kiln he fires, and the woman who reads for a living read it four times and did not put it down, and the reason she did not put it down is written in a day-book over a weigh-house in a fen county in a room with a door on a lane, four pence a year, and the reason is not a sentence about the man.**
