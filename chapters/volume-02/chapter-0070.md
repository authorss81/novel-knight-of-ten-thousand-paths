# Chapter 0070

The fifth of Wolfmonth was a Tuesday and Mara Vey was in the room over the chandler at the north end at about the fifth hour, because Tuesdays are a working day and because the lease had begun on the second and she was still doing the work of two rooms in one and had told Sarn Oyelaran so on the third and Sarn had said, in the way of a woman who had been saying it for four years and now had company, that one room is two rooms' work and no more.

There was one thing on the desk that was not the office's own. It had been on the desk since the second of Fallowmonth and it was a fair copy, in her own hand, with the hour on it, of four lines that had come into this office in an outer room three days before that, and it had been entered in the day-book against no person and sent to nobody, and it lay face up under the day's work because she had not had a reason to put it away and did not now.

*1. There is a man here who fires at the kiln at Kilnholt and was struck off the roll of persons eleven months ago. 2. He has not gone anywhere. He is at his kiln. 3. He cannot be found because a man who is struck cannot be asked anything. 4. Tell us what a record owes a man who is in one and not in it.*

A county called Ash, on a river it called the Kiln, in a hand that was not a clerk's. Eight claim numbers in four years and eight in one room, and this was the ninth, and it had come in on somebody else's initiative, and she had spent about two months being unable to find out whether it was a second road or a forgery, and had written that down.

---

The lane door made a noise she knew, because it was a door on a lane and a lane door is a door a man comes through and a door you shut behind you, and the noise was somebody arriving from outside who had not decided to come in.

He came in at about the sixth hour and he had a fever on him and he was grey about the gills and his coat was wet at the shoulders where the fog had got into it, and he came across a room with a long table in it and got about eight feet and then put his hand out and took hold of the end of the table, which is what a man does when the floor has become a thing he has opinions about.

"Sit down," said Mara Vey.

"I have got twenty-six days of absence to give you," he said.

"Sit down, and I will enter it, and it will keep."

He sat down. He put his bag on the floor. It had a sett-cutter's bag's shape and not a bridgewright's, and there was grey clay in the seam of it, and she saw it and she did not ask about it then or afterwards.

"Twenty-six days," she said, and wrote it. "From the fourth hour of the ninth of Embermonth to this morning. I have it as a figure in the day-book already because there is a figure in a carrier's note that came to the yard on the seventh and I entered it, and you are now telling me the figure I entered is a count of a man's absence and not a count of anything else, and I would like you to say so in those words so that the two of them are the same fact in my book."

"The twenty-six days is mine," he said, "and not the carrier's, and the carrier's is wrong by about a day and I have not corrected him and I am not going to, because he has it in a boy's hand and it is more or less right and a correction from me would put a name on it."

"Entered. Now the other numbers, and you will give me all of them, because you always do, and because I have got a book with a page in it that has nothing on it and it is getting on my nerves."

"Twenty-three days," said Aren Kest. "Days of being there. Three rollings and part of a fourth and two low waters. I have been in that county twenty-three days and I have not lost a day and I have not been there a day that was not one. Path interval twenty-three days, main-world absence twenty-six, and a printed warning of three weeks, and I have done the sum on the last two and not on the pair I say out loud first, and the sum is five days, and I have had it in the notebook since the seventh of the last month in front of four men who did not understand one word of it."

"Twenty-six days against three weeks." She wrote it. "In the last year it was minus fifteen."

"It was minus fifteen," he said, "and I have not moved it, and it is in a notebook on a bench-board in a gateway in Weir Street under a date, and the reason the sum has got worse is that I have been working, and I wrote that down in Greenmonth in a cost sheet before any of it, and I would like to be allowed one true thing that I predicted."

"You may have it," said Mara Vey, "and I am writing it down as a prediction, and a prediction entered in advance is worth about a third of what it is worth entered afterwards, and I am telling you that so that you do not get proud of it in front of me."

Then she pushed the four lines across the table.

He read them with his hand still on the edge of the table and he read them twice, and he did not go white and he did not make a noise, and she watched a man read four lines about a man he had stood eleven inches from in a shed and be told nothing about.

"That is the county," he said.

"That is a copy. Entered on the second of Fallowmonth, at the eleventh hour, against no person. The original is a leaf of a book and not a sheet, and it came into the outer room on the twenty-ninth of Longlight with a man who wanted to be recorded and I did not record him, and that is the only reason I have it."

"This is the man at the kiln."

"You have not told me that."

"No," he said, "and I have known since the third day and I did not know it until the sixth hour this morning, and I would like that entered in the same book with the hour on it, and I am aware of what it is that I have done."

He was quiet for a moment. Then he said: "I have got one question for you and I have had it since Thursday and I have not asked it, and I am not going to, because I have used up my asking in this county and I have nothing left over for a filing room."

"Which is fortunate," said Mara Vey, "because I have exactly one, and I have had it since the seventh of this month, and I am going to ask it, and then I am going to be sorry about it, because that is the arrangement and I do not break it."

"Go on."

"Twenty-six days of absence, and a fever of three days beginning on the day you came back, and a road that was refused on the ninth of Embermonth and a county that has not been on any map in this kingdom for as long as this office has been open." She put her pen down. "A man in a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths was struck from a count eleven months ago and has not gone anywhere and cannot be found because he cannot be asked. And a man in this county goes out of a road and comes back of a fever with twenty-three days of a clay country in the seam of his bag, and the two of those are in the same entry, and I have been a keeper of records for four years and a half and I have never once had a document that joined two things like that, and I would like to know whether you think they are joined, and if you say no I would like to know what you would call the entry, because I have four hundred and ninety holdings on this table and not one of them has a word for a thing that happens twice in two countries and is written down once."

He did not answer for about nine seconds.

"I think they are joined," he said, "and I cannot show you the join, and it is not a join you can argue, and I am not going to try to argue it with you, and I have spent twenty-three days in a place where four hundred yards of road is a record and the word *record* means something different than it means in this room, and when I come back into a room like this one my hands still want to do it the other way, and that is the honest state of me at the sixth hour of a Tuesday with a fever on."

"That is an answer of a kind," she said. "I am sorry. That was an unfair question and I asked it because it was the one I wanted and not because it was the one you could take, and I have taken it, and I am not going to ask you anything else about it today or this week."

"You will ask me on the second of Greenmonth," he said.

"I did not say that."

"You did not say that," he said, "and I have written a great many dates down in my life and I am not going to do it with yours, and I am sorry. Give me the fever. Have you sent for anybody?"

"The physician in the county town has been sent for and will come this evening and will be paid out of the box and not out of any office, and if he wants to enter anything in a book about you he can come and ask me for it and I will read it before he does." She wrote the hour against it. "Three days. The fifth, the sixth and the seventh. It breaks on the eighth or it does not, and the man in the county town says that is the way they come and go, and he has been wrong about me twice and right about me once, and I have told him which was which."

---

Tovan Kest came at about the fourth hour and did not knock, because a yard does not knock, and stood in the doorway of a room with a press in it for about four seconds and then came all the way in and shut the door behind him, which he had not done in this room in the whole four years of it.

He looked at his brother for about two seconds and said: "You are grey."

"I am."

"You are in that chair and I am going to stand there, and if you try to get up off that chair while I am standing here I shall take you out of it, and I am not going to touch you and I am not going to help you, and both of those are on purpose and you can ask me about either of them and I will not answer."

"Thank you."

"There is a thing," said Tovan Kest, "and it is not about you, and I am going to say it in about a minute and a half and then I am going to go and do my own day's work, and I have got about eleven days in the yard and I am going to tell you why I have got eleven and not three, and you are not going to be glad about it."

"Go on."

"The yard had four weeks and a day on the ninth and it is spoken for. That is twenty-nine days. Less the twenty-six you were gone is three days." He said it the way he said everything, which was the way a man reads out a line out of a ledger. "A county town's wall was the last of it and it is still going, and on the seventh a man came in off the Fallow Elms road and said his house was falling down and would pay in wood and not in money, and I took it. That is eleven days. It is a bad job and it pays in wood and I have a yard that burns wood for a living in a county where there is a cart in the mud by the mill that I have not looked at since Fallowmonth, and I took it because it was three days' work and I could not feed four men on three days' work."

"That is not about me."

"It is entirely about you and I have just told you the arithmetic of it and there is not one word of that arithmetic about whether I forgive you," said Tovan Kest. "I want it written somewhere that I did that sum at about the second hour this morning before I came up here, and I did it in the yard, and the yard's book has it, and I put it in the book because the yard's book has a thing set in it and that is a thing set."

"Was it in the book."

"It is in the book with the hour on it and you were not in the yard when I wrote it and I did not write your name against it and that is the first time in about a year that I have written a figure in that book that is about you without your name on it, and I did not do that for you, I did it because a yard is not an office." He put his hand flat on the table, on a fair copy of four lines in a woman's hand, and took it off again. "There is the Sedge Reach."

"Tell me."

"The scour under the nose of the third pier is a washtub. It was a bread loaf on the sixth of Mudmonth and it is a washtub now and it has been a washtub for eight weeks, and the honest repair is eleven tons of stone and a fortnight with the road shut, and nobody has read it since you went, because the man who has read it twice in this year went out of the county at the fourth hour with a roll of paper in his coat." He said it flatly and without any weight at all, which was the way he said the worst things. "The bench had a reading set for the ninth. It did not happen. So the bench set it again for the twenty-fourth, and I was in the courtyard when the twenty-fourth came round and they waited until dark, and the mark-day reading was not taken on the twenty-fourth either, and that was eleven days ago, and it is eleven days now. There is a quarter of an inch of weather in the two numbers that ring has and there is a third number nobody has, and a bench of nine that has named a day twice and had it twice is going to name a third, and when it names a third it is not going to be my problem and it is not going to be yours and it is going to be the guild's man with the levels and he will do it alone and he will send me a figure and I will have to believe it."

"Nobody's read it, and eleven days past a day the bench set. And the copying post?"

"The post is gone," said Tovan Kest. "The county's surveyor has stopped holding it. There is a line came on Monday and it is a line and not a letter and it says the post is not held and a man is to be put in it, and it is dated the third, and it does not say who, and it does not say when, and it does not say the three Fridays, and the three Fridays is nine shillings that is in the county's hands and has been since the seventeenth of Fallowmonth and there is no post for it to be in. Three shillings a week at a man who was not in the county to initial the returns. Nine shillings."

"Entered?" said Mara Vey, from the other side of the table, without looking up.

"You are not the office for it," said Tovan Kest.

"I am the office that has been entering things for this county in a day-book for four years because there is nowhere else to enter them, and you know that, and if I write it down it will be in a book on a table in a room with a door on a lane and that is the only place in this county where a thing like that can be written down at all." She had the pen out. "I will enter the three Fridays and the hour and I will enter that the surveyor's line is dated the third and does not name a man and does not state a day, and I will not enter whether the post can be got back, because I do not know and neither do you, and a keeper who enters a want where she has a fact is a mouth and not a cell."

"Enter the want," said Tovan Kest. "Enter the whole want. There is not one thing in that book of yours that anybody has ever asked to be entered and I have watched four years of it from the other side of a street and I would like one line in it in my brother's hand that somebody wanted."

"That is not yours to give," said Mara Vey, "and I am not taking it, and you have been in this room for six minutes and you have said the true thing twice and both times it was about money."

"I know," said Tovan Kest, and went out, and shut the door, which he had not shut in four years.

---

He slept for about three hours on the bench in the corner with his coat over him and she put a blanket over the coat, and she did not sleep, and at about the eighth hour she went and stood at the table and wrote two things.

The first was the return, in the ordinary form: **Out, the ninth of Embermonth, YR 312, the fourth hour. Return, the fifth of Wolfmonth, the first light. Path interval twenty-three days. Main-world absence twenty-six days. Warning of a road held, three weeks, printed on the reverse of a form of a series in an office two hundred miles off. Error, five days. Fever, three days, the fifth, the sixth and the seventh. No melody cost. No stage claimed and none awarded.** And under it, because it was his figures and not hers, she wrote: *Entered on his statement, at the seventh hour, against no person. Three figures of six are his account and are not verified by this office, which has no means of verifying them and did not ask, and the reason this office did not ask is entered elsewhere and has a date on it.*

The second was on the page she had been keeping in the back of the day-book for about a month, which was the page with nothing against it and no reason on it, and the twenty-ninth of Fallowmonth's line, and the eighth of Embermonth's line about wanting to fill the eighth ruled line, and the ninth of Embermonth's line about the three empty cells in a week. It was not a file. It was a day-book, and a day-book is the only book in that office that is not a record, and she had made that decision in Fallowmonth and had wanted somebody to ask her about it ever since and nobody ever had.

She wrote:

*Fifth of Wolfmonth, YR 312, a Tuesday, at the eighth hour. I am writing this in the day-book and not in a file and I am writing down why, and the why is that a file is where a fact about a person goes, and this is not a fact about a person. This is a thing I have done.*

*He came up at the first light this morning out of twenty-six days and he had a fever on him, and it will break on the eighth or it will not, and the physician is coming and is being paid out of a box. He gave me every number he has got. He gave me the interval and the absence and the error and the fever, and he gave them to me in that order, and he gave them to me before I asked, and he has given me every number he has got since the fourth month of this year, and there is a kind of man who reports and he is that kind of man, and I have built about eleven months of my own work on that.*

*And I am not going to ask him about the way he stands.*

*He came up the lane door at about the sixth hour and he came up it the way a man comes up a slip when the deck is moving. Weight a little back. Hands loose at his sides and not in his pockets, which he has never once done in four years, because a man on a slip does not put his hands in his pockets and a man in a room does, and he did not, and he came four paces into a room with a long table in it and had to put his hand on the end of the table to stay up. He has been standing like that since about the ninth of Embermonth, which is the day he went, and he has not noticed, and I have watched a man do it on a slip from this window for four years and I knew it before I knew that his face had changed. There is nobody else in this county who would have seen it. He is the only person in England who could have told me and he cannot, and I am the only person who has seen it and I am not going to tell him, and I have known since Fallowmonth that I was not going to, and I want it written down that I knew for a month before I did anything about it.*

*Reason, written now and not afterwards, because afterwards is how a keeper makes a document out of a thing she has done. On the twenty-second of Fallowmonth, at about the tenth hour, in this room, I wrote down that this office is not going to ask him again. It is entered. It has a date on it. It is the reason I have not asked him about a letter in a room over a cooper since Longlight, and it is the reason I have not asked him where he goes, and I put it in a day-book rather than a file because a day-book is the only book in this office that nobody can serve, and I wanted one line in here that was mine.*

*And the not asking has changed, and I am writing the day I noticed it, and I noticed it late.*

*A protection is a thing that is done to somebody, and a protection goes in a file, because a file is where a fact about a person lives, and a fact about a person is supposed to be available to the person and to a court and to nobody else. This is not a file. This is a day-book, over a weigh-house, in a room with a door on a lane, four pence a year, and there is no other room like it in this county. And what is in it now is that I have seen a thing about a man that he has not told me and is not going to tell me, and the reason he is not going to tell me is the line four inches above this one.*

*So it is not a protection any more. A protection is a thing a person does so that a thing cannot happen to them. This is a thing I am doing so that a thing does not have to be said, by him or by me. The difference between those two is about two months, and I have been doing this one for about two months, and about two months is how long it took me to write it down instead of doing it.*

*I am not going to ask him. I wrote in Fallowmonth that I would not and I am not going to, and this entry is not a rescission of that and I am going to be exact with myself here, because in about nine years a man may come into a room and read this page and say she had the fact and she sat on it, and the answer will be on this page, and it is: because I promised, and because I have noticed that I stopped doing it for him and started doing it for me, and a promise that has quietly turned into a thing I want is not a promise, and I am not going to break it, and I am not going to pretend that I have not noticed that I have stopped noticing.*

*And that is the last line I am going to write about it.*
