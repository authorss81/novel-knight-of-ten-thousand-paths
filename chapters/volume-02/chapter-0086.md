# Chapter 0086

A man at the cutting bench turned over a board in the fourth row in the eighth month of the year and said, "That is a river," and about nine people in a shed at the north end of a bridge looked at a wet board.

That is how it came out. Not out of a room, not out of a book, not out of a form, and not out of a man off a road. It came out of a man at a cutting bench who has been in that shed nineteen years, and who has cut clay out of the bank of the Kiln for nineteen years, and who has never in nineteen years had to look at the mark on the fourth row because the fourth row is a child's row and a child's row has never once been anybody's business.

They had aired the shed. That is all it was. There had been nine days of wet in the eighth month and the racks had to be turned and the cloths off the boards and the boards up on their edges to dry, and that is a thing a shed does twice a year and it took two men about a morning.

The mark on the face of a soft tile, in wet clay, about the width of a thumb: a bend, coming in from the left and turning away to the right; a wheel on the bend with eight spokes, which is more spokes than there are in any wheel in this county; and under the wheel, six short lines across a thicker one, which is a weir, and every man in that shed who has been near running water knew it inside a second and a half.

"There is no such thing in this county," said the man at the cutting bench.

---

Bel was at the fourth row. She is four years old and she does the check round and she has done the check round since the sixth month of the year and she was on her third row that morning because the third row belonged to a man who was at a kiln and could not come, and nobody had told her to take it and nobody told her not to.

She was on the fourth row, on a stool, with a finger on a mark, when the man at the cutting bench turned the board over, and she looked up and said, "That is mine."

"Yes," said the man at the cutting bench. "That is yours."

And then he said, "Who did that."

And Bel said, "I did."

Which is the only account of the matter anybody has ever given, and she gave it standing on a stool, and the man at the loading end said afterwards that it was the only straight answer he had heard in this county in nine years, and what made it straight was that she was not defending it. She was reporting it. In the way a person reports which board a thing is on.

---

Nobody touched it.

That is the first fact about the eighth month and there are four of them and the other three are less comfortable. Nine people were in that shed and not one of them put a finger on the clay. A soft tile in this county is the only record a person can change, and every man in that shed knows what that means, and a child was standing four feet off it, and it took about nine seconds for nine people who work with clay every day to work out, all at once and without a word being said, that **not one of them was going to be the last pair of hands on that clay.**

The man at the cutting bench put a cloth back over it, and Bel said, "It does not want pressing," and the man said, "No," and Bel said, "Good," and went back to the third row.

---

The woman who fires at the Kilnholt kiln was sent for and came, and she is not named anywhere because nobody in that shed ever asked her name and she has never given it, and she has fired that kiln for sixteen years.

She could give the date without being asked twice. It was the last spring, in the rolling before the low water, on a morning when about nine women were at the loading end and the boards were on the racks in the middle of the shed with the cloths off because the shed had been aired. Bel had been four for about five months. She had been left on a board the way a child is left on a board, which is to say not left, put down, and forgotten about, and about ten minutes later she had taken a thumb and gone into the clay about four inches from the edge and had done that.

"Did anybody stop her."

"No," said the woman who fires at the Kilnholt kiln.

"Was there anybody watching."

"There was me," she said. "I was across the shed with a face full of kiln and I watched a child do it and I did not go over, and I have been not going over about four months, and I have been not going over because there is a boy of fifteen in this county who copies blanks for a living and there are going to be four hundred of these boards in a shed by the seventh year and I did not want it in a book."

She said it in about eleven words and then stopped, and the reason she had come at all — and it was not the shed and it was not the man off the road — was that the rollkeeper had asked her, in the ordinary way, in the ordinary course of a morning, for the date of a thing, and a woman who has been not going over for four months can put a date down in about four seconds once somebody asks her for one.

"I have not told anybody I was there when she did it," she said. "I have told the keeper of the roll because she asked me a question about a date and I am not a person who can hold a date in my mouth, and because she is the one person in this county who has to be told when a mark goes onto a board, and because I have thought about it every day for four months and I have come to the end of what I have got, and what I have got is that I did not go over."

---

Aren Kest got the mark at about the fourth foot of it and knew it in about a second and a half, the way he knows a load path, which is not by thinking.

It is the Sable.

Not the whole of it and not any part of it he could put a name to in this room. It is the shape that a river takes above a mill on the Sable, four hundred miles from this bridge, on the mill side of the water where the bank is cut and the mill race comes off it and there is a weir with six slats in it, and he has stood above that weir about nine times in his life and has set a pair of stepping stones in the tailrace twice, and a man who has stood above a weir knows what a weir looks like the way a man who has swept a bridge knows what a missing bell is.

The bend is right. The wheel is right and has eight spokes, and there is a mill on the Sable with eight spokes on its undershot wheel, and a child who has never seen it has drawn it out of a thing she has seen four times, and there is not one person in this county who can account for where a four-year-old in Kilnholt has been.

He did not say the name.

He stood about eleven inches off the board with a wet cloth in his hand — there is always a wet cloth in his hand in this county and there has been for eight months and he has never once been asked about it and nobody in this county has ever noticed it, and it is not a cloth, and it has not been a cloth since the fifth of the fourth month — and he did not say the name of the river, and he did not say the name of the county it is in, and he did not say the name of anything.

The room wanted an answer, because a room in this county that gets a mark it cannot account for wants an answer, and it wanted it from him, because he is the one it asks.

"That is a river," he said, "and there is a mill on it and there is a weir on it, and the wheel has eight spokes and there is no wheel in this county with eight spokes on it, and I have not been able to say where I have seen it, and I am not going to."

"You have seen it," said the man at the cutting bench.

"I have."

"And you are not going to say."

"No."

The room waited. It is a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths in which nobody has ever refused anything, and a room that waits is a room that is not used to it, and the man at the loading end looked at the floor and the boy with the slate looked at the board.

"It is a very old thing of mine and I have never had a use for it," said Aren Kest, "and I have said a name in a room with a clerk in it five times in eleven years and I have watched one of the five go into a file four hundred miles off from a single stone on a letter, and the clerk in that room refused to write the fifth one down and gave me the reason, and the reason was that a clerk who keeps a word out of a book for me is doing my work and not his. **I am not saying a name in this shed. There are eleven people in it and a shed is a room and I have had one room go on me and I am not having two.**"

That is what he said, and the whole of it was true, and it was the longest sentence he has said in a room in this county, and it was about himself, and he said it in a room of eleven people on a wet morning in a shed, and the clerk of the assizes was not in the room and there is no clerk in that county but a kiln clerk with a ledger, and it is the only sentence he has ever put into a room about himself, and he put it there to avoid putting a river's name into a room, and the man at the loading end understood not one word of it and did not ask, because a man at a loading end in this county does not ask a man off a road a second question.

The hand of the Ninebridge said, from the doorway, without coming in:

"Then let the room have the mark."

And that was the end of it, and it was the right sentence, and it was said by a person who keeps nine spans and four hundred yards of kerb and has never once in nine years put a name in a room.

---

Pernel Quist came out to the shed in the afternoon with the rollkeeper's authority and about nine words, and she looked at the mark for a long time, and then she said the sentence that is the most frightening thing anybody in this county has said:

**"We have been looking at the wrong tile."**

And nobody in that shed had any idea what she meant, and she had not come out to say it to anybody in particular, and she said it standing up in a shed with wet boards on edge in it, and then she said the rest of it, which was about four minutes and which is the arithmetic of the whole of it done by a woman with a black thumb to the second joint who has counted fires for nineteen years and has never once been asked to count anything else.

"Sit down, all of you, and I am going to say a thing and I am going to be short about it because I have got the assizes behind me.

"**Every record in this county, with one exception, can be changed by the person it is about.** The soft tile — that is the exception the other way, that is the one a person can change, and that is why we have spent four years putting people back through a room, and it is right and it is the best thing anybody in this county has done and I will not have it said otherwise in my shed.

"And a fired tile cannot be changed. **A fired tile is the only record in this county a person cannot alter. Not the clerk. Not me. Not a magistrate. Not the county.** It is the only one. And that is why this county has trusted it for nineteen years, and it is why I have been proud of this roll for nineteen years, and I have just worked out in a wet shed in about a minute what that means and I am going to say it and then I am going to go back to the assizes.

"**A record a person can change is a record of that person. A record a person cannot change is a claim about that person. And we have spent nineteen years firing things, and every single thing we fired was a claim, and I have never once in nineteen years had a person able to argue with one of them, and I thought that was the safety of this county.**"

She put her hand flat on the end of a rack.

"It was the disease. It is the disease. The whole of the softening for four years has been a county reading claims about people, four hundred and fifty-odd of them a year, and there has been no arguing with any of them, and the man with the ledger has been right about every figure in this county for nineteen years and he has never once been right about a person."

And then she said the thing that nobody in that shed wanted to hear and that she said anyway, in a keeper's voice, about a record, and it is the sentence the whole of this turns on and it is not a moral sentence when she says it:

"**A child draws what has been in front of her face. And in about three years this board is going to be fired, and the mark on it is going to be a hand, and the river is going to be gone, and there is not going to be one thing in the world that says a woman of Kilnholt put a bend and a mill and a weir on the only account of herself that anybody will ever be able to take off her.**

"And there is no instrument. I want that said in this shed by me and not found out in nine years, and I have said the same shape of this about the roll twice in nineteen years and I have been wrong both times and I have been right twice, and I would like a clerk to enter both of those and not tidy them into one."

She said it to nine men and a woman and a boy of fifteen and a child of four, and the child of four was looking at a rack and not at any of them.

---

He did not sleep that night and the reason was not the river.

It was that the woman who fires at the Kilnholt kiln watched a child do it and did not go over, and about nine women were at the loading end, and the mark was a bend and a mill and a weir, and a four-year-old had been in a shed with about four hundred boards on racks within reach, and every one of those four hundred boards is a record a person can change, and there is not one person in this county who could say to a four-year-old, *you may not do that there, and here is why*, and the reason is that a soft tile is not a county's and a board is not a county's and a shed is not a county's and nothing anybody would want to keep a child from is, in this county, a thing that belongs to anybody.

He wrote it in the notebook at about the eleventh hour, in the flat hand, at the back, and he wrote the twelve lines and the last of them is the one that is going to be read in a month by a woman in a fen who reads for a living and has not been told and is going to be told by a form:

*Eighth month. A child of four has drawn a river on a board in the fourth row and there is no such river in this county and the wheel has eight spokes and the weir has six slats and it is four hundred miles from this bridge and I have not said the name of it in a room and I am not going to and I have told eleven people why and the reason was a file four hundred miles off and a clerk in a provincial town who refused to write down a word for me, and I have never told this county any of it and I told eleven people in a wet shed a fifth of it today, and I would do it again, and I am aware that the sentence I used was the longest thing I have said in a room in this county and it was about me and not about them.*

*And here is the thing I cannot put down, and I am putting it down because a man who does not put it down gets it put down for him.*

*There is no river here. There is a river in my county that my brother knows and a woman in a fen knows and about four men in this room know of, and a four-year-old has it in wet clay about four inches from the edge, and it will be fired in three years, and after that it will be a hand and a hand is what a fired tile is for, and a county of clay has just been shown what a claim about a person looks like when it is made by somebody four years old who was not trying.*

*I am going to have to tell somebody. Not the name. **The fact.** That there is a mark on a board in the fourth row in this county that nobody in this county can account for, and that a child made it, and that the date is the rolling before the last low water, and that a woman at the Kilnholt kiln saw a child do it and did not go over.*

*There is nowhere in the County of Ash to put that. Not the roll — it is a roll of persons and the child is not on it and will not be for three years. Not the bench — a bench votes and this is not a thing anybody can vote about and if it were voted about there would be a column with a number against it. Not the assizes — a fact about a person goes into a book in a room and a room is where a thing like that ends up, and I have been told that by the person who keeps the road and she was talking about a different thing and she was right.*

*There is no column for a thing that is true and about nobody. **I have now been in this county eight months and I have found two of those and I have written them down and I have not been able to put either of them anywhere, and the man with the ledger has been putting them in a book for nineteen years and his book is a press and a press is the only thing in the world you cannot quietly take out of, and that is why the man with the ledger is the only man in this county who has never once needed anybody to believe him and never once had anybody, and I have been here eight months and I understand about a third of that and the third is enough to write it down.**

*Closed the book. There are about four hundred soft tiles in this county between here and Kilnholt and one pair of hands and mine, and one of them has a river on it, and it is the only one of the four hundred that is not a record of anybody, and I am going to do the bottom of the rack now.*
