# Chapter 0046

The paper went north on the twenty-seventh of Longlight at the first hour, and she wrote it in the fair hand, and it was four hundred words, and she had spent two days on it and had thrown out a version of it that was nine hundred and had kept this one.

It went to no person. It was addressed *to the city of Bellweather, and to any person keeping the road agreed with it*, and it went under the fourth term of an agreement made in a hall in another world by a bench and a ferrywright and a clerk, on the eighteenth day of the ninth month of a year this office does not keep.

*That the keeper of the Bellweather Claims Archive, in the county of Merefen, in the north, has entered in her own day-book on the twenty-second of Longlight, YR 312, the fact that a chain of four documents in one order, with a date and an hour against each, was made on that day and is held in three places in this county; and that the first of the four is a sworn account of the thirtieth of Mudmonth, YR 312, entered in a magistrate's court in this county with a claim number; and that the second is a finding of a court of this county, dated the fourth of Greenmonth, YR 312, in which that court has recorded that an instrument sealed by an office it could not name was lawful, and has asked that office to produce its authority, and has not been answered, and that the asking is dated; and that the third is a minute of a bench of a town's guild in this county, dated the fifteenth of Longlight, YR 312, at which eight men and a reeve resolved to keep a crossing at their own cost and recorded that they are not its owner; and that the fourth is a statement made by the maker of the chain in his own hand on the same day and not held in any office and not copied.

*And that the said keeper states that she is not asking the city to produce anything, and has not asked, and will not ask, and has entered this paper in the terms of the fourth term of an agreement to which she is a party in her own name and not as a keeper, which is that any paper out of the north addressed to this city comes to the person keeping the road and is answered by her or not at all.*

*And that this office will answer a question put to it. It has answered four this year. It answers the fifth, and it answers it in this form, and it does not ask for a name in exchange.*

*And that the spine of the chain is a phrase, and the phrase is* the office of keeper of the Bellweather hinge, *and the phrase is in three of the four pieces and is in none of them by itself, and it reached this county free, on the back of a returned paper, in the hand of a Crown's own clerk, in order to refuse the paper properly, and the county clerk's refusal is dated the eleventh of Longlight and is in the guild's roll and was read out loud in a guild hall to eight men and a reeve.*

*Entered at the second hour of the twenty-seventh of Longlight, YR 312, on the office's own account, against no person, with no opinion, and sent by the hand of a man of this town who is not a clerk and is not to be named in this paper and has not been asked what it is.*

Sarn Oyelaran read it back and initialled the copy and said, without being asked, "That is the best paragraph of English I have ever copied and it is a list."

"It is a list," said Mara Vey. "That is what it is for. I have spent the whole of this year refusing to write a motive and I am not going to start in a paper that is going into another world, because a motive is a thing a clerk can be got to change and a list cannot. When it comes back there will be a thing in it or there will not be, and either way it will be a thing I can read out in a room."

---

Then the six days went, and they went the way six days go.

The writ of the ninth of Longlight was still running. The second writ, on the reeve's office, was running with it, and a clerk in the county town had a third one in a tube and had not sent it, and the county clerk of Merefen, who was the wrong man for the job in a way that nobody had noticed for eleven years, wrote a note about the reeve's records on a sheet of county paper and put the sheet in a drawer and told the reeve about it at the third hour of the second of Fallowmonth in a corridor, out loud, in front of two men, because he is a clerk who does not leave things in drawers and who has never in his life understood why he ought to.

The town said the chalk. There were six lines on the Market Square board without a rule and the clerk had stopped wiping the lower half, and Tovan Kest had been down at the Old Toll on the twenty-sixth of Longlight with four men and a dressed stone and a splice he had cut out of a cooper's bin, and had written a road in his yard's book for the first time in nineteen years, and had not told his brother, and would not.

And on the second of Fallowmonth at about the second hour of the morning, the stone of the Old Toll crossing came up a quarter of an inch and set down on the other side of itself, and nobody in Merefen has ever been able to say why, and there is a description of it in a yard's book in a carpenter's hand with a date and an hour and a rule over it, and it is the first document in this county that says a thing nobody here can do anything about and nobody here has ever tried to conceal.

---

The fourth hour of the first of Fallowmonth, at the lowest water, on the Old Toll deck.

There were seven people on it, and every one of them had a job, and four of them had been told in advance and three of them had not.

Tovan Kest held the near end of the line. Reeve Aldis Fenn stood at his left shoulder with a sheet and a rule, and she was the independent witness of this side, and she had come in a cart and had left the county's seal in the cart because a county seal is an office and she had learned that in Greenmonth. Sarn Oyelaran had a lamp and a notebook and had been told nothing at all about what she was here for, on purpose, because Mara Vey had worked out in a filing room on the twentieth of Longlight that the only person in a thing who cannot be got to change is the one who does not know what the thing is.

Mara Vey had a bar in her right hand, which she had asked for and been given and had not expected to be able to lift, and had been able to lift.

And Aren Kest was on the deck with his left hand on a dressed block in the fourth course of the parapet, cut and set by four men of a yard on the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of Longlight on the first of the two visits a year that the bench of the Merefen bridge guild voted on the fifteenth of Longlight and works at its own cost out of its own materials and its own men's time, with forty yards of tarred hemp through the abutment on a splice cut by a carpenter out of a cooper's bin, and set on a bed of lime and oak dust because a specification in the guild's man with the levels' own hand had said so in its fourth line.

The specification's eighth line said: *that a seat so cut will be used and will fail, and that the number of uses is not stated here, because it is not a thing this bench may know.*

It had been used once, at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of Longlight, by this man, going the other way, and it was the second of three and there is no third, and the man who cut the glass told him so in his own hand on a page he would not charge for and he has carried the number about since Greenmonth and told a bench of nine rather than keep it to himself.

---

He did not go through the glass. There was no glass. The last of the two discs had been used twice and had cracked a bed of lime in the fourth course and been prised out and carted away by a man in a wagon in Greenmonth who told Tovan Kest it was broken and Tovan Kest had believed him because it was broken.

What came through on the first of Fallowmonth was a line, and two ends of it, and the whole of the trade in it.

Tovan Kest had the near end. A hand on the far end of a line cannot be in two places, and if the hand at the far end is not there the line comes slack and the road does not open, and it does not fail and it does not tear and it simply does not open, which is the best kind of not opening there is. That is the whole of a stitch. Not a hole in a stone. A hand, at each end, on a day, with a length of line between them, and the day in a book at both ends and a person against it.

Four and a half seconds. He felt it in the back of the left hand before the glove and he counted them, and on the fourth second somebody's voice came up out of the air on the north side, very close, in a trade's flat register, saying a name he had not heard in six months and twenty days.

"—that is the man and not a message. Say it again slowly on this side, and the woman on the far bank has to hear a name, because a name is the only thing that is a person."

"Go on, then," said Aren Kest, out loud, on a bridge, to nobody. "The line is holding. I have got four of you on a deck in the middle of a market day and I want the page first and I want the conditions on it read out loud before anybody touches it."

---

It came through in a wrapper of oiled linen with a cord and a wax that was not a Crown's wax and had a device on it that was a boat with a rope in it, and it was the size of a folded shirt and it was heavier than that.

The four conditions were on the outside of the wrapper, in a fair hand, in a clerk's hand, and the hand was not the hand of the person who wrote the arrest.

*No copy of anything.*

*A fair copy in this office's own press, unsigned, not produced in court, and held in this city.*

*The fact leaves. The line does not.*

*No motive.*

*And entered on the twenty-second day of the ninth month, YR 312, at the second hour, in this city, that the fourth of the above was broken in this room by a stranger from the north, in the mouth, four words into a fourth sentence, and that the clerk of this office stopped him and entered the arrest against him and not against the man whose hand signed the line; and that the said clerk has been a clerk in this office for sixty years and has entered that before and has not entered an arrest in nineteen years.*

Reeve Aldis Fenn read that out loud on a bridge deck in Merefen at the fourth hour of the first of Fallowmonth, twice, in the voice she uses for a minute, and nobody in Merefen understood a word of the trade in it except two people and neither of them said anything.

Then the cord came off.

The wrapper inside said four lines and the four lines were a fair copy of a fair copy of a line in a book in a city four hundred miles off, and the clerk's hand that had copied it was beautiful in the way a frightened clerk's hand is beautiful, and it had been copied twice, because the second copy is the one that goes.

*A writ severing the crossing at Alder Vale and its dependent places, and the removal of the persons found within the sealed limit, and the handing of the arch to officers of that office, and the tolls of the arch released to that office for the term of the severance. Tendered on the fourteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, and tendered against the arch. Returned on the twentieth. Withdrawn on the twenty-second.*

*Press four. Die eleven. Impression two of the season.*

*In the column headed* tendered by: *Edric Hunn, marshal.*

And on the back of the fair copy, in the same beautiful hand, four more lines, which are not a copy of anything, and which are the reason a creditor's office can send a page out of a city and a woman in a fen can hold it in her hand:

*That the copy was made in the fourth press of the counting house of the tolls of this arch, at the fourth hour, on the last day of the low water, and is not produced in court, and is held in this city, and is a copy of a copy of a line in a register, and the register is not produced and has not been asked for and will not be.*

*And that the four conditions were kept in this city for six months and twenty days and were broken once, in a room, by the stranger, and that the arrest was entered against the stranger and not against this office, and that this office has not asked the stranger for anything in exchange for the conditions and would have had to be able to prove in a court that it had not.*

*And that the stranger is the reason the copy was made and that this office has not said so in writing before and has now said so in writing and would like the saying entered with the day and the hour and would like the day and the hour to be the ones on the sheet.*

*And that the woman who keeps this road says that the argument in the north is not about this city, and that this city has spent six months and twenty days arguing about whether it is about this city, and that it is not, and that when it turned out not to be about this city it became a question about whether to answer a list of four documents off the back of a county clerk's refusal, and that a man on a bench in this city put that paper face down on the table at a sitting of the bench and said* not here, *and that nobody on that bench has ever read a page of that register and none of them ever will, and that the four of them who wanted to read it did not get up from the table.*

Mara Vey read it twice on a bridge deck and gave it back to the ferrywright, who was standing on the Old Toll deck with her hand on the line and iron dust in the creases of her hands and an oiled linen wrapper under her arm, and who had carried a page out of a city that had spent eleven years refusing to produce one, across a road, through a hole in a bridge, on a line, with a splice cut out of a cooper's bin in Weir Street.

"You have a name in your hand," said Sera Quen.

"I have had a name in my hand once before in a room with two of your clerks in it," said Mara Vey, "and I said it out loud, and the man with the mallet read it out in a hall, and a clerk in a county town two hundred miles from here read a particular off a wrapper in Greenmonth and wrote the words* the marshal's emergency office *into a file of the superior court, and that is how a Crown's writ of enquiry into a record office got issued in Longlight. So the name has been in a file in this country for five months and nobody in it knew where it came from, and this afternoon it has come out of a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists, and it has come out through a line and two hands, and in about a year somebody in a provincial office is going to put those two facts side by side on a table and it will be the best day of his life and it will be the worst day of mine."

"Then it should not have come," said the ferrywright.

"It should not have come and it did come and it is in my hand and it is going in a press over a salt merchant on Friday morning in front of a copyist of twenty-six years old, and I am not going to pretend to you that I have not thought about burning it, and I did think about it on a bridge about four minutes ago, and I want you to know that I thought about it, because a woman who has thought about burning a thing and has not burned it has to say so to somebody or it is a thing she has kept to herself and kept things to herself is the whole of the trade she is accusing other people of."

Sera Quen looked at her for a moment.

"Four minutes is quick," she said, "and I have known you eleven weeks and you have been in my city twice and you have never once told me a thing I did not already know, and the last of that was six months ago and it was a bench order with a date on it, and I wrote the date in a book on the north bank and I wrote *not here* under it, in a book, in a hand, with the hour, because that is what a road is. You are not the mechanism. I told a hall eleven weeks ago that the north does not get to decide what the road is for, and I have not said a word about it since, and the reason I have not said a word about it since is that there has been nothing to say, and today there was a list of four documents from a county clerk's refusal and a page with a name on it, and neither of them is about my city, and both of them are the answer to a question, and I would have been ashamed to have answered it out of anything but a schedule and two hands and a person at each end."

She took the line up off the coping where Tovan Kest had it and did not let go of it, and looked north, and the arch was not there and the north bank was not there and the air was ordinary air on a Wednesday afternoon on a bridge in a fen county with the tide out.

"We have to have another one," she said. "Not this. Another one, on a day, with a date on it, and both ends written down before anybody moves. And you will have a steward at your end and I will have a steward at mine and a witness at each end who is not the steward, and if the witness at my end is not here on the day then the road does not open, and it does not fail. It does not open. Do you understand that?"

"I do."

"Then say it back to me," said Sera Quen, "because you are the man who cut the seat in the stone and I am the woman who keeps the road, and the difference between those two facts is the entire of what has gone wrong in this business for eleven years, and I am not going to have it go wrong in a line."

"I did not open the road," said Aren Kest. "The road opened because four men of a bench in a fen county cut a block into a parapet and paid for it out of a chest with two marks in it, and a carpenter held one end of a line and you held the other, and I was in the middle of it with my hand on a stone doing nothing at all. I translated. That is the whole of what I am for and it took me six months and twenty days and a third of three to find out."

And the woman who keeps that road wrote it in the book on the north bank that evening, in a living hand, with the hour, and it is the second entry under a date that has been blank for eleven years, and the entry says:

*First of Fallowmonth, at the fourth hour. The road was opened and it was kept by a person and the person was a man of the north with a bar, and the north side was held by a carpenter of Merefen and this side was held by me and there was a witness at each end and the witness at this end was the toll-clerk and he read the wrapper out twice before anything was touched. A paper came. It is a copy of a copy and it is held in this city and it is not produced in a court in another country and I have read it and I do not understand a word of the trade in it. I have written the date and the hour and the name of the person who kept the road because a road that is not written down is a door, and I have been told that twice in a hall and I am writing it down.*

And the argument that the argument was not about the city had gone out of the city four hundred miles behind a man with a bar on his shoulder, who had gone out on a Wednesday in a dry month in a fen county at the lowest water of the year, and the city had spent six months and twenty days finding out.
