# Chapter 0557

**A street in a market town of about two thousand people had a market in it on the fourteenth of Greenmonth, YR 320, a Thursday and a market day, between the second hour and the seventh, and a woman of about thirty-eight who had walked nine days with a foot of earth in a pot said one thing in it that nobody had said in that room in about five months, and it was not about the letter, and it was not about the certificate, and about nine people were within about four paces of her at the time and about nine people in this town of about two thousand were not within a mile of her at any point that day.**

Her name is Ilsa Wend and she keeps a block of ground in the Long Set, which is a portion of a terrace between two head stones and not a terrace, and she has walked nine days to get here and nine days is not the whole of the time it took her to decide, and she has a piece of bark tied to her belt that she has not explained to anybody and has not been asked about.

Nobody asked her name. The copyist of about twenty-six asked it, because the copyist asks everybody's name and writes it down and does not read it back, and she said it, and it went on a bare leaf with no head over it, and nobody thanked her for saying it.

**And then she said the thing that the rest of this is built out of, and she said it to a man of about thirty-eight with a satchel who had asked her about the ground she kept, and he asked it because he wanted to know about the ground and not about anything else, and nobody in that market heard it but about four people standing near the fish board, and those four have said since that they were not close enough to hear it properly and have not corrected themselves.**

“**I would like to change my mind about something,**” she said, “**and I do not know what I would change it to, and I have not been able to say that out loud anywhere before today and I have said it here in a street because there is a woman here who sells fish and I have been in this market for about an hour and the woman has not asked me a single thing about where I am from.**”

Nobody thanked her. The woman who sells fish said *there is nothing to ask, love, it is a market*, and went back to her board, and that was the whole of it and it was the only courtesy anybody offered her in that market town and she has not told anybody that it mattered and it did.

**Then the man of about fifty-eight asked her about the graft, because she had brought it up herself and nobody had asked her to.**

She grafted four years ago in the second week of the set sitting. She took a variety the Standing had called a bad risk and she put it on a block that had never held anything but the ordinary kind, and it took, and the Long Set ate well for two years and better than the Wet Set in a bad year, and the Standing told her at the end of the second year that she had done it wrongly, because she had not asked, and not because the graft was wrong.

“**And I cannot say what I think about that,**” she said. “**That is the part I have not been able to say for four years and I would like somebody to write it down exactly as I say it. When I open my mouth to say what I think, the sentence comes out in the oldest voice in that valley, and I can hear it come out, and it is not mine. It is not anybody's now. It is the voice everybody in that valley was given at the table when they were small so that they would never have to raise it, and it is a good voice and it is very calm and it is about four generations of people who decided what to say on your behalf, and I have had it in my mouth since I was about nine.**”

**Nobody in that market answered her. About four of the nine people within earshot of her afterwards agreed with her and about five thought she was a woman who had come a long way and was homesick, and the two sets do not overlap. The four of them did one thing afterwards because of it and the five of them did another: the four of them stopped asking her about the letter and began asking her about the water, and she answered that for about as long as it takes a man to walk nine terraces, and the question in that market stopped being about a certificate before the seventh hour.**

**And Aren Kest asked her a question, and it was the wrong question, and that is said here before anybody in that room can be polite about it.**

He asked her what the Standing would say if she asked to be the person who answers for a road in that valley.

She said they would be pleased. She said it without any bitterness at all and she said it the way a person reports the weather. **And then she said the thing about it, which is that being pleased is not the same as being allowed, and that in that valley the two are kept in different buildings and that nobody has ever been in a position to say which one is which.**

“**How do you know that?**” he asked, which was the second of his two questions.

“**Nobody told me,**” she said. “**I worked it out. I have been working it out since I was about nineteen and I have never had it in front of anybody who was in a position to tell me I was wrong, and that is not the same as being right, and I would like somebody to write down that it is mine and not on anybody's sheet.**”

Nobody thanked her. The copyist of about twenty-six took a bare leaf and wrote it and ruled no head over it and read none of it back, and there was a disagreement in that room about whose question had been the better one, and the disagreement was not about her and was not settled and the room did not vote.

**And in the middle of all of that a man of about forty-three came into the market with a sheet of paper in his hand and asked the copyist to copy it, because that is what the copyist is for in a market town and has been for about nine years.**

It was a return with three of its four heads filled. He had brought it back from the clerk of about twenty-eight in the next county and the clerk had written **not known** in the third head in a good hand, and the man wanted a clean copy of it for his own records because the clerk's hand was not his clerk's hand.

Nesta Braith looked at it for about as long as it takes a woman to say four and then said the thing that her whole trade is, and it took about nine words.

“**I will copy the first head and I will copy the second and I will copy the third exactly as it stands, including a man writing *not known*, and I will not rule the fourth, because it is on your paper already and it is empty and it is not mine to rule and it is not mine to fill.**”

“**It is only a line,**” the man said.

“**It is the line that has been empty in nine counties for about five months,**” she said, “**and I have ruled it four thousand times and I have never once been asked what it is for, and today somebody in this market is telling me it is only a line, and I am not going to argue with a man about a paper he has to walk home with.**”

She copied the three heads and left the fourth alone and gave it back and did not charge him for the copy because she has never charged anybody for a copy, and the man went off up the street holding it in both hands, and nobody in that market thanked her and about four people saw the exchange and about nine did not, and she went back to the fish board and stood next to the woman who sells fish for the rest of the hour without saying anything.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the copyist of about twenty-six's, and it is the first one she has ever kept, and the oldest voice in a valley is not in it and has no price.**

**a sheet of blotting paper at two, a pot of tea at five, a second pot of tea at eight, a third broom at nine, a sheet of foolscap at fifteen, a second sheet of foolscap at sixteen, a pound of candles at twenty, a rule of brass at twenty-six, a bundle of kindling at thirty, and a stone of tallow at twelve.** Two, five, eight, nine, fifteen, sixteen, twenty, twenty-six, thirty and twelve is one hundred and forty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and forty-three pence is two marks and forty-seven over**, and four years and about nineteen years and about nine terraces are three figures of time and of distance and none of them is a price and none of them is in this column, and the two halves of that little crowd are the same nine people counted under two opinions and are not a figure of anybody, and a piece of bark tied to a belt is not in it, and the fourth head of the Crown's form is on the side table in the room over the saddler's and is empty and does not appear in a column in a street.

At about the seventh hour the pot went back on to the cart for the road to the inn and the woman went with it and did not ask anybody to walk with her and nobody walked with her, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet asked her one thing at the door of the inn, which was whether the Standing would want to see a certificate, and she said that she did not know and that she would find out, and he wrote it on a bare leaf and read none of it back.
