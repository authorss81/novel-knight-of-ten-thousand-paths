# Chapter 0563

**The Long Set, the fourth hour of the swell sitting, eleven terraces in the lower part of a slope with about two hundred fruit trees on it and a press-house standing at the foot of it, a man of about forty-four with two hoes and a third knife, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who was put to work in the first hour and was no use in the second.**

They put him on the second terrace with a woman of about thirty-one and a boy of about fifteen and a man of about forty-four, and the man of about forty-four handed him a knife with a wooden grip gone black and told him to take out the wood that is not in the way of the fruit, and told him that in those words, and told him it twice in the same words.

“**The wood that is not in the way of the fruit,**” he said. “**Take out anything that is not in the way of the fruit and leave the rest and do not ask me what the rest is until the fourth hour, and then ask me.**”

Nobody thanked him and the man of about thirty-one said the one thing that the man of about forty-four had not said, which was that in a valley with about eleven terraces and about two hundred trees on them there is not enough hands in the swell sitting to do it right, and that they had got a wrong pair of hands, and that she was sorry about it and was not going to be nicer about it than that.

**And he was good with his hands for about nine minutes and then he was not.**

He could see the whole of what he was doing and he could not do any of it. The stone of that valley was about a hundred years old and laid by people who knew what a lip was for, and he had spent eleven years under spans of it, and the fruit on that slope was a thing he had never once cut in his life, and the difference between a bridge craft and a pruning knife is that a bridge is cut in the direction you already believe in and fruit is not.

He took out a limb that ought to have gone and the wrong side of the limb, and the woman of about thirty-one said nothing and did not stop him, and he took out a second one the wrong side, and the boy of about fifteen said *that is a good one* in the voice of a boy who is being shown something and not in the voice of a boy who is correcting a man, and it was the boy of about fifteen being kind and it was the worst three minutes of his working life.

At about the third hour he stood up off the terrace with a knife in his hand and looked at the whole of the Long Set from the second step and could not tell one variety from another at four hundred paces, and could tell from four hundred paces exactly which of the two hundred trees was carrying too much on its low side, and could tell from four hundred paces that the stone holding the second terrace up on the valley's side was out of plumb by more than it had any business being, and both of those were the same kind of seeing and only one of them was worth anything at four hundred paces.

**And he said it out loud, in the trade's own words, to nobody in particular, in about nine seconds, and four people heard it, and it is a thing he has said in about nine counties in front of people who did not know what to do with it.**

“**That wall is out of plumb by more than a finger, and it is not out of plumb evenly, and the trouble is not at the top of it. The trouble is at the bottom. Somebody has been letting water out at the foot of that and putting something back in at the top of it, and the top of it looks sound and the top of it is not sound, and I have seen about nine walls in my life that were rebuilt from the top down and every one of them looked better for it and every one of them went the same way in about nine years.**”

Nobody argued with him. The man of about forty-four put his hoe down and looked at the wall for about as long as a man takes to say four, and then picked the hoe up again and went back to his block.

**And a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water at the top of the Long Set was standing nine paces off with a foot-rule in his hand and he did not say one word, and he looked at the wall, and then he looked at the man, and then he went up the slope about eleven terraces and did not come back down that sitting.**

Nobody asked the man of about fifty-one what he thought. Nobody has asked him since. He has said once, to a woman of about sixty-four and not to anybody else, that a man from off the hill said a thing about the second wall and that he is not going to say what the thing was, and the woman of about sixty-four did not ask him and he did not tell her.

Then the man of about forty-four called the hour out, and the four of them went down to the bar, and the woman of about thirty-eight was asked the four things and answered them, and what she said was about fruit and not about the wall, and the woman of about thirty-one said, when the man of about thirty-two asked her what the right way of taking out the wood was, that it was not a question she could put into words for a man who has not done it, and that the only way to learn it was to be wrong four times in a row on a block of ground that was not hers, and that she had four blocks of her own and two of them were not hers either.

“**Then I will be wrong on yours,**” he said.

“**You may be wrong on mine,**” she said, “**and you will be, and I will not stop you, and I am not going to stand there and teach you because a man has come up a lane with a stick and a bad leg and I have been asked nothing about it.**”

Nobody thanked her. About four people in that valley have said, at one time or another, that a man from off the hill knew about walls, and about four have said that a man from off the hill put his knife in a limb the wrong side twice in the first hour, and the two sets do not overlap and nobody asked either of them how they knew.

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the man of about forty-four's, and a knife is in it and a wall is not.**

**a pruning knife at three, an oil-stone at six, a leather grip at nine, a cord of wool at eleven, a hurdle at fourteen, a bushel of chaff at eighteen, a sack of lime at twenty-one, a second hurdle at twenty-five, a mallet at thirty-two, and a wedge of steel at twenty.** Three, six, nine, eleven, fourteen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-five, thirty-two and twenty is one hundred and fifty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and fifty-nine pence is three marks and fifteen over**, and eleven terraces and about two hundred trees are two figures of a slope and not one figure and are not added to each other and neither of them is a price, and the fourth hour and the third hour and the nine minutes and the nine paces are four figures of time and of distance and none of them is a price, and a plumb wall and a bad cut limb are two things and one of them was wrong and neither of them has a price, and about four people who heard it and about four who did not are two halves of one afternoon and are not a figure of persons and go in at neither end of this column.

The block of ground was done at about the seventh hour and the woman of about thirty-one went over it at about the eighth hour and took out two limbs he had left in and put back one he had taken out, and neither of them said the word for what he was, and the boy of about fifteen carried the knives down to the press-house and was paid a penny for it and was not paid for being kind.
