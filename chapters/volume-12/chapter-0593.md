# Chapter 0593

**Fallowmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 320, a Wednesday, the third of the four lowest terraces at the fifth hour of the bar sitting, nine doors and a slate, one household told a thing that cannot be refused, and a rule made in about four minutes that nobody has repealed.**

The four lowest terraces lay under a clear sky at the fifth hour, and the water ran where it should run, and the ditch about two paces wide along the top of the fourth terrace stood cut by one person where six should have cut it, and the six were four terraces below laying stone, and the woman of about thirty-one who keeps those four terraces walked the row with a slate in her hand and stopped at the third door of nine.

She had walked that row before, at the fifth hour on the morning the water went, with a stick and about nine stones in her apron and the same slate under her arm, and that walk had taken a morning because everything on it had to be counted. This one took half of one. There was nothing to measure. A slate is a poor thing to tell a door with, and she had asked the man of about thirty-one of the Standing to write this one out for her at the fourth hour that morning because she did not trust herself to keep it straight nine times over, and he had written it and handed it back without saying anything, and she had read it once and had not looked at it again.

About nine households held those four terraces, and one of the four neighbours who stand with the woman of about thirty-one kept the third house down, and that household had never had a vote in anything the valley decided, because the valley votes on nothing, and it was not in the room when the four sets were asked, and it had paid all the same.

Aren Kest was on that terrace carrying stone up from the fan, and he set his load down when he saw the slate, and he was not asked to stay and he stayed, because the slate meant telling, and telling on that row had always cost somebody, and the telling that morning was written on it in the hand of the man of about thirty-one of the Standing, who had written it at her asking.

Behind the third house the ground went up to the bank in a step about as high as a knee, and the strip between the step and the back wall of the house was about as wide as a man can dig and about as long as the house, and there was an old fruit tree standing in the middle of it with a gate that had not been oiled since the child behind that door was born. It was the best ground on the row. That was the whole of the reason it was wanted, and the woman of about thirty-one had known that before she had knocked and had not said it at the door, and the not-saying was the only preparation that telling of this kind has.

The woman of about thirty-one knocked and the door opened, and one of the four neighbours stood in it with a child behind her and flour on her hands, and she said the thing the household had to be told, reading nothing and remembering everything, because the slate held what she had to say and nothing more.

“**The ground at the back goes back to the Standing for a season,**” she said. “**The bank above the channel has to come down to mend the hundred paces nobody has touched, and your back strip is where the stone will lie. You will have it back after the pick.**”

The woman in the door said nothing for a moment, and the child behind her said nothing, and the flour stayed on her hands, and then she said the thing the valley's rule lets anybody say.

“**I would rather not,**” she said.

It did not stop it. The rule the man of about sixty had made in about four minutes says a holder who says she would rather not does not stop it, and does not have to give a reason for saying it, and will be believed about anything else she says for the rest of her life. He had said out loud, before anybody had to find out, that it is not a good rule. Nobody on that row took it back that morning and nobody on that row repeated it either, and it went on doing its work with those two facts sitting on either side of it.

“**I know,**” the woman of about thirty-one said. “**I wrote it on nobody's sheet that you said it, because there is no sheet, and I will remember it, and anybody who asks me I will tell them you said it.**”

“**Is it a punishment,**” the woman in the door said.

“**It is not,**” the woman of about thirty-one said. “**Is it a payment? It is not. It cannot be refused and it is not for anything you did. The four sets found the money and the money is spent, and the mend wants ground and yours is where it lies. About nine households are a season poorer and back on their own ground after, and yours is one of them.**”

“**Nine days and a morning we lost the water,**” the woman in the door said. “**And a fifth of the pick off the bottom two. And now the back strip.**”

“**That is the cost,**” the woman of about thirty-one said, “**and it has landed on people who never had a vote and are not the people who were wrong, and I am the one telling you because I counted the water with a stick and about nine stones and I am the one who will stand in it.**”

“**Will you want the house too,**” the woman in the door said.

“**Nobody wants the house,**” the woman of about thirty-one said. “**The house stands. The strip only. And when it comes back it comes back with new stone at the edge that will hold your side for about nine years.**”

The woman in the door nodded, and the child moved back into the dark of the house, and the door stayed open, and the slate went back under the woman's arm, and the telling was done, and nobody in that valley was angry, and nobody was glad.

Then the woman of about thirty-one walked round the side of the house to look at the thing she had just given away, because she was the one who would stand in it. She put the toe of her boot on the strip and her weight on it and looked along its length the way a person looks at a length of cloth before cutting it, and the ground was firm under the boot and the old tree was in leaf and the gate did not close. The woman in the door watched her do it from the doorway, and neither of them said anything while she was doing it, and when the woman of about thirty-one came back past the door she stopped there for the length of a breath, and the woman in the door said nothing then either, and the slate was under her arm again and she went on down the row to the next door with it.

Aren Kest picked up his stone again, and his being there cost the household something, because a man from off the hill watching a telling makes it harder to answer, and he knew it, and he did not go, because to go would have been to say the telling was shameful, and it was not. He went back to the fan with it and the fan took the rest of the morning, and by the ninth hour that terrace was as it had been at the fifth hour except for one door standing open that had not been standing open the morning before.

At the ninth hour the man of about sixty was told that one household had been told and that she had said she would rather not, and he asked nothing, and he did not say whether the rule was right, and he would not say, and the valley went on with the mend with the refusal inside it like a stone inside a wall, and the flour dried on the hands that had opened the door, and the child learned that morning how a telling is done, by watching it done straight.

**And the cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is the woman of the four lowest's, and a back strip for a season and a refusal that stops nothing are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a nail bag at twenty-two, a rushlight at five, a milking pail at twenty-six, a yeast cake at ten, a tallow candle at sixteen, a scythe stone at twenty-eight, a hank of yarn at seven, a sieve at twenty-five, a jar of oil at thirteen, and a whetstone at eighteen.** Twenty-two, five, twenty-six, ten, sixteen, twenty-eight, seven, twenty-five, thirteen and eighteen is one hundred and seventy pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seventy pence is three marks and twenty-six over**, and four lowest terraces and nine doors and the third door and the third house and a back strip and a step about as high as a knee and a strip about as wide as a man can dig and about as long as the house and a ditch about two paces wide and the fourth terrace and four neighbours and about nine households and about nine stones and nine times over and about a hundred paces untouched and a fifth of the pick and the bottom two terraces are sixteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the fourth hour and the fifth hour and the ninth hour and the bar sitting and a season and nine days and a morning and a half of one morning and the length of a breath and about nine years are ten figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A woman of about thirty-one who keeps the four lowest and one of four neighbours in a door and a child behind her and a man of thirty-two with a limp carrying stone and a man of about thirty-one of the Standing and a man of about sixty and six who should have cut the ditch and four sets of people are eight figures of persons, and no two of them is one figure, and the fourth is on no roll of any of them.** Nobody thanked anybody.
