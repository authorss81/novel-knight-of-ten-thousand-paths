# Chapter 0599

**The four lowest terraces at the fifth hour of the bar sitting, about nine households a season poorer on their own ground, twenty-two winters of not arguing given up, a count that was never a count, and a man further from the work than at the start.**

The bill came due on the four lowest terraces at the fifth hour, and it was presented and it was not paid, because there is nothing in this valley that pays a thing like it, and the woman of about thirty-one who keeps those four terraces stood in the ditch about two paces wide with a mattock and read it out to the ground as much as to anybody.

About nine households had lost their ground for a season and were back on their own ground after, and were a season poorer for it, and the back strips lay under stone, and nine days and a morning of water had gone, and a fifth of the pick off the bottom two terraces had gone, and about a third of about nine hundred drying cloths had come out damp, and about four hundred paces of good new bed held at the fourth hundred pace while the top three hundred paces stood wrong and about a hundred paces between stood untouched.

Aren Kest stood on that terrace with his stick, further from the work than he had been at the start of this ground, and he knew it, and the man of about fifty-one who keeps the water knew it, and neither of them made it into a safe place to end, because it is not one.

“**Read it,**” the man of about fifty-one said.

“**About nine households and their ground,**” the woman of about thirty-one said. “**A valley's twenty-two years of not arguing, given up and not got back. A woman of about sixty-four whose count was never a count. A cutting that arrived in a pot and opened nothing and was carried back. And a man of about thirty-two with a limp who never put a straight edge on the lip and never held one end of it, and is further off than when he came.**”

Nobody answered that. About four people on that terrace took it that the bill should have been laid at the door of the two sets that said no, and about five took it that it should have been laid at the door of the two that said yes for spending what was never enough, and the two sets did not overlap, and about four hundred people in a market town four days and about nine off say the man is the reason the rest of the record stands up and about four hundred say he is the reason anybody should have believed any of it, and those two sets do not overlap either, and neither pair was chosen between.

The woman of about sixty-four came down the middle with her bundle of cords and stood at the edge of the ditch, and her count of thirty years was never a count and everybody on that terrace knew it now, and she tied her cords all the same, about a hundred and forty of about nine hundred in a sitting, paid in fruit and not in money, and nobody asked her to stop counting and nobody asked her to go on.

“**I counted,**” she said, “**and I did not count, and both are true, and the valley paid for the second one.**”

The man of about thirty-one of the Standing came down to ask the question and did not ask it and said nothing at all, which is the second time in this ground he has stood silent, and nobody has asked him why and he has not been asked.

Aren Kest picked up a stone from the ditch side and laid it on the edge where he was shown, and it was the wrong stone for the place, and the woman of about thirty-one took it up and put it back in his hand and showed him where it went, and he laid it right the second time, and being wrong about something on that terrace cost him nothing and cost the terrace nothing, and that was the only cheap thing on it.

At the ninth hour the light went off the four lowest, and the bill stood where it had been laid, and the valley had argued in public on purpose and had not been tidied, and nobody called it good and nobody called it bad, and the water ran where it should run, and the ground held what it held, and the mattock stayed where she had left it against the ditch side, and the stone she had shown him sat right in its place, and that small rightness was allowed to stand beside the large unpaid bill without excusing it.

**What that hour cost is a column, and it is the woman's of the four lowest, and a bill presented and not paid is not in it and has no price.**

**a hay knife at twenty-eight, a cake of tallow at three, a milking stool at twenty-two, a hank of yarn at nine, a gate hinge at thirty-one, a rushlight at six, a leather rein at nineteen, a jar of oil at twelve, a corn riddle at twenty-five, a whetstone at fifteen, and a hand file at eighteen.** Twenty-eight, three, twenty-two, nine, thirty-one, six, nineteen, twelve, twenty-five, fifteen and eighteen is one hundred and eighty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and eighty-eight pence is three marks and forty-four over**, and about nine households and four terraces and a ditch about two paces wide and a fifth of the pick and the bottom two terraces and about nine hundred cloths and a third come out damp and about a hundred and forty of them hers and about four hundred paces of good new bed and three hundred paces wrong and about a hundred paces untouched and the fourth hundred pace are twelve figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the bar sitting and a season and thirty years and twenty-two winters and nine days and a morning and the fifth hour and the ninth hour are seven figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **About nine households on four terraces and a woman of about thirty-one who keeps them and a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water and a man of thirty-two with a limp and about four at one door and about five at another and two sets that said no and two sets that said yes and a woman of about sixty-four who ties the cords and about four hundred people holding one true thing and about four hundred holding another are eleven figures of persons and are not added to one another.** Nobody thanked anybody.
