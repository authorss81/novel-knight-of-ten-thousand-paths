# Chapter 0566

**The drying floor at the foot of the lowest slope, the second hour of the swell sitting, a night of which a man of thirty-two with a limp could afterwards say nothing at all, a woman of about fifty-eight turning drying cloths at the press-house, and the cost of a road that is a person and not a door and not a bell.**

He had been asleep on the boards and he woke on the boards and the valley gave him a sitting and an hour inside a sitting and he could put himself in neither of them.

**Nobody in that valley was asleep at the second hour and nobody came and looked at him, and there is no way to say that a man was moved, because nothing was moved and nothing came and the boards were where the boards were and his stick was against the wall where he had put it.**

The woman of about fifty-eight turning the drying cloths at the press-house asked him one question and it was the only one anybody asked him about the night.

“**Did you walk in your sleep?**”

“**No,**” he said.

“**Then I do not know,**” she said, “**and I have a floor of cloths to turn and it is the second hour, and I am not going to stand here and be the person who tells a stranger with a bad leg that something happened to him in a place where nothing happens.**”

Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked her anything else, and he has not asked her anything else, and about four people in that valley have said, at one time or another, that a night went missing off him, and about four have said that nothing went missing off anybody because nothing in that valley has ever gone missing, and the two sets do not overlap and neither of the two has been checked by anybody standing at the drying floor at the second hour of a sitting.

**And his left palm was warm, and it was not the fourth hour of anything that he could name, and he put it flat on the boards and the boards were cold and the palm did the thing, and he has never told anybody about it in eleven years and he did not tell her and he has not told anybody since and he is not going to.**

He was not asleep again after that, and he has not been able to account for how long the rest of the night was, and he is not going to put a figure on it, and there is nobody in that valley he could put it on it with.

At about the fourth hour he went down to the press-house floor because the boards at the drying floor are cold enough to be felt through a coat, and the woman of about fifty-eight was on the floor with a rail of cloths and there were about a hundred and eleven on it and a hundred and eleven on the floor, and she had not been asleep either, and neither of them asked the other about that.

“**Do you know what hour it is?**” she said.

“**No,**” he said. “**I have been awake since about the second and I have told myself four times that it was the fourth and it has not been the fourth once, and I do not know whether that is because it is early or because I cannot place anything on this slope.**”

“**In a place with nine counties in it, a man knows what hour it is,**” she said, “**because somebody tells him. Somebody rings a bell, or a man comes round the door, or a hundred yards of metalling goes past in the dark and you count. In this valley there is a bell and a boy and about four grown men who could ring it and not one of them has a rule about the hour, and so a person in this valley knows what hour it is the way a boat knows where it is, which is by the bell, and the bell is rung by a boy of about fifteen who is paid a penny for the four of them and who has never once been wrong.**”

Nobody thanked him. He asked her the question he had been carrying and it was the ninth of the same kind and she had heard it before and she answered it before he had finished it.

“**Because a man who has been somewhere nine days cannot be expected to be able to say when he was last awake. I am sixty. I have had nights of that.**”

“**That is not what I asked,**” he said.

“**It is the only answer I have,**” she said, “**and you asked me nine times in nine days and the answer has not changed and it is not going to, and I am going to turn about nine hundred of these in this sitting and if you want the hour you can ask the boy and the boy will tell you and I would not bet the price of a cloth on what he says and neither would the man who pays him.**”

Nobody thanked her. He stood at the rail of cloths for about as long as a man takes to say nine and turned one over, and the turned one had been turned about an hour before, and the other side of it had begun to go the colour of the underside of a leaf, and that was a thing about a cloth and he told her so and she told him it was the oldest thing anybody knew about that floor and that he was welcome to it.

Then she came off the press-house floor with an armful of cloths and stood over him, and the thing she said next is the whole of what that sitting did to him and it is nine sentences and she did not stop for a breath in the middle of them.

“**I have turned cloths in this room for about thirty-one years and I know what a man from off the hill sounds like and you sound like one. You come into a place and inside about nine minutes you have said out loud what is wrong with it, in the trade's words, in the voice a man uses when a thing is going to be mended tomorrow, and it does not matter in the least here whether you are right. There is nobody on this slope who is going to mend anything tomorrow. There has not been a mend in this valley that anybody can name in about nine years and there are about nine things in it that want mending and every one of them has been wanting for twenty-two, and I have watched about four men from off this hill come up this lane since before the fruit was planted in that order and about two of them said a thing like you said on the second terrace and about two of them said nothing at all, and I am not able to tell you which of those two kinds of man does more good, and I am not going to be the one who works it out, because I have a floor of cloths and eleven hundred of them and they do not get turned by themselves.**”

Nobody thanked her and nobody argued with her.

Aren Kest said the thing he said, which is that in his county that was how a wall got looked at, and that a man who saw a fault and did not say it inside nine minutes was a man who had not looked, and that there were about nine men in nine counties who had said that to him and about nine men who had said the other thing.

“**I know what your county is,**” she said. “**I have had two men off it on this floor and they were the same as you and I did not like either of them and I did not know which of them I disliked for what.**”

Nobody thanked her.

**And then she said the other thing, and it came out of her in the oldest voice in that valley, even and calm and about four generations old, and it was not her voice and she stopped in the middle of it and looked at her own mouth.**

“**That is not the way I would have said it,**” she said.

“**No,**” he said.

“**Do not.**”

He did not ask her about it. **He had heard that voice come out of one woman's mouth on the head of the Long Set two sittings earlier and he had not asked her then either, and the reason he did not ask is that he has been hearing a thing come out of his own left palm for eleven years and he has never asked anybody about that, and a man who will not ask a stranger about his own hand has no standing to ask a woman about her mouth.**

Nobody thanked him. Nobody thanked her. She put the armful of cloths on the rail and went back up to the press and turned the first cloth, and it is a hundred and eleven cloths on that floor and she turns about nine hundred in a sitting and she is paid a penny in about nine and does the rest for the fruit.

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the woman of about fifty-eight's, and a night of which a man can say nothing is not in it and cannot be priced.**

**a nail at three, a lump of chalk at seven, a skein of thread at eleven, a sheet of sailcloth at fourteen, a pound of tallow at eighteen, a pot of grease at twenty-two, a night's straw at twenty-six, a bucket at thirty, a pair of shears at forty, and a kettle at sixteen.** Three, seven, eleven, fourteen, eighteen, twenty-two, twenty-six, thirty, forty and sixteen is one hundred and eighty-seven pence. **One hundred and eighty-seven pence is three marks and forty-three over, and a mark is forty-eight pence in this valley as it is in every other place a person counts anything**, and eleven hundred cloths and about nine hundred turned in a sitting and a penny in about nine are three figures of three different series and none of them is derived from another and none of them is a price of anything in this column, and the second hour and the nine minutes and the thirty-one years and the nine years and the twenty-two and the eleven hundred are six figures of time and of work and not one of them is a price, and a voice that comes out of a woman's mouth and is not hers is not in this column and is not in any column in nine counties.

He went up to the Long Set at about the fourth hour with a knife and a block of ground that was not his and he was wrong on it four times in a row, exactly as he had been told he would be, and the woman of about thirty-one who keeps that block watched him do it and did not stop him and did not go away.
