# Chapter 0573

**The settlement at the middle terrace, the ninth hour of the bar sitting, a board on a wall with a leaf on it, a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water at the top of the Long Set who has come down nine terraces to argue, a woman of about thirty-eight who asked a question about the oldest tree and has not had it answered, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who is going down the lane in the morning.**

He came down nine terraces in about as long as it takes a man to boil a kettle and stood at the foot of the second step with his foot-rule still in his hand, and he said the first thing before anybody asked him anything, which is the way a man says a thing he has been carrying a long way.

“**I told four households at the top of this slope that a man from off the hill walked four miles of my channel and said nothing at all, and I said it in the pick sitting before there was a single fact behind it, and I said it because he walked it and said nothing and I could not stand a fourth month of that. I have said it out loud now in front of you and I cannot unsay it, and one of those four households has a daughter on the second terrace who heard me say it and came and looked at him while he said it.**”

Nobody thanked him. The woman of about thirty-eight put her knife down in the ground, which she does not do.

“**Say the rest of it.**”

“**I want the ground opened.**”

“**That is four sentences and I have not got one of them back.**”

The man of about thirty-one of the Standing said what a room says, and he said it before either of them had gone further, because he has been in that room nineteen years and this is the shape of it.

“**There is an oath on that ground and the oath has got about ninety people on it and two of them are dead, and neither of you has any business lifting it with a spade whatever the two of you want off it.**”

“**Then nobody is lifting anything,**” the man of about fifty-one said. “**I am not asking for the oath to be lifted. I am asking for the ground to be dug, which is what a person does when a thing has to be looked at, and I will hold the spade and there will be about eleven people standing round watching and every one of them can go away at any hour they like.**”

“**And the ninety?**”

“**The ninety are on the oath,**” the man of about sixty said. He had not been in the room and nobody had heard him come up. “**The ninety are not on the spade. What I will do is this, and I have thought about it for four minutes since I heard the question, and it is a rule and it is not a good one and I am not going to pretend it is. Nobody who holds a block of ground inside nine yards of that tree will dig it. Every one of the four of them is asked, and a holder who says she would rather not does not stop it and does not have to give a reason and will be believed about anything else she says for the rest of her life.**”

“**That is not a fair rule,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**and I have been in that room about nineteen years and I am the only person in it who is allowed to say so. A holder who says she would rather not and then stands there and watches is worse off than one who says nothing at all, and you have just made that into the price of a woman being honest in this valley, and you made it in about four minutes.**”

“**Yes,**” the man of about sixty said, “**and I have said it out loud before anybody has had to find out, which is the only thing I have ever been able to do in this room, and I am not going to be thanked for it and it is not a good rule and it is the one we have.**”

“**Then the four of them dig it, and not one of us, and not one man from off this hill, and the record that comes out of it comes out into four people's hands and not into a room.**”

Nobody thanked him.

Then the argument happened, and it happened at the ninth hour with about nine people still on that ground, and neither of the two people in it was angry and neither of them was right and both of them were right, and this is what each of them said and neither of them said the other thing.

**The man of about fifty-one, who keeps the water:** “**I have four households at the top of this slope and one day in about nine years the water at the top of that Long Set is going to stop being water, and it is going to stop because of something that was done twenty-two years ago on a day about ninety people went out on a strip of ground at the foot of this slope. I do not know that. I know it because I have had the water at the top of that Long Set for nine years and I know what is under the fourth hundred pace without having walked four miles of anything in about nine years, and I have not got a reason for it and a man who says a fault without a reason is not a man who has found a fault. If the reason is in that ground then I want it out of the ground and I want it out before there is nothing left under the lip to find it, and I do not care what it costs anybody else.**”

**The woman of about thirty-eight, who keeps a block between two head stones:** “**Then it is out of your mouth that you do not care, and it is about four days' walk off and about four hours up a lane from a person who has spent eleven years writing down what a road is doing. I have stood at that gate and said a sentence out loud on my own account and about nine days ago and a carter went down the lane with two sacks of pears and has not come back in. If there is a thing in that ground then it will come out into about eleven people's ears on a working morning and it will not be a finding, it will be a thing a person heard, and I have been that person twice and I am not doing it a third time in front of people who will remember where I was standing.**”

“**Then you do not have to stand there.**”

“**I have not been asked whether I have to.**”

“**I was about to.**”

“**I know you were, and that is the reason I said it,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said, “**and I have noticed that I do that, and I have noticed it about nine times in nine days, and I have not been able to stop.**”

Nobody thanked either of them and nobody said that the other was wrong.

Then she said the thing that decided it, and she said it to the board and not to the man of about fifty-one.

“**It is dug at the eighth hour of a working sitting, when the cords are done and there are about eleven people at that tree tying, and it is not called a sitting and there is no room and nobody writes anything down who does not have to write something down for a living. I will be there at the eighth hour because I am there at the eighth hour about four days in nine anyway and everybody on this slope knows that and nobody has ever asked me why.**”

“**I am not going to ask you why,**” the man of about fifty-one said.

“**I know you are not, and that is a different thing from not asking,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said, and the man of about fifty-one did not answer that.

**And Aren Kest stood about nine paces off for the whole of it and said one sentence, and it was to her, and it was at the end.**

“**I do not know whether it should be opened. I have been in this valley about nine days and I have not learned one single thing about how to do any of this differently, and I am going down the lane in the morning for about nine days, and I am telling you so that you can tell the two people who are going to open it that I am not here.**”

“**You are not asking me for anything.**”

“**No.**”

“**Then you have told me for a reason that is not mine,**” she said, and he did not say what the reason was, and nobody asked him, and four people in that valley have told other people in that valley that a man who goes away before a thing happens is a man who has arranged not to be asked about it afterwards, and four have told other people in that valley that a man who stays is a man who will be asked about it by everybody for the rest of his life, and neither set has been asked which of the two he would have done.

Nobody thanked anybody.

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the man of about fifty-one's, and nine terraces and four miles and the fourth hundred pace and about ninety people and about nine yards and about four days' walk are six figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and nine people still on that ground at the ninth hour are one morning's attendance and not a figure of this valley, which is eleven households on about eleven terraces, and none of them goes in at either end of this column.**

**a foot-rule at six, a line of chalk at thirteen, a bucket at nineteen, a sieve at twenty-four, a hod at twenty-eight, a piece of lead at thirty-one, a wedge of steel at thirty-two, a coil of tarred rope at thirty-eight, and a barrow-wheel at forty.** Six, thirteen, nineteen, twenty-four, twenty-eight, thirty-one, thirty-two, thirty-eight and forty is two hundred and thirty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and thirty-one pence is four marks and thirty-nine over**, and four households and eleven households on eleven terraces and ninety people twenty-two winters ago and four blocks inside nine yards are four figures of four different things and are not added to each other and none of them is a price, and nine days of hard going and four days' walk and four hours up a lane are three figures of time and of distance and none of them is a price, and a spade and a board and a leaf about nine inches by six are three objects and none of them is in this column.

The four of them who hold ground inside nine yards of that tree were asked that night, in their own doors and one at a time, and every one of the four said she would rather not. It made no difference. Nobody thanked anybody, and the man of about sixty wrote nothing down about it, and nobody in that valley has asked any of the four what she would have said if it had been asked a different way, and one of the four has said out loud that she would rather not, twice, and both of those are on nobody's sheet.
