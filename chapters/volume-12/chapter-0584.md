# Chapter 0584

**The four lowest terraces of the Long Set, the sixth hour of the pick sitting, nine men on the channel bank four hundred paces above with the ninth barrow of the morning going down the high side, a woman of about thirty-one who keeps those four terraces standing in a ditch line with a mattock in her hands, and about nine households who have not been asked anything and are not going to be.**

The first cost did not arrive as water. It arrived as a mattock with nobody else holding it, about nine days before the water was due, and it landed on a woman of about thirty-one who is not in the argument and has never once been in it and has been in this valley all her life.

Her name is not a thing anybody in the Long Set uses. What she is called is the woman who keeps the four lowest, and that is a job in the way that a person who ties about nine hundred cords a sitting is a job, which is to say that nobody has ever given it a name and everybody has relied on it.

The four lowest terraces hold the water. That is the whole of the reason they are the four lowest. And what keeps the water off them is not the channel and never was. It is a ditch line cut along the top of the fourth terrace, about two paces wide, cleared every year in the first sitting with six people on it for about nine days, and the reason the four lowest have never gone under in twenty-two winters of not arguing is that ditch.

Nobody has ever written that down. There is no ditch in the plank and no ditch in the record and the man of about fifty-one who keeps the water at the top of that Long Set said so himself when he was asked, out loud, in front of about nine people, and said that the ditch was not a fault and was not a reason for anything and was a piece of ground about two paces wide that six people cut every year.

The six people are the four lowest. The woman of about thirty-one, and her four neighbours, and a boy of about nineteen who comes down from the middle when he can.

At the sixth hour on this morning there was one.

The nine men who can lay four miles of stone properly are the same nine men whichever terrace it is, and the mending started at the top on the second day, because at the top the stone is out where a cart can get to it. So the six who cut the ditch line are four terraces below nine men, and the woman of about thirty-one had been in that ditch for five days with a mattock and on the sixth day she was in it alone, and she had about four days of it left to do, and there was nobody.

She came up out of the ditch at the sixth hour and stood at the end of her own row and did the thing she does, which is to look at what is in front of her and say what is not in it, and this time she said it to nobody in particular, and about nine people were on the Long Set above her and about four were within earshot.

“**There is no water on the four lowest and that is the good news and I am the one person in this valley who can tell you it is the good news, because I have been standing in the ditch that keeps it off for five days and I know what is left in it. There is about four days of cutting in that line and I am one and there is nobody. And in about nine days the water is going to come down off that channel and go across the first of these four terraces, and in about sixteen days it is going to be on all four, and the reason it is nine and not sixteen is a ditch that six people cut every year and that is going to be cut this year by one.**”

Nobody argued with her. The man of about fifty-one came down the high side of the cut about as long as a man takes to say four and stood on the fourth terrace with a barrow-wheel in his hand.

“**I can put four men back on that ditch this afternoon,**” he said.

The woman of about thirty-one looked at him for a while.

“**Then the top of that channel does not get done,**” she said. “**And the top of that channel is the part that is holding the lip up at the fourth hundred pace. And if the top does not get done then the fourth hundred pace does not get done at all this year, and the fourth hundred pace is what is keeping the water off the four of us in the first place, and the water comes down about nine days from now whether the ditch is cut or not. You put four men on my ditch and I have my ditch in four days instead of sixteen and the four of us are dry this year, and I am not going to take them, and I want it said that I am not refusing you because I do not think you would do it. A ditch is a thing six of us cut every sitting and the channel is a thing nobody has mended in about nine years, and four men on my ditch buys me one year off you and I am not going to buy one year. I have lost seven days of a season that nobody is going to give back, and you have lost nothing at all.**”

Nobody thanked him for offering. Nobody said that he should have offered it four days ago, and the man who keeps the water said out loud, before anybody had to work it out, that he had worked it out on the second day and had said nothing on the second day and had said nothing on the third, and that the reason he had said nothing was that a man who takes four men off a channel in the first three days is a man who will take four men off it in the first three days of everything for the rest of his life, and that he had wanted very much to be a man who had not had to have that thought.

The woman of about thirty-one said the other half, and she said it without any heat, and it is the sentence that has been said on that slope more often in about twenty-two years than any other.

“**You have not done anything to me,**” she said. “**I want that said. I said yes before you had finished the question and I have not been asked to change my mind and I am not going to be. And I am going to lose a season on four terraces and about nine households are going to lose the water for about nine days and it is not going to be anybody's fault, and that is a thing I have not got used to saying out loud and I have had about four days to get used to it and I have not.**”

He put the barrow-wheel down in the grass, which he has not done in front of anybody in about nine years.

“**I know,**” he said, “**and I have been carrying a fault for about nine years and there is now a reason for it, and I have got a finding with three names on it, and I have not got a single thing I can do with any of it that is not somebody else's ground.**”

“**Then do not do anything,**” the woman of about thirty-one said. “**Go up. There are nine men on that bank and six of them have never laid a course in their lives and the four hundredth pace is the one that matters.**”

He went up. Nobody watched him go. About four people on that slope have put it out that a woman of about thirty-one sent a man away, and nine have put it out that she had no choice and that saying a thing out loud in a valley like this is the only weapon there is and she is holding it like a knife at a wedding, and the two sets do not overlap and she has not been asked which of the two she thinks she is doing.

She went back into the ditch and worked until the light went and she did about as much in that afternoon as she would have done with two, and the boy of about nineteen came down at the ninth hour and did not say he had come and she did not say she was glad, and they finished about a third of what six people would have finished.

**And the woman of about thirty-one is not one of the three who signed the finding and is not one of the four who chose the oath and is not the man with a limp, and she was never asked whether she might speak for this valley and has not said that she wants to, and the one thing she has done about all of it is say yes before anybody finished the question, and nobody wrote that down either.** The four lowest terraces are where the water is going to arrive and they are the four lowest because that is where the water arrives, and the woman of about thirty-one has been on this ground since she was born and has no vote in any of it and has never asked for one, and there is nobody in this valley she could ask.

**The cost of that afternoon is a column, and it is hers, and seven days of a season is not in it and seven days has no price.**

**a spade at four, a hand file at six, a nail at nine, a jar of oil at twelve, a sack at fourteen, a coil of cord at seventeen, a cake of tallow at twenty, a pruning knife at twenty-three, a night's straw at twenty-seven, a pot of glue at thirty-two, and a copper wire at thirty-seven.** Four, six, nine, twelve, fourteen, seventeen, twenty, twenty-three, twenty-seven, thirty-two and thirty-seven is two hundred and one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and one pence is four marks and nine over**, and four terraces and about two paces of ditch and about four days of cutting and six people and nine men and about nine households and four hundred paces and about nine yards of lip are eight figures of distance and of count and of persons and none of them is a price, and the sixth hour and the ninth hour and the second day and the third day and about four days and about nine days and about sixteen days and twenty-two winters and about nine years are nine more figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **Three names on a slate and the two sets who said no and the two who said yes and about ninety people twenty-two winters ago are four figures of persons and are not added to one another and a penny for the bell was paid and is not in this column.** A ditch about two paces wide is not a channel and is not four miles and is not a road, and the five sittings are five and a turn of them is a year and neither of those is a price. **Nobody thanked the man of about fifty-one for putting the barrow-wheel down in the grass in front of about nine people, and nobody thanked the woman of about thirty-one for saying out loud that none of it was his fault, and the boy of about nineteen came down at the ninth hour and was not thanked either and did not say he had come.**
