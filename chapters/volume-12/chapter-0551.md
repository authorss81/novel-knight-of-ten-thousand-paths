# Chapter 0551

**A hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people was shut on the twenty-sixth of Mudmonth, YR 320, a Sunday and not a market day, from the second hour to the ninth, and about nine people who had come to it were in the town and not in it, and the room had been shut on Sundays since before the man with the badge came to it, and the floor of that room has not been washed since the eighth of Wolfmonth.**

There was no market. That is the whole of what a Sunday is in a market town of about two thousand people and it is not a small thing to be in one, because a market town's streets are built around the assumption that nobody is doing anything anywhere else.

The nine came in on the cart from the made road on the day before, at about the seventh hour, and the woman of about forty-four who sells fish near the weigh-house saw them get down and counted them by looking, which is a thing a person does at a weigh-house and does not do anywhere else, and she did not tell anybody and there was nobody to tell.

A man of about twenty-nine with a handcart knew by the second hour on the Sunday that none of the nine was from this county, and he knew it because he has counted about nine hundred carts down one street on a market morning for about nine years and he can tell a stranger's cart by the way a stranger loads it, and he did not say anything about it to anybody and he did not go and find out and nobody sent him to find out.

**And the man of about fifty-eight with a chain of office that is a badge came down the outside stair at about the third hour and looked at the nine of them under the board outside the weigh-house and said the whole of what he had come down to say, in about four sentences, to nobody in particular.**

“**The room over the saddler's is shut on a Sunday. It has been shut on a Sunday for every Sunday since I came except one, and I was not in this county on that one, and the floor of it has not been washed since, and I am not going to open it. I am not going to stand here and tell you the reasons either. One of the reasons is a rule and the other one is that the last person who opened that door on a Sunday said out loud that whoever washed the floor of it afterwards would be washing it alone, and nobody has washed it, and that is the state of that room and it is not a thing I am going to improve for nine people from off a made road.**”

Nobody thanked him. Nobody argued with him. One of the nine, a man of about thirty-four with a bedroll, said *we can wait*, and the man of about fifty-eight said *you can*, and went back up the stair, and the door was shut behind him at about the fourth hour and was shut at the ninth hour too.

Aren Kest came up the lane at about the third hour because he had decided before the second hour that he would come up the lane, and he was in the town and not in the room, and the man of about twenty-nine with a handcart said his name out loud once in the street and then said it a second time to a man who had not asked, and he stood there and let a market town say his name on a day that was not a market day and did not say it back.

**And then a woman of about twenty-four with a basket he had not seen before, who was one of the nine and who had been asleep for most of the morning and had come out because of the noise, asked him a question in the street, and it was the first question anybody in nine counties had put to him about a place, and it was not about a form and it was not about a rule and it was not about the fourth head.**

“**Is it safe to send people there?**” and nobody else in that street asked it and nobody who had sent anybody to that valley was in that street.

“**I do not know,**” Aren Kest said, and that was the whole of the answer and he did not add to it.

He did not add to it because about four hundred people in this town of about two thousand had carried a sheet of foolscap with four ruled lines on it out of a market on a Thursday in a month carried at thirty, and the fourth of those four lines had his name in it, and a man whose name is in a line on a piece of paper that four hundred people have carried is not a man anybody should be asked about whether a place is safe. **That is the reason and it is a good one and it is not the only one and this paragraph does not know the other one and neither does he.**

Nobody thanked him for the answer and nobody said it was a good one. The woman of about twenty-four with a basket said *that is not an answer* and he said *no*, and she said *no is not an answer either*, and he said *I know*, and that was three turns and it was the whole of it and nobody in that street heard more than three turns of it.

**And after the three turns she told him where they were from, which was about two days and about six miles, and which is not far, and which she said as though the distance were the interesting part of it and it was not.**

“**We were told it has never had a quarrel in it. That is the phrase. The man who said it to us said it four times over two days and he said it the way a man says a thing he has been told by somebody he believes, and I want to know whether a place that has never had a quarrel has never had one or has had one and been told not to say so, and I am asking you because nobody else in this town has been out there.**”

“**I have not been out there.**”

“**Then you are the second person in two days to tell me that,**” she said, “**and I did not ask them and I did not tell them anything and I want that said in whatever room you write it in, because if it goes round this market that a woman with a basket came up a lane on a Sunday and made a man say a thing, I will not be able to come back here.**”

Nobody repeated any of it in that street. The man of about twenty-nine with a handcart was forty paces off and did not move, and the woman of about forty-four who sells fish looked at the two of them for about as long as a woman takes to say four and went back to her board, and about four people in this town of about two thousand saw a man with a limp stand in the street with a woman who had come two days and about five did not, and the two sets do not overlap and neither of them is a figure of anything and neither of them went into anybody's book that day.

Then a man of about sixty who had come in with the nine said one thing to the four of them who were nearest him, and he said it in the tone of a man who has been somewhere and is not making a point.

“**They call it quiet. That is what it is called where the letter came from. It has been quiet for about twenty-two years and it has never once been loud and that is the whole of what anybody will be able to tell you about it, and I would like somebody to write down that the word in the report is quiet and the word in this town is market and those are not the same word.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody wrote it down on the day, because on the day there was no room open to write in.

The man of about fifty-eight had come down that stair about two hours before and he had said the hearing stands out loud on the first day of every month and nobody has stopped him, and it had been said on the first of the month carried at thirty, and it was not said on a Sunday because a Sunday is not the first day of anything.

**And about nine people from off a made road stood under a board outside a weigh-house for the whole of a Sunday in a market town of about two thousand people because nobody in that town had told them where else to stand, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key said the one thing about it that anybody said, and she said it to the board and not to them.**

“**There is a room over a saddler's with a door on a stair and it is shut because it is shut on Sundays, and there is a floor inside it that has not been washed since the eighth of Wolfmonth, and I have the key to that door and I have not used it on a Sunday in five months, and nine people from two days of here standing under a board in the rain is what happens when a town has one rule and no second thing. I am not opening it and I am not asking anybody to open it and I would like it written down that nobody asked me to.**”

Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her to and she was not thanked later.

**And the cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key's, and it is the fifth one she has ever kept, and nine people from outside the county are not in it.**

**a farthing of salt at one, a bootlace at two, a second broom at three, a pot of paste at seven, a lump of chalk at nine, a night's straw at thirteen, a wedge of bread at sixteen, a sheet of oiled paper at twenty-one, and a pound of tallow at thirty-five.** One, two, three, seven, nine, thirteen, sixteen, twenty-one and thirty-five is one hundred and seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seven pence is two marks and eleven over**, and nine people is a figure of persons and is not in this column, and the four hundred who carried a sheet in a market and the about four hundred at the end of a made lane four days the other way are one figure and are not one figure and go in at neither end of it, and a floor that has not been washed since the eighth of Wolfmonth is not in it and has no price, and the twenty-two notches on a piece of driftwood four days off are years and are not tides and are not in it either, and nobody has been sent to that coast and nobody is going to be sent to that coast from this county on the strength of a Sunday.

Upstairs the half leaf was face down on a table where it had been since the sixth of a month carried at thirty, with a question under its fourth head that nobody in nine counties has answered, and the Crown's form was on the side table with four heads ruled on it and the fourth of them empty, and both of those objects were in a shut room on a Sunday and neither of them was looked at and neither of them was turned over and that is the state of them.
