# Chapter 0567

**The cut, the third hour of the pick sitting, about four miles of it from the High Set down to the foot of the slope, stone laid about a hundred years ago and about two paces wide with a lip along the high side, a man of thirty-two with a limp walking it on the high side, and a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water at the top of the Long Set walking it with him and not asking him one thing.**

The rule of that valley is not a rule anybody can point at, because it was never written, and it is the single thing that keeps about four hundred paces of stone at the top of this valley from being touched, and it is this: **a man who can see what is wrong with that channel may say so out loud to one other person and may not say it to a second person, and the reason is not law and is not a vote and is that the reason the channel wants mending is in a record that is not allowed to be read, and a fault with no reason behind it is not a finding, it is an accusation, and an accusation in this valley does not fall on the man who makes it.**

He saw it in about nine minutes, and he was not asked, and nobody knew that he had.

**It is the fourth hundred pace from the top. The lip on the high side carries for about nine yards on a bed that has gone, and the bed has gone because the water has been let out at the foot of it and put back in at the head of it for about twenty-two years, and what the lip is standing on is a line of rubble that is not doing one thing for the water above it, and a man can see the whole of that in about nine minutes if he has spent eleven years looking at the underside of things, and a man who has not cannot see it in a sitting.**

The man of about fifty-one walked the first mile with him and the man of thirty-two said nothing in the first mile, and the man of about fifty-one said nothing in the first mile, and the man of about fifty-one said at the end of the first mile that he had been told that the man from off the hill was going to walk it, and that he had come because it is his water and not because the man asked him, and that the man had not asked him, and that he wanted the man to know that he had not been asked.

Nobody thanked him for saying that. It is the only thing he has said in four months that anybody in that valley has written down.

At about the two-mile mark the man of about fifty-one said that he had a terrace to see to and would come back down at the seventh hour, and the man of thirty-two went on up the high side alone, and there are about nine more things in the next two miles that he could have said out loud and he said none of them, and one of them is at about the third mile where the wall on the valley's side has been rebuilt from the top down and looks better for it and is holding a fill four feet high on the wrong side, and the fill is the ground the Long Set is planted on.

**And he stood in the channel at about the fifth hour and put his hand flat on the lip of the high side, and the stone was cold and then was not, and that is twice in this valley and the first time was on the drying floor at the foot of the slope and the second time was four miles up a channel, and his palm has done it for eleven years and he has never told anybody and he did not tell the man of about fifty-one when the man came back down at the seventh hour.**

A boy of about fifteen was working the low side at about the sixth hour with a barrow and a hook, and he is the same boy who rang the bell at four points of a sitting and is the same boy who was being kind about a limb on the second terrace, and he asked the man one question and it was the only question anybody asked that man in four miles.

“**Is it going to come down?**”

“**No,**” Aren Kest said. “**Not in my lifetime, not in yours, and not in the lifetime of anybody who is on this slope now. That lip is out about nine yards and the bed under it has gone, and what is under the lip now is a line of rubble about two hands thick, and a line of rubble two hands thick under a lip like that holds for a long time longer than anybody imagines and it does not hold forever, and I have said to you the two facts I can say to you and I have not said the third one and I am not going to.**”

“**Why not the third one?**”

“**Because I have not got the reason for it,**” he said, “**and a man who says that a wall wants mending without being able to say why it wants mending is not a man who has found a fault, he is a man who has made an accusation, and I have been eleven years in a trade where an accusation with nothing behind it costs somebody their water.**”

Nobody thanked the boy. The boy said that he had not asked for the third one and that he had asked because the men at the press-house said there was a man on the channel and he wanted to know whether to barrow fast or slow, and the man told him to barrow slow, and he barrowed slow, and he was not paid for being careful.

**And at about the seventh hour the man of about fifty-one came back down the high side and walked the last of it with him, and the man of about fifty-one asked him twice whether he had a fault, and the man of about thirty-two said no twice, and both of those answers were true and both of them were about a different thing, and the man of about fifty-one knew that and said so.**

“**You have a fault,**” the man of about fifty-one said. “**You have had one since the fourth hundred pace and I have watched you not say it for about four hours, and I am not going to ask you for it, and I am not going to tell anybody in this valley that you have not got one, because that would be me saying the channel is sound and I do not know that it is sound and I have not walked four miles of anything in about nine years.**”

Nobody thanked him. He said the rest of it at the bottom of the cut where the water goes into the Wet Set, and he said it out loud, to the water and not to the man.

“**I am going to tell four households on the top of this slope that a man from off the hill walked my channel and said nothing, and those four households are not going to like it, and it is not the same as saying the channel is sound, and I want the two of those written down in different places.**”

Nobody wrote them down. About four people in that valley have said, and none of them has been asked when, that a man who walks four miles and says nothing knows there is a fault, and about four have said that a man who walks four miles and says nothing has not been taught which way is up, and the two sets do not overlap and the man of about fifty-one has not been asked which of the two he believes.

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the man of about fifty-one's, and four miles of stone and one fault are not in it and a fault has no price.**

**a mason's line at two, a lump of chalk at five, a bucket at eight, a barrow of sand at eleven, a barrow of rubble at fifteen, a sieve at nineteen, a hod at twenty-three, a second hod at twenty-seven, a piece of lead at thirty-one, a wedge of steel at thirty-four, and a mason's hammer at twenty-four.** Two, five, eight, eleven, fifteen, nineteen, twenty-three, twenty-seven, thirty-one, thirty-four and twenty-four is one hundred and ninety-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and ninety-nine pence is four marks and seven over**, and four miles and the fourth hundred pace and about nine yards and two hands thick and a fill four feet high are six figures of distance and of size and none of them is a price, and about four households on the top of the slope and about four who have said one thing and about four who have said the other are three sets of one afternoon's talking and are not a figure of persons and go in at neither end of this column, and the ninth yard of a lip that has been out since before a man of fifty-one was born is not in this column and cannot be.

The water went on down into the Wet Set and the two of them came off the cut at about the eighth hour and the man of about thirty-two got up the last step onto the second terrace in about four seconds longer than it takes to sit down, which is a figure about a leg and is not a figure of a night and is not in this column and is the only thing about him that anybody in that valley has ever measured.
