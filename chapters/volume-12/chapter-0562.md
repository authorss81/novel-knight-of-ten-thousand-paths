# Chapter 0562

**A drying floor at the foot of the lowest slope, the second hour of the swell sitting, a bar of wood lying in the grass at the Low Gate about a day's walk above the end of a made road and nobody lifting it, a board with four things written at the top of it, a woman of about thirty-eight who has answered the fourth of them once, and a lane that is not a door and is not a bell.**

He was on the boards and he did not know the hour. The valley does not keep the hours in the way a market town keeps them, and what the valley keeps is the sitting and the hour inside the sitting, and he had been there one night and could not put himself inside the second hour of anything.

**Nobody in that valley was going to tell him what hour it was, because nobody in that valley knew, and the not knowing is the ordinary condition of a place where four things are asked at a gate and a fifth thing is not asked at all.**

He went down to the bar at about the third hour and the woman of about fifty-five was on the stool with the board face up on her knees, and he asked her for the fourth question, and she did not ask him why.

“**It is the same four,**” she said, “**and I will read you all four because the fourth is the one you want and it is no use giving you the fourth on its own, because the first three are how I know you have come up the lane and not down it.**”

She read three of them out loud, in the voice of a woman reading a shopping list that she has read since before she could read.

“**And the fourth is this. It is not about a lane.**”

“**It is not a question at all,**” he said.

“**No,**” she said. “**I have asked it for about thirty years and my husband's father had it on the board and I have never known what it is for, and I ask it because it is the fourth one and I have never had a fifth.**”

He listened to the fourth one and it was the question that had gone under a fourth head on nine leaves in a room two hundred miles off in Thawmonth, and it is the same four questions, and the two wordings are not the same words and one question is not added to the other.

**And he went up the lane and found the woman of about thirty-eight at a block of ground between two head stones with a knife in her hand, and he asked her the one question he had been carrying up and down a slope for a night, and it was whether it had been the asking or the answering, and she put the knife down.**

“**I do not know,**” she said, “**and I have thought about it for most of a night, and I have decided that I am not going to find out, and I would like you to understand that this is a decision and not a shrug. I have been the fourth thing asked at that gate for about thirty years without knowing it, and nobody in this valley has ever been asked anything on their own account, and I said a sentence to a woman with a board and the sentence came out of me and not out of the oldest voice in the valley, and if I go back up that lane in a day to see whether it happens again then I will be doing it for the lane and not for me.**”

Nobody thanked her. Nobody argued with her.

Then she picked the knife up and went back to the block, and he went down to the gate and out under the bar and along the lane and out onto the made road, and that was the easiest thing he did in this valley, and it took him about four hours and it cost him nothing, and about four days of carting from the end of that road is a market town of about two thousand people in which about four hundred people can say where a man of thirty-two with a limp sleeps.

**He came back up the lane in the middle of the sitting and at the bar there was a carter of about forty with an empty cart and two sacks of pears on it, and the carter was asked the four things and he answered three of them, and when the woman of about fifty-five came to the fourth one he said out loud that it was a formality and that he had come down for the fruit and not for a talk.**

“**Then I have asked it,**” the woman of about fifty-five said, “**and you have not answered it, and I am not going to ask it again, and I am not going to tell anybody that you did not, and the lane will do what it does.**”

Nobody thanked the carter. The bar was not lifted and there is nothing to lift and he came up the lane and sat down on the same stool on the other side of it and put his hands on his knees, and the cart went up the slope with the pears still in it and the two of them stopped at the second terrace and came back down about an hour later lighter by nothing, and the pears went into the press-house and the pears from that day are not on any road in nine counties, and that is a fact about fruit and not about a person.

**And Aren Kest stood on the far side of a bar of wood for about as long as a man takes to say nine, and then went up the lane, and somebody asked him four things, and he said he did not know.**

Nobody said anything about it. The woman of about thirty-eight was coming down at about the seventh hour with a basket on her arm and she saw him at the bar, and she stopped, and she read the four questions on the board without being asked to, and then she answered the fourth one, out loud, to a woman holding a board and not to the man behind her, and whatever it was that she said he has never written down and she has never said it twice.

Nobody thanked her. The man walked in behind her and the bar stayed where it was.

**And it is not a door. There is no door in it, there is no building over it, there is no lock and there is no keeper of a lock, and the two men who lift the bar when a cart comes down in the pick were not called and did not come, and the bar is a length of wood lying in grass and it has been lying there for about thirty years.**

**And it is not a bell. The press-house has a bell on a bracket and it is rung at four points of a sitting to call the hands in and out, and it was rung at the fourth point of that sitting by a boy of about fifteen who does not know what he did, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was standing nine paces off with his hand flat on the stone of the bar and it was not the bell.**

Nobody has ever asked him about that. The woman of about fifty-five wrote the first three things on her board and did not write the fourth, and the fourth line on that board has been rubbed out and written over and rubbed out again and nobody in that valley can read what is on it now, and she has not asked for it to be made out again.

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the driver of the cart's, and two sacks of pears are not in it and a pear has no price until it is on a road.**

**a bit of chalk at two, a bootlace at five, a stone of tallow at seven, a stick of sealing wax at eleven, a night's straw at fourteen, a pound of candles at eighteen, a hank of yarn at twenty-one, a pot of tar at twenty-three, and a coil of tarred rope at twenty-seven.** Two, five, seven, eleven, fourteen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-three and twenty-seven is one hundred and twenty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and twenty-eight pence is two marks and thirty-two over**, and the four things asked at a gate are four things and are not a figure of anybody and are not in this column, and the day's walk between that bar and the end of a made road and the four days of carting between that made road and a market town are two figures of distance and not one figure and neither of them is a price and neither of them is in this column, and the nine who were on the lane and the four who were near the bar are two figures of one afternoon and neither of them is a figure of the people who live at the top of that slope and neither of them goes in at either end of this column, and a bell rung at four points of a sitting is a figure of hours and is not a price, and the carter is a person and is not a figure of persons and is not in this column and is not added to anything.

The boy of about fifteen who rang the bell at the fourth point of that sitting has said, and has been asked once, that he rang it because the hour was the hour and not because of anything, and nobody has asked him anything further, and about nine people in that valley heard him ring it and about four did not and the two sets do not overlap, and the nine who were on the lane and the four who were near the bar when the cart came up are not those two, and nobody has voted on which of them was right about the bell.
