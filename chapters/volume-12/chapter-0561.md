# Chapter 0561

**The thirtieth of Greenmonth, YR 320, a Saturday and a market day, from the first hour to the ninth, a cart went out of a market town of about two thousand people on a made road with a pot of loam and a scion of about nine inches in it under a wet sacking, and a woman of about thirty-eight who had walked nine days with that pot in her hands and was going to be carried instead this time, and a leaf about nine inches by six with four ruled heads on it against her chest inside her coat, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who got on at the end of the lane and was not sent and had not been asked.**

The market was on until the ninth hour and that is the man of about twenty-nine's figure and not this one's, and the cart that went out of the town on that morning was not a market cart and had been loaded before the market opened by a man of about fifty who is not paid by anybody in this county to drive anything.

**Nobody in that town knew where it was going. The woman had not told the driver and the driver had not asked, and a town of about two thousand people is a place in which about nine of them will tell you a thing they have not been told.**

He got on at the end of the lane without saying anything to the driver. The driver looked at him for about as long as a man takes to say four, and then at the stick, and then at the leg, and then at nothing in particular at all.

“**You are not on this cart,**” the woman said.

“**No,**” he said.

“**Then you are on it,**” she said, “**and I would like to know why, because nobody has asked me a single thing about this cart and I did not tell that man what was under the sacking.**”

“**I do not know,**” Aren Kest said, “**and nobody has asked me a thing about it either, and I got on, and I had about nine days to think about getting on and I did not spend them on it.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody argued with her and the driver put the pot at the foot of the load where a man could get his hand round it without stopping.

The made road ran on for about six hours and the last two of them were uphill, and about half way up the last hill a woman came out of a door and said his name out loud, at about the fourth hour, in a yard, to a man standing in it, and the man in the yard did not use it and the woman went back inside. Nobody in that yard knew where he had gone and nobody in that yard asked and about four people in that market town can still say where he sleeps and none of them is on that cart.

He got up the last hill in about four seconds longer than it takes to sit down, and then sat down on a stone wall at the top of it for about as long as a man takes to say nine, and the woman did not say anything to him about that and neither did the driver.

**The made road ends at a cross of two stones and a heap of broken metalling beside it, and the driver put the load down there and said that he was not taking a cart any further, and that the rest was not a road and had not been one in the memory of anybody he had ever spoken to.**

Nobody thanked him for stopping. The sacking came off the pot. The cord on the scion was loose where she herself had tied it tight six days before, and the woman said so, in the flat voice she uses for a fact she cannot account for, and he said nothing, and it was not the fourth hour and his palm was not warm.

Then the four of them went on, and the four were a man of about fifty, a woman of about thirty-eight, a man of thirty-two with a limp, and a boy of about nineteen who had been hired in the market town to walk beside the pot and who carried it, and who had been told the same as everybody else, which is nothing.

The lane goes up out of the wet ground and along the foot of the lowest slope, and the water on that side is a channel of stone about two paces wide with a lip along the high side of it, and it goes up past nine or ten terraces before the lane gives up and turns into a stair between two walls.

**And the lane ends at a bar, and a woman of about fifty-five was sitting on a stool inside the bar with a board on her knees, and the board had four things written at the top of it in a hand that had been used for about thirty years, and when the four of them came up to it she put the board face down and did not write anything and asked four questions, in order, and she has asked those four of everybody who comes up that lane since before the driver was born.**

“**What is this lane called?**”

“**The Low Lane,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said. “**And above it the Middle Way, and above that the High Row, and there is a name for the middle of this valley and I am not going to give it to you and it is not mine to give.**”

“**Who lives at the end of it?**”

“**Eleven households on the Long Set and the settlement at the middle terrace. I keep a block of the Long Set between two head stones. I am not the Standing and I am not speaking for the Standing and nobody in this valley has asked me to.**”

“**What is carried on it?**”

“**Fruit down and loam up, and a leaf once, and a pot of earth with a tree in it, and a man with a limp,**” she said, and the woman of about fifty-five wrote the first three of those on the board and did not write the fourth.

“**And the fourth. What is the name you use for it?**”

**And the woman of about thirty-eight did not answer that one at once, and the boy of about nineteen said afterwards that she had gone the colour of the wet stone, and the driver said afterwards that he had not been looking, and neither of those two accounts has been checked against anybody else's.**

“**That is not a gate question,**” she said. “**You have asked me that for about thirty years and nobody has ever answered it and you ask it because a gate has four things to ask and you have run out of a fourth thing that is about a lane.**”

“**I have,**” the woman of about fifty-five said, and put her thumb on the fourth line of the board, and did not read it out.

“**Then I will answer it and I will say why I am answering it, and I want it said afterwards that I said it on my own account. The name I use for the person who may speak for a road in this valley is a person and not an office, and I have worked that out on my own, and I have never been asked it and I have never been allowed to be asked it, and nobody here has been asked anything for about twenty-two years, and I am asking it today on my own account and not for the Standing and not for anybody at the end of any made road.**”

Nobody thanked her. The woman of about fifty-five turned the board over and looked at the fourth line and did not say what was on it and did not ask what was on it.

**And the bar of wood was never lifted. It is lying in the grass where it was lying before the four of them came up, and the two men who have to lift it when a cart comes down in the pick were not called and did not come, and the four of them walked under it and up the stair between the two walls, and it is not a door and there is no door in it and there was no sound.**

He was on the same side of it at the sixth hour and he was not on the same side of it at the seventh, and the woman of about thirty-eight was in front of him and did not appear to have done anything, and the driver was behind him and said afterwards that he had not noticed the crossing at all and had been looking at the load.

Nobody in that valley has ever been asked to account for it, and about nine people who were on the lane that day have said, at one time or another and without being asked, that they were not near the bar, and about four have said that they were, and the two sets do not overlap, and nobody has asked any of the thirteen.

Then the pot went down onto the boards of the drying floor at the foot of the slope, and the sacking came off it, and the cord was tight again.

“**That is not a thing a pot does on a cart,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said.

“**No,**” he said.

“**I know it is not. I have been carrying it nine days and in nine days it has not done that once, and I am not going to work out what it is, and you are not going to work out what it is either, and if you do work out what it is you are not going to tell me first.**”

Nobody thanked her. The man of about fifty drove back down the lane with the empty load and did not come up it again that day, and the boy of about nineteen slept on the boards of the drying floor and was paid in the morning and went down the lane before the sun was up, and nobody thanked the boy.

**And then a woman of about seventy came in from the Low Set with a lamp and looked at the leaf, because the woman of about thirty-eight had taken it out of her coat to dry it, and she said one thing about it and then went home, and what she said is the whole of what came home on the leaf.**

“**That is a leaf with four divisions on it,**” she said, “**and not one of them is a head, and the fourth one is for a person, and the mark is written under it and not in it, and I have been in this valley for about seventy years and I have never once seen anybody write a mark under the fourth division of anything.**”

Nobody asked her why. Nobody asked her what a head is. She went home with the lamp.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-eight's, and a pot of earth and a leaf are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a length of cord at four, a board at seven, a sheet of cartridge paper at nine, a knife at eleven, a night's straw at twelve, a loaf at fourteen, a strap at sixteen, a second knife at twenty, a willow band at twenty-four, and a pot of water at thirty.** Four, seven, nine, eleven, twelve, fourteen, sixteen, twenty, twenty-four and thirty is one hundred and forty-seven pence. **Forty-eight pence is a mark in this county and one hundred and forty-seven pence is three marks and three over**, and a thing carried in a coat is not a price and is not in this column, and the nine days there and the nine days back are two figures of the same figure of time and neither of them is a price, and four days and about nine of hard going is a figure of distance and is not in it, and eleven households and eleven terraces are one figure of a slope told two ways and are not added to each other and neither of them goes in at either end of this column, and four things asked at a gate are four things and are not a figure of anybody and are not in it, and the nine who were on the lane and the four who were near the bar are two sets of one afternoon and the two of them together are about thirteen and none of them is a figure of the people who live at the top of that slope and none of them is in this column.

The lamp went down the slope and the boards of the drying floor were cold by the fourth hour and he slept on them, and in the morning a woman of about thirty-eight was already at the Low Gate with her back to the bar, and she had a knife in her hand and a leaf in her coat, and neither of those two objects was the object it had been the day before.
