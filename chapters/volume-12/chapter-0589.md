# Chapter 0589

**The fourth hundred pace of the cut, the ninth hour of the pick sitting, about nine yards of lip carrying on a bed that has gone, a barrow standing empty on the low side, a man of about thirty-two with a limp who has been given nothing to do since the second hour, and about nine people on a bank who are four days and about nine of hard going from the only person in nine counties who could do what is in front of them.**

The job came to him the way it came the first time, which is that it was offered out loud and in order and with a reason attached, and this time there was one reason and it was the good one.

The man of about fifty-one said it at about the fourth hour with a barrow-wheel in his hand and about nine people on that bank and the three hundred paces of wrong bed behind them and the fourth hundred pace in front of them.

“**There are four of us who can lay that bed. Me, the man who holds the second hoe, the carter of about nineteen, and a man of about sixty-four on the second terrace whose hands will last about nine days and I have watched them and they are going. You have laid three hundred paces of bed under a span in a county nobody here has heard of and you could put about forty paces of the fourth hundred in down in a day and I am not asking you for the four hundred. I am asking you for the forty.**”

Nobody thanked him for asking. Nobody interrupted.

“**I am not going to tell you the rest of it,**” the man of about fifty-one said, “**because I gave you the rest of it the last time and I have not found a better version of it and I have had about nine days, and I am not going to stand here and pretend the reason has gone away. It has not gone away. You know what it is.**”

He did not say it. That is the whole of what he did that day, and it is more than he has ever done, and it took him until about the seventh hour, and about four people on that bank have taken the view that a man who does not say no out loud has not refused, and about nine have taken the view that a man who does not say no out loud has not agreed either and that in this valley that is the same as no and it is worse, because a thing that was said can be argued with and a thing that was not said can only be obeyed, and the two sets do not overlap and the man of thirty-two has not been asked which of the two he agrees with.

He barrowed from the second hour to the ninth hour. He is good at it. He carried about nine barrows of spoil to about four paces and then to the fan and then down the low side, and the boy of about fifteen kept the line with him without being asked, and nobody watched them and nobody told him he had done it well.

Then at the ninth hour the woman of about thirty-eight came down off the high side and stood at the end of about nine yards of lip and did not say anything for a moment, and then she said nine sentences in the oldest voice in that valley, and she heard every one of them arrive in a voice she was given at a table when she was small, and he heard it too, and neither of them said so afterwards.

“**You were wrong about the first one and you have been wrong about it every day since and nobody has told you and I have been waiting about four days to see whether the woman of about thirty-eight would say it and she has not and I have.**”

Nobody answered that. About nine people were on that bank.

“**A finding about a road in this valley has to be made by somebody who can be moved off the ground here. That is the rule and the rule is good and I have said so twice.**”

“**Then you are not wrong about the rule,**” Aren Kest said.

“**I am not wrong about the rule and I am not going to let you stand on the rule either, because you have been standing on it since the sixth hour of the set sitting and you have not said one word out loud, and a man who stands on a rule is not obeying it. The rule says a person who can be moved off the ground here. It does not say a person who holds nothing.**”

He did not answer that and she went on and the voice did not change.

“**You have been treating that as the same thing since the set sitting and it is not the same thing and I am the proof and I am standing here. I hold four blocks. I have held them for nine years. I dug a hole at the foot of the oldest tree with a spade in my own hand in front of about eleven people, and I said yes to about ninety people when I was about sixteen and I did not go, and nobody in this valley has ever been able to move me off this ground, including me.**”

Nobody said anything at all on that bank for about as long as a man takes to say nine.

“**So the rule is the rule and you are not outside it, and the only reason nobody has asked you to do a second thing is that you have not said yes and nobody in this valley has yet worked out that a man who does not say no is a man who has already been told. That is the worst place in the world to be a man who does not say no, because it is the one place where being quiet is not a thing anybody can do for you.**”

Then she stopped, in her own voice, and it took her a moment to get there, and the man who keeps the water stood four hundred paces off and did not come over.

“**That is the whole of what I have got and I have said it in the wrong voice on purpose because I could not get it out in the right one, and I have not said it to be kind to you, and you should be going up that bank right now and laying forty paces of that bed, and I am not going to say it again, and nobody in this valley is going to thank you either way.**”

**Nobody thanked him for not doing it. Nobody said out loud that he should have done it. Neither of those things was said by anybody on that bank or on that slope or in that valley, and the man of about thirty-one of the Standing, who had come down to ask the question and had not asked it, said nothing at all, which is the first time in about nineteen years that he has stood on a channel bank and said nothing at all, and nobody has asked him why and he has not been asked.**

He did not put a straight edge on that lip. He did not hold one end of it. The line of chalk on that lip is where it was, and it was cut in the set sitting by the man of about fifty-one with the boy of about fifteen holding the chalk and the woman of about thirty-eight at the far end of it, and nobody in this valley has cut another one and the finding with three names on it is on a stone at the head of that Long Set and it is not his and it will not be.

**And the barrow stood empty on the low side from the seventh hour to the ninth hour and the boy of about fifteen rang it, meaning the bell, at the fourth point for the penny, and the man of about thirty-one of the Standing came back up the slope alone and did not tell anybody where he had been and has not been asked.**

**Nobody thanked anybody, and this is not going to end the way the other nine end, because a thing a man gave up by not saying one word out loud on a channel bank is not a price and is not going to be called one here. The column below is not his and it is not hers and it belongs to nobody who was on that bank except a boy and a woman of about thirty-eight, and the boy was paid his penny and the penny is not in it.**

**The four miles and the forty paces and the nine yards of lip and the three hundred paces of wrong bed behind them are four figures of distance and none of them is a cost anybody can carry in a hand. The finding is a slate with three names on it and a chalk line on a lip is a mark and not a head and the fourth head of the Crown's form is ruled and empty about four counties off and is not any of them. The answer is not made. The water is where it was. And a woman of about thirty-eight has told a man of thirty-two that he was wrong, which is the thing she asked for twice and was not given, and she gave it to a man who had not asked for it and is not the answer for a road and has never claimed to be, and about four people in that valley have said that she has finally said it to somebody and about nine have said that she has said it to the one man in nine counties who cannot do anything with it, and the two sets do not overlap and she has not been asked which of the two she meant.**

**a plumb-line at two, a canvas apron at six, a wicker creel at ten, a coil of marram at thirteen, a rubbing stone at seventeen, a set of punches at twenty, a pitch fork at twenty-four, a bundle of withies at twenty-eight, a grease pot at thirty-two, a hand bar at thirty-five, and a hand scraper at thirty-seven.** Two, six, ten, thirteen, seventeen, twenty, twenty-four, twenty-eight, thirty-two, thirty-five and thirty-seven is two hundred and twenty-four pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and twenty-four pence is four marks and thirty-two over**, and about four miles and the fourth hundred pace and about nine yards of lip and about three hundred paces of wrong bed and about nine barrows to four paces and about four paces and about four hundred paces are seven figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the fourth hour and the seventh hour and the ninth hour and the second hour and the sixth hour of the set sitting and four days of a sitting and about nine days are seven more figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column, and the nine households on the four lowest are a figure of persons and are not to be added to another, and the boy was paid his penny and that is not in this column. Nine sentences, in the oldest voice in that valley, from a woman who can hear it come out, to a man who is not the answer for a road and has never claimed to be. Nobody thanked anybody.
