# Chapter 0552

**A letter came up a made road into a market town of about two thousand people on the twenty-ninth of Mudmonth, YR 320, a Wednesday and not a market day, between the second hour and the ninth, and it was read out loud in a hired room over a saddler's to about nine people, and it asks a man of thirty-two with a limp to put his name at the top of a statement about a road, and eleven people came with the man who brought it and two of them came on the Monday and were not counted into the nine who were here on the Sunday, and none of those figures was added to any other and no two of them were added to each other.**

The man who brought it is a man of about fifty-three and his name is Merritt Gallow and he walked into that room at about the third hour with a satchel he did not put down and he put it down at the fourth hour and not before, and about four of the nine people in that room had it that he had come a long way and about five had it that he had not, and neither of those is a fact about him and both of them are facts about a room.

**He is not a knave and he is not a villain and no part of what has been said in that room says whose plan the letter is and it is not going to be said in this county in his hearing or out of it, and nobody in that room asked him and he did not offer.**

The letter is four hands long. It is written in four different hands, which is a thing a person can see, and the man of about fifty-eight with a chain of office that is a badge read all of it out loud at about the fifth hour in the voice of a man reading a thing he has already read, and nobody stopped him and nobody asked him to stop.

The letter says that a valley of terraced fruit four days and about nine of hard going from the nearest made road has had no quarrel in it for about twenty-two years. It says that the fruit of that valley keeps where fruit in nine counties does not. It says that eleven people went into it and came back and that none of the eleven was asked for anything and none of the eleven was paid and that is a fact about the valley and not about the eleven. It says that a road which cannot be found out about cannot be closed, and that a road which can be found out about can be closed, and that the second half of that sentence has been proved in nine counties twice inside about five months by people who are not in this room.

And it asks, in the last line, for a date.

“**I have read that last line nine times,**” Gallow said, “**and I am going to say out loud what it is, and then nobody in this room has to be polite about it. The last line is not asking for a date. The last line is asking whether the man who is going to sign will do it in public, and a date is the smallest way a person asks that. I have asked him the small way because I did not think I would get the large way.**”

Nobody thanked him for saying that and nobody argued with him out loud, and a good deal of that room had it that he was right, and the rest of it had it that he was a man who had come four days to get one line, and the room did not vote and does not vote.

**And then he said the thing that made the room stop, and he said it without being asked, and it is the one thing in the whole of that day that nobody has yet been able to get past.**

“**A certificate is not a fact about a road. A certificate is a fact about a man. I have had that out with two clerks in a county two hundred miles off and I have been told that a thing with a man's name at the top of it is a thing a person can be shown, and I would like it read out that I said that a certificate is a name on a piece of paper and that I came a long way to put one on a piece of paper, because I am not going to be able to say it again in about four years and this is the room I am going to remember saying it in.**”

Nobody thanked him. The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key said one thing and it was about the size of a room and not about the letter, and she said it to the table.

“**You want the certification made in that street with about four hundred people standing up in it, because about four hundred people stood up in that street and read four lines off a board outside that weigh-house over eight days at the end of Thawmonth and the beginning of Mudmonth, and you want that again with a name on it. I have been in this room four months and I have been the only person in it who has ever been walked to, and I would like it minuted that I know exactly what a room with four hundred people in it is for, because it is what it is for.**”

Nobody argued with her and nobody agreed with her out loud and Gallow said that she was right and that he had not come to argue and that he had come to be refused if the refusal was going to happen, and that he would rather be refused in a room than be given a thing in a street that is not true.

**And Aren Kest asked four questions in that room, and all four are here, and not one of them was a question about a form.**

He asked who had sent the letter. He was told an office that is not named and that he did not press, because a man who has come four days with eleven people and a letter is not obliged to name the office at the table and everybody in that room understood that and nobody thanked him for not pressing it.

He asked how many of the eleven had been in the valley and he was told that it was not all of them and that if he wanted a figure he could put one down himself. **So he put one down, and the man of about fifty-eight ruled a head over it, and nobody filled it, and nobody in that room said anything about that, and the leaf is not the Crown's form and the fourth head of the Crown's form is on the side table with nothing in it and is not this one. It has been empty for about five months and it was empty before that room was hired and it will be empty in about four months, and no office and no person has filled it, and the man of about fifty-eight is not going to fill it and has said so out loud.**

He asked what the valley was called by the people who lived in it. He was told it was called the Orchard, and that the word *quiet* was not in it and that the word *quiet* was in the letter and had come from a report, and that the man of about fifty-three did not know where the report was and had never seen it and had the word from the man who told him the letter was worth having.

**And then he asked the fourth question, and it is the fourth question he has asked in that room, and it is the one that decides what happens next, and he asked it at about the seventh hour and he asked it to Gallow and not to the table.**

“**If a thing is to be certified, who certifies it?**”

“**The Standing does,**” Gallow said. “**They meet when there is fruit and they are the whole of the government in that valley and nobody in nine counties has ever written down how many of them there are, and I have not asked, and there has never been any dispute about it in as long as anybody here has been writing about it.**”

“**Then the letter wants a name,**” Aren Kest said, “**and it has wanted a name since the first line of it, and there is a head in this room and it is on the table by that door, and it has got four heads on it and three of them are filled and the fourth of them is empty and has been empty for about five months, and the fourth one is the one that takes a name for a road.**”

Nobody in that room said one word for a count of four.

**And the man of about fifty-eight said the whole of it out loud, and nobody had asked him and nobody wrote it down and it is here because he said it in a room with about nine people in it.**

“**Then I am not going to rule a head for it and I am not going to fill that one and I am not going to rule a second head over that one and I am not going to put a man's name on a leaf of mine with nothing over it, and I will tell the two of you why, and it is not modesty. It is that in about four years there will be a clerk in this county who picks up a sheet with a name on it, and he will not be able to say who put it there, and he will not be able to say who was asked. A head is a thing that says somebody is going to be asked. I have not got anybody to ask. Nobody in nine counties has asked anybody anything about that valley in twenty-two years and that is not a fault of the valley and it is the whole of my difficulty.**”

Nobody thanked him and nobody argued with him and Gallow said *that is fair* and *that is not an answer* in the same breath and would not say which he meant.

**And the cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is the man of about fifty-three's, and it is the first one he has ever kept, and a letter four hands long is not in it.**

**a farthing at one, a sheet of oiled paper at two, a pot of paste at three, a case of quills at six, a stick of chalk at eight, a night's bed at eleven, a pound of candles at fourteen, a bundle of kindling at seventeen, a ream of paper at twenty, and a second night's bed at six.** One, two, three, six, eight, eleven, fourteen, seventeen, twenty and six is eighty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-eight pence is one mark and forty over**, and eleven people is a figure of persons and is not in this column, and the empty head under which a figure was to have gone is a shape and not a figure and is not in it, and no two of the figures of persons in that room are added here, and a road four days and about nine off is not in it, and twenty-two years is a figure of time and not a price, and a month carried at thirty is not in it and is not a figure of days anybody could have counted in that room.

At about the ninth hour the eleven who had come with the letter went up to the room over the inn with the man of about fifty-three and the room over the saddler's was shut behind them at the door with the key in the pocket of the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key, and the pot of earth that was not in the room on the Sunday was not in the room on the Wednesday, and it came up the lane on the Monday, and that is the next day and not this one.
