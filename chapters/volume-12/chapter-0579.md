# Chapter 0579

**The cut, the fourth hour of the set sitting, the fourth hundred pace from the top, about nine yards of lip carrying on a bed that has gone and a line of rubble about two hands thick under it, a man of about fifty-one with a foot-rule, a boy of about fifteen who rings the bell, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who came up the lane in the morning and has been told there is a job.**

He came up the lane at about the second hour with a blister on the heel of the left boot and no money for a cart, and he went straight to the cut because there was nothing else in that valley he was part of, and he got up the first step in about four seconds longer than it takes to sit down on one.

Nobody asked him where he had been. The woman of about thirty-eight said one thing about it and the thing was not a question.

“**You told me you were going and you went and I have said out loud to about nine people that I have not been asked where you went, and I would like it noticed that I did not ask and that it is not the same as not wanting to.**”

“**It is not the same,**” Aren Kest said.

“**No. Go up. There is something at the fourth hundred pace and it is not mine and it is not yours.**”

**And there was, and it had not been there about nine days ago in that shape, and the shape was this: a plank of green wood lying on the grass in the light at the head of the Long Set with marks cut into it, and a reason.**

For about nine years a man could see what was wrong with four miles of stone channel in about nine minutes and could not say it, because the reason it wanted mending was in a record nobody was allowed to read, and a fault with no reason behind it is not a finding, it is an accusation.

The reason was on a plank in the daylight at the head of the slope. It was a mark and not a list and nobody had said yet what it was a mark of. But it was there, and it could be picked up and carried to about nine yards of lip and set beside it.

The man of about fifty-one was at the fourth hundred pace with a foot-rule and a length of chalk and a straight edge about the length of his own arm, and the boy of about fifteen was there with a barrow because he barrows stone, and they had been a quarter of an hour on it when the man of thirty-two came down the high side.

“**There is a job,**” the man of about fifty-one said, before he was asked. “**And you are the only man in nine counties who can do it, and I am going to tell you what it is and then I am going to tell you why you are not going to do it, and I have thought about it for about four days and I have not found a better way of saying it.**”

“**Say the job first.**”

“**Put a straight edge across that lip and find out what is under it, in front of three people of this valley, and write down what is under it. Not for me. For whoever comes after. That is what a finding is. Three people saw it and three people wrote it and the three people can be found and refused and told they are wrong.**”

Nobody thanked him for the offer.

“**Say the rest.**”

“**You cannot do it and there are three reasons and the third one is the one I would say if I only had one.**”

The man of about fifty-one put the foot-rule down on the lip.

“**First, a finding about a road in this valley has to be made by somebody who can be moved off the ground here. Not because it is a better finding. Because the only person in this valley who could put his name at the top of a thing about this water is a person who holds nothing, and you hold nothing, and that is exactly why you cannot be the one who writes it down. If a man from off this hill holds the pen, then in about nine years there will be a paper with a name on it and this valley will be a place with a name on it, and I have watched what that does to about four other places and I am not going to do it to this one.**”

Nobody argued with him.

“**Second, you have been the man who finds the load-bearing thing in a room for eleven years and everybody in that room knows it, and you know it, and I know it, and about nine people in a market town know it and cannot be got to agree about what it means. A man who finds a fault and then gets it acted on is a man who gets put in charge of it inside about four days. I have seen it. You have been in this valley about nine days and about four people have already stopped asking me things and started asking you.**”

Nobody argued with that either.

“**Third,**” the man of about fifty-one said, “**which is the one I would say if I only had one. In the pick sitting you stood on that lip with a fault in you and you did not say it, and I watched you not say it for about four hours, and I came back down to you at the seventh hour and asked you twice and you said no twice, and both of those answers were true and both of them were about a different thing, and I have never said one word about that since and I am not going to start now. But you told that boy the two facts and not the third and you said you had not got a reason. Now you have got a reason off a plank, and the whole of what a valley is, is a set of people who can be told a reason and can act on it without asking a stranger. If you put that straight edge down, then every finding this valley ever makes about its own water has a man from off the hill underneath it, and there will not be a second time anybody trusts this slope to say what is wrong with itself.**”

Nobody thanked him.

Aren Kest stood on the high side of four miles of stone and said the thing he had come down the lane to say, and he said it out loud, and about four people in that valley heard it and about nine were not on that slope.

“**I am not going to do it. I have been the man who says what is wrong with a road for eleven years and I have wanted this job since about the fourth hour of the pick sitting and I have wanted it so much that I could not account for how much, and I am not going to do it, and the reason is not modesty and it is not the rule you made about a person who can be moved off the ground. The reason is that you are the one who keeps the water, and you have carried a fault you could not say for about nine years, and a finding with a stranger's hand on it is not a finding by this valley. It is a finding by a man who came up a lane and is going down it again.**”

Nobody thanked him for that either, and nobody said out loud that he should have done it, and four people have put it about that a man who walks four miles of stone and then says no to the one thing he is able to do has found the only honest work there is, and nine have put it about that it was the fourth thing in nine days he has refused and that about four hundred people in a market town would call it cowardice, and the two sets do not overlap and nobody has asked the man of about fifty-one which he believes.

**And nobody may thank him for not doing it, and nobody has.**

So he carried barrows. The man of about fifty-one pointed at nine barrows of spoil that had come out of the bed over about nine years and had gone down the low side and had made a fan about four paces across where a person walking up the cut in the dark would put a foot wrong, and he took the barrow down to the Wet Set and emptied it there and came back, nine times, and the man of about fifty-one did not watch him and did not thank him and did not tell him he had done it well.

**And the straight edge went down on the lip of the fourth hundred pace at about the sixth hour by the man of about fifty-one, with the woman of about thirty-eight at the far end of it and the boy of about fifteen holding the chalk, and about four people stood and looked and the man of about thirty-one of the Standing came down and looked and nobody in that valley has said what was under the lip. It was not a mend. Nobody in that valley has had a mend anybody can name in about nine years and this was not one, and a line of chalk on a lip is not a finding until three people have written it down and none of the three has written anything yet.**

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the boy of about fifteen's, and a line of chalk and a straight edge and about four miles of stone are not in it and a fault has no price.**

**a rushlight at two, a bobbin at six, a wedge at ten, a piece of chalk at twelve, a hobnail at twenty-two, a hank of string at twenty-six, a trowel at seventeen, and a second wedge at fifteen.** Two, six, ten, twelve, twenty-two, twenty-six, seventeen and fifteen is one hundred and ten pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and ten pence is two marks and fourteen over**, and the fourth hundred pace and nine yards and two hands thick and nine barrows and four paces and the fourth hour and the sixth hour and nine days and nine days ago are nine figures of distance and of time and of count and none of them is a price, and four people who looked and nine who were not on that slope are two figures of one afternoon and are not a figure of persons and go in at neither end of this column. **A line of chalk across a lip is not a head and is not a division and is not a fifth line, and the fourth head of the Crown's form is ruled and empty about four counties off, and a mark cut into a plank is not a document and is not a form and none of them is a price.**

The barrows were emptied and the fan at the low side is four paces across and will be there in the morning, and the boy of about fifteen was paid a penny for the bell and not paid for the chalk, and the man of about fifty-one wrote nothing down, and nobody thanked anybody.
