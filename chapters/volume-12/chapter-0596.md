# Chapter 0596

**Embermonth the sixth, YR 320, a Saturday, three names on a slate at the sixth hour of the bar sitting, four blocks held nine years by a woman who can be found, a man of the Standing who would have to write a name he will not write, and a person who can be refused.**

The slate with three names on it had lain on the stone at the head of the Long Set since before the bar sitting came in, and nobody had been asked to receive it, and the man of about thirty-one of the Standing came up at the sixth hour with a leaf in his hand and stood looking at it, and the woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a block between two head stones stood beside it, and Aren Kest stood below with a barrow, and the boy of about fifteen stood above with nothing in his hands for once.

The certification had been refused in Greenmonth in a market town four days and about nine off, and the refusal was on the ground before anybody in that room could say it, and no man in nine counties had been asked to sign since, and the man who would have signed it was the man most worried now about producing the thing that stood in its place, which was not a leaf with a name at the top but a person.

“**Write her,**” the man of about thirty-one said to the man of about fifty-one, who had come up behind him. “**Write who speaks for the water and we carry it down.**”

“**I will not,**” the man of about fifty-one said. “**A finding is three people who can be found and told they are wrong. What you are asking for is one person who can be pointed at, and I am not going to make her.**”

“**She would say yes.**”

“**She would,**” the man of about fifty-one said, “**and that is the whole of what is wrong with it. If she is the water because she is the only one who will stand up, then the water is one woman and the water is refused whenever she is. Write that down and see what you have.**”

The man of about thirty-one held the leaf out and then took it back and put it against his chest the way a man puts a thing away he has decided about, and the boy of about fifteen above the stone looked at the leaf and then at the man of about thirty-one and did not offer him the chalk, and the chalk stayed in the boy's own pocket.

The woman of about thirty-eight said nothing for a while, and then she said the thing the valley had to say in its own words, because no room two hundred miles off could say it for them, and the man of about thirty-one wrote nothing while she said it, which was the first true thing about that hour.

“**A road may be described only by its own answer,**” she said, “**and the answer is a person who can be refused by her own valley, and she is not a keeper of anything and is on no roll and was not thanked.**”

She said it in the same breath, all of it, and the three facts stood together the way three names stand on a slate. The person was the woman of about sixty-four who ties about nine hundred picking cords in a sitting and counts stones round the oldest tree, and she keeps nothing and holds no block, and her name is on no roll in this valley because she is paid in fruit and not in money, and nobody has thanked her in thirty years and nobody thanked her that morning either.

The man of about thirty-one looked at the leaf in his hand, which had four lines under a fourth head and nothing on the fourth, and he did not write her name on it, and the leaf stayed what it was, and the slate stayed what it was, and the Crown's form stayed four counties off with its fourth head ruled and empty, and a division on the board off the middle terrace stayed a division and not a head.

“**Will she speak,**” he said.

“**She is not on this slope,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said. “**She is at the lines and she will be at the lines until the cloths are in, and if you want her answer you will have to walk down there and ask her in front of the woman of about fifty-eight, and I am telling you now that you will not get a different answer from her than you get from me.**”

“**Ask her,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said. “**She has said she counts, in her own mouth, and she told me she did not find out until one morning that there was anything else in it. If she says no, the valley has its answer all the same, because a valley that can be refused has said yes to something for the first time in twenty-two years.**”

The man of about thirty-one said then that the low roads were making a gathering for themselves and that this valley's ground was wanted at it, and that a person going was better than a leaf going, because a leaf with nothing under a mark says yes to whatever a reader brings to it. The woman of about thirty-eight said that the person had not been asked. He said that was not a reason not to have a name ready. She said it was the only reason she had, and that a name written down before it is asked is a name somebody has been made to carry, and that nobody in this valley has had a name written down before she was asked for twenty-two years, and the answer to a road is a person and it is not made and not found, and she said all of that in her own voice and not in anybody else's.

The boy of about fifteen turned the slate round on the stone so that the three names faced the two of them, and read them out in order because nobody else was going to, and the man of about thirty-one said they were right in the order they were in, and the boy said that was not a thing anybody had told him they would be, and the man of about fifty-one said the three of them had put their names to a thing they had all seen with their own eyes, which was the only condition that had ever been asked of that slate and the only one that was on it. Then the boy put the chalk on the stone beside the slate and left it there, and went down the bank to help with a barrow he had not been carrying, and the man of about thirty-one put the leaf back inside his coat and went down the slope without it, and the two men and the woman stayed where they were for a while longer with nothing on the stone but the names.

Aren Kest lifted his barrow and carried it past them, and nobody asked him to witness anything, and he witnessed it all the same, and the man of about fifty-one did not watch him and did not tell him he had done it well. The slope lay quiet round them all and the fruit hung unripe and green on the blocks above, and the leaf went back into the coat of the man of about thirty-one and down the slope with him, and the slate stayed on its stone, and by the seventh hour the only thing on that stone was the three names and the weather working on them.

**What the sixth hour on the bank cost is a column, and it is the woman who keeps the block's, and a leaf unwritten and a name unmade are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a wicker creel at twenty-three, a bundle of withies at five, a grease pot at twenty-six, a canvas apron at ten, a rubbing stone at fifteen, a hand bar at twenty-seven, a pitch pot at seven, a plumb-line at twenty-five, a coil of marram at twelve, a set of punches at twenty-one, and a dung fork at seventeen.** Twenty-three, five, twenty-six, ten, fifteen, twenty-seven, seven, twenty-five, twelve, twenty-one and seventeen is one hundred and eighty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and eighty-eight pence is three marks and forty-four over**, and four blocks and three names on a slate and four lines and a fourth head and about nine hundred cords and three facts together and nine counties and four counties off and a chalk in a boy's pocket are nine figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the sixth hour and the seventh hour and Greenmonth and the bar sitting and thirty years and one morning and nine years held and twenty-two years are eight figures of time and not one of them is put at either end of this column. **A man of about thirty-one of the Standing and a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a block and Aren Kest with a barrow and a boy of about fifteen and a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water and a woman of about sixty-four who ties the cords and a woman of about fifty-eight who turns the cloths are seven figures of persons, and the seventh of them is not on the slope this hour and is not asked.** Nobody thanked either of them.
