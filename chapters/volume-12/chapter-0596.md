# Chapter 0596

**Three names on a slate at the sixth hour of the bar sitting, four blocks held nine years by a woman who can be found, a man of the Standing who would have to write a name he will not write, and a person who can be refused.**

The slate with three names on it had lain on the stone at the head of the Long Set for nine days and a morning, and nobody had been asked to receive it, and the man of about thirty-one of the Standing came up at the sixth hour with a leaf in his hand and stood looking at it, and the woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a block between two head stones stood beside it, and Aren Kest stood below with a barrow, and the boy of about fifteen stood above with nothing in his hands for once.

The certification had been refused in Greenmonth in a market town four days and about nine off, and the refusal was on the ground before anybody in that room could say it, and no man in nine counties had been asked to sign since, and the man who would have signed it was the man most worried now about producing the thing that stood in its place, which was not a leaf with a name at the top but a person.

“**Write her,**” the man of about thirty-one said to the man of about fifty-one, who had come up behind him. “**Write who speaks for the water and we carry it down.**”

“**I will not,**” the man of about fifty-one said. “**A finding is three people who can be found and told they are wrong. What you are asking for is one person who can be pointed at, and I am not going to make her.**”

The woman of about thirty-eight said nothing for a while, and then she said the thing the valley had to say in its own words, because no room two hundred miles off could say it for them, and the man of about thirty-one wrote nothing while she said it, which was the first true thing about that hour.

“**A road may be described only by its own answer,**” she said, “**and the answer is a person who can be refused by her own valley, and she is not a keeper of anything and is on no roll and was not thanked.**”

She said it in the same breath, all of it, and the three facts stood together the way three names stand on a slate. The person was the woman of about sixty-four who ties about nine hundred picking cords in a sitting and counts stones round the oldest tree, and she keeps nothing and holds no block, and her name is on no roll in this valley because she is paid in fruit and not in money, and nobody has thanked her in thirty years and nobody thanked her that morning either.

The man of about thirty-one looked at the leaf in his hand, which had four lines under a fourth head and nothing on the fourth, and he did not write her name on it, and the leaf stayed what it was, and the slate stayed what it was, and the Crown's form stayed four counties off with its fourth head ruled and empty, and a division on the board off the middle terrace stayed a division and not a head.

“**Will she speak,**” he said.

“**Ask her,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said. “**She has said she counts, in her own mouth, and she told me she did not find out until one morning that there was anything else in it. If she says no, the valley has its answer all the same, because a valley that can be refused has said yes to something for the first time in twenty-two years.**”

A delegate from that valley would go to the gathering the low roads are making for themselves, and the delegate's first act would be to insist that its right to refuse contact stays explicit, and that was not tidied up on the ground that morning, and nobody called it a victory, and the man of about thirty-one put the leaf back in his coat and went down the slope without it.

Aren Kest lifted his barrow and carried it past them, and nobody asked him to witness anything, and he witnessed it all the same, and the man of about fifty-one did not watch him and did not tell him he had done it well, and the slope lay quiet round them all, and the fruit hung unripe and green, and the stone waited, and the slate waited, and the leaf went back in the coat, and nothing was decided that could be undone by deciding.

**What that hour on the bank cost is a column, and it is the woman's who keeps the block, and a leaf unwritten and a name unmade are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a wicker creel at twenty-two, a bundle of withies at three, a grease pot at twenty-five, a canvas apron at eight, a rubbing stone at fourteen, a hand bar at twenty-six, a pitch pot at five, a plumb-line at twenty-four, a coil of marram at eleven, a set of punches at twenty, and a dung fork at seventeen.** Twenty-two, three, twenty-five, eight, fourteen, twenty-six, five, twenty-four, eleven, twenty and seventeen is one hundred and seventy-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seventy-five pence is three marks and thirty-one over**, and four blocks and three names on a slate and four lines and a fourth head and about nine hundred cords and three facts together and nine counties and four counties off are eight figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the sixth hour and nine days and a morning and Greenmonth and the bar sitting and thirty years and one morning and nine years held and twenty-two years are eight figures of time and not one of them is put at either end of this column. **A man of about thirty-one of the Standing and a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a block and Aren Kest with a barrow and a boy of about fifteen and a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water and a woman of about sixty-four who ties the cords are six figures of persons and are not added to one another.** Nobody thanked either of them.
