# Chapter 0600

**Embermonth the twenty-fifth, YR 320, a Thursday and a market day, the second hour in a lane, a hearing that stands with no bench in it, a fourth head still empty, a floor washed by somebody nobody knows, and a leaf face down on a table upstairs.**

The market filled the street at the second hour, and the lane behind the weigh-house stood clear of it, and Aren Kest came up the lane on his stick with the dust of four days and about nine of hard going on his coat, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room over the weigh-house stood in the lane with the key in her hand, and nobody else stood in that lane, and no third person came into it the whole hour.

Upstairs the room held what it holds. The hearing stood with no bench in it. The Crown's form lay on the side table with three heads filled and the fourth ruled and empty, and it had been about six months, and nobody had filled it and nobody had said that anybody anywhere had filled one. The floor had been washed by somebody nobody knows, and nobody was thanked, and no interval was given for it. The half leaf lay face down on a table upstairs, and nobody had turned it over and nobody was scheduled to turn it over, and nothing said what was on the other side of it.

The copyist of about twenty-six had written on a bare leaf that the delegate of a valley had sent word, and the word was a written refusal of the right to be thanked for coming, and it was not softened, and nobody thanked the delegate, and a document about a place where every argument was once put in the ground lay beside it in the room and it was not the Crown's form, and the five documents were in five places and none of them was the Crown's form.

“**The hearing stands,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, “**and there is no bench in it, and the fourth head is empty, and the floor was washed by somebody nobody knows, and the leaf is face down, and the delegate will not be thanked for coming, and that is the whole of what this room has to show for six months.**”

Aren Kest looked at the lane and the room above it, and the market sounded beyond, and he had crossed back out with a person in his hand instead of a certificate, and the person was gone down the made road to the gathering, and he had no leaf with a name at the top and no finding with three names, and the valley behind him had argued in public on purpose and had not been tidied, and a fourth head was still ruled and still empty, and nobody had thanked anybody at any point in that valley.

“**It is a beautiful place,**” he said.

“**It is,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, and she said the sentence he needed said, and she said it in her words and not in his, and neither of them said one word about the four and a half years, and nothing between them was repaired in a scene or an aside or a document or an apology.

“**A beautiful place is still an owned place,**” she said.

He stood in the lane a while after that, and the key turned in the door above, and the market sounded beyond with the noise of carts and voices, and the dust of the road stayed on his coat, and the room above held its quiet with the leaf face down and the form with its head empty, and the lane held the two of them without a third, and that was enough, and the day went on toward evening with nothing settled and nothing broken, and the press stood open with no date anybody will give, and the register stayed at seven entries all questions and no findings, and the day-book stayed at nine, and the drawer about eleven inches deep held about four hundred returns, and about nine hundred sheets a month went out into nine counties and about a hundred and four in one band on a fixed day and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month came in, and none was derived from another.

Nobody thanked him for not signing. Nobody said out loud that he should have signed. About four hundred in that town say the rest of the record stands up because of him, and about four hundred say anything was believed because of him, and the two sets do not overlap and he has not been asked which of the two he holds to, and this ground does not choose between them.

**What that hour in the lane cost is a column, and it is the woman's who keeps the room, and a hearing that stands and a head unfilled and a floor washed unknown are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a sieve at twenty-three, a cake of soap at four, a bridle bit at thirty-two, a hank of yarn at eight, a water pail at twenty-six, a jar of lamp oil at eleven, a stirrup strap at twenty-nine, a whetstone at fourteen, a leather lace at nine, a hoof rasp at twenty, and a hand file at seventeen.** Twenty-three, four, thirty-two, eight, twenty-six, eleven, twenty-nine, fourteen, nine, twenty and seventeen is one hundred and ninety-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and ninety-three pence is four marks and one over**, and three heads filled and a fourth head ruled and empty and a half leaf face down and a bare leaf and five documents and five places and three names not carried and eleven inches of drawer and about four hundred returns and about nine hundred sheets and about a hundred and four in one band and about four hundred and nine parish rolls and nine counties are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the second hour and a Thursday and about six months and four and a half years and four days and about nine of hard going and a fixed day are six figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a copyist of about twenty-six and somebody nobody knows and a delegate of a valley and Aren Kest and about four hundred people holding one true thing and about four hundred holding another are seven figures of persons and are not added to one another.** Nobody thanked anybody.
