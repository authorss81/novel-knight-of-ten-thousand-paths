# Chapter 0594

**A man of thirty-two with a limp on the low side of the channel from the third hour of the bar sitting to the ninth, about nine barrows of spoil, about four hundred people holding one true thing in a market town and about four hundred holding another, and a thing he is not asked to do.**

Aren Kest had refused one job out loud on this slope in three reasons and had not said no out loud to a second, and he did not decide either again, because neither was put to him again, and the morning laid out what was left, which was barrowing from the third hour to the ninth and being good at it when the valley needed something else.

The fourth hundred pace wanted hands. The man of about fifty-one who keeps the water had four who could lay the bed, and three were himself and the man of about forty-four who holds the second hoe and a carter of about nineteen, and the fourth was a man of about sixty-four on the second terrace whose hands would last about nine days, and about nine men in all were on the bank, and the top three hundred paces stood wrong above them and about a hundred paces between stood untouched, and about four hundred paces of good new bed held below.

He barrowed. He carried about nine barrows of spoil down the low side to the fan before the middle of the morning, and the boy of about fifteen kept the line with him without being asked, and nobody watched them and nobody told him he had done it well, and the spoil dried on the low side while they worked.

At the fifth hour the man of about fifty-one came down the high side and stood four hundred paces off and looked at the bed, and what he saw was a real and correct and necessary thing, which was that the low side wanted a second fan cut to take the spoil or the bank would slip under its own weight before the hundred paces were touched, and it was not a job anybody had asked for, and it was the difference the whole day turned on.

A thing he was asked to do was barrowing, and he was doing it. A thing he was the only man in nine counties who could do was to see where a fan should be cut so a bank does not slip, and to say it out loud to one other person and to no second one, and he had said the fault once to nobody in particular and the man of about fifty-one had heard it and never repeated it, and the standing rule of this valley was unchanged.

He said nothing about the fan. He barrowed past the place where it should be cut four times, and each time the line of rubble about two hands thick under the lip came back into his head unasked, and each time he put it down again, because to say it to the man of about fifty-one would have been to act on the load-bearing problem, and he may see it and he may not be the person who acts on it, and this valley is the last ground where that rule holds him.

About four hundred people in a market town four days and about nine off say he is the reason the rest of the record stands up, and about four hundred say he is the reason anybody should have believed any of it, and the two sets do not overlap, and he walks between them the way a man walks between two fields, and neither field is his.

At the ninth hour the woman of about thirty-eight came down off the high side with a basket of small stone for the top of the bed, and she saw where his eyes had gone four times, because she keeps a block and knows what a bank does under weight, and she said nothing about it, and he said nothing about it, and the fan was not cut that day, and the bank did not slip that day either, and both of those are true and neither is an answer.

“**You barrow well,**” she said, and it was not praise and not blame, and nobody thanked him for it.

“**I was shown,**” Aren Kest said.

“**The man who showed you did not watch you,**” she said, “**and did not tell you that you had done it well, and that is how he does it, and it is not against you.**”

He nodded and went back to the barrow, and the third hour to the ninth hour went the way barrowing goes, and the cost of the day was in his leg and in the bank uncut and in the thing seen and not said, and he paid it without a word, and nobody asked him to pay it and nobody told him not to, and the dust settled on his coat and stayed there, and the boy walked beside him on the returns without speaking, and that company was enough, and the channel ran below with the valley's water in it, and the work went on.

**What that day cost is a column, and it is his, and a fan uncut and a thing seen and not said are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a drench horn at twelve, a tallow candle at two, a girth strap at twenty-six, a cake of soap at nine, a feed scoop at twenty-two, a hank of twine at five, a shoeing hammer at twenty-nine, a whetstone at fourteen, a nail at nineteen, and a hand file at seventeen.** Twelve, two, twenty-six, nine, twenty-two, five, twenty-nine, fourteen, nineteen and seventeen is one hundred and fifty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and fifty-five pence is three marks and eleven over**, and one job refused and three reasons and four who can lay a bed and three of them named and a line of rubble about two hands thick and four times past the place and about nine barrows of spoil and three hundred paces wrong and about a hundred paces untouched and about four hundred paces of good new bed are ten figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the third hour and the fifth hour and the ninth hour and the bar sitting and about nine days of hands are five figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of thirty-two with a limp and a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water and a man of about forty-four who holds the second hoe and a carter of about nineteen and a man of about sixty-four on the second terrace and about nine men on a bank and a boy of about fifteen keeping the line and a woman of about thirty-eight with a basket and about four hundred people holding one true thing and about four hundred holding another are ten figures of persons and are not added to one another.** Nobody thanked him.
