# Chapter 0594

**Fallowmonth the twenty-eighth, YR 320, a Friday, a man of thirty-two with a limp on the low side of the channel from the third hour of the bar sitting to the ninth, about nine barrows of spoil, about four hundred people holding one true thing in a market town and about four hundred holding another, and a thing he is not asked to do.**

Aren Kest had refused one job out loud on this slope in three reasons and had not said no out loud to a second, and he did not decide either again, because neither was put to him again, and the morning laid out what was left, which was barrowing from the third hour to the ninth and being good at it when the valley needed something else.

The fourth hundred pace wanted hands. The man of about fifty-one who keeps the water had four who could lay the bed, and three were himself and the man of about forty-four who holds the second hoe and a carter of about nineteen, and the fourth was a man of about sixty-four on the second terrace whose hands would last about nine days, and about nine men in all were on the bank, and the top three hundred paces stood wrong above them and about a hundred paces between stood untouched, and about four hundred paces of good new bed held below.

He barrowed. The spoil came off the face in a steady line and he took it down the low side to the fan, one wheel and a shoulder, with the bad leg taking the turn of the barrow and not the lift of it, and the boy of about fifteen kept the line with him without being asked and without talking, and the two of them had a rhythm by the fourth hour and the boy matched it and neither of them had set it. Nobody watched them and nobody told him he had done it well, and the spoil lay on the low side in the sun and went hard enough to break under a heel by the sixth hour, which is the only measurement of a morning that the low side gives anybody.

On the second run down the low side the wheel found the wet ground lying under the spoil that had been there the day before, and the barrow went a hand's width off the line and stopped, and he had to get his weight over it and lift before it went further, and he did that, and the boy came back up for it without being called and they took the rest of that run in single loads, and it cost them about twenty minutes that nobody wrote down.

At the fifth hour the man of about fifty-one came down the high side and stood four hundred paces off and looked at the bed, and what he saw was a real and correct and necessary thing, which was that the low side wanted a second fan cut to take the spoil or the bank would slip under its own weight before the hundred paces were touched, and it was not a job anybody had asked for, and it was the difference the whole day turned on.

The place where the second fan wanted cutting was the one place on that low side where he was the only man in nine counties who knew anything. He could have put a finger on the face and shown the man of about fifty-one where the water would come out of it, and he could have said the two words that would have had it opened inside the week, and neither of those cost him anything to do, and he was asked for neither of them, and that was not the difficulty.

He said nothing about the fan. He barrowed past the place where it should be cut four times, and each time the line of rubble about two hands thick under the lip came back into his head unasked, and each time he put it down again, because to say it to the man of about fifty-one would have been to act on the load-bearing problem, and he may see it and he may not be the person who acts on it, and this valley is the last ground where that rule holds him. On the third of those four passes there was a stone in the face above that place with a fresh crack across it and grit come down under it where there had been none, and no man in nine counties could read that stone the way he read it, and the man of about fifty-one was off the face with his back to it at the time, and it was still there at the ninth hour.

About four hundred people in a market town four days and about nine off say he is the reason the rest of the record stands up, and about four hundred say he is the reason anybody should have believed any of it, and the two sets do not overlap, and he walks between them the way a man walks between two fields, and neither field is his.

At the ninth hour the woman of about thirty-eight came down off the high side with a basket of small stone for the top of the bed, and she saw where his eyes had gone four times, because she keeps a block and knows what a bank does under weight, and she said nothing about it, and he said nothing about it, and the fan was not cut that day, and the bank did not slip that day either, and both of those are true and neither is an answer. She set the basket down and turned the small stone out of it onto the bed course and looked at his leg the way a person looks at a thing they are not going to name, and then she looked at his face instead, and he had not been asked a question and answered none.

“**You barrow well,**” she said, and it was not praise and not blame, and nobody thanked him for it.

“**I was shown,**” Aren Kest said.

“**The man who showed you did not watch you,**” she said, “**and did not tell you that you had done it well, and that is how he does it, and it is not against you.**”

He nodded and went back to the barrow, and the third hour to the ninth hour went the way barrowing goes, and the cost of the day was in his leg and in the bank uncut and in the thing seen and not said, and he paid it without a word, and nobody asked him to pay it and nobody told him not to, and the dust settled on his coat and stayed there, and the boy walked beside him on the returns without speaking, and that company was enough, and the channel ran below with the valley's water in it, and the work went on. At the last run of the day he put the barrow down at the fan and stood a moment with his hand on the handle before he let go of it, which is a thing a man does when his leg has had the day, and the boy took the handles to empty it and was not thanked for that either.

**What that Friday cost is a column, and it is his, and a fan uncut and a thing seen and not said are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a drench horn at fourteen, a tallow candle at five, a girth strap at twenty-eight, a cake of soap at eleven, a feed scoop at twenty-four, a hank of twine at seven, a shoeing hammer at thirty-one, a whetstone at sixteen, a nail at twenty-one, and a hand file at nineteen.** Fourteen, five, twenty-eight, eleven, twenty-four, seven, thirty-one, sixteen, twenty-one and nineteen is one hundred and seventy-six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seventy-six pence is three marks and thirty-two over**, and one job refused and three reasons and four who can lay a bed and three of them named and a line of rubble about two hands thick and four times past the place and a second run and a hand's width and about nine barrows of spoil and three hundred paces wrong and about a hundred paces untouched and about four hundred paces of good new bed are twelve figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the third hour and the fourth hour and the fifth hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour and the bar sitting and about twenty minutes and about nine days of hands are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A man of thirty-two with a limp and a man of about fifty-one who keeps the water and a man of about forty-four who holds the second hoe and a carter of about nineteen and a man of about sixty-four on the second terrace and about nine men on a bank and a boy of about fifteen keeping the line and a woman of about thirty-eight with a basket and about four hundred people holding one true thing and about four hundred holding another are ten figures of persons, and this column does not add them, and two of them are still holding opposite things about the same man.** Nobody thanked him.
