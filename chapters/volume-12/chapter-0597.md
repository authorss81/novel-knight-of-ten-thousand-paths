# Chapter 0597

**The Low Gate at the eighth hour of the bar sitting, a bar of wood lying in the grass that nobody lifts, four questions on a board, a woman answering in her own words, and a man crossing out with a person and not with a leaf.**

The Low Gate stood a day's walk above the end of the made road, and the bar of wood lay in the grass at the foot of the lane where it had lain for years, and nobody lifted it and nobody was scheduled to lift it, and the woman of about fifty-five who keeps it stood by her board with its four questions, and the eighth hour came down the slope with dust in it.

Aren Kest came down the lane with his stick and his coat, four days and about nine of hard going from the nearest made road above a bar nobody in nine counties has been told he is above, and beside him walked the woman of about sixty-four who ties about nine hundred picking cords in a sitting, with her slate in her apron and nothing in her hands, and neither of them carried a leaf, and neither of them carried a certificate.

Going out is free and coming in is not. That is the whole of it, and nobody on that lane names it, and nobody says what it does to anything a man carries, and nobody says why a man can stand in two places, and the lane keeps those silences the way it keeps everything else.

The woman of about fifty-five looked at the two of them and then at the board, and the fourth of the four questions is not a gate question, and she asked the three that were hers to ask of anybody going down, and the woman of about sixty-four answered in the valley's own words, with nothing above the answer but the sky over the lane.

“**Will you speak for your ground and may your valley refuse you,**” the keeper of the gate said.

“**I will speak and it may,**” the woman of about sixty-four said. “**I keep nothing and I am on no roll and I count, and I told a man from off the hill so in my own mouth, and I did not find out until one morning that there was anything else in it. If my valley says no to me, that no stands, and I will carry it back up.**”

“**Who sends you,**” the keeper said.

“**Nobody sent me,**” the woman said. “**Four holders said she would rather not and it made no difference, and two sets said no and the money was found without them, and I am going down to say the same, that we will come to the gathering and we may be refused, and that our right to refuse stays explicit, and if that is not wanted we will go back up.**”

“**Pass,**” the keeper of the gate said, and she did not touch the bar, and the bar stayed in the grass, and the two of them went past it on the lane side, and no hand was laid on wood that day.

They went down together, the man of thirty-two with a limp and the woman of about sixty-four, and the lane dropped away below them with the valley mist coming up, and the stones of the made road showed far off where the walking ends, and neither looked back, because looking back is for people who are unsure, and they were not unsure, and the cost of the night was spoken by somebody other than him, and it was not a wound and it was not a leaf.

“**He will not remember this night,**” the woman of about fifty-five said to the lane after they had gone, “**and that is what it costs to go out with a person instead of a paper, that the going leaves nothing on him but the going.**”

Nobody thanked them for going. Nobody in nine counties had been told he was there, and nobody on that lane told anybody, and the lane did what it does, and the fruit of a day twenty-two winters gone stayed on no road, and a carter of about forty who went out in the swell sitting with two sacks of pears had still not come back in.

**What that hour cost is a column, and it is the woman's who keeps the gate, and a bar unlifted and four questions answered are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a leather headcollar at twenty-nine, a tallow dip at two, a stable bucket at twenty-four, a yeast cake at eight, a hoof oil at twenty, a rushlight at five, a rein strap at twenty-six, a tallow candle at fourteen, a cake of ink at eleven, a corn sieve at twenty-three, and a fleam at seventeen.** Twenty-nine, two, twenty-four, eight, twenty, five, twenty-six, fourteen, eleven, twenty-three and seventeen is one hundred and seventy-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seventy-nine pence is three marks and thirty-five over**, and a day's walk and four questions on a board and three that were hers to ask and about nine hundred cords and two sacks of pears and four holders and two sets that said no are seven figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the eighth hour and the bar sitting and four days and about nine of hard going and one morning and twenty-two winters gone and the swell sitting are six figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A woman of about fifty-five who keeps the gate and a woman of about sixty-four who ties the cords and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a carter of about forty out on a road are four figures of persons and are not added to one another.** Nobody thanked anybody.
