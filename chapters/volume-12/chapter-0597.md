# Chapter 0597

**Embermonth the eleventh, YR 320, a Thursday, the Low Gate at the eighth hour of the bar sitting, a bar of wood lying in the grass that nobody lifts, four questions on a board, a woman answering in her own words, and a man crossing out with a person and not with a leaf.**

The Low Gate stood a day's walk above the end of the made road, and the bar of wood lay in the grass at the foot of the lane where it had lain for years, and nobody lifted it and nobody was scheduled to lift it, and the woman of about fifty-five who keeps it stood by her board with its four questions, and the eighth hour came down the slope with dust in it.

Aren Kest came down the lane with his stick and his coat, four days and about nine of hard going from the nearest made road above a bar nobody in nine counties has been told he is above, and beside him walked the woman of about sixty-four who ties about nine hundred picking cords in a sitting, with her slate in her apron and nothing in her hands, and neither of them carried a leaf, and neither of them carried a certificate.

They went at the pace of the woman of about sixty-four, which was the pace of somebody who has been tying cords since she could reach a post, and he matched it, and his leg was the reason there was no faster version of that walk available to either of them. The lane came off the slope in three turns and lost about a mile of height doing it, and by the time they had the last of the turns the sun was behind the ridge above them and the dust they had brought down off the Long Set was settling on the backs of their coats. She carried her slate in her apron and her hands were otherwise empty, and she put a hand on the wall where the lane ran out on one side for a stretch of it, and he carried nothing at all, which is the part of a journey like this one that a man from off the hill finds hardest to get used to.

The woman of about fifty-five looked at the two of them and then at the board, and the fourth of the four questions is not a gate question, and she asked the three that were hers to ask of anybody going down, and the woman of about sixty-four answered in the valley's own words, with nothing above the answer but the sky over the lane.

“**Will you speak for your ground and may your valley refuse you,**” the keeper of the gate said.

“**I will speak and it may,**” the woman of about sixty-four said. “**I keep nothing and I am on no roll and I count, and I told a man from off the hill so in my own mouth, and I did not find out until one morning that there was anything else in it. If my valley says no to me, that no stands, and I will carry it back up.**”

“**Who sends you,**” the keeper said.

“**Nobody sent me,**” the woman said. “**Four holders said she would rather not and it made no difference, and two sets said no and the money was found without them, and I am going down to say the same, that we will come to the gathering and we may be refused, and that our right to refuse stays explicit, and if that is not wanted we will go back up.**”

“**Pass,**” the keeper of the gate said, and she did not touch the bar, and the bar stayed in the grass, and the two of them went on down the lane.

**No hand was laid on wood that day.**

The lane went on down for about a mile more with the valley mist coming up it behind them. Halfway down it passed the last house on that side, and a dog came out of the yard and went up the lane ahead of them and did not come back, and the woman of about sixty-four said good evening to the dog and not to the house, and the house said nothing. The lane narrowed where it went under the old pear trees and the ground came up on the right of it, and he could hear the channel on the left, a long way down and behind the bank, and he could not see it, and the woman of about sixty-four said it had been louder that week and would be louder again in a month, and said it the way she said the numbers of stones, which is to say without stopping to be asked.

Then the trees came out of the lane and the stones of the made road showed in the last of the light, grey and square and laid by somebody who will never know what they were laid for. The light had gone off the top of the ridge behind them while the bottom of the lane was already in the dark of the trees, and there was a line between the two of those that you could see and not step across, and he saw it and did not say that he had seen it. They stopped at the trough at the top of the made road because his leg wanted it and not for any other reason, and the woman of about sixty-four sat down on the wall beside it and did not ask him why they had stopped, and after a while she got up and went on, and he got up at his own speed and followed her. The mist came up the lane as far as where they were sitting and then stopped, the way it does, and neither of them said anything for the length of it. Then they went down onto the made road together, and the made road took their feet the way a made road does, and the valley went out of sight behind the ridge within about two hundred paces, and neither of them looked back, and neither of them said why.

“**He will not remember this night,**” the woman of about fifty-five said to the lane after they had gone, “**and that is what it costs to go out with a person instead of a paper, that the going leaves nothing on him but the going.**”

Nobody thanked them for going, and nobody in nine counties had been told he was there, and nobody on that lane told anybody where they had gone, and the lane did what it does. The fruit of a day twenty-two winters gone stayed on no road, and a carter of about forty who went out in the swell sitting with two sacks of pears had still not come back in, and a man of thirty-two with a limp walked out of that valley on a made road beside a woman of about sixty-four, and there was no arrangement anywhere in nine counties for either of them to be thanked for it.

**And the cost of that hour on the gate is a column and it is the woman of about fifty-five's, and a bar unlifted and four questions answered are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a leather headcollar at twenty-nine, a tallow dip at three, a stable bucket at twenty-four, a yeast cake at nine, a hoof oil at twenty, a rushlight at six, a rein strap at twenty-six, a tallow candle at fifteen, a cake of ink at twelve, a corn sieve at twenty-three, and a fleam at eighteen.** Twenty-nine, three, twenty-four, nine, twenty, six, twenty-six, fifteen, twelve, twenty-three and eighteen is one hundred and eighty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and eighty-five pence is three marks and forty-one over**, and a day's walk and four questions on a board and three that were hers to ask and about nine hundred cords and two sacks of pears and four holders and two sets that said no and three turns and about a mile and about a mile more and about two hundred paces are eleven figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the eighth hour and the bar sitting and four days and about nine of hard going and one morning and that week and a month and twenty-two winters gone and the swell sitting are eight figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **A woman of about fifty-five who keeps the gate and a woman of about sixty-four who ties the cords and a man of thirty-two with a limp and a carter of about forty out on a road are four figures of persons, and the second of them is the answer and the third of them is the man who went out beside her, and neither of those two is a keeper of anything.** Nobody thanked anybody.
