# Chapter 0569

**The twenty-sixth of Longlight, YR 320, a Thursday and a market day, from the third hour to the sixth, the end of a made road about nine yards short of a cross of two stones, a man of about thirty-one with a folded paper in the inside of his coat who had been standing there for two days, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who came round a corner of the metalling and found him.**

Going out was free. He had known it would be, since the sixth hour of the first sitting, and about four days and about nine of hard going confirmed it and cost him a blister on the heel of the left boot and nothing else at all, and the made road took him down the slope and out onto the flat and there is no gate on a made road.

**And the crossing is not a gate that lets anybody out and stops anybody coming in. It is a bar of wood lying in the grass at the foot of a lane, and what stands at it is a woman with a board and four questions, and a man who cannot answer the fourth one does not come in, and that is the whole of it, and it is not a rule anybody made and it is not a door and nobody can say why.**

The man of about thirty-one was standing where the metalling has been dug out on the valley's side for about nine yards and put back by somebody in a hurry, and he had been standing there since about the ninth hour two days before, and he had a fold of paper in the inside of his coat and he had not unfolded it in two days.

“**I have been waiting for you,**” he said, “**and I want to say the first thing before the paper, because I have thought about the order of the two and I have decided the first thing goes first. I asked her to be the answer. I asked her in that room in front of four people and I asked her in a way I am not proud of, and I said the thing about a person who holds ground and I said it in the voice, and she heard the voice, and I have been walking for two days since.**”

“**Then you have two days of it,**” Aren Kest said, “**and you are about to spend it on the paper, and I am going to let you, and then I am going to tell you about the paper.**”

The man of about thirty-one took the paper out and did not open it and held it in both hands the way a man holds a thing he has carried a long way.

“**There are four sets of people on that slope. If the water is put right, all four are asked to find it. I have done the sum on it with two other men and about four of the eleven terraces will be under water for about nine days while the work is done, and the four terraces are the four lowest, and there are nine households on the four lowest, and there are nine and not eight.**”

Nobody thanked him for the sum.

“**If the four refuse, the work does not happen and the water at the top of the Long Set goes on going out at the foot of the wall for another twenty-two years. If they agree, about nine households lose their ground for a season and the water is sound. That is the whole of it. There is no third figure and I have looked for one for about four days.**”

“**What does the paper do?**”

“**It is a statement that the cut is out of repair and that the water of the Long Set is not safe, and it goes to a county, and a county will pay for four miles of stone and will not send anybody, and the four sets find the money between them. There is no head on it for the county to put a name in. There is a line at the top of it that wants one.**”

“**And you want the man from off the hill to put his name in that line,**” he said.

“**Yes,**” the man of about thirty-one said. “**And I have thought about why, because I am not a fool, and it is because you are the only person anybody in nine counties has ever heard of who could put his name at the top of a thing about a road, and there is nobody else, and that is the whole of the reason and I have not dressed it up.**”

“**And you are one of the nine,**” Aren Kest said. “**I am told the second terrace is under the fill and I have walked past about eleven houses on it and one of them has a door open and a pot on the step.**”

“**It is my mother's,**” the man of about thirty-one said. “**And I have said that in front of you on purpose and I would like it written down if there is a place, and I am not going to ask you not to mend the channel because of it. If the channel is not mended my mother drinks that water for another twenty-two years and so do eight other houses, and if it is mended she is off her ground for a season and comes back to it, and both of those are true and one of them is a great deal worse than the other, and I would like somebody who is standing in a made road about nine yards short of a cross of two stones to tell me which one I am asking you to do.**”

Nobody argued with him. The wind came up the made road and went down it again.

“**No,**” Aren Kest said.

The man of about thirty-one did not put the paper away and did not unfold it.

“**Then say the reason, because I have walked two days and I am not going back without one, and I am not a child.**”

“**I am not going to say no because a name in a line is a name in a line,**” Aren Kest said, “**and anybody who tells you the reason is that I will not put my name on a piece of paper has told you a reason and not the reason. I have put my name at the top of a thing about a road once. I did it in a market and about four hundred people read it standing up and about four hundred of them think it was the reason anybody should have believed anything, and the two sets do not overlap and I have never said a word to either of them about which of them is right, and that is my own business and it is not yours.**”

The made road was quiet for about as long as a man takes to say nine.

“**The reason is that there is a woman of about thirty-eight at the top of that slope who asked to be the answer in front of four people about nine days ago, and nobody has told her whether she may, and I have not been told either, and I have not asked her, because asking her is a thing that has to be done by the people who own the question, and four days' walk from here there are four people who own it and one of them is a woman who has been waiting about nine days for somebody to say one word to her.**”

“**Then go and say the word,**” the man of about thirty-one said.

“**No,**” Aren Kest said, “**and that is the whole of the reason and there is not another one behind it, and I will tell you why I will not go and say it, and it is the worst thing I have said anywhere and I have said it in a lane before. Nine days before I came up your lane I stood in a lane behind a room in a market town and I told a woman of about thirty-eight that her place could not speak, and I told her in a lane on purpose, and I decided it in about nine minutes in a room with about nine people in it, and I did not ask her first, and I had the days to ask her and I used them on something else. That is the mistake. I have come four days and about nine to be somewhere where nobody in nine counties can find me and I have not learned one single thing about how to do it differently, and the only thing that has changed is that this time I know what the mistake is while I am making it.**”

Nobody thanked him and the man of about thirty-one said *that is fair* and then said the other half, and it is the last thing he said on the made road and he said it standing still with the paper in his hands.

“**I asked her to be the answer and then I walked two days to ask you for a paper, and I have not once asked her whether she wanted the paper. That is mine and not yours. I am going to go back up and I am going to be in that room again and I am not going to know what to say in it, and you are not going to be able to tell me, because you have just told me that telling people what to say is the whole of the thing that is wrong.**”

“**I am not going to be able to tell you,**” Aren Kest said, “**and I have been in a trade for eleven years in which being able to tell people what to do with a thing they own is the only thing I have ever been good at, and I have put it down.**”

The paper went back inside the coat and the man of about thirty-one went up the lane and did not look back and was gone by about the sixth hour, and the made road was empty, and nobody in that market town of about two thousand has been told that the man of about thirty-two with a limp was standing at the end of it at about the fourth hour on a Thursday and a market day, and two people in that valley have said that he was not in it and two have said that he was, and none of the four of them was at the end of that made road, and nobody has asked them.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man of about thirty-one's, and nine households on four terraces and four miles of stone and a line at the top of a paper that wants one name are not in it.**

**a strip of leather at four, a pot of tea at seven, a loaf at ten, a night's straw at fourteen, a stick of tar at seventeen, a bootlace at twenty-one, a bushel of bran at twenty-five, a kettle at twenty-nine, and a blanket at thirty-seven.** Four, seven, ten, fourteen, seventeen, twenty-one, twenty-five, twenty-nine and thirty-seven is one hundred and sixty-four pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and sixty-four pence is three marks and twenty over**, and two days of standing at the end of a made road and about nine yards of metalling dug out and put back in a hurry and four days and about nine of hard going and about eleven houses on the second terrace are five figures of distance and of time and of houses and none of them is a price, and nine households and four sets of people and four terraces are three figures of three different things and are not added to each other and none of them is a figure of persons in a made road about nine yards short of a cross of two stones, and two people who say he was there and two who say he was not are two halves of one afternoon and go in at neither end of this column.

He stood at the cross of two stones after that for about as long as a man takes to say four and then he turned and went up the lane, and the bar of wood was lying in the grass where it was, and it took him about a day and a half to come up it and he stood on the made road side of the bar at the fourth hour of the next sitting and nobody crossed it in his sight, and he was four seconds longer at the last step than he had been at the first, and that is a figure about a leg and about a made road and a bar of wood and is not a figure about a decision.
