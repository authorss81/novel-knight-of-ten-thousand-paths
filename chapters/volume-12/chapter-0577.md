# Chapter 0577

**The fifth of Harvestmonth, YR 320, a Monday and not a market day, from the second hour to the seventh, a made road out of a market town of about two thousand people running down toward the coast, a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane two hundred miles the other way, and nobody else on the road at any hour of it.**

She was sitting on a milestone with her boots off and her feet against the kerb and she had been sitting on it long enough that the dust had taken the shape of her, and he came round a bend at about the second hour and she did not get her boots on.

“**You are the man with the bad leg,**” she said. “**I have had a sheet about you since the second week of Greenmonth and it has never once said where you were, which I am aware is a fault in the sheet and not in you.**”

“**Yes.**”

“**I am not going to ask where you have been,**” she said. “**I keep a room with a door on a lane and about four hundred returns in a drawer about eleven inches deep and seven entries in a register and every one of the seven is a question and not one of them is a finding, and there is not a person alive who can tell me where anybody has been. If you tell me I will have to write it down and I have a rule about the fourth line and I am not going to break my own rule for you on a milestone.**”

“**Then do not ask,**” Aren Kest said, “**and I will not ask you anything that goes on a sheet either.**”

“**That is a fair trade and I would like it said that you offered it before I did.**”

Nobody thanked anybody.

They walked. The road was made for the whole of that stretch and there was nothing on it but the two of them and a cart that went by at about the fourth hour and did not stop, and for about an hour there was nobody else on it at all.

She said her name when he had not asked for it, and he said his, and neither of them gave the other anything else, and **neither of them said one word about the four years and a half, and there is no room on either side of that road with a third person standing in it, and nothing was repaired on a milestone and nothing was asked to be.**

At about the third hour she asked him what a leaf with four heads on it and nothing under the fourth was, and he told her, and he told her the whole of it in about nine sentences, and the shape of what he said was a leaf about nine inches by six with three of the four filled and the fourth ruled and empty and a question under the fourth written by a person and not by an office, and nobody anywhere had filled the fourth.

She thought about that for the length of about nine paces.

“**Then it is not a document,**” she said.

“**It is a leaf.**”

“**It is a leaf with a hole cut in it that every person who comes after you can put something in,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**I have four hundred of those in a drawer eleven inches deep and I could take any one of them out and write in the fourth line whatever anybody wanted written in it, and I have not done it in four years and a half, and the reason I have not done it is not honesty. It is that the moment one of those lines has a thing in it that somebody is relying on, the drawer is not a drawer any more, it is a place where somebody can be found.**”

She said that at about the fourth hour, at the side of a made road, to a man with a stick, and she said it in the voice of a person reading out an entry.

“**What did you say to me at the gate?**” she said then, and he told her about the four questions and about the fourth of them not being about a lane, and about a bar of wood lying in the grass for about thirty years, and about a woman of about thirty-eight who had answered the fourth one on her own account and had not been told she may.

“**And what did you say to that?**”

“**I said nothing, and I have said nothing to about nine people since, and I was asked twice and I answered both times that I did not know.**”

“**Good,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**Not because you were right. Because a man who is the only person anybody has heard of who could put his name at the top of a thing about a road is the man every frightened room wants, and you have been refusing that for about four months and it has cost you a place to sleep and about four hundred people who know where you sleep.**”

“**You have read about that.**”

“**I have read a sheet,**” she said, “**and a sheet is not a finding, and I wrote most of the sheet, and the parts of it that are true are the parts about four counties and about a form and not one word about the man.**”

Nobody thanked her.

Then she stopped walking, and stood on the edge of the made road with her back to it, and said the sentence.

“**A beautiful place is not a free place.**”

He did not say anything. She had not said it to him.

“**That valley is the most beautiful ground I have stood on in about eleven years,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said, “**and it is beautiful for the reason it is not free, and those are one thing and not two things, and I want to be careful here because I have said a version of this before to about nine people and every one of the nine made it into a speech about fences. It is not about fences. It is that a place where nobody has been able to say what they think for about twenty-two years is not peaceful because the argument was settled. It is peaceful because the argument cannot be had. And the peace is the holding and the holding is the beauty and they come apart the moment you ask a person what they think.**”

“**And what happens when you ask them?**”

“**About nine households of the low ground find out that the thing they were peaceable about was a holding,**” she said, “**and they did not have a vote in it and they are not the ones who were wrong, and that is the whole of the arithmetic and it is not a speech about anything.**”

Nobody argued with her. He had nothing to answer, and he has said so to himself about nine times since, and about nine minutes is the whole of what he says about it.

Then she said the other half, which is why she came up the road.

“**I am not going to write that down for you.**”

“**I did not ask you to.**”

“**I know you did not, and I am saying it in advance because you will want it written,**” the woman of about twenty-nine said. “**You will want a page with the finding on it in a hand, and I am the nearest hand to you for two hundred miles and nine counties, and if I write it you will carry it up a lane four days and about nine and put it in front of about nine people, and then it is a document, and then it is a thing about four heads with something in the fourth, and then whoever is standing nearest to that place is whoever is holding it. That is how every one of them started. That is what happened to mine.**”

He put his left hand flat on a gatepost at the side of the road and the post was cold and then was not, and nobody was within nine paces of him and nobody has ever been told about it.

“**Then I go up with nothing,**” he said.

“**You go up with a sentence that is not yours,**” she said, “**which is worth more than a page and is worth exactly nothing you can hand anybody, and if you ever find yourself explaining that sentence to a room then it has already stopped being the thing I said.**”

Nobody thanked anybody.

**And the cost of that hour is a column and it is his, and four days and about nine of hard going is at neither end of it, and neither has a price that anybody can put on a page.**

**a roll of twine at two, a pot of tea at seven, a slab of cheese at eleven, a whetstone at eighteen, a leathern strap at twenty, a pot of tar at twenty-three, a night's straw at twenty-six, a bundle of faggots at twenty-nine, and a hurdle at thirty-two.** Two, seven, eleven, eighteen, twenty, twenty-three, twenty-six, twenty-nine and thirty-two is one hundred and sixty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and sixty-eight pence is three marks and twenty-four over**, and four hundred returns and seven entries and nine entries in a day-book and fourpence a year and a deed of thirteen words and a drawer eleven inches deep and nine households and four years and a half and four months and two hundred miles and eleven years and thirty years and nine counties are thirteen figures of thirteen different things and not one of them is a price and none of them goes in at either end of this column. **A leaf about nine inches by six with four heads on it is not a form and is not a division and is not the fifth document, and the fourth head of the Crown's form is ruled and empty and about six months empty, and a sentence said out loud on a made road is not a document and is not in any drawer and cannot be produced.**

They parted at about the seventh hour where the road turns and the made part stops being made, and she went down the road toward the coast and he went up the road toward a market town and then up a lane, and he got up the first bank in about four seconds longer than it takes to sit down on one, and there was nobody with him to measure it and he did not write it down.
