# Chapter 0574

**The head of the Long Set, the eighth hour of the blossom sitting, about eleven people tying cords under the oldest tree, a ring of stones set in an arc on the side of it that faces down the slope, and two women who have never once asked each other a question.**

She worked it out while she was tying cords, which is the way she works out everything, and she worked it out in about the time it takes to tie forty of them badly.

The digging was set for the eighth hour of a working sitting and the sitting had not come round to it, and that was the reason she was at the tree with a basket and not a reason anybody had asked her for.

A number that has not been a different number once in thirty years is not a count of anything. Counts change. Stone goes into the ground and stone comes out of it and a thing that a person has walked round three hundred and sixty times a year for thirty years is not going to come to the same figure twice, and if it has, then nobody has been looking.

She had thought about it for two days and she had not said it to anybody, because a woman who has been in a valley about nine days and starts saying out loud that a thing everybody has relied on is not a thing has to be able to finish the sentence, and she was not able to finish it.

Then the eighth hour came and she went up under the tree with a basket because that is where she goes at the eighth hour about four days in nine, and the woman of about sixty-four was walking the arc.

She asked her what she was counting.

“**What am I counting?**”

“**You have been round that arc about nine hundred times a year for thirty years,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said, “**and you do not have to answer me and I am asking anyway.**”

Nobody thanked her. The woman of about sixty-four stood at the north end of the arc where the bigger stones are and looked at her for about as long as a man takes to say four, and then she took her hand out of her apron and showed her the slate, and it was a slate about the length of a hand with a number cut into the top of it and about four smaller numbers under it that were not in the same hand.

“**I am not counting,**” the woman of about sixty-four said. “**I have not counted since the first year. I say the next number out loud and I put my hand on the next stone and I go round the arc, and it has taken about as long as a woman takes to say sixty and it has been the end of my day since I was about thirty-four.**”

“**That is not the same thing as counting.**”

“**No,**” she said. “**Counting is what you do when you want to know how many there are. That is what I do when I want to know whether the day is done. And you are the first person in about thirty years to work out the difference and I would like it noticed that you worked it out on your own and did not ask me first.**”

“**Where did the number come from?**”

“**The woman of about seventy told me it in my first year and she told me two things with it and I have done both of them for thirty years. She said the number was what it was and that I was to say it, and she said that if anybody ever came up here and asked to count them for themselves, I was to say the number and not let them.**”

“**And has anybody ever asked?**”

“**Two men have asked me and only two of them. One was a boy of about fifteen and he asked me in my first year and the other was the man of about sixty and he asked me in about the eleventh,**” the woman of about sixty-four said, “**and you are the third and I have never told either of them that I do not count, and I am telling you because you did not ask to count them. You asked what I was counting. That is a different question and it is the only one anybody has ever asked me.**”

Nobody thanked her.

The woman of about thirty-eight put her basket down and stood in the arc, which is about two paces wide, and looked at about sixty stones going down the slope, four of them in gaps where the ground had gone and been made good twice.

“**What is the number a number of?**”

“**I do not know,**” the woman of about sixty-four said, “**and I have had thirty years to want to know and I have never once wanted it enough to ask, because a woman who has asked gets an answer and then she has to do something with the answer and I have about nine hundred cords in this sitting and about a hundred and forty of them are mine and I am paid in fruit and not in money and the fruit they pay me in is the worst of it, and I could not do a day's work with an answer I had not asked for.**”

Nobody argued with her. The woman of about thirty-eight said the only thing she was going to say about it, and she said it standing in the arc.

“**Look at them. Once.**”

“**No.**”

“**You have said the number out loud about nine hundred times a year for thirty years and you have never once put your eye on the stones while you said it.**”

“**No,**” the woman of about sixty-four said, and then she said the rest of it. “**And if I do, then it will not be the number, and there will be about nine people on this slope who have never once wondered what the number was and who will go up here in the next four years because somebody has started, and the number I say will be a different number every year after that. I have been the reason a day ends for a woman of sixty-four on this slope and I am not going to be the reason a day does not end.**”

The woman of about thirty-eight did not say anything to that.

Then the woman of about sixty-four went round the arc anyway, with her hand off the stones and her slate in her other hand, and it took her about as long as a woman takes to say sixty, and eleven people tying cords at that tree did not stop and did not look up.

When she came back to the north end she said one sentence.

“**It is not the number.**”

“**What is it?**”

“**I am not going to tell you,**” the woman of about sixty-four said, “**and that is not because of you. I am sixty-four and I have about nine hundred cords to tie in this sitting and I am going to tie them, and a number of stones is not a thing that can be got back once it has been looked at.**”

Nobody thanked her. Nobody thanked the other one.

**And the woman of about thirty-eight did not tell anybody, and about nine people were on that slope that hour and none of them knows, and about four people in this valley have said that a man of thirty-two with a limp went down the lane on purpose and nobody has said that nobody saw him go.**

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and the column is the woman of about sixty-four's, and a number of stones is not in it and a slate is not in it and neither of them can be given a price by anybody.**

**a slip of whalebone at three, a thimble at seven, a sheet of oiled paper at nine, a palm of twine at eleven, a cake of wax at fourteen, a hobnail at seventeen, a pint of vinegar at thirty, and a hoop at thirty-nine.** Three, seven, nine, eleven, fourteen, seventeen, thirty and thirty-nine is one hundred and thirty pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and thirty pence is two marks and thirty-four over**, and sixty stones and four gaps where the ground has gone and been made good twice and thirty years and nine hundred cords in a sitting and a hundred and forty of them hers and eleven people tying cords are six figures of six different things and none of them is a price, and nine days and nine days back are two figures of the same figure of time and neither of them is a price, and a fifth sitting is not a month and no month has been said out loud on that slope and neither of them goes in at either end of this column. **A division on a board in a valley with no office in it is a division and is not a head, and a head and a division are not the same shape and are not the same object, and the fourth of the four on that board is ruled and empty and nobody has filled it and nobody in this valley can.**

The cords were done at about the ninth hour and the two of them carried them down together and did not speak on the way, and the slate went back into the apron and has gone back into that apron every eighth hour since, and about nine hundred cords in a sitting is what that slope runs on and about a hundred and forty of them are hers and that is not in any column.
