# Chapter 0587

**The four lowest terraces of the Long Set, the fifth hour of the pick sitting, water coming down a channel about two paces wide and standing in the first line of fruit trees, about nine households on four terraces who were told on the day before, a woman of about thirty-one with a stick and a line of stones, and a boy of about fifteen who rings the bell and is paid a penny and has not been asked what the bell is for.**

They were told the day before and not after it, and the telling was done by a woman of about thirty-one who is not a keeper of anything anybody can put a name to, and nobody thanked her for it and she did not expect to be and has not said that she did.

What she did was go up the row to about nine households at the fifth hour of the day before and say the same thing at each door, and what she said is on a slate because the man of about thirty-one of the Standing wrote it there at her asking and would not write it from memory and would not carry her round himself.

Tomorrow the water comes down.

Nobody argued with her at any of the nine doors. That is the part about the four lowest terraces that nobody above them has understood in twenty-two years. Four of the nine said nothing. Four of the nine said that it would not happen. One said that it had happened before and did not last, and the woman of about thirty-one said that it had happened once and that the ditch was cut that year by six people and that it is being cut this year by one, and the one said nothing to that.

Nobody thanked her. About four people on the Long Set have taken the view that a woman who walks nine doors with a slate in her hand is doing the work of an office, and about nine have taken the view that nine households on four terraces have no office to walk nine doors for and never had, and the two sets do not overlap and she has not been asked which of the two she thinks she is.

**And the nine days was counted.**

Nobody in nine counties had ever checked it. It was a figure two men had. The man of about fifty-one who keeps the water had it from watching the channel himself for nine years and the carter had it from coming up and down it in a cart, and neither of them had counted and the woman of about thirty-one told the man of about thirty-one that a figure two men had is not a number and that she wanted it counted by a person who was going to be standing in it.

So it was counted at the fifth hour on the morning it happened, by her, with a stick and about nine stones put down the high side of the channel at the water's pace, and the count is a count and it is on the slate and it is the first number about this water that anybody in this valley has ever arrived at themselves.

**Nine days and a morning.** That is what two men said and that is what the count came to, and the man who keeps the water read it off the slate and said that two men who had not counted were right, and that he had expected them to be wrong, and that being right by accident is not a thing to be glad about and is not a thing to be sorry about either.

“**Write the morning on there,**” the woman of about thirty-one said, “**because in about nine days it will be ten days and in about nine days somebody will want to know whether the two men were right and the answer will be that they were right to a morning.**”

Nobody wrote anything else. He wrote the morning because she asked him to and he has not understood why he did it and has not been asked.

The water came down at about the seventh hour and stood in the first line of trees by about the eighth, and it was not a flood and it did not carry anything away. It did the thing water does when it has been let out at the foot of a wall and put back in at the head of it twenty-two years ago and has been finding the low ground for nine years without anybody mending the bed it runs on. It spread. It went from the first terrace to the second in about a day and a half and to the third in three and to the fourth in five, and the fourth is the one with a house on it.

The bell went at the four points. The boy of about fifteen rang it the way he rings it, and he was paid his penny, and there are four points and a penny and nobody in this valley has ever said which of the two the penny is for, and he has been asked why he rang it once and said the hour was the hour, and nobody has asked him again.

**The fruit is the loss and the water is only the reason for the loss.** The four lowest terraces carry about a fifth of what the Long Set will pick this year and it is the earliest fruit on the slope because it is the lowest and the warmest, and fruit that stands in nine days of water does not go on a cart. It goes soft on the tree and then it goes down, and a cart cannot get to the bottom of this slope in the wet anyway, and the woman of about thirty-one has said out loud that there is no road in nine counties that this fruit is on and that this fruit is not going to be on one, and that the difference between losing a thing and losing a thing that can be sold is the whole of what has happened to her this month.

Nobody argued with her. The man of about thirty-one of the Standing said that the settlement could take the fruit, and she said that the settlement had said no to four miles of stone on the grounds that a record read by four people is not a record the settlement can fund, and that she was not blaming the settlement and that she had said yes on the same slate, and he said he was not offering charity and she said she knew he was not and that the offer was made anyway.

**And about nine households on four terraces are under water and none of them read the plank and none of them chose the oath and none of them is the man with a limp, and the man who read half of it out loud is four hundred paces above them laying stone the wrong way up with about nine people, and the woman of about thirty-eight who signed the finding is two terraces above the water line tying cords, and neither of them has come down.**

**Nine days and a morning. The woman of about thirty-one put about nine stones in a line and counted the water and it is on a slate and it is the only number about this water that anybody in this valley got for themselves, and it is nine days and a morning, and the man of about fifty-one wrote the morning on it because a woman asked him to and he has not been thanked and is not going to be.**

**What nine days of no water costs is a column, and it is the woman of about thirty-one's, and a fifth of a pick and nine days and a morning and four terraces are not in it and not one of them has a price.**

**a pail at three, a coil of rope at seven, a bucket at ten, a sieve at thirteen, a hand file at sixteen, a nail at twenty, a jar of oil at twenty-three, a mat at twenty-six, a cake of tallow at twenty-nine, a night's straw at thirty-five, and a whetstone at forty-one.** Three, seven, ten, thirteen, sixteen, twenty, twenty-three, twenty-six, twenty-nine, thirty-five and forty-one is two hundred and twenty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and twenty-three pence is four marks and thirty-one over**, and four terraces and about nine households and the first line of trees and about a fifth of a pick and nine stones and about nine yards of lip and about four paces are seven figures of count and of distance and none of them is a price, and the fifth hour and the seventh hour and the eighth hour and the day before and nine days and a morning and a day and a half and three days and five days and about nine years and about six weeks and twenty-two years are twelve figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **Four of the nine said nothing and four of the nine said it would not happen and one said it had happened before and the boy of about fifteen and two men and about ninety people twenty-two winters ago and three names on a slate are figures of persons and are not added to one another, and the penny was paid and is not in this column because it was paid and it was not a cost of that morning.** Nine days of a season that nobody has got back. Nobody thanked anybody.
