# Chapter 0581

**The head of the Long Set, the second hour of the swell sitting, a plank of green wood lying flat side up in the light a hundred yards up the slope, three people who have seen what is under a lip, a slate, a stick of chalk, and a boy of about fifteen who is not going to be paid for this.**

The first trouble is not the writing. It is the three.

A finding about a road in this valley has to be made by three people who can each be found afterwards and told they are wrong. Not a room and not an office. Three people, and every one of them within a day's walk of this slope when somebody comes to say the finding is no good.

The man of about fifty-one who keeps the water at the top of that Long Set can be found. The woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a block between two head stones can be found. The third is the boy, and the third being the boy is not a detail to be got round. He is the only other person who was on that lip.

He was there because he barrows stone. He is there most working days. On the sixth hour of the set sitting he stood about four paces off and looked where he was told to look, which is a thing boys do on a channel bank, and what he saw he has not said out loud to anybody and did not know was worth saying until seven days ago, when a man read half a piece of green wood aloud under the oldest tree.

Three is three. Nobody has tried to make it four, because four names would need four people who saw the thing, and the only fourth person available saw it afterwards.

“**Is this the same as the bell,**” the boy said. He had the slate under his arm and the chalk in his hand, and the chalk is not his and has never been paid for. “**Because the bell is a penny and this is a different thing, and I want to know which one it is before I put my name on it.**”

“**It is not the bell,**” the man of about fifty-one said. “**The bell is a penny. This is nothing, and you are not going to be paid for it, and I am not going to pay you for it out of my own hand either, because then it is a wage and a wage can be stopped.**”

“**Then I will do it for nothing,**” the boy said, “**and I want it written that I said that before I did it and not after.**”

Nobody wrote that down either. The woman of about thirty-eight wrote it down.

That is how the thing came to have a second line on it, and neither of the two of them decided it should. She writes on a slate because there is no foolscap in this valley and has not been for about twenty-two years, and she writes with a stick of chalk because ink is a thing a house has and her house has not had one in four years. It is a bad surface for it. It is the only surface in this valley that three people can be found on.

She wrote the first line before she sat down on the grass.

It is the shape the board off the middle terrace has, and the shape a leaf of foolscap has, and the shape a man in a market town has ruled on a sheet of paper, and she has never once been told that she may write a line in any of them. She has kept a block of ground for nine years and has been asked about the ground and about the fruit on it and about a graft she did in one year without asking, which took, and the Standing told her she had done it wrongly. Nobody has ever said to her that she may speak for a road. She asked to be, in a market town four days and about nine off. She has asked twice since, in the open, and the second asking was about whether anybody would let a person dig at the foot of the oldest tree, and she was let, and she has not been told she may.

Then she was signing her name to a finding about four miles of his water.

“**Stop,**” she said.

The man of about fifty-one looked up. He has been waiting since the sixth hour of the set sitting for a person to write this down and he had not once imagined that the person who offered would stop.

“**I have just noticed something and I am going to say it out loud, and I would like it noticed that I noticed it and not that somebody pointed at it.**”

Nobody interrupted her. There were about nine people on that grass and it is a working morning and the fruit on that Long Set is about six weeks off being picked, and about nine of them stopped what they were doing, which is not a thing that happens here.

“**I have never been told that I may speak for a road,**” she said. “**Not in a room and not here and not in the open at the ninth hour. I have asked twice. The second time I asked about a hole in the ground and I was let dig it, and being let is not the same as being told, and I worked that out on my own in a market four days and about nine off and I said it there and nobody has written it down since.**”

“**That is true,**” he said.

“**I know it is true. I am not asking for it. I am asking how I come to be putting my name on a finding about your water.**”

He put the chalk down on the grass. He has carried a fault for about nine years and he has said it out loud twice in three weeks, once to nobody in particular and once to nine people, and he has never once been thanked for either and is not going to be.

“**Because it is not a voice,**” he said. “**That is the whole of it and I have thought about it for about four days. A voice is what comes out of a person and a person can be told she is wrong and can go home. A finding is three names against a thing on a channel bank. It does not say what anybody thinks. It says three people stood here and here is what was under it, and you may come and put your hand on the stone and tell the three of us we are lying, and we will put that down too.**”

“**Then it is three people being counted,**” she said.

“**It is three people being counted,**” he said, “**and I know what that is and I know it is not the same as being told, and I would not have asked you if there had been anybody else on that bank. I want that said in whatever order it wants saying in.**”

Nobody thanked either of them for it. The boy moved the slate round so it was the right way up for a person who is left-handed, which the woman of about thirty-eight is, and did not say that he had noticed, and she did not say that she had seen him do it.

Then it came out in the oldest voice in that valley, and she heard it come out.

“**I will do it,**” she said, in a voice four people on that grass had heard a hundred times at a table, “**because it is asked of me and because it is the right thing and because this valley does not write things down and if we do not then it is gone again for another twenty-two years.**”

Nobody answered that. The man of about fifty-one has the water at the top of that Long Set and has not walked four miles of anything in about nine years and knows what is under the fourth hundred pace, and he heard her, and what he said next is not on the slate.

“**That was the voice,**” he said. “**You know it was.**”

“**I know it was,**” she said, “**and I am going to write my name under it anyway, and I would like the three of us to be clear that I have heard it and am writing regardless. I have been hearing it since I was small. It is not going to stop and the finding is not going to wait for it.**”

Nobody thanked her for that either, and about four people on that grass have put it about, and what they put it about is that she said the truest thing anybody has said in this valley in twenty-two years, and nine have put it about that she said a formula she was given at a table and dressed it up, and the two sets do not overlap and nobody asked her which of the two she meant.

Then the three of them wrote it, and it took most of the rest of the hour, and the shape of it is four lines and the valley's own order, which is a person first and then where it is and then what it is and then what goes on it.

The man of about fifty-one dictated the middle of it and she wrote it and the boy held the chalk and rubbed out the two words she got wrong, which were the two words that mattered, and they are right.

At the fourth hundred pace from the top of the cut, the lip on the high side carries for about nine yards on a bed that has gone. Under the lip is a line of rubble about two hands thick.

And under that, in the fourth line, in the same three hands and in chalk, is the thing the record says and nobody had said out loud until a man read half a plank aloud under a tree: the water was let out at the foot of a wall and put back in at the head of it, on the day the strip of ground at the foot of this slope was let go, and about ninety people went out on it, and four sets of them argued about it for nine days.

Nobody put a name at the top of it. That was the fourth thing they settled and it took longest, and the boy asked about it and was told that a name at the top of a thing is a thing somebody can be pointed at, and he said that a slate with three names under a thing is also a thing somebody can be pointed at, and nobody had an answer to that and he has not asked again.

**And it is a finding and not a form. It is a slate and not a leaf, and it is not the board off the middle terrace, and it is not the foolscap with four heads on it, and it is not the Crown's form, and the fourth head of the Crown's form is ruled and empty about four counties off and has been about six months, and there is no office in nine counties that can fill it and nobody in this valley has tried. A division on a board in a valley with no office in it is a division and is not a head and is not a fifth line. The five documents are in five places and none of them is the Crown's form, and a mark cut into a plank is a sixth object and is not any of them, and neither is a line of chalk on a slate.**

Nobody has been given the finding. The man of about fifty-one has it under his arm and has not carried it to anybody, and the four sets of people who would have to find four miles of stone between them have not been asked and two of them have not heard of it, and about nine households on the four lowest terraces of that Long Set have not been asked anything and are the people who will pay for it and are not the people who were wrong.

**What that morning cost is a column, and it is the boy's, and a slate and a line of chalk and three names are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a stick of chalk at two, a whetstone at five, a hank of twine at seven, a tallow candle at nine, a cake of ink at ten, a goose quill at twelve, a mallet at fifteen, a slate at eighteen, a drawing knife at twenty-one, an iron hoop at twenty-five, and a wooden rule at thirty-three.** Two, five, seven, nine, ten, twelve, fifteen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-five and thirty-three is one hundred and fifty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and fifty-seven pence is three marks and thirteen over**, and the fourth hundred pace and about nine yards of lip and about two hands of rubble and a hundred yards of grass and eleven terraces and about six weeks are six figures of distance and of time and of count and none of them is a price, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour and four days and twenty-two years and about nine days are six more figures of time and none of them goes in at either end of this column. **Three names on a slate and the boy who is one of them and nine people on that grass and ninety people twenty-two winters ago are four figures of persons and not one of them, and a penny for a bell was paid and is not in this column because it was not a cost of that morning.** The three who signed it can each be found. Nobody thanked anybody.
