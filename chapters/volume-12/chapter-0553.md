# Chapter 0553

**A market went on in the street of a market town of about two thousand people on the second of Greenmonth, YR 320, a Saturday and a market day, from the first hour to the eighth, and about eleven people who had come up a made road put their hands on other people's carts in it for about nine hours because nobody had asked them to and nobody had told them not to, and the man of about fifty-three who brought the letter told them to, and the man of about twenty-nine with a handcart did not say anything about it and his yard was a quarter slower than it was and he did not mind.**

He said the thing about his yard out loud at about the sixth hour to nobody, and the man of about twenty-nine with a handcart has counted about nine hundred carts down one street on a market morning for about nine years and about five hundred and seventy-one on another, and what he said was that eleven strangers on his yard had cost him about a quarter of a morning's carts and that he could tell which four of the eleven were carrying wrong and had been told so once each and had not been told twice, and that this was the only figure in that market anybody could have checked by standing in the same place on another Saturday.

Nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody asked him to say it again and he said it once.

Then the woman of about forty-four who sells fish asked one of the eleven where the fruit was, and she asked it the way she asks everybody, which is by standing there, and the one she asked was a woman of about thirty-one who has been in the valley and can count.

**And the answer was the first wrong thing anybody in that market had heard about that valley, and it was wrong the way a true thing is wrong when it is the only thing anybody knows.**

“**It keeps,**” the woman of about thirty-one said. “**A fruit out of that valley will keep until the next picking and into the one after that if you put it in a cold room, and I have eaten an apple in this market town in Frostmonth that was cut off a tree in a different year. Nobody there throws any away. That is the thing about it. That is the whole of the thing anybody could tell you and it took me about four days to believe it and I have not got over it.**”

About half the people in that market heard the words *nobody there throws any away* and repeated them to somebody else before the hour was out, and nobody in that market could afterwards have said which half it had been, and no count of it went into any column in this market town and it is not a figure of persons.

**And the man of about fifty-eight with a chain of office that is a badge said out loud what it is, in about nine words, standing at the board outside the weigh-house where the sheet went up in Mudmonth, and he did not say it to anybody and nobody thanked him for it.**

“**A place where nothing is ever thrown away is a place where nothing has ever been decided.**”

Nobody agreed with him out loud and nobody argued with him out loud and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key said that it was the nicest thing anybody in that room had said in five months and that it was also a thing about a fruit and not about a place, and the man of about fifty-eight said that it was about both and that he was not going to improve it.

Then the man of about twenty-nine with a handcart asked a question that was not his question and that turned out to be the question.

He asked the woman of about thirty-one whether the road was made. She said no. He asked what there was instead, and she said there was four miles of it, made of stone, about two paces wide, with a lip on the high side, and that it took the water off the top of the slope and down through the terraces and that it was built about a hundred years ago and that it was the only thing anybody in that valley had ever had to mend on a day they chose.

“**Then it wants mending,**” the man of about twenty-nine said. “**Everything wants mending. I have a lane out past the common that goes under water twice a year and I mend it twice a year and I have never once asked anybody to help me and nobody has ever offered.**”

**And the woman of about thirty-one stopped, and put the basket down, and said the sentence that the rest of this is built out of, and she said it to a man with a handcart and not to anybody else in that market.**

“**It has not been mended since before I was born. And I will tell you why, and it is the true reason and I have never said it out loud anywhere and I have been in nine counties and I am saying it in a street. Nobody in that valley is permitted to say out loud why that channel wants mending. There is a thing that was done there about twenty-two years ago and it was done on purpose and it was agreed to by people who are dead, and the way it was done means that the record of it is not allowed to be read, and if you cannot read the record then you cannot say the channel wants mending, because the reason it wants mending is in the record, and there are people there who will tell you that it wants mending and they will say it very quietly and then they will stop talking.**”

**Nobody thanked her and nobody made her a witness and she is not a witness and she is not a symbol and she is not a subject and she went back to her basket and picked it up and carried it into the market and nobody in that street stopped her.**

**There was one of the eleven who did not lift anything all morning, and people in that market noticed it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet noticed it and has said nothing about it since and nobody has asked him and nobody is going to.**

She is a woman of about forty-five and she is the only one of the eleven who is not from this county or from the six counties about it, and she stood at the end of the weigh-house board with her hands behind her back and looked at the street the way a person looks at a street they may have to live in. She was not working because nobody had told her to and she had not asked to be, and about half an hour after the market opened she asked the man of about twenty-nine with a handcart one question, which was how many people there were in the town.

“**About two thousand,**” he said. “**Count them off that wall yourself at the eleventh hour if you want a figure of your own and it will come out nearer nineteen hundred and nobody in this town will thank you for saying so.**”

She looked at the wall for about as long as a woman takes to say four, and then said a thing that was not a question and that she did not say to anybody in particular, and it was that in three days she had stood in three other places where people had told her a number, and had not one of the three numbers been the same kind of number as either of the other two.

**Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her what she meant by it. The man of about fifty-eight with a chain of office that is a badge heard it and did not ask her what she meant by it and said out loud afterwards that he was not going to ask, and gave a reason, and the reason was that a man who asks a stranger what a number means has begun keeping a book of numbers instead of a book of answers, and about four of the nine people standing round that board had it that this was caution and about five had it that it was the same as being afraid, and the two sets do not overlap and nobody voted on which it was.**

And the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel asked the woman of about thirty-one a single question about what she had said, which was whether she had said it twice anywhere, and she said she had not, and he said that was the correct condition for having said it and wrote none of it down.

**And the man of about fifty-eight said out loud at about the eighth hour, standing at the board with a wet thumb on his coat, that he was going to write it on a leaf with no head over it and rule nothing over it and read none of it back, and that he had written four leaves with no head over them in five months and that this one would be a fifth, and that nobody was to be allowed to say the word fifth out loud in that street.**

Nobody said it out loud.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine with a handcart's, and it is the first one he has ever kept, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month come into a room two hundred miles off and that is a figure of rolls and is not a figure of carts and is not in this column.**

**a string of twine at one, a whetstone at two, a second whetstone at three, a bushel of bran at five, a pot of tea at six, a hoop of osier at eight, a night's straw at eleven, a pound of tallow at fifteen, a sheet of foolscap at seven, and a cake of ink at twelve.** One, two, three, five, six, eight, eleven, fifteen, Seven and Twelve is seventy pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and seventy pence is one mark and twenty-two over**, and about nine hundred carts on one market morning and about five hundred and seventy-one on another are two figures of carts and neither of them is a price and neither of them is in this column and the eleven is a figure of persons and is not in this column and is not added to anything, and four miles of stone channel is a figure of distance and is not a figure of persons and is not in it, and the twenty-two years is a figure of time, and the about four hundred who read a sheet in this market in Mudmonth are a figure of persons and are not the about four hundred at the end of a made lane four days the other way and go in at neither end of this column.

The market went on. A woman of about sixty-three behind a pan called her price out twice and did not say it a third time, and about nine people read four ruled lines on a board outside a weigh-house in that street on that morning and every one of the nine had read it before, and nobody said so and nobody was asked.
