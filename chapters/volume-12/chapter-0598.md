# Chapter 0598

**Embermonth the sixteenth, YR 320, a Tuesday, the middle terrace at the third hour of the bar sitting, a delegate chosen without a vote, a written refusal of the right to be thanked, a document about ground where every argument was once put under a tree, and nobody angry.**

The middle terrace lay quiet at the third hour, and the settlement stood with its doors open, and the board off the middle terrace stood by the wall with four divisions ruled on it and three filled, and a division on a board in a valley with no office in it is a division and is not a head and is not a fifth line, and everybody who stood there knew it. A man had been going along the doors with a basket of the small stone that wants laying on the top of a bed, and he had stopped when the doors stopped opening towards him, and he stood at the low end of the terrace with the basket on his hip through all of it and nobody asked him to put it down.

The woman of about forty-four who keeps that board stood beside it, and the man of about sixty of the Standing stood opposite, and the man of about thirty-one of the Standing stood between, and the woman of about sixty-four who ties about nine hundred picking cords stood with her hands empty, and about nine people of the settlement stood round, and nothing was voted, because nothing in this valley is voted.

“**Somebody has to go down,**” the man of about thirty-one said. “**The low roads are making a gathering and our ground is wanted at it, and a person going is better than a leaf going, because a leaf with nothing under a mark says yes to anything a reader brings to it.**”

“**I will go,**” the woman of about sixty-four said. “**I keep nothing and I am on no roll, and I can be refused by my own valley, and if I am refused I will carry it back.**”

The man of about sixty asked his three questions then, and they were the same three he asks anybody who is about to leave that terrace, and he answered none of them. Who is going. What she holds while she is gone. And who in this valley could call her back if the gathering said something she had not agreed to. Nobody on that terrace answered the third one either, and the woman of about sixty-four answered the second one out loud, and what she held was nothing, and the holding of nothing is the whole reason she is the one standing there.

Nobody asked for another name. Nobody wrote her name at the top of anything. The woman of about forty-four ruled nothing new on the board, and the delegate stood made and not found.

Then the delegate did the first thing a delegate of that valley does, and she did it out loud, and she was believed, which is the resolution and it is not a victory.

“**Our right to refuse contact stays explicit,**” she said. “**Write it so. If the gathering will not have us on those terms, we do not come. And write this beside it, that I refuse to be thanked for coming, and that no thanks in that room is owed to this valley.**”

The woman of about forty-four wrote it on a bare leaf in her own hand, and the refusal to be thanked for coming went down in ink, and it was not softened, and nobody thanked the delegate, and nobody in that settlement said the valley should be glad to be asked.

On the wall behind them lay a document about a place where every argument was once put in the ground, written by four hands twenty-two winters gone, and it is in the room and it is not the Crown's form. The man of about thirty-one counted the objects on that terrace out loud once, because he could not be trusted with a silence, and he got as far as the leaf the delegate was going to carry and stopped there, and did not go on to say which of the others it was like, because it was not like any of them.

The man of about sixty looked at the delegate for a long time, and he did not say whether going was right, and he would not say, and that refusal stood beside her insistence the way two true things stand in this valley without being added.

“**Go,**” he said at last. “**And come back refused or received, and either will do.**”

The woman of about sixty-four asked him one thing then, which was what she should say if she was asked up there who had sent her, and the man of about sixty said that was hers to carry and not his to write, and she said that was what she had wanted to know and thanked nobody for it, because there was nobody in it to thank. The man of about thirty-one wrote the Low Gate on the back of nothing at all, having been asked to and having nothing to write it on, and the woman of about forty-four said a person leaving a valley needed no paper from anybody in it to get to a gate three miles away, and he agreed with her and did not stop saying it, which is how the rest of that morning went.

Nobody was angry. The woman of about forty-four read the two sentences back off the leaf before the ink had set, out loud, in the valley's own idiom, and got them right both times, and asked the delegate whether she wanted a word changed, and the delegate said no, and that was the last anybody said about the leaf. About four of the nine put it about that the valley had sent its oldest counter to do its youngest thing, and about five put it about that it had sent the one person nobody would miss at the lines, and the two sets did not overlap and she was not asked which of the two she thought she was, and the leaf dried in the sun with the ink dark on it, and the board stood by the wall with its divisions quiet, and the man with the basket picked his basket up and went back along the doors, and the settlement went back to work with the sound of fruit being handled, and the delegate stood a while looking up the slope she had lived on all her days, and then went in to pack nothing, because she carries her slate and her hands and needs nothing else.

The Low Gate was three miles down the lane from that terrace and the woman was going to reach it in the evening, and nobody offered to walk her down and nobody said that they would not, and the bar of wood was still lying in the grass at the foot of the Low Lane at that hour and still going to be lying there when she came past it.

**The cost of that hour on the terrace is a column and it is the woman of about forty-four who keeps the board's, and a delegate unwaged and a refusal written down are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a leather strap at fifteen, a rushlight at six, a cart tire at thirty, a hank of twine at eight, a seed sieve at twenty-four, a cake of soap at ten, a scythe at twenty-seven, a jar of oil at thirteen, a nail at twenty-one, a whetstone at sixteen, and a hand file at nineteen.** Fifteen, six, thirty, eight, twenty-four, ten, twenty-seven, thirteen, twenty-one, sixteen and nineteen is one hundred and eighty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and eighty-nine pence is three marks and forty-five over**, and four divisions on a board and three filled and three questions and a basket of small stone and three miles down the lane and about nine hundred cords and four hands of writing and five documents and five places and a sixth object and a seventh and three names and an eighth are thirteen figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the third hour and the bar sitting and in the evening and twenty-two winters are four figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of the column. **A woman of about forty-four who keeps the board and a woman of about sixty-four who ties the cords and a man of about sixty and a man of about thirty-one of the Standing and about nine people of the settlement and about four of the nine and about five of the nine and two sets that did not overlap and a man with a basket of small stone are nine figures of persons, and the second of them is the one who is going and the second of them is not on any roll of the nine.** Nobody thanked the delegate and nobody thanked anybody.
