# Chapter 0570

**The middle terrace, the third hour of the pick sitting, a board with four divisions ruled on it and three of them filled, a woman of about thirty-eight who asked four people a question at the second hour and has not been told she may, a block of ground on the second terrace being thinned by a man who has not been taught it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who has decided the one thing he knows how to do.**

He was let back in behind a carter of about nineteen who was asked the four things at the bar and answered all four of them, the fourth one badly and in a hurry, and who came up the lane without stopping. He himself was asked the four things at the bar on the second night after he came out of that valley and he said that he did not know to the fourth one, and the woman of about fifty-five wrote nothing, and then the woman of about thirty-eight was asked and answered and he walked in behind her about two paces off, and it is the second time that has happened to him, and a carter of about nineteen came up that lane on his own answers, and a carter of about forty who went out in the swell sitting with two sacks of pears on a cart has not come back in at all.

**And nobody asked. The woman of about fifty-five has said once, to a man of about fifty-one, that a bar of wood in grass and four questions and a person answering is not a door and is not a lock and is not an order, and that she has kept that bar for about thirty years and has never once been told by anybody what she is to do with a person who does not answer the fourth.**

At about the second hour of that sitting the woman of about thirty-eight had gone to the board off the middle terrace, on her own account, in the open, at the hour the board sits, and had asked four people one question.

“**I am asking in front of people and not in the room with the four stools in it, and I am asking at the second hour and not at the ninth, and I have been told both of those are the wrong way to do it, and I have done both of them on purpose because I would rather be refused in the open than permitted in a room.**”

Two of the four were not at the board, and one of those two sent a child with a message, and the fourth was there and said nothing for about as long as a man takes to say nine.

Nobody thanked her. The man of about sixty said *it is put* and the man of about thirty-one said nothing at all, and the man of about thirty-one has said, once, that he said nothing because he had said the other half of that sentence in a made road about nine yards short of a cross of two stones about eleven days earlier and did not want to say it twice, and nobody has asked him whether that was the reason.

**And the block on the second terrace was being thinned by a man of about forty-four and a boy of about fifteen, and they were doing it the way it is done on the man's own ground, which is not the way it is done on hers, and a year of that block is gone, and the man of about forty-four knew it by the second hour and said so himself and nobody has said anything to him about it since.**

“**It will want doing again in about four days,**” the man of about forty-four said, “**and it will want doing by whoever wants to do it right, and I do not mind, and I would like it said that I said I did not mind.**”

Nobody thanked him. The woman of about thirty-one came off the terrace at about the third hour, looked at about eleven yards of it, said one sentence, and went back to the gate.

“**That is about four days of somebody else,**” she said.

Then the woman of about forty-four, who keeps the board off the middle terrace, had ruled four divisions on a board and filled three of them, and the order of them is not the order that goes on a leaf about nine inches by six with four ruled heads on it, and the third of them is not the third of the leaf, and the woman of about thirty-one read the leaf's order out of her own head and against the board and did not put it right. The woman of about forty-four, who keeps that board, said it out loud.

“**Ours is a person first,**” she said. “**Then where it is. Then who it is. Then what goes on it. A thing that is not on a hill is not the first line. A hill is where it is and a person is who it is, and the two of them do not stand in one another's place.**”

**And in the inside of his coat there was a leaf of foolscap on which he had written four lines for himself on the eighth of Thawmonth and had carried ever since, and he did not take it out, and the third line on that leaf is a trade and the third line on the board is a name, and the order on the board is one he did not put in and is not going to take out.**

“**You are not going to argue,**” the woman of about forty-four said.

“**No,**” he said. “**I have been in a trade for eleven years in which my whole work is telling people the order of four things on a board, and I have been in this valley about nine days, and I do not think I have ever in my life been in a place where the order was not mine.**”

**And five documents are in five places and none of them is on that board and none of them is the Crown's form. The Crown's form is in a county four days and about nine off and its fourth head is ruled and empty and has been empty for about six months, and no office has filled it and no person has filled it and nobody in that valley can. The foolscap sheet with four ruled lines and a man's name in the fourth of them is in a market town. The returned form from a county two hundred miles off has a woman's word in its fourth head. The piece of stone a foot and a half long is in a room in another county. And a leaf about nine inches by six with a mark in a tree at the top of it and nothing under it is in a satchel four days and about nine the other way. A fifth line ruled on a Monday morning in that market town by nobody who ordered it is a fifth line and is not a fourth head and is not a sixth, and a division on a board in a valley with no office in it is a division and is not a head and is not a fifth line.**

Nobody ruled a head. Nobody filled one. Nobody in that valley has ever been asked to.

And then he said the decision out loud, once, to a person, and it is the whole of the block and nobody thanked him for it.

**He told the woman of about thirty-eight, at about the fourth hour, standing at the top of the second terrace with a pruning knife in his hand that he had not been given, that he was not going to say what was wrong with the cut, and that she was going to have to say it, and that she was going to have to be asked first, and that he was not going to ask her on anybody's behalf either, including his own.**

“**You walked four days and about nine to tell me that,**” she said.

“**I walked four days and about nine to find out that I had said the same wrong thing twice,**” Aren Kest said, “**and I have told you at the second hour in the open and not in a room, because that is what you did at the second hour, and I noticed that I was copying you, and I am telling you that as well and I am not going to pretend I did not notice.**”

“**I heard that,**” the man of about forty-four said, from about nine paces off. “**I do not know what it is about and I am not going to ask and I would like it noticed that I did not ask.**”

Nobody thanked anybody.

**And the road is open, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is on the wrong side of it, and about four hundred people in a market town of about two thousand can say where he sleeps, and nobody in nine counties has been told that he is four days and about nine above a bar of wood lying in the grass, and the leaf on the table in a room over a saddler's is still face down and nobody has turned it over, and the floor of that room was washed before he came up here and nobody in nine counties knows who washed it and nobody was thanked.**

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the man of about forty-four's, and a year of a block of ground is not in it and a year is not a price and cannot be made into one by anybody.**

**a pruning knife at two, a sharpening steel at five, a leather grip at seven, a sack of lime at ten, a bushel of chaff at thirteen, a hurdle at seventeen, a second hurdle at twenty, a pot of tar at twenty-four, a stone of chalk at twenty-eight, a coil of withes at thirty-nine, and a bootlace at twenty-six.** Two, five, seven, ten, thirteen, seventeen, twenty, twenty-four, twenty-eight, thirty-nine and twenty-six is one hundred and ninety-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and ninety-one pence is three marks and forty-seven over**, and four divisions and four ruled heads and a fifth line and a fourth head and a line at the top of a paper in a made road are five shapes and not one object and none of them is a price and none of them goes in at either end of this column, and the eight of Thawmonth and the six months of an empty head and the four days and about nine and the four days again and the nine days and the four days and the eleven days and the about thirty years and the about sixty stones are nine figures of time and of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the about four hundred who read a sheet in a street in Mudmonth and the about four hundred at the end of a made lane are one number and are not one number and are in this column at neither end.

The board with three divisions filled stayed on the wall off the middle terrace with nothing on the fourth, and the fourth was not cut, and the woman of about forty-four did not rule it, and the knife went back into the block, and the second terrace was thinned wrong and will be thinned wrong again in about four days by somebody, and a man of thirty-two with a limp carried a pruning knife down to the press-house at about the ninth hour and put it on the rail and could not account for the hour of the morning, and nobody asked him and nobody thanked him.
