# Chapter 0595

**Embermonth the first, YR 320, a Monday and not a market day, the second hour in a lane outside a weigh-house, a key, a room, a press standing open, and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge who says the hearing stands.**

The lane behind the weigh-house stood empty at the second hour except for the people who had come anyway, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room over the weigh-house was in it with the key in her hand, and the copyist of about twenty-six stood beside her with a satchel, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet stood further down by the board. About nine people had come, and they had come on a notice put up in the market place that said the hearing stood on the first of every month and did not say which first, and this was the first of a month and it was a Monday and it was not a market day, and the nine of them had walked down the lane at the second hour on the strength of a notice and had found a lane and a key and a woman standing in it, and they were standing about the way people stand who have been told to wait and have not been told what for.

The man of about fifty-eight with a chain of office that is a badge came down the lane on foot and stopped in the street and not in the room, and he said out loud what he says on the first of every month, and the only first of a month inside these weeks is this day, which is a Monday and not a market day, and he said it where everybody could hear it and nobody could mistake where it was said.

“**The hearing stands,**” he said. “**There is no bench in the room. Nobody is sentenced to anything. The room opens on market mornings at the second hour and shuts at the seventh, and the key stays in the lane because the door has to be locked from the lane while there are people on the stair.**”

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet asked the man with the badge, in front of all of them, whether the room would stand on a Tuesday as well, and the man with the badge said the room opens on market mornings and is shut the rest of the week, and the man of about thirty-four wrote that down on the sheet he had brought, and nobody in that lane said out loud that a room which stands on the first of every month and opens on market mornings is two different rooms. Nobody left because of what had been said. They stood the way people stand who have been told to wait and have not been told what for, patient and unsatisfied, and the lane held them without crowding, and the morning stayed cool.

Upstairs the room held what it holds, and the woman of about twenty-nine went up first and stood in the middle of it for a while before she touched anything in it. A register at seven entries, all questions and no findings. A day-book at nine entries. A press standing open with no date anybody will give. A drawer about eleven inches deep holding about four hundred returns, and she drew it out to show the copyist how deep it was and pushed it back in without counting what was in it, because counting it was the office's business and not hers. The three series went out and in as they always had, and none was derived from another, and this ground adds none: about nine hundred sheets a month out into nine counties, about a hundred and four in one band on a fixed day, about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month in.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key locked the door from the lane while the copyist went up, and stood in the lane because the key is in the lane and she is in the lane, and she said what the room had cost her, which was a place in it for four weeks and now a place out of it on sitting days, and she said it was a place and not a punishment, because a punishment would mean somebody did it to her.

“**There is a difference between a Thursday and a market Saturday,**” she said, “**and I have worked it out and nobody has thanked me for working it out, and the door stays open on market mornings for one more week, and it is his and not mine and he will not put it on me.**”

The copyist wrote on a bare leaf with no head over it and said again that she is not going to write the fourth head twice, because once is a copy and twice is a habit, and she wrote the first line and read it back and then put the pen in its case and shut the case, and nobody put a name at the top of anything that morning. One of the nine, a man who had come about a sum of money that was not the hearing's, asked the woman of about twenty-nine whether the room would be shut on a Thursday, and she told him it would stand on a Thursday, and he said he had it the other way round from the notice in the market place, and she said the notice was not written by her, and he said he had not thought it was, and went off up the lane with his errand not done and no hard feeling about it whatever.

**The key lives in the lane and not upstairs, and that is not a small arrangement, and nobody in that lane was thanked for it.**

The woman of about twenty-nine came down at the fifth hour and stood in the lane beside the key and said, without being asked for it, that a valley above a made road four days and about nine off had reopened an argument it had kept shut for twenty-two years and had put about nine households' ground into a mend that would not finish, and then she stopped talking. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet was the only other person standing in that lane and he did not ask her one question about it, and she went back up, and the copyist wrote nothing down. What is broken between that lane and that slope stayed broken through the whole of that morning and was not mended in a scene or an aside or a document or an apology, and the two of them were never in one room with a third person in it, and the four and a half years were not said in that lane at any hour of that day by either of them or by anybody else in it.

At the seventh hour the room shut, and the key came back up the lane, and the register stayed at seven and the day-book at nine, and the press stood open, and the drawer held its four hundred, and the hearing stood with no bench in it, and that was the whole of the first of the month, and the lane emptied the way lanes do when there is nothing more to wait for, and the key hung where it hangs, and the room above settled into quiet with the leaf on the table and the form on its side table, and nobody stayed to watch it.

**And the cost of that Monday is a column and it belongs to the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key, and a place in a room and a hearing that stands are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a hand file at nineteen, a cake of soap at five, a bucket at twenty-seven, a tallow candle at ten, a sieve at twenty-four, a hank of yarn at seven, a wooden rule at thirty-one, a whetstone at sixteen, a leather strap at six, a nail at twenty-one, and a jar of oil at thirteen.** Nineteen, five, twenty-seven, ten, twenty-four, seven, thirty-one, sixteen, six, twenty-one and thirteen is one hundred and seventy-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seventy-nine pence is three marks and thirty-five over**, and a drawer about eleven inches deep and about four hundred returns and about nine hundred sheets and about a hundred and four in one band and about four hundred and nine parish rolls and four days and about nine off and seven questions and nine entries and a bare leaf and a fourth head and nine counties are eleven figures of distance and of count and not one of them is a price, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour and the first of a month and a Monday and four weeks and a season and four and a half years and twenty-two years and a Thursday and a market Saturday and one more week and a fixed day are thirteen figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **About nine people in a lane and about nine households in a valley and a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key and a copyist of about twenty-six and a man of about fifty-eight with a badge and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a man who had come about a sum of money that was not the hearing's are eight figures of persons and the column is the fourth of them and is not the sum of them.** Nobody thanked anybody.
