# Chapter 0583

**The second terrace of the Long Set, the third hour of the pick sitting, a house with the shutters still shut, which have been shut since before the sun got up, a man of about thirty-one of the Standing, who has come down that row to do a thing he has been given to do, a woman of about thirty-eight on a ladder with her back to about nine people, and a thing that was never asked.**

He did not ask her to come down. That is the part he has gone over since and he has not been able to make any other part of it sound better by going over it.

He stood at the end of the row with a slate in his hand and waited until she had finished the length she was on, and then he said the thing, and the thing was not what he had come to say and he has not told anybody what he had come to say.

“**The money is in for two of the four and the stone goes in at the top.**”

She did not turn round. She finished the length, which takes about as long as a woman takes to say four, and came down two rungs and looked at him from there, which is higher than he is.

“**Say the rest of it, because you have said the easy half and I have known men in this valley say the easy half and stand about.**”

“**The cut gets mended from the top down.**”

“**Why.**”

“**Because the two sets that said yes have got money and the two that said no have not, and the men who can lay four miles of stone properly are the same nine men whichever terrace it is, and if you start at the top the men are working above the money and the work is the same work. And because at the top the stone is out where a cart can get to it and at the fourth hundred pace it is not, and a cart that cannot get to it is four days of barrowing a day and nobody in this valley has four days.**”

He put the slate against his knee.

“**So it is the top, and then down, and the fourth hundred pace is last, and the fourth hundred pace is the one that is holding the lip up. It will be about nine days before the water reaches the four lowest and it will be about nine days of the bar being dry and there is no way to do that mend first and still mend the rest of it this year.**”

She was quiet for a moment. About nine people on that terrace had stopped, and then most of them did not start again, which is the thing about a row of fruit trees, that there is nowhere to put your hands if you are not working.

“**You have had that written down,**” she said. “**Nine days. Where did you get nine.**”

“**From two men. One of them is the man who keeps the water and one of them is a carter who has been up and down this channel in a cart. Neither of them counted it and I did not count it and it is a figure two men had.**”

“**Then it is two men's figure,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said, “**and I want it written that I have said that, and I want it written that I have not said it to make it longer, because if it is longer than nine days then the four of us at the bottom have a month and if it is shorter than nine days we have a week and either way it is not a thing anybody has checked.**”

Nobody checked it. The man of about thirty-one said that he would have it checked and she said that she had not asked him to have it checked and that those were two different sentences and she would be obliged if he noticed which of them was which.

Then she came down off the ladder and stood in front of him, and this is where the morning actually went, and it went on a thing that has nothing to do with four miles of channel and everything to do with a piece of paper.

“**Now say the other thing,**” she said. “**You have been standing there for about as long as it takes a man to boil a kettle with a slate in your hand and you have not said why you are on my terrace at the third hour.**”

“**I have come to tell you what the fund is going to do.**”

“**You have. And I have asked you a different question and you have answered the one you came with. I have watched you do it four times since the day that ground was opened and I want to know what you are doing here at the third hour on a working morning in the pick sitting.**”

The man of about thirty-one of the Standing is thirty-one years old and has been in that room nineteen years and his mother's house is at the end of this row with the shutters shut, and he has never once been in charge of anything that anybody could be harmed by, and he stood on a terrace and could not get out of it.

“**I came about a leaf,**” he said.

“**I know what leaf.**”

“**A leaf about nine inches by six with four heads ruled on it, three of them filled and the fourth ruled and empty, and four lines written under the fourth one in a hand that is not mine and not an office's, and it went out of a market town in the coat of a woman who was not sent, and it came back, and I have had it in a drawer in this house for about four months and I have not taken it out.**”

Nobody on that terrace was listening on purpose. About nine were.

“**You never asked me whether I wanted it,**” the woman of about thirty-eight said, and her voice did not change at all, which was the part that undid him, because he had come up the row braced for the oldest voice and got the one that is hers and cannot stop coming out and got it deliberately, on purpose, in front of about nine people.

“**No,**” he said.

“**Say the rest of it.**”

“**No, and here is the rest of it and I have not said it out loud before, and you are not going to have it easy. I did not ask you because I thought if I asked you would say no, and a woman who keeps four blocks in this valley and has been the only person in it who argued out loud is exactly the woman who would say no to being made into a thing that a room can point at. So I asked a person in a market town and I had nine days of walking and a made road and a place to sit down, and I asked you the way you ask a person who has already been asked once and has not said no.**”

“**I did not say no.**”

“**You did not. I noticed. You said a different true thing about a voice coming out of your mouth and I took it and I have been living on it for about four months and I have not once gone back and said, the other question, the first one.**”

“**And now four miles of channel are going to be mended from the top down and about nine households are going to be dry for about nine days,**” she said, “**and you have come to tell me that, and you have not come to tell me that you put a piece of paper in my coat and never asked, and both of those are this morning.**”

“**Yes.**”

“**Then I want the first one out, and I want it out now and in front of these people, and I do not want you to do it well.**”

The man of about thirty-one of the Standing stood at the end of a row of trees and told her that he had been afraid of her answer for about four months, and he said it badly, and he did not sit down while he said it, and about nine people on a terrace heard a thing that has not been said in this valley in about twenty-two years, which is that a person with a piece of paper to give should have asked.

Nobody thanked him for saying it. Nobody said he should have said it four months ago. **And the arrangement that broke is about thirty years old and he did not keep it and he did not break it either, and it is the reason that this morning is happening at all.**

The paper that goes out of this valley has gone out with whoever could be made to carry it for about thirty years. It goes to a market town in a coat or on a cart, and it comes back into a drawer in a house, and nobody in between asks anybody anything. There has been a Standing for longer than that and the Standing has never once put the question to a person, because a Standing that asks a person is a Standing that can be told no, and this one has not had to find out what it would do about being told no.

He is the first man in about thirty years to send out of this valley a person who holds ground. He did it because she could walk nine days and had already walked nine days, and nobody else on that slope could have stood in a made road for that length without a room being built round her first. He was handed the paper by a room. He was not asked whether there was anybody else, and he did not ask, and that is the whole of what is broken, and it was handed to him.

Nobody thanked him for thirty years of an arrangement that was not his. He is the one it happened to. The woman of about thirty-eight picked her knife up off the grass and went back to the row and did the next length, and after a while she said one more thing over her shoulder without stopping.

“**Two men's figure, and I will take it, and I want it written on the slate that I took it from two men and not from a count.**”

**And the leaf is in a drawer in a house at the end of that row with the shutters shut, and it has four heads on it and the fourth is ruled and empty and there are four lines written under it and there is nothing on it that anybody can put anything into, and it is not the Crown's form and it is not the foolscap with four heads and a man's name in the fourth and it is not the returned form from a county two hundred miles off with a woman's word in its fourth head and it is not the piece of stone a foot and a half long in a room in another county, and the five of them are in five places and none of them is the Crown's form, and the board off the middle terrace with four divisions ruled on it and three filled is a sixth object and a division is not a head. Nobody has filled a fourth head. Nobody is going to.**

**What that morning cost is a column, and it is his, and four months of a drawer and a leaf that was never asked for are not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a cake of ink at two, a sheet of cartridge at five, a rushlight at seven, a hank of yarn at ten, a sieve at fourteen, a wooden wedge at fifteen, a barrow at eighteen, an iron hoop at twenty-two, a hand file at thirty-five, a round of bread at forty-one, and a leather grip at thirty-four.** Two, five, seven, ten, fourteen, fifteen, eighteen, twenty-two, thirty-five, forty-one and thirty-four is two hundred and three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and two hundred and three pence is four marks and eleven over**, and four miles and the fourth hundred pace and about nine yards of lip and nineteen years in a room and about four months in a drawer and about nine terraces are six figures of distance and of time and of count and none of them is a price, and the third hour and the ninth hour and nine days and about four days of barrowing and four months and nineteen years are six more figures of time and not one of them goes in at either end of this column. **Two men and about nine people on a terrace and about nine households on four terraces and ninety people twenty-two winters ago are four figures of persons and are not one figure and are not added to each other, and four heads and a fourth head and four divisions on a board and a fifth line ruled on a Monday morning in a market town by nobody who ordered it are four shapes and one is not an object at all.** Nobody asked him whether he wanted to give it to her and he did not ask her whether she wanted it and neither of those is in this column. Nobody thanked anybody.
