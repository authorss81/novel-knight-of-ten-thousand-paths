# Chapter 0568

**The head of the Long Set, the eighth hour of the pick sitting, the oldest tree on that slope with a ring of stones set round it, a woman of about sixty-four who has gone to that tree at the eighth hour for thirty years and counted those stones, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who stood beside her for the whole of it and did not ask her one question.**

Her work is cords. She has cut and tied the picking cords on that slope for thirty years and there are about nine hundred of them cut in a sitting and she ties them in about a hundred and forty and she is paid in fruit and not in money and the fruit she is paid in is the worst of it, and nobody has ever tried to change that arrangement because nobody has ever wanted the job.

**She counts the stones at the eighth hour because that is the hour at which the cords are done and because a woman who has been working for a hundred and forty cords needs to know that the day is finished, and she has never found a better way of knowing than walking round a tree and counting, and she has been doing it for thirty years and the number has not been a different number once.**

There are about sixty of them and they are not laid in a circle and they are not a ring in any sense a person from off the hill would call a ring. They are set in a rough arc on the side of the tree that faces down the slope, and the bigger ones are at the north end, and there are four gaps in it where the ground has gone and been made good twice.

He stood beside her at about two paces off for as long as it takes a man to say nine and she counted and he did not ask her one question, and it is the only time anybody in this valley has stood next to that tree and not asked, and he has not decided that he was being careful. He has decided nothing at all about it, and later, on the made road four days and about nine below this slope, he would say that he did not know why he had not asked and that he had thought about it a great deal and had come up with nothing.

“**You have not asked me,**” she said, when she had finished.

“**No.**”

“**Thirty years,**” she said. “**And the boy of about fifteen asked me in the first year and the man of about sixty asked me in about the eleventh and you are the third and you are the only one of the three who stood there the whole time and said nothing at all, and I am going to tell you the thing about that and it is not about the stones.**”

Nobody thanked her.

“**I have been in this valley for sixty-four years and I have never once been asked anything by anybody in it that I could not have answered in a market, and I have made my own arrangements about the rest. I know when my day is finished because I count. That is all it is. It is not a thing I am keeping and it is not a thing anybody put in the ground and it is not a secret and I have never once wanted a man from off this hill to make me into one.**”

“**I did not think you were keeping anything,**” Aren Kest said, and it was the first untrue thing he said in that valley, and it was untrue because he had thought something and had not found the name for the something yet.

“**You did think it,**” she said, “**and that is fair, and I would rather you had thought it than not, because a man who thinks it and does not says it is a man who is about nine years old and a man who thinks it and says it out loud is a man I can deal with. It is the third kind who is a problem and there has never been a third kind in this valley and I do not expect one off you.**”

Then she asked him one question, and it was about his leg, and it was the first question she had asked anybody in about nine years.

“**That leg. How long?**”

“**Since I was about twenty,**” he said, “**and it is a bad join and it is not a mystery and there is no story about it and anybody in nine counties can look at it and tell you what it is. It is worse going up than coming down and it is about four seconds longer getting up a bank than it is sitting down on one, and I have measured that about nine hundred times and I have never once written it down.**”

“**Four seconds,**” she said, “**is worth knowing. My left hip does the same and it is worth about two seconds and I have never written that down either, and the reason neither of us has written it down is that there is nobody in nine counties to read it, and I am not going to be the first one to start a book of two-second figures in a valley that cannot be asked anything.**”

Nobody thanked her.

Then she gave him about forty cords to tie, because the picking had come in and the hands at the top of the slope were short, and he tied them badly, and he tied them badly in a way that was specific and particular to him, which is that he tied them the way a man ties a lashing on a pier, twice round and a hitch, and a cord that is tied twice round and a hitch will not come off a hook without a knife and a picking cord has to come off a hook with two fingers.

“**You have tied forty cords like a man tying a boat,**” she said, and untied all forty of them in about as long as it takes to boil a kettle, and nobody watched her do it except the man and a boy of about fifteen who was paid a penny and should not have been there.

“**That is what I know,**” he said.

“**Then you know a thing that is worth about nine counties and nothing at all,**” she said, “**and I am sixty-four and I have about nine hundred cords a sitting to tie and I am going to tie them, and you may carry the bark for me and you may not touch a cord, and I am not going to thank you for the bark and you are not going to ask me to.**”

Nobody thanked her.

**And the cost of that sitting is a column and it is the woman of about sixty-four's, and a number of stones that has not been a different number in thirty years is not in it and cannot be given a price by anybody.**

**a basket at two, a length of cord at four, a sheet of cloth at seven, a needle at nine, a skein of yarn at twelve, a pot of paste at sixteen, a night's straw at nineteen, a pound of candles at twenty-three, a jar of oil at twenty-eight, a second basket at thirty-eight, and a hoop at twenty.** Two, four, seven, nine, twelve, sixteen, nineteen, twenty-three, twenty-eight, thirty-eight and twenty is one hundred and seventy-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seventy-eight pence is three marks and thirty-four over**, and about sixty stones and four gaps where the ground has gone and been made good twice and thirty years and about nine hundred cords a sitting and about a hundred and forty of them hers and nine counties and the slope above and below her are eight figures of eight different things and not one of them is a price, and none of them goes in at either end of this column, and the about four hundred who carried a sheet out of a market in Mudmonth and the about four hundred who live at the end of a made lane about a mile and a half from a market town of about two thousand are one number and are not one number and are in this column at neither end.

The last of the light went off the slope at about the ninth hour and about four people from the press-house came up to the tree to look at it and did not ask her anything either, and about nine people in that valley know that a man from off the hill stood at the oldest tree for about as long as it takes a man to say nine and said nothing, and about four of those nine have said that the whole of what happened is what they were standing next to, and about four of those nine have said that something else happened, and the two sets do not overlap and the woman of about sixty-four has not been asked which.
