# Chapter 0529

**A sheet came up the made road on the twenty-fourth of Frostmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and it is not off a press in this town and it is not off a press in nine counties, and it is a foot and a half long, and it had been lying on a flat stone at the landward end of a coast four days off. It was carried into a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people at the first hour, and it was put down on the table by the door beside a Crown form at the fourth hour, and at the eighth hour it was still there, and that is the first time in four months that those two documents have been in one room.**

The man of about thirty-eight with a satchel brought it, and he brought it in a bundle with a sheet of oiled paper round it, and he said three things about it in the order he said them, and the first of the three was that it had come off a boat and the second was that it had come off a boat in a man's arms and not in a case and the third was that a man had put it in oiled paper and a woman had wrapped the parcel and neither of them had said anything to him about it because neither of them had asked him anything about it, which is what nobody on that sand does.

“**It is not a form,**” the man of about thirty-eight said, “**and it is not a copy of a form, and it has four heads cut into it with a knife and not with a press, and there is a word in the fourth head and nobody has ever written down what the word is.**”

“**Then it is a copy of the thing it was cut from,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**It is the thing,**” the man of about thirty-eight said. “**It is off the stone.**”

**Nobody in that room had ever seen the stone and about nine people looked at a piece of stone for about as long as a man takes to count four and did not touch it, because the man of about thirty-eight put it down flat and put both hands flat on the table on either side of it and said he would not have anybody in that room touch it in the first hour, and the reason he gave was that it had been lying on a flat stone in the open at the landward end of that coast and he did not know what was on the underside and neither did the woman who wrapped it.**

The four heads are the same four heads. That is what the man of about thirty-eight said and what the woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years said, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not say it and did not disagree with it and went on with the straightedge.

The woman of about forty-four asked for the Crown form off the table by the door and put it down beside the stone, head to head, and worked down them with a finger the way she had done with two sheets in the second week of Wolfmonth, and she did not hurry and she did not ask anybody to help her, and at the fourth head she stopped for about as long as a woman takes to say nine.

“**They are the same four words,**” she said, “**and they are the same four words on a piece of stone cut with a knife on a coast and on a piece of paper cut with a die in a press in a town, and the fourth of them is the same width on the stone as on the paper, and the fifth of them, where there is one, is about a sixteenth of an inch narrower on the stone than on the paper, and nobody in nine counties cut either of them and nobody in nine counties has ever compared the two.**”

“**Then the stone is not a copy,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**The stone is not a copy and the paper is not a copy,**” the woman of about forty-four said, “**and the two of them are two castings off one thing, and the thing they were both cast off is not in this town and is not in this county, and I have been copying for about nineteen years and I have never had cause to think about it and I have thought about it now for about nine seconds and I would like to go back to the straightedge.**”

She went back to the straightedge and nobody stopped her and nobody asked her the next question, which was a question about nine counties.

**They are two documents in two counties, and one of them is a foot and a half long on a flat stone at the landward end of a coast four days off, and the other is a sheet off a press in a market town, and nobody in that room has the right to say which one of the two is older.**

The man of about fifty-eight with the chain of office round his neck said the thing he had been holding since the sixth of Frostmonth out loud at about the fourth hour, and nobody argued with it, and it is not a finding.

“**A Crown form in this room has a press on it and a date cut into the corner of it and the date is the month the press ran and not the month anybody answered anything,**” he said. “**That stone has a knife on it and a hand on it and a weather on it, and there is no month cut into either corner of it and there is no hand in nine counties that could be dated to a year, and I have been in this room four months looking for a date and I have found one on the paper that I can buy in this town and I have not found one on that, and that is the whole of what I have to say about which is older, and I am not going to say more than that because I am not entitled to say more than that.**”

Nobody in that room said which one was older.

**And nobody in that room filled the fourth head of the Crown form on the table by the door.** The fourth head of that form is ruled and it is empty and it has been empty for about four months and nobody in nine counties has filled it, and the word in the fourth head of the stone is the coast's own and it was cut into a piece of stone by a person on that sand and it is not a Crown form and it is not in a form and it is not on a sheet of paper at all.

Aren Kest, a man of thirty-two with a limp, was in that room at about the fourth hour and put his hand flat on the table beside the stone and did not touch the stone, and said one sentence about it, and the sentence was that a thing cut into a thing by a person who was not asked to cut it is a different object from a thing cut into it by a press, and that nobody in that room had ever been in a room with the first of those.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet asked one question and it was the only question he asked in that room that day.

“**Can a Crown form be got from a stone?**”

“**No,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and the other way round it can, and that is the thing I have been arguing with myself about for two days and it is why I did not want it brought in here, and I want it said in this room that I did not want it and that I would not have asked for it, and that I am glad it has come, and the two of those are both true and about four of the nine people here believe one of them and about five believe the other and I am one of the five.**”

**And the twenty-second notch in a piece of driftwood at the seaward end of that coast is not cut, and the water has not been off that mark in twenty-two years, and nobody has been sent to that coast at all, and there is no head in nine counties for a man who goes to look at a stone.**

The stone went back into the oiled paper that evening and the oiled paper went into a bundle with nine returns in it and the bundle went up the made road on the Monday with the four ruled heads on top of it, and the man of about fifty-eight with the chain of office round his neck said that he had ruled no head over the stone and had written nothing on the oiled paper, and the copyist of about twenty-six said she was not going to write a head over a piece of stone either, and neither of them said the word again.

**And a man in a saddler's shop in this town opened that bundle about four minutes before the copyist of about twenty-six could get to it, and the four ruled heads came off the top and went onto a board, and the oiled paper went down under eight returns, and nobody in nine counties has told the man in that shop what was in it, and the form at the bottom of a return says in the same ink as everything else on it that nobody is to be sent to find out.**

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man of about thirty-four with a sheet's, and it is the twelfth one he has kept in about nine years, and a foot and a half of stone is not in it.**

**a bundle of kindling at seven, a ream of paper at twenty-nine, a bootlace at five, a box of quills at twenty-one, a broom at nine, a cake of ink at seventeen, a night's bed at eleven, a pot of paste at twenty-three, a pound of candles at eight, a rule of brass at thirteen, and a cheese at fifteen.** Seven, twenty-nine, five, twenty-one, nine, seventeen, eleven, twenty-three, eight, thirteen and fifteen is a hundred and fifty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and fifty-eight pence is three marks and fourteen over**, and a foot and a half of stone is not in it, and about two hundred miles is not in it, and four days is not in it, nor nine years, nor twenty-two years, and the notches are years and are not tides, and the nine hundred on that sand are not in it and the two thousand of this town are not in it and the two are not added to one another, and a word cut into a stone with a knife is not in it and never was.

The woman of about forty-four took the stone back off the table herself and put it into the oiled paper herself and folded the parcel, and she is the only person in nine counties who has put a hand flat on that stone, and she has said that out loud in that room and has not said it anywhere else, and the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel carried it off that coast and put it down on a table and did not pick it up again, and the woman on that sand who wrapped the parcel in oiled paper has not been asked about it and does not know there is a room.
