# Chapter 0544

**In a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people, on the eighteenth of Thawmonth, YR 320, a Thursday and a market day, the hearing stood at the second hour in front of about nine people and went on to the ninth, and a man of about thirty-eight with a satchel asked his question for the third time in a room in nine counties and got an answer, and the answer was not given by a man with a badge and was not given by the man of thirty-two with a limp, and nobody in that room thanked him for asking and he did not ask to be thanked and nobody in nine counties has ever heard him say that he wanted an answer.**

He had said it in Frostmonth and got nothing. He had said it again in Goatmonth and a woman answered it and the answer was wrong and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said why it was wrong in three sentences and was right.

The question is the same question. It has never been worded differently in three rooms in nine counties and it is on the page in the same words:

“**Is a rule still a rule when one man changed it in a hand?**”

He said it at about the second hour and he said it in front of the other eight and he put it the same way, flat, with the satchel between his knees, and then he sat down and waited, and half of that room was waiting for the man of about fifty-eight to give one of the two sentences that are a way of not answering, and the other half was waiting for the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key to answer it again, and the two did not overlap and neither of them was right to be waiting.

**And it was answered at about the fifth hour by the man of about fifty-three with a barrow, who has a form in his coat with a fifth line on it and a stroke through the fifth line and who read one page out loud at a rail of a common on a market morning in Goatmonth to about four people who live at the end of a made lane, and who was not asked the question and answered it.**

He stood up. He does that about as often as a man gets up in a room.

“**I have got about four sentences and I am not a keeper of anything and I do not carry a book of forms, and I carry a barrow, and I have carried a barrow up and down this county for about nine years, and I am going to say the answer and then I am going to sit down and I am not going to say it twice.**”

Nobody said anything.

“**Yes,**” the man of about fifty-three with a barrow said. “**A rule is a rule when one man changed it in a hand, and here is the whole of it and it is about as long as the walk from here to that rail.**”

He held up the form in his coat and did not take it out.

“**A rule that nobody can name the maker of is a rule that anybody can be shown. That is the only thing I have got and I have had it since Frostmonth and I did not have it in Frostmonth because I had not seen a room where a man put his own name at the top of a page and then read it out loud to about four hundred people in a room hired over a tallow chandler's. I have now seen that. And the reason I have seen it is not that the man was brave. It is that the man wrote his name on a page, and a page with a name on it is a thing you can be shown to a man with a satchel, and I have wanted to be shown a thing for about nine years and nobody has ever shown me one.**”

**And about four of the nine people in that room understood what he had said and about five had not, and the two sets do not overlap, and the room did not vote, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote the whole of it on a bare leaf with no head over it and did not read it back.**

“**That is not a finding,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, at about the sixth hour, and nobody argued with him, and he said the reason out loud and it was that the room had been asked a question about a rule and a rule is a thing in nine counties and the room is four people with a chain of office that is a badge, and that a room of about nine people in a hired room over a saddler's is not a place where a thing about a rule in nine counties can be found, and that the man who said the answer is a man with a barrow and not a man with a badge and that is the only reason the room is going to write it down at all.

“**Write it down,**” the man of about fifty-three with a barrow said, “**and do not put a head over it, and do not read it back, and I would like it in about four years to turn up in a room that is not this one with a man in it who has never heard of me and that is the whole of what I would like and I am not going to say it twice.**”

Nobody in that room thanked him. The man of about thirty-eight with a satchel said thank you to him for the answer and not for anything in it, and the man of about fifty-three with a barrow said he was welcome, and the room split over it the way it splits, and the half that has the answer right has said that a man with a barrow is not a person a room can check a rule with, and neither of those is a fact about the answer.

Then the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel did the thing that made it the third time and not the fourth, and it took about as long as a man takes to say nine, and it was in his own words and not anybody else's.

“**I have carried that question down about nine counties since Frostmonth and I have carried nobody knowing, and nobody knowing did the same work as a rule because a rule is a thing a clerk can be told and nobody knowing is a thing a clerk can be told the same way in four years as today. And now I have a man with a barrow saying a rule is a rule and I have to write down what he said and not what I think he meant, because what I think he meant is a thing I would have to defend in a room in about four years and I am not going to be in this county in about four years.**”

“**You might be,**” the man of about fifty-three with a barrow said.

“**I have been in this county on the same lane about nine years,**” the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel said, “**and I have never once been asked to stay, and I am not going to be asked, and I would rather carry a sentence somebody else said than a finding I made, and I have never in about nine years been able to say why and I have said it now and I am not going to say it again.**”

Nobody improved on it and nobody thanked him and he did not ask.

**And a man of about fifty-one in a grey coat said one thing about the answer and it was short and it was about the room and not about the rule and nobody thanked him for it.**

“**That is the first answer this room has got in five months that was given by somebody who is not paid and is not a clerk and has not been in this room before the second of Thawmonth, and four months of building something meant to stay small, and the smallness turns out to have been in the room rather than in the county, and I am not going to say anything else about it and I would like somebody to write down that I said it and did not say it again.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six wrote it on a bare leaf with no head over it, ruled nothing, and turned the leaf over onto its face. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet said he had watched her do that and was not going to describe the side now underneath, and she said he was right not to.

**And Aren Kest, a man of thirty-two with a limp, sat in that room from the second hour to the ninth and nobody asked him anything at all, and no one there put the answer, or a form, or a rule to him, and he said two things, and the first of the two was that a man with a barrow had said the only sentence anybody had said in that room in five months that could be carried down a made road by a man with a satchel, and the second of the two was that a rule a man can be shown and a rule a man cannot be shown are two different objects and the second of them is going out into nine counties every week on a cart and has been for about a hundred and forty years, and nobody argued with either of them and nobody wrote either of them down.**

**Nothing was opened on that Thursday and nobody was sent. The Crown's form on the table by that door still has its fourth head ruled and empty, empty these five months, and no office and no person has put anything under it, and not one head on any form in that room was filled that day.**

**And the hearing stands and it is not adjourned and it is not refused and it is not retold and it is not closed, and the man of about fifty-eight said it out loud at the ninth hour to about four people instead of about nine, and a thing said out loud to four people on the ninth hour of a market Thursday is worth exactly as much as a thing said out loud to nine.**

**A woman of about fifty-one stands at a post on a sand four days off with a piece of driftwood in her hand, and there are twenty-two notches in the edge of it and the twenty-second of them has not been cut, and the water has not come off that mark in twenty-two years, and what a notch counts there is a year and not a tide, and nobody has been to her.**

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key's, and it is the fourth one she has ever kept, and an answer given by a man with a barrow and not written down twice is not in it.**

**a bit of chalk at one, a second farthing at two, a sheet of foolscap at three, a night's straw at four, a candle-end at five, a third broom at seven, a pound of candles at eight, a wick at ten, a stone of tallow at fifteen, a sheet of oiled paper at eighteen, a second cake of ink at twenty-eight, and a bootlace at forty.** One, two, three, four, five, seven, eight, ten, fifteen, eighteen, twenty-eight and forty is a hundred and forty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and forty-one pence is two marks and forty-five over**, and a rule is not in it and a badge is not in it and a barrow is not in it, and the four heads are not in it and the fifth line is not in it and the fifth line is not a fourth head, and the three words for one lane are not in it and the three of them are still three and the room has adopted none of them and nothing has been scheduled about choosing, and about four hundred at the end of a made lane and about four hundred under a second head of a return in Harvestmonth are two figures that are the same figure and are not the same figure and go in at neither end of this column, and about nine hundred on a sand four days off and about two thousand in this town are two more counts of two other things and are in neither end either, and the twenty-two notches and the twenty-two years are one figure told two ways and neither of them is in this column, and about four years and about nine years are not in it, and nobody in that room was paid and a man with a barrow is not paid and a man with a satchel is not paid and neither of those is in this column because a man who is not paid has no column.

The man of about thirty-eight with a satchel went down the lane at about the ninth hour and stopped at the door and turned round and said one more thing to the room, and it was not about the answer.

“**I have been up this lane twice a month for about nine years on the business of a man who could see my face and not my voice, and I have got the voice, and I am going down it to a county four days off a coast and I am going to say what was said in this room in the order it was said in, and I am not going to say which of the two of them was right, and that is not a decision and I am not making one.**”
