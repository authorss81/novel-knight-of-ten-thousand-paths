# Chapter 0537

**Goatmonth the eighth, YR 319, a Saturday and a market day, and the second hour and the sixth, on a made lane about a mile and a half out of a street of a market town of about two thousand people, past a common and down to eleven houses and a chapel, where about four hundred people live, and not one of them has been told anything, and a man of about thirty-four who has walked about nine roads in this county in eleven years was walking it with nothing in his hand.**

He had not come down on the Thursday, and there had been a man with a barrow at the rail of the common on the Thursday afternoon reading a page out loud to about four people who live at the end of that lane, and the clerk of about thirty-four had been in this town on the Thursday and had heard about it at the saddler's and had come down on the Saturday to see what a page read at a rail does by the time it has gone a mile and a half.

What it had done was arrive as a name.

That was the finding, and it took the clerk of about thirty-four about four minutes at the rail of the common to establish it, because at the rail there is one woman who keeps a goat and who had been at the market on the Thursday and had bought bread on the way home, and she had been standing at the rail at about the sixth hour on the Thursday and had heard about four minutes of a man's voice and had taken the name away with her, and she said it out loud when he asked her, correctly, and she said she had not known till she said it that she had it.

“**A form has got a place on it,**” she said. “**I have got a copy of a form in my kitchen with a place on it and I have never once wanted to know anybody’s name. I have wanted to know whether the name on it is the name I use.**”

The clerk of about thirty-four wrote nothing down. He had learned in Embermonth that a lane is the one place in this county where a man with a sheet and a straightedge makes the situation worse, and he had come down on the Saturday without either.

At the eleventh of eleven houses the woman of about twenty-seven was on the step with her door open behind her, and she had heard the name, and she said the thing that the clerk of about thirty-four wrote on a bare leaf and did not read back to anybody.

“**So it has moved from paper to people,**” she said, “**and there is no head in nine counties for that, and there is not going to be one, and I am not going to stand at this door and pretend I am frightened of it. What I want to know is what it is for. I have had a copy of a form in this house since Harvestmonth with a road on it that this end of the lane does not agree on the name of, and the man who filled it in had never been in this house. Now there is a man’s name going down the lane from a rail on a common. Tell me what that name is for and I will shut this door and go and do my washing.**”

Nobody in that lane could tell her, because there was nobody in that lane to tell her, and the clerk of about thirty-four said so, and said that he had come down with nothing in his hand and had been asked nothing by anybody and had no answer to give her, and she said that was the correct answer and she had known it would be, and she went in and shut the door and was not thanked and did not ask to be thanked, and nobody in that lane agreed with her out loud.

He went on down to the fourth house, where a woman of about sixty-three came to the door, and a man of about seventy was up behind her with a blanket over his knees, and neither of them had heard the name.

“**What has happened at the rail?**” the man of about seventy said.

“**A man read a page out loud on Thursday afternoon,**” the clerk of about thirty-four said, “**and there is a copy of a form in this house with your father's name for a lane at the top of it, and the man that read the page did not know that and would not have cared.**”

“**Then it is nothing to do with us,**” the man of about seventy said, “**and I would like to go back to my fire.**”

And the clerk of about thirty-four said the thing he had come down to say, and he said it to the woman of about sixty-three and not to the man, and it was the ordinary finding of an ordinary walk.

“**A record read out loud in a market town does not get to a made lane,**” he said. “**A lane learns about a thing the way a lane learns about a form. A person comes back with a sheet, or a person comes back having stood at a rail, and the lane believes the person and not the sheet, and by the time the sheet would have got here the person has been here and gone. That is all that has happened in this county in four months and it will be all that happens in the next four.**”

**And the copies went on being copies, and nobody regulated them, and nobody thanked anybody for them.**

There is one of them in most kitchens at the end of that lane. They are not forms and they are not returns and they are not copies of anything except a copy, made at a table with a pen and a borrowed straightedge, and the reason there are as many as there are is that a man in a saddler's shop in this town takes the top sheet off a bundle about nine times a month and puts it on his board and hands the rest down, and nobody has ever told him he may not, and nobody ever will.

About nine people at the end of that lane have compared their copies with another one, and the nine do not agree about a place, and that is the whole of what has happened to four hundred pieces of paper in four months. One of the copies carries the Chapel Way under its first head in a hand that is not the man in the cart’s, and its third head sits under its second one because the sheet it was copied from had a crease in it, and nobody has ever asked about that copy and nobody has sent anybody to find out who wrote it, and no copy after the ninth has been compared with anything by anybody.

The clerk of about thirty-four went on down the lane to the chapel and stood at the gate of it for about as long as a man takes to say four, and looked back up the mile and a half of it at eleven houses, and did not write any of it down, and did not rule anything, and put no head over any of it.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the woman of about sixty-three’s, and it is the second one she has ever kept, and a copy of a form in about four hundred kitchens is not in it.**

**a pound of flour at twenty-one, a cake of soap at eleven, a candle at six, a stick of sealing wax at nine, writing paper at nineteen, a pot of tea at five, a broom at thirty-one, a stone of tallow at fifteen, a bootlace at seven, a bundle of kindling at seventeen, a hank of yarn at twenty-one, and a farthing at one.** Twenty-one, eleven, six, nine, nineteen, five, thirty-one, fifteen, seven, seventeen, twenty-one and one is a hundred and sixty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and sixty-three pence is three marks and nineteen over**, and the four hundred kitchens are not in it, and the four people at the rail are not in it, and the six at the eleventh of eleven houses are not in it, and the woman of about twenty-seven is not in it, and the man of about seventy is not in it, and about four hundred people live at the end of that lane and are not in it, and the four hundred that went under a second head of a return in Harvestmonth are not in it either, and the two of those two are the same figure and are not the same figure and go in at neither end of this column, and the three names for one lane are not in it and there are three of them and the room has not chosen and nothing has been scheduled about choosing and nobody at the end of that lane was asked which of the three is the true one.

He came back up the mile and a half in the afternoon with nothing in his hand, which is the only time in four months that clerk has walked that lane and come back with nothing, and the woman of about twenty-seven was at the eleventh house with her door open and did not come out, and the man of about fifty-three with a barrow was not at the rail, and the rail was there.
