# Chapter 0508

**The twenty-fifth day of Harvestmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour, in a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people four days up a made road from a coast, and a return went out of that room on that Thursday with three heads filled in and a fourth head ruled and empty, and the second head on it says about four hundred, and about four hundred people are at the end of a lane about a mile and a half from that room and none of them has been told.**

## One

**The lane is a made lane and it is about a mile and a half long and it goes from a street of that town past a common and down to eleven houses and a chapel, and about four hundred people live at the end of it and about four hundred was written under the second head of a form on the twenty-fourth day of a month carried at thirty by a man of about forty-one who has a cart and who walked it on the Tuesday and filled the form in on the Wednesday at a table by the door.**

He did it without being asked and he brought the form in himself and he gave it to the clerk of about twenty-two and he said one sentence about it and then he went out and did not wait.

“**That is the figure of the people who were in the lane on the Tuesday,**” the man of about forty-one said, at about the second hour, on the Thursday, to the room, on his way out. “**Not the ones who live there. There is a man of about seventy in the fourth house who was in his bed and there were nine women at the well and none of them lives at the end of that lane.**”

**And that is fifty-six words in one turn of one exchange, printed in two spans of fifteen and forty-one, and the man of about forty-one is not on the list of about nine people in that room and is not a keeper of anything and has a cart and is about forty-one and about four hundred people live at the end of a lane and about four hundred is on a form, and the two of those are about four hundred and are not the same about four hundred and are not added to each other and the man of about forty-one is not told that anybody has written anything down.**

## Two

“**You have put the wrong figure on it,**” the woman of about thirty-four said, at about the second hour, to the clerk of about twenty-two.

“**I have put the only figure anybody gave me,**” the clerk of about twenty-two said.

“**Then put no figure,**” the woman of about thirty-four said, “**and put his sentence on the second head instead, because his sentence is true and the four hundred is a number of bodies in a lane on a Tuesday morning and in about four years a man in this county will take the four hundred and move a school to that lane because of it.**”

The clerk of about twenty-two looked at the form for about as long as it takes a man to say four.

“**The head says a figure of people,**” he said.

“**The head says a figure of people and it does not say whose,**” the woman of about thirty-four said. “**It is a head cut into a piece of wood in about four counties and it has been there about four years and nobody has ever asked it to be any more specific than that, and the man who asked for that is not in this room and I do not know his name and he is not named anywhere on that sheet.**”

**And that is one hundred and fifty-nine words in five turns of one exchange, of which the first is eight, the second is nine, the third is fifty-nine printed in two spans of four and fifty-five, the fourth is seven, and the fifth is seventy-six printed in two spans of thirteen and sixty-three, and a woman of about thirty-four said the thing the form was for out loud in a room of about nine people and about four of the nine wrote it down and about five of the nine did not, and the clerk of about twenty-two did not write down the word *whose*, and it is the only word in that sentence that nobody in that room wrote down.**

## Three

**And the return went out on the Thursday, and the clerk of about twenty-two put the four hundred in and did not put the sentence under the second head, and he did not leave the second head empty either, and about nine people in that room did not notice the choice and about four of the nine noticed it and one of the four is the copyist of about twenty-six and she has said since the second hour that she noticed it at about the fourth hour and said nothing.**

The clerk of about forty-four, who has been copying for about nineteen years and who has not been asked to copy anything since the second hour, copied the whole of it at about the sixth hour, including the fourth head, which is empty, and her copy has the same four heads and the same three figures and the same empty fourth line, and it is a second object in two counties that says the same thing about a lane and about four hundred people at the end of it, and nobody told the four hundred.

**And the form says at the bottom, in the same hand and the same ink as everything else, *Nobody is to be sent to find out*, and nobody was sent to that lane, and about four hundred people at the end of it are not on anything and are not a figure of a market and are not a figure of a weigh-house and are not the about nine hundred people on a coast four days off, and the four of those figures are not added to one another in that room or anywhere else.**

## Four

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was in that room at about the sixth hour and about the ninth hour and was not asked anything at either of them, and when he was asked something else he said four words, and the four words are the first time in about nine years that he has said he did not know something about a piece of paper.**

“**Can a form be stopped?**” the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel asked him, at about the ninth hour, and he asked it the way a man asks a thing at the end of a day and not the way a man asks a thing he wants answered.

“**I do not know,**” Aren Kest said, and then did not say anything else about it, and about four people in that room wrote *I do not know* on the leaf in the man of about thirty-eight's book and about five of the nine did not write it down and about nine of the nine have said since the second hour that they thought a man who has read about four hundred and nine returns in two days would know.

**And that is nine words in two turns of one exchange, of which the first is five and the second is four, and the question was not the question anybody had been working on for eighteen days, and the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel put the leaf in his own book and did not read it out again and about four people in that room have said since the second hour that the four words were the most useful thing said in that room in three weeks and about five of the nine have said that a man who cannot stop a form is not the man to ask, and the two sets do not overlap at all.**

## Five

The cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man of about forty-one's, and it is the fourth one he has kept in about four months, and a form with a figure of about four hundred on it is not in it.

**a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a knife at two, a second knife at two, a rule at four, a second rule at four, a week's straw at one, a second week's straw at one, a candle at two, a second candle at two, a third candle at two, paste at two, a pot for the paste at five, a second pot for the paste at five, bread at one, a second bread at one, cheese at four, a second cheese at four, a third cheese at four, small beer at two, a second small beer at two, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a second bucket of tar at eight, a night's bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a third night's bed at four, a fourth night's bed at four, a waxed cord at four, an inkwell at three, a pot of tea at three, a bootlace at one, a string at two, a farthing at one, a second farthing at one, a third farthing at one, a fourth farthing at one, and a fifth farthing at one.** one, one, three, two, two, four, four, one, one, two, two, two, two, five, five, one, one, four, four, four, two, two, five, eight, eight, four, four, four, four, four, three, three, one, two, one, one, one, one, and one is one hundred and ten pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and ten pence is two marks and fourteen over**, and two marks is ninety-six pence and fourteen pence over, and about four hundred people at the end of a lane is not in it, and the four hundred that went on a form on the Thursday is not in it either, and the two are about the same figure and are not the same figure and are not added, and a fourth head is empty on a form in two counties and is not in it.

**And on the twenty-fifth day of Harvestmonth in this year a return went out of a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people with a lane's worth of about four hundred on it under a head cut into wood that does not say whose, and a clerk of about twenty-two wrote the figure in and did not write the sentence, and about four hundred people at the end of that lane are on a piece of paper and are not on anything else and have not been told and were not sent for, and the form says at the bottom that nobody is to be sent to find out, and that sentence is on it in the same ink, and about nine hundred people on a coast four days off are on two returns and were not told either, and the two sets of people are not added to each other and the tide decides twice a day and is not paid.**
