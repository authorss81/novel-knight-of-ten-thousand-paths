# Chapter 0501

**The twenty-sixth day of Rainmonth, YR 319, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a market town of about two thousand people four days up a made road from a coast where eleven houses stand along two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand, outside a weigh-house with a board on it that has been there since the twenty-ninth of a month carried at thirty, and a sheet of paper is nailed to that board at about the second hour on that Wednesday and is still on it at the eighth hour, and about nine people have copied an address off one of the two sheets on that board and about four of the nine do not know which of the two they copied.**

## One

**What was nailed to that board on that Wednesday was two sheets, side by side, and they are the same length, and they are folded once, and they have four heads cut into them, and the difference between them is that one of them has three lines filled in and one of them has nothing filled in at all, and the man who nailed them up is a man of about twenty-nine who puts up notices for the road office and has done it for about eleven years.**

He nailed the first one at the second hour and the second one four minutes later and he put them at the same height because a board takes one height, and the first one is a notice and the second one is a return, and the notice says that a thing is going to be sat in a hired room over a saddler's in that town beginning on the sixth day of a month carried at thirty, and the return asks for three things and has a fourth line under a fourth head that nobody has filled in on any return that has ever come back to that board.

The three things the return asks for are the ones anybody would expect, because they are the ones on every form anybody has: **a place, a figure of people, and a trade.** The return says *enter the name of the road as the people who live at the end of it call it, enter the number of people who use it, and enter what is carried on it.* It says *send this back to the room above the weigh-house by the second day of Fallowmonth.* And it says, at the bottom, in the same hand and the same ink as everything else, **Nobody is to be sent to find out.**

**And under the third line there is a fourth head, and the head is cut and the line is ruled, and the line is empty, and there is no sentence anywhere on the sheet that says what goes in it, and the man of about twenty-nine who nailed it up was asked about it by a woman of about thirty-one at the fifth hour and he said that nobody had told him and that he had asked once, in Wolfmonth, and had been told that it was not his to decide.**

## Two

A man of about forty-four with a stall four doors down had been reading both sheets for about as long as it takes a man to say nine before anybody spoke to him, and he had read the first one and then the second one and then the first one again, and the woman of about thirty-one who had asked the man of about twenty-nine stood at his shoulder and did not ask him anything for about four minutes.

“**You have read that one twice,**” the woman of about thirty-one said.

“**I have.**”

“**Which one is the one you are waiting for?**”

The man of about forty-four with a stall thought about that for about as long as it takes a man to say four.

“**I do not know yet,**” he said, “**and I have been in this town thirty-one years and I have never once not known which of two things I was waiting for, and I would like somebody to tell me which one I am waiting for before I put a day's takings into it.**”

“**Then you will lose a day's takings,**” the woman of about thirty-one said, “**because whichever one it is, the answer comes back on a sheet and not on a cart, and there is nobody in that room who is going to come and find out, and they have written that on the bottom of one of them in the middle of the sentence.**”

“**Who wrote that on the bottom of it?**” the man of about forty-four with a stall said.

“**I did not see,**” the woman of about thirty-one said. “**I read it and I went to ask the man who put it up and I did not look at the hand, and I have been doing this for about nine years and not looking at the hand is a thing I do.**”

**And that is one hundred and eighty words in seven turns of one exchange, of which the first is six, the second is two, the third is nine, the fourth is fifty-one printed in two spans of five and forty-six, the fifth is fifty-seven printed in two spans of seven and fifty, the sixth is eight, and the seventh is forty-seven printed in two spans of four and forty-three, and the man of about forty-four with a stall has not lost a day's takings, because a market day came four days later and the takings were the same as any other market day, and nobody in that street knows that he was told about a sheet in a board and about four of the people in it were told by him and about five were not.**

## Three

**And the two sheets on that board are the same length and they are folded once and they are pinned at the same height, and about nine people read them and about four of the nine said afterwards that they had read the same sheet, and about four of the nine could not say afterwards which of the two they had read, and the man of about twenty-nine who nailed them up was asked which was which by four people and gave the same answer to three of them and a different one to the fourth and has not decided to this day which of the two he got wrong.**

There is a reason the two are hard to tell apart and it is not the folding and it is not the ink. **It is that the notice has three lines filled in at the top, because a notice has a room and a day and a place on it, and the return has three lines empty, because a return has not been filled in yet, and both of them have four heads, and on the notice the fourth head is empty and on the return the fourth head is empty, and the two fourth heads are cut in the same wood by the same hand in the same county and are the same width.**

A man of about thirty-eight with a satchel came at about the fifth hour to look at the date on the notice, because that is his job and because a date that is wrong costs a county about nine pounds of carriage, and he read it and found it correct and said so and did not say anything else and went off toward the landward end of that street with the satchel against his hip.

**And what he did not do, and did not say he would not do, and did not know he was not doing, is pick the two sheets up off the board and look at the one underneath, and the return underneath was for a road about nine miles off and had come back on the Thursday before with a figure of forty-one against it and a trade against that, and the fourth head on it was empty, and it is the fourth returned sheet in nine days and the second one in that town with a figure of forty-one or more against it, and the two figures are forty-one and thirty-eight and they are not the same road and they are not added.**

## Four

**And the fourth head is not an invention of that town and it is not an invention of that office, and a man of about fifty-one in a grey coat has four counties' forms in a satchel and all four of them have four heads, and not one of the four is out of that satchel yet, and nobody on that board knows it.**

What is known on that Wednesday, at the fifth hour, by about four people, is this. A form with four heads has been going out of that office for about four months. About nine hundred of a kind come out of one room in this county alone in a month, and about a hundred and four of them go out in one band on a fixed day, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come in a month, and those are three counts of three different series and none of them is derived from another. **The fourth head has been empty on every return that has come back. Nobody in that town can say who cut it. A copyist in another county has said out loud, once, in another room, that it ought not to be there, and nobody agreed with her, and she has not said it again.**

And the notice on the left of that board says that something will be sat in a hired room over a saddler's in that town from the sixth day of a month carried at thirty, and it does not say what will be sat, and it does not say by whom, and the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel read the date and found it correct and said so.

## Five

The cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's who put the notices up, and it is the eleventh year of it and the fourth board this month and the two sheets on it are his twelfth and thirteenth for the month, and a sheet with an empty line on it is not in it.

**a quart of paste at two, a second quart of paste at two, a bundle of paper at four, a second bundle of paper at four, a third bundle of paper at four, a pound of tallow at three, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a ladder hire at six, a boy's day at three, a second boy's day at three, a knife at three, a whetstone at one, oil for the hinge at one, a pot for the paste at nine, bread at one, cheese at four, a second cheese at four, small beer at two, a second small beer at two, a night's bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a bootlace at one, a second bootlace at one, a farthing at one, and a second farthing at one.** Two, two, four, four, four, three, two, two, six, three, three, three, one, one, nine, one, four, four, two, two, four, four, one, one, one and one is seventy-four pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and seventy-four pence is a mark and twenty-six over**, and a fourth head is not in it, and a notice and a return are not in it and are two sheets and the two of them are never added to each other, and eleven houses on a coast two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand are not in it, and a hundred paces is not in it, and four hundred yards is not in it and is a distance between two things on a beach a long way off and not a distance in a street.

**And on the twenty-sixth day of Rainmonth in this year a sheet asking for a place, a figure of people and a trade was nailed to a board outside a weigh-house in a town of about two thousand people, with a fourth head cut into it and a fourth line ruled and empty, and about nine people copied an address off one of the two sheets on that board and about four of the nine do not know which of the two they copied, and the day the notice names is the sixth day of a month carried at thirty, which is a Saturday and a market day, and the market town is four days up a made road from a coast where a woman of about fifty-one has twenty-two notches cut in a piece of driftwood and the twenty-second is not cut, and nobody in that town can be got at about any of it and nobody has been sent.**
