# Chapter 0526

**The third of Frostmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour, on a made lane about a mile and a half out of a street of a market town of about two thousand people, past a common and down to eleven houses and a chapel, where a clerk of about thirty-four who has walked about nine roads in this county in eleven years was walking it for the second time in a season with two sheets in his hand, and about four hundred people live at the end of it and none of them has been told anything.**

He had the one sheet in his left hand, on which two words were written side by side under a head that asks for one, and the hour under the pair of them. He had a copy in his coat, and a copy is a thing nobody regulates and a copy comes out of a bundle in a saddler's shop and into about four hundred kitchens at the end of a made lane, and he took it out at the rail of the common and looked at the first line on it, and then he put it back in his coat and went on down the lane.

**At the rail of the common the lane is called the Green Lane and about four people who live at the end of it call it that, and at the eleventh of eleven houses, where the lane comes out on the chapel, it is called the Long Lane and six people call it that.** A man of about fifty-three with a barrow was at the rail and said the Green Lane before he was asked, and a woman of about twenty-seven was at the eleventh house and said the Long Lane, and the clerk of about thirty-four wrote nothing down and asked neither of them anything, because he had asked both of them in Embermonth and both of them had said the same thing they said now.

“**You have been down here twice since Harvestmonth,**” the woman of about twenty-seven said, from the step of the eleventh house, with the sheet in his hand in plain sight of her.

“**I have,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**And you have not asked me anything and I have nothing to say to you about that, and I want to say one thing about the sheet so that you can carry it up the lane with you and not leave it here.**”

“**Say it.**”

“**It asks for one,**” she said, “**and there is one. I have said that to a room and to a man in a cart and to you, and none of the three of them has been in this house, and the man in the cart filled the sheet in at a table by a door in a town a mile and a half off that he could have walked to in an hour and a half on a day with the light.**”

“**He had a cart,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**He had a cart and a form and about four hundred other people on the lane that day,**” the woman of about twenty-seven said, “**and I am not angry and I have told that room I am not angry and I am going to tell you the same thing so that the two of us are saying the same words, and what I am angry about is a line, and there are about four hundred of us at this end and about ninety of us are on that paper and one line on that paper is for one word.**”

The man of about thirty-four did not answer that and looked down the lane towards the fourth house, and the woman of about twenty-seven went inside and shut the door behind her, and nobody thanked her and she did not ask to be thanked, and the clerk of about thirty-four stood at the eleventh house for about as long as a woman takes to say four before he went on down the lane.

**The third word came out of the fourth house.**

The man of about thirty-four got to the fourth house at about the third hour and knocked, and a woman of about sixty-three came to the door, and the man of about seventy was up and sitting by the fire with a blanket over his knees, and he had been in his bed on the Tuesday in Harvestmonth when somebody walked half this lane and came back with a figure of about four hundred and four trades on a sheet.

The clerk of about thirty-four asked him what people at this end of the lane called the lane.

The man of about seventy thought about it for about as long as it takes a man to say nine, and then he said a word, and it was neither of the other two.

“**The Chapel Way,**” the man of about seventy said. “**And your four men and your nine women at the well would tell you the other names before they told you that one, and that is not because it is a worse name. It is because it is the name my father gave it and his father before him, and nobody at the rail has ever used it because the rail is not the lane.**”

**The form does not ask a place its name.** It asks a person for the name the person has, and the sentence under the first head says the name of the road as the people who live at the end of it call it, and the man of about seventy is a person who lives at the end of it, and the man in the cart who filled in the return in Harvestmonth was also a person, and both of them wrote down what they had and neither of them was lying and the two of them were not in the same house.

**A place that has three names has three people.**

The clerk of about thirty-four stood in the lane outside the fourth house for about as long as a man takes to count four and then he asked the man of about seventy one more question, which was whether anybody at the eleventh house had ever said the Chapel Way out loud, and the man of about seventy said that he had heard it said twice, once by a woman who is dead and once by a woman who has moved, and that both of those were at the eleventh house and not here.

Then he took the copy out of his coat again and put it flat on the wall of the fourth house, under the hand of the woman of about sixty-three, who had come out of the door and had not said a word and had not gone back in.

**The first line on that copy is the Chapel Way.** It is in a hand that is not the hand of the man in the cart, and the third head on it is under the second one because the sheet it was copied from had a crease in it, and the fourth head is ruled and empty.

Nobody in that lane filled any head on that paper. A person at the end of a made lane filled in the first head in a kitchen, at a table, with a pen and a borrowed straightedge, in about nine minutes, the way anybody in about four hundred kitchens has filled in the first head of a copy since a man in a saddler's shop took one out of a bundle. **The third is the word on the return, and nobody has sent anybody to find out who wrote it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote the three words in the order the lane gave them to him, which is the Green Lane first and the Long Lane second and the Chapel Way third, and ruled nothing between them and put no head over any of them and wrote the hour under the three.**

**Nobody at the end of that lane was asked which of the three was the true one. Nobody was asked to choose and nothing was scheduled about choosing, and the man of about thirty-four came back up the mile and a half to the street in the afternoon with two sheets, one of them with two words on it and one of them with three, and a man of about fifty-three with a barrow saw him go past the rail of the common and did not stop him and did not say anything about it.**

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the woman of about sixty-three's, and it is the first one she has ever kept, and she keeps it on the back of a bill for a yard of straw, and a mile and a half is not in it and about four hundred people are not in it.**

**a bundle of kindling at seven, a ream of paper at twenty-three, a bootlace at four, a pot of paste at nineteen, a pound of candles at fifteen, a stone of tallow at six, a cake of ink at seventeen, a night's bed at eleven, a rule of brass at thirteen, a broom at eight, a cheese at nine, and a bun at five.** Seven, twenty-three, four, nineteen, fifteen, six, seventeen, eleven, thirteen, eight, nine and five is a hundred and thirty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and thirty-seven pence is two marks and forty-one over**, and the four hundred who live at the end of that lane are not in it, and the four hundred that went under a second head in Harvestmonth are not in it either, and the two of them are the same figure and are not the same figure and go in at neither end of this column, and three words are not in it, and eleven houses and a chapel are not in it, and the man of about seventy, the woman of about sixty-three and the woman of about twenty-seven are not in it, and none of the three keeps anything and none of the three is on a sheet in nine counties and none of the three was thanked and one of the three was not asked.

The woman of about sixty-three shut the door of the fourth house and put her hand on the latch and left it there, and the clerk of about thirty-four went on down the lane to the chapel with the three words on a sheet in his coat and the copy of a return in the other pocket, and the copy has a crease through the third head and the crease is not a head and nobody has ruled anything over it.
