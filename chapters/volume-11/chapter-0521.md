# Chapter 0521

**A man of about sixty-three, who keeps a lock-up of waste paper in a lane off the same street, put a sheet on the table by the door in a hired room over a saddler's, on the fourteenth of Wolfmonth, YR 319, a Saturday and a market day, at the second hour and again at the fifth and the ninth, in a market town of about two thousand people four days up a made road from a coast, and he would not take it away again.**

The sheet was folded twice and it was brown at every fold, and it is the paper a shop wraps a kettle in, and the ink on it is a brown-black that has gone the colour of tea. Four heads are cut into the top of it in the same four words as the four heads cut into a piece of wood about four counties away, and the third head sits a little lower than the other three because the paper has stretched about a sixteenth of an inch along the fold and nobody has ever had it ironed.

**There is a fourth-figure number at the head of the sheet, cut into it and not written, and it reads 1840, and about four of the nine people in that room said out loud that it was a year and about five said it was a number belonging to a press, a tally, a shelf, a price, a page in a book or a debt, and the woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years said the only thing she had ever said in that room about what a figure on a form is, which is that a figure you cannot place is a figure somebody put in so that a later person would think the paper was older than it is.**

The man of about fifty-eight put his thumb on the corner of the sheet and turned it round so that the cut heads were the other way up, and then turned it back.

“**It is not a year,**” the man of about fifty-eight said. “**Nobody in this county has a calendar that begins in 1840 and the ink is not the ink of a year anybody cut into paper to be remembered. It is a number and the number is on the paper, and the paper is the argument, and the ink and the paper are better evidence than the number is.**”

The man of about sixty-three said he had had the sheet since the middle of a month in the year before this one, in a chest of waste paper he buys off about nine houses in this town and two in the next, and that it had been in the chest folded twice, and that he had not unfolded it in about a year because it was in his way and it was somebody else's paper, and that on the day he came up that lane he had unfolded it because he had heard there was a room in this town that looked at sheets and he wanted to know whether it was worth the trouble.

**The four heads on that sheet are the four heads.** A place, a figure of people, a trade, and a fourth head ruled and empty. Nobody in that room had said the four words out loud at all before the man of about thirty-four with a sheet did it, and he had come in at the fifth hour and had not sat down, and he read them once and then put his finger on the second of them and left it there for a while.

Below the four heads there is a fifth line.

**The fifth line is ruled from the same left-hand edge to the same right-hand place as the four above it, and it is the same weight of rule, and there is no head cut into the wood or the paper above it, and there is nothing on it.** On the form on the table by the door, going out of this room in the Monday bundle, there is no fifth line at all. It has never had one. About nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane about two hundred miles off and this month's has four lines and about four hundred of the ones behind it have four lines and the woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years said she has never in all her life ruled a fifth line on a return, and the man of about fifty-eight said that a clerk of about forty-six with grey in her hair had ruled one in this room in Fallowmonth because a book in her county had five columns, and that the copyist of about twenty-six had taken it off in about four minutes with the knife she rules with, and that the line that came off was empty and was empty before it came off.

The copyist of about twenty-six said one thing about that and it was not about the fourth head.

“**I have ruled four thousand lines,**” Nesta Braith said. “**And every one of them had a head over it or a book behind it, and a line with neither is not a mistake anybody makes twice.**”

Nobody argued with her and nobody agreed with her and she went back to the straightedge.

**The man of about thirty-four with a sheet put the sheet flat beside the form on the table by the door and put one finger on the head of each of the four and then said the four words, twice, once at each, and the two fours were the same four words and the two sets of four were cut about a hundred and eighty years apart and neither of them was cut in this town.**

The man of about fifty-eight said the room had been looking at a fourth head for four months.

“**The fourth head is the thing everybody in nine counties can see,**” the man of about fifty-eight said. “**I have had nine counties of forms in this room in four months and every one of them has four heads and the fourth is empty, and I have asked twice what it is for and been told twice that it is a county matter. What is on this sheet is not the fourth head. It is a fifth line with no head over it, and it has been going out of presses in nine counties for about a hundred and eighty years under a form with four heads on it, and the difference between this month's form and that one is not the heads. It is a line.**”

Nobody said anything for about as long as a man takes to say nine.

**And the hand on it is not any hand in nine counties today.** The woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years asked nine people in that room, one after another and without hurry, to write the word *witness* on the corner of a leaf, and she put the nine of them side by side against the head on that sheet, and the nine were nine hands and the tenth was a tenth hand, and the tenth hand has a longer downstroke on the second letter than any man in this room has ever put on a second letter in his life.

“**That is not a county hand,**” the woman of about forty-four said. “**I have been copying for about nineteen years and I can put a county to about nine words out of ten and this is not one of the ten.**”

**And Aren Kest, a man of thirty-two with a limp, was at the side table and had not said anything for about four minutes, and nobody asked him anything all day, and the only thing he said about the work in front of him was about the downstroke and was not about a form, and nobody in that room asked him for it, and nobody in that room has asked him for anything in four months.**

“**Ask nine people in this town to write a letter to somebody they have never met,**” Aren Kest said, “**and the downstroke on the second letter will go short in some of them and long in the rest, and I have watched nine men do it in about four counties and the short stroke belongs to the man who is thinking about the letter and the long one to the man who is not.**”

The woman of about forty-four said that was the most use anybody had made of her nine leaves in nineteen years and that she was going to write it down and that he was not going to be thanked for it, and he said he had not expected to be, and nobody thanked him.

“**Then it is nobody's county,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, and read the downstroke again, “**and it is a hand from about a hundred and eighty years ago, and I can tell you that much and not one word more, and the reason I can is that nobody alive in nine counties writes like that and about four hundred people in this county were taught to write by somebody who was taught by somebody who could.**”

**And there is a second copy.** The man of about sixty-three said he had found it in the same chest on the same morning, folded once and not twice, and it is a copy of the sheet, and the copy is on better paper than the sheet, and the copy is later than the sheet, and the copy has a hand on it that is a hand of about a hundred and forty years ago and not about a hundred and eighty, and the copy is the one with a man's hand in the margin of it in nine words that nobody in nine counties has read out loud until the ninth hour of that Saturday.

The room read them out loud, and the woman of about forty-four read them twice, once to herself.

*Leave the last line blank until you are told.*

“**Nine words,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, and put them down on a leaf with no head over it, which is where two figures of carts went in Fallowmonth and where a road with no name went in Embermonth, and the leaf has no head on it and no keeper and no fixed day and the man of about fifty-eight said he would rule it himself in about four minutes with the knife he has never used and it would show.

Nobody in that room can say whose the nine words are.

They may be the clerk's. He made the second copy and he may have written them for whoever came after him, in the ordinary way that a man writes a caution on the back of a paper he has copied. They may be copied off the older sheet, in which case somebody wrote them before the clerk and the clerk copied them and the room is reading a sentence at second hand out of a chest of waste paper. **And the room did not spend an hour of that Saturday on it, and the man of about fifty-eight said out loud that a room that spends an hour on whose nine words are is a room that has decided the nine words are the most important thing on the paper, and the nine words are on the paper in the margin and not on the form, and a man in four years who is holding nine counties of forms is going to be looking at the form.** The man of about fifty-three with a barrow, who had come up the lane from the rail of a common that morning and had not said anything at all until then, said that the ninth hour of a Saturday was a good hour to have spent on nothing, and he put his hand flat on the table and left it there, and nobody in that room took his hand off the table and nobody thanked him and nobody thanked the man of about sixty-three, and nothing was conducted.

Nobody in that room said the word that would name whoever is answerable for that fifth line, and the man of about fifty-eight was asked about it twice that afternoon and gave the same answer both times, which was that there is no such word in any form in nine counties and that the room was not going to make one. Nobody sent anybody. Nobody filled a head, and the fourth head on that sheet is empty and the fifth line on that sheet is empty, and the two of them have been empty for about a hundred and eighty years.

The man of about sixty-three looked at his sheet on the table and then at the man of about fifty-eight and asked him a question that had nothing to do with the paper.

“**Do I want it back?**”

“**No,**” the man of about fifty-eight said. “**I want it here, and I want you to come back on Monday and look at it before anybody writes anything on it, and I am telling you that I want that and not telling you why, and the reason I am not telling you why is that I have not got one that would survive being said out loud in a room of about nine people.**”

The man of about sixty-three said that was the first time in about a year anybody had told him not to take his own paper away, and he laughed about as long as a man takes to say four, and he left the sheet.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the man of about sixty-three's, and it is the first one he has ever kept, and he keeps it on the back of a receipt for the chest of waste paper, and a sheet that has been folded twice in a chest for about a year is not in it.**

**a ream of twine at twenty-three, a cake of ink at seventeen, a jar of paste at fifteen, a night's straw at twelve, a pound of candles at ten, a besom at eight, a cheese at six, a pot of tea at five, and a bootlace at three.** Twenty-three, seventeen, fifteen, twelve, ten, eight, six, five and three is ninety-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and ninety-nine pence is two marks and three over**, and no fifth line goes in it, and a fifth line is not a fourth head and is not a figure of persons, of money, of days or of inches, and neither goes in it the four-figure number nobody in that room could place, nor the hundred and eighty years, nor the nine words in a margin, and the lane's four hundred and the sand's nine hundred are two different counts and the column has neither and joins neither to the other.
