# Chapter 0531

**On the tenth of Hearthmonth, YR 319, a Saturday and a market day, a man of thirty-two with a limp carried a packet of sheets up a lane and into a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people, and he came in at about the second hour, and there were about nine people in that room, and he put the packet on the table and did not have a name for what was in it.**

He had come up off the made road and he stood against the wall side of the table with his stick in his right hand for a moment before he sat down, which is a thing a man does when he has been carrying a thing with both hands for a distance and has just stopped.

“**I have not got a name for it,**” Aren Kest said, “**and I have been three days trying to find one, and there is not one, and I would rather say that out loud in this room than carry it about and have somebody in a street give it one.**”

“**Then what is it?**” the man of about fifty-eight said. He had the chain of office round his neck and he was sitting where he sits. “**That is not a young man’s question to ask at the second hour of a market Saturday; I am asking it anyway, and I would like the room to give me the true answer and not the quick one.**”

“**It is a record,**” Aren Kest said, “**of what I did.**”

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as a man takes to say four. Below them, in the street, a market was going on, and the two nailed-shut windows and the one that was open let in the sound of a town that was not in the room.

The woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years got up off the far side of the table, went to the press-corner, and came back with the current form off the top of the pile, and put it face up in front of the man of about fifty-eight, and said nothing about it.

“**Four heads,**” she said. “**A place, a figure of people, a trade, and the fourth.**”

“**And the fourth is empty,**” the clerk of about twenty-two said, “**and it has been empty for about four months, and nobody in nine counties has put anything in it, and that is on a sheet that has been out of a press in this town since before either of us came to it.**”

“**What I am asking,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**is what that thing goes under.**”

And the room went looking, and it went looking in the ordinary way, out loud, one at a time, the way a room looks when nobody in it wants to be the one who decides.

The day-book in the room over the weigh-house two hundred miles off has a head for a day and a head for an hour and a head for a person and a head for a thing. The parish rolls that come into that same room, four hundred and nine of them in a month, have a head for a parish and a head for a year. A toll-house roll has a head for a gate. A muster has a head for a company. The bare leaves on this table have no heads at all, and the man of about fifty-eight was told in the last ten days by about four people in this room that a thing with no head over it is going to turn out to be the most useful object in it.

“**There is no head in nine counties for a record of a man’s own wrongdoing,**” the man of about fifty-three with a barrow said, at the second hour, from the end of the table, and he said it the way he says things, which is once and without decoration. “**A man’s own wrongdoing is a thing a man keeps to himself, or a thing a court keeps against him. The first of those two has no head because nobody wants to look at it. The second of those two has a head with a name over it, and the name is not his.**”

“**That is two heads,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said. “**A head with a name over it, and no head at all, and both of them are worse than a head that says what the thing is.**”

“**Then we will rule one,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

He said it the way he said the thing about the head cut into the wood over the line in the room’s book, which is to say that he said it out loud and then waited to see whether anybody would argue, and about four of the nine did not argue and about five said nothing, and the room did not vote, and the deciding was his and he made it in front of them without asking them for it, which is the thing he has been doing for eleven years in this county and which is not the same as asking.

The copyist of about twenty-six did not argue either. She got up from the side table with her knife and her straightedge and stood over the first sheet of the packet for about as long as a woman takes to say nine, and then she cut a line across the top of it, and turned the sheet, and cut down the other side, and cut across, and she had a rectangle about two inches deep ruled at the head of the first page with a clean edge on all four sides, and she did not rule anything under it.

That was the part the man of about fifty-eight noticed.

“**Say what you have done there,**” he said.

“**I have ruled a head,**” Nesta Braith said, “**and I have not ruled a line under it, and there is not going to be one, and if anybody in this room rules a line under it in my absence I will take the knife out of it.**”

Nobody argued with her about that either, and nobody thanked her, and the man of about fifty-eight wrote two sentences on a bare leaf with no head over it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote them out again in his own hand and made him read the second version before it was put by.

Then the man of about fifty-eight asked the question, and he asked it to the man of thirty-two with a limp and not to the room, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key turned her pen over once and put it down.

“**What goes in it?**”

Aren Kest took a while. He put his right hand out flat on the table to steady the other one, which is not a thing a man does to steady a hand, and then he took the sheet, and he turned it so that the head was the right way up for a person standing on the other side of the table, and he wrote in it in his own hand, and it took him longer than writing a name takes a man with a good hand, and nobody offered him a better one and nobody said anything while he did it.

The room did not read it out. The man of about fifty-eight looked at it and did not read it out either, and that is the second time in ten days that he has looked at a thing with a name on it and not read it.

Then he said out loud, in front of the other eight, what the head was for, and he said it as a finding; it was not one, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it on a bare leaf and ruled nothing over it.

“**A record with a head over it can be shown to a person,**” he said, “**and a record without one can only be produced by the man who made it, and that is the whole of what the head is for. It is not there to order anybody. It is not there to be filled in. It is not a fourth head and it is not on a form and there is nothing under it to put an answer in, and I want that said in this room now while there are nine of us in it, because in about four years there will be one of us left in this room and the one will be me.**”

Nobody in that room argued with that either.

The man of about fifty-one in a grey coat said one thing about it and it was short and nobody thanked him for it.

“**You have just given a man a handle on himself,**” Gault Prentice said, “**and he has taken it, and there are people at this table who think that is the only honest paper anybody has put on it in four months, and there are people at this table who think you have handed a town a way of finding him that it did not have in Hearthmonth, and both of those are true and I am one of the second ones.**”

Nobody improved on it, and the copyist of about twenty-six wrote both halves on the same leaf and left about two-thirds of it blank, and the blank is not a figure and is not counted.

Then the sheets were counted out and looked at, and there are four of them under the head, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote on a second bare leaf what each one was a page of, in his own words, and read it back to the man of thirty-two with a limp before he put the pen down, and the man of thirty-two with a limp said that the fourth of the four was wrong by about half and named the half, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet rubbed it out and wrote it again, and nobody thanked him for that either.

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key’s, and it is the third one she has ever kept, and a head cut into the head of a man’s own record is not in it.**

**a pint of lamp oil at nine, a sheet of foolscap at seven, a thimble at four, a candle-end at two, a cake of ink at seventeen, a leather strap at twenty-one, a pot of tea at five, a bootlace at three, a night’s bed at eleven, and a bit of glue at nine.** Nine, seven, four, two, seventeen, twenty-one, five, three, eleven and nine is eighty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-eight pence is one mark and forty over**, and a name is not in it, and a head is not in it, and a knife is not in it, and the four sheets under the head are not in it, and about nine people in that room are not in it, and about four hundred at the end of a made lane are not in it, and about two thousand in this town are not in it, and those are three counts of three different things and none of the three is in this column and no two of the three are added together here.

The market went on in the street until about the sixth hour and then thinned, and the man of about fifty-eight carried the packet to the press-corner himself and put it under the flat weight with the returns, and did not lock it, and said out loud that a thing which can be shown had better not be locked, and that a locked record is a record only one man can produce, and that he had said the true thing about the head at the second hour and had gone and made it awkward by the seventh, and that both of those were him.
