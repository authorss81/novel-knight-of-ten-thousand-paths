# Chapter 0502

**The twenty-ninth day of Rainmonth, YR 319, a Saturday and a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in one room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane about two hundred miles up a made road from a coast, in a county of about nine thousand people, and a rider comes eleven miles off that lane at the first hour with a letter of four sentences in it, and the room is a body corporate with a door, a number and the capacity to sue and to be sued, out of a deed of thirteen words, and it costs fourpence a year, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of it.**

## One

**The four sentences of that letter are addressed to the keeper of that room by her office and not by her name, and the third of them asks for a bundle of fifty of the sheets that go out of that room every month, and the fourth of them says that the bundle will be read in a hired room in a town of about two thousand people and by nobody else.**

The first sentence says that a thing is being sat from the sixth of a month carried at thirty. The second says that the keeper of the room is asked to help it. The third says what is asked for. The fourth says where the fifty will be read and who will read them and it says **read, and not copied, and not carried out of the room, and not taken away.**

A woman of about thirty-one with a pen was in that room at the first hour when the rider came and she read the letter twice before she put it down, and then she said one word, and the word was not a question.

“**Fifty,**” she said.

“**Fifty,**” Mara Vey said.

**And that is two words in two turns of one exchange, of which the first is one and the second is one, and it is the whole of what either of them said about it for about four minutes afterwards, and about nine people in that county use that room in a week and about four of the nine have been inside the door and about five have not and the five knock.**

## Two

**And about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room into nine counties in two rounds a year on a fixed day, and about a hundred and four of them go out in one band on a fixed day, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month come in at that room, and those are three counts of three different series and none of them is derived from another and this room has never added them and does not add them now.**

A press stands open in that room and has stood open since a day nobody will give, and a register in it has seven entries and every one of them is a question and none of them is a finding, and a day's book has nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and a drawer about eleven inches deep is not open. **About eleven hundred people are on no roll and no sheet and no list and nothing in this year has put them on anything, and nothing in this room has ever put them on anything, and a bundle of fifty sheets going to a town four days off a coast would not put them on anything either and that is not why the fifty are not going.**

## Three

**What Mara Vey did with the four sentences is that she took the third of them and did not do it, and took the first and the second and the fourth and sent them back with a sheet of her own paper in the same packet, and the sheet of her own paper has four heads on it and three of them filled in and the fourth of them ruled and empty, and under the fourth head she has written one sentence and the sentence is fourteen words long.**

“**I am not sending fifty sheets,**” she said, at about the fourth hour, to the woman of about thirty-one with a pen. “**I am sending you the four heads, and you are going to rule them with the same knife and the same rule and the same pressure, and the fourth one is going to come out the same as the other three, and then you are going to write one sentence under the fourth one and then you are going to read it back to me.**”

“**What sentence?**”

“**You will know it when you have ruled it,**” Mara Vey said, “**because it has been on the bottom of every sheet that has come out of this room for four and a half years and I have asked twice about it and been told twice that it is not mine to cut.**”

**And that is one hundred and twenty-three words in three turns of one exchange, of which the first is seventy-one printed in two spans of six and sixty-five, the second is two, and the third is fifty printed in two spans of nine and forty-one, and the woman of about thirty-one with a pen wrote the four heads out in about four minutes with a straightedge and a knife and did not ask a second question, and Mara Vey was not thanked and did not say thank you and went back to a piece she had been on since the first hour, and the piece was finished on the sixth day of a month carried at thirty, which is a Monday and not a market day.**

## Four

**And the sentence the woman of about thirty-one with a pen wrote under the fourth head is fourteen words long and it is this: *the fourth head is empty here too and I have not been told why.* That is a fact about a room and it is not a rule and it is not a request and it is not a refusal, and it went up a made road to a town of about two thousand people four days off a coast on a Tuesday and was on a table in a hired room over a saddler's on the Saturday after that, and a man of about fifty-one in a grey coat read it twice and put it face down and then turned it back over.**

**She has been in that room for two years and a half and she has asked about the fourth head once and did not ask again, and the sentence is fourteen words and it is hers and she is not a keeper of anything and the sentence is the first sentence anybody has written under that head in nine counties, and about four people have now read it and about nine have not.**

“**You have put a sentence under a head that is not for sentences,**” the woman of about thirty-one with a pen said, at about the seventh hour.

“**I have put a sentence under a head that nobody has ever written a word under,**” Mara Vey said. “**Those are not the same head and you have just made the second one, and the second one is worse, because the first one is a line and the second one is a complaint, and a complaint is a thing somebody can answer.**”

“**And if somebody answers it?**”

“**Then it will be the first answer anybody has ever given anybody about that line,**” Mara Vey said, “**and it will be in a hand, and I would rather it were in mine or yours than in the hand of whoever picks it up next.**”

**And that is a hundred and nineteen words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is thirteen, the second is fifty-nine printed in two spans of sixteen and forty-three, the third is five, and the fourth is forty-two printed in two spans of fifteen and twenty-seven, and the woman of about thirty-one with a pen did not write the sentence down again and has not written it down since and it is on a table in a town four days off a coast in a hired room and the man of about fifty-one in a grey coat has read it twice and put it face down and then turned it back over.**

## Five

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is Mara Vey's, and it is the sixth one she has kept in four and a half years and the first one that has an item on it that is not paper, and fifty sheets are not in it and a bundle of fifty is not in it.

**bread at one, a second bread at one, cheese at four, a pound of candles at four, a second pound of candles at four, a quill and ink at three, a knife at two, oil for the knife at two, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a fourth sheet of paper at one, a packet at two, a stick of sealing wax at two, a rule of brass at nine, a straightedge at two, a second straightedge at two, a rider's day at three, a second rider's day at three, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a pot of tea at three, a second pot of tea at three, a night's bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a bootlace at one, a farthing at one, a second farthing at one, a third farthing at one, and a fourth farthing at one.** one, one, four, four, four, three, two, two, one, one, one, one, two, two, nine, two, two, three, three, five, eight, three, three, four, four, one, one, one, one, and one is eighty pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty pence is a mark and thirty-two over**, and the fourth head of a Crown form in a market town four days up is not in it and is empty, and the fourth head on the sheet that went in the same packet as this rider's letter is not in it and is empty, and two empty heads are two lines on two pieces of paper in two counties and are not added, and a form is a thing that finds out and that sentence is not in it either because it was said on a beach a long way off by a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar and it is not a rule and this column is not a record of rules.

**And on the twenty-ninth day of Rainmonth in this year, which is a Saturday and a market day, a woman of twenty-nine in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane two hundred miles up a made road from a coast refused to send fifty sheets of paper out of that room to a town of about two thousand people, and sent four ruled heads and fourteen words instead, and about nine hundred sheets a month still go out of that room and about a hundred and four go out in one band and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come in, and the three of those are not added, and the fourth head of the sheet that went in that packet is empty and will be empty on Saturday when the room in the town sits.**
