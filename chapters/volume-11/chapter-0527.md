# Chapter 0527

**The eighth of Frostmonth, YR 319, a Tuesday and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the eighth hour, in a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people, and about nine people were in that room and the strips of paper and the sheets and the flat box were on the table, and a man of about thirty-eight with a satchel came in at about the fourth hour and asked one question out loud and nobody in that room answered it.**

He had come up that lane twice a month for about nine years on that business and he had said nothing in that room in four months, and everybody in that room knew his face and not his voice, and the copyist of about twenty-six said afterwards that she had not heard him use that voice before and that she did not know what to do with a voice she had not heard before. Aren Kest, a man of thirty-two with a limp, was at the side table for the whole of it.

“**Is a rule still a rule when one man changed it in a hand?**” the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel said.

He said it to the man of about fifty-eight and he said it at about the fourth hour and he said it in front of the other eight and he did not put it any softer than that, and then he sat down on the end of the table with his satchel between his knees and waited.

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as a man takes to count four.

“**That is a fair question,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and I have got about four sentences and every one of them is a way of not answering it, and I am going to say the first one so that the room can hear what it sounds like, and then I am going to stop.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**I could say that the man who changed it is dead, and a dead man is not a rule, and therefore what is on the paper now is what was always on the paper.**” The man of about fifty-eight turned the strip of paper over and looked at the back of it. “**That is a good sentence. It is also a sentence that would let me tear up about ninety returns in this room on a Tuesday afternoon, because if a dead man is not a rule then about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month in another room are not rules either, and the second is easier for me and the first is easier for the man who asks.**”

“**Then say the second.**”

“**I could say that the answer is yes, because the rule is on the paper and the paper is what the clerks use, and a clerk does not care whose hand it was.**” He put the strip down. “**That is also a good sentence, and it is worse, because it lets me put a name on a form and know that the name is not doing anything, and that is what everybody in nine counties has been doing since the day the four heads were cut.**”

Nobody in that room argued with either sentence. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet said one thing about the second one and it was short.

“**A clerk does care,**” he said. “**A clerk cares about about four things, and one of them is whether the question is going to be asked again next month with a different head over it, and a hand that has been through a line is a hand that says no to that, and that is worth something to a man with a pen in a county two hundred miles off even if it is not worth a rule.**”

The man of about thirty-eight with a satchel said that he had not come up that lane to be given a sentence.

“**I asked whether it is a rule,**” he said, “**and I have been in this room four months and I have not seen anybody answer a question and I have seen a great many sentences, and I am not saying that anybody has been dishonest with me because nobody has, I am saying that I am about to go back down a made road and work in about nine counties and I would like to know one thing, and if the answer is that nobody knows then the answer is that nobody knows and I will carry that down the road instead of a rule and it will do the same work.**”

**And the room did not answer it.**

The man of about fifty-eight said that the room was not going to answer it and that he was not going to answer it either, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said the same, and the woman of about thirty-four said the same, and nobody said why and nobody was asked why, and the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel sat on the end of that table for about as long as a man takes to count nine and then said *all right* in two words and put his hands on his knees.

**And the copyist of about twenty-six said one sentence about it, and it was not about the fourth head, and it is the last thing she says in that run of days.**

“**I have ruled that head four thousand times,**” Nesta Braith said, “**and I have never once been told what it is for, and I am not going to say that again in this room, and I have said it once in another county and I have said it here and that is twice, and there is a man in this town who has a form with a stroke through a line on it and there is a word in nine counties that somebody said out loud on a Saturday, and neither of those two things has changed one single thing about what I do at the side table on a Tuesday, and I would like somebody in this room to decide whether that is a finding or whether it is a woman with a knife and a straightedge and no head over her table.**”

Nobody in that room said yes and nobody in that room said no, and she went back to the straightedge, and nobody thanked her, and the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel said thank you to her for the sentence and not for anything in it, and she said he was welcome and that it was the only thing she had said in four months that had cost her anything, and the man of about fifty-eight said that a thing which costs somebody something and is not written down is a thing that will be said again by somebody else and got wrong.

**And nobody in that room is a villain and nobody in that room is a keeper and the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel is on no sheet in nine counties and was not thanked for asking and did not ask to be.**

He went down the lane at about the eighth hour and the cart went at about the ninth hour with nine returns in it, and the four lines went under the return and went through about nine hands in a saddler's shop, and the man of about fifty-eight stood at the door and watched the cart go and said that a man who asks a question in a room and gets nothing is a man who will be asked by somebody else in about four years, and that whoever that somebody else is will not be a man in a hired room.

**And the thing that changed on that Tuesday was not a decision and not a rule and not a finding. It was that the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel had asked a question out loud in that room and about nine people had heard it and none of them had answered it, and that every one of them had then said something else, and that the seven of those somethings were now written on a bare leaf with no head over it, one under another, with no head and no ruling and no name at the foot.**

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote them and he did it at about the eighth hour, and the seventh of them is his own, and he put the leaf on the table and said that a leaf with seven things on it and no head over it was going to be the most useful object in that room and that nobody was to be allowed to say so.

**And none of the seven agreed with any of the other six, and the six did not hear themselves described on the leaf and were not asked whether the leaf was right.**

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a column and it is the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel's, and it is the eleventh one he has kept in about nine years, and a question nobody answered is not in it.**

**a box of quills at twelve, a ream of paper at twenty-seven, a bun at eight, a pot of paste at nineteen, a bundle of kindling at ten, a stone of tallow at fifteen, a jar of ink at seventeen, a night's bed at fourteen, and a bootlace at twenty-three.** Twelve, twenty-seven, eight, nineteen, ten, fifteen, seventeen, fourteen and twenty-three is a hundred and forty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and forty-five pence is three marks and one over**, and no question is in it, and four sentences are not in it, and four thousand lines are not in it, and of the three series kept in a room two hundred miles off, none of them is in this column and no two of them are added together here, and the one about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month is a count of rolls and the one about a hundred and four in a band is a count of sheets and the one about nine hundred sheets a month is a count of sheets, and three counts of three series is not a sum, and nine hands in a saddler's shop are not in it and were never a figure of persons on a form, and the nine in that room are not in it, and the two thousand of this town are not in it.
