# Chapter 0535

**The twenty-ninth of Hearthmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth, in an upper room over a tallow chandler's in a market town of about two thousand people, and the room had been hired for the day by a man who was not from the inquiry, and about four hundred people were in it, and the office was not in the room.**

The man of about fifty-eight had sent word on the Wednesday. He was not coming, and he had given the reason in nine words written out by the clerk of about twenty-two and read out at the door to anybody who asked, and the nine words were *this record is not what a badge can hold*. Nobody in the town argued with the nine words and about half the people in that room had not heard them at all, and neither of those two facts turned out to matter.

The notice had gone up in the market on the Saturday morning. It said that a record would be read out loud in an upper room over a tallow chandler's at the second hour on the Thursday, that anybody might come, and that the first page would be pasted to the door at the first hour so that a person could read it standing up. The first page had been up at the first hour, and there was a queue on the step outside it the whole of the time anybody up that lane cared to look, and people on that step said out loud that they had come to see about a form.

Hester Ollett was forty-one and she read out loud at the chapel at the end of a made lane and at the market cross on Thursdays, and she had been asked on the Monday whether she would read a man's record of himself in a room, and she had said she would read anything anybody asked her to read, and then asked what she was paid before she said yes, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet told her, and she took it.

“**I have read a will,**” she said, to nobody in particular, while she was arranging herself on a stool with the four pages squared on a table in front of her, “**and I have read a price of wool, and I have never in my life read a thing out loud that a man had written about himself, and I would like it said that I asked what I was paid before I said yes and not after.**”

“**It is said,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

Then she read it, and about four hundred people in an upper room over a tallow chandler's heard a man of thirty-two say what he had done at the second hour of the night six years ago and a little, in his own words, and they heard the sign at the foot of the third page described by a woman of about forty-four and not explained by anybody, and they heard the last of it read slowly, because the last of it is a list.

And about four hundred people who had come to hear a thing about a form were told a thing about a man.

It was not a shock. That is the part that has to be got right, and nobody in that room got it right while it was happening, and the man of about twenty-nine who keeps an inn nine doors from a weigh-house was not in that room, and nobody in that room has said since whether he heard it in the street.

It was a change of subject. The people who had come in expecting four heads and a fourth head that was empty went out holding a day and an hour and a crossing with about nine people on it, and what they did with that was not what anybody in the room had prepared them for. A woman near the door said *the fourth head* to a man beside her at about the third hour, in the voice of a person checking a thing, and the man beside her said *he put his own name on it*, and she said *there is not a name on a form*, and he said *there is not a form*, and neither of them was talking about the fourth head after that.

Nobody filled anything in. There was nothing to fill in and there was no form in the room and there was no clerk in the room and no press and no die and no badge, and the man of about fifty-eight had said on the Wednesday that if there was a form in that room the whole afternoon was worth nothing, and that he had said it as a man who has watched a thing go wrong and not as a man with an interest in it.

The change came at about the fifth hour, and it came out of the floor, and nobody in that room started it.

A man on the second step said, out loud, to nobody, *where is he*. That is all. It was not shouted and it was not a demand and it was not addressed to the room. And the answer came back out of the floor, because about four hundred people had all been holding the same four pages in their heads for three hours, and the answer that came back was a place, and it was wrong, and it was the room over a weigh-house two hundred miles off, which is a room that half the people in this town have heard about because a cart goes to it on Mondays.

Nobody in that room said the answer out loud again. The man of about thirty-four with a sheet stood up off the end of the table and said one thing, and it was short, and it was the only thing anybody said in the next nine minutes.

“**That is a room with a door on a lane and a woman in it and about four hundred returns in a drawer, and there is nobody in this county who can say what is in the drawer, and I want everybody in this room to hear the difference between a room and a road.**”

About four hundred people in that room heard it and about half of them believed him and about half of them had already decided that a man who puts his own name on a piece of paper does not get to tell a room where to look for him, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet knew both of those and sat down.

The reading finished at about the seventh hour and Hester Ollett read the fourth page last and slowly, because the fourth page is the one that is about the things he cannot answer, and the fourth page is a list, and the list has no head cut into the wood over it and is not in the room’s book and has not been copied into anything, and it says that he does not know where the fever came from and will not guess, that he does not know how many were behind the second buttress and has not asked, that he does not know what is at the foot of the sign and has not asked that either, that he does not know what the four hundred and nine parish rolls a month in another room are for, that he does not know whether the man who took a word off a cart six years ago and a little knew what he was doing, and that he is not going to be asked about any of the five in a room by anybody with a badge.

Nobody in that room thanked her, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet paid her, and what she was paid is not in her column, and she has never said whether it should be.

**Aren Kest was in the street outside that door from about the fourth hour to the seventh.** He had not gone up and he had not sent anybody and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet had seen him there and had not gone out to him, and about four hundred people went past him on that step in about three hours, and a woman with a basket said *that is him* to a man beside her and the man beside her said *I know, I was in there*, and neither of them stopped, and he stood on the other side of the door with his stick in his right hand and let them all go by, and he has not said anything about the three hours since and he was not asked about them.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is Hester Ollett's, and it is the first one she has ever kept, and four hundred people in an upper room is not in it.**

**a crust at two, a candle at six, a glass of water at nine, a bushel of coals at thirty-one, a room hired for the day at twenty-five, a man to open the door at fourteen, a loaf at nine, a sheet of paste at seventeen, a cord at eleven, a bowl of soup at fifteen, and a bootlace at seven.** Two, six, nine, thirty-one, twenty-five, fourteen, nine, seventeen, eleven, fifteen and seven is a hundred and forty-six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and forty-six pence is three marks and two over**, and the four hundred in that room is not in it, and the four hundred at the end of a made lane is not in it, and the four hundred that went under a second head of a return in Harvestmonth is not in it either, and the four hundred at the end of a made lane and that one are the same figure and are not the same figure, and about two thousand in this town is not in it, and about nine hundred on a coast four days off is not in it, and the five of those are five counts of five different things, none of the five is in this column, and no two of the five are added here.

The room emptied into the street at about the eighth hour in about as long as a market empties, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet walked down to the door and stood outside it and let about four hundred people go past him, and did not speak to any of them, and at about the ninth hour he shut the door of the chandler's and locked it and gave the key back, and did not find out what anybody had said in the street afterwards, and has not found out and has not asked.
