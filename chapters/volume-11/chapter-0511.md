# Chapter 0511

**The twenty-third day of Fallowmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the ninth hour, in a market town of about two thousand people four days up a made road from a coast where eleven houses stand along two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand, in a hired room over a saddler's, and a man of about twenty-nine who keeps an inn nine doors from a weigh-house came up the lane at about the first hour with two tally-sticks in his hands and put them down side by side on a table, and the two of them do not agree.**

## One

**The two sticks are the same length and out of the same wood and he has had them about six years, and he keeps one for the Saturday market and one for the Thursday market, and the reason he keeps two is that a Saturday is not a Thursday and that a week is not a market day, and he worked that out by himself inside the first year and has never once been told that it was right.**

On the Saturday before this one he cut **six hundred and eight** notches. On the Thursday morning of this day he cut **five hundred and seventy-one**. The six hundred and eight went into the first column of the first form that was ever filled in inside that room, under a head cut into a piece of wood in about four counties, and the head says a figure of people, and the clerk of about twenty-two wrote it there because a head says people and the man gave him a number, and nobody has taken it out and it is still on the paper.

**And the two numbers are two numbers of two different things, and neither of them is a week and neither of them is a town and neither of them is a road, and about four of the nine people in that room on that morning had already worked out the second half of that sentence and about five had not.**

The man of about twenty-nine with a tally-stick put his thumb on the head stick and said nothing for about as long as a man takes to say nine.

## Two

“**I have been counting them six years,**” the man of about twenty-nine with a tally-stick said, “**on a Saturday and on a Thursday, and I have never once been told what the number is for, and I did not ask in the first year because I did not think there was anybody in nine counties to ask, and then a form came out with a line on it that says a figure of people, and I gave you my number on the first day, and I have been up to that board every market morning since to see whether it wants a second one.**”

“**It does not,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and I am not going to invent one for you, and I have to tell you the other half of that, which is that I do not know what the first one was for either.**”

“**You are in the room.**”

“**I am in the room,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and I have been in it about six weeks, and I have asked twice, and both times I was told that it is a county matter, and I would rather stand here and say that I do not know than hand you a sentence I made up on a Thursday morning and have you cut a second stick for it.**”

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as a man takes to say four.

**And the whole of the first exchange before that is two hundred and three words in four turns, of which the first is ninety-five printed in two spans of seven and eighty-eight, the second is thirty-eight printed in two spans of three and thirty-five, the third is five, and the fourth is sixty-five printed in two spans of five and sixty.**

“**That is an answer,**” the man of about twenty-nine with a tally-stick said.

“**It is the only one I have got,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and I am going to write it on a leaf in that book in the same hand I write everything in, and it is going to say that the man of twenty-nine asked and the room does not know, and then the next man who comes up this lane with a number is going to read all of it, and he is not going to have to ask.**”

**And that is eighty words in two turns of one exchange, of which the first is four and the second is seventy-six printed in two spans of eight and sixty-eight, and a chairman of about fifty-eight, with a chain of office round his neck, wrote one line in the back of a book of carts and did not read it out, and the man of about twenty-nine with a tally-stick did not ask again and did not thank anybody, and nobody in that room was thanked and nobody in that room tried to be.**

## Three

“**Which of the two goes under the second head?**” the clerk of about twenty-two said, at about the fourth hour, with the form in front of him and the nib not yet in the ink.

“**Neither,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**I have to write something.**”

“**You have to write what the form asked for,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and the form asked for a figure of people on a road, and you have two figures of carts in a street, and the one on the paper already is the wrong thing, and I am not going to let a second wrong thing go under the same head in the same week because two wrong things are no better than one.**”

“**Then it goes on nothing.**”

“**Then it goes on a leaf,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**with no head over it, and if the leaf is full it goes on a second leaf with no head over it either, and I am the one who rules the leaf and not the copyist, because I want it to be the worst ruling in this room and not the best one.**”

“**You have never ruled anything in your life.**”

“**No,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and it will show.**”

**And that is one hundred and sixty-three words in eight turns of one exchange, of which the first is nine, the second is one, the third is five, the fourth is seventy-one printed in two spans of nine and sixty-two, the fifth is five, the sixth is fifty-nine printed in two spans of six and fifty-three, the seventh is eight, and the eighth is five printed in two spans of one and four, and a chairman of about fifty-eight with a chain of office round his neck ruled a line with a knife and a straightedge in about four minutes in front of about nine people, and about four of the nine looked at the ruling and about five looked at the man, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not look at either and went on with what she was doing, which was a third copy of a return that has a word under a first head that four people read as a place.**

## Four

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was in that room at the fourth hour and at the ninth hour and was not asked anything at either of them, and the sentence he said was not said to anybody in particular, and it is twenty-three words long and it is the only thing he said in that room.**

Aren Kest has read about four hundred and nine returns in two days and not one of them carries two figures under one head. He said that where a return has two numbers against it, one of the two got chosen somewhere, and the place where it got chosen is not on the sheet, and the sheet is what goes up the road.

“**In about four years,**” he said, “**somebody is going to send nine counties of these up a made road in a cart, and a man is going to choose between two numbers without knowing he is choosing, and then the county that lost will be wrong on paper for the rest of time and the county that won will never find out that it won.**”

**And that is sixty-three words in one turn, printed in two spans of four and fifty-nine, and it is the longest thing that has been said in that room by anybody who was not on a road, and about four of the nine people in that room will tell you it was the most useful thing said in the room in a month, and about five have said that a man who reads four hundred returns in two days and does not sleep is not a man to take a rule from, and the two sets of four and five do not overlap at all.**

**And the two sticks went down the lane with the man who cut them, and the six hundred and eight is on a form in this town and the five hundred and seventy-one is on a leaf with no head over it, and the leaf is in the back of a book of carts, and the two of them are a figure of carts and are not a figure of people and are not a figure of a road and are not added to each other or to about four hundred at the end of a made lane or to about nine hundred on a sand four days off.**

## Five

The cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's, and it is the sixth one he has kept since the third year he kept the inn, and two sticks of tally wood are not in it and the two figures on them are not in it.

**chalk for the tally at one, a second nail at one, a second leather tie at two, a second taproom lamp's oil at one, string for the bundle of sticks at two, a second splinter at one, a splinter for the fire at one, a second broom at five, a fourth pound of candles at four, a broom at five, oil for the taproom lamp at one, a nail for the tally board at one, a second night's bed at four, a second pot of tea at three, a night's bed at four, a second string at two, a ream of paper at nine, a leather tie for the stick at two, a second rushlight at one, a third pound of candles at four, cheese at four, a bundle of kindling at two, a pot of tea at three, a bucket of tar at eight, a rushlight at one, a pound of candles at four, a second pound of candles at four, and a second stick of chalk at one.** One, one, two, one, two, one, one, five, four, five, one, one, four, three, four, two, nine, two, one, four, four, two, three, eight, one, four, four and one is eighty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-one pence is a mark and thirty-three over**, and five hundred and seventy-one is not in it, and six hundred and eight is not in it, and the two of them are two counts of two different mornings and are not added, and a head cut into a piece of wood about four counties away is not in it, and a leaf with no head over it is not in it, and the four hundred and nine is not in it.

**And on the twenty-third day of Fallowmonth in this year, which is a Thursday and a market day, a man of about twenty-nine who keeps an inn put two tally-sticks on a table in a hired room over a saddler's and found out that six years of his life have two numbers in them, and an office that has been collecting returns for about four months told him the truth, which is that it does not know either, and wrote it on a leaf in a book of carts so that the next man does not have to ask, and the two numbers went down the lane with him, and the tide decides twice a day and is not paid and is not on any of this.**
