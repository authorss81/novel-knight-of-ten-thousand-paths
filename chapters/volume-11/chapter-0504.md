# Chapter 0504

**The sixth day of Harvestmonth, YR 319, a Saturday and a market day, and the first hour and the third hour and the seventh hour, in a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people four days up a made road from a coast, and about nine people are in that room and about four hundred are in that town, and the thing that is being sat in that room has no name on the notice that brought anybody there, and the notice is on a board outside a weigh-house in the same town and is a different piece of paper from anything in the room.**

## One

**The room over the saddler's has four windows, two of which have been nailed shut since before the town had a market on this street, and a table, and nine chairs, and about nine people in it, and the four heads on the form on the table are the same four heads that are on the sheet nailed to a board outside a weigh-house in this town, and the man of about fifty-eight in the room with a chain of office round his neck read them out loud to the room and the fourth of them took him about as long to say as the other three put together.**

The chain of office is a badge. It is a piece of stamped metal on a leather strap and it has no seal on it and no office on it and it is the only thing in that room that says anybody is in charge, and about four of the nine people in that room have said since Saturday that they did not know until they saw it whether the man wearing it was in charge.

Two clerks sit at the end of the table. One of them is a man of about twenty-two and has a book of his own that is not the room's book, and the other is a woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years and who has not been asked to copy anything since the second hour.

A copyist of about twenty-six is at a side table with a straightedge and a knife and a pot of paste, and her name is Nesta Braith, and she is not one of the two clerks and does not sit at the table and has not been introduced to the room.

**And at the third hour she ruled a form. Four heads, four lines, the same knife, the same rule, the same pressure, and the fourth line came out the same width as the other three, and about nine people in that room watched a woman of about twenty-six rule an empty line and about four of the nine looked at it and about five did not.**

## Two

“**What is the fourth head for?**” the man of about fifty-eight said, at about the third hour, to the room and not to anybody in it.

About nine people were in that room and about four of the nine had a copy of the form in front of them and about five did not, and the question stood for about as long as it takes a man to say nine, and the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel, who had come up from the weigh-house board and who is in that town about twice a month on that business, looked at his own copy and did not say a word.

“**Nobody knows,**” Nesta Braith said, from the side table, without getting up, and she said it to the form and not to the man.

“**I did not ask you,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**I know you did not ask me,**” Nesta Braith said, “**and that is why I answered, because if you had asked me I would have had to stand up and say it, and I have said it in another room once and I am not saying it twice standing up.**”

The man of about fifty-eight with the chain of office looked at the side table for about as long as it takes a man to say four.

“**Say what you said in the other room,**” he said.

“**No,**” Nesta Braith said, and went back to the straightedge.

**And that is sixty-nine words in six turns of one exchange, of which the first is six, the second is two, the third is five, the fourth is forty-seven printed in two spans of seven and forty, the fifth is eight, and the sixth is one, and a woman of about twenty-six refused to say a thing twice in two counties in a month and said it once in one of them without being asked, and the man of about fifty-eight did not ask her a second time and did not press it and about four of the nine people in that room have said since Saturday that he should have, and about five of the nine have said that he could not have, and the two sets of four and five do not overlap at all.**

## Three

**And the first figure a person put into the first column of the first form in that room was a figure of carts, and a man of about twenty-nine who keeps an inn in that street gave it, and it is a good figure and it is a figure of the wrong thing, and the clerk wrote it in under a head that says people, and nobody in that room knew it was wrong for about four minutes.**

The man of about twenty-nine keeps an inn nine doors from the weigh-house and has kept it for about six years, and he keeps a tally-stick because his father kept one, and on a market morning he cuts a notch for every cart that comes into that street and turns round and goes out again, and on the Saturday before this one he cut **six hundred and eight** notches, and he said the number without being asked and without looking at the stick, because he has said it to himself every market morning for six years.

“**Six hundred and eight,**” the man of about twenty-nine with a tally-stick said.

The clerk of about twenty-two wrote it in. The clerk wrote it under the second head, which is the head that says **a figure of people.**

“**That is carts,**” a woman of about thirty-four who had come in off the street said, and she was about two minutes late and she had not heard the question.

“**It is what comes down that street on a market morning,**” the man of about twenty-nine with a tally-stick said. “**There are about two thousand people in this town and about nine of the market carts go to four different roads and the rest go nowhere in particular, and I have never once pretended that a cart is a person, and if the head wants carts I will give you carts all morning and I will be glad to.**”

“**The head wants people,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**Then you have a town of two thousand people and not a figure of six hundred and eight,**” the woman of about thirty-four said, “**and I do not know which of those two things is on that form, and neither does he, and if you leave the six hundred and eight on the paper it will be in a book in a year.**”

**And that is one hundred and thirty-eight words in five turns of one exchange, of which the first is four, the second is three, the third is seventy printed in two spans of eleven and fifty-nine, the fourth is four, and the fifth is fifty-seven printed in two spans of eighteen and thirty-nine, and the clerk of about twenty-two did not rub it out and did not write anything else under that head, and the six hundred and eight is on the first form in that room and is a figure of carts and is still on the paper, and about nine people in that room have said since Saturday that the woman of about thirty-four was right and about four have said that a form is a form and is not a place for an argument.**

## Four

**And at about the fifth hour the clerk of about twenty-two began a second book, and nobody told him to, and the second book has the same four heads on its first leaf and the fourth of them is empty, and about four people in that room have said since Saturday that he copied the heads off the form because he had nothing else to copy and about five have said that he copied them off a form from another county that he had in his own book, and he has never said which.**

The book of the man of about twenty-two is his own and not the room's. It is a book of about two hundred leaves and about nine of them have something on them, and eight of the nine are figures of carts on market mornings in this town in a season, and the ninth is a list of the four saddles in that street.

**Two counts of two different things are not added. Eight market mornings of carts and about four thousand nine hundred souls in a town of about two thousand people are two counts of two different things, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month come in at a room two hundred miles off and are a third, and about a hundred and four sheets go out of that room in one band on a fixed day and are a fourth, and none of the four is derived from another and this room has not derived one from another.**

## Five

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the man of about fifty-eight's, and it is an office column and not a man's, and it is the first of about nine hundred a year, and a figure of carts and a figure of people are not in it and are not added to each other anywhere.

**a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a fourth sheet of paper at one, a fifth sheet of paper at one, paste at two, a second paste at two, a pot for the paste at five, a quill and ink at three, a knife at two, a rule at four, bread at one, a second bread at one, a third bread at one, cheese at four, a second cheese at four, small beer at two, a second small beer at two, a broom at five, a second broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a second bucket of tar at eight, a night's bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a bootlace at one, a waxed cord at four, a second pot for the paste at five, an inkwell at three, a farthing at one, a second farthing at one, a third farthing at one, a fourth farthing at one, and a fifth farthing at one.** one, one, one, one, one, two, two, five, three, two, four, one, one, one, four, four, two, two, five, five, eight, eight, four, four, one, four, five, three, one, one, one, one, and one is ninety pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and ninety pence is a mark and forty-two over**, and six hundred and eight is not in it and is a figure of carts and not of people, and about two thousand people and about four hundred in a town are not in it and are not the same figure, and four heads are not in it, and an empty fourth line is not in it.

**And on the sixth day of Harvestmonth in this year, which is a Saturday and a market day, a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people sat for the first time, and four heads were read out loud and the fourth of them was empty, and a woman of about twenty-six ruled it and would not say a thing twice, and a man of about twenty-nine gave a figure of six hundred and eight carts and it went into a column with a head on it that says people, and it is still on the paper, and about nine people were in that room and about four hundred were in that town, and the two figures are not added, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of a room two hundred miles off with four heads on them and the fourth one empty, and the road that the six hundred and eight carts partly go down is not a toll road and has no rate printed on it in nine counties.**
