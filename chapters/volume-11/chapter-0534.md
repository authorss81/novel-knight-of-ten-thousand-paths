# Chapter 0534

**A market was going on in the street of a market town of about two thousand people on the twenty-second of Hearthmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and in a hired room over a saddler's a man of thirty-two with a limp read out one page of his own record at about the second hour, and it was the only page in the whole of it that he read himself, and about nine people were in that room and one of them was not thanked for asking a question that was about a date and not about a road.**

The first two pages had gone into other mouths on Wednesday and on Saturday, the first of them read back by a woman of about forty-four in the voice she uses for a form and the second of them read out by a man of about thirty-four with a sheet in the same voice, and neither of those had been his own voice, and he had let both of them go.

The third page he kept.

It is a copy, and it is a copy of a thing that is not in this county and is not in this town, and the thing itself is a piece of paper about the size of a form with something on the bottom of it that nobody in that room had seen before and that nobody in that room was shown. On the copy there is a sign, made with a pen and pressed hard, that does not spell anything and is not a hand, and above the sign there is a day written out in full, in a hand of about six years ago that the woman of about forty-four said she could pick out of a dozen sheets without being told which one of them it was.

Aren Kest read the copy out. It is one page and most of it is a sentence saying what the thing is not: it is not a form, it is not a return, it is not a copy of a form, it is not in any room in this county, and the sentence under that says that he is not going to say what the sign at the foot of it is, because he does not know, and because a record of what a man did is not an explanation of anything.

Then he read the last four lines, and they are these, and he read them in a voice about nine people in that room had not heard from him before and that the copyist of about twenty-six put down afterwards as a voice with no practice in it.

*My mother made a crossing. It was on the day written above and not on any day I can find out. I was not there. I have not asked her, and I have not said any of this out loud before.*

Nobody in that room said anything at all for about as long as a man takes to count nine, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet did not write it down, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not write it down either, and both of them were asked about it afterwards by people who were not in that room and both of them said the same thing, which was that the paper had a head on it and a name in the head and that was where it was.

Then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet asked the question he had come to ask, and it was not about a road.

“**Whose hand wrote the day?**”

“**Mine,**” Aren Kest said. “**Six years ago and a little, and I wrote it the same week, and I have not looked at the paper since until I copied it out for this.**”

“**Then that is not the day,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**that is the day you found out, and you have written it in a record as though it were the other thing.**”

“**I have written it as the day I wrote it,**” Aren Kest said, “**and it is the day I wrote it, and I have said on the page that it is not the day she crossed, and I have said on the page that I have not asked her which day it was, and I have said on the page that I am not going to.**”

Nobody argued with him about that, and nobody improved on it, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote on a bare leaf with no head over it that the day on the page is the day it was written on and is not the day of the thing, and read it back, and asked him one more thing, which was whether he wanted the leaf kept with the record or kept apart from it, and he said apart, and it was kept apart, and it is not with the record.

The copyist of about twenty-six asked the one question that day that was about the sign, and it was asked to the woman of about forty-four and not to the man.

“**Is that a hand?**”

“**It is a pen pressed hard and it is not a hand,**” the woman of about forty-four said, “**and I have been copying for about nineteen years and I could not tell you what made it and neither could anybody in this room, and the record does not say, on purpose, and the man who wrote the record has told me in the last four days that he is not going to find out in a room either, and I have written that down.**”

“**Then a person can be shown a piece of paper that says somebody crossed,**” the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key said, from the door end, “**and there is nothing on the paper about how, and there is nothing on the paper about whether it can be done again, and there is nothing on the paper that would let anybody find the place, and every one of those three things is a reason the paper is safe and every one of them is a reason the paper is worthless, and I do not know which of the two we are looking at and neither does he.**”

Nobody answered her, and the man of about fifty-eight said that the room was not going to answer her, and she said she had not expected it to, and he said he knew she had not, and the two of them had said the same thing by not saying it and neither of them was thanked.

Then the man of about fifty-one in a grey coat said the thing that the page was for, and he said it in a flat voice and he said it about himself and not about the man at the table.

“**In about four years somebody is going to read that page and ask whether the sign at the foot of it opens a road. And if it does, then this is the first document in nine counties that has a door in it, and the man who put it in a record is the man who wrote the door down. And I do not know whether it opens anything, and neither does he, and neither does anybody in this room, and that is the only reason I am going to let it stay in a public record.**”

Nobody argued with that either, and the copyist of about twenty-six wrote it on the same bare leaf as the day and ruled nothing over either thing.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man of about fifty-eight’s, and it is the third one he has kept in that room, and a page with a sign at the foot of it is not in it.**

**a pot of tea at seven, a broom at twenty-one, a rule of brass at thirteen, a ream of paper at twenty-nine, a bundle of kindling at nine, a jar of paste at seventeen, a night’s bed at eleven, a half-pound of candles at six, a bootlace at three, and a bit of whiting at ten.** Seven, twenty-one, thirteen, twenty-nine, nine, seventeen, eleven, six, three and ten is a hundred and twenty-six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and twenty-six pence is two marks and thirty over**, and the sign is not in it, and the day is not in it, and the copy is not in it, and the thing the copy was made from is not in it and is not in this county, and a woman is not in it, and a crossing is not in it and no crossing was made and none is booked and nothing was cut, and the years since are not in it, and thirty-one nights still stand against five roads and thirty-four on a chalk board over a yard stair two hundred miles off and the difference of three is not in it and has not been found, and the notebook in his coat has eleven lines on it and no twelfth and did not come out of his coat that day.

The market went on until the sixth hour. The page was put with the other three and the four of them went under the flat weight with the returns, and the man of about fifty-eight did not lock the press-corner, and did not copy the page into the room’s book, and said out loud, to nobody, that he was the man who had said a thing should be written down so that it could be shown, and that a page like that one was going to be shown, and that he had better be the one who said so while there was still somebody in the room who had not read it.
