# Chapter 0545

**The twentieth of Thawmonth, YR 320, a Saturday and a market day, and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, up a lane that is made for about a mile and a half from the edge of a market town of about two thousand people, and then past a rail of a common and down to eleven houses and a chapel, and a man of thirty-two with a limp walked the whole of it with nothing in his hand and nobody asked him anything for about four weeks, and he got to the end of the made surface and stood there and did not go on.**

The made lane is made for about a mile and a half and then it is not made, and the place where it stops being made is not a gate and not a stile and not a boundary stone and nobody has ever put a mark on it, and it is about as far from the eleventh of eleven houses as a man takes to walk without hurrying, and a person who lives at the end of that lane walks over it every day and does not notice it.

He stood at it for about as long as a man takes to say nine, with his stick in his right hand and his right leg out at the angle it goes out at when he has been standing a while, and there was nothing in front of him and there was a cart road about two hundred paces on that is on no sheet in nine counties and has a name on it that about four people use and about five do not.

**And he did not go on, and nothing happened, and no crossing was made and none was booked, and nobody in nine counties knows he stood there except the woman of about twenty-seven at the eleventh house, who came out of her door with a bucket and saw him and went back in.**

Nobody sent anybody. Nobody was sent. At the foot of a return, in the same ink as the rest of the printing, there is a line that says nobody is to be sent to find out, and there is a shut road four hundred miles off that is not reopened and is not scheduled and nobody has been sent to it, and about nine people are on the wrong side of it and the word is not used about it by anybody and has not been used about anything at all in this county in five months.

He walked back up the mile and a half in the afternoon. The clerk of about thirty-four was at the rail of the common at about the sixth hour with a sheet in his hand and no straightedge and he did not ask the man of thirty-two a question and the man of thirty-two did not give him one, and half the people who were in that lane say the two of them did not speak and the other half say that two men who have said nothing to each other in a lane for about nine years cannot be expected to start on a Saturday in Thawmonth, and neither of those is a fact about either of them and both of them are facts about the lane.

The man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote one sentence on a bare leaf with no head over it that evening and did not read it back, and the sentence is on a bare leaf and he has not told anybody in nine counties what it is, and the man of about thirty-four has not said the sentence to the man of thirty-two and was not asked to and did not offer it.

**And what the edge cost him is the page, and the page is the whole of the cost, and it is a thing that happened in about four seconds and nobody in that lane saw it and nobody in nine counties has been told.**

He could have gone on. That is the only part of it anybody can say. The two hundred paces of cart road are not shut and are not scheduled to be shut and are not on any form in nine counties and nobody has said they are not to be walked, and a man of thirty-two with a limp who can get up a cart road has nothing in nine counties to stop him and about a day and a half of road between that point and a coast, and a boat, and eleven houses and two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and about nine hundred people on it, and at the seaward end of that sand a woman of about fifty-one is at a post with a piece of driftwood in her hand and twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and the twenty-second is not cut.

**And what is on the other side of the edge is not a place and it is not a road and it is not a crossing and nobody in nine counties has ever said what is there, and nobody in that county is going to say, and there is no word anybody in that county has for what a man can do that the man of about fifty-three with a barrow can also do with about nine years and a barrow and a made lane.**

What he gave up at that point was the page. That is the whole of the price and it is a strange price for a thing nobody saw and it is the correct one.

**A man in a room in a path and not in this one is a man who can be asked a different question in each, and a man who can be asked a different question in each is a man who cannot be shown a page, because a page is a thing with one man's name at the top of it and a man who is in two rooms at once is a man whose page is a thing about two men, and a thing about two men is a thing nobody can be shown.** The record is four pages and a head and it is true and it is in public and it has been read out loud once to about four hundred people in an upper room over a tallow chandler's, and a copy of its first page is on a board in a saddler's shop in this town and has gone down a made road, and there is one of those copies in about four hundred kitchens at the end of a lane a mile and a half from where he was standing.

He kept the page. That is what the edge cost and it is the only thing it cost and he paid it in about four seconds on a Saturday in Thawmonth with his stick in his right hand.

**And about four hundred people who came to hear that record read out loud say he is the reason the rest of it stands up, and about four hundred say he is the reason anybody should have believed any of it in the first place, and the two sets do not overlap, and the man of thirty-four with a sheet wrote both on one bare leaf in Goatmonth and ruled nothing over either and refused to count either, and about four weeks later that leaf is still on the table at the side table in a hired room over a saddler's and nobody has turned it over and nobody in that room has asked him which of the two he thinks is right, and he has not been asked and has not said.**

He was asked nothing at all on that Saturday. Nobody in that lane put a question to him about a form and nobody put a question to him about a rule and nobody put a question to him about the fourth head, and the woman of about twenty-three — there is no such person at the end of that lane and there is a woman of about twenty-seven and a woman of about sixty-three and a man of about seventy, and none of the three said anything to him.

The woman of about twenty-seven opened her door about two paces wide and said one thing to the lane and not to him, and shut it again before he was out of earshot.

“**The cart road behind the chapel has been under water twice since the snow went and once before that, and it is not on any paper anywhere and it is not a made road and nobody has been to see whether a cart goes along it, and I am not asking you to go and see, because a person who goes and sees is a person somebody sent.**”

**And she shut her door and was not thanked and did not ask to be thanked, and the man of thirty-two with a limp did not answer her and was not asked to and did not go and see, and a lane keeps a version of a road the way a room keeps a finding, and the two of those are not added to each other and neither of them is a figure of persons.**

He said two things in the whole day and both of them were said to nobody. The first was the name of a road he has not been on in about four years. The second was *no*, and nothing had asked him anything, and the clerk of about thirty-four heard the second of them from about nine feet away and has not written it down and has not asked him about it.

**And nobody in that lane knows what he stood at, and half of them say he came down that lane for a reason and half say he came down it because there was nowhere else, and neither of those is a fact about the man and both of them are on the page because a lane keeps a version.**

**And the cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the man of thirty-two's, and it is the tenth one he has kept since the twenty-fifth of a month carried at thirty in the year before the year before this one, and the page is not in it and the edge is not in it and neither of them has a price.**

**a night's straw at two, a bit of chalk at four, a sheet of oiled paper at twelve, a stone of tallow at fifteen, a third pound of candles at twenty-six, a second broom at thirty, a ream of paper at thirty-two, and a case of quills at thirty-four.** Two, four, twelve, fifteen, twenty-six, thirty, thirty-two and thirty-four is a hundred and fifty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and fifty-five pence is three marks and eleven over**, and a mile and a half is not in it and is not a figure of persons, and the two hundred paces beyond the end of the made lane are not in it, and about a day and a half of road is not in it, and the eleven houses and the two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand are not in it and are not the eleven of a lane in a town four days the other way, and about nine hundred people on that sand are not in it and about four hundred people at the end of this lane are not in it and those are two counts of two different things and go in at neither end of this column, and the twenty-two notches and the twenty-two years are one figure told two ways and neither of them is in this column, and the four hundred miles of a shut road is not in it and about nine people are on the wrong side of it and neither of those is in it, and two hundred miles off, one room keeps three tallies that have nothing to do with each other: about nine hundred sheets a month go out of it, about a hundred and four go out in a single band, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come in. None of the three is a price in this column and no two of them are put together here, and the two counts of his own life do not go into it: thirty-one nights set against five roads, and thirty-four on a chalk board over a yard stair two hundred miles from here, and the three between them is not in this column and is still not found. The notebook stayed in his coat. It has eleven lines on it and one word on the eleventh and no twelfth line.

He came back up the mile and a half at about the seventh hour and went into the room over the saddler's at about the eighth, and the room was not shut because it is a Saturday and the room is shut on Sundays, and the floor of that room has not been washed since the eighth of Wolfmonth and was not washed on that Saturday and nobody in that room said anything about the floor, and the man of about fifty-eight was at the head of the table and the man of about thirty-two sat down at the wall side with his stick against the leg and nobody made room for him and nobody said anything to him for about as long as a man takes to say four, and then the man of about thirty-eight with a satchel said *nothing about your Saturday* and Aren Kest said *nothing about your nine years*, and neither of them said one word about either of those afterwards and both of them are on the page because a room hears a thing.
