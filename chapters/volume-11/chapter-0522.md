# Chapter 0522

**The eighteenth of Wolfmonth, YR 319, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people, and about nine people were in the room and the sheet from the last Saturday was on the table by the door where the man of about sixty-three had left it, and the woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years laid the second copy beside it about two inches off and said one word.**

She said it standing up, at about the fourth hour, and she said it to the two sheets and not to anybody in the room.

“**Line.**”

The man of about fifty-eight said he did not know which line.

“**The one on the left has got a line where the other has not got anything,**” the woman of about forty-four said, “**and the one on the left is the one you have been looking at for four months, and the one on the right is the one that came out of the chest on Saturday, and I have been copying for about nineteen years and I put them side by side about two inches off because about a sixteenth of an inch is nothing to a pair of eyes and two inches is everything.**”

Nobody said anything for about as long as a woman takes to say four.

**And it is a ruled line and there is a stroke through it.** The stroke is not the rule and it is not the weight of the rule and it is not in the same ink; it is a single line drawn through the ruled line from above the left-hand edge to below the right-hand place, and it goes past the rule at both ends, and whoever made it was not trying to rule anything and was not trying to write anything and was doing one thing with a pen, which is putting a stroke through a line because a line ought not to be there.

“**A stroke through a line is not a line,**” the woman of about forty-four said. “**It is a stroke through a line, and I have copied about four hundred of them in nineteen years and I have never once been asked what one of them meant, and I am telling you that I know the difference between a thing that is there and a thing that somebody has been through, and the difference is the whole of my trade and nobody has ever paid me for it.**”

“**Then what is it?**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**It is a line that somebody asked about,**” the woman of about forty-four said.

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as a man takes to say nine, and then the man of about fifty-eight said the sentence that the rest of that Wednesday was built out of, and he said it slowly, and he said it to the sheets.

“**A form that goes out with a struck-out line on it has told somebody something,**” the man of about fifty-eight said. “**Not the man who fills it in. He will not see it. A clerk in four years will see it. It has told him that somebody was asked, and that somebody said no, and that the asking and the no are both older than the form he is holding. It is the only sentence on that paper that is not a question, and I have had about ninety returns in this room in four months and there is not one instruction on any of them that says what to do with a line somebody has been through, and the reason there is not one is that everybody in nine counties has assumed the answer and the assumption is that a line somebody has been through is a line somebody was told to leave alone.**”

“**That is what I would have done,**” the clerk of about twenty-two said.

“**That is what everybody would have done,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and I have done it myself twice this month, and one of the two was on a sheet that came in a bundle from a county two hundred miles off and the other was on a bare leaf with no head over it, and I did not write it down, and the reason I did not write it down is that I did not want a head over it.**”

The copyist of about twenty-six looked at the stroke for a while and then said one thing about it, and what she said was not about the fourth head.

“**I would have left it,**” Nesta Braith said, “**and I have left about nine of them in four years and four counties and none of the nine is in a book, and if you rule a head over one of them then you have decided what it was, and I do not know what any of the nine was, and neither do you.**”

Nobody thanked her and nobody agreed with her out loud and she went on with the straightedge.

**And the man of about thirty-four with a sheet came in at about the seventh hour and said two things, and the room asked him one question between the two of them and he answered it, and both of the two were about whoever made the stroke and neither of them was about the stroke.**

“**He did not rule it,**” the man of about thirty-four said. “**He could not. There is no head over it. You cannot rule a line under a head that is not there and get a fifth line; you get a mark in the middle of a form, and a mark in the middle of a form is a thing a man does with a pen and a reason, and the reason is the only thing on that paper anybody can be got to swear to.**”

“**What reason?**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**That he did not want the question asked,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and he did not want it asked badly, and there is a difference between a man who cuts a head out of a form and a man who goes through a line with a pen, and the second one is a slower, smaller, more careful act and he did it in a hand and he did not sign it.**”

Nobody in that room argued with him, and going through a line with a pen is the more careful of the two acts and it is also the more dangerous of the two acts, and the reason is that a cut is a cut and a stroke can be mistaken for a rule, and about four of the nine people in that room had said the first of those and about five had said the second and the two sets do not overlap.

**And nobody in that room filled the struck-out line, and nobody in that room can say that anybody has.** The line is ruled and there is a stroke through it and there is nothing on it and it was nothing on it before the stroke went through it, and a man of about fifty-three with a barrow, who had come up off the common and had put his hand on the table at about the fourth hour and had not taken it off again, said the thing about the line that the rest of that room did not say for another four days.

“**If you fill that in,**” the man of about fifty-three said, “**then whatever you put in it is a thing a man who was dead a hundred and eighty years ago said no to, and you will have put it in for him, and there is nobody here to be asked whether he would have minded.**”

The woman of about forty-four said that was the first time in nineteen years anybody in a room had said a thing about a struck-out line that was not a question, and she did not thank him and he did not ask to be thanked.

**Aren Kest, a man of thirty-two with a limp, was in that room from the fourth hour and did not say a word for about nine minutes, and then he said one, and it was the plainest thing anybody said in that room that day.**

“**A line somebody has been through is the only kind of mark on a form that means somebody was in the room,**” Aren Kest said. “**Every other mark on a piece of paper can be put there by a man who was not there. That one cannot.**”

The man of about fifty-eight said that was the second time in two days that a person in that room had said a thing in fewer words than it took to argue about, and that he was not going to argue about it, and the copyist of about twenty-six said that a thing which cannot be said twice is not a rule and is not a finding and is a line somebody drew.

**And the cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is the woman of about forty-four's, and it is the first one she has ever kept, and she keeps it on the back of the leaf with no head over it, and a line with a stroke through it is not in it.**

**a bundle of kindling at nineteen, a pot of paste at fifteen, a pound of candles at thirteen, a night's bed at eleven, a broom at nine, a stone of tallow at seven, a cheese at six, a bun at five, a bootlace at four, and a farthing at three.** Nineteen, fifteen, thirteen, eleven, nine, seven, six, five, four and three is ninety-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and ninety-two pence is a mark and forty-four over**, and it carries no stroke, and a stroke is neither a line nor a figure of anything, and the two inches between two sheets of paper are a distance on a table and are not a figure of distance in the world, and the hundred and eighty years are not in it, and the four hundred who live at the end of the made lane are not in it, and the nine hundred on that sand are not in it, and one is not added to the other anywhere in this.

The woman of about forty-four folded the two sheets together on the table by the door, put the fold at the top, and put the pair under the leaf with no head over it, and nobody in that room asked her to and nobody in that room stopped her, and the fold is the same fold on both and the two of them will come apart along it in about nine seconds if anybody ever wants them to.
