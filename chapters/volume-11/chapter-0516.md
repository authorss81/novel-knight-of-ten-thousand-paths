# Chapter 0516

**The thirtieth day of Embermonth, YR 319, a Saturday and a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, on a made lane about a mile and a half long that goes out of a street of a market town of about two thousand people past a common and down to eleven houses and a chapel, and a clerk of about thirty-four who has walked about nine roads in this county in eleven years is walking it with a sheet in his hand, and the sheet says at the bottom in the same ink as everything else on it that nobody is to be sent to find out, and he is being sent.**

## One

**The form asks for the name of the road as the people who live at the end of it call it. That is the sentence under the first head and it has been there about four months and it is the only sentence on the sheet that anybody has ever read twice.**

A man of about forty-one with a cart filled the first of these in on a Wednesday in Harvestmonth at a table by the door in about four minutes. He had walked about half of the lane on the Tuesday and had come back with a figure of about four hundred and a third head with four trades on it, **and he had written under the first head the word that about nine people in this county use for that lane, and the people who live at the end of it do not use it, and none of them was asked, and the second head says about four hundred and about four hundred people live at the end of it and those two about four hundreds are not added to each other.**

A clerk of about thirty-four is on that lane at about the fourth hour on a Saturday with a sheet, and he has about two hours of daylight and about a mile and a half of lane, and about four hundred people live at the end of it and the fourth house has a man of about seventy in it who was in his bed on the Tuesday in Harvestmonth, and none of this is on the sheet.

**Aren Kest is at the rail of the common and has been since the first hour and has not gone down the lane, and he is not on the sheet and nobody asked him to go, and he has said out loud that if he goes down it then the lane has been walked by a man of thirty-two as well as by a clerk of about thirty-four and that is two men where the form asked for one.**

## Two

**At the landward end, where the lane comes off the street and turns past the rail of the common, four people said the lane is called the Green Lane, and three of the four said it without being asked and the fourth said it after being asked, and nobody at the landward end said anything else about it.**

**At the eleventh of eleven houses, where the lane comes out on the chapel and there is nowhere else for it to go, six people said the lane is called the Long Lane, and five of the six said it without being asked, and the sixth is a woman of about twenty-seven who came down the front of the eleventh house to say it and who had walked up to a hired room over a saddler's in Harvestmonth with a copy of a return in her hand.**

Both of those are people who live at the end of it. That is the sentence on the form, and the form asked it correctly, and a man of about forty-one with a cart answered it with the name he had already got.

“**The Green Lane,**” the man of about thirty-four said, at the rail of the common, to a man of about fifty-three who was loading a barrow.

“**The Long Lane,**” the man of about fifty-three said.

“**It is the Green Lane,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and it is written on a sheet in a room in this town under a head that says the name of the road as the people who live at the end of it call it.**”

“**Then somebody has written it wrong,**” the man of about fifty-three said.

“**No,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**somebody has written it right, and about four miles back it is called the Green Lane by everybody who has to say it, and it is the same lane and the two names are on the same lane and the form has one line.**”

**And that is ninety-seven words in five turns of one exchange, of which the first is three, the second is three, the third is forty printed in two spans of five and thirty-five, the fourth is six, and the fifth is forty-five printed in two spans of one and forty-four, and a man of about thirty-four has now walked one end of a lane and been told by a man of about fifty-three that somebody has written it wrong, and he has said no, and he is right, and about four hundred people live at that lane's other end and about nine of them use the second name.**

## Three

The woman of about twenty-seven was at the eleventh of eleven houses and she had seen him coming down the lane at about the fourth hour because she had been watching the lane since about the second hour, and she did not ask him anything and he did not ask her anything.

“**You are the one they sent,**” she said.

“**I am the one they sent,**” the man of about thirty-four said, “**and the sheet says at the bottom that nobody is to be sent to find out, and I have read that sentence out loud four times in a room in about four months, and I came anyway, and nobody made me, and I would rather say that first than anything else. At the rail of your common a man of about fifty-three with a barrow called this lane the Green Lane, and I called it the Green Lane, and that is the word on the sheet under a head that says the name of the road as the people who live at the end of it call it, and there are six people at this end of it who have never once used it.**”

“**I am not going to say anything else,**” the woman of about twenty-seven said, “**and I told that room in Harvestmonth that I was not angry about the counting and I am not angry about that either, and what I am angry about is a line.**”

“**I know which line.**”

“**You do not,**” the woman of about twenty-seven said, “**because you have been in that room about four months and you have not worked out which of its lines there are most of, and there are about four hundred of us at this end and about ninety of those on that paper. A place has more names than a form has lines, and a form that asks for one of them gets whichever the person filling it in already knew, and the man of about forty-one who filled it in knew the word at the rail and not the word at my door, and he was not lying, and I am not saying he was, and I am saying the form asked me a question through a man in a cart and I was not in the room.**”

**And that is three hundred and twelve words in five turns of one exchange, of which the first is six, the second is one hundred and thirty printed in two spans of six and one hundred and twenty-four, the third is forty printed in two spans of eight and thirty-two, the fourth is four, and the fifth is one hundred and thirty-two printed in two spans of three and one hundred and twenty-nine, and a woman of about twenty-seven told a man of about thirty-four that a place has more names than a form has lines, and that is the whole of what the walk was for, and she was not thanked and did not ask to be.**

**And about four hundred people live at the end of that lane, and about four hundred is the figure on a return, and the two are about the same figure and are not the same figure, and about four hundred is not added to about four hundred, and the woman of about twenty-seven is not on any sheet in nine counties and the man of about thirty-four is not on any list and he was not thanked either.**

## Four

**He wrote both words on the sheet and he ruled nothing between them and he put no head over either of them, and he wrote the hour under the pair of them, and he did not write the four hundred again because the four hundred is not his to write and the man of about forty-one wrote it in Harvestmonth in about four minutes at a table by a door.**

The sheet went back up the lane to the street and into a hired room over a saddler's on the Monday, and about nine people read it, and the room did not decide what the lane is called. **About four of the nine call the lane the Green Lane, and about five call it the Long Lane, and the two sets do not overlap at all, and the man of about fifty-eight read both words out loud in the same order twice and wrote nothing down and has not written it down since.**

**And nobody turned it into a boundary, and nobody turned it into a dispute, and nobody asked anybody to say which of the two words was the true one, and a form with a place, a figure of people, a trade and an empty fourth head has one line under the first of those, and a lane that is a mile and a half long has two words on it, and neither of those is a mistake and both of them happened in about four months on the same piece of paper.**

## Five

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the man of about thirty-four's, and it is the ninth one he has kept in eleven years and the first one with a lane in it, and a mile and a half is not in it and about four hundred people are not in it.

**a quart of small beer at two, a bucket of tar at eight, a bed at four, a quill and ink at three, a second broom at five, a second string at two, cheese at four, a second quart at two, a third pound of candles at four, a third candle at two, a firkin of oil at nine, a third cheese at four, a second candle at two, a third bed at four, a pot of tea at three, a pound of candles at four, a bundle of cords at two, a fourth bed at four, a third broom at five, a second pot of tea at three, a fifth bed at four, a third pot of tea at three, a second bucket of tar at eight, a string at two, a second firkin of oil at nine, a third bucket of tar at eight, a second cheese at four, a broom at five, a second pound of candles at four, a candle at two, and a second bed at four.** Two, eight, four, three, five, two, four, two, four, two, nine, four, two, four, three, four, two, four, five, three, four, three, eight, two, nine, eight, four, five, four, two and four is a hundred and twenty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and twenty-nine pence is two marks and thirty-three over**, and two words for one lane are not in it, and the hour under the two of them is not in it, and about ninety returns that came in under the wrong question in Harvestmonth are not in it, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month are not in it, and about nine hundred sheets a month are not in it, and the three of those are not added to one another, and a name is not in it.

**And on the thirtieth day of Embermonth in this year a clerk of about thirty-four walked a made lane about a mile and a half from a street of a market town of about two thousand people to a chapel and eleven houses and came back with two words for one lane, and he wrote both of them under a head that asks for one, and the room read them and did not choose, and about four hundred people at the end of that lane were not asked anything and a form at the bottom of a sheet says in the same ink that nobody is to be sent to find out, and a man was sent, and the tide decides twice a day and is not paid and is not on any of this.**
