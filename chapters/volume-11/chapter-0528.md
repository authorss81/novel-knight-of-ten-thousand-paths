# Chapter 0528

**The sixteenth of Frostmonth, YR 319, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the eighth hour and the eleventh hour, in a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people, and Aren Kest, a man of thirty-two with a limp, was in that room for all four of them and was asked nothing about a form.**

He was not asked anything by the man of about fifty-eight, at the second hour or at the sixth or at the eleventh. The woman of about thirty-four did not put a question to him. Nobody in that room put a question to him about a form, and nobody put a question to him about a rule, and nobody put a question to him about the fourth head, and the two questions he was asked that day were both asked out loud in front of the other eight: the first was asked by the man of about fifty-three with a barrow and he answered it with one word, and the second was asked by the man of about thirty-four with a sheet at about the seventh hour and it was about the eighth sentence and he answered it in one sentence. He spoke twice, and the second time he spoke it was about a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and it was nine sentences, and the copyist of about twenty-six wrote all nine of them down on a leaf with no head over it and did not read them back.

The first hour of it was quiet. He had come in at the second hour and had sat down on the wall side of the table with his walking stick between his knees and his right leg out at the angle a man puts it out at when he has been sitting on a cart wheel, and he had listened to the room argue about a strip of paper for about four minutes before he said anything at all.

“**That man has been up and down this lane in a month more times than anybody in this room has been to the rail of that common.**”

“**He has,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**He has a form in his coat and a satchel and a reason for both, and he has not asked this room for one thing for himself since Harvestmonth.**”

Nobody said anything.

“**He asked me a question about a rule in Harvestmonth and I did not answer it, and he wrote down that I did not answer it.**”

“**I know he did,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**it is in the back of a book of carts in my own hand.**”

“**Everything this room has written down for four months is about a piece of paper.**”

The woman of about thirty-four put her pen down.

“**The reason there is a piece of paper is that I made one, six years ago and a little, and I held a thing for about a day and a night.**”

Nobody in that room said anything at all.

“**About nine people on the other side of it were never told that I had done anything at all.**”

The clerk of about twenty-two closed his own book of about two hundred leaves and put it inside his coat, and the man of about fifty-eight did not look at him when he did it, and the clerk of about twenty-two had not been asked for the book since the ninth day of Wolfmonth.

“**What you are making here is a record of that, and everything that has been written down in this room since the twenty-fifth of Harvestmonth has been a record of that.**”

“**Then say what the thing was,**” the man of about fifty-three with a barrow said, and he had not said anything in that room since the twenty-eighth of Wolfmonth.

“**No,**” Aren Kest said.

“**You have just told nine people that there was a thing and not told them what it was, and that is not a confession, that is a shape with a hole in it.**”

“**It is what I have,**” Aren Kest said, and he did not raise his voice and he did not put his hand on the table, and he went on, because the ninth sentence was not finished.

“**I have not said it in a room before and I am not going to be asked to say it in a room again.**”

“**Then what was the eighth sentence for?**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**The eighth sentence was for you,**” Aren Kest said, “**and not for him.**”

And that was the nine. **The copyist of about twenty-six wrote all nine down and ruled nothing under them and left about two-thirds of the leaf blank, and the blank is not a figure and is not counted, and the man of about fifty-eight did not copy them into the room's book and did not ask for them to be copied into the room's book, and the leaf stayed on the table at the side table and the leaf has no head over it and no keeper and no fixed day.**

**About four of the nine people in that room believe him and about five do not, and he did not correct anybody.**

He did not correct the man of about fifty-three with a barrow, who said at about the seventh hour that a man who tells a room of nine that a record is a record of his own business is a man who has been in nine counties asking a different question and has come at last to the right room, and that he would not be the first and would not, on the evidence of a shape with a hole in it, be believed. He did not correct the copyist of about twenty-six, who said nothing at all and shut her leaf and put her hand flat on it. He did not correct the man of about thirty-four with a sheet, who said that the ninth sentence was the only one of the nine that was about a person in the room and that a man who puts the sentence about other people last has thought about it, and that thinking about it is not the same as having a witness. He did not correct the man of about fifty-one in a grey coat, who got up off the wall at about the eighth hour, which he had not done in that room in eleven years, and stood at the open window with his back to about nine people and looked down a made road for about as long as a man takes to say nine, and sat down again, and said nothing.

**And then he did the one thing in ten days that costs him something, and it was not a speech and nobody asked him to do it and nobody thanked him for it and it is not going to be reported anywhere but in this room.**

He waited until about the eleventh hour, when the room had gone back to the sheets and the strips and the strips of the Monday bundle, and he said to the man of about fifty-eight, out loud, in front of the other eight, that he wanted his own name written in the back of the book of carts beside the man's own.

“**Beside mine,**” the man of about fifty-eight said.

“**Beside yours.**”

“**Why?**”

“**Because a book with one name in it is a record of a private man's failures,**” Aren Kest said, “**and a book with two is a record anybody can be shown. And that book goes up a made road on a Monday with a bundle on it and a man in a saddler's shop takes the top off it and hands the rest down, and in about four years somebody with a warrant is going to have it and he is going to be right to ask what is in it, and if there is one name in the back of it he is going to have a private man's failures and nothing else, and if there are two he is going to have a record and he will be able to tell the difference between the two without asking either of us.**”

The nine of them sat with it. The copyist of about twenty-six turned her leaf over and put her hand flat on the blank two-thirds of it, and nobody at that table knew what the blank was for, and it was not for anything, and it stayed blank.

“**I am not asking you to put a claim against my name,**” Aren Kest said. “**I am asking you to put a name on a piece of paper that goes up a made road, and that is the whole of it, and it is not a reward and it is not a favour and it does not make me easier to be in nine counties. It makes me easier to find.**”

The man of about fifty-eight took the pen up at about the eleventh hour and wrote his own name in the back of the book of carts, under nine market mornings of figures and above a list of four saddles, which is where he had written it in Fallowmonth, and he wrote the second name beside it, and he did not read either of them out, and that is the second time he has written a name in that book and not read it out.

Nobody saw him do it except the copyist of about twenty-six, who has said that she saw it and that she said nothing, and that being right to say nothing is the second time in ten days that she has been right to say nothing and the first time it had cost her a sentence.

**And he conducted nothing on that Wednesday and he got nothing back from it and he is not the reason any of the four months of that room works, and the man of about fifty-one in a grey coat got up and sat down again, and the clerk of about twenty-two shut a book of his own, and the copyist of about twenty-six turned a leaf over, and the man of about fifty-eight wrote a name, and none of the four of those was him.**

**And nothing was reconciled. The two words for a lane are still two words and a third word has come up the same lane on a copy, and the room has not chosen and has not scheduled choosing. The four heads are still four heads and the fourth is still empty on the form on the table. A man is dead and is not a villain and was not a keeper and has no name. A nine-word sentence and a nine-word sentence and a stroke through a line are on three pieces of paper and nobody has said whose hand made any of them. Thirty-one nights stand against five roads and thirty-four on a chalk board over a yard stair in a fen town two hundred miles off and the difference of three has not been found and he did not look for it and the notebook in his coat has eleven lines on it and no twelfth line and did not come out of the coat that day.**

**And the cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is the man of thirty-two's, and it is the eighth one he has kept since the twenty-fifth day of a month carried at thirty in the year before this one, and nine sentences and a name are not in it.**

**a rule of brass at thirteen, a ream of paper at twenty-nine, a pot of tea at five, a broom at nineteen, a bundle of kindling at ten, a box of quills at twenty-three, a besom at eight, a pot of paste at seventeen, a waxed cord at twelve, and a cheese at fifteen.** Thirteen, twenty-nine, five, nineteen, ten, twenty-three, eight, seventeen, twelve and fifteen is a hundred and fifty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and fifty-one pence is three marks and seven over**, and the nine on the other side of a thing six years ago are not in it, and the nine in that room are not in it, and those are not one nine and are not added to one another, and a difference of three is not in it and has not been found, and eleven lines are not in it, and a book of carts is not in it, and the lane's four hundred and the sand's nine hundred are two counts of two things and the column holds neither of them.

He went down the lane at about the eleventh hour with the stick in his right hand, and the man of about fifty-eight shut the room behind him, and the book of carts was left open on the table with the two names in the back of it and the nine market mornings above them and the four saddles below, and it closed at about the twelfth hour and went on the Monday in a bundle with nine returns in it, and nobody in that room read the two names out.
