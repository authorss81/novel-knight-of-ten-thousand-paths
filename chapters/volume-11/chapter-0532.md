# Chapter 0532

**The fourteenth of Hearthmonth, YR 319, a Wednesday and not a market day, and the first hour and the third hour and the seventh and the eighth, in a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people, where about nine people were in that room and the first page of a man's record of himself was read out loud, and nobody in that room argued with it and nobody in that room was thanked for reading it.**

**The first page is not the city.** He said that out loud before he turned the sheet round, and he said it to the room and not to the man of about fifty-eight, and the room had come in expecting the city and several of them had said so in the street on the way up, and the man of about fifty-eight put his pen down and said nothing.

Aren Kest had the sheet in both hands and his stick against the leg of the table.

“**It is the command,**” he said. “**That is all it is. It has got a day on it and an hour on it and it has got one thing on it, and the one thing is that I was told to hold a road and I did not hold it, and I am going to say it in the plainest words I can find and if the plainest words are ugly then the ugly ones are what happened.**”

Then he said it, and it went the way a thing goes when a man has had four days to take the knots out of it.

He was twenty-one. He was a captain by brevet. On the nineteenth of Mudmonth, six years ago and a little, at about the second hour of the night, a courier gave him a paper at a gate, and the paper named a crossing, a district and forty men, and it gave him a term of about a day and a night, and it did not tell him what the term was for, and he did not ask, and he has written both of those on the page because both of them are true and only one of them is a defence. He took the crossing. He held it for about a day and a night. He let about nine people over it who had not been told that there was anything to be held, and he did not tell them either, and he did not send them back, and he has written that down in the plainest words he could find and the plainest words are *I did not send them back*.

Nobody in that room said anything at all while he said it, and the copyist of about twenty-six did not write it down, which was noticed by the man of about thirty-four with a sheet and by nobody else.

“**Turn it over,**” Aren Kest said, when he had finished, and the woman of about forty-four who has been copying for about nineteen years turned it over, and read the rest of the page back to the room in the voice she uses for a form, about as long to read as it takes a man to say nine, and stopped at the end of it and did not go on to the second page.

She read it back because that is her work, and because a man who has written a thing about himself in the plainest words he can find cannot be the only man who decides whether the plainest words are the ones he meant.

“**The nine,**” she said when she had finished, and she put her finger on the line and did not look up. “**It says about nine people. It does not say nine. It says *about nine* and the reason it says about nine is that you counted them on a bridge in the dark with a lamp in your hand and you are not a man who counts well and you have said so on the page.**”

“**I counted nine,**” Aren Kest said. “**They were nine and there may have been two more behind the second buttress and I have written about nine because I have not got the two.**”

Nobody argued with that either, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote the whole of it out in his own hand on a bare leaf with no head over it, and he wrote *about nine* and not *nine*, and read it back the two words to make sure, and Aren Kest said that was right.

Then the man of about fifty-three with a barrow said the thing nobody else in the room was going to say.

“**You have written down that you were told to hold a road and did not hold it,**” he said, “**and about nine people came over it that were not told there was a thing to be held, and not one of the people on it has ever been told what you did on it. You have just said that in a room. That is a different sentence from the one in the room's book, which says a rule was changed by one man in a hand and that nobody knows who. That sentence in the book has got a day and an hour on it and a head cut into the wood over it. This has got you at the top of it.**”

“**That is the difference,**” Aren Kest said, “**and I am not going to argue with you about it, and I am not going to pretend it is a small one. A line in a book says that a thing happened and does not say to whom. A page with a name at the top of it says that a man did it.**”

“**And the four counties that answered in the first fortnight,**” the woman of about thirty-four who keeps the key said, from the door end, “**and the about four hundred and nine parish rolls a month that come into a room two hundred miles off, and the about nine hundred sheets a month that go out of it: none of those three has ever had a name at the top of anything. If you put yours on, you have done a thing in this room on a Wednesday that a hundred clerks with a die could do in a morning, and none of them ever has, and I would like somebody to say why not before you go on with the second page.**”

The man of about fifty-eight gave her the reason and it was one sentence and he gave it without ornament.

“**Because a head with a name over it is a thing a clerk is given to fill in,**” he said, “**and if it is a thing a clerk is given to fill in then within a year there will be a second head, and then a third, and the first page of your record will have a name at the top of it and a number under the name and a date under the number, and by the time there are four of them it will be a return, and a return goes out.**”

Nobody argued with that, and the copyist of about twenty-six said nothing at all, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote it on the leaf with the first page on it and did not rule a head over the leaf, and the clerk of about twenty-two wrote nothing in the room's book, as he has written nothing in that book since the seventh of Hearthmonth.

The seventh hour was the part where the room had to be told what the record was for, and Aren Kest said it, and he said it flatly, and it was not a speech, and it was about himself.

“**I am putting in what I did and who did it.**”

And then he said the thing that the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote down and the room did not argue with, and the thing was that the record does not say what the fever was or where it came from, and will not, and that he was the man standing on the crossing and cannot tell and will not guess, and that about four of the nine people in that room thought a record ought to end in a cause and about five did not, and that the room did not vote and that the record is not going to ask them.

“**A record that ends in a cause is a record that has found somebody,**” he said, “**and the somebody is me, and I would rather it did not find me.**”

Nobody in that room agreed with him out loud and nobody in that room argued with him out loud, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote the whole of it down and did not read it back, which is the first time in about nine years he has written a thing in that room and not read it back.

**The cost of that Wednesday is a column, and the column is the man of about thirty-four with a sheet's, and it is the thirteenth one he has kept in about nine years, and a page with a name at the top of it is not in it.**

**a straightedge at thirteen, an inkstand at twenty-seven, a wedge of bread at four, a night's straw at nineteen, a bit of chalk at two, a pot of paste at fifteen, a leather case at twenty-three, a bucket of water at three, a loaf at six, and a thread of twine at two.** Thirteen, twenty-seven, four, nineteen, two, fifteen, twenty-three, three, six and two is a hundred and fourteen pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and fourteen pence is two marks and eighteen over**, and the crossing is not in it, and the nineteen of Mudmonth is not in it, and the second hour of the night is not in it, and forty men is not in it, and the about nine on a bridge in the dark is not in it, and six years and a little is not in it, and the figure the crown's finding gives is not in it, and that figure is not a figure of anybody in this column, and the four hundred at the end of a made lane and the two thousand of this town and the nine hundred on a coast four days off are three counts of three different things and go in at neither end of this column and are not added to one another here.

The room emptied at about the eighth hour and the man of about fifty-eight read the leaf with the first page on it twice and did not copy it into the room's book, and said out loud, to an empty room, that a thing which is not copied into a book can be shown to whoever comes up that ladder and can be taken away by whoever carries it, and that this was on the list of things he had been told was a mistake in his first year in a county office, and that he had never known which list it was on until that Wednesday.
