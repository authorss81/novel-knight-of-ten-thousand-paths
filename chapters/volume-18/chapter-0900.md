# Chapter 0900

**Frostmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 325, a Tuesday and not a market morning and the last day of this manuscript, the Old Toll bridge at Merefen at the fifth hour with the boards down on the near side and nine feet of coping gone out of the second pier, four people on the boards, a tool bag on the stone, and the water nine inches below where it was on Friday.**

The bleed is seven hundred and seventy days old and it is not cured and not explained and no one of the four people on those boards has noticed it.

The engine has been damaged five hundred and sixty-one days and it is nobody's achievement and nothing in this year mended it.

Nobody thanked anybody on that bridge, and about two of the four on those boards had been ready to say thank you and one had not, because there are four of them and not nine, and nine hundred people in nine counties will hear that Tuesday in nine words each.

What was wrong with the bridge is nine feet of coping and one sill stone and a staple, and about two of the four people on those boards had known that at the fourth hour on Friday and five had known it on Saturday.

He has been on it since the fourth hour with a bar and a line and a wedge and nine feet of cord, and he is thirty-two and he has a stick in his right hand and a limp that has been the same since his first year in this manuscript.

about three of the four on that bridge had worked with him before and one had not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a bridge is the only work in this manuscript which has never once asked anybody for anything.

The coping is nine feet of it and it weighs four hundredweight, and about three of the four there had done this nine times before and five had done it once each.

The sill stone is four feet by nine inches and it goes under the burnt and not under the water, and about two of the four on those boards had wanted the sill done first and five had wanted the coping done first and the water did it their way.

Nine feet of coping came out of that pier on Saturday and nine hundred people in nine counties have said a bank of frost takes a coping off a nine-hundred-year-old pier the way it takes a tooth out, and four hundred have said it takes it the way a bailiff takes a coat.

The water is not up and two of the four people on those boards had wanted the stone in before the water came and five had wanted it in a week and nine hundred people in nine counties have said the first of those is a bridgewright and the second of those is nine hundred people.

nine hundred crossings a day are worked in nine counties and every one of them is worked by a person who has to be asked, and about three of the four on that bridge had heard that said in a hall in four months and five had not.

There is a message due at that crossing at the seventh hour and it is nine days old on the schedule and it goes on that schedule four times a winter, and about three of the four there had wanted it to wait and five had known it cannot wait.

The schedule for that message is nineteen years old and it is kept in the room at the back and it has four crossings on it, and about two of the four on those boards had seen that sheet and five had not.

Every crossing on it is opened by a person who stands at it, and about two of the four people on those boards had wanted an officer to open it for them and five had known there is no officer of anything within nine days.

It has gone at that hour forty times and three of the four people on that bridge had counted the forty and one had not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties trust a schedule of forty entries without ever meeting about it.

The message is four lines long and it is a thing nine ships and a tide. Two of the four people on that bridge had read it and two had not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a road which is only opened for a schedule is a road that is open for nine minutes.

Then he touched the bell.

It is on the gate post at the head of the boards, and it is a hand bell the size of a fist and a half, cracked right across the shoulder, and there is no clapper on it and has not been one for nine years.

Nothing happened. Four of the four people on those boards had known that nothing would happen and five had not known what they were waiting for.

He has put his hand on that bell nine hundred times in four and a half years and it has answered him nine times in nine years, the last of them when he was nine years old and did not know yet what he was.

about two of the four on those boards had not known that figure and five had known it and had never heard him say it, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said a man who has been answered nine times in nine years knows exactly what a bell is for.

The crack goes two-thirds of the way round the shoulder and about two of the four people on those boards had looked at it and five had not, and there is no clapper and there is a staple and there is iron.

about three of the four on that bridge had wanted to be told what it is for and five had known that the man who keeps that crossing has never told anybody and has never been asked twice.

He put his hand flat on it and took it off, and about three of the four there had watched him do that and five had not been watching him at all, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a hand on a bell is a thing four people watched and a fifth person did not.

He did not say anything it and nobody at that bridge asked him it, and four of the four people there had wanted to and five had known in advance that they would not.

He cannot open that road. That is not a matter of the bell and about two of the four on those boards had wanted him to try and five had known that he cannot and have never seen anybody who could.

What happened next is that he asked, and he asked three people, and two of the four people on those boards had wanted him to stop asking at the second one and five had let him finish.

The first of the three keeps that crossing. He is fifty-eight and has kept it twenty-two years and he has been put in that place by a thing that renews his appointment every year and that he can be replaced by without asking anybody.

A crossing is kept by a person that a place has chosen, and four hundred people in nine counties have that in writing and five hundred have it in a lane, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said it is the only sentence in this that anybody would repeat from memory.

He can be asked that bridge by anybody in Merefen and he can be replaced by Merefen without asking anybody, and about three of the four on that bridge had wanted to replace him that morning and five had not wanted anything at all.

about three of the four there had known about the trust column for three years and two had not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a man who signs for himself and is chosen by others is the whole arrangement and is not an office.

He is a custodian in the sense that a place chose him, and about two of the four on those boards had used the word that morning and five had not.

The second of the three is a girl of nine who is a witness at that crossing on the record in her own right, and about two of the four people on those boards had wanted her kept off the boards and five had known she is on the record and had not asked how.

She has a witness's place in the yard book because she held the gate once for an hour while a chain was changed, and about three of the four on that bridge had wanted it struck out and five have said a gate held is a gate held.

The third of the three is a steward of Bellweather-Living and she is independent of that town's own road and two of the four people on that bridge had wanted to know what independent means and five had known and had not asked.

She came to that crossing nine days off with the delegation and she stayed in this town when the delegation went on, and about two of the four on those boards had asked why and five had not asked.

What she is not is that town's road. She is a steward of a community that keeps its own road and answers for it, and about two of the four people on those boards had wanted her to say what her community's road owes this one and five had known she would say it has not been asked.

Nobody asked her. That is the thing that Tuesday and about three of the four on that bridge had wanted somebody to ask and five had known that asking is how a thing becomes somebody else's business.

She is Bryenna Faal and she has been in this manuscript since an early year of it, and about three of the four there had known that and five had not.

four hundred people in nine counties have said that three hands on a thing is a ceremony and five hundred have said that three hands on a thing is the number of hands it takes, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said both of those are correct different things.

What they did is what a bridgewright does when a sill goes, which is that they put a hand on the iron on the near side and hold it while it is bedded, and about two of the four on those boards watched two hands do it nine hundred times and five had never watched anything but four.

Nobody told them to. Four of the four people on those boards had wanted to be told and five had known that a thing told to three people is a thing commanded, and that he does not command anybody in it any more.

He asked them once and he did not ask them twice, and two of the four people on those boards had wanted a second asking and five had known that a second asking is a demand with a question mark on it.

Then he put the bar down and stood against the parapet and about three of the four on that bridge had watched him put it down and five had not looked up, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that is the ninth time he has put a bar down in nine years.

about three of the four there had wanted to go and stand with them and five had known he could not, and neither of them asked him why he could not and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that neither of them knowing is the last thing this year does.

The sill went in at the sixth hour and forty minutes and the coping was dressed and the staple was drawn, and about two of the four on those boards had wanted the stone at the fourth hour and five had known the water.

Then the three of them put their hands on the iron together, at the seventh hour, and the road went, and about two of the four people on those boards had not expected it and five had not expected it either.

The iron is nine feet long and it is let into the stone of the near pier and it is the oldest piece of iron in that bridge and it is not a thing anybody chose in this year.

The three of them were not asked whether they would do it and about three of the four on that bridge had wanted them asked and five had known that a question would have had to be answered.

Then the girl asked whether she had to, and about four of those on those boards had wanted to say she did not and about four had wanted to say nothing at all.

“**Do I have to put my hand on it**”

That was nine words and every person on that bridge heard all nine of them, about four of the four people on those boards hearing what was in them and about four hearing only that she was nine.

She was told she did not have to and she put it on anyway, and about four of those on those boards had watched her decide in about two seconds and about four had not watched her at all, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have put the whole of a witness in those two words.

Nobody at that bridge can say what happened. Two of the four people on that bridge have said since that three hands went onto nine feet of iron and the road went and five have said that the road was going to go anyway at about the seventh hour because the schedule said so, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said both of those are true and that they are different sentences.

It went for the message. It did not go for anybody crossing and about four of the four people on that bridge had wanted it to stay open afterwards and about five had known that a road opened for a message is a road opened for a message.

Four lines went across at about the seventh hour, three of the four people on those boards watching them go and one with his back turned, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said a message is four lines and nine minutes and that everything else is a road somebody wants open.

Then the bell went, and it went across the water, and it was not for anybody at that bridge.

It answered and he was four paces off with his hand down, and about two of the four people on those boards had worked that out at once and about five had not worked it out at all by the end of that morning.

It went on the far side and about four hundred people at that far end heard it and about five hundred did not, and about nine of the four hundred had been told in a lane in nine weeks that the bell on this side does not answer anybody.

About four hundred people in nine counties believe it can never be settled who touched it on which morning and about five hundred believe it will be settled in about four years, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that it does not matter because it answered somebody either way.

About three of the four on that bridge had heard it and about five had not, and about four hundred people at the far end of that water heard it and about five hundred did not, and nobody at that bridge knows who touched it.

The man who keeps the crossing put his hand on it and got nothing, and about three of the four there had seen him do that and about five had seen him do it four hundred times.

He stepped back four paces with his tool bag at his feet and he did not go any further than that, and three of the four people on those boards had wanted him to go further and about five had known why he did not.

A woman came up the made road at about the eighth hour with a printed sheet in her hand, and about two of the four people on those boards had known her and about five had not, and she is about twenty-nine.

She is Mara Vey and she is the first accountable keeper of the distributed Archive and about four of the four people on that bridge had known the second half of that and about five had known the first.

The sheet is the public copy of that morning's record and there are nine copies of it in nine counties and this is the one for Merefen, and about three of the four on that bridge had read one of the other eight and about five had not.

She gave it to the man who keeps the crossing and not to him, and about two of the four people on that bridge noticed it while about five did not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have called that the last errand of the morning and say it was done properly.

What is on that sheet is the crossing count, the bell, the four lines, and about nine feet of coping dressed, and about two of the four on those boards had wanted the bell left off and about five had known the record is a record.

Then the two of them walked home.

They walked up the four miles at about the ordinary pace of two people who are not going anywhere in particular, and two of the four people on those boards had watched them go up the made road and about five had gone down to the yard.

It is four days of made road and about four miles of it is the smoothest in nine counties and about three of the four on that bridge had been told that and about five had not.

There are about nine hundred crossings in nine counties and about five hundred of them have no name against them this year, and about three of the four there had carried a copy of that sheet and about five had not.

About two of the four on those boards had wanted to ask the man who kept the crossing what he would do with the sheet when he had read it and about five had known the answer before the question and did not ask.

Home is four days of made road off and about nine hundred yards of yard and a door that shuts, and two of the four people on those boards watched them go and two did not look up.

Nobody thanked anybody on that bridge and nobody has yet, and about three of the four on that bridge had expected the word and one had not, and the three were wrong.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is nine feet of coping and one sill stone and a staple, a bar and a line and a wedge and about nine feet of cord used by a man of thirty-two who stood four paces off with his tool bag while three other people opened a road for nine minutes, a bell that was silent under his hand and answered across the water for about four hundred people who were not there, a sheet of paper given to the man who keeps a crossing and not to the man who repaired it, and two people walking four days of made road toward a door that shuts.
