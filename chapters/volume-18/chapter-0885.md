# Chapter 0885

**Fallowmonth the seventeenth, YR 325, a Tuesday and not a market morning, an assembly hall over a guild hall in a main-world town of four thousand people, about forty chairs and a table the length of the room and about nine people at it, and nobody in that town who sells on a Tuesday.**

That assembly has sat about four times a year since before anybody in that room was born and its standing orders are nineteen lines long and have not been rewritten since about YR 301.

Four of the nineteen lines are about roads and about four of them are about who may open them, and about nine of the nineteen lines are about what may not be done at a gate without a paper, and nine people in that room have said that the standing orders were written by people who were frightened of gates.

An assembly in a town of four thousand people is a room with a table the length of it, a clerk at one end with a book about two inches thick, and about forty chairs that nine people sit in and thirty-one people leave empty.

It has no soldiers and no power over anything outside the room, and four of the nine people in that room have said that is why nobody has ever asked it to do anything and about five have said it is why it could be asked to do anything at all.

The book is two inches thick and it is about four hundred years old and the clerk of it has said in that room that everything that body has ever decided is in it, in that hand, in about nine columns.

There is one empty chair in that room and it has been empty for about nine years and it is not at the table. It is on the floor at the end nearest the door, and it is a chair and not a seat, and four of the nine people at that table had noticed it and about five had not.

What was asked of that assembly on that Tuesday is written on a sheet of four lines and it was carried up that road by a carter and it asks the assembly to fill that chair.

The four lines that were carried up that road say four things. The first is that the assembly is asked to fill the chair. The second is that the person who fills it does not thereby agree to anything that assembly has ever refused. The third is that a representative may refuse in that room. The fourth is that the refusal is not a matter for that assembly.

Two of those four lines were asked for by the woman of thirty-eight and two of them were asked for by a carter who is not in that room and has never been to that town, and four of the nine people in that room knew that and about five did not.

Nobody in that room can produce the carter and four of the nine people there said he is nine days off and about five said he is four days off, and about nine people in nine counties have said the whole of this year's trouble is a difference of five days in a road.

That is the whole of the errand and four of the nine people at that table had thought it was four lines and no more and about five had thought it was the largest thing anybody had put to them in four and a half years.

Six anchor fragments where there were ten.

Then the resistance happened and it took nine minutes and it was not raised by anybody who came to raise it.

It was raised by the woman of about thirty-eight who holds a mandate and carries no soldiers, and she did not argue against the chair, and she said out loud in that room that the chair was the easy part.

She said that a chair is a thing a body can be asked to fill, and that this body would be asked, and that a body which fills it has agreed to be filled into, and that about nine hundred places had spent four and a half years refusing to be filled into anything.

That is the whole of the resistance and four of the nine people in that room had heard that argument before and about five had not, and about four of the nine have said since that it is the best short piece of reasoning anybody in that room has ever heard and about five have said it is the reason nobody came to the meeting at all.

What she asked for instead was a standing and not a chair.

Then a man of about forty-four who runs a weekly cart to a ford got up and asked what a standing is, and nine people in that room heard him ask it and about four of the nine had an answer ready and about five had not.

He said that he was struck off a list of men who may witness a count nine years ago and that nobody has ever told him why and that he has gone on carrying a cart across that ford for nine years and nine hundred people in nine counties have used it.

That is his own trade and four of the nine people in that room understood all of it at once and about five heard only the cart.

The standing is four lines long and it was written that afternoon in that room in about eleven minutes and four of the nine people there had wanted it shorter and about five had wanted it longer.

It says that a representative sits with a standing to refuse, and that a refusal made in that room is a thing the room did and not a thing a person did in it, and that the refusal is entered in that assembly's own book in that assembly's own hand, and that no standing order of that assembly is evidence against anything a person refused in it.

The third of those four lines is the one that made the change and four of the nine people in that room had not wanted it and about five had, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a refusal has to be written down by the body it was made to or it is a favour and not a right.

Then the chair was filled and it was filled twice in about nine minutes and about four people in that room understood what had happened and about five had not.

A woman of about forty-one who keeps a crossing nine days off took it on the Tuesday and gave it up on the Thursday of the same week, and four of the nine people in that room said she had been bought and about five said she had read the third line first.

She kept it for three days and on the three days she read the four lines of the standing twice a day and said nothing at all, and four of the nine people in that room had wanted to ask her why and about five had said nothing to her on purpose.

On the Thursday she gave it back and she said nine words about why and four of the nine people in that room heard all nine and about five heard four of them, and nine people in that town have said that she gave it back because of the third line and about five have said she gave it back because a chair at the end nearest the door is where you send somebody you intend to use.

Then she went back nine days and kept a crossing and has not come to that town again, and four people in that room have said that is a person refusing to be a symbol and about five have said it is a person refusing to be visible.

The engine has been damaged four hundred and sixty-three days and nobody in that assembly hall was mending it and nobody in that room mentioned it.

The place four hundred yards under that hill has about two hundred people at the far end of the cut and it lost its contact in Mudmonth and it has not had it since. There is a lamp at the near end that went out on a Wednesday and has not been lit. About four tons of coal a week are not coming up that road. A school there closed and a court there is out of reach.

Nothing in those four lines names that place and four of the nine people in that room wanted the first line to name it and about five said a paper does not get stronger by naming the worst thing on it.

That is the standing argument of this year in one room and four of the nine people in that hall have said that somebody is going to lose an argument over it and about five have said that nine hundred people are already living inside it.

Then the first refusal was made in that room and it was made by the man with the cart and it is the ending of that Tuesday.

He stood up where he had been standing for about nine hours and said no, and nine people in that room heard it and about four of them had expected it and about five had not.

“**I will not fill a chair in a room that has refused contact to a place four hundred yards under a hill for about four months, and I will not fill it in a room that cannot say the name of the place in its own book, and if you write my no down in that book tonight then I will stand in that street tomorrow and be the man who said no here, and if you do not write it down then this chair is a thing two of us sat in and the third line on that sheet is worth nothing.**”

That was one hundred and three words and about forty people in that hall heard every one of them and the clerk wrote all of it down in that assembly's own book while he was standing there.

That is the ending and four of the nine people in that room said it was the first refusal that hall had entered in about nine years and about five said it was the first one anybody had entered in one that had cost somebody something.

The clerk of that assembly is about sixty-three and he has been that clerk for about nine years and he has entered four hundred things into that book in that time and about four of them were refusals.

He said in his own trade, and he said it to nine people in that room and not to the forty, that a book is not a place facts go to be safe. He said that a fact written in a body's own hand can be pointed at by anybody who comes in with a question, and that this is the only protection anybody has ever found, and that a fact kept in a private book by the person it is about protects nobody.

That was not asked for by anybody and four of the nine people in that room have said it is the answer and about five have said it is another piece of the argument about writing things down.

Then he opened the book at the end of the year and ruled a place for it, and nine people in that room watched a clerk rule a place for a thing nobody had refused yet.

What that clerk ruled was five lines in that book and nothing else, and the first of the five is a date and the second is the name of the room and the third is the name of the man and the fourth is the refusal and the fifth is that the assembly accepted it.

He entered it while the man was standing there and he entered the whole of it and he entered it in that assembly's own hand and nine people in that hall watched him do it.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that a refusal entered in a body's own book is the only kind of refusal anybody can be held to and about five have said it is also the only kind anybody can be reminded of.

Nobody in that hall told him he was wrong and four of the nine people there wanted to and about five did not, and nine people in that hall read the entry in that book out loud afterward in about nine voices.

What the chair is now is a thing four of the nine people in that room have a different answer about and about five have the same.

It is on the floor and it is a chair and not a seat and it can be filled by anybody who comes in and there is nothing on the wall above it and no name board and no plate, and four of the nine people in that room had wanted a plate put on that wall on the Tuesday and about five had said that a plate is the thing the standing is against.

What the standing is against is a thing anybody in that hall can point at and say that this body agreed to this person on that day, and four people in nine counties have said that is the whole of the year in one line of a standing order.

The entry has five lines in it and the last one says that the refusal was made by the person named in the third line and that the assembly accepted it, and four of the nine people in that room had wanted the entry to be about the assembly and about five had wanted it to be about the man.

It is about both and that is the finding of that Tuesday and four of the nine people in that room have said that a body which enters its own refusal has admitted that the refusal is a thing it did and about five have said that is the same thing said the other way round.

The four lines that were carried up that road by a carter are in that book too and four of the nine people in that room have said the assembly filled the chair and refused to fill it on the same afternoon, and about five have said it filled it nine minutes before it knew it was refusing.

Nobody at the top of that stair has heard of that assembly, and about nine hundred yards of occupied corridor is nine days from that town by the road that goes, and nothing done in that hall on that Tuesday is answerable to anything in that corridor and nothing in that corridor has to agree with it.

The chair is on the floor and it will be on the floor on the next Tuesday, and the clerk will rule a place at the end of the year for anything anybody refuses in that room, and about nine people in that town have said that is the only new thing in that hall in nine years.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is one chair on the floor at the end nearest the door of a hall in a town of four thousand people, empty for about nine years and filled twice inside one week by two different people, a standing of four lines written in about eleven minutes, nine refusals that were not entered in that hall's own book in nine years and one that was, and a man of about forty-four who carries a cart across a ford and who will now be the man who said no in that room for the rest of his life. Six anchor fragments where there were ten. The engine damaged four hundred and sixty-three days and nobody mending it. No road was opened in that town and none was shut, and nothing that assembly did on that Tuesday creates anything anywhere.**
