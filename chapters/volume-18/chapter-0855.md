# Chapter 0855

**Thawmonth the fifteenth, YR 325, a Tuesday and not a market morning and about nine people in the yard at the foot of that stair, the interval seven hours and holding, the corridor of ten plates standing open since the bar came out of its brackets on the eighth, and about nine hundred yards of stair between that corridor and the only door out of this county that a cart can use.**

He went up on the Tuesday at about the fifth hour with a lamp and a piece of chalk and about nine feet of cord and nothing else, and about four of the nine men on that place watched him go up and about five of them had stopped watching him some months ago.

The corridor is about four paces long and about six feet wide and has ten plates in it in a line on the left-hand wall at the height of a man's chest, with about nine inches between them, and a lamp bracket at each end and a floor of stone flags with a channel cut across it about nine inches from the wall for water to run in.

About four hundred and fifty days ago he stood at that end and looked at that line from nine feet off. On the Tuesday he went along it with the lamp in his left hand and his right hand behind his back, which is a thing he does in a place where he has promised not to touch something and about nine people in that corridor have watched him do it.

He did not open anything. Nothing on that wall opens, and there is nothing on that wall that has ever been opened, and about four of the nine men on that floor would say that to anybody who asked and about five of them would say that nobody has ever asked.

What he got out of about two hours with a lamp in a four-pace corridor is this. The shapes cut into the faces are not roads. A road in that place is a line with two ends and the ten shapes are not lines, and about four of them are closed curves and about six of them are open, and one of the ten is a shape he has seen cut into the sill of a door about four hundred miles off and never anywhere else.

Under each plate, cut into the stone of the wall and filled with a grey stuff that has been there a long time, there is a place name in a script he cannot read, and about four of the ten have a name four inches long and about six have a name nine inches long, and the two that are four inches long are the two nearest the door.

That is all he got, and he wrote none of it down, and the chalk stayed in his pocket, and four people in that corridor noticed that he wrote nothing down and five did not.

The corridor is used by nobody as a corridor. It is a place where ten plates stand in a wall and a lamp is kept burning at each end from the fourth hour to the eleventh, and nine people a day walk its four paces without stopping, and four of them look at the shapes and five look at the floor. There is a channel cut across the flags about nine inches from the wall and it has been dry for as long as anybody can remember, and four people have said the channel is for water and five have said a channel nine inches from a wall is not for water.

There is one rule about that corridor and it is not written anywhere. You do not put your hand on the wall beside a plate, and you do not stand in front of one closer than about nine inches, and the reason given for the second half of that rule is that a plate takes an impression of what is in front of it and has done since before anybody on that floor was born. About four people in that corridor know why and about five repeat the rule without the reason.

A shoe came off the ninth man's boot on the flags at about the fourth hour of that Tuesday and he picked it up and put it on again and about four people in that corridor heard the leather go and about five did not, and the officer holding this floor, who was not in the corridor and was on the landing, said afterward that a plate will take an impression of what is in front of it and that a shoe is a thing that has been in front of one.

What he got in the two hours was in his head and about nine hundred yards of stair had to be climbed to get it out of it, and he did it on the way down, and four people on the fourth landing watched him do it and five of them thought he was counting the steps, which is a thing he has done nine hundred times.

What he came up for was the other thing, and the other thing was not in the corridor.

There is a woman in that place who came with the delegation from the coast, and she has been on that floor four times in about nine days, and she goes up there and looks at the plates and does not touch them, and four of the nine men on that floor worked out that she is not looking at the plates and five have not.

What she has is a slip of paper about nine inches by four with nine rows of writing on it in two columns, and she has had it since the eleventh of Thawmonth, and she was given it by a man on the fourth floor in a conversation about about nine days of sea weather and about nothing else at all.

He asked her for it on the Tuesday at about the tenth hour and she did not want to be asked, and the reason she did not want to be asked is a reason that belongs to her community and not to him.

She said the first thing she said was that the slip was not hers to hand over and that she had not asked for it either, and nine people in that corridor heard the first sentence and four heard the second, and he let the second one stand.

Then she asked him, which is a thing about nine people in that building would not have done, whether he had asked her because he had worked out what was on it or because he had not.

He had not. He had come up that stair with a lamp and about nine feet of cord to find out what ten plates were, and he had not thought about a slip of paper until the sixth of Thawmonth, and four people in that corridor believed him and five did not.

The asking was noticed. The man who keeps the count was on the anchor floor at the time with the book under his arm because the plates are counted at the fourth hour and the tenth hour, and he counted the nine at the fourth and the nine at the tenth and came down, and about an hour later he went up the stair past the officer holding this floor and stood at his shoulder for about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice and then went back down.

What was reported is not printed here and it was not about the slip.

What came back up to that corridor in about nine days is the second half of the thing, and the officer holding this floor brought it himself and put it on the low table under the near lamp and did not sit down and did not say a word about where it had come from.

What he was told next came in two pieces and the second piece was carried up that stair by a man who is not on that floor and has never been on it and whose trade is writing things other people have to read, and the second piece said four things and one of the four things was that the other three are sealed at the bottom of the stair with the selector records and that nobody in nine counties will be told what they are in this year.

He asked for that seal to be broken and was neither refused nor granted, and four people in that room understood the shape of the answer and five did not.

The Key does not select on its own. It cannot. There are ten pieces in ten plates and the ten have to be held by ten people at once, and holding is not gripping, and holding means answering a question that is put to you, and the question is put by a thing in the wall of a floor about nine hundred yards below that corridor which has been the original protection of that place against one history being chosen for it, and nine men on that place call that thing the First Witness and four of them call it the Witness and five of them will not use either word.

And the trigger of it has been reversed.

The man who wrote that out is a man of about fifty with a fair hand and about twenty years of a trade that involved writing things other people would have to read, and he is not on that floor and was not in that corridor and has not been in that place. What came back up about nine days later was four lines of writing on a sheet about nine inches by six, and it said three things and it said them in a way that took about nine minutes to read and about an hour to be understood by anybody.

There is a word at the head of one of the columns on that slip and the word is custodian, and nine people in nine counties would use the word for a person a place has chosen and for nobody else, and four of them would use it for the man who holds a warrant as well, and five would not.

The three things are that the ten do not answer unless one of the ten answers first. That one of the ten answers first in the view of that thing in the wall, and has answered first since before this county knew the place existed. And that the name it has been given, and has been giving out about nine times a day to about nine men on that place, is his.

He read the four lines twice and put them down on the table and did not say anything for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over.

Then he said the part he came up that stair to find out, which is the part he had been wrong about for four and a half years and had never once been shown to be wrong about by anybody, because nobody had ever been in a position to show it to him.

There is a way of writing a thing that a person cannot refuse. It is done by putting the person into the form before they arrive, and leaving the form filled in, and then asking them afterwards whether they object. It is not a forgery in the ordinary sense. It is four true sentences in the right order on a sheet that somebody else has signed, and it takes about nine days to write and about two years to notice, four people in nine counties have said that it is the most dangerous kind of lying there is and five have said that it is only dangerous if you know it was done.

The working was not done by anybody in that corridor and nobody has admitted doing it to anybody. The sheet that came back up the stair had four lines on it and no name on it and no date on it, and the working had been done well enough that nine men on that place have been reading nine counts a day out of a form that has his name in it.

The woman from the coast was on the anchor floor at about the seventh hour that evening and she had been waiting about two hours for him and she had the slip of paper out on the table in front of her and she did not put it down.

“**There are nine rows and nine names in the left column, and beside each of them a number from one to ten, and the numbers run one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, ten. The man at the fourth floor gave me that slip on the eleventh and he said it was a list of who answers first, and I have carried it four days without knowing what it was for, and the tenth row has his name in the left column and a nine in the right, and there is no plate nine on that wall, and I have wanted to ask him that for four days.**”

That was eighty-nine words. Nothing in that room wrote it down.

That list had been read out to him by nobody before and shown to him by nobody and announced to him by nobody, and the woman who came with the delegation read ten rows of it out loud on the fifteenth of Thawmonth in a room with a low roof and no window in it, and the tenth row was his, and the number beside it was a nine, and there are ten plates in that corridor and the nine of them are the two nearest the door and the six of the far ones and the one in the middle that made a noise on the twenty-sixth of Goatmonth that was not the noise it makes when it seats itself properly.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is that a thing done to him about two years ago by somebody who never spoke to him was found out on a sheet about nine inches by six with four lines on it and no name on it, and that four lines of true sentences in the right order on somebody else's paper had made him the tenth of ten, and that a room about nine hundred yards below that corridor has been asking him a question about nine times a day since before this county knew the place existed and about nine men on that place have believed the answer. A woman from the coast read ten rows out loud and one of them was his and it was not shown to him before and it was not written down. A bar of ash out of its brackets since the eighth. The engine damaged two hundred and eighty-one days. Two hundred and sixty-seven days of one man in a lower archive. One ear, one leg, and four hundred and ninety days of a bleed on the inside of one wrist that nobody in nine counties has asked him about and that has had no panel and no explanation and no page of its own.**
