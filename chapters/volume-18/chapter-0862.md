# Chapter 0862

**Mudmonth the thirty-third, YR 325, a Monday and not a market morning in the market town of about two thousand people four days off the coast, thirteen stairs up from the lane to the room over the weigh-house with the register shut on the table and the press standing open with no date on it and the fourth ruled head of that page still empty and no fifth head ruled anywhere in this town, about six people in that room, and a wind off the coast that has been up four days.**

She came back on the Wednesday before with nothing and she has had five days with it.

The province she rode for is four days off in the mud month and the road into it goes over one watershed and down one long made stretch that is about nine miles of the best road in nine counties.

She went up it alone with a hood and a bundle and came back down it alone with the bundle.

About four people in that town have said that a woman who rides four days alone in the mud month and comes back with nothing has paid for the nothing.

About five have said that the woman who rode was a woman of about forty who has run a crossing crew for about eleven years and had run one before that, and that a crew of about nine people had been used to her not going anywhere.

What she brought back was a sheet of nothing. It had the province's own mark on it, being a curved iron that the province's own clerk puts on a thing and not a seal, and the mark was on it and no writing was on it, and the reason there was no writing on it is on a page in the room over the weigh-house and is six words long.

The words are that the province had not sat on it.

It had not sat on it because it had not been asked to sit on it in the right form.

It had been sent a question. A question is not a form and a province will not sit on a question. About four people in that town have said that a paper nobody can seal binds nobody and about five have said that a province nobody can refuse binds nobody either, and both of those had been said in that lane before this and neither of them had been said on this.

So the seventh sheet was not written. It was not written because a fragment cannot be altered after it has been read aloud, because from that moment two people are holding two different papers, and because none of the six writers may write a seventh, and because the one hand on this earth that would have made the seventh lawful is in a room four days off that has not sat.

That is what the five days were.

What happened in the five days is worth putting down because it is the whole of what is left to a man who has run out of lawful things to do.

On the Wednesday night he went up nine hundred yards of stair with a lamp in his left hand, which is a thing he has done about nine hundred times, and stood in a corridor about four paces long at a distance of about nine inches from a wall of ten plates, and he did not touch anything and he did not stand any closer than he has stood for four and a half years.

On the Thursday he wrote out what a seventh sheet would have to say, in his own hand, on a sheet of about nine inches by six, in about four hundred words, and it was good work and it was about nine lines long when it was finished.

On the Friday he read it out in that room over the weigh-house to about nine people and the woman who keeps that room asked him one question about it, and the question was whether a paper can mend a join that has already been read aloud, and about four of the nine people in that room heard her say it and about five heard the end of it.

On the Saturday he tore it up at the corner and dropped it in the lamp and watched about nine inches of it go brown, and the same thing had happened to a sheet of his own on the twenty-second of Mudmonth in a room above a boat-yard and about four people in two towns have since said that this man destroys his own work in front of witnesses and about five have said that he does it so that nobody can say the work was taken off him.

On the Sunday nothing happened, which is the only day in the five that he has not put down anywhere.

On the Monday morning six people went up thirteen stairs in that town. The woman of about forty. The man of about fifty with the fair hand who had copied twelve sheets in a passage in about four minutes. The woman who keeps that room. The copyist's apprentice of about twenty-six, who had been told to come and did not know why. The man of about forty-four who is clerk to the third province and whose seal is still not his. And him.

There are thirteen words in the deed of that room and the man who drew the deed is dead, and fourpence a year buys thirteen stairs, a floor, a press, a table and a window that does not shut on the windward side, and about nine people in that lane have read the thirteen words and four of them have read them twice.

On the top shelf of that press there are four copied pages with a date on the top one and no date on the other three, and above them there is a half leaf that lies face down and has lain face down since about four and a half years ago, and nobody in that room has turned it that week or any week, and the sixth fragment went onto that press on that Monday and not onto the top shelf and not beside the four.

About four of those six had wanted to mend the join and about five had not, and by the fourth hour of that Monday all six had stopped wanting either.

About four of them had wanted a seventh sheet and about five had wanted the paper to go on with the fault in it, and the second of those two was right and about four of the six had worked that out before the woman who keeps that room said anything at all.

She put them on the table side by side and squared the edges against the lip with the heel of her hand, which is what everybody who works at that table does without being told, and the third of the six was to the left and the sixth was to the right.

Then she said the fault out loud, and she said it in her own trade, and it was the woman who keeps that room who said it and not him, and that is the whole of what that Monday is for.

“**The third of these two is not wrong about a community and it is wrong about a person. A community that refuses at any hour without notice to anybody is refusing notice to itself, and then there is nobody on the other side of it to be told anything at all, and I am the woman who keeps the record of this room and I am telling you that a record which cannot show who was not told is not a record of what happened.**”

That was eighty-five words. Four of the six heard all of it and about two heard the end of it, and the apprentice of about twenty-six wrote nothing down and has said since that she was not asked to write anything down.

What she said cannot be taken back and that is not a matter of will. It was said in the room that keeps the record, about two sheets that are the record's own copies, by the person whose work it is to say what a record can and cannot show, and about four people in that room have said that a fault named once in the room where the copies are kept is a fault that lives there.

Before she said it he had done the thing that was his part of it and he did it without being asked and he did it in front of six people.

He took the sixth fragment out of the inside of his coat on the left where the hand goes, where it had been since the Saturday morning of the twenty-second of Mudmonth, and he unfolded it on the table and squared it against the other one, and then he put his hand flat on it and pushed it across the table to the woman who keeps that room.

He did not ask her to keep it. About four of the six in that room understood that he was not going to take it back and about five understood that he had never meant to keep it and had not known that until he let go of it.

What the sixth fragment is, now that it is not in his coat, is a page of about eleven lines in a hand that is not as good as the hand it copies. It says that nobody may do a thing to a road without telling the two people the thing happens to, being the one who keeps it and the one who stands on it, and that the telling comes before the doing and not after it.

It also says, on the fourth line, that telling a person is not the same as asking that person, and about four of the six people in that room had read that line before and about five had not, and the woman who keeps that room read it twice on that Monday, which is the only time anybody has seen her read anything twice.

What he said about it was nine words and it was not a speech anybody has written down.

It is in no document in that town and it is not on the day-book and the day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none, and about four people in that lane have said that a man giving a written thing to the person who keeps written things is either the beginning of a record or the end of a habit, and about five have said it depends entirely on what she does with it.

He did not feel that day like a man who had given something away. He felt like a man who had stopped carrying a sheet around a stair, and about four of the six in that room watched him not feel it that way and about five watched him feeling it that way, and neither of those was printed.

The register was shut on the table for the whole of that Monday and it was not opened at any point.

The claim entered on the ninth of Thawmonth still stands in that page as a claim with a reason written beside it in six words and the row still empty.

About four people in that lane have said that two rows in that register were left empty inside a fortnight of each other. About five have said that one of them was left empty because a man was four days off a made road, and the other because a woman rode four days and came back with a curved iron and no writing on it.

Then the woman of about forty said the second half of it, and the second half was hers and it had been hers since the Saturday above the boat-yard, and about four of the six in that room had known it since the reading on the Sunday and about five had not.

“**The third one is mine. I wrote it in about four days in that room above the boat-yard and I wrote it because my crew was shut out of a crossing in the year before last and nobody sent us word and we found out from a man selling lime, and that is the whole of my reason for it, and it is not a good enough reason, and I would write the same four lines again in the same four days.**”

That was eighty-two words. All six of them in that room heard every one of it, and the clerk of the third province put his pen down, which he had not done in three hours, and the copyist of about fifty wrote none of it down and has never claimed to have.

She stayed in the room. About four of the six would have gone out at that point and about five did not, and the one who went out was the clerk of the third province and he went out to the stair and stood on it and came back in at about the fourth minute.

Then the apprentice of about twenty-six asked the question the whole six weeks had been waiting for and she asked it in the plainest words anybody in that room had heard.

Nobody answered her. The copyist of about fifty said afterwards that there was no answer to give and that this was the first time in four and a half years he had been in a room where that was true, and the clerk of the third province said that there were three answers and all three of them were bad, and the woman of about forty said that there was one answer and it was hers and she would give it in a room with the province in it.

She asked who was going to write the seventh sheet.

The wind came off the coast and the window that does not shut on the windward side was doing the thing it does.

About four people in that lane have said that room has been cold at the top of those stairs for two years and about five have stopped saying it.

**And the cost of that Monday is four days of road ridden alone in the mud month and back with a curved iron on a sheet and no writing on it, a join about two inches long named out loud in the only room in nine counties where a claim can be checked, and a sixth fragment handed across a table and not taken back. Six people up thirteen stairs about two and a half days after the first of six fragments was written about nine miles from that room and four days from that room. The bleed five hundred and thirty-eight days old on the inside of one wrist, and it is not cured and it has had no panel and no page of its own and nobody in that town has asked him about it. A drawer eleven inches deep in a county he is not in, not opened once and not asked about. One ear, one leg, and a year, none of them on either of the two sheets and neither of them in the register, and the fourth ruled head of that page still empty and the day-book at eleven entries.**
