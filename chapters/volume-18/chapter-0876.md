# Chapter 0876

**Rainmonth the eighteenth, YR 325, a Saturday and a market morning in a market town of about two thousand people two days off a made road, a low workshop with a furnace at one end and a bench about nine feet long at the other and about six people in it, three of whom built the working and none of whom wanted to be in the room.**

The workshop is at the low end of a street of about nine hundred yards in a town of about two thousand people, and it is a shop that sells nothing anybody can see, and the furnace is lit four days a week and the glass goes out through a side door to four people who come for it on a Saturday morning.

It is a market morning in that town and the market is in the same street nine hundred yards up, and about four hundred people are in it, and about four of the six people in that workshop came up from the fire and have been in it since before the market opened.

She is sixty-eight and she cuts glass and she has not left that county in about nine years, and four people in nine counties know where she is and five have been told and did not believe it.

Aren Kest went to her. That is the whole of the errand and it took two days each way on a made road and about nine feet of cord and a stick, and four people in that county have said that a man who walks two days to ask a person a question is a man who could not ask anybody else.

The other two came with him as far as the county and then separately and later.

She is the first hand in nine books. About four of the six people in that workshop had worked that out from the shape of her hands before anybody said anything and about four had not.

What he wanted from that morning was the whole of how the working is put together, and he wanted it from the three people who made it and not from the man who has been using it for nine years.

That sentence was in a room at the top of that stair on the Thursday and about four of the nine people on that floor had heard it and about five had heard only the first half of it.

The bridgewright is fifty-one and laid the corridor of ten plates and the four courses under them, and he came because he was asked, and the timekeeper is forty-four and set the hours and he came because the bridgewright told him the man was going to be there.

Those two have not spoken in about nine years and neither of them said why in the first hour.

The furnace is at one end of that workshop and it was banked and not lit and the woman of sixty-eight lit it at about the eighth hour with nine hours of work in it and about four of the six people in that room understood what she was doing and about four did not.

The assessor came up that made road with him on the Friday and she had been in that workshop twice before in her life and both times she had been sent to look at something that was not glass.

She is an assessor of the Glasswright Cantons and she has never made a piece of glass in her life, and four of the six people in that workshop have said that a person sent to look at glass who cannot make it is the most useful person in the building and four have said it is the most dangerous.

What she had with her was a list of four ways a batch of glass can be wrong. She did not take it out that morning, and four of the six people in that workshop saw her put her hand on it twice and four did not notice her doing either.

What she made that morning was nothing anybody could use. She took four pieces out of a crate, looked at the edges of three of them, put one of them back, and swept the other three into the corner, and about four of the six people in that room watched a woman of sixty-eight lose about nine pounds of glass in about four minutes and about four of them have said that she does that every week and has for nine years.

Then she sat down on a stool at the bench and she asked for the whole of it and he asked the three questions he had come with.

The whole of it took until about the fourth hour of that day and it is written out here in nine lines because nobody in that room could write it down and he did.

A thing about nine feet across stands in a floor and answers a question put to it by a wall. It is asked twice. It answers yes or no and the answer is a thing nobody may argue with afterwards.

The second half of the mechanism is the part nobody will explain, and she was the one who explained it, and she did it by putting her hand flat on the bench.

When enough of the answers stop being yes, the working does not stop. It puts the shortfall somewhere. It always picks the shortest road that nobody is standing on, and about four of the six people in that room had heard that said before in about nine counties and about four had not heard it at all.

He had known it since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth as a thing that happened to somebody else, and about four of the six people in that room understood that he had known it in that shape and about four understood that he had known it in a different shape, which was that a road four hundred yards long under a hill was the cheapest place in nine counties to stop holding one.

About nine of the answers a day come from a thing about nine feet across standing in a floor nine hundred miles off and about nine of the answers a day come from a person, and there is no third kind.

That is the ninth point of the nine and it is the one he wrote on the sheet that went to the bridgewright.

He asked her how it chooses. She said that it does not choose and that that is the whole trouble, and that a thing which answers a question cannot be asked which question to answer next.

About four of the six people in that room had understood that and about four had needed it said again, and the timekeeper said it again and made it worse and then made it right.

Then the woman of sixty-eight asked him one question about the four wrong ways and he said he had not got that far, and she asked the assessor who had the list in her coat whether she knew the fourth one, and the assessor said she knew the fourth one and could not say where she had got it.

Four of the six people in that workshop understood what was being said and four understood only that two people had been in a room before and had not said so.

Then the two of them told him the things about his own road.

The bridgewright told him that the fourth plate in the corridor is a reading plate and not a holding one, and that about nine thousand crossings a day go through a floor that was built to be stood on and not to be leaned on, and that he had been leaning on it for four and a half years.

About four of the six people in that workshop had known that and about four had not and one of the four is the man with the stick.

The timekeeper told him that there were three hours once and not two.

The third hour was taken out about nine years ago and it is not in the nine books and it is not in the fourth book either, and about nine columns in nine books have a gap in them that nobody has ever filled and about four of the six people in that room had looked at those gaps and about four had not.

What he said about the third hour was that a person had been answering it by hand, for about four years, and that whoever it was did not go into the record, and that he had been the person who did it and had stopped.

Four of the six people in that room had got that and four had not, and the woman of sixty-eight said one thing about it and what she said was that the hand was quicker than the glass and that is why it stopped.

About four of the six people in that workshop have said since that this is the better of the two things he was told on that Saturday and about four have said it is the worse, and the two of those are the same observation with a different man in it.

Then he did the thing he came for, and it took him about an hour and he did it in three hands.

He wrote nine sheets. One in the glassmaker's terms and one in the bridgewright's terms and one in the timekeeper's terms, and he wrote all three from what the three of them had said and not from the nine books, and he compared them afterwards and they agreed on nine points out of nine.

He gave the three sheets to the three of them and kept none of them and has never had a copy of the whole of it in his own hand.

The sheets were numbered and he numbered them himself, and the numbers are one to three and not one to nine, and four of the six people in that workshop have said that he made nine points and gave away three of them and four have said that three trades is the number of people who can be in a room.

About four of the six people in that workshop have said that a man who writes a thing out in three trades' hands and gives all three copies away has done the only sensible thing anybody has done with it this year, and about four have said he should have kept the one that was his own trade.

Then the refusal came, and it came out of the ordinaryest thing anybody said all day.

The bridgewright said that the fourth hour used to be set by a mark on his own parapet, a notch cut nine years ago at the hour the water came over the sill, and that he rebuilt the parapet in the spring and put the notch nine inches to the left because the new coping was nine inches narrower.

He said it as a detail of a job and he did not know what he was saying, and the timekeeper heard nine inches and stood up off a stool.

What the timekeeper said was that the fourth hour has been asked four minutes early every day for about nine years and that the ninth mark in the fourth book against the fourth hour is the same nine minutes early every day and that he had checked it in the spring and had said nothing.

About four of the six people in that workshop understood that those two facts belong together and about four understood them separately and then together.

The timekeeper said that he would not be in a room with the bridgewright again and he said it in his own trade, and about four of the six people in that workshop heard all of it and about four heard the first half of it.

“**We rebuilt it and we did not tell each other and we are both right and I am not going to sit in a room with him about it.**”

That was twenty-nine words and it is the last thing said in that workshop on that Saturday and about four of the six people in it heard all of them and about four did not.

He did not arbitrate it. That is the finding of that Saturday and about four of the six people in that workshop have said since that a man in a room with a stick and no authority over two trades has no business arbitrating the price of an hour, and about four have said that he could have said one thing that would have settled it and did not.

He had wanted for nine weeks to be handed the whole of a thing from somebody who had built it, and what he got was nine points in three hands and two of the three people in that room would not be in it together, and he had wanted it whole and had got it in pieces, and about four of the six people in that workshop could see him deciding not to make them agree.

The woman of sixty-eight asked one question in that workshop and it was the only question she asked all day and it was about the third hour.

She asked whether the hand that answered it for about four years was ever going to be written down, and about four of the six people in that room had understood what she was asking and about four had not.

What she was asking was whether a person's four years of answering could be put in a record as a fact, and nobody in that workshop answered her, and the furnace was still banked at the fourth hour when they all went out.

**And the cost of that Saturday is two days each way on a made road for a man with a bad leg, about nine pounds of glass swept into a corner by a woman of sixty-eight, one plate of ten that is a reading plate and has been leaned on for four and a half years, one hour asked four minutes early for about nine years because a coping was nine inches narrower, one third hour with a gap of nine columns in nine books and nobody's name against it, three sheets written in three trades' hands and given away with no copy kept, and two men who will not be in a room together. Three hundred and ninety days of one man in a lower archive and he is not in this county. Eight anchor fragments where there were ten. One ear, one leg, and a year, and not one of the three of them is the hand that answered the third hour for four years.**
