# Chapter 0866

**Mudmonth the forty-ninth, YR 325, a Wednesday and not a market morning, the anchor floor of that place one storey above a corridor of ten plates and about two hundred steps below a yard where about nine men were doing nothing because it was not a market morning, a record room about nine paces by five with a table in it and a fire that had not been lit since Thawmonth, and four people at that table and nine more on the landing outside it.**

The two of them came up nine hundred yards of stair on the Tuesday and they came up together, which nobody on that place had expected and which about four of the nine men on the landing thought was a kindness and about five thought was a statement.

They had been told to come. He had sent for them himself on the Monday morning in a note of nine lines.

The note said that a clause had gone into a paper that anybody might read, and that he wanted them in a room with him before anybody else put anything in a column about it.

The man is about fifty-eight and keeps a crossing nine miles off a made road. He has kept it twenty-two years.

The woman is about forty-one and keeps a road in a community four days off in the mud month. She has kept it eleven years, and the road she keeps is the one whose refusal wording the third fragment is about.

Four people in nine counties have said the two of them were the two most obvious names in that list. Five have said they were the two who had most to lose by being on it.

What the man said when he got his wind back was that he had read the clause four times on the Monday and that his own hand had put the third fragment into the world and no, he had not, a woman he had worked beside for nine years had, and she had read it out in a passage above a boat-yard and he had heard it and had not understood it.

What the woman said was that the clause gave one person the right to question a refusal, once, and that a person who can question a refusal is a person the refusal has to be argued to, and that a community that has to argue its refusal has been asked to do a piece of work and the piece of work takes about nine days and the refusal takes about four hours.

Four of the nine people on that landing heard that and five heard the end of it. The officer holding the fourth floor was not on the landing and did not come in.

Then he said the thing he had gone up that stair to say and he said it to the two of them and not to the room.

He said that the cost of that clause was not going to land on the two of them, and that the four days and the nine days and the asking would be paid for out of somewhere that was not them, and that he had nine hundred crossings behind him and about nine thousand that morning and that a person who keeps a road for twenty-two years does not get asked to prove the road is a road.

Four of the thirteen people on that floor understood what he was offering and five heard the end of it.

The man of about fifty-eight put his hand flat on the table and said no, and he said it in his own trade and in eleven words, and about four of the nine people on the landing heard all of it.

“**I keep that road. I will not sign for a man.**”

That was eleven words and about five of the nine people on that landing heard only the first of them and about four heard both, and neither of those is the sentence anybody in that room has repeated since, and the eleven words have never been printed on any paper and are not in any document.

The woman of about forty-one did not say anything at all for about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, and then she asked him one question and it was the only question anybody asked in that room that day and it has never been printed in any document anywhere.

She asked him what the fourth column of that paper was for.

He said it was for the person who may question a refusal.

She asked him whether that person was him.

He said yes.

She asked him who had filled it in.

Nobody in that room could answer that. Four of the thirteen people on that floor tried and five did not try.

The truth is that nobody had filled it in at all. The column had been ruled and left empty in a room above a court in a main-world town on the thirty-eighth of Mudmonth, by a woman of forty-four who writes out what other people have to agree to.

What happened next took about four minutes and it is the whole of that Wednesday and no character in it can say it out loud.

The man of about fifty-eight said that he would withdraw a confirmation, and the woman of forty-one said the same, and they said it in the same four minutes and neither of them said it to him and both of them said it to the table.

A confirmation is not a thing a keeper can hand over. It is a thing a keeper does nine times a day in a corridor or at a gate or on a road about four days off, and it cannot be taken back by a man with a sheet, and four people in that room had worked out that before it was said and five worked it out afterwards.

They took the two of them back down nine hundred yards of stair on the Wednesday afternoon. They did not go together.

The count on that floor went from ten to eight between about the fourth hour and about the sixth hour of the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and nine men on that place watched it happen as a number in a book and not as anything else at all.

Then the Key, which is assembled out of ten anchor fragments and is the only reason the whole network can be forced toward one approved continuation, tried to do the only thing a thing made of ten pieces can do with eight of them, and it tried to compensate.

Nobody on that place has ever seen a compensation and nobody on that place has ever caused one, and Four people in nine counties have said the arrangement is not a machine and five have said a thing which answers a shortfall by closing something is a machine and that nobody built it and it built itself out of about nine hundred crossings and about nine questions.

The hill is four hundred yards long and the road runs in under it in a cut about nine feet wide with the stone dry-laid either side, and about nine hundred years is the age somebody has put on the stonework and nobody has checked it. There is a room at the middle of it with a lamp in it that has been kept burning every night since the breach by somebody who is paid in butter.

That lamp went out on the Wednesday afternoon. Not because anybody put it out. Because a thing about nine hundred miles away that had been holding two pieces of glass in a line decided that a road four hundred yards long under a hill was the cheapest place in nine counties to stop holding one.

A compensating closure is not a decision. It is a thing the working does when it is holding less than it was built to hold, and about four people on that place had seen one about nine years ago and about five had not seen one at all.

It put one narrow path anchor out of use.

The anchor is about nine feet of it and it stands in a floor about nine hundred miles from this one, in a place where the road runs in under a hill for about four hundred yards, and about four and a half years ago it was the best piece of work on the network and four people in nine counties have said so and five have never heard of it.

There were about four and a half years of good road under that hill and about nine hundred crossings a week going over the top of it into a community of about two hundred people, and the closure put the anchor out of use and did not take it out of use.

The people there survived. That is said here because it is the fact and not because it is the good part.

Their contact does not. It went on that Wednesday afternoon at about the seventh hour and it did not come back and about four people in nine counties have said that a road is not shut when the water is off it, and about five have said the other thing and have been right about the other thing.

What is lost is not goods and is not a trade and is not a person. What is lost is that nobody on either side of that four hundred yards of hill will be told anything about anything else for as long as the arrangement stands, and about four hundred people along that road found that out in nine days and five of them had been doing it for nine years without knowing there was a name for it.

What came back from that hill over the following nine days came back as nine things and not as one.

Four of the nine were about goods and were about the price of lamp oil and about a load of lime that had been on the road under the hill on the Wednesday morning and had been on the other side of it since. Three of the nine were about a wedding and a burial and about a woman of sixty-eight who had one of her own family on each side of that cut and had spoken to neither since the previous spring.

And two of the nine were about the assessor.

About four of what came back over those nine days was about goods and about five of it was about people, and none of the nine was about a road.

The assessor came up that stair on the Monday before and she was sent by the Glasswright Cantons and she has been on that place four days.

**“My name is Nissa Carrow. I assess anchor-glass and I have never made a piece of it in my life, and I was sent here to look at an anchor that has been damaged and I have stayed four days longer than the looking takes, and I have a list of four ways a batch of glass can be wrong and two of the four are in my own canton and my own canton has kept both of them quiet for money for about nine years.**”

That was eighty-six words and it is the only time in this manuscript that the name has been said out loud in a room by the person it belongs to, and about four of the nine men on that landing heard all of it and about five heard the first sentence.

She has stayed to write down what she saw. What she saw was a compensating closure and two people who had come up nine hundred yards of stair to take something back that nobody had the power to make them take back, and she wrote both of those down in a hand of her own in nine lines and she has said since that neither of the two is the interesting part and that the interesting part is the hill.

About four and a half years ago he had stood at the middle of that cut and put his own hand on that anchor and it had answered, and he has never told anybody that and it is not in any record and about four people in nine counties know that he was at that place in that year and about five of them know what he did there.

He did not tell anybody about the hill on that Wednesday.

Four people on that landing worked out that he had not said the name of the place out loud. Five worked out that he had said the word hill twice and had never once said a name. Neither of those is on any paper.

At about the ninth hour he took the book off the low table and opened it at the page with his name in the head of the form that had been on the hook.

He tore that page out along the fold where the four lines of the Wednesday before had gone in. He put it on the table in front of the assessor and did not put it in the press and did not put it back.

Four of the people on that landing saw him do it and five had gone to the stair.

**He is entered against him now, and it stands that he is a keeper nobody may rely on. That finding is on no board and in no book and in no mouth in that room, because the only place on that floor where a keeper is found untrustworthy is a thing nine hundred yards down that asks a question and has never yet had anywhere to put an answer.**

**And the cost of that Wednesday is two confirmations that cannot be handed back and about nine hundred yards of stair taken twice by a man of thirty-two with a bad leg, two people who came up together and went down apart, eight where there were ten and two plates in a corridor about four paces long that will never be answered again, and about four hundred yards of hill with a community of about two hundred people at the far end of it who are alive and who will not be told anything for as long as the arrangement stands. One narrow path anchor out of use for good and it was nine feet of it and it was the best piece of work on the network. One page torn out of a book along an old fold and put on a table and not into a press. The bleed five hundred and fifty-four days old on the inside of one wrist and not cured and no panel and no page of its own. The engine damaged three hundred and forty-five days and nobody mending it. Three hundred and thirty-one days of one man in a lower archive and he is not in that room and was not on that landing. One ear, one leg, and a year, none of them in the book.**
