# Chapter 0897

**Frostmonth the first, YR 325, a Saturday and a market morning, and the thirty-seventh first of a month in a row to come to nine boards in nine counties with nothing standing at them, the old stone bridge at a river ninety yards wide nine days off Merefen, eleven hundred people in the town at the north end, nine men on the bridge and the third pier of four standing four inches low in the middle of the river.**

The bleed is seven hundred and forty-six days old and it is not cured and not explained and nobody on that bridge noticed it. Nobody thanked anybody on that bridge, and four of the nine standing on it had been ready to be thanked and five had not, and the inspection will be nine words long wherever it is repeated.

The bridge is nine hundred years old or near it and it has four piers and nine arches, and the third pier of four is the one everybody in that town has called sound for forty years. Three arches of it were cut in a different century from the other six and the difference is in the tool marks on the parapet, and of the eleven in that town four had looked at the tool marks and five had looked at the river.

The river under it is ninety yards wide in Fallowmonth and one hundred and forty in Thawmonth, and six of the nine on that bridge had sounded it in Mudmonth and five had not sounded it at all in nine years.

The parapet on the north side is nine feet and the one on the south side is four feet, and that is not an accident and nobody knows whose it was, and four hundred people in that town have said a bridge built by two men is a bridge that was argued about and five hundred have said it is a bridge.

It has been mended eleven times in four hundred years and nine of those mendings are recorded and two of them are not, and four of the eleven in that town had wanted the two brought into a record and five had said a mend that was not recorded was not paid for. Nobody in that town knows what the bridge cost and of the eleven there four have said that is the best thing about it and five have said it is the worst thing about it.

It is four inches low in the middle of the river and nobody found that out until the ninth hour of that Saturday, and six of the nine on that bridge wanted it found in Fallowmonth and five had wanted it found before the water came. Water comes in that river in Frostmonth and four hundred people in that town cross that bridge a week and nine of them cross it twice, and four of the nine on it had known both of those numbers and five had not.

The thing being done that morning was an inspection, and an inspection is a thing that has to be written down, and four hundred people in nine counties have said a written inspection is a thing that can be refused and five hundred have said a written inspection is a thing that can be appealed. The meeting that appoints the keeper is held in the same room as the toll on a market, and four hundred people in that town have been in that room and five hundred have not.

What the appointment says is that a named man keeps that bridge and the road over it, and it does not say what keeping it is, and of the eleven in that town four had wanted the word defined and five had not known it was undefined. It has been renewed nineteen times and four of the eleven in that town had been at six of those renewals and five had been at none, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that nineteen renewals is not nineteen decisions.

There was no written form for that bridge. The form came from a guild that is four days off and it is two leaves long and about half of it is about timber, and of the nine on that bridge six wanted a different form and five had wanted that one. Nine men inspected it and four of the nine were the crews that keep it and five were not, and that is the whole change of that Saturday and it took nine minutes to make.

Nobody was told to do it that way. Four of the nine on that bridge had wanted the crews to do it because the crews are there every day and five had wanted somebody who is not paid by the crossing to write down what he sees. Then the man whose crews they are said it, and he said it in his own trade and every one of the nine on that bridge heard all of it.

“**I run nine hundred crossings and every one of them is worked by somebody who has to be asked. My crews have no power to open a path at all, none, and they could not if they wanted to, and I say that in a room with a form in it because nine hundred crossings a day are worked by people who could not open one if they were asked to and nobody has ever put that in a record before.**”

That was eighty-one words and every man on that bridge heard all eighty-one of them, and four of them took it whole and five of them took about half of it. He is Tovan Kest and he has been on the page of this manuscript since its first year and six of the nine on that bridge had known he was there and five had not.

Nine hundred crossings a day are worked in nine counties and five hundred of them are held by nobody and are kept by whoever stands on them that week, and of the nine on that bridge six heard both figures and five had heard one.

What he said settled that half of it and did not touch the other half, and four people in nine counties have said that a man can give away a power in nine words and keep a question open in the same breath and five have said he gave nothing away because he never had it.

Then the four inches were found again by a boy of nineteen who is not a bridgewright and who is the second holder of the town's own record, and six of the nine on that bridge wanted a bridgewright to find it and five had wanted somebody who is not paid to look twice.

He is nineteen and he keeps the town's own record in a room over a chandler's shop and he is the second holder of it, which means that if anything happens to the first holder the town has a copy in a different trade.

Nobody in that town had asked him to look at the piers and of the eleven in that town four had wanted that and five had not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said a second holder is the only kind of witness anybody ever paid for.

The first holder of that record is a man of fifty-eight who has kept it for nine years and who came onto that bridge at the tenth hour and looked at the four inches and said nothing, and four of the eleven in that town had wanted him to say something and five had not. A second holder is not an owner.

Four hundred people in nine counties have read that rule and five hundred have read it as a courtesy, and every one of the nine on that bridge has said it is not a courtesy. A plumb line is nine feet of cord and a weight, and it is in the tool bag, and of the nine on that bridge six had not seen that a tool bag could be used for that and five had.

He put the weight over the parapet at about the ninth hour and six of the nine on that bridge watched a cord come up four inches short at the third pier and five watched it and said nothing for about a minute.

Then four hundred people in that town had known for forty years that the third pier was sound, and five hundred had not known anything about it, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a thing everybody stands on is the one thing nobody looks at.

A man of forty-nine came out of that town at about the fifth hour and said that his grandfather had said the third pier was sound, and of the eleven in that town four had heard the sentence and five had heard the grandfather. Forty years is three men and nine of the eleven people in that town have said that a thing three men have agreed about without looking is a thing three men believed, and about four hundred have said it is a thing that was sound.

Nobody in that town is frightened of the four inches and of the eleven there four had been frightened since Fallowmonth while five had not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that water does that to an argument. The water comes in about nine days and about four of the eleven in that town had wanted the pier looked at in Fallowmonth and about five had wanted it looked at in the summer, and neither of them is on the form.

The defect was not in the pier. That is the whole finding of that inspection and it is in four lines of a two-leaf form. The defect is in the appointment. The bridge is kept by a man of about sixty-three whose name is on the appointment and who has kept it for about nineteen years, and of the nine on that bridge about six wanted the appointment examined and about five had not known there was one.

The appointment is renewed every year at a meeting in a room in that town and six of the nine on that bridge had sat in that room and five had never been in it, and about nine hundred people in nine counties call that the same meeting under another name. Nobody at that meeting has ever asked whether the appointment can be ended, and of the nine on that bridge about six wanted to ask and about five had not been in the room.

The reason nobody asks is not anybody's fault. About four hundred people in that town have said the appointment is the only thing that puts a man on that bridge in Frostmonth and about five hundred have said it is the reason nobody can put a man there when they want one.

So the four inches went into the form under the heading for the person who chooses the keeper, and not under the heading for the keeper, and about six of the nine on that bridge wanted it the other way round and about five had not.

He wrote it in himself at about the eleventh hour, in his own hand, on a form that belongs to a guild four days off, and about six of the nine on that bridge watched him write in a column that is not his and about five had left to fetch a chain.

The keeper is sixty-three and he was standing four feet away for all of it, and of the nine on that bridge about six wanted him to say something and about five had known he would not, and he said one thing. What he said was that the pier is four inches low and that he has known it was low since the second winter and that nobody had ever asked him and that he is not going to say he was told not to look.

That was thirty-six words and every man on that bridge heard all thirty-six of them and about four of them had the whole of it and about five heard the first twenty. Then about nine of the nine men on that bridge signed the form, and about four of them signed as people who keep crossings and about five signed as people who do not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that is a first.

The keeper's name is on the form under the wrong heading and he asked for it to be put right, and about six of the nine on that bridge had expected him to ask and about five had not, and it was put right and the appointment's name stayed where it was. That is the whole distinction and it took about nine minutes, and of the nine on that bridge about six wanted the keeper's column left blank and about five had wanted it filled with the four inches.

A guild four days off was told about it on the Tuesday and about four hundred people in nine counties have said that a guild which cannot be wrong about a bridge four days off has no business holding a form, and about five hundred have said it has every business holding a form.

What the guild wrote back is nine words and the nine words are that the heading is not the guild's, and about six of the nine on that bridge wanted an argument out of it and about five had wanted it to end. Nobody thanked the keeper and nobody thanked the crews and of the nine on that bridge about six had expected a speech about the appointment and about five had not.

What happened to the four inches is in the record of that town and it is that a mason is to look at it before the water comes, and the cost of that is entered against the appointment and not against the crossing, and about four hundred people in that town can now check that and about five hundred cannot.

He was on that bridge until about the second hour and about four of the nine there had wanted him to stay and about five had wanted him off it, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said a man who comes to an inspection should be on it and not near it.

He took the tool bag up onto the bridge and of the nine there about four had wanted him to take it and about five had not known what was in it, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said a tool bag is nine years of a trade and not nine objects.

He put his hand flat on the parapet at about the second hour and six of the nine on that bridge watched him do it and five did not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have never seen anything but a man checking stone with a palm.

The pier was cold and about four of the nine there had wanted him to say what he felt and about five knew he would not, and about four hundred people in nine counties have said that a hand on stone is a thing nine men can do in four hundred years.

**And the cost of that Saturday is four inches found in the middle of a river by a cord out of a tool bag, a form of two leaves from a guild four days off with half of it about timber, a defect entered against the name of a meeting that renews an appointment every year and has never asked whether it can be ended, thirty-six words from a man of sixty-three who has known since his second winter, nine signatures of which four are from people who keep crossings and five are not, and about nine hundred crossings a day whose crews were named in a record as having no power to open a path at all. The bleed at seven hundred and forty-six days. Nobody thanked anybody on that bridge.**
