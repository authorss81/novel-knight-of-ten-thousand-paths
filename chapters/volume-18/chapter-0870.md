# Chapter 0870

**Longlight the twenty-fifth, YR 325, a Thursday and a market morning in the town at the foot of about nine hundred yards of occupied stair, the room over the saddler's with nine chairs in it and about four hundred people buying what is on nine carts in the yard below, a lamp on the table and a pad of about nine sheets and a list of crossings on a second sheet, about four people in that room, and the interval not printed because nobody in it had asked for it.**

The woman who keeps the room over a weigh-house came over on the coast road on that Thursday morning with a book of her own and no register.

Four people in that town have said a woman carrying her own book up a coast road and not the book she keeps is a woman who has come to argue about a number.

She said in the first half hour that she would not do the arithmetic for him.

She said it in her own trade, before he had asked, and about four of the people in that room heard all of it and about three heard the end of it, which is the wrong way round and nobody in that room corrected it.

“**You have come up here to find out what you are still holding and what you owe and what anybody may still ask you for, and I am the wrong person to ask, because every answer I gave you would be a record and a record is the one thing this arrangement has been getting wrong for a fortnight, and about four people in this room know that and about three of us have not.**”

That was seventy-five words and it is the only time in this manuscript that the register has been described by anybody who keeps it, and about four of the people in that room heard all of it.

So he did it himself, on a sheet of about nine inches by six, with the lamp on his own side of the table, and it took about two hours and about four people in that town went on buying string in the lane below.

What is on that sheet is three columns and nine lines, and it is in no document anywhere and not in the register and not in the day-book.

The day-book stands at eleven entries.

A market morning in that town runs from the eighth hour to eleven and about four hundred people are in it and about nine hundred people along about nine miles of made road are not in it and will not hear about any of this.

He had come up that made road on the Monday with a lamp and about nine crossings still on a list and had got as far as the second one by the Thursday, and about four of the nine on that list have been looked at by somebody else this week and about five have not.

A list of about nine hundred crossings that nobody has looked at is not a piece of work. It is a piece of paper, and about four people in that town have said so and about three have said it is the only piece of paper in this business that is not trying to be anything else.

The ninth crossing on that list is four miles up a made road from the toll bar where a bar did not come up about nine inches on the fourth of Longlight, and he has not been there and about four people in that town have said he will not go and about three have said he will.

The three columns are in nobody's hand but his. There is no second copy and there is no carbon.

Four people in that town have said a thing written once and put in a tool bag is a thing that exists. Three have said it is a thing that can be lost in four miles of made road.

Four of the nine lines in the first column have a place in them and five have a word. Four of the people in that room understood the first column inside a minute and three understood it by the seventh hour.

The nine chairs in that room have still never been carried down an outside stair. They went up one at a time on the tenth of Thawmonth in front of nine people and have been carried about that room ever since.

Four people in that town have said nine chairs going up a stair means a room. Three have said it means a man who is about to need them for nine more people.

Four people were in that room on that Thursday morning and nine were in the yard below, and four hundred people were buying what was on nine carts. The lamps on those carts were lit at the fifth hour and went out at the ninth.

The first column is what he holds. Eight of the nine lines in it have a word in them that is not a number. The second column is what he owes, and about four of those nine lines have a name in them and five have not, and one of the five has a place in it, being four hundred yards of hill and a community of about two hundred people.

The third column is what he may still be asked to do, and about nine of the nine lines in that column have a tick against them and the ticks are not his.

He got as far as the third column at the ninth hour and read it out, once, in his own trade.

“**I can be asked to look at about nine hundred crossings and about nine of them I am not the person for, and I can be asked to be answerable for about four things of which one is a lamp, and I can be asked to stand about nine inches off a wall and say nothing, and there is about four hundred yards of hill that I am to be asked about never.**”

That was seventy-three words and about four of the people in that room heard all of it and about three heard the end of it.

The woman who keeps that room wrote one line in her own book at that point and about four of the people in that room did not see her do it.

Then she put her pen down and said the only other thing she said that morning, in her own trade.

She said that a register is a page with questions on it and no answers, and that about four of the ten questions in the one four days off have been put by her and about three of them have never been put to anybody, and that a question with no answer under it is not a failure and is a thing that has been left open on purpose by somebody who was not frightened of the answer.

She said that in forty-nine words and about four of the people in that room heard all of it and nobody in that room wrote it down.

He had stopped being the person the arrangement was built around somewhere in the two hours and he did not find out when.

Nobody in that room told him and nobody in that room wanted him to stop. Four people in that town have said since he did not find out when because he was doing arithmetic. Three have said because nobody was going to be the one who said it.

Nine weeks is the length of the middle. It is long enough that nine people in nine counties stopped counting the days of it and four of them started.

What he gave up on that Thursday morning is written in three columns on one sheet. It is not signed by anybody and it is not witnessed.

Four people in that room have said that is the first thing anybody has handed over in nine weeks without a seal on it. Three have said a thing handed over without a seal on it can be taken back by whoever has the sheet.

The finding came at about the tenth hour out of the second sheet, which was the list.

The room over the saddler's is about nine paces by five with a fire in it and nine chairs in it and a low table, and it was taken on the eighteenth of Thawmonth and has been used since by about four rooms of people at different times, and the fire has not been out in it since.

The list of crossings to look at had been copied out four times since the twentieth of Mudmonth. The copy on that table was the fifth.

Four lines on it had a name against them and nine hundred lines had not, and the four names on it were not his.

One of them was a woman of thirty-one who walks a lantern run in a valley nine days off. One was a man of fifty-eight who kept a crossing nine miles off a made road until the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and who had stopped keeping it.

One was the keeper of a gate in a town of nine hundred people where a bar did not come up nine inches. And one was a woman of thirty-four who had come down a coast road with two children and nine bundles in her arms and who now holds the only record of a valley there is.

Four people in that room worked out what a list with other names on it is. Three of them had worked it out on the Friday before.

He got up out of the chair at the tenth hour and picked up the tool bag he had carried up that outside stair on the tenth of Thawmonth and had not put down since.

He put the sheet of nine inches by six into it, and put the pad on the table, and left the second sheet where it was, because it was not his.

Then he said the last thing said in that room that morning, and he said it to the room and not to the woman who keeps that room.

“**There is about nine hundred crossings on that list and about four names on it and none of them is mine, and I have been the middle of this for about nine weeks and about nine people in nine counties have wanted me in the middle of it, and I am going to go and be about nine feet off a wall somewhere and I would rather somebody told me the order of the work than tell me where to stand in it.**”

That was eighty-three words and about four of the people in that room heard all of it and about three heard the end of it.

Nobody in that room thanked him for it and nobody in that room argued with it.

**And the cost of that Thursday is three columns and about nine lines on a sheet of about nine inches by six that went into a tool bag and not into any document, about four hours of arithmetic done by one man in a room over a saddler's because the person who keeps the records would not do it for him, and one register four days off that holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and a fourth ruled head empty since the first of Hearthmonth two years back. Eight anchor fragments where there were ten on the twenty-sixth of Goatmonth. The bleed five hundred and ninety days old on the inside of one wrist and not cured and not explained and no panel and no page of its own and nobody in that room asked him about it. Three hundred and sixty-seven days of one man in a lower archive, and he is not in that room and has not come out of it. The engine damaged three hundred and eighty-one days and nobody mending it. One ear, one leg, and a year, and none of the three is on the list of about nine hundred crossings and none of the three is in the third column.**
