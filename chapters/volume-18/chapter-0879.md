# Chapter 0879

**Harvestmonth the sixteenth, YR 325, a Saturday and a market morning in a harbour on the coast four days off a made road, a room over a net-loft with a long table and about nine people in it, about four hundred people on the quay below between the eighth hour and the eleventh, and the interval fifteen hours.**

The harbour is a river mouth with a quay on the west side and four hundred people on it between the eighth hour and the eleventh on a Saturday, and the market is on the quay and has been on the quay since the year a hundred and forty.

There is a point nine miles off with a light on it and a reef inside that light two miles out, and nine boats a week come in and about four of them go out on a Tuesday and a Friday and one of them is a boat of nineteen tons that has made the crossing nine hundred times.

The record is four bound books on a shelf above a tide table and it is nineteen years of tide, wreck and landing in one man's shorthand, and it has never been copied.

About nine people in nine counties know it is not copied and about four of them have asked to copy it and about five have never asked anybody for anything at all.

The man who keeps it is forty-seven and he wrote the first of the four books when he was twenty-eight, and he is the only person alive who can read what he wrote in them.

That is the whole difficulty and it is not about trust. About four of the nine people in that room had come to that room expecting it to be about trust and about five had not known before the ninth hour that it was about a shorthand nobody else has learned.

The four books are eleven inches by nine and the first of them is in a piece of oiled cloth and the other three are not, and they were all four begun on the same day in YR 306 and they are not a set and they were never meant to be one.

There are nine hundred entries in the first and about nine hundred in the fourth and the second and the third are shorter and are the only two that anybody has ever read besides the man who wrote them.

The shorthand is not a cipher and it is not a code. It is a hand of nine hundred signs and about two hundred of them are ordinary words written wrong on purpose, and four of the nine people in that room can read the four words on the front of the first book and about five can read nine hundred signs out of nine hundred.

What he came down the coast for on that Saturday morning was a second hand.

The woman who keeps the net-loft above that room is thirty-one and her loft has a mending floor with nine women on it on a Saturday and the record is on her coast and has been for nineteen years without her asking for it.

She offered the room and offered the table and four of the nine people in it have said that she offered it because the record is on that coast and not on his, and about five have said because she wanted to see the four books.

Aren Kest would not teach it in a week. That is the finding of that morning and four of the nine people in that room had believed he would and about four had not.

He said that nineteen years of a shorthand cannot be taught in a week by anybody and that it cannot be taught in a year by anybody who is not doing it every day, and that nine hundred signs take about nine years, and that he had got them in about four because he had started at thirteen and had written every day of it.

About four of the nine people in that room understood what he had said and about four understood only the arithmetic and about one person in that room said the arithmetic out loud twice.

Then he said the thing he had not come down that coast to say, and he said it about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, and nine people in that room heard it.

He said that nineteen years ago, in the winter of YR 318, when there was ice in that harbour for nine weeks and about nine boats were lost off the point, he refused a copy of the first two books to a man he liked.

Four of the nine people in that room had known the refusal was coming and five had not, and the second keeper of a crossing on that coast said afterwards that he had heard the story in four counties and that this was the first time he had heard it in a room with the man in it.

The year is YR 318 and there was ice in that harbour for nine weeks and about nine boats went off the point in a single night in the second week of it, and four hundred people who were on that quay then are on it now and nine hundred are not.

What that winter did to the coast is not the point of the story and he told it in nine sentences anyway, because four of the nine people in that room had not been born and about four had been.

What the reason was, he gave in nine words, and they were good.

He said that the man he refused it to took a copy out to a place nine days off, and that the road to that place was closed for nine weeks in the following year, and that the copy went in with him and nobody has seen it since.

“**He took it out. The road shut for nine weeks. I never saw it again.**”

That was fifteen words and all nine people in that room had every one of them, and four of the nine heard the years in them and about five heard only the middle sentence.

Nobody in that room argued with him. About four of the nine people there had wanted to argue and about four had looked at the woman of thirty-four's face in a lane about nine counties back and about four had not wanted to at all.

That is the good reason and it is exactly right and it is also the reason nobody in nine counties has had a copy of that record for nineteen years.

He cannot produce the paper. That is the second finding of that morning and four of the nine people in that room asked for it and about four did not ask, and he said that the paper was not a paper, it was a thing said on a quay, and that the year is the year and the reason is the reason and that nobody wrote either of them down.

Four of the nine people in that room have said since that turning a copy down for a sound reason and not being able to produce the sound reason is not a man hiding anything, and about four have said that he is a man protecting himself, and that both of those are one act and not two.

Then the second hand was found in the room and nobody in that room had thought to look for one.

It is a man of about twenty-nine who sails out of that harbour on a Tuesday and a Friday and has sailed for about eleven years and whose own grandfather and the man before him both sailed out of it, and he reads nine words of the shorthand by accident because he learned the two signs for a shoal and a wreck off the knee of the woman who taught him.

About four of the nine people in that room had not known he had any of it and about four knew and about four have said since that nobody had thought to ask him because he has never once asked anybody for anything.

Nobody asked him twice. That is the whole of what that room did with the no and four of the nine people in it had expected to ask him twice and about four had not.

He said no. That is the third finding of that morning and four of the nine people in that room had expected the no and about four had not, and he gave a reason in eleven words and the reason was that he has a boat on Tuesday and cannot carry books and does not want a second thing to carry.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that he said the true thing and about four have said that the true thing was not the reason.

What changed on that Saturday is not that a second man agreed to learn it. It is that the record was read out.

About nine people in that room heard four years of it read aloud in an hour by a man of forty-seven with his finger on the page, and about four of them understood a part of it and about five understood the shape of it, and one of the five was the woman of thirty-one who keeps the net-loft and has since been able to find nine hundred things in it that are in no other book anywhere.

The tradesman of the bridge crews came down that coast on the Friday and he is a man this manuscript has known since the first year of it and he did not come about the record.

Tovan Kest came about a crossing four miles up the coast road where a sill had gone out under a cart in the thaw, and he stood in that room and listened to an hour of a shorthand he cannot read, and four of the nine people in that room have said that he understood more of it than anybody else in there and about five have said that he understood the tide parts and not the wreck parts.

Then he asked the one question that changed the shape of that Saturday.

He asked where a second copy would be, and the man of forty-seven said that he had been asked that for nineteen years and that the answer is nowhere, and that a second copy in a second building is a second thing to lose.

What Tovan Kest said was that a second copy in the ordinary post in the ordinary week to a person who will answer for it is not a second thing to lose, because the post is the one thing in nine counties that has never once stopped.

About four of the nine people in that room heard that as an argument and about four heard it as a fact about a road, and the man of forty-seven heard it as a fact about a road and four of the nine people in that room have said that he had been arguing with himself about it for nineteen years and that a bridgewright came four miles to do it in nine seconds.

About four of the nine people in that room had said that the whole errand was a key and about five had said it was a man learning to write, and about four of the nine people in that room have said since that it was neither and that what happened was that a man of forty-seven found out what his own handwriting sounded like in another pair of ears.

Then a key to the shorthand was written out.

It is nine sheets and it is nine hundred signs and it took two of them from the ninth hour until about the second hour of the Sunday, and four of the nine people in that room watched it being written and about five went and did something else.

The four bound books were not copied and are not copied. What was made is a key, and four of the nine people in that room have said since that a key is not the record and is a way of reading the record and that this is a smaller thing than a copy and a larger thing than a promise.

The sharing is the point. About four of the nine people in that room have said that nobody lost anything by reading it out loud and about four have said that a man who has kept a thing in his own shorthand for nineteen years and then read it out for an hour to nine strangers has not lost a thing, and about four have said that he has lost the thing about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice and has got it back.

Then the copy was tied up, and the address went on it in his own hand, and nine people in that room read the address and about four of them did not know the name on it.

It is a person's name and not a place. It is not a town, it is not a court, it is not a market and it is not a road office. It is Tovan Kest of the bridge crews, and the wrapping sheet has about four folds in it and about nine days of ordinary post between that harbour and the crossing four miles up the coast road.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that a thing addressed to a place is put on a cart and looked for, and that a thing addressed to a person is put on a cart and brought, and that the difference between those two is nine hundred years old and is the whole of what the record needed.

On the Friday after, the sill that went out under the cart was relaid by about nine men of the bridge crews in about four hours.

Tovan Kest was on that coast road for two days and did not open the wrapped sheet in front of anybody. Four of the nine people in that town who saw it go up the coast road with him have said that he carried it the whole way in his coat, and about five have said that he put it in a tool bag.

He put it in a tool bag, and about four people in that town said so afterwards and nobody wrote it down.

The carrier came at the fourth hour on the Sunday and it goes twice a week and it comes on the Friday and it does not come for anybody who asks it to.

**And the cost of that Saturday is nineteen years of a shorthand read out loud for about an hour and about nine hundred signs taken out of a man's shorthand and written onto nine sheets in a room over a net-loft, one refusal from YR 318 that cannot be produced because it was said on a quay, one refusal of a second learner that was true, four hundred people on a quay and about nine hundred along a coast road who are not in that room, and one wrapped sheet with a person's name on it going out on the Friday in the ordinary post. The bleed six hundred and forty-one days old and not cured and not explained and no character in that room has noticed it. The interval fifteen hours. Eight anchor fragments where there were ten. One ear, one leg, and a year, and not one of the three of them can write a shorthand.**
