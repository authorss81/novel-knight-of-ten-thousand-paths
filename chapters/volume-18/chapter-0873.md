# Chapter 0873

**Rainmonth the sixth, YR 325, a Monday and not a market morning in the market town of about two thousand people four days off the coast, thirteen stairs up from a lane to the room over the weigh-house with the register open on the table and a day-book beside it, about nine people in that room, and the interval thirteen hours.**

The weigh-house stands at the low end of that lane and it weighs carts and it weighs nothing else, and the room is thirteen stairs above it and the stairs are in the open and everybody in that lane uses them, and about four hundred people come up them in a year and nine of them come up twice and five of them have never come up at all.

On a Monday morning that weigh-house weighs nothing at all before the ninth hour, and the carts stand in the lane in the order they arrived, and there were nine of them standing there on the sixth of Rainmonth, and two of them had been standing there since the Friday.

Aren Kest came up those thirteen stairs on the Monday with a sheet folded inside his coat and the sheet was not a copy of anything. Thirteen stairs is four flights and a turn and it took him about as long as it takes a man to read a page slowly, and he has never once told anybody in that lane what that costs him and nobody in that lane has ever offered.

There is no second copy. Three columns and about nine lines went into a tool bag on the twenty-fifth of Longlight and about four hundred people have not seen them and about nine have heard of them.

What he wanted on that Monday was one line, and the line was about the four and a half years, and the line was true.

It said that for four and a half years the question that every anchor on the network was asked was put by him and by nobody else, at about the fourth hour and about the tenth hour, and that where a road could not be opened it was his word that opened it and not a vote and not a form and not a count.

Mara Vey read it standing up, with the register open on the table in front of her, and she read it twice.

Four of the nine people in that room understood what he had come up those stairs for inside a minute, and five of them had understood it again by the time she put it down.

Nobody wrote anything for about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice.

The nine people in that room on that Monday were the woman who keeps it, the woman of thirty-four with the nine bundles, a carter of twenty-two, and a tanner who has kept a shop in that lane for forty-four years.

The other five were a woman of thirty-one who keeps a dairy at the high end of the lane, a man of nineteen apprenticed to the tanner who had not said anything all morning, a woman of about fifty who does other people's washing and came up on the strength of hearing the sheet read out in a lane on Friday, and two people from a town four miles off who came about a bond and stayed.

The four of them who wanted his line entered were the carter, the dairy woman, the tanner and the woman of fifty.

Three of those four have known him for between two and nine years and have eaten at his table in that lane inside that time, and about four people in that lane have said that is why the line wanted to go in and about four have said it is not the reason at all.



Then the woman of about thirty-four put her hand flat on the top of the nine bundles she had carried up a coast road on the tenth of Longlight and said the thing that made the rest of the morning happen.

She said that if it goes in as true then the paper has a man in it again, and that every place which chooses its own keeper is going to read that line and hear itself told what it is, and that she did not come four days down that road to be told what she is by a person who was never in her valley.

About four people in that lane have said since that a person who has lost the contact with a place is the last person anybody thinks to ask and the only one who can answer. About four have said that is a different pair of sentences.

The carter of about twenty-two came up those stairs with the rest of them and had not been asked to and was about to be about nothing at all that morning.

What he said about the line was not an argument against it and it is the only thing anybody in that room said that nobody could repeat afterwards.

He said that if it is in as true then it is the only sentence about the last four and a half years that anybody can check, and that if it is in as a claim then in about nine years somebody will find the sheet and read the six words beside it, and that six words is better than nothing.

About four of the nine people in that room had understood what he meant and about five had understood what he had not said, which was that a carter of twenty-two who has been on four roads in his life is the person in that room most likely to still be there in nine years.

Then the woman of about thirty-four said that he was right about the six words and wrong about the nine years.

She said that in nine years the road to her valley will either be open or it will not, and that if it is open there will be a person there who can look at the sheet and say what it means, and that if it is not there will be nobody, and that the six words are worth more to the second of those than to the first.

About four of the nine people in that room understood that at once and about four had to have it said again in shorter words.

The register in that room holds ten questions and no finding has ever gone under any of them. The fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth two years back, and no fifth head is ruled anywhere in that town.

The day-book stands at eleven entries. It has stood at eleven entries on every dateline of two parts and it is not going to stand at twelve because of a Monday morning.

The press in the corner of that room stands open with no date on it, and it stood open through all of it, and about four of the nine people in that room had looked at it once each and about five had not looked at it at all.

A leaf at the back of that day-book carries nine figures and a date and the names of nobody, and it is not the record and has never been offered as the record, and the nine figures on it are the same nine figures it has carried for about two years.

She would not enter it.

That is the finding of that Monday and it took about four minutes and it was not argued and nobody in that room was asked to agree with it.

What she did instead was take a clean sheet, which is not a leaf and not a page and not a head, and write his sentence on it in one line in her own hand.

Then she wrote six words beside it.

“**He claims it, we cannot check.**”

That was six words and it is the reason, and it is written in her own hand and not in his and not in a book and not in the register, and all six of them were read out loud in that room by the man they were about.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that those six words are the whole of what an Archive is for and about four have said that they are six words and that a county could have written them.

The word on that sheet is check and not confirm, and about four people in nine counties have said that the difference between those two words is the whole difference between a record and a notice, and that the word confirm was in an earlier version of that sentence and was taken out on the Friday and nobody has said out loud who took it out.

The row stayed empty. That is what an empty row with a reason beside it is, and it is the second one inside about a month, and the first was on the fifth of Longlight and was about a bar that came up about nine inches twice with no explanation offered to anybody.

There is no first of a month on that page and there is not going to be one.

He did not argue.

That is the second finding of that Monday, and four of the nine people in that room noticed that he did not argue, and five of them had expected him to argue for about nine minutes and had come up those stairs ready for it.

What he did instead was read the six words out loud, twice, and then ask one question about them and it was not a question about the six words.

He asked who checks a claim, and she said anybody who was not in the room when it was written, and he asked whether anybody in nine counties had been in that room, and she said no.

He asked what happened to a claim nobody could check in about nine years, and she said that it stopped being a claim and became a thing that one person said once, and that about nine hundred people along about nine miles of made road would not know which of those it was.

Then she said the other thing about the record, and it is the thing that room is for, and she said it before anybody had asked her about it.

She said that there is exactly one record in nine counties that nobody can argue with about quantity, and that it is a set of brine weights kept by a man who is dead, and that the weights are the record and not anything the man said about them, and that about four hundred years of somebody else's mistakes can be checked with a steelyard and about nine years of a person's word cannot be checked with anything.

About four of the nine people in that room had never heard of the weights and about five had heard of them and one of the five had been in the room where the weights are.

He had not asked for that either.

Then she put the sheet down on the table beside the register, and she did not put it in the press and she did not put it in the day-book, and she put her hand flat on it for about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice.

What she said then was in her own trade and every person in that room heard all of it.

“**You have been the middle of this for about nine weeks and you want a line in a register that says you were the middle of it for four and a half years before, and both of those are true, and I will write the second one down for you today as a claim and not as a fact, and if you ask me for the other thing I will not do it in this room or in any other.**”

That was eighty words, and about nine people at that table heard every one of them and none of them wrote any of it down.

About four people in that lane have said that she was not refusing him and that she was refusing the arrangement, and that he had brought her a sentence about himself and she had made him bring it back about the work.

About four have said that she did him the greater service and that neither of those two things is a thing anybody thanks a person for.

He wanted, on that Monday, one sentence in a book saying what the last four and a half years had actually been, and there is nobody else alive who can write that sentence and never was.

Then the woman of thirty-four said the second thing, and she said it to the sheet on the table and not to him.

She said that if the claim stands in the record as a claim then a person in nine counties can read it and check it against whatever they can find, and that about nine years is not very long, and that in nine years there will be about nine hundred people who were not in the room and can look at six words instead of at him.

He picked the sheet up off that table at about the ninth hour and he read it once more and he put it in the inside of his coat on the left where the hand goes.

Four of the nine people in that room watched a man put a sheet away instead of giving it to them, and five of them had expected to be asked to hold it.

He had wanted, since he went up, to hand that sheet to somebody in that room and walk out of a lane with a thing off him, and he stood there with it in his hand and worked out that handing it over would have made him a man with a record in that town and not a man with a claim, and that he would have been easier to find in nine years and not harder.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that he did not want the sheet gone and that he wanted the sheet kept by somebody, and about four have said that those are the same thing and that one of the two is a harder want.

The register was shut at the eleventh hour with the fourth ruled head of that page still empty, the day-book at eleven entries, the press standing open with no date on it, and one clean sheet with a claim and six words on it in an inside coat pocket going down thirteen stairs.

About nine people along that lane are aware that a man came up those stairs and went down them again and about four of them have said that he came up with a sentence about himself and went down with a claim about the work.

**And the cost of that Monday is four and a half years of the network's own account of itself that is not in any record, one clean sheet with a true sentence and six words beside it going down thirteen stairs in an inside pocket, one row left empty with a reason and one register shut with ten questions and no finding under any of them and a fourth ruled head empty since about the first of Hearthmonth two years back. The day-book at eleven entries. The interval thirteen hours. One narrow path anchor out of use for good. An ear, a leg and a year, and not a thing anybody can check about any of the three.**
