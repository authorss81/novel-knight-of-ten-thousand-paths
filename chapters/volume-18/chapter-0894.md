# Chapter 0894

**Wolfmonth the fifteenth, YR 325, a Thursday and a market morning on the count anybody keeps here, the long room over the customs house at a river crossing with a door at the bottom of four steps, one table of nine feet, about eleven people at it, and two books that came into the town on the cart from the coast on the Wednesday.**

The bleed is seven hundred and thirty days old and it is not cured and no one of the eleven people at that table has noticed it. Nobody thanked anybody in that room, and about four of the eleven there had said a week before that somebody would say something and about five had said that nobody would, and about nine hundred people in nine counties will be told about that room in about nine words each. What the room was for was exhibits.

That is the whole of it and there is nothing else on the table except nine sheets of paper, a lamp, a jug, and two books. The customs house is a hundred and forty years old and the room over it has been used for four different things, and one of them was a place where people who had crossed a river without paying were made to stand in a line, and four of the eleven at that table had been in that line and five had not.

The town is about two thousand people and the river is forty yards wide in Fallowmonth and about ninety in Thawmonth, and the crossing has four piers and about nine of them are sound, and of the eleven at that table four had checked them and about five had not.

Exhibits are heard in that room because it has one table of nine feet and a door with four steps and about nine benches, four hundred people in nine counties have said that a room is chosen for its furniture and about five have said it is chosen so that the people behind can be seen.

The first of the two books is about two hundred leaves of a yard book, and it belongs to an anchor yard that keeps anchors, weighs them, and then either seals them into a road or sells them, and the yard's whole argument with the world is in the weights column. Halven kept that book for nineteen years and he is in that room and four of the eleven at that table had not expected him to be and about five had.

He put it on the nine feet of table himself at about the ninth hour and did not sit down, and four of the eleven sitting there had seen the book before and about five had only seen it described. He kept it for nineteen years and he began as a boy in that yard at about fourteen, and of the eleven at that table four had known him at a different trade in a different county and five had only ever heard of the book.

A yard book is not a book of accounts. It is a book of what left, what it weighed, who took it and what was paid, and the price is in it because a thing with a price on it is a thing somebody chose, and four of the eleven there had not heard that argument and about five had.

The yard is four days off by cart and it is still working, and four of the eleven in that room had been told this year that it is still working and five had heard it in a lane. The four pages that are in another hand are the ninth of a month in the year of the culling, and they are four consecutive entries and not four pages of argument, and four of the eleven sitting there had wanted them bound in and about five wanted them loose.

Then he said the thing about it in his own trade, and it is that a book of weights is the only kind of book that cannot be argued with after the event, and of the eleven in that room about nine heard all of it and about two heard the first half.

“**Four pages of this are in a hand that is not mine and they are the four that matter, and what they say is that nine anchors left this yard weighed and were paid for and were not sealed, and a man can seal an anchor he has not weighed but he cannot weigh one he has not got. Nine is not a mistake. Nine is a Tuesday.**”

That was sixty-eight words and every person in that room heard all sixty-eight of them, about four of them with all of it and the other five stopping somewhere around the fortieth word. Then four of the eleven in that room said that nine anchors which were sold are nine pieces of road that somebody paid for twice, and about five said it is nine pieces of road that somebody paid for once and sold.

He said the other half of it himself, unasked, and four of the eleven at that table had not expected a man to hand his own yard the second half of an argument.

What he said is that a sealed anchor goes into a road and stays there and cannot be weighed again, and that nine of them were weighed and paid for and did not go into any road, and that the yard's own book says so in nine words and has said so for four and a half years.

Then he said the thing that stopped the room, which is that there is no anchor left anywhere in this business, none, and that a corridor of ten plates nine hundred yards up a stair has had nothing in it that answers anybody since the ninth of Embermonth. That was the count said out loud in that room and it is the whole of the year said in nine words, and of the eleven at that table about nine heard all of it and about two heard the first half.

Then the second book came in, and it came in in the hands of a man who has not left that place nine hundred yards up a stair and who is about forty-one and who was not asked to come and came. The second of the two books is the refuge register, and four of the eleven in that room knew what it was before it was put down and five had only heard the word.

It is a bound book of about six hundred leaves and it is a list. It is a list of people, by street, by household, and by a column headed in a fair hand, and the column says which of them counted as somebody who had come from somewhere. That column is the instrument.

It is the piece of paper that made about nine hundred people into a category, and four of the eleven sitting there had known that on the first page and about five knew it by the time the book was open at the fortieth.

A person in that column can be a resident and a person outside it cannot, and the difference between the two is not a fine and is not a judgement and is one word in a fair hand at the head of a column, and four of the eleven at that table had not thought about a column being a decision and about five had.

What it does is the size of a nail, being one word that nine hundred people are then counted by, and of the eleven in that room four had wanted the word reduced and about five had said that a nail is not less for being a nail.

A page of that book was turned over at about the fourth hour on the first day and the first name on the next page is a household of six and the column beside it says resident, and four of the eleven there knew that household and five had not known anybody on that page. That was not said out loud.

About four of the eleven sitting there had wanted the household read out and five had said that a household on a list is a household with a door open, and nobody read it out. Nobody in that room called it a list of names. It is a list of people who had a different name in it from the one they went by.

Four of the eleven there have said since that a book which changes what a person is called is not a record and is a decision written in a cramped hand, and about five have said it is a record and the decision was taken before the book.

The man of about forty-one said one thing in that room and said it once, and four of the eleven at that table had known he would and about five had not expected it from a man who had carried a book up four steps.

“**I put this in a cart because a cart is not a hand and a hand can be asked what it is holding. Nobody has ever asked me what this is holding and I would have said road furniture if they had, and I would have said it in a room with nine people in it, and I have thought about that for about four days.**”

That was sixty-six words and of the eleven at that table four heard all sixty-six of them and about five heard the first thirty. The book was put on the table beside the yard book and the two of them lay there for four days, and four of the eleven sitting there wanted them nine feet apart and about five wanted them touching, and they lay touching.

Those four days are in the record of that room as four lines and not as four days, and four of the eleven in it had wanted the four days written out and about five had said that what happened between the two books is not a thing anybody can be shown. What happened between them was that people came and looked at them.

About four hundred people in that town came up those four steps over four days and about nine of them touched nothing and about five hundred did not come at all.

A woman of about thirty-eight came on the second day and read about forty lines of the register and then asked whether her own street was in it, and it is, and four of the eleven people at that table had known it was and about five had not, and she went back down the four steps without saying anything.

A man of about sixty came on the third day and wanted to know what his wife had been counted as, and nobody could tell him, and four of the eleven there had wanted somebody to look it up and five said that a lookup in that book is a thing that cannot be undone once it is made.

Then the thing that made the four days is that somebody in a council wanted the second book read out in a market square on a Saturday, and about four hundred people in nine counties wanted the same thing and about five hundred had not heard of it at all.

The argument against it took about an hour and it is the argument that a register is an instrument and an instrument belongs to the trial, and that a market square is not a trial, and of the eleven at that table about four had wanted the reading and about five had not.

What they did instead was enter the second book in the record of the trial that has been ordered and has not been held, and of the eleven at that table about nine knew what that meant and about two had not known there was a trial.

That entry is four lines long and it says that the register is the instrument by which the culling was carried out in writing, and that the trial is ordered, and that the register is exhibited, and that nothing in that room is a finding.

Of the eleven at that table about four had wanted the four lines to say what the register did and about five had wanted them to say what the register was, and what the register was took four days and what it did took four lines.

Then on the second of the four days a carter came up those four steps with a slip of paper, and it was from a road eleven days off, and it was not the cut and it was a different road, and four of the eleven in that room had been there that morning and about five had not.

The slip says that on the ninth hour of the day before, the people at the far end of that road had put a bar across it and had said in nine words that the road is shut until the children are older. The slip is one of about four hundred of them that week and it came in the ordinary post with a crease in it and the name of the far end on the outside and nothing on the outside about what was in it.

About four hundred people in nine counties put a bar across a road in that year and about nine hundred wanted to and about five hundred had reasons, and four of the eleven at that table had been in one of those four hundred and about five had not.

What the far end did was shut the far end and leave the near end open, so that anybody who wanted to come in could still come in, and four of the eleven sitting there had read that as a breach on the way in and about five had read it as the reason they were being asked for nothing.

Nobody in that room asked for a rider and of the eleven at that table four had wanted one for four days and five understood on the first day what a rider is. There are nineteen children in the four streets at that far end and about nine of them are under nine years old, and four of the eleven there had wanted the number entered and about five had wanted the reason entered.

Then the clerk found the form, and that is the whole of the resistance of those four days, and it is not anybody's fault at all. The form has four headings and they are date, place, cause, and remedy, and there is no heading for a place deciding about itself.

About nine of the eleven people in that room looked at the fourth heading and two had not looked at it, and of the eleven four had wanted the word breach written in the cause column and about five had not.

A form is not a law and a form is not a decision, and a form is a person nine weeks ago guessing at what a person nine weeks hence will want to have written down, and four of the eleven there had said that in a council and five said it in that room.

About nine of the eleven people in that room looked at the fourth heading for a long time and about two of them had not looked at it, and of the eleven four had wanted a fifth heading ruled and five said that a fifth heading is a thing you rule after you have needed it twice. One of them said the word out loud.

It is a man of about thirty-nine who keeps a crossing two days off and of the eleven at that table four had heard him use it before and about five had not, and what he said was that a bar across a road is a breach until somebody says otherwise and that nobody had said otherwise yet.

What answered him was the yard book lying open on nine feet of table with four pages in another man's hand, and of the eleven in that room four understood at once that a place may shut its own road and about five understood it four minutes later. Nobody in that room sent anybody to that far end. About four of the eleven there had wanted a rider sent to ask whether the bar was still there and about five had known that a rider is a knock.

So the fourth heading was filled in with the wrong word and then the right one, and it took about nine minutes, and four of the eleven in it watched a clerk take a word out and put another in and about five had gone out of the room.

The word that is in the record now is decision, and the reason is in the record in nine words, and the children are not in the record at all because of the eleven at that table four had wanted the number of them in and about five had said a record is not a place for a number of children.

He wrote it in himself, in the cause column, in his own hand, and four of the eleven there watched him do it and about five had their backs to the table, and that is the same hand that enters a sum of nine numbers in another town four days off a coast.

About nine people in nine counties have said since that a closure entered by the man who used to open everything is the only sensible hand for it, and about five have said it is the only hand nobody should have been given. Nobody in that room said the word breach again after the ninth minute, and of the eleven at that table four noticed that while about five did not, and the slip went into the case with the two books and the four sheets.

He had wanted, for about four and a half years, to be the person a thing was written down by when the thing was his own doing, and four of the eleven sitting there had known that and about five had not, and neither of them said anything about it then.

The slip and the two books went into the case together on the Friday and the case has a strap on it, and of the eleven at that table four wanted the case carried to a room with a press in it and about five wanted it left on that table where anybody could come up four steps and look at it again.

**And the cost of that Thursday is nineteen leaves of a yard book with four pages in a hand that is not its keeper's, six hundred leaves of a register entered into a record of a trial that has not been held, one slip of paper from a road that shut itself, four minutes of a clerk taking one word out of a record and putting another in, and a closure written in a cause column in a hand that can be asked about an arithmetic and about nothing else. The bleed at seven hundred and thirty days. Two books on one table for four days and nobody sent to the far end of anything. Nobody thanked anybody.**
