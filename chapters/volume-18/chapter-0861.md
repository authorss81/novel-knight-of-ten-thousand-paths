# Chapter 0861

**Mudmonth the twenty-ninth, YR 325, a Thursday and a market morning in the town at the foot of about nine hundred yards of occupied stair, the room over the saddler's with nine chairs in it that were carried up an outside stair on the tenth of Thawmonth and have not been carried down since, about nine people in that room, the market in the low end of that street shut behind its own shutters by eleven, and the interval eight hours and holding.**

A vote is the easiest thing in the world to arrange and the second easiest to call, and the whole of the difference between the two is about nine days of road.

That is what the twentieth of Mudmonth was for, being a Wednesday and not a market morning, nine days before this one, when he stood at the low table in that room with a knife in his right hand and a pad of about nine sheets under his left forearm and cut three sheets off it one at a time and laid them face down so that nobody in that room could see what was on any of them.

He wrote the same question on all three of them and he wrote it out three times rather than once and having it copied, and about four of the nine people in that room understood why he was doing it that way and about five thought it was a matter of not trusting a copyist, which was not the reason and which he did not correct.

The reason was that a man with one hand on a sheet and nine hundred yards of stair in him has four and a half years of knowing what a copy does.

What a copy does is arrive. It arrives looking like the thing it was made from. By the time it has been folded twice in a saddle in the mud month nobody can tell which of two of them is the one with the writing on it, and the rider stops being able to tell either and starts being able to say things about the sheet that are not on it.

He had wanted for four and a half years to be the person a paper is built around.

What he wanted on that Wednesday was to be a man who has cut three sheets off a pad in front of eight people. About four of those eight worked out which of the two he was and about five thought it was the first one.

The question is one sentence and it has no name in it. It asks whether a community that refuses contact may be questioned about the refusal by anybody at all before the ninth day, and it asks the three rooms to answer yes or no and to write the reason beside the answer if the answer is no.

That is all it says. About nine words and a question mark nobody had to write down.

The copies that travel are copies, and that is the third of the three difficulties and it is the one nobody in that room argued about, because there was nothing to argue about. A copy of a question is a question, and nobody has ever found out how to tell one from the other from the inside of a room that has only got the copy.

A sheet that has been folded twice in a saddle for nine days in the mud month is a sheet with somebody else's damp in it and about nine creases in it and a rider's hand on the corner where he held it.

The answer that comes back on it is an answer to the copy and not to the sheet, because there is no sheet left to compare it with. About four people in that room had said that the whole of the paper's difficulty is that a paper nobody can seal binds nobody and a paper carried by a rider binds a rider.

About five had said that about four hundred places are writing the same paper out by hand this winter and no two of about four hundred copies agree because nobody has asked them to. A question put in three places on one morning is the first thing anybody has done about that and it is very nearly nothing.

Three places were asked and the three of them are nine days, four days and one day from that room by road, and not one of the three is in this county. The coast town where six rooms were taken above a boat-yard. A town in the third province, where a man carries a seal that is not his. And a town nine days north on a made road, where about four hundred people come down to a market on a Thursday and go back up again.

That is the whole of the arrangement and it is where it began to come apart, because about four of the nine people in that room said afterwards that a question nine days out is a question about the weather, and about five said it is a question, and the five were right and the four were also right and the nine days is the price of asking at the same time.

The three words are the difficulty and the words are not English.

In the coast town the word is refuse and it has one meaning there, which is that the road stops and nobody is on it. In the third province the word is a review and it has another meaning, which is that somebody may look at a refusal again and decide whether it was a refusal or a stoppage in the works.

And in the northern town the word is a hearing and it has a third, which is that a hearing is a room with a table in it and a person writes down what was said and everybody afterwards knows what was said.

About four of the nine people in that room said that those were three words for three different things and about five said they were one thing said three ways, and the five were right on the coast and the four were right in the third province, and it took about nine minutes for that to become obvious to everybody in the room and it took no more than nine minutes because nobody in that room had ever asked.

The woman of about forty who has run a crossing crew for about eleven years said it out loud, and she said it in her own trade and about four of the nine heard all of it.

“**A review means somebody looks again and a hearing means somebody writes it down and a refusal means the road stops and nobody is on it, and if you put those three into one word then the answer you get back is an answer to a question none of us asked, and about four people in this room know it and about five of us have been reading the same word for nine days.**”

That was seventy-four words. Four of the nine heard every one of them and five heard the first sentence, and the man of about thirty-four who is clerk of the warrant was not in that town and has not been in that room since the tenth of Thawmonth and did not hear it.

Then he did the thing that was actually the errand and he did it in the open.

He cut the three sheets, which is the part nobody can undo. About nine days later there were three pieces of paper in three saddles going three ways, and each of them had the same sentence on it in his own hand, and none of the three had a seal on it because the three places do not use a seal on a thing like that and one of them has no seal at all.

What the riders were told is worth putting down because about four of the nine people in that room thought it was soft and about five thought it was hard.

They were told that the sheet is not to be left anywhere and is not to be put on a table in a public room and is not to be read by anybody who has not been asked to read it. They were told to ask the question out loud in a room with about nine people in it and to write down the answer as the room gave it and not as the room gave it afterwards.

And they were told, in those words, that if the room would not answer, the rider was to write that down as the answer, because a room that will not answer has answered.

About four of the three riders wrote that last part down at the time and about five have it by heart, and one of the three wrote the whole of it out again at a crossroads four days later because he thought he had dropped a page and had not.

The room over the saddler's has nine chairs in it and the nine of them were carried up an outside stair one at a time on the tenth of Thawmonth by one man on his own back in front of about nine people.

They have been carried about that room ever since and have never been carried down. About four people in that town have said that nine chairs going up means something and about five have said that nine chairs going up means a room, and both of those were said on the day and neither of them has been said since.

The reason neither has been said since is that about four hundred people buy what is on nine carts in the yard below every Thursday, and a room on an upper floor is not in the street and does not cost anybody anything.

The answers came back over the following nine days and they came back as things rather than as sheets, because no seal goes on any of them and the riders carried them in the inside of a coat against the wet. The near place answered on the twenty-first. The town in the third province answered on the twenty-fourth.

The far place answered on the twenty-ninth, at about the sixth hour, and that was the third of the three, and two of the nine people in that room had stopped expecting it about four days before it came.

The coast answered yes and gave three reasons.

The first of the three was that a road has one keeper and not two, and that a question put to anybody else is a question put to nobody.

The third of its three reasons was that a ferry, a bridge and a made road are three different animals.

A question which fits one of them does not so much as touch the other two. The third province answered no and gave one reason, which was that a refusal made at the fourth hour and questioned on the fifth is a refusal that has been broken by being asked about, and a man who thinks he has been answered and has not is worse off than a man who was never asked.

The northern town answered no and gave four reasons and the fourth of them was about a child.

Two of the three are close enough to be counted as one and the room did the counting itself in about four minutes without anybody putting it to a vote, which is the first thing that has happened in this paper's short life that nobody afterwards had to argue about.

They are both no. They are both no for a reason that is about the person on the far side of a refusal and not about the person who made it. The coast's first reason and the northern town's fourth reason say the same thing in different trades and the third province's one reason says the same thing again in a third, and the third province put it in a single clause about a man who thinks he has been answered and has not.

About four of the nine people in that room had worked out by then that the three places had answered the same question three ways and about five had worked out that this had happened without anybody arranging it, which is the whole of what anybody wanted out of a vote in three places on one morning.

He wrote the four answers down on the back of the sheet that had the three on it, in the order they came, with the day each one arrived on, and he wrote nothing else.

Then at about the ninth hour on that Thursday a rider came up the outside stair of the saddler's with a fourth answer in the inside of a coat, and he was a man of about thirty-one who keeps a toll on a ferry and who had not been asked anything by anybody, and he had come up that stair because he had heard in a market four days ago that a question was going round three towns and he had a fourth thing to say about it.

About four of the nine people in that room wanted to send him down without hearing him and about five wanted to hear him and the five were right, and the four were also right, and both of those are true.

He came in with his hat in his hand and stood at the low table and read out what his own road had answered him when he asked it the same question in the same words, and his own road had said that a refusal made on a ferry is a refusal made by about nine people standing on a deck and that about four of them are passengers and that a question put to a passenger is a question put to somebody who did not agree to be there.

Then he put a fifth thing on the table, which was that the northern town had told him, at a market, on the Saturday, that the third province's answer had been read out in the northern town before the northern town had written its own, and that nobody in the northern town had known that until he said so in that room, and that the northern town had not yet had nine days in which to find out whether it had been copied or guessed.

He said the fifth thing and then he stood at the low table with his hat still in his hand and waited, and about four of the nine people in that room found that they could not think of a single thing to ask him.

About five of them found that they could think of about nine and did not ask any of them. The rider from the fourth place looked at those four and said that a man who comes nine days to say nothing is worth more than a man who comes nine days to say something.

Then he went back down the outside stair to a horse that was tied to a post at the low end of that street.

**And the cost of that Thursday is three sheets cut one at a time off a pad with a knife in a room over a saddler's, three saddles and three riders and about nine days of road, four answers on the back of a sheet with a day against each, and one answer from a place that was not one of the three that comes up an outside stair at the ninth hour with its hat in its hand and puts a fifth thing on a table. A vote in three places on one morning and a fourth place that nobody asked and answered anyway. About four people in a room who wanted the rider sent down and about five who did not, and neither of them was wrong. The engine damaged three hundred and twenty-five days. Three hundred and eleven days of one man in a lower archive at this page and nobody in nine counties knows that figure but him. Ten anchor fragments where there were ten on the twenty-sixth of Goatmonth. Eight hours and holding. One ear, one leg, and a year, none of it on any of the four sheets and none of it in the room.**
