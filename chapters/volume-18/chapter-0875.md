# Chapter 0875

**Rainmonth the sixteenth, YR 325, a Thursday and a market morning in the town at the foot of about nine hundred yards of occupied stair, about four hundred people buying what is on nine carts in the yard, the selector room at the bottom of the water stair with its records sealed and about nine people on the floor above it, and the whole descent made in about nine hours.**

They went down at the eighth hour and it took the four of them about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice and it took the other four about twice that.

A stair of nine hundred yards is not nine hundred steps and it is not one straight climb. It is about nine flights with landings between them and the landings are four paces wide and two of them have a bench and one of them has a water jar and about nine men are on that place who will carry a thing down it if it is asked of them properly.

Four of the four people in front went down at the speed of the man with the bad leg and the other four went down at the speed of the man of forty-four who cannot go down nine hundred yards at the speed of a man who can.

The stair goes down beside a river and the lowest flight is cut into the rock of the far bank, and the water stair is the part of it that floods, and nine feet of it went under on the first of Mudmonth and nine days of clearing got it back.

Two of the landings have a lamp on a bracket and two of them have a lamp that is not lit, and the people who pass them light their own, and nine men on that place keep the oil in them out of a tin that is refilled on a Thursday.

The yard at the top of that stair had four hundred people in it and the town at the foot of it has a market on a Thursday, and the four hundred in the yard at the top are the same four hundred people who are in the street at the bottom, and nine hundred along nine miles of made road are in neither place.

The seal on the selector room was nine years old and it was not wax. It was a strip of lead about two inches wide run through a staple in the door and folded over itself, and the fold carried the Marshal's hand, and about four of the nine people on that floor had seen the lead before and about five had not.

Nobody had asked the man in the lower archive for permission and nobody had gone and told him.

That is the whole of it and it is why nothing stopped them.

Aren Kest had it open at about the fifth hour of the descent's end, with a knife he had brought for a different reason, and he cut the fold at the staple and did not tear the lead and did not take the staple out and did not put the strip back.

Four of the nine people on that floor watched him do it and five had gone on down ahead of him.

Then the door was open and it was not shut again at any point in the rest of that day or the four days after it.

The town at the foot of that stair has about nine hundred people in it and one street of it is made and the rest is not, and the market is on a Thursday and the boats come up about four days behind the market, and about nine hundred people along nine miles of made road hear about that market from a carter about once a fortnight.

The selector room is about nine paces by five and it is full of shelves and the shelves are full of books and nine people in nine counties have said that a room like that is the most frightening kind of room there is and five have said it is the least frightening, because there is nothing in it but what people wrote down.

There is no window in that room and there never was, and the light in it is two lamps and the door, and the shelves run three deep and the books on the third depth have not been moved in about nine years because the people who used them did not need the third depth.

They smell of lamp oil and of the dust that comes off a shelf nobody has wiped, and four of the four people who came down that stair noticed the smell and four did not, and the man of forty-four is one of the four who did not.

The books are accounts. Every one of them is a day, and every day has about nine columns in it, and four of the nine people who came down that stair with him could read one column and five could read two.

What is in those books is not a history. That is the finding of that Thursday and about four of the nine people on that floor had expected to find a history and about five had not known what they had expected to find.

What is in those books is a description of the working, written by the people who built it, in their own hands, over about nine years, with the dates they did each part on and nothing else in them at all.

What is in those books about the working is not written in any order at all.

It is written the way three trades hand work to each other, being that one of them writes down what he did and the next one writes down what he was given and the third one writes down what he did about it, and the three of them did not consult about the order because the order was not the point.

The first of the three hands is a glassmaker's. It is in nine books and it is the only hand in nine books that writes the word batch and the only one that writes the word wrong.

It writes the temperature and the hour and the word batch, and every ninth entry in those nine books is the same three words, being that it was the fourth and it was not fit and it went in the wall.

Four of the nine people on that floor read that line eleven times and five read it once and understood it, and one of the nine has said that it was the first line of the nine books that anybody above that floor ever had any business reading.

The second is a bridgewright's and it writes in about eleven books and it puts a date against every stone and about four of the nine people on that floor have said that a man who dates his stones has been mending something for a long time and about five have said he has been building something new.

It writes every course and every course is dated to the day, and nine of the dates are the same day. Four of the nine people on that floor worked out that nine courses on one day means a floor and five worked out that it means a bridge deck, and neither of them was wrong.

The third is a hand nobody on that floor could name the trade of, and it writes in four books and it writes the hours.

It writes the fourth hour and the tenth hour on every page of all four books and it never writes anything else except nine marks against the fourth hour in every book and no marks at all against the tenth, and four of the nine people on that floor saw the nine marks and five did not look for them.

Four of the nine people on that floor understood by the seventh hour what the third hand was for and five understood it by the ninth, and what the third hand was for is that the questions are put at the fourth hour and at the tenth hour and somebody wrote down which of those two hours was the one that mattered and did not say why.

The three hands are not signed. About four of the nine people on that floor looked for a name at the foot of each of the nine books and about five did not look.

He read about nine hours of it standing up with a lamp and he read it aloud to the four people who came down with him, and three of those four had the whole of it before the fourth book and one of them did not, and the young woman of twenty-two had to sit down on the bench on the fourth landing on the way up.

What is in those books, and this is what unsealing them did, is a complete description of the mechanism, given away free, in three trades' hands, to anybody who can read nine columns.

The man who has been holding that place from below for three hundred and eighty-eight days has not been able to read the description of his own instrument for nine years because it was sealed in a room he does not come up to.

About four people on that floor said out loud that this had been a stupid thing to do and about five said that nothing else could have been done with nine years of somebody else's patience.

What was in the fourth book is about nine hundred crossings a day and about nine thousand on a Saturday and a figure for a Sunday that is nine more than a Saturday, and about four of the nine people on that floor had worked that out from the columns in about nine minutes and about five needed about an hour and one of them has said she is sorry about the hour.

Then the door at the end of the selector room opened.

He had not come up out of the lower archive. That is the finding and it is the thing none of the nine people on that floor had expected, and about four of them had been standing at that door for about four minutes before it opened and about five had been on the shelves.

He is the man who went down into that place three hundred and eighty-eight days ago and has not come out, and his name is in no record in this manuscript and it is not going to be in this one.

He came up three steps into the selector room and he did not come further, and about nine people on that floor saw him four of the nine people in that room were frightened of him and five were not.

What he said was one sentence, and he said it in a flat voice, and he said it to the man with the lamp and not to the other eight.

“**Nobody in this room knows how any of it works and I have been down here for three hundred and eighty-eight days with a seal on the door above my head, and none of you has once come down and asked the man who cut the glass, and I am not going to tell you to stop, because I have wanted it read for about nine years and it is worse for me that it is open now than it was when it was shut.**”

That was eighty-five words and every person in that selector room heard all of them, four of the nine people there understood the whole of it at once and five understood the last two sentences.

The assessor was on that floor and she had been on that place four days in the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and stayed longer than the looking took, and she had not gone down nine hundred yards since because of her own leg and not because of anybody else's.

What she did in that selector room was read the glassmaker's hand for about nine minutes without moving her lips and then go up the water stair two at a time, and four of the nine people on that floor had seen her do it and five had not.

About four people in nine counties have said since that it is the better of one argument and not all of it, and about five have said it is the whole of his argument and that a man who has been in a room for three hundred and eighty-eight days has had a long time to find one.

It is the better of one argument. What it is the better of is the argument that somebody ought to be asked, and it does not touch the argument about what happens the next time two paths separate, and about four people in nine counties have said that leaving that one alone for nine weeks is not a mistake but it is going to be expensive.

Then he went back down the three steps and the door shut, four of the nine people in that selector room heard it shut and five did not.

He read about nine hours of it and he read it aloud and about four of the four people who came down with him had all of it before the fourth book.

Then they went up nine hundred yards, and the lead strip was on the floor of that selector room where it fell, and nobody picked it up, and nine people on that place walked past it for four days, and about four of them stepped over it and five of them did not, and one of the five has said since that stepping over a thing on a floor is what people do with a thing on a floor.

About four of the nine men on that place have asked the same question about that strip and got the same answer and five have not asked it at all.

**And the cost of that Thursday is about nine hundred yards of stair taken twice by four people and once by five, one strip of lead nine years old cut at the staple and left on the floor where it fell, about nine hours of a lamp and about nine books of three hands read aloud to four people in a room about nine paces by five, and a complete description of the whole of the working given away free to anybody who can read nine columns, including the man it was about. The engine damaged four hundred and two days and nobody mending it. Three hundred and eighty-eight days of one man in a lower archive and he came up three steps. Eight anchor fragments where there were ten. The bleed six hundred and eleven days old and not cured and not explained and no character on that floor has asked him about it. One ear, one leg, and a year, and none of the three of them has been on a stair of nine hundred yards in a while.**
