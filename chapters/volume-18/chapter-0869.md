# Chapter 0869

**Longlight the fifteenth, YR 325, a Monday and not a market morning in the market town of about two thousand people four days off the coast, thirteen stairs up from the lane to the room over the weigh-house with the register shut on the table and a bundle of about nine paper bundles on it that came up four days off the coast road in the ordinary post, about nine people in that room, and the fourth ruled head of that page still empty with nothing standing at it.**

The room over the weigh-house is thirteen stairs up from a lane and it is about nine paces by five, and about four hundred people come up those stairs in a year and nine of them come up twice and five of them have never come up at all, and on that Monday morning nine of them came up and sat down.

The register was shut on the table for the whole of it and was not opened, and the fourth ruled head of that page is empty and has been empty since the first of Hearthmonth two years back. No fifth head is ruled anywhere in that town. The day-book stands at eleven entries and gained none and the press stands open with no date on it.

About four people in that lane have said that the right thing to have done with nine bundles from a place that has lost its contact was to enter them, and about five have said that entering them would have meant a finding and that a register with ten questions and no findings under any of them cannot take a finding off a woman who came down a road with two children and nine bundles in her arms.

What came back from that place in nine days came back in the hands of people and in nothing else.

That is the whole of the rule and nobody worked it out on the page.

A person can go up that road. A person can come down it. A letter cannot, and a price cannot, and a doctor's advice cannot, and a record of what a court decided last month cannot.

Four people in nine counties have said this is a road that is open and closed at the same time. Five have said it is not a road at all and cannot say what it is.

Nine bundles came up the coast road between the sixth and the fifteenth of Longlight. They came with people.

Eight of them came with men who had gone up on a market day and had come down again. Six of those eight were asked at the low end of that street by four people each what the place was like and five of them answered.

The ninth bundle came with a woman of about thirty-four and her two children and she came down on the tenth of Longlight, five days before anything happened, and she has been in this town since and she came up those thirteen stairs this morning because a carter had told her in a lane that something had happened at the end of that road.

She is from the place.

Four people in that room have said somebody who lives inside a loss is the only person who can say what the loss is. Five have said somebody who lives inside a loss is the last person anybody will ask. Four of the nine had noticed that both were said and five had not.

She named it herself, in her own trade, and it was not goods and it was not a trade and it was not a person, and about four of the nine people in that room heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**It is not that we cannot get anything through. We can walk in and we can walk out with our arms full. What we cannot do is be told anything, and about nine days after a thing happens nobody at the end of that road knows that it happened, and then in about nine days more nobody here knows that they do not know, and then in about nine days more the whole of it is a thing that four people said once.**”

That was eighty-three words and every one of them was heard in that room and about four of the nine people in it understood the whole of it inside a minute and about five understood the end of it.

A place of about eleven hundred people at the end of a valley with one made road in it has one doctor and about four hundred books and one school of about nine children and a market that is a market day and not a building.

The school closed on the Thursday of the following week, because the woman who teaches it is sixty-eight and could not get up the road.

Four people in this town have said a school closing is a thing that is always somebody's fault. Five have said that on that road nobody's fault is available and the school closed anyway.

There is a court at the low end of the coast road that sits on a Thursday, and the people at the end of that valley cannot get to it.

Nine hundred people along four miles of made road have worked that out inside a fortnight. Four of them have written to somebody about it and five have not.

It is not a reset. About four people in that room said the word and about five said the other of the two, and the other of the two is that the eleven hundred people at the end of that road are exactly where they were on the Wednesday before and not one single thing about their week has been different.

The room at the middle of that cut is about nine feet by eleven and has a bench in it and a lamp on a bracket and a door that is shut every night at about the seventh hour by whoever is going down the road.

About nine people have been inside that room on about four thousand nights and about four of them have said that a lamp in a room in the middle of a four hundred yard cut is the reason a road four hundred yards long under a hill is a road anybody has ever relied on, and about five have said it is the reason it is not.

The lamp in the room at the middle of that cut went out on that Wednesday afternoon. It has not been lit since.

There is a woman at the bridge end who has been paid in butter to keep it burning every night for nine years. She is not paid now and she has not asked. Four people in this town have said a woman who is not paid and does not ask is the saddest kind. Five have said it is the ordinary kind.

The lime kiln at the top of that valley is nine hundred yards above the bridge and it is a kiln of about nine feet square inside and it burns all winter because lime will not burn itself.

It needs about four tons of coal a week. That coal comes up a made road from a yard about forty miles off and it comes in about four carts a week and about nine of those carts have not come up that road since the Wednesday.

About nine people at the end of that cut are burning what is in the kiln and about five of them are not, and the five are the households with nobody in them between about the sixth hour and about the sixth hour, and Four people in this town have said five households in a valley of eleven hundred people is a normal night. Five have said it is not and cannot say what it is.

The lime kiln is still burning. It is nine hundred yards above the bridge and it burns all winter and it needs about four tons of coal a week that came up that road from a yard about forty miles off, and about four tons a week has not come up that road since the Wednesday and about nine people at the end of that cut are burning what is in the kiln and about five of them are not.

There is a bridge at the south end of the valley and the bridge is nine hundred years old.

Four of the nine people in that room have said a bridge nine hundred years old with no coal on one side of it and two hundred people on the other is a winter and not a week. Five have said they have had worse winters. Four of those five have said that was before the bridge.

Then she said the last thing, and she said it because about four of the nine people in that room had asked her to and about five had not.

She described an ordinary evening at one house at the bridge end on the Wednesday morning of that week, before anything happened, in the plainest words she could find, and about four of the nine people in that room heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

“**We had the fire in and the girl was doing the bottom of a bucket with a nail in it and my husband was mending the strap of a sack and the old woman was asleep in the chair with the cloth over her, and the man from the next house came in about the eighth hour with a lamp that would not light and said the thing about the weather, and that is all of it and that is what it is every night.**”

That was eighty-five words and about four of the nine people in that room heard all of it and about five heard the first sentence, and nobody in that room wrote any of it down.

He had wanted for four and a half years to be the person a paper is built around and what was in front of him on that Monday morning was nine bundles and a woman with her hand flat on the top one of them.

He could have written it all into that register. There was a page for it and there was a column for a source and there was a woman who keeps that room who would have asked him one question about the source and he would have known the answer in about four seconds.

He took the bundles off the table instead and gave them to the woman they came with, and the record of that Monday in that town is that the holder of the nine bundles is the woman they came with and that the Archive keeps a copy of none of them and that the fourth ruled head of that page is empty and the day-book stands at eleven entries.

Then the woman who keeps that room asked her one question, and it was the only question asked in that room that day, and it was not a question about the road.

She asked who was going to keep the record of it.

About four of the nine people in that room said the Archive and about five said the delegation, and about four of the nine said that both of those had said so and had never yet written one line down about anything anybody could check.

What he did was take the nine bundles off that table in both arms and put them down in front of the woman they came with, and he said that the holder of them was her and that the room would keep a copy of any of them she chose to leave and not one of the rest, and about four of the nine people in that room heard all of it and about five heard the end of it.

She put her hand flat on the top bundle and did not move it for as long as it takes to read a sheet twice.

Then she said the woman before her had kept a record of that valley for nine years in a book, and the book was four hundred yards from the lamp that had gone out, and it was the only copy.

Four of the nine people in that room understood what she had just said and five understood the end of it.

**And the cost of that Monday is nine bundles carried up a coast road by people because nothing else can be carried up that road, about nine hundred days of somebody not being told anything at the end of a four hundred yard cut, an eleven hundred pound question answered by a woman with a nail in a bucket and a strap on a sack, and one record four hundred yards from a lamp that has gone out. The lamp out since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth. One narrow path anchor out of use for good and about four tons of coal a week not coming up a made road. Eight where there were ten. Nine rows in a register with no finding under any of them and the fourth ruled head of that page still empty and the day-book at eleven entries and gained none. One ear, one leg, and a year, and none of the three of them is what was lost off that road.**
