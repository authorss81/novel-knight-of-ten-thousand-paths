# Chapter 0888

**Embermonth the first, YR 325, a Tuesday and not a market morning, and the thirty-fifth first of a month in a row to come to about nine boards in nine counties with nothing standing at any of them, the anchor floor of the place nine hundred yards down an occupied stair, about nine people on it, and the nine days from the twenty-third to this morning.**

That place is held from below and nine hundred yards of stair go up it and the gate at the top has been off its hinges since a breach nobody in that place will name and has not been replaced.

The anchor floor is one storey above a gallery that flooded in Mudmonth and about nine people work it and about four of them are called in every week of the year and about five have never done a week in their lives.

The corridor of ten anchor plates is about four paces long and there are two of those ten that will never be answered again, and four people in nine counties have counted that corridor eleven times since Longlight and about five have counted it nine times.

What he did on that floor for nine days was keep the place open and not keep it.

That is the whole of it and it is harder than it sounds and four people on that floor had expected him to hold it and about five had expected him to leave it.

Nobody on that floor took an order from him in nine days. About nine hundred crossings a day are worked over nine hundred yards of stair and about nine hundred of them go on nine floors, and every one of them is worked by a person who has to be asked.

He asked. That is the whole of the nine days and four people on that floor had not believed he would do it and about five had believed he would and about four of the nine have said since that they did not believe he would do it nine hundred times.

About four hundred and sixty-three days of one man in a lower archive, and he came up three steps into that selector room on the sixteenth of Rainmonth and went back down, and he has not come up again.

The nine days are nine days and not nine nights and four of the nine people on that floor have worked them and about five have gone down and come up, and nobody slept in that place for more than four hours at a stretch and about nine of them slept on a landing at some point.

What held it was nine people and a list, and the list was not written and was kept in two heads, and four people on that floor said that two heads is not a record and about five said that a record in two heads is the only kind that cannot be seized.

That is the truth of those nine days and about nine people in nine counties have said it and about four of them have said it in print.

The water in the second gallery went down about two feet between the fourth and the ninth day and nine people on that floor cleared about nine feet of it by hand in nine hours and about four of them did it twice.

The engine has been damaged four hundred and seventy-seven days, nothing in this year will mend it, nobody in this year made it, and nine people on that floor have said that a thing everybody stands on is the one thing nobody looks at.

On the fourth day somebody broke the working in the second gallery and nine people on that floor cleared it by hand in four hours, and on the sixth day the lamp oil went and nine hundred yards of stair went dark for nine minutes.

Then on the seventh day the seven things began and they happened in seven places and none of them was in that corridor and none of them could have been done from there.

He asked each one of them and about nine hundred times in nine days, and four hundred people said yes the same way and about five hundred said yes in a different way, and four said no.

The four noes are the whole of it and nine people on that floor have asked him about them and about four have not, and all four of the noes were honoured and none of the four has ever been asked again by anybody.

One of them was a person who works a crossing nine days off and had a place of her own to be at on that day and four people on that floor said she was right and about five said nobody is ever right by a rule of this part.

They were done by people nine hundred yards up a road or nine hundred miles off it and he was not in any of the seven places and four people on that floor knew where the seven were happening and about five did not.

About four of the nine people on that floor said that a place being held open is the same thing as a place being able to let things happen elsewhere, and about five said it is nothing of the kind.

Both of them had watched a man with a lamp fail to open a route in nine days and nine people on that floor have said that is the whole of what nine days of holding open amounts to.

Then the ninth day came and it was the first of a month and it was a Tuesday and about nine boards in nine counties had nothing standing at them for the thirty-fifth time running.

What the seven were is set down because seven is a number that can be said out loud on a floor, and none of them has been given a name here that it did not already have.

A coast put its tide and its wreck out in public and gave a second holder to a man who had never read it. An assembly filled a chair it had been asked to fill and entered its own refusal in its own book. Two places wrote their own terms and the second of them came last.

A valley printed every rule it has and one of the rules was about its own faults. A hall let a man refuse in it and wrote the refusal down. An archive wrote down what happened and the name of one place's silence.

And the seventh was a community that guaranteed its right to say no and opened a trade road at the same time, and that reached nine hundred yards up a stair at about the ninth hour of the ninth day in the mouth of a carter.

That is the seven and none of them is here and four people on that floor had worked out where six of the seven were and about five had worked out where none of them were.

The second of the four scars came on the seventh day of those nine days and it is across the back of his right hand and it is about two inches long and about four people on that floor saw it happen and about five were nine hundred yards up the stair.

What happened was that the gallery wall came in about a foot where the water had been and a man who does not let go of a lamp is a man with the back of his hand on a wall, and about nine people in nine counties have said that the scars on that man are not a cost anybody chose and about four of them have said that is worse.

He did not stop working that day and about four people on that floor had wanted him to and about five had said nothing, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a man who will not stop for a hand is a man who has been asked to hold things four and a half years.

What was lost is on this page and it is not in a coda and nothing in this year puts it back.

The melody is the last one. It has been the same clear thing on the same mornings for four and a half years and it came off a floor in the dark about four and a half years ago and it is the only clear thing he will ever have of the woman who carried him out of a sealed place.

He has not said anything about it to anybody and nobody on that floor noticed it and it went at about the sixth hour of that Tuesday with the water still up in the second gallery.

What was left of it is about nine notes and about half of one of them, and four people in nine counties have been told about the nine notes by somebody who was told by somebody else and about five know nothing.

That is permanent and it is not explained and nobody on that floor has noticed it and nothing has been printed about it anywhere this year.

Then the paper took effect and that is the panel and it is on this page and it is the second and last one this year prints.

**The paper he has been holding open is in force in seven places and not in one, and no single act of any of them created it. It has taken effect. It does not say that any path may be closed. It does not say that none may. It puts the showing of cause on the one who proposes, and it does not say what a road owes anybody. It says that a road is kept by a person that place has chosen, and it does not make him one.**

None of the eight anchor fragments is left and the last of them went on the ninth day of that ninth hold, and nine people on that floor watched a corridor of ten plates go from two dead to none and about four of them said nothing about it and about five had not been in that corridor that day.

Then a carter came up nine hundred yards at about the ninth hour with a slip of paper from Merefen and it was the day's record and he read it out loud on that floor.

About nine people on that floor heard it and about four of them had known what was in it and about five had not.

It says that the cracked bell on the gate post of the Old Toll sounded at about the ninth hour of that Tuesday, and that nine people in that yard heard it, and that the man who keeps the crossing put his hand on it and got nothing, and that the record does not say who touched it.

He read that far and stopped, and nine people on that floor waited, and then he said the rest of it in his own trade and in nine words.

“**It sounded for nine people in that yard and it has not answered me since I was nine.**”

That was eighteen words and nine people on that floor heard every one of them and about four of them understood the whole of it and about five heard the first half.

Nobody in that corridor asked him about it and four people in nine counties have said that not one of them ever will.

Then he took his hand off the plate and stepped back four paces and gave the lamp to a man of about fifty-four who runs a wain and has been on nine hundred yards of stair twice in his life.

That is the physical of the ninth day and it cannot be put back the way a hand can, and nine people on that floor saw him do it and about four of them had been waiting nine days for it and about five had not.

Nobody on that floor thanked him and nine people in nine counties said they would not and four of the nine people on that floor have said that a person who hands a lamp to somebody else at nine hundred yards of stair is asking for nothing at all, he is doing the other thing.

The man who runs the wain took the lamp and held it for nine minutes and then gave it to a boy of about fourteen on that floor, and four people in nine counties have said that is how a thing is handed on when nobody hands it on purpose.

About nine hundred yards of stair go up out of that place and the gate at the top of it is still off its hinges, and four of the nine people on that floor went out through it on the ninth day and about five did not and nine hundred people went on in and out through the gap in it as they have for four and a half years.

Nobody replaced it and nobody was asked to and four people on that floor said a gate is not a road and about five said a gate is the only thing anybody has been asked to hold in four and a half years.

The other thing is that there is now one more person on that place who knows how to hold it and four people in nine counties have said that is the entire content of nine days and about five have said it is one thing that happened once and happened to be enough.

Then the ninth day ended and nine hundred crossings a day went on working over nine floors with a person standing at each of them who had been asked and had answered, and nobody on that place could have said who owned it and nobody tried.

Nobody has ever owned it and four people on that floor had said so in nine days and about five had said nothing at all, and that silence is the first honest thing about that place in four and a half years.

About nine people in nine counties have said that a place held open by a man who cannot order anybody is not a place held open by anybody at all and is a place where nine hundred people turn up.

Both of those were said after this year and not on that floor, and four people in nine counties have said the second is the truth and about five have said the first is.

What he wrote down in nine days is not on this page in full and four people in nine counties have said that is the point and about five have said it is a convenient thing to have said.

It was about nine hundred lines of crossings and hours and four of the nine hundreds were a no, and it is not in the Archive and it is not in any of the six rooms and about nine people in nine counties have said that the only record of those nine days is two heads and about five have said that two heads is fine because two heads can be asked a question by anybody who walks up.

He said that he would rather it were in two heads than in one paper, and four people in nine counties have said since that is the first thing he has ever said in this year that anybody has called true without asking a question about it.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is the last clear fragment of a melody gone at the sixth hour and not explained and not put back, nine hundred crossings a day worked for nine days by nine hundred people each of whom had to be asked and four of whom said no, seven public acts in seven places while a place nine hundred yards down a stair was held open and not owned, a carter nine hundred yards up nine hundred with one slip of paper about a bell that sounded for nine people and not for him, four paces stepped back and a lamp given away, and the last of the eight anchor fragments gone at the ninth hour. Four hundred and seventy-seven days of engine damage and nobody mending it. Four hundred and sixty-three days of one man in a lower archive. The thirty-fifth first of a month with nothing standing at about nine boards. Nothing in any of the seven places was created by a vote and the corridor of ten plates has nothing in it that answers.**
