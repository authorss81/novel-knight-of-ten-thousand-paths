# Chapter 0898

**Frostmonth the tenth, YR 325, a Monday and not a market morning, a room over a smithy at a crossroads with six hundred people in it, thirty-one people at two tables pushed together, four standing orders of four hundred years on the wall, and the eleven miles of road between that crossroads and a market town closed since the sixth hour.**

The engine has been damaged five hundred and forty-six days and it is nobody's achievement and this year does not mend it. The shortest interval the occupied timetable has allowed anybody is seventeen hours, and that is the figure that decides whether a thing can be said to somebody before the water moves, and of the thirty-one in that room four had it in their heads and five had not.

Nobody thanked anybody in that room, and of the thirty-one there four had brought a thing to say in that direction and five had not, and nine hundred people will repeat that assembly in nine words each. The room is over a smithy because the hall is being whitewashed, and the smith has been paid nine pounds for eleven days and is not in his own room, and four of the thirty-one there had wanted to wait and five had not.

The smith's fire is under that floor and of the thirty-one in that room four had known it would be hot and five had come in from the cold and were not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said a crossroads assembly in Frostmonth has been warm in whatever room it sat in since the year anybody started writing it down.

There are thirty-one of them and nine hundred people live at that crossroads, and four of the thirty-one had come because the road is shut and five had come because a road is shut at this crossroads four times a winter and a shut road has always been the one thing that room has ever been about.

The chair is a chair and it is the same chair, and of the thirty-one in that room four had wanted a second chair that week and five had not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that one chair is the whole of why nine counties know what they know.

The road is eleven miles and it goes down into a cutting nine hundred feet long that has gone into the river twice this winter, and four hundred people use that road a week and nine of those use it twice. It was shut at the sixth hour by a man with a bar and a chain and no order, and of the thirty-one in that room four had wanted that done before it was shut and five had wanted it shut in an order.

He is forty-four and he keeps the eleven miles from this end and of the thirty-one in that room four had known him and five had only known his name on a board at the near end.

He shut it because the far end had sent word on the Friday that the wall had gone again, and four of the thirty-one sitting there had wanted that word in writing and five had known a word from a far end is a thing you act on and ask about afterwards.

The bar is four feet of oak and the chain is nine feet and both of them came from the smith's shop under that floor, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had wanted them back at the fourth hour and five had not. The standing orders on that wall are four and they are four hundred years old and of the thirty-one in that room four had read all four and five had read the first.

They have a form for a breach and a form for a toll and a form for a repair and a form for a closure made by an officer of the county. There is no form for a place deciding about itself, and of the thirty-one in that room nine knew that before anybody said it and four had gone to the wall to check.

Form one is a breach and nine hundred breaches have been entered on it in that county in four hundred years, and of the thirty-one in that room four had wanted to enter a breach that morning and five had not wanted it entered at all. Form two is a toll and four hundred toll disputes are on it and nine of them are still open, and four of the thirty-one there had wanted the orders explained and five had said the orders explain themselves.

Form three is a repair and it requires a name against a cost, and of the thirty-one in that room four had read it twice that morning and five had read it once.

Form four is a closure made by an officer of the county and there have been four of those in four hundred years and the last one was in YR 288, and of the thirty-one in that room four had wanted to summon an officer and five had known how far away the last officer was. Nobody in that room could say where the last officer of that county is.

Four of the thirty-one there had wanted somebody to try and five had already given it up, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a form which cannot be filled is a form nobody has filled in four hundred years. That is the whole of the resistance of that Monday and it is nobody's fault.

Four hundred years of forms have no shape for nine feet of cutting and of the thirty-one in that room four had wanted a shape invented and five had wanted the road shut. Then the clerk said it, and she is sixty-one and has been the clerk of that crossroads for nineteen years, and of the thirty-one there four had expected her to say it and five had not.

“**Orders three and four together say that a road shall be reported closed within the day and by whom it was closed and for what, and I am the clerk and I have said before that I would report it, and I am not going to now, because I have been asked by nine households at the far end to write the reason in my own book instead and a road shut by weather is not a breach of anything.**”

That was eighty words and every person in that room heard all eighty of them, and four of them had every word of it before she sat down and five had fifty. Four of the thirty-one in that room said the orders are the orders and five said the orders are four lines written by four men in a room like this one, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said the second answer has never once been found wrong.

Then the road was shut, formally, in nine minutes, and the entry is in the crossroads book and four hundred people in that village can go and read it and five hundred cannot. It says that the road is shut from this crossroads to a market town, that it is shut for the season, that the reason is that the cutting has gone into the river twice in one winter, that the far end asked for it, and that the near end agrees. The far end asked for it.

Four of the thirty-one in that room had not known that until it was read out and five had known, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that one half of that sentence is the whole of the finding. Nine households at the far end and of the thirty-one in that room nine had spoken to one of those nine and five had not.

The far end is a hamlet of two hundred people with a smith of its own and a road of its own and no meeting of its own, and of the thirty-one in that room four had never been to it and about five had been twice.

What the far end asked for is in nine words on a slip of paper and the nine words are the same nine words the cart gave at the fourth hour, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had wanted the far end's own book and about five had wanted the slip. There is no book at the far end.

About four of the thirty-one in that room had wanted one started and about five had said a hamlet with no clerk has no book and that is not the fault of a road. Nobody was sent anything. That is what the word season means in that book and it took about nine minutes to establish, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had wanted a rider sent to the county seat and about five had not. No rider went.

About four hundred people in nine counties have said that is the first time in about four hundred years that a road in that county was closed by the people at both ends of it, and about five hundred have said it is the fortieth. Then the other thing happened, and it happened after the vote, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had known it was coming and about five had not.

Nobody had put it on the agenda and four of the thirty-one in that room noticed it while five did not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that the two biggest things a crossroads room ever does both arrive unasked for. The councillor is fifty-four and has been in that room for about nine years and he weighs timber, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had wanted to hear him ask and about five had wanted to hear somebody refuse him.

What he said was that a man who can be asked by nine hundred places a day is the only person anybody can find when a road is shut, and that is true, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had agreed and about five had not agreed and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said both.

Then he gave the reason, which is that the man can be asked by nine hundred places a day and that a man who can be asked by nine hundred places a day will be told what nine hundred places want, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had heard that reason before and about five had not.

A councillor of about fifty-four put it in front of the room, and what he put in front of the room is that the man sitting by the door should be made the standing witness of every road in the network for as long as he lives. About four hundred people in nine counties had wanted that in about four years and of the thirty-one in that room about nine had wanted it that week and about five had wanted it never.

He refused it in nine words at about the second hour and he said it standing up and about four of the thirty-one in that room had wanted him to sit down and about five had wanted to hear it.

“**A person who keeps every road is the thing this year spent itself getting rid of, and I will not be it again, and you may write that I said so in a room of about thirty people and did not look at the man who asked.**”

That was forty-seven words and every person in that room heard all forty-seven of them, about four of them to the end and about five to the thirtieth word. Then the same councillor asked whether the room would enter the other thing, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had not known there was an other thing and about five had.

The other thing is that the name Knight of Ten Thousand Paths is to be written into the minute book of that crossroads as an honour for service and not as an office, and of the thirty-one there about four had wanted it written and about five had not known it could be either.

It was entered in about four lines and about four of the thirty-one in that room had wanted it entered in nine and about five had wanted it entered in one, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said four is right. Nobody in that room thanked him for refusing and about four of the thirty-one there had expected a vote of thanks and about five had expected nothing at all, and nobody thanked anybody.

A woman of about forty-one in that room said that a man who refuses an office in front of about thirty people has made it easy for the next four hundred people to refuse one, and about four of the thirty-one there had agreed and about five had said it makes it easy for the next man and not for the next four hundred.

He did not answer her and of the thirty-one in that room about four had wanted him to and about five had known he would not, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that is a man who has learned one thing.

The name that was entered is nine words long in four lines and it says the name is a mark of service and is not an office and carries no authority and binds nobody, and about four hundred people in nine counties have now read that and about five hundred have not. Then the first cart came back. That is the last thing that happened at that crossroads on that Monday and it is the whole of the ending of it.

It came back at about the fourth hour with nine hundredweight of stone on it and a man of about thirty-four driving, and about four of the thirty-one in that room had gone out to meet it and about five had not. He did not get out of the cart.

He put the rein on the near side and of the thirty-one in that room about four had wanted him to get down and about five had known a man with nine hundredweight does not get down in a cutting.

About four of the thirty-one in that crossroads had wanted him to go back to his yard and about five had wanted him to go to the far end and look, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that the second of those is how a road gets opened by the wrong person. Nobody asked him anything else.

About four of the thirty-one in that crossroads had wanted to ask him whether he thought the wall would stand and about five had known it would not matter by then. The nine hundredweight stayed on the cart and about four of the thirty-one people in that room had wanted it taken off and about five had known a man with nine hundredweight on a hill is not a man who takes nine hundredweight off.

The reason he gave was in a tradesman's terms and it was nine words long and nobody had asked him for it and about nine of the thirty-one in that crossroads heard all nine of them.

“**Water got under the wall again and the wall is not mine to argue with.**”

That was fifteen words and of the thirty-one in that room about four had all fifteen of them and about five had nine of them, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said it is the best sentence anybody said that year and about four hundred have said it is only that.

**And the cost of that Monday is eleven miles of road closed by the people who live at both ends of it, four hundred years of standing orders with no form for a closure that is not a breach, a clerk of sixty-one who refused to report a thing she had said before she would report, an entry in a crossroads book that about four hundred people can go and read, nine words in which a standing witness for every road was refused in a room of about thirty people, four lines entering a name as an honour and not an office, and one cart of nine hundredweight turned back at the fourth hour with a tradesman's reason nobody had asked for. The engine at five hundred and forty-six days. The shortest interval seventeen hours. Nobody thanked anybody.**
