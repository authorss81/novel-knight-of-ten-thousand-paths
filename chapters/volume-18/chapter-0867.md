# Chapter 0867

**Longlight the first, YR 325, a Monday and not a market morning and the thirty-first first of a month in a row to arrive at those boards with nothing standing at it, a house of stone and lime about nine days off by road from any town in this county, a room with a fire in it and about eleven people in it and a table that has been in that room for a hundred years, and the interval four hours.**

The form asks for nine years.

That is the whole of the difficulty and nobody in that room argued about it, because the form was on the table with a name against a line on it and the name was not the name anybody had brought.

He came up that road on the Friday with a lamp, a stick and nine feet of cord and nothing else.

He came up it in three days because the leg was at its worst on the second morning. Four people along that road offered him a bed and five did not. He took one bed and slept in a barn on the second night.

About nine miles of the road is over a watershed and the rest of it is in a valley with a river in it that has been up nine times a winter for as long as anybody there can remember.

There is one made road in that valley and it is four miles long and it goes from a lime kiln to a bridge. Nine hundred years ago it was made by somebody for the lime and not for the people. Four people in that valley have said a road built for the lime is the reason the lime burns. Five have said it is the reason they can walk it at night at all.

The house is at the bridge end and there is a lane off the road to it about two hundred yards long, with a gate at the road end that is shut every night at the eleventh hour by somebody.

Four people in that valley have said the gate has been shut every night for twenty years. Five have said nobody knows who shuts it.

Nobody knew who had put the woman's name forward.

That is on the record and it is the whole of the finding of that Monday. Four of the eleven people in that room said eleven people in two meetings agreed on a name. Five said nobody agreed on anything, and that nine people came to each meeting and the name was the same on both nights.

The man who brought the form had brought it from the town four days off and he had it in a leather case.

He had not filled any part of it in before he came up that road. Four people in that valley have said a man who comes nine days with an unfilled form is a man who expects to be told. Five have said he is a man who has been told what to expect.

The finding of that Monday is not that a woman can keep a road. It is that the form did not ask her and eleven people did.

A form is a thing that goes to a person and asks a person. About four of the eleven people in that room had brought the form nine days up that road and about five of them had watched it not work, and about nine people in that valley had said before he arrived that he would be asked to fill it in himself because that is what the last one did.

He had heard that on the Friday night in a barn and he had said nothing about it on the Saturday and nothing about it on the Sunday, and about four people along that road have said that a man who says nothing for two days while somebody is doing something is either respecting it or is out of his depth, and about five have said it is a third thing, which is that he had nothing to add and knew it.

What he added on the Monday was the ring and about nine minutes of waiting.

The first place to choose anybody was not chosen by anybody else. That is what the arrangement says and that is what happened, and about four of the eleven people in that room had understood it before they came and about five had not.

They had been asked on the nineteenth of Longlight and again on the twenty-sixth.

Between those two dates the meeting happened twice. Nine people came to the first one and nine came to the second, and nobody has said since which of the two was the meeting.

The person that place chose is a woman of about thirty-one who earns her living at a lime kiln about a mile off and who has walked a lantern run on that road since she was eleven years old.

There are about four hundred people in that valley and about two hundred of them are in the four households at the bridge end and about nine of the households keep a dog and about five keep a horse and every one of the about nine households at the bridge end has somebody who walks the run, and about four of the eleven people in that room have said that is a thing you cannot appoint and about five have said that is a thing you cannot dismiss either.

The run begins at about the seventh hour in the winter and about the tenth hour in the summer and it goes to the kiln and back and it takes about an hour and ten minutes and it has never once been shortened. About nine people in that valley have been on that run and about four of them have been on it for more than twenty years and about five have been on it for less than five.

The lantern is a thing with a candle in it and the candle is about four inches and it is carried in the left hand and the right hand is free, and about four people in that valley have said that the right hand being free is the whole of the custom and about five have said it is so that a person can take hold of anybody who has fallen in the road.

A lantern run is a mile of made road walked after dark with a lantern, to see whether the road is under water, and it is done the length of a mile and back and it is done by people who are not paid for it, and this one has been walked every night of the year for about twenty years and has never once been stopped for weather.

Four people in that room had known about the run and five had not.

The four who had known about it had all assumed that a person who walks a mile of road in the dark every night for twenty years is a person who keeps a road. Four of the eleven had assumed the opposite, that a person who does a thing anybody can see is not the person anybody has to ask.

The form asks for nine years standing in the keeping of a road. She has eleven years of walking it in the dark and no standing in it at all, and she has never been asked to decide anything in her life, and about four of the eleven people in that room have said that the form is wrong and about five have said the form is right and does not matter, and the five were right about that and the four were right about the form.

What she said when she was asked is worth putting down whole, because nobody has printed any part of it.

It was said in her own trade and four of the eleven heard all of it and five heard the end of it.

“**I do not know what a road owes anybody and I have never been asked. I know that it is under water about nine times a winter and that somebody has to walk it to find out which nine, and that if I stop walking it then somebody else has to walk it tomorrow night, and that is the whole of what I am able to say about it.**”

That was sixty-nine words, and about four of the eleven people in that room heard every one of them and about five heard the first two sentences, and the man who had brought the form did not write any of it down.

Then he did the thing he came nine days off a made road to do, and he did it in front of eleven people, and about four of them understood what it was and about five understood that something had been handed over and not what.

What he brought was in the inside of his coat on the left where the hand goes, and it is a ring of iron about four inches across and about a quarter of an inch thick with a tongue on it that answers a question put to it by a thing in a wall nine hundred miles away.

He took it out and put it on the table and then he picked it up again and put it into her hand.

He did not say anything at the moment he did it. Four of the eleven people in that room have said he was right not to say anything. Five have said he should have, and that not saying anything is a kind of putting a person on the spot.

What he said was nine minutes later and it was forty-five words and it was not to her.

**“There is a column in a paper in a town about four days off and it is empty and it is yours to fill, and nobody in this room has any business filling it and I have nine of them to do in nine counties.**”

That was forty-five words and four of the eleven people in that room heard all of it and five heard the end of it.

The woman put the ring on her own table and not in a pocket, and left it there for as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, before she picked it up again and put it in a pocket. Four of the eleven have said the leaving of it on the table was the whole of the answer. Five have said the putting of it in the pocket was.

A ring of iron about four inches across has a tongue on it and the tongue answers a question, and the question is put by a thing in a wall nine hundred miles away at about the fourth hour and about the tenth hour and at no other time, and about four people in that valley have understood what she was being given and about five have understood that she was being given a job and not a ring.

There are nine of them. One is on that valley road. The other eight are held by people who did not get them from anybody and have not handed one to anybody since the day they were given it.

A person who keeps a road on a community's choice is what the arrangement calls a custodian.

The word is used in nine counties. Four people who have heard it said have said it is the only kind of keeper there is. Five have said it is the only kind anybody has ever been afraid of.

There are nine hundred crossings on the network and that Saturday in Mudmonth nine thousand of them were worked.

On that Monday morning in Longlight there was one road with a keeper that its own people had chosen and eight hundred and ninety-nine with keepers that somebody had put in.

The room had been a farmhouse a hundred years ago and it is a house with a fire in it and a table that has not been moved.

Four of the eleven people in that room had eaten off that table and five had not. The difference is not about the table.

What the twentieth year of that run is, and it is printed here because it is printed on a stick and not on a paper, is this.

The tally stick of that place is a stick of hazel about a foot long with a notch cut in it for every tenth night, and the flat at the top of it about four inches long is chalked on about once a week and washed off by nobody.

Four people in that room had seen the flat of that stick chalked in the past and five had not.

The woman of thirty-one has chalked it herself every week since she was about eleven. The woman before her chalked it, and the woman before that one chalked it, and there has never been a week in twenty years that anybody had to start again.

Nobody on that stick has ever restarted it. There is no run of empty years on it and no year with a gap in it.

Four people in that room said this is not a road. Five said it is the only road anybody in that room has ever been sure of.

On the Monday morning he asked to see it and she gave it to him.

He did not chalk anything on it and gave it back. Four of the eleven people in that room watched him not chalk it. Five had not known there was anything to chalk.

Then she chalked it herself, in front of eleven people.

The date went on the flat of it in the trade hand of a woman of thirty-one who walks a mile of made road every night of the year. The year on it was the twentieth of the run. It was not restarted and it is not going to be.

**And the cost of that Monday is about nine days of made road in Longlight for a man with a bad leg and a stick, one ring of iron about four inches across put into a hand and not taken back out of it, one column in a paper left empty on purpose, and about eight hundred and ninety-nine crossings with a keeper that nobody asked them to have. A form that asks for nine years and a woman with eleven years of walking a road in the dark and no standing in it whatever. A tally stick about a foot long with a notch cut in it for every tenth night and twenty years on it and no gap in it and no year restarted. The bleed five hundred and sixty-six days old and not cured and no panel and no page of its own and nobody in that room asked him about it. The interval four hours. One ear, one leg, and a year, none of the three on the stick and none of the three in the room.**
