# Chapter 0884

**Fallowmonth the twelfth, YR 325, a Thursday and a market morning in a harbour four days off a made road, a room over a boat-yard with a long table in it and about nine people at that table, four hundred people on the quay below between the eighth hour and the eleventh, and a wrapped sheet that came on the ordinary post nine days after it was sent.**

That harbour is a river mouth with a quay on the west side and there are nine boats a week in it and four of them go out on a Tuesday and a Friday, and about nine days of ordinary post is what a thing takes from there to a crossing four miles up that coast road.

The wrapped sheet reached that crossing on the seventh and the man it was addressed to opened it in a tool bag on a Friday and read nine sheets of a key to a shorthand he has never written in his life.

He is Tovan Kest and he came down that coast road on the Thursday with the four books in a case and about nine people in that harbour knew he had them and hundred on the quay did not.

The man who keeps the record is forty-seven and his name is Corvin Marchbank, and he wrote the first of the four books when he was twenty-eight, and he is the only person alive who can read what he wrote in them.

The four books are eleven inches by nine and the first of them is in a piece of oiled cloth and the other three are not, and they were all four begun on the same day in YR 306 and they are not a set and were never meant to be one.

An entry is one line and the line is about nine words long in a hand that puts the tide first and the place second and never writes a full stop, and four hundred people on that quay cannot read one line of any of them and have never wanted to.

What they can do is come and put a thumb on the page for the tenth of Fallowmonth YR 318 and be shown the ten lines either side of it, and that has been possible for four hundred years by anybody who asks, and about nine people in nine counties have said that nobody knew that and about five have said that everybody knew it.

He has kept it in one hand for nineteen years and he has refused a copy twice and one of the two refusals is the reason nobody in nine counties has had a copy of it.

That refusal was in the winter of YR 318, with ice in that harbour for about nine weeks and about nine boats off the point in one night, and the reason was given on a quay and it was good and it cannot be produced.

He said it again on that Thursday and he said it in nine sentences and he did not soften it, and four of the nine people at that table had heard it before and about five had not.

He said the nine sentences in the order he said them on the first day, which was a thing nobody asked him to do and four of the nine people at that table noticed the order and about five did not.

The order is the year, then the ice, then the nine boats, then the man he refused it to, then the road that shut for nine weeks in the year after, then nobody has seen it since, and then three sentences that are not about the harbour at all.

The last three are the reason the reason cannot be produced. They are that a reason given on a quay in bad weather to a man you like is not a record, and that nineteen years is a long time to lean on nine words spoken once, and that he has been the only person alive who could confirm it since the winter and there is now nobody at all.

About four people in nine counties have said those three sentences are worth the whole of the two hours of reading that followed them, and about five have said the whole of the two hours was worth more.

Then he said the part of it that he has not said before and four of the nine people at that table understood it at once and about five had to be asked what it was.

He said that the reason cannot be produced because it was never written, and that a reason nobody can produce is not a reason anybody can lean on, and that nineteen years is a long time to keep a thing on a sentence nobody wrote down.

About four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time he has admitted that and about five have said it is the first time he has said it out loud and both of those are the same fact.

That is the resistance of that Thursday and it was nine sentences long and it cost him something that four of the nine people at that table could see and about five could not.

Then he put the four books on the middle of that table, which nobody in that harbour has seen them on since about YR 312, and he opened the first of them at the third page and read nineteen years of a coast out loud.

About nine people in that room heard about two hours of it and about four of them understood a part and about five understood the shape of it, and four of them have said since that this is the one kind of record a person cannot argue about.

Nine of the entries he read were tides and nine were wrecks and about nine hundred were landings, and every one of the wrecks has a place on it that a person can stand in, and four of the nine people in that room had known that and about five had not.

Then the second holder stood up.

He had the nine sheets of the key in a case of his own and about nine days of reading behind him, and he read nine lines out of the third of the four books in a room with about nine people in it and got about seven of the nine right.

That is the whole of it and four of the nine people in that room had expected nine and about five had expected two.

What he could not read is not tide and it is not a wreck. It is a mark that means a boat came in light and that is the ninth thing in that hand that nobody has ever worked out, and he said so out loud in that room and did not guess at it.

About four of the nine people at that table had wanted him to guess and about five had wanted him to stop, and he stopped, and people in that room heard a man say out loud that he does not know a thing in a book he has been asked to keep.

Then the act happened and it is a local verb and it is not a vote.

The Morrow Coast Commons shares its sea routes and its records, and the sharing is done by the record being read out loud in that room twice a week at the ninth hour by two people, and by the four books standing on that table where anybody in that harbour can come and put a thumb on a page.

One of the two people who read is the man who wrote it and the other one is the man who cannot write it and can read it, and about four people in that harbour have said that is the only kind of sharing that could have been done and about five have said it is the only kind anybody would agree to.

And the second holder is named, and the naming is the gift, and it was done out loud in a market street on that Thursday afternoon with about four hundred people in it.

Then he said it, in the middle of that street, with the boats behind him and about four hundred people in it.

“**This coast's record is nineteen years of tide and wreck and landing and it is Corvin Marchbank's in his own shorthand and it is his and not mine, and I am the second holder of it because I was given a key and not the books, and no man on this quay should take a page of it out of that room.**”

That was sixty-two words and about four hundred people in that street heard every one of them and about nine of them had heard it before and about four hundred did not know until that afternoon that the record had been read aloud anywhere except in that room.

The naming in the street is the part nobody in that harbour expected, and four of the four hundred people in it had come out to hear a thing handed over and five had come out to look at the boats.

A man who can read a record is not the man who wrote it. A market street has four hundred people in it who cannot tell the two apart in nine words, and four of them have said that if nobody had said the name there would be a version of this by next year in which the reader wrote it.

Nobody in that street thanked either of the two men and about four of the four hundred had said beforehand that they would and about five had said that they would not and the five were right.

Then the four books went back onto the shelf above the tide table at about the fourth hour, and they are on the shelf, and four people in that harbour have said that anybody who comes in on a morning can go over the whole of that Thursday for himself.

They went back onto the shelf above the tide table about two hours later, and about nine people in that harbour watched a man put four books back the way he had found them.

What the naming costs the man who was named is not on the page and four of the nine people in that room worked it out at the time and about five did not.

He had said two days before that he wanted other places holding it and had said it in nine words and had said it to nobody in particular, and a name said out loud in a market street is not other places, and about four people in nine counties have said that he got less than he asked for and about five have said he got the only version of it anybody can use.

He said one thing about it on the Friday, alone, to a carter who was loading, and about four people in that harbour know what he said and about five do not and it is not on the page here.

Toward the charter that is being written in six places this year, this coast sent nine lines and about four people in that room had wanted them to be about nine and about five had wanted them to be about four, and about nine lines is what went.

The nine lines are that the record of a coast is the coast's own and not a copy of anything, and that no page of it may be taken out of that room by anybody including the person who wrote it.

The other five are about the hours. A reader may come between the ninth and the eleventh on a Tuesday or a Friday. The second holder takes the ninth and the man who wrote it takes the tenth, and answer to neither of them is owed by either of them to anybody.

The last of those five was written by the man who walks a lantern run nine miles off and it went in without anybody asking for it, and about four of the nine people in that room had not expected it and about five had.

Nine lines went from that room to the charter being written in six places this year, and about four of the nine people in that room wanted nine lines and about five wanted four, and about nine lines is what went.

What went to the charter in six places is this: that a coast's record belongs to the coast and is nobody's copy of anything, that nobody walks out of that room with a page of it, that the books lie open on a Tuesday and a Friday for anybody who asks, and that nine lines of them have gone into a paper being written this year.

Four hundred and forty-four days of one man in a lower archive, and he is not in that harbour and his name is in no record in this manuscript.

Two of the four hundred people in that street had been in that room on the Thursday and had come out of it and had heard all four books read, and about nine hundred did not know by the fourth hour and about four hundred knew by the second day.

What the man of forty-seven said after the books were back on the shelf is the last thing said in that room and it is in his own trade and it is about nine words.

“**A thing one man keeps is a thing one man loses, and I have been losing it for nineteen years in about nine words, and today it is on a table in a room with a door and about nine hundred people know it, and the sharing is the point and not the loss.**”

That was fifty-four words and every one of the nine people in that room heard all fifty-four, and four of them had the whole of it before he reached the last of them.

About four people in nine counties have said that a man who has kept a thing in one hand for nineteen years and then says the sharing is the point has said the only true thing anybody in his position could say, and about five have said it is easy to say when somebody else is going to read it out for you on a Tuesday.

Nothing was ratified in that harbour and nothing in that room was sealed and the nine lines went up the coast road in the ordinary post on the Friday in a wrapping sheet with a person's name on it.

Nobody in that town has been told what happens to a paper and about four people in that harbour have asked and about five have not, and the answer that went back was that it is not a paper yet and there is nothing to be told about a thing that has not got a name.

**And the cost of that Thursday is nineteen years of a shorthand read out loud for about two hours in a room over a boat-yard with the four books standing on a table anybody could touch, one refusal from YR 318 said a second time and not softened and the reason still unproducible, nine sheets of a key carried nine days in an ordinary post to a person and not to a place, about two hundred lines read badly out of a third of the four books and about nine hundred of them not read at all, a mark that means a boat came in light that nobody has ever worked out, the whole of it put into nine lines going to a paper being written in six places, and a man's name said out loud in a market street so that nobody thinks the record is free or that the reader wrote it.
