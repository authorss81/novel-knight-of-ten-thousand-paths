**Wolfmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 325, a Monday and not a market morning, the weigh-house and the yard of the Old Toll in Merefen, the winter market standing in nine hundred feet of the street outside it because the street is four days of made road off and four days of made road is nearer nine days of walking, nine stalls and four hundred people, and nine carters at the counter at about the ninth hour.**

The engine has been damaged five hundred and thirty-two days and nobody's achievement and nothing in this year mends it.

Four hundred and ninety-eight days of a man in a lower archive and nobody at that counter asked about him, and four of the nine carters there had not thought about him that morning and five had.

Nobody thanked anybody in that yard, and four of the nine carters at that counter had come in expecting thanks and five had not, and that market will be nine words long in every county that hears of it.

The winter market at that crossing is a Monday thing and the summer one is a Saturday thing, and four hundred people come on a Monday and nine hundred come on a Saturday, and four of the nine stallholders in that street had asked which of the two is the real market and five had not.

The nine stalls are a butcher, a chandler, a man who sells nothing but needles, a woman who has sold the same forty kinds of cheese for nineteen years, two that sell the same thing and are not in the same trade, a saddler, a boy who holds that stall on Thursdays, and one that is a cart.

The market has stood in that street through nine winters, and four of the nine stallholders had wanted it moved to the made road and five had said a market that moves is not a market, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said both of those are about the same thing.

Nobody in that street sells at that market who lives outside nine days of it, which is a rule nobody wrote, and four hundred people in nine counties have read it as a custom and five hundred have read it as a wall.

The protagonist is in that yard because there is a bridge contract on that four miles of made road, being nine yards of coping and two gates, and four of the nine carters at that counter knew it and five did not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said a man who comes to a dispute with a tool bag has already lost half of it.

What was wrong with it was the toll, and it has been wrong for eleven months, and four of the nine carters at that counter had come that morning to have it settled and five had come to trade.

The toll on that crossing is a farthing for a hand barrow and a penny for a cart with four hundredweight in it, and eleven months ago the trust raised the cart toll by a halfpenny and nobody has ever voted on that raise.

The halfpenny is four ninths of what a cart pays and nine thousand halfpence a year, and four of the nine carters at that counter had worked that out and five had not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that is what a dispute is.

The raise was made at a quarter's meeting in the room at the back and nine men were in it and four of those nine had wanted the boards renewed instead and five had wanted the coping done.

What is on the wall in that room is a list of four leaves and it names the boards, the two hinges, the coping, the gate and the gate post, and four of the nine men at that meeting had read the list before they voted and five had not.

The halfpenny is on the quarter page and not on the list, and nine people in that town have said a thing which is charged and is not on the list is the whole definition of a thing nobody voted for, and four hundred have said it is on the quarter page and that is enough.

Nobody has ever asked those nine men whether they remember raising it, and four of the nine carters at that counter had wanted to ask and five had said a man remembers what he was angry about and nothing else.

A carter of forty-four kept a tally on the back of his hand in the spring and rubbed it out before he got to the town, and four of the nine carters at that counter had seen him do that and five had not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said it is the ninth time that trick has been done in nine counties.

The guild says the minute book has it. The minute book is a bound book of nine hundred leaves with a quarter's entry on each of four of them and the entry for the quarter eleven months ago has a halfpenny in it and no vote against it and no minute of anybody asking.

Nine carters came because of that entry. Four of the nine had wanted the entry read out and five had wanted a man sent for, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a minute nobody voted on is a minute a man wrote.

Seven of the nine carters come into Merefen on a Monday from nine days off and two of them live in the town, and four of the nine had wanted the four-day road made shorter and five had wanted it made level.

One of the nine is a woman of thirty-seven who has carted the same nine miles for eleven years and who says the halfpenny is not about a quarter, and four of the nine carters at that counter had believed her and five had not.

“**I am not here about a quarter's meeting. I am here because I paid it nine times and I want to know what I paid it for, and if the answer is nine feet of coping then I will pay it again and I will stop, and if the answer is a line in a book with no vote on it then somebody owes me nine halfpence and I would like them in front of nine people.**”

That was seventy-seven words and nine of the carters at that counter heard all seventy-seven of them, four of them to the end and the other five to about the fortieth word.

That is the whole shape of the dispute and four of the nine carters at that counter have said since that it took eleven months to arrive at a question a person can answer and five have said the question was there on the first day.

The man who keeps the crossing is Wendall Crake and he has kept it twenty-two years and he is fifty-eight, and half of those nine carters knew the name and half of them did not, and four hundred people in nine counties know it.

He has been putting his own name in the trust column for three years where the guild's name goes, and four of the guild found out a year later and five said nothing about it, and nine carters at that counter have seen that column and nine hundred have not.

What that has to do with a halfpenny is that the guild asked him to stop on the ninth of Fallowmonth and he has not stopped, and four of the nine carters at that counter had wanted to know which side he was on and five had wanted a number.

He said a number. He said that the boards were lifted nine times a day in the summer and twice in the winter and that nine feet of coping on the far side had been cracked and mended three times, and that a halfpenny a cart is what nine feet of coping costs.

That was fifty-three words and all nine of the carters heard all fifty-three of them, and about four of them had the whole of it before he came to the end and about five stopped at the thirtieth.

Then the protagonist did the thing that is the physical of that Monday, and it took nine minutes and it cannot be undone by anybody in that street.

He took the nine toll slips off the counter and read the halfpenny off nine of them out loud, one at a time, with the cart's own weight on the slip against the figure, and four of the nine carters at that counter had wanted that done and five had wanted a vote.

What came out of it is that nine carters paid the halfpenny on nine days out of four hundred, and four of the nine carters at that counter had assumed they had all paid it every day and five had not paid it at all.

Nine days is not a decision either. About four of the nine carters at that counter said so and about five said a number that thin could not be a mistake, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said both of those are the same sentence said twice.

Then a boy of eleven came up to that counter and about four of the nine carters at that counter had seen him every week of the winter and about five had not noticed him.

The cutting and the stitching is done over that shop and has been done there for about nine years, and the stall on Thursdays is his and has been his since he was nine, and about four of the nine carters at that counter had known the household and about five had only known the boy.
