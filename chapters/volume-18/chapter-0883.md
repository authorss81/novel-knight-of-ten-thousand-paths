# Chapter 0883

**Fallowmonth the seventh, YR 325, a Saturday and a market morning, a gate at the low end of four miles of made road about nine miles off that town, a crossing house and a barrier and a lantern run that goes from the last house to the first, hundred people in that town asleep for most of what follows, and the shortest interval the occupied timetable has allowed sixteen hours.**

The road is four miles long and it is made and it is straight and there is a toll bar at the low end of it and a gate at the top, and crossings a day go over the sill between them on foot, on a cart and on a horse.

The lantern run goes out at about the ninth hour in the evening and comes back at about the fifth hour in the morning, and it is a woman of thirty-one and she walks it with a lantern and a stick and she has done it for years.

She has a ring of iron in her hand since the seventh of Longlight and four people in nine counties know what that is and about five have asked and about four of those five were told.

About four hundred people in that town were asleep at about the fourth hour of that Saturday morning, and nine of them were not, and four of the nine were not asleep on purpose.

Nobody knows yet who the nine were. That is the finding of the nine days and four people in nine counties have worked at it since and about five have not, and four have said it was about nine men and five have said it was nobody and four have said it was four people who had been given a reason by somebody who is not in that town.

What was taken off the sill between the bar and the gate was a plate about the width of two hands, and it was taken with a crowbar and about nine men and it took about forty minutes, and it was done at the fourth hour because that is when hundred people are asleep.

There were four of them at the fourth hour on that road who were not asleep, and one of them was the woman with the lantern and the ring.

Then the work that defended it began, and it was not a fight and nobody in that town used the word.

The plate is not a plate of glass. It is a seat and a channel and nine inches of the floor it sits in, and the people who made it are two days off a made road and four hundred miles off in a valley with four furnaces in it.

What is under it is a channel about the length of a forearm, and what is in the channel is a thing that does not have a name in any trade anybody in that town has, and four people in nine counties have said the name does not matter and about five have said that it matters entirely.

The sill of that crossing is laid in about nine courses and the channel is in the fourth course from the top, and about nine men had to get a crowbar into a joint that is nine inches deep and four inches wide with a lamp and their hands in about forty minutes.

A bridgewright came about nine hours after the plate was off and was on that road for nine days, and he is Tovan Kest and he is a man this manuscript has known since the first year of it.

What he did was not to put the plate back. About four of the nine people on that road said he should have and about five said he would not, and he said he would not put it back in the same bed because the bed had been got at.

That is the whole of the resistance of that Saturday and it is nine words long and nine people on that road heard them.

He said that a thing got at once will be got at again and that about nine crossings a day went over that sill in months and that he would rather have a floor that could be mended than a seat that could be taken.

About four people in nine counties have said that is the correct answer and about five have said it is the answer of a man who has never been on a road at the fourth hour.

Neither of those is on the road and both of them were said afterwards, and the road went on without either of them.

The first of the four days did nothing that anybody watching from that street would have called work. The four of them took the sill stone up on a bar and a lever and laid it on the grass, and they dug the old bed out with their hands and put the spoil in a barrow and wheeled it to the low end and tipped it there.

Nine men stood at the bar at the top of that road for two hours of the second day. Four of them offered to help and five offered to carry stone away, and the man of sixty-one who keeps the cart said no to both of those in the same words, and four people in that town have said that a man who says no to help and no to carrying is refusing to be helped and about five have said he is refusing something nobody has offered him.

That is the whole of what the five said and none of it was said at the time.

So what happened is that people did bridge work.

Not nine and not nine hundred. Four, and one of the four is a woman of thirty-one who walks a lantern run and four of the nine people on that road had not known before that morning that she knew how to do it and about five had not known at all.

What the fourth of them is and how she came to be it is the other finding of those nine days, and it took four people about nine days to find it out and nobody had to ask her a single question about it.

She had been doing it since she was about twenty-two and she had been doing it with the things a lantern run gives a person, which is a bar, a length of cord and about nine years of knowing where the frost is under a sill in this county. Nobody had ever asked her to know it and people in that town have said that is a thing nobody asks a person to do and about five have said it is the only kind of knowing anybody ever pays for.

On the fourth day she said the thing that is said out loud about half the road, and nine people on that road heard it.

“**I have walked this road for nine years and I never once knew what was under that sill, and now I do, and I am telling you that I do, and a hundred and forty people live on this four miles and not one of them knows a thing of it happened, and those are two facts and I have stopped trying to make them sit together in me.**”

That was sixty-nine words and every one of the nine people on that road heard all sixty-nine of them, and four of them had the whole of it before she sat down and five were given the middle of it afterwards.

Then Tovan Kest said the other half of it, and he said it on the sixth day to four people and not to the nine, and about four of the nine found out about it later and about five never did.

The other three are a man of about fifty who keeps a cart and has crossed that sill nine thousand times, a boy of about seventeen whose family is nine hundred yards up that four miles, and a man of about sixty-one who has not crossed it since a thing happened there and who came on the fourth day on his own two feet.

Nobody hired them. That is the whole of what happened between the seventh and the sixteenth and four people in nine counties have said it is the only piece of work anybody has done in four and a half years that nobody ordered.

He said it standing in the channel with the lamp beside him and he did not raise his voice and he did not look at anybody while he said it.

“**A bed that has been got at once will be got at again, and I have put plates back into beds that had been got at twice, and both of those are the same mistake with a different name on it. This time I am leaving you a floor you can mend with a bar and a shovel instead of a seat a crowbar can take out in forty minutes at the fourth hour, and if anybody in this county wants to thank me for that they can carry the barrow.**”

That was ninety-one words and the four people who heard all of it understood every word of it and nine people on that road know that it was said.

The barrow went to the low end of four miles of made road about eleven times that week and every load of it was tipped in the same place, and about four people in that town have said that a barrow is a thing a county can argue with and about five have said it is a thing a county can count.

The frost is what makes the sill move and it is under the north side only, and the north side of that sill is the side a cart wheel runs on, and the boy of seventeen found the place where the frost gets in on his second morning and told the man of fifty where it was without being asked and without knowing what it was for.

Nothing about any of that was written down. Two people in that town have said that it should have been and seven have said that a thing four people know on a four-mile road does not need a paper on it, and both of those are the same sentence with the paper left out.

About nine crossings a day kept working over those nine days because about four people were on that sill, and nine hundred people along those four miles knew that by about the fourth day and about five hundred of them did not know it by the sixteenth.

The bleed is six hundred and sixty-two days old and is not cured and is not explained and no character on that road has noticed it.

Seven of the eight anchor fragments where there were ten, and the seventh went on the eighth of that month because a place nine days off voted about it on a Tuesday and about four people in that place had not wanted to and about five had.

Then he put his hand where it should not have gone.

That is the ending of the ninth day and it is about the fourth hour again and it is not a fight and nobody has called it one.

He was holding the sill stone down with his left hand while Tovan Kest worked a lever under the seat with his right, and the sill stone moved about half an inch, and the edge of the old bed came up through the place his hand was, and it went four inches and it did not go deep.

About four of the nine people on that road saw it happen and about five had their backs to it, and the woman with the lantern saw it and had her hand on his arm before he had got the hand off.

That is the scar and it is on the inside of his right forearm and it is four inches long and four people in nine counties know about it and about five will.

What he lost with it is a certainty and not a thing anybody else had, being that he had believed for four and a half years that a person standing at a road can be found by walking a made road to the road.

About four of the nine people on that road had believed it too and about five had not and about four of the five have said since that they never believed it because they walk those roads for a living and they know what is at the far end of four miles of nothing.

What happened on the tenth day is on the page because four people in nine counties have said that it is the day the thing stopped being about him.

The man of sixty-one who had not crossed that sill since a thing happened there crossed it on a cart on the tenth day at about the ninth hour with about nine other people in that town behind him, and none of them had been asked to come and about four of them had asked why and about five had not.

He crossed it because there was a floor to cross and about four people in that town have said that is the whole reason and about five have said it was not the reason and did not say what was.

Then the barrier went up at the low end of that four miles for the first time in about four months and nine hundred people used it in a week and about nine of them did not know it had been closed at all until the ninth day.

He did not lose it in front of them and about four of them worked out later what it was and about five did not and nobody asked him about it in that road at any point in the next nine days.

What he did on that ninth night, after the band had gone on his arm and been looked at by a woman of thirty-one who said one thing about it and then went back to her round, was to stop and to give the lantern to somebody else.

That is the physical of the whole nine days and it cannot be undone. He did not walk the lantern run that night and he has not walked one since, and four people in nine counties have said that is the first thing he has given away in four and a half years that he could not have given away by asking somebody else to take it.

No route was opened by him in nine days and no route was closed by him in nine days and about nine crossings a day went over a sill that four people mended, and four people in nine counties have said that is the whole of what happened on that road and about five have said it is more than that.

**And the cost of that Saturday and the eight days after it is one anchor plate gone off a sill about nine inches deep and not put back in the same bed, four people doing about nine days of bridge work that nobody ordered, nine crossings a day going over that sill by hand and by cart and by horse because about four people were on it, one night of a lantern run not walked by the man who has been given a ring of iron to keep, four inches of scar on the inside of his right forearm that will not close over in the way the other one does not, and one certainty about roads gone out of him on the ninth night. The bleed six hundred and sixty-two days and uncured and unremarked. Seven anchor fragments where there were ten. The shortest interval sixteen hours. No road was opened on that stretch by anybody, and no road was shut on it either.**
