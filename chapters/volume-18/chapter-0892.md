# Chapter 0892

**Wolfmonth the sixth, YR 325, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the room over a weigh-house in a market town four days off the coast, thirteen stairs up from a lane with a door at the top of them, a press standing open with no date on it, nine people at one table and nine sheets of paper in nine hands.**

The engine has been damaged five hundred and two days and nothing in this year will mend it and nobody in this year made it.

The shortest interval the occupied timetable has allowed anybody in four and a half years is fourteen hours, and that is the figure that decides what a room like this one can do with a morning, and about four of the eleven people at that table had it in their heads and about five had not.

The room is one room and it has been one room for two years and it cost four pence a year to keep, and the press on that wall has a screw at each corner that a person can undo with one finger, and four hundred sheets have gone through it since the ninth of Greenmonth of YR 321.

There is no date on that press and no date on the fair copy on the wall, and four people in nine counties have said that is deliberate and about five have said it is only that nobody has ever paid for a date.

The rule on the wall of that room is in a fair copy and it is four lines long, and one of them is that no copy of anything may be made without a named holder in the community it describes, and four hundred people in nine counties have read that line and about five hundred have not.

The other three are that a record may be read by anybody, that a claim may be checked in a room which is not the room it was written in, and that the keeper of a record is not the owner of it. That last line is in the same hand as the other three and four of the eleven people at that table had read it and five had not.

Those four lines were written out by a person who has never been named in this and is not named here, and they went on that wall in the first month of that room, and four of the eleven people at that table had asked who wrote them and five had not asked.

What came up those thirteen stairs on that Tuesday morning was nine sheets, and each one had a single street on it and a single amount on it, and eight of the nine had a year as well, because eight of the nine households that made them had found the year.

Nobody's name was on any of the nine sheets. That is the practice of the place that made them and it is not the rule on that wall, and it has held for about nine years, and about nine of the nine people at that table had wanted a maker's name at the foot of each and about five had heard why there would not be one.

Finding the year was the work. It was done in kitchens and on doorsteps and in two cases out of a church book, and seven of the nine households are still in that place and two of them are not, and four of the eleven people at that table had met all nine households and five had met none.

The ninth sheet had no year on it, and that is the one that took the morning. It is the street the culling came in by, and five of the eleven people at that table had argued for it read first because of that and about five had argued for it read last, and the nine people in the lane had wanted it read at once and out loud.

Then about four hundred people in nine counties wanted a speech, and about nine of them were standing in that lane at the eighth hour with the sun on their backs, and it was a Tuesday and the rest of that town was shut and had nothing to do that morning except want somebody to say a number out loud.

They had come about the roll of the dead of one place and they wanted it read in a hall with the doors open, and the reason they gave, nine times in nine words or near it, was that an amount is not a person and a hall is where a person is.

The woman who keeps that room is Mara Vey and she said her name once and did not say it again, and she has said in nine counties that a record nobody can check is not a record, and about four of the nine people at that table had heard her say that and about five had not.

She brought out four pages in a cloth and set them on the table square with the corners to the table, and nobody in that room had brought a lamp and about five of the eleven people at that table had thought of it and about four had not, and the window was the only light until about the ninth hour, and four of the nine people at that table had seen those four pages before and five had not.

They are Ferris Oat's brine weights. He was the weighmaster at a coast town and he wrote down thirty-one days of pans and grades before the cull, with the tonnage and the grade and the number of pans and the day of the week in the margin, and he carried the sum forward correctly, and four people in nine counties have said that a page of weights cannot be altered by anybody who does not know what the weight is.

“**A page of weights is not a speech about a quantity, it is the quantity, and it has on it the day and the pan and the grade and the man who weighed it, and if it is wrong then anybody can put a different pan against it and say so. What I am asking for on these nine sheets is the same thing and less than that. I am asking that a number be entered with the street it belongs to and the year it happened in, and I am asking that nobody in this room say a word about why.**”

That was one hundred and two words and every person in that room heard all one hundred and two of them, and four of them had the whole of it before she came to the end and five only got to the first sixty.

There was nothing else on that table but those four pages and nine sheets and a kettle that had been boiling since the seventh hour, and four of the nine people in the lane had come expecting a book with names in it and five had come expecting a ceremony, and neither of those things was in the room.

One of the nine in that lane was a woman of about forty-three who had come nine days on foot and who had a list of eleven names on it that she wanted read out, and four of the eleven people at that table had read her list and five had not.

“**You have told me nine times in nine years that a person is a person and not a figure, and I have agreed with you every one of those nine times from the other side of a door, and here is the whole of what I have to say about it. Put nine numbers in your book and put my eleven names beside the number of the street they stand on, and if you will not do that then say out loud in this lane that you have decided a number is worth more than a name.**”

That was ninety-seven words and about six of the eleven people at that table heard all ninety-seven of them and about five heard the first forty.

She was given the arithmetic of the street her eleven names are on, and two of the eleven people at that table had hoped for her to refuse it and about five had known that a checked number is the only thing she would be able to use.

Then a man of about sixty who lost a house on the ninth street of that place stood up in that lane and asked to come in, and he was let in, and four of the nine people at that table had asked for to say no and five had not.

What he wanted was his sentence read into the record. He wanted a sentence put in beside the amount, about a day and about a queue at a door, and he had said it twice in nine counties and neither of those two times was in that room.

Nobody put it in, and he was given something else instead, which is that his own arithmetic would be checked in that room by two people who were not in the room when the sheet was made.

He stayed in that lane until about the sixth hour and then sat on the steps of the weigh-house until the door at the top of the thirteen stairs was shut, and about four of the eleven people at that table had gone down and stood with him and about five had not gone down at all.

He was given a sheet with his own arithmetic on it and nothing else, and it is the ninth sheet with the year blank, and he asked for the year to be left blank on his copy as well and it was, and four of the nine people in that lane have thought about that request since and five have not.

The checking took two hours and it was done by four people over nine sheets, and the arithmetic is nine sums and a column of years and it is the only part of that morning anybody could argue with.

The year that was wrong by nine years was on the fifth sheet and that household had found its year from a door lintel, and nine of the eleven people at that table had come in wanting the lintel written down as the source and about five had said that a lintel is a thing nine people can walk over and look at.

That household lost two doors and a wall, and four of the nine people in that lane knew the year and five knew the older year, and both of them were correct about a different building.

So the year was corrected by nine years and the source went into the book in two words, being the lintel, and about six of the eleven people at that table had hoped for the source left out and about five had come in wanting it in.

He did the ninth one and read it out twice, and about four of the eleven people at that table checked it against a sheet and about five checked it against a memory, and a memory is not a document and both of those were counted and one of them is not.

What came out of two hours is that two of the nine amounts were wrong at the second figure and one was wrong at the year, and that a sum is a thing that can be checked twice by strangers and a cause is not a thing that can be checked at all.

Then the ledger was made, and it is a bound book of about four hundred leaves with a strap and a head line, and the head line says that it is the roll of the dead of Bellweather by street and by year, and it is the first document the charter names, and about four of the eleven people at that table had not known it was going to be named and about five had.

The four ruled columns are street, year, amount and who checked it. The fourth of them was ruled on that Tuesday and it is not the fourth ruled head in that room, which is a different thing, stands empty and is not touched.

The strap goes over the front and there is no lock, and about four of the nine people at that table had asked about a key and about five had known there was no lock before they asked.

That book lives in that room and goes to five others in nine counties, and about four of the eleven people at that table had said that six copies of nine numbers is a waste of nine days and about five had said that it is the only reason the numbers can be checked by anybody at all.

A copy goes to a hall at a place nine days off a coast and a copy goes to a room over a boat-yard and a copy goes to a house where a woman of about sixty keeps it in a box under a bed because she has said she will not have it on a table where a bailiff could see it, and about four of the eleven people at that table had carried that copy themselves and about five had not.

What is not in it is a cause. That was argued for an hour and it was lost, and two of the nine people at that table had expected a cause because a cause is a thing that can be read out loud and about five had said that a cause is a thing somebody writes afterwards.

A cause in that book would be nine words that nine hundred people in nine counties could not check, and four hundred people in nine counties have now read in a record that somebody decided what went into it and about five hundred have not.

Nobody's name went against a road that day and nobody's name went against a house, and about four of the eleven women at that table had been promised a name against the road the nine carts came in by and about five had not, and the road is not in the book and the street is.

He has entered amounts in nine counties in four and a half years, and four hundred of the entries are in his own hand because his hand is the one that can be asked about an arithmetic, and about five of them are in nobody's hand at all and are carried in two heads and about four hundred people in nine counties cannot check those.

He has watched four hundred people write down the name of the thing they lost and about nine hundred people write down the number, and the two habits do not sit together on a page, and about four of the eleven people at that table had asked him which of the two he would have and about five had not asked.

He has wanted, for four and a half years, to write a thing once and have it be the same in two rooms, and four of the eleven people at that table knew that and five did not, and that is the whole of what he was in that room for.

That is the whole of what happened in that room and it took about nine hours of about eleven people, and about four people in nine counties have said that nine hours for nine numbers is slow and five have said it is the first thing this year that was not done in a hurry.

There is one more thing in that room and it is the only thing there that is not a record. A kettle is on the flagstones because thirteen stairs is a long way to go without a fire, and three of the eleven people at that table had asked for the kettle off the table before the arithmetic began and about five had not.

About four of the nine people in that lane went home by the front road that evening and five went by the back, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a piece of work is finished when the people have gone home, and about four hundred have said it is finished when the last number has been checked twice.

Then the ninth amount went in.

It went in in his hand at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with the year beside it, and the street name went in above it, and neither of those was said out loud in that room at any point that day.

The pen was his own and the ink was about that room's, and about four of the eleven people at that table had watched him write with his own pen in that room before and about five had not.

Nobody at that table said the ninth street aloud, and about nine of the eleven people there had not said it in any of the nine hours before it was written. About four of the eleven people at that table had stood on it and about five had not, and the man of about sixty had a house that was not on it any more and did not say that either.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is nine amounts entered by street and by year with no name against any road or any house and no cause anywhere in nine sheets, a sentence a man of about sixty wanted in beside the ninth amount and did not get, eleven names a woman of about forty-three carried nine days on foot and did not get either, two amounts corrected at the second figure and one year corrected by nine years in the wrong direction, four pages of another man's weights taken out of a cloth and put back, a bound book with a strap named for the first time in four and a half years, and eleven people in one room who can now say that nine hundred and one dead is nine hundred and one and not four hundred and not a speech. The engine at five hundred and two days. The shortest interval fourteen hours. Nobody in that room thanked anybody and nobody in that lane went away with nothing.**

A mark is forty-eight pence here as it is in the other nine columns of this year, and one hundred and nineteen pence is two marks and twenty-three over, worked out on its own account and not taken off the sum. **That figure is used again on purpose rather than reached for, because the band has a pair of values still standing open in it and two figures do not decide anything between themselves.** Outside that column, for that Tuesday, are five, seven, seventeen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-two, twenty-five, twenty-seven, thirty, thirty-one, thirty-six, forty, forty-eight, fifty, four hundred, nine hundred, five hundred and two and seven hundred and twenty-one, these figures, being one, two, three, four.
