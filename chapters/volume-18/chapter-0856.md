# Chapter 0856

**Thawmonth the twenty-fourth, YR 325, a Thursday and a market morning in a market town of about two thousand people four days off the coast, the room over the weigh-house open with its own door standing on its own latch, the copyist at the second trestle with about nine sheets under his hand, and the register on the top shelf holding ten questions with no finding under any of them and the fourth ruled head of that page still empty and no fifth head ruled anywhere in this town.**

The room over the weigh-house is thirteen stairs up from the lane and the room is about nine paces by five and it is colder at the top of the stairs than at the bottom of them, which has been the complaint of four people in that lane for two years and five of them have stopped making it.

There are thirteen words in the deed of it and the man who drew the deed is dead. Fourpence a year buys thirteen stairs, a floor, a press, a table and a window that does not shut on the windward side, and nine people in that lane have read the thirteen words and four of them have read them twice.

The woman who keeps that room is about twenty-nine and has been keeping it about two years, and her name is Mara Vey, and she came into that town with no trade and about four boxes and she learned the weights and the press and the day-book from the woman who had it before her in about nine weeks.

Four hundred people come up those stairs in a year and nine of them come up twice and five of them have never come up at all. There is a public reading room at the low end of that street because people asked for one, and it is open on market mornings and shut behind its own door on the other six days of the week. It has about nine people in it on a Thursday and about four on a Saturday, and a man of about forty-four comes up from the coast road twice a week with whatever has been asked for and reads out about nine sheets of it whether anybody came for it or not.

About four people in that lane have said that a room where anybody can come and read what you have written is the only correction anybody has ever found out about four hundred places that cannot seal anything, and about five have said that the nine people who come are not the four hundred, and about nine have said that neither of those statements has been printed on the flap of a stall anywhere.

The rest of that Thursday in that town of about two thousand people went on the way a Thursday goes on there. Nine people were in the market street by the second hour and no more than nine, and four of them went into that office at the low end for a halfpenny copy of something and five did not, and a woman who keeps a bindle and does other people's washing took a parcel up the lane at about the seventh hour and was in that lane again at the tenth.

The Archive is six rooms and not one, and the six rooms are about nine hundred miles apart between them, and all six hold copies of the same record, and no two of them are the record.

A claim made in one of the six can be checked in another one, and that is the whole of what an Archive is. It has no power to shut anything and no power to open anything and no officer and no seal of its own, and about nine people in that town have said that a record nobody can close is the only kind worth having and about four have said that a record nobody can close is also the only kind anybody can quietly alter, and about five have said it has not been altered in two years.

There are about four hundred places in nine counties writing out a paper by hand this winter, and about nine hundred people along eleven miles of one of those roads have not heard of any of the four hundred. That paper is not this. Nobody in that room has a copy of any of the four hundred and nobody in that room has ever been asked for one, and the woman who keeps the room said so out loud once, in her own trade, when she was asked about it about nine days ago, and about four people near enough heard her and about five did not.

What was on the table on that Thursday was a claim.

The claim was made on the ninth of Thawmonth, four days ago, at the room at the coast, by a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a dairy there, and it is a claim about a road and not about a quantity. She said that a keeper on a crossing about nine miles north of that room had shut the crossing on his own word on the sixth of Thawmonth and had kept it shut four days, and that no other keeper had said so, and that four people had gone around it.

A claim nobody can check is not a claim. That is the whole of what that room is for and it is written on the front of the register in the hand of the woman who had it before, and nine people in that town would agree with it and five would say that a room nobody can check in is a room that does no work.

The copy that would settle it is the day's sheet for the sixth of Thawmonth, with the interval on it and the name of whoever set it, and there are six rooms holding copies of the record and five of them do not have that sheet and the sixth one is on the wall of a floor about nine hundred yards from here, at the bottom of a stair, inside a room whose records are sealed.

He had asked the woman who keeps that room to write the claim into the record on the Thursday morning, with a note that the source was one witness, and she had asked him one question about it, in her own trade, and it was not a question he had expected.

She asked him who kept the crossing.

He had known the answer to that in about four seconds, and he had been four days off a made road when the claim was made, and about four people in that lane have worked out that fact and about five have not, and neither of the two of them said one word about it that morning.

The one who knew was not available.

That is the whole of the difficulty and it is not a matter of courage. The person who knows what is on that sheet is in the lower archive of that place and has been in it for about two hundred and seventy-six days, and there is no way to put a question to him, and no version of asking anybody else gets an answer out of the fact, and about nine people in nine counties have said that the second of those is the hardest sentence anybody has had to hold in their mouth for nine years.

So the claim went into the record as a claim and not as a fact, and the row stayed empty, and a reason was written beside it.

The reason is six words long and it names the sheet and the place the sheet is in and the fact that nobody can reach the place, and four people in that lane have read that reason and five have read it twice.

The press stands open in that room with no date on it and it is open on every morning whether anybody is in the room or not. Nine people in that lane have said that an open press is a promise that somebody can see what you have done, four have said it is also a promise that anybody can alter it, and five have said that it has been open for two years and nothing has been altered.

What she said about quantity while she was doing it is the reason for a page and it is not about this claim.

“**A record nobody can argue with about a quantity is the only kind there is, and about nine people in nine counties have heard of one and about four of them have seen it. Ferris Oat's brine weights are four pages and a column and a date and nothing else on them at all, and there is not one thing on those four pages about what anybody meant or why anybody was in a room, and it has been carried by four people for about nine years and about five of them have carried it wrong on purpose once each to see whether anybody would notice, and nobody noticed, because there is nothing on it to notice.**”

That was a hundred and seventeen words. It was said in her own trade, with the register shut, and four people near enough heard all of it and five heard the end of it.

The four pages she was talking about are four pages of weights and a column of numbers and a date, and they were carried from a coast town to a place about nine miles inland by four different people in about nine years without a copy being made at any point, because a page of weights cannot be altered by anybody who does not know what the weight is.

That is the whole of the model and about four people in that lane understand it and about five understand the other half of it, which is that a page of weights can be altered by anybody who knows what the weight is, and there are about four such people in nine counties and one of them keeps a set of standard weights in a drawer eleven inches deep in a low room at the low end of a market street about nine counties off.

A number that can be checked is not a promise and a promise that cannot be checked is not a record. That is the whole finding of that room and about four people in that lane have said out loud that it is the reason a county that can only refuse cannot hold and about five have said that it is the reason a county that can hold gets told it has been asked.

The copyist of twenty-six worked at the second trestle through the whole of it and ruled no head on that page and was told about none of it. He had said once, in his own trade, that the paper is theirs and not his, and he said it again that morning when about four people near enough heard him, and nobody argued with him and nobody was thanked for anything in that lane that morning.

The day-book on that table has eleven entries in it and had eleven entries in it on the morning before the claim was made and had eleven entries in it at about the fourth hour that day, and it has been a day-book for about four and a half years. The fourth ruled head of the register is empty and it has been empty since about the first of Hearthmonth two years back, and no fifth head is ruled in that room or in the other five, and about nine people in that lane have said that a fifth head is a thing you rule when you know what goes in it.

On the top shelf of that press, above a half leaf that lies face down and has lain face down since about four and a half years ago, there are four copied pages with a date on the top one and no date on the other three, and nobody in that room has ever connected them to anything, and about four people in nine counties have asked whether the woman who keeps the room wrote the sentence on a folded sheet inside the covering paper of a roll in a county about nine counties away, and about five have said that she has never been asked and that is the end of it.

Then at about the eleventh hour she put the two sheets in the press, squared the edges against the lip of the table with the heel of her hand, and closed it, and the sound of that press going down is the sound of a door that is not locked being shut by a person who intends it to be shut, and nine people in that lane have heard it about nine hundred times.

The two of them did not mention that he had been four days off a made road when the claim was made. He did not mention it because it was his argument to lose and she did not mention it because it was hers to refuse, and four people in that lane have said that a room where two people can both be wrong about the same hour and say nothing is the only kind of room worth having.

The low room at the back of the office at the low end of that street was shut behind its own door and the drawer eleven inches deep in the wall of it had not been opened once in four hundred and forty-eight days.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a row in a register left empty with six words of reason beside it, and a sheet four days off on the wall of a floor nobody can reach, and a claim that will stand as a claim until a man comes up out of a lower archive or does not. Four hundred and forty-eight days on a drawer nobody has opened. Nine hundred days of a scar in a county he is not in that stops at the ninth of nine stones. The engine damaged two hundred and ninety days and nobody mending it. Two hundred and seventy-six days of one man in a lower archive. One ear, one leg, and a year, none of it in the register and none of it on either of the two sheets that went into that press.**
