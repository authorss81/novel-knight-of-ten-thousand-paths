# Chapter 0895

**Wolfmonth the twentieth, YR 325, a Tuesday and not a market morning, the last four hundred yards of a made road before there is nothing to arrive at, a boundary at the end of it with a post and a step and a bench, nine people standing in nine yards, and the nine days since he read the last line of what is written there.**

The engine has been damaged five hundred and twenty-six days and it is nobody's achievement and nothing in this year mends it. Nobody thanked anybody on that road, and of the nine standing in those yards four had wagered a morning's work on somebody saying something and five had wagered against it, and nine hundred people in nine counties will hear about that road in nine words each.

The rite happened nine days before, in a room over a weigh-house four days off a coast, and it took nine minutes and it was a civil rite and two witnesses stood in it and neither of them said anything they had not come to say.

There is a deed of thirteen words on the table in that room and there were two names on it and one of them is a keeper of a record and the other one is a man who builds and mends crossings, and four people in nine counties have read the deed and five hundred have not. Nobody thanked them for it.

Four people in that room had expected the word and five had not expected it, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that two people who have been careful in four hundred rooms ought to be careful in the ninth as well. Then he went four days and nine days of ordinary post and arrived at that road at about the eighth hour with a stick in his right hand and a tool bag at his feet and no notebook.

He is there as a witness and not as anything else, and four of the nine in those yards knew that and five had been told it in a lane. The nine people standing in those nine yards are four from the last made town and five from nowhere in particular, and one of the five has been on the page of this manuscript since the year the first thing happened in it.

Of the nine there four had met him before at a crossing or a table and five had not, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said that the nine people who stand at a boundary are always strangers to whoever arrives at it. Nobody checked who he was.

That is the second thing about that road and it took two hours to happen and four of the nine who stood there had wanted a name taken and five had said a name taken at that post is a knock with a book. A bench is at that boundary and it is nine feet long and it is in nobody's list of anything, and four of the nine who stood there had wanted to sit on it and five had stood, and they all stood for four hours.

Nobody asked him anything on the way up. That is the first thing that happened and it took about an hour and it is the thing four of the nine standing there had been waiting for and five had not. What a witness is, at that boundary, is a person who can be asked afterwards what the boundary looked like at a stated hour on a stated day.

That is the whole of it in nine words and of the nine there four had a copy of those nine words and five had not. At about the ninth hour he put his hand on the step at the boundary and nothing answered, and four of the nine who stood there had known that would happen and five had not known and nine hundred people in nine counties will hear that sentence repeated as though it were news.

Then a man of forty-nine who is not from there and is not from anywhere went to the post and did four things with it in nine minutes, and he did them in front of nine people because that is the whole of what a witness is for. He is forty-nine and he has stood at that boundary eleven times, and four of the nine in those yards have counted the eleven and five have not, and he has done the four things on nine of those eleven times.

On the two other times he did two of them, and of the nine standing there four had asked him about those two and five had understood that he will not answer. He came up that road four days before a man of sixty, who is seven years older than him, and that is the whole of what is known about the order the four of them are done in.

The four of them were settled by a man of thirty-nine who keeps a crossing two days from a customs house and who said, in a room with a table of nine feet in it, that a boundary is not a door and that a door is not a boundary, and of the eleven in that room four agreed and five went out. He did not knock.

That was the first thing and four of the nine who stood there had not thought of it as a thing that had been decided not to happen and five had. The first of those four took two minutes and nobody in those nine yards said anything while it was being done, and of the nine there four had expected the word please to be said and five had known it would not be.

The second took about a minute and four of the nine who stood there had wanted to hear what he would ask and five had already known the answer was nothing. Nobody in those nine yards had ever asked that boundary anything and four of the nine standing there had wanted to be the first and five had not wanted to at all. That is not a rule that anybody put in a book.

It is nine people standing four feet apart from a step, and nine hundred people in nine counties have said it is the only unwritten thing this manuscript ever put on a page. He did not ask. That was the second, and of the nine standing there four had wanted him to ask and five had understood before the ninth hour that asking is a knock with a longer name. He did not leave anything.

That was the third, and he had a paper in his coat for about a year and four of the nine who stood there had wanted him to leave it and five had known what leaving a paper is. And he did not write down where the road turned. That was the fourth, and the turning is four miles back and nine of the nine people in those nine yards have never been told where it is and four hundred people in nine counties have wanted to know.

Nobody sent anybody to find out. Four of the nine standing there had expected somebody to try and five had expected nobody to try. Then the fourth man of the four did the last thing that was done on that road, which was to read out the last line of what is written at that boundary, and he read it in nine words and the nine words are addressed to a child. The line is not addressed to him.

It is addressed to an infant, and it is four hundred years old or near it, and it says in nine words that whoever stands here is not asked and is not to ask and that this is not a wound and is not a refusal and is a thing that has been decided.

“**You are not asked here, and you are not to ask, and none of it is a wound.**”

That was eighteen words and every one of the nine in those yards heard all eighteen of them, and four of them had heard the line before and five were hearing it for the first time in four hundred years. The line is four hundred years old or near it, and four of the nine who stood there had read something that old before and five had only read contracts.

What it is written on is a board of nine inches by four, and the board is older than the post and the post is older than the bench, and of the nine standing there four had wanted to know who cut the board and five had not asked.

A chisel mark is on the underside of that board and it is not a letter, and four of the nine in those yards have said it is a mark made by somebody who cut nine boards for nine communities and five have said it is a nail hole that has been dressed. That is the whole of it and four of the nine in those yards heard it as a kindness and five heard it as a door. He read it twice.

The second reading was for himself and about four of the nine who stood there had known that it was and about five had not, and a man who reads a thing twice in front of nine people is a man who has been asked something he is not going to answer. Then he read it as an adult.

Nobody in those nine yards knew he was doing it, and about four of the nine standing there had their faces turned away at that moment and about five were watching him, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that the five were right to watch.

A child is a person who has not yet been asked about anything, and being spared an asking is not the same as being kept safe, and the difference between those two is nine words on a board four hundred years old. That is what he read it as, and about four of the nine in those yards would have read it as a wall and about five would have read it as a hand.

About four people in nine counties have said since that this is the only reading of that line anybody has ever had that the people it was written to would not have disagreed with, and about five have said it is the only reading anybody has ever had at all.

What that means, and nobody in those nine yards knew he was doing it, is that a child is a person who has not yet been asked about anything and that being spared an asking is not the same as being protected, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that four and a half years ago he would have read that line the other way.

About four of the nine standing there would have read it the other way and about five would not, and both of those readings are in that line and neither of them is wrong.

Then he went to the register, which is a board on the post with two ruled columns on it and a heading over each, and the heading over the left one is witness and the heading over the right one is keeper, and about four of the nine who stood there had noticed the two headings and about five had not. He wrote in the left one.

That is the physical of that Tuesday and it cannot be put back into the other column, and about nine of the nine in those yards watched him do it and about four of them had known which column he would use and about five had not.

He wrote his name and not his trade, and of the nine standing there about four had wanted his trade and about five had wanted his name, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a man who can be asked has to be findable. Then a woman of about thirty-six who keeps the records of four other places asked him one question, and it is the only question anybody asked him on that road in four hours, and it was not about the line.

She asked whether the four pages he put in a record in a court room eleven days ago would be copied into the other five rooms, and about four of the nine in those yards wanted the answer to be yes and about five wanted it to be no.

“**Tell me whether the four pages you gave the court are going into the other five rooms or whether they stay where you put them.**”

That was twenty-five words and about nine of the nine in those yards heard all twenty-five of them, and four of them had not known the question was about the four pages at all. He said that he had asked for one room and that one room had answered him, and about four of the nine standing there had wanted the words the room used and about five had not.

The words that room used are on a wall four days off a coast, and the one that matters at that boundary is the rule about copies, and about four of the nine who stood there had heard that rule once before in a different room and about five had never heard it at all. He did not say the other three and of the nine standing there about four had noticed that he did not say them and about five had noticed nothing.

That is the second time in eleven days that he has kept a thing in a room on purpose, and about four of the nine who stood there had worked that out and about five had not, and neither of those was said out loud.

Nobody in nine counties knows what the answer was except four people, and about four of them have said that it was nine words long and that it is not in this record and that a rule about copies is the answer and not a promise. The rule is on a wall in a room over a weigh-house four days off a coast.

It runs to four lines, and the line that matters here is that nothing may be copied out of a community without a holder named inside that community. That is what closed that boundary on that Tuesday and it had been printed on that wall for two years without anybody knowing it was about anything.

About four of those nine people had worked that out in about a minute and about five had not worked it out at all, and the nine of them stood there and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said that a rule written before it was needed is the whole argument for writing rules. Nobody sent anybody to the far side.

About four of the nine standing there had wanted to see what was written on the fourth of the nine and about five had wanted not to and both of them were right and neither of them is in this. The fourth of the nine stones is not on that road and about four of the nine in those yards have been asked about it and about five have not, and the man of about forty-nine has never given the ninth and will not.

What is at that boundary is a post and a step and a board and a bench and nine people, and of the nine standing there about four had wanted to write all five of those things into the record and about five had said that a record of a boundary is a record of a post.

He wrote the hour into the witness column and nothing else, and the hour is the ninth, and about four of the nine who stood there had wanted the date and about five had said the date is on the deed and on the post and is not needed twice. Then the day went.

It went at about the fourth hour in the afternoon and about four of the nine who stood there had wanted to stay until the light went and about five had wanted the light to go, which is the same wish held the other way up. The thing he had come up that road for was a contact, and he did not get one, and about four of the nine standing there had known he came for one and about five had known he came to stand there.

About four of the nine in those yards have said since that the day was painless and about five have said that it was the worst day any of them has watched anybody have, and about nine hundred people in nine counties have said those are the same statement made by two kinds of person.

He has wanted, for four and a half years, for one thing in this manuscript to open because he was standing near it, and about four of the nine in those yards knew that and about five did not, and neither of them said anything to him about it. Then he went back down.

Four hundred yards of made road, nine days of walking, and about four hundred people in nine counties have said the walk is the worst part and about five have said the walk is the only part that is his. He was down the four hundred yards by about the seventh hour and on the made road by about the ninth, and about four of the nine who stood there had stayed at that boundary after he left and about five had gone.

What they did after he left is not in this record and about four of the nine there have said it is not in any record and about five have said there was nothing to write. He had nine days of road and about nine hours of daylight and about four of the nine who stood there had thought about how he would get through that and about five had not.

The house he goes to is four days off a made road and it has two rooms with a floor in each and a door that shuts, and about four hundred people in nine counties know the deed and about five hundred know the town and not the house.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is a hand on a step that answered nothing, four things not done at a boundary in front of nine people, nine words about a child read twice and understood two ways, a name written in one column of a board and not in the other, a rule printed on a wall two years before anybody knew what it was for, and a man going back down four hundred yards with a tool bag and no road open at either end of the day. The engine at five hundred and twenty-six days. Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was sent anywhere.**
