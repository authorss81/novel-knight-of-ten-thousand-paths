# Chapter 0878

**Harvestmonth the first, YR 325, a Friday and not a market morning, and the thirty-third first of a month in a row with nothing standing at any of the boards in nine counties, a glasswright's canton, one bench about nine feet long with a furnace at the end of it, about nine people in the room, and a printer's forme standing on a trestle at the low end.**

A canton of glasswrights is a valley with four furnaces in it and nine hundred people and no market on a Friday, because the trade does not stop for a Friday and the market day is on a Tuesday.

The valley is four miles long and the sand is dug out of the floor of it and the furnace fuel comes up a made road from forty miles off in about four carts a week, and about nine of those carts did not come in the last week of Mudmonth and did not come again for nine days, and nobody in that valley has said out loud why they stopped or who told them to.

About four people in nine counties have said that a valley which supplies the glass and the sand and the fuel and has four furnaces in it is not a place that anybody should be asked a question about by a court above a market, and about four have said that it is the only place in nine counties where a person can be asked a question and be answered to the face.

The bench in that room is nine feet long and it is the bench the four ways are cut on and every person in that room has stood at it and about four of them have stood at it for more than nine years.

What was wanted on that Friday was the whole of the rules for making anchor-glass, printed, with the four ways a batch can be wrong printed inside the rules and not in a footnote.

The assessor had the four ways written down for about nine years. She wrote them down in the winter of the year the third canton took her on and she has never shown the list to a printer and about four people in that room have seen a copy and about five have not seen anything.

Two of the four are in her own canton. That is the whole of the difficulty and about four people in that room had said it out loud before she got there and about five had worked it out while she was setting her bag down.

One of the two is that a batch that has been at the annealing lehr for too long comes out of the fire perfectly and does not answer, and that nobody finds this out until a thing about nine feet across has been stood up for nine days.

The other is that the same fault can be put in by a person on purpose in nine minutes with no tools at all, and that nine hundred people have heard of it being done and about four of them have said it was done.

About four of the six people in that room had said those two sentences were the reason the rules could not be printed and about four had said they were the reason the rules had to be.

A woman of about forty-four who sets glass in that valley sat down at the bench about the fourth hour and said the thing that made the argument a real one.

She said that a rule printed in full is a rule that a person in another canton can be taken to, and that nine hundred people in this valley have been asked in the last nine years to make a thing that nobody has ever printed the rules for, and that they have all of them said yes, and that not one of them has been told what the two ways of being wrong were.

“**We have been asked nine hundred times to make a piece of glass and we have never once been told how it can be wrong, and every one of us found out how it can be wrong by being wrong at somebody, and I would like that printed before I am asked again.**”

That was fifty-three words and every person in that room heard the whole of them, and four of the nine people in it had heard something like it said before and about five had not, and about four of the nine have said since that the fifty-three words decided that Friday and that nobody in the room knew it while it was happening.

The two cantons that will not publish those two are not the assessor cantons. One of them buys every batch that leaves that valley and the other one supplies the sand, and four of the nine people in that room had worked out which of the two was which and about four had not.

The printer in that room is about thirty-four and has a press that is older than the bench and he is a man who will print anything he is handed and has printed two things he should not have and does not think about either.

What he said on that Friday was that he will print the four ways in full if four people in that room stand in front of him and say so, and that he does not want a name on any of it and that he will not put one there if a person asks him not to.

About four of the nine people in that room had said since that the printer is the only person in it who has not been overruled from a distance for nine years and about four have said that he has been and has simply never mentioned it.

The overrule is hers and she said so before anybody asked her.

She said that in the second year she wrote a fifth way down and sent it to her canton and that the canton wrote back in nine words and told her not to put it in front of a bench, and that she took it out and has never put it in front of a bench since, and that the four on the list are the four that survived being written down by a person she could not overrule.

About four of the nine people in that room had heard that before and about five had not, and four of the nine people in that room have said since that a person who cannot be trusted on her own bench is a person who should not be assessing anybody's glass.

Then she took out the list and put it on the bench and left her hand on it for about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice.

What the rules are is nine hundred lines and they were written by four cantons over four hundred years and four of the nine people in that room had read all of them and about five had read the first nine hundred lines and stopped, which is the whole difficulty with nine hundred lines about glass.

They are printed because a rule nobody can read is a rule nobody can be caught by, and about four people in that room have said that a canton which does not print its rules has nothing to point at when a batch comes out wrong, and about four have said it has the only thing it has, which is the maker's word.

What the list is, is four faults in four sentences, and three of them are the kind that happen and one of them is the kind that can be made.

The fourth one is the kind that can be made. She knows the fourth one and has never seen it done and has never been told where she got it, and she has said that twice in that room and about four of the nine people there believed her and about five did not.

About four of the nine people in that room have said that the fourth one is in the sixth county and about four have said it is in the fourth, and the assessor said neither and about four of the nine people there believed that too.

Then the letter came, and it came on the Thursday by the ordinary post, and it was four lines long and it was in a hand of about sixty-eight years and it said one thing.

It said that the fourth way is not hers and that it was given to her by a man who is dead and that he was given it by a man who is dead, and that if it is printed it will be printed without anybody's name on it or it will not be printed.

About four of the nine people in that room read that letter twice and about five read it once, and about four of them have said that two dead men is not a source and about four have said that it is the only source there will ever be.

Aren Kest read the letter out loud to the room because about four of the nine people in it could not read and about five could, and reading it out was his errand and not the assessor's.

Then the terms went as the four of the nine people who wanted them to go.

The rules are printed in full at nine hundred lines and the four ways are printed inside them at about the two hundredth, numbered, and two of the four carry an attribution to the canton that wrote them down first and four hundred years ago, and two of the four carry nothing at all.

About four of the nine people in that room have said that the two that carry a name about nine hundred years ago are the two nobody minded losing and about four have said that the two that carry nothing are the two that will still be here in about nine years and about four have said that all four of those observations are the same observation.

Then he put the four lines into the printer's forme himself.

He did it at about the seventh hour and he did it with his own hands because the printer's hands were in the case and nine people in that room watched a man who is not a printer set type, and he set it wrong twice and the printer fixed it twice and neither of them said anything about it.

He did not sign the sheet, and the printer did not sign it, and four of the nine people in that room had looked for a name at the foot of it and about five had not.

The forme was locked at about the seventh hour and that is the whole of his part in it.

He had been in four cantons in nine years and had been asked for the rules in three of them and had been refused in three of them by three different people for three different reasons, and about four people in that room have said that the reasons were all correct and about four have said that being correct three times in nine years is how a person ends up not asking anybody.

Then the four mistakes went on the bench, and that is the last thing that happened in that room on that Friday, and about four of the nine people there have described it since and about five have not.

There are no names on the four and that was arranged by four people independently and not by agreement.

The assessor did not sign hers because she has written down once that a thing could be wrong and been told by post not to put it in front of a bench, and she has said in that room that she is not going to hand a bench a second time anything of her own that she cannot stand behind.

The four makers did not sign theirs because four of the nine people in that room have said that a maker who signs a fault has to answer for the batch that carried it and that the batch that carried it went into a floor about nine hundred miles away in a year that the man who wrote to them about it is not alive to be asked.

The two cantons that objected did not sign because they did not want a name in a printed sheet, and the printer did not sign because a printer who signs is a printer who can be refused.

And the man with the stick did not sign because four of the nine people in that room have said that a man who has been in the middle of this for four and a half years does not get to put his name at the bottom of the only piece of paper anybody can pick up, and about four have said that he would have been asked to and that he would have said no and that this is the reason nobody asked him.

The four pieces of glass are about nine inches across and they were cut that morning by four different people in that room, and each one of them is wrong in one of the four ways and each one of them is wrong on purpose.

They were cut in nine minutes each and none of the four takes longer than about nine, and the furnace was not lit for any of them, and the whole of what was needed for the four of them is about nine feet of bench and about four hands and the letter of four lines that came on the Thursday.

The first one came out of the lehr perfectly and does not answer. It is clear and it is nine inches across and there is nothing wrong with it that a person could see.

The second one has a fault put in it by a fingernail against the cold side before it goes into the fire, and four of the nine people in that room had watched it be done and about five had not known that it could be done with a fingernail.

The third one is at the wrong heat and has a strain in it that will come out on a cold morning about nine years from now.

The fourth one is the fourth way and four of the nine people in that room said that they could not see anything wrong with it and about five said that that was the fault.

There is a name on none of the four. Not the assessor's and not the two cantons' and not the four makers' and not his.

Four of the nine people in that room have said since that a printed sheet with four faults on it and nobody's name on any of them is a sheet that nine hundred people can pick up and use, and about four have said that it is a sheet that nine hundred people can pick up and use, which is a different reason and a better one.

**And the cost of that Friday is one letter of four lines in a hand of sixty-eight years read aloud in a room where half the people in it could not read, nine hundred lines of rules printed at about the two hundredth with two of the four ways carrying a canton of four hundred years ago and two carrying nothing, one forme locked at the seventh hour by a man who is not a printer, and four pieces of glass about nine inches across broken on purpose with nobody's name on any of them. The thirty-third first of a month with nothing standing at any of the boards. The bleed six hundred and twenty-six days old and not cured and not explained and no character in that room has noticed it. Eight anchor fragments where there were ten. One ear, one leg, and a year, and not one of the three of them is the fourth way a batch of glass can be wrong.**
