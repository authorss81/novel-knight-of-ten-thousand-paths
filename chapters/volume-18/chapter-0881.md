# Chapter 0881

**Harvestmonth the twenty-eighth, YR 325, a Thursday and a market morning in a main-world town of about two thousand people four days off a made road, a public reading room over a tailor's shop with about nine benches in it and about forty people in it from the eighth hour, about four hundred in the street below, and the shortest interval the occupied timetable has allowed twenty hours.**

The reading room is let out on a Thursday for the sweep, and the sweep is nine pence, and the tailor who owns it takes it on nine words and does not argue about it.

About four hundred people go down that street on a Thursday morning and about nine of them go up.

A public reading room in a town of about two thousand people is nine benches, a lamp, a swept floor and about four people in it on a Tuesday and about nine on a Thursday.

There are about nine such rooms in nine counties and every one of them exists because about nine people asked for it and not because anybody built it. The tailor downstairs has swept that floor about nine hundred times and has never once been in the room while it was in use, and about four people in that town have noticed that and about five have not.

The man who came to it came for two days and had been in that room for one of them, and about four of the about forty people there had heard the whole of what he has to say twice and about five had heard it once.

He is fifty-four and he keeps a mill, four miles of made road between two counties, and a house at each end of the mill, and he has read forty papers and met none of the other three hundred and sixty.

That is the whole of him and four people in nine counties have said that a man who has read forty papers is the most dangerous kind of reader there is, and five have said it is the only kind anybody has.

Four miles of made road between two counties is the smoothest four miles anybody in that part of the country knows of, and the miller has said so out loud in about four rooms in about nine years. About four people in nine counties believe him and about five have said that a man who says his own road is the smoothest has an interest in the saying of it.

The mill has ground there since before anybody in that room was born and about four hundred people eat bread out of it every week and about four hundred more eat flour bought in from it by the quarter. The miller has never once been asked about either of the two things and about four people in nine counties have said that is the part of it that frightens them.

What he says is that the engine and the paper are one thing and not two, and that both of them were made by men who knew what they were doing, and that he has never once been asked about either.

Four of the forty people in that room had come to the room with him and five had come to hear him be answered, and four of the forty had worked out by the fourth hour that he was not going to be answered and five had not.

Then Aren Kest stood up and tried to answer him.

That is on the page because nine people in that room have said it was the last time in four and a half years that he tried to win a room, and four have said that he had tried to win rooms before and had never said so.

He had it worked out on about two days of a made road, and it was about nine lines long, and the ninth line said that the first of the two things had been made for one purpose and the second of them for another, and that a thing made for one purpose is not the same thing.

Four of the forty people in that room heard all nine lines and five heard six of them and waited for the other three.

Then the man of fifty-four asked him one question in his own trade, and nine people in that room heard the question and four heard the answer before it was finished being given.

He asked how anybody in that room was to know which purpose.

That is the whole of the resistance and it is as old as mills, and the answer was ready in the heads of four of the forty people there before he had got to the end of his own, and it was not ready in five, and nobody in that room said the word purpose twice except the two of them.

He did not say it twice.

What the argument is, in the miller's own words, is about nine lines long and it does not need any of the machinery to be said out loud. The first of the two things was built to answer a question that nobody alive now has the wording of. The second is being written by about nine hundred people who have never met each other and who agree, so far, that the answer to a question nobody can word is to go on keeping the question.

About nine of those nine hundred have said this year that they would rather have kept the question, and about four of them have said the first one should have been shut for that reason.

That is the better of the argument and it is not all of it, and four people in nine counties have said that about nine hundred people keeping a question is not a system and five have said that it is the only thing anybody has ever had.

For four and a half years he had wanted to be the person a thing is answered by, and what he wanted on that Thursday was narrower than that and cost him more, being to stop.

That is the change and it happened in about the time it takes to turn a chair round, and it was four of the forty in there who saw the whole of a thing being given up and five who saw a man sit down.

What he did instead of answering was to write it out.

Nine lines on a sheet of foolscap, in his own hand, in eleven minutes, and the nine lines are the argument and not an answer to the argument, and they are in the miller's words and not his own where they can be.

Then he read the nine lines out in a public room with forty people in it, and he read them twice, and the second time was slower and four of the forty people in that room heard a difference the first time and five heard the same nine lines.

That is what happened and it is not a thing anybody would have thought of, and four people in nine counties have said since that he gave up the only thing he has ever been good at in a room and four have said that giving it up is not the same as being asked to.

The miller did not say thank you and nobody in that room said anything for nine minutes, and then the miller asked him what the nine lines were for.

That is the question that changed the Thursday, and five of the forty people in that room did not have an answer of any kind and four of them had one written out before he stood up.

He said that they were for carrying.

Then he turned round in that room and put them into the hands of the one person in nine counties who was going to have to hold them.

The copyist is twenty-nine and he has been on the page of this book for a year without a name in it, and he copies what people in a county cannot pay a clerk to copy, and he has said in his own trade twice that the paper is theirs and not his.

He was in that room because four hundred people in nine counties cannot pay for a copy and nine of them can pay for one.

The copyist's trade is nine words long and he says it the same way every time. He said that a sheet is worth what can be got off it, and that four hundred people who cannot pay a clerk are the reason a county has a copyist at all, and that he has copied about nine hundred things for them in about four years.

He has never once charged for any of them. About four people in nine counties have offered him money and about nine hundred have not thought about it, and about nine people have said that he is the only man in nine counties anybody can hand four hundred things to.

About nine hundred copies of about nine lines is not work anybody has asked him to do and it is about nine weeks of his hands, and nobody has asked him whether he will do it, and the number in that room who had worked that out before he stood up was four and the number who had not was five.

Nobody asked him twice. That is the whole of what that room did with it, and about four of the forty people there had expected to be asked twice and about four had not.

He said he would want it in his own hand and not out of the room, and nine people in that room heard that and four of them heard the reason in it.

Then he tore the leaf out of the pad, and read the nine lines out loud for the second time to the whole of that room, and put the leaf into the copyist's hands and kept nothing.

That is what he did and it cannot be undone by anybody, and about four of that room had it at the time and the five who did not had it in the street.

He is the one person in nine counties that a thing goes to when there is nowhere else to put it, and four people in that room had said so a year ago and five had said it was the safest place in the county, and four had said it is not safe at all and it is only the one place that has never refused anybody.

The copyist put the leaf inside his coat and did not look at it again in that room, and the count of that room split the way it splits, being four who had him having it copied by Tuesday and five who had him keeping the original.

Then the miller got up and put his hand flat on the table and said the last thing that is said in that room, and it is not the argument and it is not the nine lines.

“**You have just written down the best version of the thing that is against you and handed it to a man who cannot read, and if that is the answer then I have wasted two days of a made road and I will say so in front of the forty people in this room rather than in a lane.**”

That was fifty-nine words and forty people in that room heard every one of them and not one of them wrote any of it down.

Four of them had understood the leaf before he said it and five had not, and four have said since that the miller is right about the man who cannot read and wrong about the answer, and that both of those are one act and not two.

It is not that the copyist cannot read. He can read, and he reads about nine hundred signs of a coast man's shorthand in about nine minutes now, and about four of that room knew that and about five did not.

What the miller meant is that a sheet in a coat is not a copy, and four people in nine counties have said since that the miller is right about that too.

A man in a coat is also the only person in nine counties who can be carrying four hundred different papers to four hundred different houses without anybody asking him what he is doing, and that is not nothing.

It is the answer to a question nobody in that room asked, and about four of the people in it noticed that it had been answered anyway and five did not notice anything.

Nothing was made in that room and nothing was changed in it, and about four hundred people in nine counties will read about nine lines in about four weeks in four hundred places they have never been asked about.

That is the whole of that Thursday and about four of that room have said that he came to win and left holding somebody else's argument, and five have said he came to win and lost nine minutes before he knew he was going to.

The engine has been damaged four hundred and forty-four days and nobody on that Thursday was mending it and nobody in that room said so.

About four of that room have said that the nine lines will be used against him inside four months and five have said inside nine, and four have said that the miller will be asked to say them out loud in a room again and that he will, because he has said so in this one.

Nobody at the top of that stair has heard any of it and the leaf in the copyist's coat is nine lines long and it is not a key and it does not open anything.

What he did with the second of his two days is on the page because nine people in nine counties have said it was the only thing he has done in four and a half years that anybody has ever said was a right use of a day. He sat with the miller for about nine hours and wrote down about nine hundred words of what the miller had said on the first day, in the miller's own words and not his own.

He answered none of it. He read the whole of it back once at the end of the ninth hour and did not change a line of it and did not sign it, and about four of the people who were in that room for the first time had come back on the Friday for it and about five had not come back at all.

That is the carrying and nobody in that room thought it was a trick, and four people in nine counties have said that since and five have said that nine times out of ten it is one.

About four of that room had wanted a fight in it on the Thursday morning and about five had wanted a victory, and about nine got about nine hundred words and a leaf in a coat.

About four of that room had wanted to ask whether the copyist would be paid and about five had not asked, and the copyist answered it himself in four words without being asked, which is his trade.

**And the cost of that Thursday is one argument written out in nine lines by the man it is about and handed to a person who cannot be asked whether he will carry it, one hour and eleven minutes of a public room let out for a sweep, forty people in it and four hundred in the street below, and one miller who came two days up a made road and will be asked to say it again in a room and says he will. The shortest interval the occupied timetable has allowed twenty hours. The engine damaged four hundred and forty-four days and nobody mending it. Nothing was made in that room and nothing was ratified in it and no road opened anywhere on it, and four hundred people in four hundred places will now hold the better half of an argument they were not in the room for.**
