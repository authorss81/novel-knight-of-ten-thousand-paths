# Chapter 0871

**Longlight the twenty-seventh, YR 325, a Saturday and a market morning in a main-world town of about nine thousand people, a room above a court at the low end of the one street of stone, nine chairs and a long table and about nine people at it, about four hundred people in the street below from the eighth hour, and the interval twelve hours.**

The procedure is nine lines long and it is the same nine lines in three provinces and not one of the three has ever had to write one out.

A place that refuses contact has broken contact. A refusal is entered as a breach. A breach is answered under the warrant. Those are the nine lines and about four of them are about entering it and about five are about answering it, and there is nothing in the nine lines about the place.

What a refusal costs on the ground is not in the nine lines either, and about nine people in nine counties could have said what it costs, and about four of them said it that morning and about five had said it before.

About four hundred people at the far end of four hundred yards of cut under a hill have not been told anything about anything else since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth. About four tons of coal a week is not coming up that road. A school closed and a court is out of reach, and about nine households between the bridge end and the kiln are burning what is in the houses, which is a bench and a door and a ladder.

About four people in nine counties have said that this is what a breach looks like from inside it. About five have said that this is what nine lines look like from outside it.

The woman who holds the mandate is about thirty-eight and she has carried it since the twenty-second of Longlight, and the seat that gave it to her has not sat for eleven weeks, and about nine people in nine counties have noticed that eleven weeks and nobody has said it out loud above that court except her and once.

She has nine written things on her and no soldiers. Four of the nine things are dated before the twenty-second and five are dated after it, and the five after it are the ones nobody will act on.

She has asked for a form twice. About four people in that room have heard her ask, and about five have heard her say that she has asked.

The first time she asked, a province told her it was a confession of weakness. The second time she asked, two of them told her together, and one of the two said the sentence that has been carried into four rooms since, being that a paper admitting a place may shut a road will be read for forty years by people who want a road shut.

She put that on the table herself on that Saturday, in her own words, before anybody else in that room had said it, and about nine people at that table heard all of it and about four of them had heard it somewhere else first.

What she wanted on that Saturday morning was one page.

Not a law and not a finding and not anything with a seal on it. A page a person could put a hand on and say no on, in a form that does not turn the no into a shut.

About four of the nine people in that room had wanted the same thing since the forty-ninth of Mudmonth and about five had come up those nine steps to be counted.

The room is on the first floor and the stair is nine steps that turn at a landing, and the boards of that floor were planed once and have not been planed since, and a long table in a room nine paces by five has four people at it comfortably and about nine at it the way a thing is done.

The court was built first and the market came to it, and about four people in that town have said a paper argued about above a market is a paper anybody who buys anything can argue with. About five have said it is a paper nobody who buys anything can argue with, because nobody who buys anything comes up nine steps.

Then Aren Kest stood up, which nobody at that table expected, and asked for the nine lines.

The nine lines were in a leather case and the case belonged to the man of about forty-four who is clerk to the third province, and he took them out and put them on the table and did not let go of them until the woman of about forty-four had copied all nine into her own book.

He read them out then. All nine, from the top, in his own trade, standing at the end of that long table, and he read them the way a man reads a list of things he has had to carry up a stair.

Nobody in that room stopped him and nobody asked him to read them a second time, and about four of the nine people at that table had the whole of the nine lines before he reached the sixth and the other five had the shape of it and got the rest out of the woman of about forty-four saying it out loud as she wrote.

What he said when he had finished the nine lines was not an argument. It was one sentence and it was addressed to the table.

“**Nine lines, and about four of them are about a piece of paper and about five are about sending somebody up a road, and the place that said no is not in any of the nine lines at all, and that is not because anybody forgot it.**”

That was forty-seven words, and every person at that table heard all of them, and four of the nine people in that room understood at once what the sentence was doing, and five understood it by the time the woman of thirty-eight had written it down.

The engine in that place has been damaged for three hundred and eighty-three days and nobody in nine counties is mending it and nobody in that room mentioned it once, and it is not in the nine lines either.

Four of the nine people in that room have said since that a man who reads nine lines of procedure out loud in a room full of the people who wrote them has done something no advocate of his has ever done. About five have said he read them because he wanted to see how long that room would let him read.

For four and a half years he had wanted a page with his own name off it, and what he wanted on that Saturday was narrower than that and harder, being a page on which a person could refuse him.

The woman of forty-four asked him one question in her own trade, and she asked it quietly, and nine people at that table heard it, because a room above a market has no other sound in it but the market.

She asked whether a refusal that can be written down is a refusal that can be found again.

He said it can, and that it can be found again by anybody who asks, and not only by the person it was written to.

She asked whether the person it was written to has to be told that it was written.

He said no, and four of the nine people at that table sat back and five sat forward, and neither of those was a thing she had asked about.

A man of about sixty who keeps a counting house at the top of that street had counted about nine hundred tons of something a year for about forty years and had not said anything until then.

What he said was that a form with a hole in it is worth more than a form with a name in it, and that he had been counting other people's columns for nine years and the ones that had cost his county the most money were the ones that had somebody's name in them.

The wrongness was the useful part. Four of the nine people at that table had heard him make that argument before and five had not, and the man of sixty said afterwards that he had made it in eighteen words on a Tuesday four years back to four people and had been wrong about it ever since, and that the column that had cost his county its money was the column with a name in it and not the column with a hole in it.

Then she ruled nothing and wrote the page herself, and it took her about nine minutes, and the page has four things on it and not one of them is a name.

The first is the place. The second is the road. The third is the hour. The fourth is a line saying that whoever proposes that this road ought to be shut has nine days to show cause why it ought to be, and the cause has to be shown to the place and to nobody else.

About four of the nine people in that room had understood what those four things did by the third and about five had to be shown the fourth one twice.

That fourth line is the whole of what that Saturday found, and nobody in that room found it, and it was the only one of the four things that the three provinces had not asked for.

What the three provinces had asked for was a form they could hold. What they got was a form that can be held against them, and about four people in that room said the difference between those two things is the whole of this year and about five said it is one line.

Four of the nine people in that room have said since that the woman of thirty-eight could have had that fourth line written by anybody in nine counties in four days, and that she wrote it herself in nine minutes in a room above a market, and that the reason is that a woman with a mandate and no soldier has one use for a piece of paper and it is the only use that does not need a soldier.

About five have said she wrote it because she had nobody else to give it to.

The fourth line did one thing and the other three did another, and nobody in that room had asked for the split.

The first three are what a place writes down when it says no. The fourth is what happens to whoever wants to write something against it. About four people in that room understood the split inside a minute and the other five understood it when the counting-house man said the thing about the columns.

The clerk to the third province said out loud that the fourth line was not a procedure, it was an invitation to anybody with a grievance and a cart to write one down, and the woman of thirty-eight said that it was, and that the three provinces had spent nine years writing down grievances with carts.

Then the board of that court was asked to sign, and it did not sign.

The board is four people, of whom two come up into that room, and the business of a Saturday is nine sheets with a wax and a seal the court owns, and four of the nine sheets on that Saturday were copies of the page of four things and five were a dispute about a sum of money that two litigants had brought up between them with their own clerk.

None of the four was signed. Four people in that town have said a board that signs nothing has decided nothing, and five have said a board that signs a form nobody asked for has decided something it cannot take back.

The reason none of it was signed is one sentence long, and the woman of about thirty-eight said it, and it is in the record of that court and it is in no document this arrangement keeps.

“**I will not put this court's seal on a page whose only signature is somebody else's, and if I do, then in forty years a reader will take it for a thing this court decided and it will not be a thing this court decided at all.**”

That was forty-seven words, and not one of the nine people at that table missed any of them, and four of them were still holding it when she put her pen down.

Then he took the single copy down those nine steps himself, which took him about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over on a bad leg, and he gave it into the hand of a man of about fifty who had come up that morning about something else altogether and who was going that road on the Friday.

He said nothing about what the page was for, and the man did not ask, and neither of them said the name of any place out loud on the bottom step.

About four of the people in that street at the ninth hour saw a man hand a sheet to another man, and about five of them were buying salt and did not.

The man of about fifty is a lime burner and he goes that road on the Friday with four empty crates and he has gone on Fridays for about eleven years, and a house of his own family is at the far end of it, and he has had no news since the Wednesday, and he came up those nine steps on the Saturday morning to ask a court for a thing the court had already decided it could not do.

He did not know what was on the sheet. He put it inside his coat between the fourth crate and the fifth and he went down the nine steps and across four hundred yards of that street and out of it, and about nine people in that town saw a lime burner leave a market street on a Saturday afternoon with the wrong thing in his coat.

What he does with it on the Friday is not known to anybody in that town and it is in no document, and the woman of thirty-eight wrote nothing down about him, and four people in that room have said she was right and five have said she should have written the name down.

The community at the far end of that road had not asked for it. It had not been asked, and nobody in nine counties had thought to ask it, and about four people in that street have said that a thing nobody asked for is the only kind there is left at this point and about five have said it is the reason nine counties have got nine different papers on the same subject.

He went back up to that room afterwards to fetch his own hat and did not go in, and stood in the nine steps for about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice, and the four people still at that table said nothing to him about it through the open door.

He had come up that morning with one errand and it had become four sheets and a speech and a hand-over, and none of the three was his.

He had wanted a page with his own name off it for four and a half years. What he had got was a page with nobody's name on it at all, and it was better than what he had wanted, and he could not think what to do with that inside a stair.

Four of the people in that room above that court went down those nine steps after the market began and five stayed, and the room was shut at the eleventh hour with four sheets of copy on the table that nobody had signed and no seal in the wax.

**And the cost of that Saturday is nine lines of procedure read aloud in a room where about nine people had spent nine weeks agreeing they did not need reading to, four sheets of a page nobody signed and one sheet handed down nine steps to a man going that road on the Friday for his own reasons. One line about nine days to show cause, written in nine minutes by a woman of about thirty-eight who holds a mandate from a seat that has not sat for eleven weeks and who has nine written things on her and no soldiers. Four hundred yards of cut under a hill and about four tons of coal a week that is not coming up that road, and none of it in the nine lines. The interval twelve hours. The engine damaged three hundred and eighty-three days. Eight anchor fragments where there were ten. One ear, one leg, and a year, and none of the three is in the fourth column of anything.**
