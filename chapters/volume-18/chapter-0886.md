# Chapter 0886

**Fallowmonth the twenty-first, YR 325, a Saturday and a market morning, a room at the back of a long house in a main-world town of about six thousand people, a hearth in it that had been lit at the fifth hour, about nine people at a table in front of it, and four hundred people in a street that shut at the eleventh hour.**

Two delegations came into that town on that Saturday morning and one of them came in for the purpose of being last.

The Hearth Marches are two days off a made road and the Marches refused outside command in about YR 291 and have been refusing it in four languages ever since, and nine people in nine counties have said that a place which has refused for a hundred years is not a place anybody should hand an argument to.

That is the resistance and it took nine minutes to say out loud and four of the nine people in that room had heard it before and about five had not.

The woman who came from the Marches is sixty-three and she has not left that county in about nineteen years and two roads in that time have been made passable and she came down one of them.

The Marches sent two people and one of them was the woman of sixty-three and the other one was her granddaughter, and about five of the nine people in that room had expected nine of them and four had not.

The refusal of outside command in the Marches is a hundred and thirty-four years old and it has four versions of its wording in four counties and four of the nine people in that room had wanted one version and about five had said that four versions is the point.

What was refused in YR 291 was a garrison, and what has been refused since is about nine things, and four of the nine people in that room had the list of the nine and about five had not asked for it.

The Marches have not refused this paper. They have refused to be in it until it says what the fourth item says, and four of the nine people in that room had worked that distinction out before the woman sat down and about five had not worked it out until the fifth item.

Her name is Alys Kerman and she said it out loud on the Thursday and did not say it again.

She has a list and the list is eleven items long and it is not a list of grievances and it is longer than anybody in that room expected it to be.

That is the finding of the Saturday and four of the nine people in that room had wanted four items and about five had wanted eleven, and about four of the nine have said since that four items would have gone through faster and about five have said four items would have gone through in nine days.

The rest of the eleven are about a bridge at a mill that four families have repaired since before anybody in that room was born, and about a naming of a road that has been wrong in every map made since YR 298 and is wrong in this one on purpose.

They are also about a right to be asked before anything is done to a road and not to be told afterwards, and about two hundred people who live on a road that appears on no paper of any kind and who have asked for nothing and have been given nothing.

That last one is the tenth item and nobody in that room had known there were about two hundred people on a road of their own asking for nothing.

About four of the nine people in that room had wanted that tenth item left out because it costs somebody a day and about five had said that an item which costs somebody a day is the item people will remember.

The list itself is four sheets of foolscap in a hand of sixty-three years and there are about nine people in nine counties who have seen a copy of it and about four of them have read it.

It is not grievances and it is not a case and it is not a threat. Every item on it is a thing somebody has to do, and two of the eleven are things the Marches have been doing since before anybody in that room was born, and four of the nine people in that room had wanted at least one sentence of anger on it and about five had said that a list with a sentence of anger on it is a list about the past.

What the eleven items are is not nine lines and it is not a speech.

She said one more thing about the tenth item before she read it and she said it to nobody in particular and nine people in that room heard it.

She said that a road with nobody's name on it is a road somebody will one day be asked about, and that she has said for fifty years that a road with nobody's name on it is a road that is safe, and that she is sixty-three and has stopped believing it.

About four of the nine people in that room understood that and about five heard it as a thing about a road, and both of them are correct and about nine people in nine counties have said the same two sentences about four different things this year.

Then she read the tenth item and did not look up.

They are a seed vault at a town nine days off that is not in anybody's paper and nine hundred people eat out of it in a bad year. They are four water anchors at the same town, and two of the four are older than the road that serves them. They are a rule that no vault is opened by anybody outside the four families that hold its key, and that rule has never once been written down anywhere at all.

They are the eleven items and four of the nine people in that room had said in advance that there would be eleven and about five had said four.

The fourth item is the one that stopped that Saturday and four of the nine people in that room had not seen it and about five had not read it.

What it says is that the Marches will be named in the instrument before anything else is named in it, and that the seed vaults and the water anchors will be in it in the Marches' own words, and that the Marches will not confirm anything until they are.

That is the whole of the fourth item and it is nine lines long and four of the nine people in that room had wanted it in the instrument's own words instead.

The second delegation is the Orchard and its delegate is a woman of about forty-nine who has buried a memory and knows exactly what she paid for it.

What she had asked for on the Thursday was one right and it is now written into the text in the Orchard's own hand, being that the Orchard reserves the right to refuse contact with any path, at any hour, without notice, and without being asked why.

That line is in it word for word and four of the nine people in that room had wanted a shorter one and about five had said that a shorter one is the same line with the teeth out.

About nine hundred people in a county about nine days off gave up something in about YR 295 to stop a wound growing and they have never told anybody what it was and they are not going to.

That is why the right is explicit and not implied and four of the nine people in that room have said that a right which is implied is a right which can be argued about and about five have said it is a right nobody would be able to prove anybody had.

Nobody at that hearth knew what the Orchard gave up and nobody asked, and four of the nine people in that room had wanted to and about five had not.

Then the eleven items were read in and that is what the Saturday morning was for.

Alys Kerman read all eleven of them out loud in that room in about eleven minutes and did not explain one of them and did not argue for one of them, and nine people in that room heard every item and about four of them had the whole of the list in their heads before she came to the end.

The bleed is six hundred and seventy-six days old and is not cured and is not explained and no character at that hearth has noticed it.

Then she prepared for it in public and four of the nine people in that room had not known she was going to and about five had.

She told them what she was about to do nine minutes before she did it. She said that she was going to use the word and that she had used it twice in her life and both times had cost her something she has never named to anybody, and that she was not going to name it this time either.

About four of the nine people in that room heard that and about five heard a woman describing a formality.

She said it the first time nine words into her answer and she said it the second time nine minutes later, and both times she was in a room with about nine people in it and both times she looked at the person she was answering.

That is the ending of that Saturday and four of the nine people in that room had counted the word twice and about five had not noticed either one.

“**We will not confirm anything, and I will tell you the reason in the plainest words there is, and the reason is that a thing given up once on the word of a person has never in the history of the world been got back by anybody asking for it.**”

That was fifty words and nine people at that hearth heard all fifty of them, and four of them had the whole of it before she came to the end and five did not.

Then she said the second word, out loud, at the ninth hour, in the same room, to the same nine people, and about nine people heard it and nine hundred people in two counties did not.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that she was right both times and about five have said that being right twice in one morning is not a kind of argument and both of those are correct.

What the woman of thirty-four said at that hearth was that the fourth item was not a demand and was a threat, and that a Marches which has to be named before anything else will be a Marches which has to be named before anything else in nine years' time and not only in this one.

Not one person in that room said she had it wrong, and four of them wanted to and the five did not, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that she was right about the shape of it and about five have said that the shape is the price.

The Hearth Marches ratified last and on their own conditions, which were the eleven items and the fourth one, and they ratified at about the ninth hour on a Saturday with about nine people in a room and a list of eleven read out loud twice.

What he did in that room is on the page because nine people in that town have said that it was the only thing he did in four and a half years that anybody has been able to check.

He copied the eleven items into the paper in that room in the words they had been said in, and he wrote them at the end of a long table with the hearth on his left and four of the nine people in that room reading over his shoulder as he wrote each one, and about five had gone out to the front door and looked at the street.

He read all eleven of them back at the eighth hour in the order she had said them and did not add a word to any of them and did not change a word of any of them, and then he put the pen down on top of her list and did not pick it up again in that room.

About four of the nine people in that room had wanted him to summarise and about five had said the summary was what had got four hundred copies into nine counties disagreeing, and about nine people in nine counties have said that the reason nobody can now say what four hundred copies say is that nine hundred people read summaries.

Nothing was created by that and four of the nine people in that room said so out loud and about five had not needed to.

Nine hundred places on a list five weeks old cannot be told anything by a hearth in a town six thousand people off, and four of the nine people in that room had wanted a sheet of paper to leave that room with and about five had said that a paper is what got four hundred copies into nine counties disagreeing with each other.

The woman of about thirty-four who came down a coast road with nine bundles and two children said that nine items was nine too many and eleven was a number somebody chose for a reason, and four of the nine people in that room agreed with her and about five did not.

That is a number in this manuscript and it is not a page and it is not a coda and it is in a room in a town of about six thousand people on a Saturday in Fallowmonth.

The Marches confirmed nothing at that hearth and they confirmed nothing on the Monday, and the eight lines went into a paper with nothing in them about a furnace, a tide or a bell.

There are about nine hundred crossings on a list finished five weeks ago and four of the nine hundred lines on it are in counties nobody in that room could name, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the Marches are the only delegation anybody sent a list to and about five have said the Marches are the only delegation anybody would.

Nobody waited for the Marches and nothing waited for them either, and about nine hundred people along nine miles of made road went on not knowing that two words had been said twice in a room six thousand people off.

**And the cost of that Saturday is one woman of sixty-three coming down a road made passable in the last two, an eleven-item list read out loud twice in a room with a hearth in it, one right written into a text in the Orchard's own hand about something nobody in that room knew, two words said out loud by a person who had said them twice before in her life and had prepared for it nine minutes beforehand in public, a Marches that confirmed nothing until it was named, a second delegation that came into that town for the purpose of being last, and about nine hundred crossings on a list that learned nothing whatever about any of it. The bleed six hundred and seventy-six days and uncured and unremarked. Nothing was ratified for anybody else and nothing in that room created anything.**
