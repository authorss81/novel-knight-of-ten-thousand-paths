# Chapter 0872

**Rainmonth the first, YR 325, a Wednesday and not a market morning, and the thirty-second first of a month in a row to arrive at those boards with nothing standing at it, a crossing house on a made road four miles short of a toll bar where a bar came up about nine inches and stopped twice, a fire in the room and about nine people in it, and the second of two places to choose anybody.**

The crossing house is a building of stone and lime with a fire in one end of it and a bench along the other, and it belongs to nobody and is opened by whoever is there, and it has stood at that bend since before the toll bar was two miles nearer.

The second place is four hundred people at the far end of nine miles of that made road and it has one road and it is not made and it goes down to a ford.

The ford is three feet deep in the summer and in the second week of the thaw it is nine, and the cart goes across it at the hour the water is lowest and has done for nineteen years, and in that week it does not go across at all and the village knows it a day before anybody says so.


They were asked on the first of Longlight and again on the fourth. About nine of them came to the first meeting and nine came to the second, and nobody has said since which of the two was the meeting, and about four of the nine people in that crossing house think the second one was the meeting and about five think the first one was.

What came back to the town four days off was a sheet of foolscap with a name on it and a second sheet with eleven lines in it, and about four of the nine people in that room have read the eleven lines and five have read only the first three.

The name on the top sheet is a man of about forty-four who runs a weekly cart between that ford and this toll bar and has run it for about nineteen years.

Nine years ago he signed a return about a count of about nine hundred crossings and did not read it, and the count was wrong by about nine, and about nine people lost a season's toll over it, and his name came off the county's list of men who may witness a count of anything.

The list he came off is not a list anybody keeps now. About four of the nine people in that crossing house have never seen it and five have seen it once and it is a list of about nine names on a sheet about nine inches by six.

The first place's form asks for nine years standing in the keeping of a road.

He has nineteen years of a cart and none of standing in anything, and about four of the nine people in that room had seen his name on the top sheet before that Saturday and about five had not.

He came in at the eighth hour with a cart-whip he did not need and stood by the fire and did not sit down until he was asked twice.

He hauls lime out of that valley and salt up it, and he hauls four crates empty on the way back whether they are empty or not, and the four empty crates are the reason people at this end of the made road know his face.


What the second place had sent with the name was the eleven lines, and the eleven lines are the reason the two places are now arguing in public about a thing that is nine counties from anybody's front door.

The first line says that a keeper chosen by a place is a keeper. The second says that the place which chose him may replace him at any hour without asking anybody. The third says that a keeper has never been anybody's servant and never will be.

Nine people have read those three lines and about four of the nine people in that room have said the first three are the whole of the arrangement and about five have said the first three are the whole of a quarrel.

The seventh line is different. The seventh line asks the other place to say in writing whether it accepts a keeper it did not choose.

The seventh line was the only one of the eleven anybody argued over before the eleven left that village, and it was argued over for nine days, and it went in with four words changed, and the four changed words are what have cost the two places nine weeks.


That is what the two places are arguing about and it is not about the man of forty-four at all, and he has not been asked about it and about four of the nine people in that crossing house know that and about five have not noticed.

The letter from the first place came up the made road on the Monday and it is four lines long and it is signed by eleven people and it is the first thing anybody has written for that arrangement which anybody could check against a name.

It says that the eleven lines are not the choosing of a keeper, and that a man who was struck off a list nine years ago cannot be asked to answer for a road, and that the first place will not accept any answer given to him.

Nobody in that room drafted it and about four of the nine people there had expected the delegation's woman to draft it and about five had not known she was asked.

Then a woman of about nineteen read both pieces out loud.

She is not a clerk and she is not a delegate and she reads aloud twice a week in that room for the four people who cannot read, and about nine people in that crossing house heard the whole of both and about four of them could tell afterwards which of the two they had heard first.

There is no other reader in that crossing house and has not been one since the school at the bridge end shut its doors. About nine people in nine counties have said that a rule which cannot be read is not refused and is not obeyed and is simply not there.

She has read out four returns, two notices and one set of eleven lines in that room since the first of Longlight, and she is nineteen, and nobody has ever told her that what she does in that room is part of anything.

She read the eleven lines first and then the four, and she read the four slower, and about four of the nine people at that table understood the four lines and about five understood the eleven and were still working on the eleven when she had finished both.

Nobody in that room could answer the seventh line, and four of the nine people there had a view and five had a better one and none of the nine would put either of them on the table.

About four people in that crossing house have said since that a seventh line nobody can answer is not a fault in the line and is the condition the line was written for, and about five have said it is a fault in the eleven and that the eleven were written by people who had not been to a ford in the second week of the thaw.

The form from the first place was not in that room and nobody had brought it. The man of forty-four was not asked to fill anything in, and the whole of the errand took about as long as it takes to read a sheet twice.

The two pieces of paper went into the same fire at the end and were read out again from the copies, and four of the nine people in that room have said since that whoever hears both pieces read in one morning will be asked to pick one, and five have said nobody has picked one and that the reading has been the argument.

Aren Kest said one thing in that room and he said it before anybody asked him anything.
and he said it standing up with his stick in his right hand and his right ear is the ear the stick is on and about nine people at that table heard all of it.

“**I am not the one who chose either of you and I am not going to be the one who says that one of you is right, and I have about nine of these left to give away and about nine hundred crossings that nobody has stood at this year, and the only part of this I am entitled to is the putting of it down.**”

That was sixty-six words, and the two men at the back of that room got the whole of it and the four by the fire did not get past the first sentence.

About four of the nine people in that crossing house have said since that a man who came nine miles on a Wednesday morning to hand a piece of iron over and not to be asked about it is a thing this arrangement has no word for. About five have said there is a word for it and that the word is witness and that he has not asked to be one.

Then he took the ring out.

It is a ring of iron about four inches across and a quarter of an inch thick with a tongue on it, and the tongue answers a question put to it by a thing in a wall nine hundred miles away at about the fourth hour and about the tenth hour and at no other time.

The other one went on a table at the far end of nine days of made road and was looked at by eleven people before it was filled. This one did not go on a table anywhere.

He did not put it on the table. That is the difference between this and the other one and about four of the nine people in that room saw the difference and about five did not until it was in the man's hand.

He put it into the man's hand and closed the man's fingers on it and let go, and he did not say anything at the moment he did it, and about nine people in that crossing house watched a man hand a thing to a man who had come in with a cart-whip he did not need.

What he handed over asks two questions a day and answers them without him. At the fourth hour and at the tenth hour a wall nine hundred miles off puts the question and the tongue in that ring says yes or no, and the man does not have to be awake and does not have to be there, and four of the nine people in that crossing house had not known that until it was explained and five had known it since the first of Longlight.

About four of the nine people in that room have said he was right not to say anything at that moment and about five have said a man should say something when he puts iron into a stranger's hand, and neither of those was in the room at the time.

What he said afterwards was not to the man. He said it to the four lines on the table and to the eleven, and about nine people at that table heard it and the two men at the back heard it better than the four by the fire did.

He had read the fourth column off a page in his own coat that morning and had put the page back where it was, and four of the nine people in that room knew he had it and five did not know until he said so.

He said that the fourth column of a paper four days off was ruled and empty this morning and still is.
and that a place which has chosen somebody does not need a column and a place which has not chosen anybody is not going to get one by asking a court.

About four of the nine people in that crossing house understood the whole of that before he had finished it and the other five got it out of the second clause.

Then the man of forty-four said the only thing he said all that morning, and he said it to the room and not to the man with the stick.

He asked whether anybody in that village could ask him a question tomorrow, and about four of the nine people in that room understood what he had asked and about five understood that he had asked it at all.

What he was answered was that any person in that village could put any question to him about that road at any hour, and that if he did not like the answer he could put it to about nine other people, and that he could be replaced by that place without asking anybody anywhere.

He said that he had run a cart into that ford for nineteen years and had never once been asked anything by anybody, and that he would take the thing and he would rather not be asked about it for nine days, and that after nine days he would answer anything.

About four of the nine people in that room said that was the whole of the difficulty, and the other five said that nine days is nine days, and about nine people in that room had a different number in their heads and not one of them said it out loud.

What he carried down that made road on the Sunday had been four things and three of them were somebody else's, and the fourth was a ring, and he had not written one line about the morning and had not been asked to.

Then he left that room before the fire was out and he went out through the door onto the made road and he did not stay to be told what anybody had said about him afterwards, and about four people in that crossing house have said he left at the right moment and about five have said he left before the arguing started, which was the whole of what he came for.

The two places began to argue in public on the Monday and they have argued every third day since, and about nine people in nine counties have read one piece or the other of it and about four hundred have read nothing.

The argument is about the seventh line and it is being conducted in two letters at a time with a third letter from the town four days off in the middle, and nobody in it has mentioned the man of forty-four again and about four of the nine people in that crossing house have noticed that and about five have not.

The boards in the market street of that town stood empty on that Wednesday morning for the thirty-second time in a row, and about four people in that town looked at them and about five did not, and the roll is on that frame with nine lines on it and nine empty and the fourth line empty on all three copies, and it is not filled and it is not taken down.

**And the cost of that Wednesday is nine miles of made road twice over in a week, one ring of iron put into a hand that had been struck off a list nine years ago and that nobody argued about, one form not filled in and carried back down the road in an inside pocket, two places about nine counties apart arguing in public about a line neither of them wrote, and a man of thirty-two who came to hand over one piece of iron and left the room before he could be told about it. The thirty-second first of a month at those boards with nothing standing at them. Eight anchor fragments where there were ten, and one of the eight about two inches out of a ring in a cart driver's fist. One narrow path anchor out of use for good and a community of about two hundred people behind a four hundred yard cut with no contact. One ear, one leg, and a year, and none of the three of them is in the seventh line.**
