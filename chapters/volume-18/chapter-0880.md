# Chapter 0880

**Harvestmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 325, a Monday and not a market morning, that town of about two thousand people four days off the coast, thirteen stairs up from a lane to the room over the weigh-house with a press standing open with no date on it and about nine people in that room, and a list of about nine hundred crossings on a trestle in the middle of it with the gaps printed as gaps.**

The list was begun on the fourth of Longlight by four people in this room and it took nine weeks and it is in four hands and a fifth, and none of the five is him.

He wrote nine hundred of the first column out of four books that are not this town, and a carter of twenty-two wrote nine hundred of the second column out of nine returns that came up the coast road in the ordinary post, and a woman of thirty-four wrote nine hundred of the third column out of nothing at all, on her own judgement, in the evenings.

Four of the five writers have said that the third column is the only one of the three that anybody will argue with and that the second column is the only one anybody will trust.

The list is finished enough to be argued with and that is the whole of what a list is for.

About nine hundred crossings are on it and about eight hundred and ninety-nine of them have a name in the second column, and the nine hundredth does not, and that is printed as a gap and not as a dash.

It is not the roll. About four of the nine people in that room had said that on the first morning and about five had not, and the difference is four hundred yards of hill and nine counties of made road and a frame on a different board in a different town.

There is no name against any line of the roll in that other town and there is not going to be one this year, and about four people in nine counties have said that the two lists are not the same object and about four have said that they had better not be.

What is in the second column is the name of a person that a place has chosen, and about four of the nine hundred of them are in this room and about five hundred are not.

A person who keeps a road on a community's choice is what the arrangement calls a custodian, and the word is in the list under a head that was ruled for it, and four of the nine people in that room have said that it is the only kind of keeper there is and about five have said it is the only kind anybody has ever been afraid of.

The first column is a place and nine of the lines in it have a word in them instead of a place, and every one of those nine is a place where a road is called something else by the people who stand on it.

The third column is what each crossing may still be asked to do and there are ticks against nine hundred of the lines and the ticks are not his.

The second sheet is the one of eight and it is the whole of what is left of the paper that took a piece of him out of a thing in a wall.

Eight anchor fragments where there were ten. Two confirmations withdrawn on the forty-ninth of Mudmonth by two people who came up nine hundred yards together and went down apart, and neither of them could be handed back by anybody with a sheet.

One narrow path anchor out of use for good, four hundred yards of cut under a hill, about two hundred people at the far end of it, a lamp out since that Wednesday, about four tons of coal a week not coming up that road.

And a join about two inches long between the third fragment and the sixth, and a seventh sheet that nobody may write, and it is on the second sheet as a gap too.

About four of the nine people in that room had wanted the join written out in full as two sentences side by side and about five had not, and nobody wrote either of the two sentences and about four of the nine people there have said that not writing them is the only reason the paper can be carried at all.

Aren Kest read out the eight lines that are not ordinary.

That is what he did on the Monday morning and nine people in that room heard all eight and about four of them had heard them before and about five had not.

They are the two withdrawn confirmations, the compensating closure, the lamp, the coal, the school that closed, the court out of reach, and the ninth crossing, which is four miles up a made road from a toll bar where a bar came up about nine inches on the fourth of Longlight and stopped twice with no explanation offered to anybody.

About four of the nine people in that room heard nine lines and about five heard eight and about one of the five has said since that there were eight and that the ninth is the list itself.

Then the argument started, and it is the whole point of the list, and four of the nine people in that room had argued with that list since the fourth of Longlight and about five had not seen it before that morning.

A man of about thirty-one who keeps a toll on a ferry stood up and said that his crossing is on the list and that the second column has his name on it and that he did not choose himself, and that his place chose him nine years ago in a room with nine people in it and that he has never been asked.

Four of the nine people in that room had not known that and four had, and the woman of thirty-eight who holds a mandate asked him one question about it in her own trade and nine people at that table heard it.

She asked who in that room chose him, and he said about nine people in a room nine days off, and she asked whether any of them is on the list, and he said that none of them is and that he does not know their names.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that a keeper a place chose nine years ago in a room about nine days off is a person who has been a keeper for nine years and did not know it.

Four of the nine people in that room have said that this is the same argument as the seventh line in the two places' letters and about four have said it is not the same argument and did not say what it was, and about four have said that a person who keeps a road for nine years without being asked is the strongest argument anybody in nine counties has made for giving the whole thing up and against giving the whole thing up at the same time.

Then the woman of thirty-four stood up and said the thing nobody in that room had said.

“**I have been in this town since the tenth of Longlight and in nine weeks I have found nine hundred crossings on a piece of paper and nine of them I can go and stand at, and the other eight hundred and ninety-one are not mine, and they are nobody's who is in this room, and I will not read a list out that pretends the nine are the county.**”

That was seventy words and nobody in that room missed a single one of them, and four of the nine people there had the whole of it before she came to the end and about five had not known what she was refusing.

The nine of them are the lantern run, the toll bar, the crossing nine miles off a made road, the gate in a town of nine hundred people, the cut that is shut, the boat, the ford, the ferry, and a door in a coast town.

She said she had found them by asking four questions and that three of the four questions were the same question.

He wrote that second column into the ninth place on the list as a tick and not a name, and four of the nine people in that room had understood what he did at the time and about five had not, and about four of them have said since that a tick in the keeper's column is not a keeper and about four have said that it is a keeper with his name left off and that those are different faults.

About four of the nine people in that room had said since that the paper is the map and about four have said that the map is not the county and about four have said that a person can live in a county for nine weeks and not know that it is four hundred yards wide in four places and nine miles in nine others.

The list is published with the gaps printed as gaps.

That is the change and four of the nine people in that room had said that a list with the holes printed in it is a list nobody can be sent round with, and about five had said it is the only kind anybody can be sent round with.

She would not let it be printed in that room and about four of the nine people in it had expected that and about five had not.

What she said was that the press in that corner has stood open with no date on it for about two years and that the first thing that comes out of it will be the first thing anybody in that town can hold in their hand that has been made in that room, and that a list of nine hundred crossings is not a thing anybody should find out about by opening a door.

About four of the nine people in that room have said since that she was right about the press and that she was right about the door and about four have said that she was protecting a press and not a list.

Four hundred copies went out of that town on the ordinary post in the week of the twenty-one, and nine hundred people along nine miles of made road can buy one at four prices and nine thousand cannot read one.

He had wanted for nine weeks for somebody else to hold that sheet and four of the five people who have seen it have refused it and about four have said that they would hold it if anybody ever wanted to check anything against it and not before.

Then he put the sheet of nine inches by six on that trestle at about the eleventh hour.

Three columns and about nine lines, one of them in his own hand from the twenty-fifth of Longlight, with no second copy and no carbon, and it went down in front of nine people who had not been in the room over the saddler's on that Thursday.

That is the third thing he put on a table in that town in nine weeks and four of the nine people in that room had understood it at once and about five had to be told what it was, and the woman who keeps that room said that it was the first of the three that was a list of his own and not a document.

Then he read the nine hundredth line out loud.

Four miles up a made road from the toll bar. Nobody has stood at that crossing this year and about nine people along that four miles of made road have not asked anybody to and about four of them have said that they have somebody else's.

About four of the nine people in that room have said that a line with nobody on it is a line somebody has to walk and about four have said that a line with nobody on it is a line a place is going to be asked about.

He shut the list at the eleventh hour and he wrote nothing at the foot of it, and four of the nine people in that room watched a man leave a page short on purpose and about five did not notice that he had.

The press in that room stands open with no date on it and it stood open through all of it and about four hundred copies went out of that town by hand and not out of that press.

**And the cost of that Monday is one list of about nine hundred crossings and eight fragments and two keepers gone and one narrow path anchor out of use for good, printed with the gaps in it printed as gaps, about nine hundred names of which four are in the room and about five hundred are not, about eight hundred and ninety-one crossings that no person in that room can stand at, one sheet of nine inches by six with no second copy put on a trestle in front of nine people, and one line four miles up a made road with nobody on it which is not going to get anybody this year. The bleed is six hundred and fifty days old, uncured, unexplained, and no character in that room has noticed it. The engine damaged four hundred and forty-one days. Four hundred and twenty-seven days of one man in a lower archive and he is not in that room. Not one of an ear, a leg and a year is on a list of about nine hundred crossings.**
