# Chapter 0889

**Embermonth the sixteenth, YR 325, a Saturday and a market morning, a hall at a Bellweather-Living place nine days off a made road, a long table with the Archive's cases against one wall and about nine people at it, four hundred people in the street below between the eighth hour and the eleventh, and the shortest interval the occupied timetable has allowed eighteen hours.**

That place is not a ruin and is not a reconstruction and is not a copy, and it has nine hundred people in it and four hundred of them were in that street on that Saturday morning buying what was on nine carts.

It has been here since before anybody in that room was born and it was decided in nine weeks and it is the only community in this manuscript that has never once been asked whether it wanted to be here.

The Archive sits in that hall against one wall in six cases and the cases hold four hundred things in about nine hands and nine people in nine counties know which of those hands belongs to whom.

The woman who keeps that distributed record is about twenty-nine and she came down that road on the Wednesday with the cases in two carts and four of the nine people in that hall had seen her before and five had not.

She is Mara Vey and she said her name once on the Wednesday and not again.

She was in that hall and she is a steward of that community and she has been in this manuscript since YR 318 and four of the nine people in that hall knew that and about five did not.

What she said on the Saturday was nine words long and it was that a decision by a person dies with that person and four of the nine people in that hall had heard that argument before and five had heard it from her in another room nine weeks ago.

Then she said the other half of it, and the other half is that an entry in a distributed record survives whoever wrote it because the writing is not in a hand anybody is holding, and nine people in that hall heard both halves and about four of them said that is the first time she had ever admitted the second half.

What went into that record on that Saturday morning was the Sundering and the names of the dead, and it went in as amounts and not as a speech, and four of the nine people in that hall had wanted it read aloud and five had wanted it entered.

The entries are the amounts and they were checked twice by two people who were not in the room when the first count was made, and four of the nine people in that hall had wanted a third check and five had said two people and a second day is enough.

Nine streets carry nine amounts and the ninth street carries the largest of them and four of the nine people in that hall had lived on it and five had not, and nobody said which street.

What the record does not carry is a cause and four of the nine people in that hall had wanted a cause and five had said that a cause is a thing somebody writes afterwards and that it is the writing afterwards that has cost four hundred years in about nine counties.

The dead of that place are entered as counts by street and by the year and there are nine streets and four of the nine people in that hall had wanted the street a person was standing in at the hour and about five said a street and a year is checkable and a person is not.

Nobody's name goes on a road and nobody's name goes against a house either, and that is a rule that hall set for itself about four and a half years ago and has never broken.

Then the other thing was put in that record and that is the ending of that Saturday.

It is a city of eleven thousand people four days off a coast. It is nine streets and four people in that hall have called it a city and about five have said it is nine streets.

It refused a rescue nine years ago. It said no in nine words and went on saying no for four hours while nine hundred people stood outside it, and four people in that hall know the nine words and about five know that it went on for four hours and nothing else.

The nine words are known to four people in that hall and to nine people in nine counties and nobody in that hall would say them out loud on that Saturday, and four of the nine had wanted to say them and five had wanted to hear them and not to say them either.

That is the shape of it and nine people in nine counties have said that a thing nine words long which nobody will repeat is more protected than a thing four hundred years long which anybody may read.

Nobody has asked that place anything in nine years. About four people in that hall said that nobody has asked it anything because it asked for that in nine words and about five said because nobody has known how to ask without the asking being a knock.

It has said nothing at all since and that is nine years and it is not a wound and nobody in nine counties has ever once described it as one, and four people in that hall say it is not a wound and about five say they have stopped arguing about it.

About nine hundred people in nine counties think it is a wound. That is the resistance of that Saturday and it is nine hundred people and about nine of them are in one hall and four of the nine are in that hall.

The four sheets were written in about nine counties in about four months by nine hundred people, and four of the nine hundred could not read and five had it read to them, and about nine of the nine hundred had asked twice and four hundred had asked once.

A sheet of that kind has no author and nine people in that hall had noticed that and five had not, and four people in nine counties have said that a thing with nine hundred names on it and no hand on it is the only kind of petition that anybody has ever been afraid of.

Nobody in that hall has ever signed anything and four of the nine people there have said that is because a signature is a promise to keep asking and about five have said it is because nobody there wanted to be the first.

The list of the nine hundred was written on four sheets and it came to that hall in the ordinary post with nine hundred names on it and four of the nine people in that hall had read all of it and five had read their own part of it.

It is a list of people who want somebody sent, and it does not say where to and it does not say with what, and four of the nine people in that hall said that is because they do not know and about five said it is because they cannot say it.

The woman of thirty-nine who leads that community's delegation is Sera Quen and she has been in nine rooms about this in nine weeks and she said on the Wednesday that the list is not going into that record.

What she wanted was for the silence to go into the record as a thing that place has decided and not as a thing that has happened to it, and four of the nine people in that hall had wanted that too and five had not thought about it.

That is the whole of the change and it took nine minutes and four people in that hall said the nine minutes were the whole of that Saturday and about five said nothing happened.

The third of the four scars came on the Wednesday of that week and it is on the inside of his right elbow and it is about an inch and a half long and it happened on nine steps going down out of that hall with a case of about four hundred things in it.

The stair has an iron edge on the ninth step and nine people in that community use that stair about nine hundred times a week and about four of them have cut themselves on it and about five have not.

He carried the case down himself because about four people in that hall said a case of records should not be carried by a person who has not read it and about five said a person who has not read it should not carry it either and nobody suggested a third thing.

About nine people in that community have asked him about that elbow since and about four of them have been told and about five have worked it out, and nobody in that hall asked him about it on the day.

The bleed is six hundred and ninety-seven days old, uncured, unexplained, and nobody in that hall has noticed it.

He read the sentence into the record himself, in his own voice, at about the ninth hour, and nine people in that hall heard it and about four of them heard the whole of it and about five heard the first half.

“**That place said no in nine words on the sixteenth of Longlight nine years ago and has said nothing since, and the nothing is a decision and was made by the people who live there, and no person in this hall is going to be sent to ask them whether they meant it.**”

That was fifty-three words and every person in that hall heard all fifty-three of them, and about four of them had the whole of it before he came to the end.

Then he took the four sheets of the nine hundred names off that table and put them into the hands of the man whose job it is to build and mend crossings, and that man read the first nine lines and put them down.

That is the physical of that Saturday and it cannot be undone, and four of the nine people in that hall had expected the sheets to be burned and about five had expected them to be read into the record instead.

Nobody in that hall burned anything. About four of the nine people there have said since that burning four sheets is a thing a person does to prove they read them and about five have said it is a thing a person does to be finished with them.

Then the man who builds and mends crossings said why he will not go, and he said it in his own trade, and nine people in that hall heard all of it.

He is Tovan Kest and he has been on the page of this manuscript since its first year and four of the nine people in that hall had known he was in the room and about five had not.

“**I have been on that coast road and I have stood where the crossing is and there is nothing to mend on it, and a man who goes to a place where there is nothing to mend is not a repair and is not a witness and is an event, and about nine hundred people have already had one event and nine years of nothing after it. I will send men to mend a road. I will not send men to be looked at.**”

That was eighty-four words and nine people in that hall heard all eighty-four of them, and about four of them understood the whole of it before he came to the end and the five who had not were given the last of it.

About four of the nine people in that hall had wanted to be sent and about five had not wanted to be sent at all and had said nothing, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a hall in which nobody wants to be sent is a room with something wrong in it or something right.

The woman who keeps the record then entered the decision and the list together in one entry of nine lines, and it says that no person is to be sent, and it says that the four sheets of names are held by a person and not in the record, and it says why.

That third line is the one that is new and four of the nine people in that hall had not wanted it and about five had, and nine people in nine counties have said that a decision with a reason attached to it can be argued with in about nine years and a decision without one can only be obeyed or hated.

She read the nine lines back and closed the case and nine people in that hall heard all of it and about four of them have said that closing a case on a Saturday is the only irreversible thing anybody did in that hall and about five said that handing four sheets to one man was it.

He and the woman who keeps that record had not spoken in that hall before about the eleventh hour and four of the nine people there noticed the pair of them and about five did not and four of the nine have said since that what they said to each other was about the weather and about the tide.

They are not in the same office and four people in that hall had wanted to know that and about five had not thought about it, and neither of them has said anything about it in either of the six rooms the record is kept in.

That is the whole of it and nine people in that community have said it is a thing and about five have said it is not a thing at all.

Nobody thanked anybody. Four people in that hall had said beforehand that somebody would manage it and five had said nobody would, and the five turned out to be right.

Then the record went into its case and four hundred people were still in that street below and nine hundred crossings a day were worked by nine hundred people and not one of them knew that anything had been entered against anybody's wish.

About nine people in nine counties think it is a wound and about nine hundred think it is a wound and none of them was in that hall and four people in that hall said that is the correct arrangement and about five said it is not.

The place has still said nothing and that is still nine years and four people in that hall said on the Saturday that it would be something else one day and about five said it would not.

The four sheets went into that man's tool bag at about the eleventh hour and he carried them down that road himself and about nine people in that community saw him go and about four of them said where he was going and about five did not.

He is going four days and about nine days of ordinary post and he has not said what he will do with four hundred names in a tool bag and four people in that hall have said that a thing carried is not a thing filed.

The street below that hall shut at the eleventh hour and four hundred people went out of it and about nine hundred did not know anything about the day and about nine of the nine hundred had written their name on one of four sheets.

The record case has one more entry in it than it had on the Wednesday and four people in that hall have said it is the entry this year was made for and about five have said it is one of nine.

**And the cost of that Saturday is four sheets of nine hundred names handed to one man instead of to a record, one sentence about a silence of nine years entered as a decision with a reason attached to it and read aloud by the man it is about, about nine people who thought the silence was a wound and were not sent anybody, a record case closed on a Saturday with the dead entered as amounts by street and by year and no name against any road or any house, nine hundred people in nine counties left still thinking the quiet is a wound, and one man of about thirty-four who builds and mends crossings saying in his own trade that he will not send men to be looked at. The bleed six hundred and ninety-seven days and uncured and unremarked. The shortest interval eighteen hours. Nobody in that hall was sent and nobody in that hall said the silence was a failure and it is still a silence.**
