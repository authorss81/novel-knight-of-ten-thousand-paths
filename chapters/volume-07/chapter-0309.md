# Chapter 0309

**The second day of Frostmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a press room on the ground floor at the end of a passage in a building of four floors and seven presses in a provincial town two hundred miles from a fen county, and a room one floor up with a stove in it and the stove not lit, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a word in a space in the first column of one of nine sheets went out of that building in the ordinary post that afternoon in a hand that is not the press's hand.**

That building turns out something near four hundred of its forms every month, and they go into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls, and on any one of them six spaces are ruled and a head stands over five of the six, and those heads are *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper* and *How often*, and the sixth is the same width as any of the other five, with nothing set over it at all.

**About four of the six will take a figure and the fifth will take the man who knows, described by what he does and not by what he is called, and four hundred of them a month are read in this building at waist height under a window by light off the floor, and about nine hundred people in that town have never in their lives read one and are never going to.**

“**Nine of them again,**” a man said at the passage end.

“**Nine of them,**” the man of about fifty said.

The man at the stone is about fifty and has stood at a press in that building for thirty-one years, and about eleven thousand forms have gone through his hands in that time, and in thirty-one years nobody in that building has asked him what is in the four places on one of them, and nobody has ever asked him whether he reads a filled form the whole way down, which is the one thing his trade asks of him and the one thing no office has ever wanted of anybody.

---

**And at the fourth hour he looked at a drawer and did not open it, and the drawer is against the wall at the end of that press room, and there is a word in it.**

It has been in that drawer about two years. It was struck once, on a list that went out to nine counties in the second month of the year before last, and it was set once, and it is not going to be struck again, and all that any person in that building knows about it is that it is in a drawer and that nobody has taken it out.

“**Do you want the second one,**” a man said.

“**Leave it,**” the man of about fifty said.

He looked at it for the length of a bell's count and he did not put his hand on it and he did not open it and he did not say anything, and a man who looks at a drawer at the fourth hour and does not open it has understood something about the fourth hour and is not going to be the one to say it.

**And a boy of nineteen set it once, and he is not in that press room and has not been in that press room since the second month of the year before last, and nobody in that building has asked him where he is, and two people in that building have noticed that a bench he used to be at is empty and about two of them have connected the two things and neither of them has said so.**

A boy in a press room is the only person in a building whose living depends on the difference between a word and the piece of type that makes it, and about four people in that building worked that out about two years ago and about nine of them think about it perhaps twice a year. He asked a question about that sort once, in the second month of the year before last, and was told that it is a word, and then that that is not the same as a word anybody uses, and then that it is not, and about four people in that building have worked out since that a boy of nineteen who asked whether a word was real and was told that it was a word has been told the correct answer and has heard something else, and that he has gone up in the world about a floor since the second month and is not at the stone.

---

“**The copies,**” a man said.

“**On the stone,**” the man of about fifty said.

Nine sheets went out of that press room on the afternoon of that Monday, and they were nine sheets of the office's own printed series and a man of about thirty-one with a satchel took them down to a cart, and four hundred of that series go out of that building every month and about four hundred of them come back.

**And the first column of one of the nine was not empty, and there is a space in the first column of that series about the width of a word in front of the name of the place, and on eight of the nine it was empty and on the ninth there was something in it.**

Nobody saw it go in. That is the part of it that matters and it is the part that cannot be established from anything anybody in that building can be shown, and about four people in that town have said since that a thing in a space on a printed form is either set by a press or put there by a hand, and that a press does not put anything in the space in front of the name of the place, and that a man at a stone is the only person in that room whose hand it could have been and did not do it, and that there are about forty people in that building and a corridor and four floors between the hand and the stone.

**The hand is not the hand of the press. It is a smaller hand than a hand of fifty usually writes and it is steadier than a hand of nineteen usually writes, and two people in that town have worked out that a hand that is between those two is a hand that was taught to write a form, and that about two hundred people in that building fill in forms every day for offices that are not this one, and that none of them has ever been in that press room.**

And the man of about fifty saw it at the fourth hour, and the second hour after that he was still at the stone, and a man at the passage end said nothing about it and went out, and he put the ninth sheet with the other eight and he did not say one word about it, and nobody in that room said one word to him about it, and he has not said one word about it since and is not going to, and a man of about fifty at a stone has a thing he could say about a column and about two years of wanting somebody to ask him and about one Monday in the second month of the year before last when a woman of thirty-eight came down two flights and thanked him for it, and he said nothing about any of the three, and about four people in that building have said since that the true thing about that ninth sheet is the shortest silence in the history of a press room.

---

**And a carrier of about thirty-one with a satchel took the nine of them down to a cart at the sixth hour and did not read a word of them.**

He has carried about two thousand sheets of that series in six years to about two thousand doors, and he has said out loud, three times in six years, in three different places, in the voice a man uses for a price, that he has never once known what was in the four places, and about nine hundred people in that town have heard none of the three, and it was not asked of him on that Monday and it was not said by him on that Monday and it is not going to be either.

**“Nine today,**” the man of about thirty-one said, at the bottom of the stair, to nobody in particular, and a carrier has a round and a firm and a rate that is not printed in the county he is standing in, and he went down and put them on a cart and the cart went out, and four hundred of that series go a month into nine counties and about four hundred of them come back, and of what comes back about nine is entered and eight of those nine carry a figure and one carries a space left empty with four lines beside it, and all nine go into the returns press because a return is a return, and there is no column in that building for what is in a column.

---

**A man of about thirty who keeps that room's books came down the stair at the fifth hour to say that a tray had been round the passage and come back with nothing on it for anybody on that floor, and he was up the stair again before the sentence was finished, and the tray belongs to a man of about sixty who has been running it in and out of that building for twenty-two years and has not once in that time been asked whether anybody in the building wanted it.**

A man of about thirty has been in that building about nine years and carries a district from one quarter to the next, and the returns book in the room one floor up has his hand in it four times a season, and on that Monday he came down for about nine seconds, said one short sentence and went back up, and about four people in that building have said since that he is the only person in it who ever comes down for anything.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp went up one floor at the fifth hour and stood in a room with a stove in it for about a quarter of an hour and asked nothing in it.**

The stove in that room had been cold since the weather turned and there is one window in that room and it looks at a wall nine feet off, and four hundred forms a month are read under it at waist height, and there is a returns book on a table in that room ruled nine inches by six that takes every form as a line, and about eleven returns have been entered in that book in about nine years and the book is written on by a room and not by a press.

“**You have been in this building before,**” a man said on the stair.

“**Twice,**” the man with the limp said.

Nobody was in that room and two people in that building have opened that door in about a month and about two of them went in and stood in it. There is a plate on the leg of that table with a second plate riveted over the first one and a third plate riveted over that, and nobody in that building has ever taken the top plate off to see what is under it, and about four people in that building have said since that there are three plates on the leg of that table and there has never once been a fourth.

**And in the inside pocket of that man's coat there is a form of that office's own printed series folded once and a page with eleven lines on it and no words in them, the eleventh of them a man on a shift nineteen years old, which he cannot stop seeing, and a separate leaf with a seventh line on it that says *a person*, which is a question and is not finished, and he did not take any of the three out in that room, and about four people in that building have not seen a man of thirty-two in it before and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that there is a room one floor up with a stove in it that is not lit.**

---

Nobody walked any part of that Monday with him. About nine hundred people in that town went about an ordinary day in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people in it, and the presses on the second floor started at the first hour and went on to the fifth, and a tray went into a passage at the third hour and came back at the fourth with nothing on it for anybody, and a man of about sixty carried it back out again and said the one sentence he says on that floor and has said for twenty-two years, and nobody in that building has ever asked him whether it is the same sentence every time, and it is not.

**And eight of the nine have nothing standing in the space in front of the name of the place, and one of them has a word in that space that was not struck again in the second month of the year before last and will not be struck again, and it is on its way to a door in one county of nine, and a county two hundred miles from that building is on the same series and has been on it since a cart came up a lane there in the autumn with nine of them in a satchel, and there is no line on the whole of any of them for the name of the person whose hand it is, and there is no office in that building that anybody could be shown it in, and about nine hundred people in that town will not find out about it from anybody.**
