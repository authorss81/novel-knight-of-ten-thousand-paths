# Chapter 0312

**The eleventh day of Frostmonth, YR 316, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, and two rooms above a weigh-house at the end of a lane, off a market four hundred yards long, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, with a wagon arch over the lane and a number cut into the door, and a reach four hundred yards off the other end of that market with about forty men on it, and nobody in that town knows what was copied in the upper room that Wednesday morning or who copied it.**

The tide had gone back at about the third hour on the Sunday and had not come in since, and the lower room was dry for the first time in about a month and the boards under it had gone the colour of a scab, and a woman with a pail had swept the floor of that lower room at about the seventh hour on the Monday, which is the only time in four years and a half that anybody in that county has swept it, and nobody in that lane knows that either.


The upper room has a press against the far wall with a long shelf over it and about four hundred documents in bundles on that shelf, and a fair copy and a working about four inches apart on a bench under a window, and a register with six entries in it all of them questions and none of them a finding, and a day-book with nine entries in it of a thing that was not asked which is the only book in that building nothing in this kingdom can be served out of.

**And a fair copy of that register was begun on that Wednesday morning at about the second hour by a second copyist of about thirty, and it was finished at about the fourth hour and a half, and it is the first fair copy that office has made in four years and a half.**

Nobody had asked for it. The register itself asks for it every few months in the ordinary way, because a register is a book that is written on and a book that is written on is a book that gets a copy made of it once a season the way a shoemaker copies his own pattern, and the last fair copy of that register was made in the winter before the deed was signed and has not been looked at by anybody since.

---

**And a fair copy in that county has never carried the name of the person who made it in four years and a half, and it carries the person by what she does, and that is a rule of the trade and not a rule of that office.**

“**You will want it signed,**” the keeper of records said, at about the third hour.

“**It will want the person who made it,**” the second copyist said.

“**That is the same thing and it is not the same thing. The first one is a thing a body corporate does. The second one is a thing a hand does.**”

“**Then it is the second one,**” the second copyist said, “**because I am the one who made it and if it is the first one then nobody has made it and that is not true.**”

And she wrote it in the ordinary place at the foot, in the way her trade has written it for about six years, which is the trade and not the name, and it went in as *second copyist* in the same hand that had written six entries above it, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of thirty copied six questions out of one book into another book in about two hours and a half and signed the second one with the only name that trade will let her sign, and that she has wanted a line with her own name on it since the summer before last and has now got a line on a fair copy of a register and it is the only line in four years and a half in that room with a person at the end of it, and it is not a name.

“**You could put your name,**” said a voice from the stair.

“**I could,**” the second copyist said.

“**Why do you not.**”

“**Because I am not going to be the first thing in a fair copy in this county and anybody can be served out of,**” she said, “**and I have wanted a line in a book I cannot be served out of for two summers and I am not going to have it in one I can.**”

And two people in that lane have worked out that a woman who wanted a thing for two summers got the opposite of it in the ordinary way on a Wednesday morning and told the room what it was before anybody had worked it out, and that the second copyist is not the keeper of records and has no standing in that office and is on a warrant for a third post in that day-book that is not signed, and that the third post in that day-book has been hers and unsigned for six years and the day-book has nine entries in it and the ninth of them was put in by a man of about sixty in a room four hundred yards from that lane and not by her.

---

**And a fair copy of a register is a copy, and a copy is the thing this volume is about, and nobody in that room improved on it.**

The register is the one book in that office that a body corporate can be sued over and that nobody can be served out of, and its six entries are all questions, and a question that is a question cannot be produced against anybody, and a question that is a copy of a question can be produced against anybody, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of thirty found that out on a Wednesday morning by doing the ordinary thing she was trained to do.

“**Now there are two of them,**” the keeper of records said, at about the fifth hour, with the fair copy in her hand.

“**There are two of the register,**” the second copyist said. “**There is still one of the day-book. You cannot make a copy of the day-book and I have tried to think how and I cannot.**”

“**Say why.**”

“**A day-book is nine entries of a thing nobody asked,**” the second copyist said, “**and there is no column on the office's own printed series that will take a thing nobody asked. The first column takes a place. The second takes a figure under a head that says a day. The third takes a person by his trade. The fourth is a keeper. The fifth is how often. The sixth has nothing over it at all and nothing has ever gone in it, and it is the same width as the other five, and about four of the six will take a figure and one will take a person, and nowhere on the whole of it is there the name of a person.**”

“**And a thing nobody asked is none of those.**”

“**A thing nobody asked is not a place and it is not a figure and it is not a person and it is not a keeper and it is not a frequency,**” the second copyist said, “**and the sixth column has no head, and a column with no head is not a column.**”

And she said that at about the fifth hour on a Wednesday in a room over a weigh-house, and the keeper of records did not improve on it, and nobody else in that building has ever asked her to.

**And a book that cannot be copied and cannot be served with is the only instrument in that office that cannot be produced against a person, and the woman who wanted a line in it has just said out loud in a room that she cannot make a second of it, and a woman who has said that out loud has told a room that the reason she has not got what she wants is the shape of the book and not the person who keeps it.**

---

The keeper of records put the fair copy on the shelf beside the register and did not put it into the press, and any person at all who walks up that lane can put anything into that press and can take anything out of it again, and about four days after that Wednesday about four people in that lane had noticed that a fair copy of the register was on that shelf and had not noticed that a press was four feet away from it.

“**It is a copy of a register,**” the second copyist said, when the keeper of records put it there.

“**It is a copy of six questions,**” the keeper of records said, “**and that is the whole of it, and about four people in that lane worked that out in about four days and I have not worked it out yet.**”

“**Then we have made a thing that can be produced out of the one thing in this room that could not be produced.**”

“**We have made it out of the register and not out of the day-book,**” the keeper of records said, “**and I want you to notice which of the two of us in this room worked out how to do that and which of the two of us did not, and I am not going to say it out loud a second time.**”

A fair copy of a register of six questions and no finding, and a body corporate has now got two books in which everything it has been asked is written down, and a person who walks up that lane and asks for the register can be served out of either one of them, and about two people in that lane have said since that two books are twice what one was, and that a person cannot be served out of one of them and not the other without a reason that nobody in that lane has, and about two people in that lane have said since that a body corporate which cannot be sued for a question can now be produced a question out of, twice over, and that neither of the two copies has a name on it anywhere in the writing except the one at the foot of the fair one and that one is a trade.

---

**The other work in that county, and it is the only other work there is, belongs to a man of thirty-two with a limp, and it consists of a book that does not exist.**

He has kept it about two years, on about forty mornings a month out of the four hundred mornings a year, and what is in it is a count of loads put down on the deck of a bridge in a town of about four thousand people. The book carries no number, belongs to no office, cannot be produced by any person alive, and is not on any form there is. Nine people in that town have watched him at it and about four of the nine have noticed that it comes out the same every morning, and not one of the nine has ever said so to anybody, and nobody in that county has asked him what it is for and nobody knows there is a man on a deck counting anything.

Bay four went up in a summer and the pointing of it took a third day, and nothing about that bay has been on a bill since. A man of fifty and a half is spreading a second finish over a first one on the second bay with a bar in his hand he is not using, and the arches and the pointing are his work and the laying of the timber is not, and nobody in that yard has ever put the question to him, and nothing in a market and nothing in a quiet season has ever stopped a reach in this county.

“**Load points,**” a man on the first bay said.

“**Sixteen since the second hour,**” the man with the limp said.

**Nobody in that town has ever found out that the palm of that man's left hand is warm at about the fourth hour and has been for two years, and about four people have watched him set it on cold timber about forty mornings a month and have never once said a word to him about it, and the one person in this kingdom who would have been able to feel it is two hundred miles off beside a stove that has not been lit since the weather turned.**

---

Nobody walked any part of that Wednesday with anybody. The market was shut and about nine people were in that lane in it, and the tide came in at about the tenth hour and was under the boards of that lower room by eleven, about forty men came off a reach, about nine came out of a yard on a street four hundred yards up the county's own road where a boy of fifteen was copying an emptiness for the eleventh time that week and nobody has ever told him what goes in an emptiness, and behind them a man of about sixty kept a lime heap tight against a wall under a lid with his back to the yard the whole morning, and the yard does not pay him.

**And a fair copy of a register of six questions and no finding was put on a shelf in a room with a door on a lane in a building with a number cut into the door and a year of a deed cut in under the number, at fourpence a year, and the woman who made it went home down that lane with a line in it that has a person at the end of it and no name, and the day-book on that shelf was not opened that day and does not get a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.**
