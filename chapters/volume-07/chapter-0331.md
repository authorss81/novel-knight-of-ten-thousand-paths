# Chapter 0331

**The twenty-sixth day of Goatmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people on the floors of it, and one floor up from a press room at the end of a ground-floor passage there is a room with a stove in it that has not been lit since the weather turned and a single window nine feet off a bare wall, and about four hundred sheets a month go across one table under that window and are read at waist height, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a roll of paper the width of that table came down two flights of stairs on the Friday before and was opened on that Monday morning.**

The window looks at nine feet of bare wall and gets nothing out of it. The light in that room in the last week of Goatmonth comes up off the boards and not down off the sky, and a woman reading at that table holds the sheet below her face and not above it, and there is one table in that room and one woman at it and about four hundred sheets a month come across it.

**And the stove has not been lit since the weather turned, and nobody on that floor has mentioned it in about a month, and four people have noticed it twice a week and one of the four is the woman who works at the table.** It is a stove that could be lit in the length of a cup of tea and there is nobody above her who would come down and be asked whether they wanted a room warm, and the cost of not lighting it is a hand that cannot read its own writing in the middle of a winter, and two people in that building have worked that out in about two years and have never said it out loud in the same room.

About six years ago a woman of thirty came into that room with a book under her arm that she had made herself. It is about nine inches by six, it puts one of that office's forms down as one line, and in all the time it has existed about eleven lines have gone into it in her own writing and nobody else's. No press has ever touched it, it belongs to no series that office prints and to no register that office keeps, and in six years nobody from the floors above has come down to look at it, and it is the single object in that building you could put in front of a stranger and say where it came from.

---

**And the roll that came down those stairs on the Friday before was not a return and it is not on any printed series, and it is the first thing in about two months that has come into that room that she could not have told the shape of before it was open.**

A man of about thirty brought it down from the floor above at about the seventh hour on the Friday and put it on that table and said it was for the table and went back up, and he keeps that room's books and has been in that building nine years and carries a district from one quarter to the next, and he carries it in about four seconds, and four people on that floor have said since that a man who can do that in four seconds stopped for nine of them over a roll on a table before saying nothing at all, and nobody has asked him about the nine seconds.

**The roll is about two hundred and forty lines long and it is a schedule, and a schedule is not a return.** It has an object against every line and a keeper against every line and a frequency against every line, and the heads are that office's own, and about two hundred and thirty of the lines are in the same hand and about nine of them are in a hand that is not that hand and the nine are not together.

**And it has not been finished, and the reason it has not been finished is not in it.**

---

**And she opened it at about the fourth hour on the Monday and read the object column down, and about two hundred and thirty-one of those two hundred and forty lines are places, and a place in that office is a set of market towns, and about nine of them are not.**

Nine lines, in the ninth hand, in one run, and the run is against a district. What is in the object column of the nine is not a set of market towns. It is a room. It is a room over something, in a town of about four thousand people, and the nine lines give the same room nine times over in nine different weeks, and the second column has a figure in it against each of the nine, and the head standing over the second column says *Date*.

**And the fifth column has a figure in it against each of the nine, and the head standing over the fifth column says *How often*, and that head is that office's own, and the fifth column is the only head on the whole of that printed series that anybody in that building has ever been asked to fill in out of their own head, and about four people on that floor have said since that nobody was ever asked to fill it in and it has been filled in anyway.**

**And the sixth column is the same width as the other five and there is nothing set over it at all, and nothing has ever gone in it, and a column with no head is not a column, and about two people in that building have said since that the nine lines that are not places are the only nine lines in about two hundred and forty that anybody could have put into that sixth column and did not.**

She got that far at about a minute and a half before the fourth hour and then she stopped and did not go on for about nine minutes.

**And the fourth of the six columns is headed *Keeper* and it takes a person the way the third does, by what he does and not by what he is called, and the head on it has been the same head for two years, and all nine of those nine lines have a keeper against them and the keeper against the nine of them is the same trade, and one trade standing against nine lines about one room is the shape of a thing copied nine times by a room and not by a person, and about two people in that building have said since that there is no column on the whole of that series for the person who would know whether that was one room or nine.**

She did not know what the trade was. It is a trade that about nine hundred people in that town could have named and she was not one of them, and there is no column on the whole of that series for the person who could, and about two people in that building have said since that a woman of thirty could have carried the ninth line up two flights of stairs in about nine seconds and ask one person one question and did not, and that the reason she did not is the same reason she did not ask the other one, and that neither of them has worked that out.

---

**And here is the thing she worked out at that window, and she did not say it to anybody, and about four people in that building have said since that a woman of thirty stood at a window with a roll two hundred and forty lines long in front of her for nine minutes and worked out something and told nobody, and that what she worked out is true, and that a person who has worked out a true thing alone in a room with a cold stove in it is a person who has a thing to carry and not a place to put it.**

**A place does not stop being a place because the counting of it has been given up by somebody two floors up.**

She did not work it out that way. She worked it out the other way round, in the ordinary way, standing up, with a finger on the ninth line. She worked it out that the nine lines are a room in a town of about four thousand people, and that the room is in the object column, and that a room in the object column is the same shape as a place in the object column and is not the same thing, and that the difference between them is that a place is a set of market towns and a room is a room, and that the second column has a figure against it either way and the head over the second column says the same thing whichever of the two is in the first, **and that if the counting of that room has been given up by somebody two floors up then the room is not any less a room and the district is not any less a district, and there is nobody in that building who can be told either of those two things in a room.**

The floor above her is the floor the schedule came down from, and the schedule is kept two floors up in a long chamber, and there is a man in that building who is the only person in the whole of it who would know what those two hundred and forty lines are for, and he is not in the building, and his stove two doors down from that chamber is cold, and nobody in that building has asked him where he is and nobody is going to.

**And a schedule that has not been finished because the man who finishes it is not there is not a schedule that is late, and about four people in that building have worked that out in about two months and not one of them has said it in a room, and there is no line on the whole of that office's series for a head that is out of date, and a head is the only part of a form nobody can correct.**

---

**And the woman of about thirty did the ordinary thing with a roll that has come into her room, which is to ask one question about it in the room, and she asked it of the two people on that floor who were in it, and neither of them answered it and one of them is a man of about thirty and the other is a man of about sixty who carries a tray.**

“**Is that a schedule,**” she said.

“**It came off a shelf,**” the man of about thirty said.

“**That is not a thing I asked and you know it is not.**”

“**It came off a shelf and I brought it down and I have not read it and I am not going to,**” the man of about thirty said, and went up that stair, and about two people on that floor have said since that a man who carries a district from one quarter to the next in about four seconds carried a roll of two hundred and forty lines down two flights of stairs and did not read it, and that not reading it is the trade and carrying it is not.


**And a man of about thirty came down that stair at about the fifth hour and put the roll on the table again and looked at the ninth line and said four things about it, and three of the four were about a district.**

“**That is a district,**” he said.

“**It is not.**”

“**It has got a set of market towns in the first column and a figure in the second and a keeper in the fourth, and that is a district,**” the man of about thirty said, and about four people on that floor have said since that a man of thirty was asked whether a room is a place and answered it in trade terms in about nine seconds and was wrong, and that being wrong in trade terms is the ordinary way to be wrong in an office and that nobody on that floor has ever told him so.

“**What is a keeper,**” the woman of about thirty said.

“**A trade,**” the man of about thirty said, and went up the stair, and about two people in that building have said since that a woman of thirty asked a man of thirty what a keeper is and was told what it is and not what it means, and that the man of thirty has been in that building nine years and has written under that head about four thousand times.

---
**And that was the whole of what was said about the two hundred and forty lines on that Monday by anybody in that building, and the roll was rolled up and put against the wall under the window where it stands, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know it is in that room.**

---

**And what that Monday came to is set out here in pence, and the three items are a candle, a sheet of clean waste paper, and the charge for entering one line in a book.**

The candle is a penny. The waste paper is a penny. The charge is fourpence.

*Two of those are pennies and two pennies is twopence, and that twopence with the fourpence is six pence, and six pence is the whole of that Monday.*

Total: six pence. **A figure of persons standing against a district is not in that column and is not in any column of that office, and the two prices of the same two rooms over a weigh-house in a county two hundred miles off, which is two shillings a month for each of them to the man who lets them and fourpence a year to the office that has the door with a number cut into it, are two prices of two different objects and neither of them is in the six pence above and nobody in that building has ever put them in a sum together.**

---

On that Monday nobody in that town walked any part of it with anybody else. The whole of the four hundred floors' worth of stair was used by one man carrying a tray up it at the second hour and down it at the fourth, and the tray had nothing on it either time, and the man carrying it has said one sentence on that floor every day for twenty-two years and nobody has ever asked him to say a different one. Two floors up, four hundred sheets went across one table and were read at waist height by a woman who is not looking up. One floor down, a pressman stood at a stone and set a page and gave the stair not one look.

**And a roll of about two hundred and forty lines is standing against a wall under that window in a room with a cold stove in it, and about nine of its lines are a room in a town of about four thousand people, and there is a figure set against that room in the second space and another one in the fifth, and nobody in that building has been told any of it, and there is a person in a town two hundred miles off who is the only one in this county who could be shown any of it, and she has not been told that there are two hundred and forty lines and she is not going to be.**
