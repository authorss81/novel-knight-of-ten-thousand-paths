# Chapter 0334

**The eleventh day of Thawmonth, YR 317, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people on the floors of it, and on a day that is not a market day a tray goes into a passage of that building at the third hour and comes back at the fourth with nothing on it for anybody, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that the man who carries it has said one sentence in that passage in twenty-two years that nobody in that building has repeated.**

A Tuesday is a day that building is quiet on. The presses on the second floor start later and stop earlier. The table under the window on the first floor clears two hundred sheets instead of four hundred. About nine hundred people in that town went about an ordinary day and not one of them came up the stairs.

**And the tray has gone into that passage at the third hour every day for twenty-two years and has come back every time, and about four hundred sheets a month have gone into that same passage in the same twenty-two years, and there is not one person in that building who has ever asked the man who carries the tray whether anybody wanted it, and nobody is going to, and he has never once offered it.**

The passage is nine feet wide and runs from the stair to the far door with four rooms off it, and the tray goes into it because a tray cannot go through a door a person is standing in. That is the whole of the trade and it has not changed since he took the job on. He has a wooden tray with a low lip, worn pale at the front where hands go and dark at the back where the sheets sit, and he lifts it at the second hour, puts it into the passage at the third, and brings it out at the fourth.

**And about nine people a day take something off it, and about two hundred sheets a week go out of that building by that tray, and nobody who puts a sheet on it is told where the tray goes or who brings it back, and nobody who takes one off it is told what the tray is for, and about four people in that building have worked out since that the only instrument in four floors that everybody is in the habit of using is a wooden tray with a low lip, and that it has no head over it, and that a thing with no head over it is not a column.**

“**Anything for me,**” a woman said, at the second hour, in the room with the stove in it.

“**No,**” he said, and went on up the stair.

“**I did not think there would be.**”

“**There is not,**” he said, and about two people in that building have worked out since that a man of about sixty has said the same four words to about nine people a day for twenty-two years and that nobody has ever asked him where he got them, and that the four words are the ordinary answer to a question nobody asked him, and that about nine people a day is six thousand six hundred times, and that a sentence said six thousand six hundred times without being asked is a thing that has become a form.

---

**And at about the fourth hour, in that passage, with the tray in his hand and nobody else within nine feet of him, the man of about sixty said the other thing, and he said it once, and it took him nine minutes to say it including the parts where he said nothing.**

“**A copy is a room with the people taken out of it, and a room with the people taken out of it is easier to enter than a room with the people in it, and that is the whole of the argument and nobody in the building has worked out which of the two rooms this one is.**”

It is fifty-eight words and it foots at fifty-eight, and he said it in a passage of a building of four floors at about the fourth hour of a Tuesday, and nobody heard it, and about four people in that building have worked out since that a man who has carried a tray round the same passage for twenty-two years said the truest sentence anybody has said in that building in four years in a place where there was nobody to say it to.

**And nobody improved on it, and nobody said anything about it again, and two people in that building have worked out since that the man of about sixty went back up that stair with the tray and did not know that he had said it, and that a man who says a thing like that in an empty passage and then goes back to work has not said it to a room, and that the whole of what he did was to say it once to a wall.**

---

**And at about the seventh hour the woman of about thirty worked out something at that window, and it took her about four seconds, and it is the second thing she has worked out at that window in a fortnight and she has told nobody either of them.**

She had been looking at that tray for three days. It goes into the passage at the third hour and comes back at the fourth and it does the same round whatever is on the tables, and it has done that for twenty-two years, and in twenty-two years that office has printed nine hundred sheets a month and has never once been asked what a sheet is for before it goes round nine counties.

**And the discovery is that a tray is a return and a return is a tray, and the difference between the two of them is that a tray comes back empty and nobody has ever asked it to, and a return comes back and about nine of every nine that come back are entered in a press, and about eight of those nine carry a figure in the second column and one carries a space left empty with four lines beside it, and all nine go into the same press because a return is a return.**

About four hundred people in that town have never seen that tray. Nine hundred people in that town do not know that it goes round.

“**It comes back,**” she said, to the room.

“**It comes back,**” the man of about thirty said, from the stair.

“**Does it matter what is on it.**”

“**No,**” the man of about thirty said, and two people in that building have worked out since that a woman of thirty asked whether it matters what is on a tray and a man of thirty said no in one word and went up a stair, and that the two of them are the only two people on that floor who have ever said anything about that tray, and that the man has asked four times in six years for it to stop going round and has never once asked whether it comes back full.

**And that is the second of the two things and the first of the two things is in a roll standing against a wall under that same window, two hundred and forty lines long, and a tray is not a copy of a roll and a roll is not a tray, and the woman of about thirty has worked out one of those two sentences in four seconds and has not worked out which of the two it is.**

---

**And the tray came back into that room at the fourth hour with two sheets on it, and she took them off it, and they were blanks of that office's own printed series with nothing written in any of the six spaces, and about four hundred people in that town have handled one of those two sheets without knowing it was a room.**

A blank is a room. It has six spaces in it and five heads over five of them and a sixth space the same width as the others with nothing standing over it, and it goes out of that building to nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls and it is read by a person standing up in a parish and it is thrown away or it is kept, and there is no part of it anybody has ever been shown.

She held it the way she holds every sheet at that table, which is below her face and not above it, because the light in that room comes up off the boards. She read down the six spaces and there was nothing in any of them, and she put it on the table with the other two hundred, and four people in that building have worked out since that a woman of thirty read a blank sheet in four seconds on a Tuesday and did not put it in the press and did not throw it away and put it on the table with the rest, and that a blank and a return are the same object and the only difference between them is a thing somebody wrote in it, and that nobody in that building has ever worked out where a person is supposed to write the first thing.

---

**And she went down that stair at about the fourth hour and a half because the tray has to go back to the bottom of that building and put down, and the ground floor of that building is four presses and a passage and a room with a press in it, and a man of about fifty was at a stone in the press room and did not look up.**

The returns press is in that room and it is a drawer with a handle and it is shut, and in it there is a copy of a roll of about four hundred people in about nine hundred lines that came up the stairs of that building in the ordinary post about four weeks ago, and a copy of a roll of a place four hundred miles off is in a drawer in a town of about nine hundred people and it came out of that drawer on a Friday in the month before because a drawer gets emptied, and it went back in that evening, and it is in there now.

**And nobody empties that drawer, and the man of about thirty is the only man in that building who has ever emptied it, and he was up a stair with his coat on his arm, and four people in that building have worked out since that a woman of thirty went down to the bottom of four floors with a tray to put it down and stood nine feet off a shut drawer for as long as it takes to look at a drawer and went back up, and that she has said nothing about it to anybody and is not going to.**

“**Is there a sheet left,**” she said.

“**There is a sheet left,**” the man of about fifty said, at the stone.

“**How many.**”

“**There is a sheet left,**” the man of about fifty said, to the stone, and his eyes stayed on the forme under his hands, and for about a year now not one person coming off that stair has had his head lifted, and the one time anybody marked it he gave no account of it then either, and there is no such thing in that room as a man at that stone giving anybody one syllable more than the syllable they came down there for.

Osten Varel carried two more lines across that table before the seventh hour and one of them was the ninth. Two people in that building have worked out since that a man of thirty-one has carried the ninth line onto two sheets in four weeks and has not ruled a single one of them wrong, and that a man who copies a line is the only person in that room who has never once looked at a figure standing at the end of it, and that the stove in the corner has not been lit since the weather turned and nobody in that building has asked her to light it.

On that Tuesday not one person in that town kept anybody company for any part of the day. A tray went into a passage at the third hour and came out at the fourth with two blank sheets on it, and a man of about sixty carried it back out and said the thing he says on that floor, and nobody stopped him and nobody asked him anything. The presses on the middle floor ran from the second hour to the fifth. A pressman set a page at the bottom of the stair and did not look up, and a woman carried a tray down four flights of stairs and put it on the floor of that room and carried it up again empty in her hands.

**And a building of four floors with about forty people in it is one room, and the room it is has about nine hundred people standing in a town outside it, and four hundred sheets a month go into a passage and come back out of it, and a man of about sixty has said one sentence in that passage in twenty-two years and nobody has repeated it and nobody was told, and about four people in that building have worked out since which of the two rooms it is and not one of them has said which.**
