# Chapter 0308

**The twenty-eighth day of Wolfmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and a half and the sixth hour and the eighth hour, and about forty men on a reach four hundred yards down a lane from a market four hundred yards long, and a town of about four thousand people on a river at the other end of that market, and a long room over a wharf at the north end of it with nobody in it, and a yard on a street four hundred yards up the county's own road, and a lane with a wagon arch in it and a door on it, and nobody in that market knows that a form was taken out of a press and put on a table in a room forty feet from a wagon arch that afternoon and not read.**

The reach had about forty men on it whatever else the county was doing, and the road was open over it, and the salt had been over the first bay since the spring. The fourth of those bays was done in a summer and pointed into a third day, and no bill in that county has carried a line against it since, and there is nothing else about that bay that anybody has to say.

**A man of fifty and a half was on the second bay with a bar he is not using, and for about a fortnight now he has been laying one finish over another, and the arches and the pointing are his and the laying is not, and no one in that yard has ever asked him why, and a reach does not stop for a market and it does not stop for a quiet season either.**

“**Load points,**” a man on the first bay said.

“**Eleven since the second hour,**” the man with the limp said.

A carter of thirty came over with a barrow and a waggon of his own and the salt went over the first bay in his waggon and it is the salt merchant's salt and it is in his own waggon and the guild has none of it and no claim on it, and two prices of two different things have never once stood in one column in that county.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was on the deck of that reach at about the fourth hour with a book, and the book does not exist.**

That is all the work is and it has been all the work is for about two years. There is about forty mornings a month in it and about four hundred mornings a year, and on each of those mornings he stands on the deck of a reach in a town of about four thousand people on a river and counts load points into a book that is not a book of that county, has no number on it, is not in any office, cannot be produced by anybody, and is not on a form. He has never been asked what it is for. Nobody in that county has ever asked him what it is for and nobody in that county knows there is a man on a deck counting anything.

“**You have been on this bay a fortnight,**” a man said.

“**I have,**” the man of fifty and a half said, and went on with the bar he is not using.

A half tile is under a pound and it is in his coat, and it has been in his coat through two winters, and two people in that town have seen a man take a half tile out of a coat and put it back and none of them has asked what it was for.

**And the palm of his left hand was warm at the fourth hour, and it has been warm at that hour in this county for about two years, and about four people in that town have seen him lay that hand flat on a deck about forty mornings a month, and not one of them has ever said that the hand was warm, and it is the only thing about him that a person two hundred miles off in a room with a stove in it would have been able to feel, and it is not felt in a room with a stove in it and was never going to be.**

He put the book away at about the fifth hour and went up the lane and out into the market, and about four hundred people were in a street four hundred yards long, and a waggon of salt stood at the end of the channel road about an hour and went back down it, and a man who writes down what comes up that road and what goes down it wrote one line, and there have been nine lines in that book this year and nobody in that town knows that there are nine.

---

**And the long room over the wharf at the north end of that market was open, and nobody was in it, and it is not open on a Thursday for anything, and it is not going to be.**

Nine men sit along the wall under the window when the bench sits, and under that window there is a chest with two marks in it that have not moved since the ninth of a month in the year before last, and there is a long table down the middle of the floor, and between that table and the wall there are four feet of bare boards with a bench against them and its back to the room, and about nine people have ever noticed those four feet and not one of the nine has ever asked anybody what they are for.

**The bench sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week.** It has sat eight times in nineteen years and it was asked nine things in nineteen years, and four of the nine were yes and three were no for a reason and one was brought up two flights and not put and the ninth was refused at the seventh hour of the last day of the last year in eleven words with a reason in it, and nobody in that county has asked the bench anything since and nobody is going to, and a Thursday is a day the room stands empty in and a man of thirty-two with a limp went up the outside stair at about the sixth hour and stood in the four feet of bare floor for about as long as it takes to count nine things twice.

**And under that stair there is a page about nine inches by six with a figure at the top of it written by somebody who did not write figures very well, and under that figure there are nine marks, and they are not even, and five of them sit low on the page and four of them sit high.**

Nobody has ever entered that count on a form. A form is what that county has for everything else it knows, and a bench of nine that has been asked nine things in nineteen years has exactly one record of it in this kingdom and it is a page under a stair about nine inches by six where anybody coming up can read it, and every person in that town walked past that stair at about the sixth hour of a market Thursday and not one of them knew it was there.

And a count of askings is a count of askings and is not a price and is not a count of persons and is added to nothing, and nobody in that county has ever put it in a column and nobody in that county is going to.

**And the ninth mark was put there by a clerk of about sixty at about the eighth hour of the last day of the last year in about two seconds, and he did not enter the number nine anywhere else, and he has a minute nineteen years old on a table in that room and there is no line in that minute for what the figure at the top of that page is a count of, and nobody has ever asked him and he has an answer ready.**

---

A yard on a street four hundred yards up that county's own road had about nine men in it, a bench-board standing four feet off the ground and about nine feet along it, and at the end of that yard there is a press in a room that is the yard's own and that nothing can be served out of by anybody.

**And a boy of fifteen was at that board copying the empty space under a number as well as the number, and the space is the widest of the four, and he had copied it eleven times that week and nobody has ever told him what belongs in it, and nothing belongs in it, and that is what the space is for.**

“**He is not going to finish that,**” the man of twenty-five who owns that yard said.

“**He is copying the space,**” the man of about nineteen with an empty hod said, from the end of the board.

“**I know he is copying the space,**” the man of twenty-five said, and went back down the yard.

“**It is under the number,**” the boy said.

“**I know it is under the number. I have watched you do it four times this week.**”

“**It is under the number,**” the boy said, and went on copying it, and about two of the nine in that yard have worked out that a boy of fifteen has been copying a space nobody has told him about for about two years and has never once been told what goes in it, and that the space is the widest of the four, and that about nine feet of board on a street four hundred yards up that county's own road is the only place in that county where a blank is being copied out on purpose by somebody who has been told to.

At the end of that yard a man of about sixty was packing lime against a heap with a lid on it, with his back to everybody, and he is the only man in that yard who is not paid out of it, and he said one thing over his shoulder without turning round and it was the only thing he said in that yard that day and it was to nobody.

“**Somebody has to know where the lime is.**”

---

**And at about the eighth hour a second copyist of about thirty in a room over a weigh-house forty feet from a wagon arch took a form out of a press and put it on a table, and nobody came, and it was not read.**

She had been in that room six years and a warrant for a third post in that day-book is not signed and she is the only name that anybody would put under it, and she has wanted a line in that day-book with her own name on it since the summer before last and she has said once out loud, in a kitchen, that she will not have it and does not want it and that the two are one thing.

The form had been in that press since about the seventh hour on the last Saturday, in the ordinary way, because anybody who walks up that lane can serve a press in that room with anything at all. She took it out at about the eighth hour and put it face up on the table in the middle of the room, and it is a sheet of that office's own printed series with six spaces and five heads, and it says at the foot of it in the office's own hand that a return is wanted.

**And she put it there in the ordinary way, in the hope of a person, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of thirty put a form on a table in the plain way because a form on a table is the only kind of thing in that building that can be looked at by somebody who has not come yet.**

Nobody came. The keeper of records was out of the county and the salt merchant was at the wharf and the second keeper of twenty-six with the third post in the day-book and the key was at the end of the lane, and about the eighth hour there was nobody in that lane but about nine people in a street four hundred yards long, and a form lay on a table in a room with a door on a lane from about the eighth hour until about the ninth hour and a quarter, face up, in a building with a number cut into the door and a year of a deed cut in under it, at fourpence a year.

And at about the ninth hour the second copyist of about thirty put it back into the press, and she did not open the day-book, and it stands where it has stood, at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and it is not going to be given a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.

---

Nobody walked any part of that Thursday with anybody. About four hundred people went home from a market four hundred yards long with a bell in the air behind them, and the tide went back at about the third hour, and about forty men went home from a reach with the road open over it, and about nine men went home from a yard with a bench-board in it and a press at the end of a room in that yard that nothing can be served out of, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went up a county's own road on his own with a stick he does not need and has not put down, and **a form went back into a press in a room with a door on a lane that nobody read between the eighth hour and the ninth hour and a quarter, and everybody in that town went home on a market day not knowing that there was anything in that room to read.**
