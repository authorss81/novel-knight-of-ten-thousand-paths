# Chapter 0329

**The twenty-third day of Goatmonth, YR 316, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people in it, and one floor up a room with a stove in it that has not been lit since the weather turned and a single window nine feet off a bare wall, and about four hundred sheets a month go across one table under that window and are read at waist height, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a woman of thirty-three in that room filled in a column about two hundred times on that Friday without once looking up.**

About four of the six columns on every one of those sheets take a figure. The third takes a man by his trade and not by his name. The fourth is headed *Keeper*, and it takes a person too, and it takes him the way the third does, and **the head on the fourth of those columns has been the same head for two years and four people in that building think about it perhaps twice a year and none of them has ever asked anybody whether the two columns are for different things.** The sixth is the same width as the other five and has nothing set over it at all.

A woman of thirty-three keeps the register that the fourth column belongs to. She is an aid to that register and not a member of anything and never has been, and she came into that building at twenty-two, and about two hundred times a season for eleven years she has come to that column off a roll, and it takes her about a minute and a quarter, and she does not look up while she is doing it, and four people in that building have worked out since that a woman who can do a thing in about a minute and a quarter without looking up has been doing it eleven years and has never once looked up, and that the not-looking-up is not a speed and is a thing she has trained out of her hands.

**Behind her there is a case, and on the second shelf of it a book lying among about nine others, and she could put her hand on the right one of the nine without turning round. A leaf inside it is face down and has been face down since she wrote on it two years ago, and nobody has asked her about that leaf and nobody is going to. Four people in that building have said out loud, among themselves, that turning a leaf over is deciding what comes next, and about four hundred forms a month go out of that building and not one of them carries a line for what a person means to do next.**

**And at the foot of the back of every one of those sheets there are nine words in a smaller type again, and they have been the same nine words for two years, and about four people in that building think about them perhaps twice a year, and nobody has asked whose they are and nobody is going to, and the woman of thirty-three is not the person who would be asked about them and is not the person who has been asked about them.**

---

**And a man of thirty put a roll on that table at about the fourth hour and did not take it off it again, and four hundred sheets went across that table that day in the ordinary way and were read at waist height, and one of the two was a roll of about nine hundred lines folded in four and folded in four again, and it had come up the stairs of that building in the ordinary post about two weeks before and had gone into the returns press, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that it is in that building.**

“**It came back out of the drawer this morning,**” he said, to nobody in particular, “**because a drawer gets emptied, and I am looking for one name in it and it is not the name I am looking for and I have gone down about nine hundred lines twice.**”

“**Then you have got the day wrong,**” the woman of thirty-three said, without stopping, “**and it is in the first column and not the second, and you have been reading across it.**”

“**It is in the first column,**” the man of thirty said.

“**Then it is the day,**” she said, “**and you have the day wrong and not the name, and there is a figure against the day and the figure is not a day.**”

And the man of thirty went back down the roll and found the name in the second column about four minutes later, and about two people on that floor have said since that a woman of thirty-three told a man of thirty in nine seconds that he had got the day wrong, and that she said it while working and did not stop and did not look up, and that he did not thank her for it, because a man does not thank a person for something he should have seen himself, and that it is the only advice that woman has given anybody in that building in eleven years.

---

**And the fourth column of about two hundred of the office's own printed series that crossed that table that afternoon wanted a keeper, and a roll of about four hundred people was lying open on the same table, and nobody in that room said anything about that, and about two people in that building have worked out since that a clerk of that office filled the fourth column off a roll that came out of a press that nobody had emptied in four years and a half in another county two hundred miles off, and that she did it in about a minute and a quarter, and that she was not asked whether the roll was hers and it was not hers and she did not say.**

**And she filled it in, and it took about a minute and a quarter, and she did not look up once, and that is the whole of what she did that afternoon that anybody in that building would remember.**

She came to that column the way a hand comes to a thing it has done about two hundred times a season for eleven years, and two hundred times in an afternoon is not a figure about that column, it is a figure about a hand, and the hand went down the roll and across the sheet and down again without a pause at any of the places where a person who was reading would have stopped.

**And the person she was writing down was on a list she had never seen. She did not know the name and she did not know the trade and she did not know that the roll she was working from was a copy of a roll, and that the roll it was copied from is four hundred miles off in a valley that is not on any map, and she copied a trade off a copy into a column that will only take a person by what they do, and she did it two hundred times in about a minute and a quarter, and the man of thirty was standing nine feet off that table with nine hundred lines open in front of him and did not see her do it and was not looking.**

“**You are quick at that,**” the man of thirty said, at about a quarter past the fourth hour, in the voice a person uses in a room where everybody is working.

And he did not ask her about it, and she did not answer it, and about two people in that building have said since that a man of thirty said one sentence to a woman of thirty-three about her speed and got no answer and did not ask a second time, and that nobody in that building has ever asked her a second question about that column in eleven years, and that the one question she ever asked out loud about it was asked in a room once in eleven years and four people heard it, two of them heard it a second time, and nobody has done a thing about it since and nobody is going to.

**And nobody thanked her. Nobody said she had done it well. Nobody said she had done it fast or that the hand had got steadier, and nobody asked her why she did it, and nobody asked her whether she had wanted to do it, and her want is nine words and it has not got one word more in it than it had in the month she was twenty-two, and it is not printed here and nobody in that building has asked her to say it out loud in eleven years, and the woman of thirty-three is not on that table in order to be used by anybody.**

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at that window for about a quarter of an hour with his back to a wall nine feet off it, and he did not take anything out of his coat, and he did not ask her anything, and about two people in that building have worked out since that a man came two hundred miles and stood at a window through the whole of a minute and a quarter of a woman of thirty-three writing other people into a column, and that he has a question of his own in a different column in a different county and did not ask it and has not asked it, and that two people in two buildings two hundred miles apart have now not asked a question in the same week and neither of them has been told the other did it.**

**And that Friday is set out here in pence, every item, and the sum is spoken only after the last of them.**

A sheet of that office's own printed series for a return, a penny. The clerk's charge for entering one sheet, fourpence. A second quill, because the first one had a bad point, a penny. A sheet of clean waste paper, a penny. A candle, twopence. A sheet for a copy of the copy, a penny.

*One penny, fourpence, one penny, one penny, twopence and one penny come to ten pence, and the adding of them is on this line in that order, and the order is the order they are written in, and it is not a rate.*

Total: ten pence. **No rate for the hire of a room stands in that column and this town has never published one, and a figure of persons about a district is not in that column and is not set beside a figure about a county two hundred miles off, and no clerk in that building has ever put one of those two beside the other, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a roll of four hundred people came up the stairs in the ordinary post two weeks ago and was emptied out of a drawer and used.**

---

Nobody walked a part of that Friday with anybody. The town went about its day and about nine hundred of the people in it had never once come into that building and were not going to. The tray came round that floor between the third hour and the fourth, empty for everybody, and a man of about sixty carried it back out and said the thing he says on that floor. The presses on the second floor started at the first hour and went on to the fifth, and a man of fifty at a stone set a page on the ground floor and did not look up at anybody. Four hundred sheets went across one table under one window and were read at waist height, and a roll of nine hundred lines went back into the returns press at the seventh hour in a drawer that is not emptied on any day anybody keeps.

**And a woman of thirty-three went home from that building at the seventh hour with a copy of a roll she was not given, and a leaf in a book on the second shelf is still turned face down and was not turned over on that Friday and is not going to be, and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her why she did it and her want did not change, and about two people in that building have said since that a woman of thirty-three has now been on a page in other people's books nine times over and has not been asked a single question about any of it, and that the one thing anybody in that building would be willing to bet on is that she will be at that column again on Monday.**
