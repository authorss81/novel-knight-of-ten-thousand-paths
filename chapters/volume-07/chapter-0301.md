# Chapter 0301

**The twenty-fifth day of Embermonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the fourth hour and a half and the fifth hour and a half, and a market four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane from that market with about forty men on it, and a yard on a street four hundred yards up that county's own road with a bench-board standing four feet off the ground in it, and nobody in that market knows that a man went up a road that morning with a witness at each end of it and a date against it.**

The bell went at the fourth hour and a half and nobody in that market looked up. It goes on a Saturday in a market month and it went on the Sunday before that and it goes on, and the line it is rung on is pulled together on the count of four, and neither of the two people who pull it belongs to anything in this county that pays them, and nobody in that county has ever asked either of them where the count of four came from.

The market was on from the second hour until about the seventh, and a waggon of salt stood at the end of the channel road for about an hour with nobody under it, and the man who keeps the book of what comes up that road and what goes down it put a single line in that day, and the book has nine lines in it this year and one of the nine is that waggon, which is not the guild's salt and has never been in the guild's book.

Four hundred yards down a lane there were about forty men on the reach with the road open over it, and the salt was over the first bay, and the fourth bay was finished in the summer and pointed into a third day and there is nothing left on it that is on a bill. A man of fifty and a half was on the second bay putting a finish on a finish, and he has a bar he is not using, and nobody in that yard has asked him why, and a reach does not stop for a market and a reach does not stop for a quiet season either.

---

The yard on that street had about nine men in it and a bench-board four feet off the ground and about nine feet along it, and a press in a room at the end that is the yard's own and cannot be served with anything by anybody. A bundle has been lying in that press for years with nothing written on the outside of it and it is not on the yard's book. A man of about nineteen with an empty hod was at the end of that board doing nothing in particular with the hod.

A man of thirty-two with a limp came up that street at about the fifth hour with a stick he does not need and has not put down, and he asked the man of about nineteen to stand at this end of something for about four minutes and say afterwards that he had.

“**What am I standing at,**” the man of about nineteen said.

“**A road,**” the man with the stick said. “**It is not made yet. It goes four hundred miles and it comes to a place and I want a witness at this end and you are the only man in this yard who has nothing on this morning.**”

“**I have a hod,**” the man of about nineteen said.

“**You have an empty hod,**” the man with the stick said, “**and I am not asking you to fill it.**”

The man of about nineteen put the hod down against the wall and stood at the end of the board with his hands loose, and the man with the stick put his left hand flat on the top rail of the board and said a date out loud in the ordinary way, the twenty-fifth of a month in that year, and named the other end of it, and the man of about nineteen said that he had heard a date and an end named and that he had not been told what he was witnessing.

“**You are witnessing that a man said a date and an end,**” the man with the stick said, “**and that is the whole of it and there is no more of it to know.**”

“**Then I will stand here,**” the man of about nineteen said, and stood there, and did not ask anything else, and about two of the nine in that yard have said since that a man was asked to witness a road and asked what he was witnessing and was told all of it, and that all of it was about as long as a lane.

**And the road was open, and it was not made with a hand on anything, and nothing was broken, and he did not put a hand on a thing in a crossing, and nothing in that yard and nothing in that lane knew anything about it while it was happening.**

---

A man of about thirty-two with a limp has a coat over a chair and a chair nine feet off him, and in the inside pocket of that coat there is a separate leaf with a seventh line on it that says *a person*, and he has carried that leaf since a chamber with no window in it in a town two hundred miles off, and the line is a question and it is not finished, and he did not take it out in that yard and did not look at it in that lane, and nobody in that county knows it is in his coat.

He went up the lane and past the end of the market and up a road that the county keeps and nobody has ever put anything else on, and there is no bell on it and no clerk and no seal, and the road he went up carries a road over the water four hundred yards off it, and he went over that too. There is no second crossing on that road and nobody in that county has ever said so and nobody is going to.

**And on the other side of it there was a valley with about four hundred people in it, and a town at the low end of the water with two hundred feet of wharf on it, and eleven steadings and four hamlets up the water, and about two hundred of a generation that had never once left the valley, and a man with a limp arrived in it about four minutes after he had said a date in a yard four hundred miles off, and nobody in that valley knew he was there.**

---

The first person he met was a woman of about thirty with a slate under her arm and a stick of chalk tied to the string of it, and she was outside a door at the top of a lane with about nine other people going in and out of it, and she asked him one question, and it was an ordinary question, and he answered it before he had got to the end of the first clause of it.

“**You are not one of the three,**” she said.

“**He read the book out in the aisle and the aisle has not been used since before I was born, and the man who keeps the book wrote one line across it in a hundred and forty years and nobody has asked him about it, and there are about two hundred people standing at the back of that room who have never once left this valley, and I have not got up a bank since a day in the middle of a long month and I am not going to, and there is nobody at my shoulder and there is nobody at this end of that road and I did not do it and I am not able to say who did.**”

And the woman with the slate looked at him for about four seconds and did not take a step back and did not write anything on the slate, and about nine people going in and out of that door heard all of it and about four of them had heard a man say that before and in a different voice.

**And then he knew it was not his, and he knew it about half way through the first sentence of it, and it had taken him no time at all to find that out and about four seconds to finish saying what he had started.**

“**I am not right,**” he said. “**And I would like somebody to tell me what I have taken.**”

Nobody in that lane said anything. The woman with the slate put the chalk against the slate and wrote one line on it, and the line was not what he had said, and he did not see what it was because she had turned the slate over against her hip, and a woman who has carried a slate for eleven years can turn one over in about a second and a half and a man with a limp cannot get to it.

“**Nobody is going to tell you that,**” she said. “**You will have to ask a man and then you will have to believe him.**”

---

**And the man who keeps the roll was in the room behind that door, and the roll is the only record anybody in that valley has of who is in it, and he is about fifty, and his name is Marek Ollen, and he has kept it since he was thirty-one.**

It is a long sheet folded in four and ruled in three columns, and the first column is a day and the second is a name and the third is a mark that is either a gate or a market or a cross, and there is no column on it for anything else and there has never been one, and about four hundred people in that valley are on it in about nine hundred lines and a roll of that size is what a place with about four hundred people in it has.

He was not a stranger to the road. He was the witness at the far end of it, and a witness at the end of a road is not the person who keeps it, and about four people in that valley have said since that the man who keeps the roll in that valley is not the man who keeps the road to it and that the second of those two things has nobody in it at all.

“**You said a date,**” Marek Ollen said. “**You said it into a stick and a board in a yard and a man with a hod heard it.**”

“**I did.**”

“**Then that is the far end of it, and I am the far end of it, and I have not been told what is at this end.**”

“**There is a market and a yard,**” the man with the limp said, “**and about forty men on a bridge and a bell that goes at the fourth hour and a half.**”

“**Then who is at this end.**”

“**A man who keeps a roll,**” Marek Ollen said. “**That is the whole of what I am. You will have to write a trade in that coat of yours and not a name.**”

“**I have a name,**” the man with the limp said.

“**You have,**” Marek Ollen said, “**and the line wants the other thing.**”

“**I have not been told that either,**” Marek Ollen said, and put his hand flat on the folded sheet and left it there about four seconds, and about two hundred people of that generation at the back of the room over the cross had been watching his hands and not his face for about as long as it took.

And then he turned the roll round and opened it at the third fold and put his finger on a place in the day column, and there were nine days ruled across in that column with nothing in them, and the ruling was his and it was old, and it was nine days in one season of about four in a year, and they were not the same nine days in any two seasons anybody in that valley could remember.

“**That is a gap,**” Marek Ollen said. “**I ruled it and I wrote nothing in it and I have done that about four times in nine years.**”

“**What is in it.**”

“**I do not know,**” Marek Ollen said, and he said it at the height a man says a thing at when he has said it before, and he did not add to it, and he did not look up out of the roll, and about two of the nine in that room have noticed that a man who keeps a roll of everybody in a place was asked what was in nine ruled days in it and gave one answer and no second one, and that the answer was the first time anybody in that valley had been told in words that the nine days were not known, and that it was told to a stranger on the day he came.

---

**Nobody walked any part of that day with him. About four hundred people went home from a market with a bell behind them, and about nine men went home from a yard with a bench-board and a press in a room at the end of it that nothing can be served out of, and the salt was over the first bay and the tide was under the wharf, and four hundred miles off a man with a limp was standing in a room with a folded sheet on a table in it and had been entered on it that afternoon, and every one of them went about their evening not knowing that there was a second sheet with nine ruled days in it and a stranger's name in the day column, and the roll of that valley is the only record any of them will ever be in, and it has no column for what is in a gap.**
