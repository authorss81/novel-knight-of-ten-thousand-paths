# Chapter 0320

**The fifteenth day of Hearthmonth, YR 316, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and the last day of the fifteenth month of that year, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, with a market four hundred yards long and nobody standing in it, a weigh-house at the end of a lane off that market with two rooms above it and a numbered door, a wharf at the other end with a long room over it, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane off the market, and nobody in that town knows that a woman of twenty-nine in one of those two rooms decided something on that Tuesday morning that stands.**

The tide was under the wharf and had been since about the tenth hour of the day before and goes back at about the third hour. The market was shut and had been shut since the fourth of that month on a day that is not the one anybody in that town was thinking about, and about nine people went down that lane, and about four of the nine were somebody else's business. A waggon of salt came up the channel road at about the seventh hour and stood at the end of it about an hour with nobody under it and went back down it, and the salt on it is a salt merchant's salt and is in his own waggon and the guild has none of it and no claim on it, and the man whose book it is to set down what goes up that road and what comes down it set down one line, and nine lines have gone into that book this year.

**And the reach was going, about forty men on it, the road open over it and the salt over the first span of it, and a man of fifty and a half was on the second bay putting a finish over a finish with a bar he is not using, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was on the deck of it at about the fourth hour with a book that does not exist, and he counted about forty load points into it in the ordinary way and about four people in that town have seen him do it and about nine hundred people in that town do not know he is on that deck.**

---

**And at about the second hour the keeper of records took a sheet off her own shelf and put it on the table face up, and it is a sheet of that office's own printed series and it is the first one she has ever begun to fill in, in four years and a half, in order to answer.**

“**I am going to answer it,**” she said.

The second copyist of about thirty did not say anything for about four seconds, and then she came down about four steps, which she has not done for about a year, and stood at the bottom of them.

“**You have said Nothing in that room four times in about half a year,**” she said.

“**I have said Nothing in that room four times in about half a year,**” the keeper of records said, “**and the reason is the same reason every time, and the reason is that there is no line anywhere in this kingdom for the answer this office has got, and that is not a thing about this county and it is a thing about about four hundred sheets a month and nine counties.**”

“**Then what has changed.**”

“**A day has gone,**” the keeper of records said. “**It was the ninth of that month and it was the day by which cause might be shown and about four of it were left on the morning of the fifth and nobody in this county knew there was a day and not one person in this county was asked to show cause and not one person in this county did, and a day went, and there is nothing on the shelf that says it went, and a county of about four thousand people has now not answered a thing for four years and a half and has had one occasion to and was not asked on it.**”

“**And you are going to answer a thing nobody asked you about.**”

“**I am going to put on a sheet that the office has already got, in a series that office already prints, that this county has nothing to say against a figure of persons standing against it,**” the keeper of records said, “**and I am going to send it in the ordinary post, and it will go round and come back, and about four people in this lane will know that it went.**”

---

**And the instrument did not take what she put in it, and it is the only instrument in this kingdom she has ever had, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine who has had four years and a half to write nothing down wrote something down on the fifteenth of a month in a room with a door on a lane and the series would not take it.**

Six spaces on it, and a head over five of them in that office's own setting, and the sixth is as wide as any of the other five with nothing at all set above it. Four of the six are for figures, the fifth is for the man who knows and takes him by his trade and not by his name, and there is nowhere on the whole of that sheet where the name of a person goes.

She put the place in the first column, the way that office writes a place, which is a set of market towns. She put her trade in the third column, and the third column is headed *Person having knowledge* and it is the fifth one that takes a person by his trade, and she wrote it in the third because the third is the column that says who knows, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman put the only thing she is in the correct column for on a sheet that will go round nine counties and come back.

“**The second column,**” the second copyist said.

“**Is empty and will stay empty,**” the keeper of records said. “**And the head over it says *Date*, and there is no date in it, because a county that has not been asked to show cause has not got a day to put against a cause, and if I put a figure in the second column to fill it then I have answered a question nobody asked me and I will not do it.**”

“**Then it is a return with three blanks in it.**”

“**It is a return with three blanks in it and a place and a trade,**” the keeper of records said, “**and about four hundred of that series come back to that building every month, and eight of every nine that come back carry a figure in the second column, and one carries a space left empty with four lines beside it. They all go into the returns press, whatever is on them, and there is no column in that building for what is in a column.**”

“**So it will come back.**”

“**It will come back about two months from now and nobody in this county will be there when it does, and the two of us will not either, because neither of us has ever been in that building.**”

**And a sheet with a place in the first column and a trade in the third and nothing at all in the other four is the only kind of paper in this kingdom that says a county has an answer, and it is not a return, and it is not a refusal, and it is not a finding, and there is no line on the whole of that office's series that will take an answer, and the sixth column has nothing standing over it and nothing has ever gone in it and a column with no head is not a column, and the keeper of records said all of that out loud in that room at about the fourth hour and about nine hundred people in a town two hundred miles off do not know it was said.**

---

**And the decision went into the register, and the register took it the way the register takes everything, which is as a question, and there are seven entries in it now and not one of them is a finding.**

“**That is a decision,**” the second copyist said.

“**It is a decision and it is entered as a question,**” the keeper of records said, “**and I want it in the register and I want it in the register as a question, because a body corporate with a door and a number can be sued for a finding and cannot be sued for a question, and I have entered six questions in four years and a half and answered none of them, and I am going to enter a seventh and it is going to be the first of the seven that I have acted on.**”

“**Then nobody will be able to tell you did it.**”

“**Nobody will be able to tell anybody that I did it, and that is the seventh question, and it is the same as the other six,**” the keeper of records said, “**and a body corporate that has asked six things in four years and a half and answered none of them has this morning become a body corporate that has asked seven things and answered one, and the one is on a sheet of a printed series going two hundred miles in a bag on an ordinary round with nothing in four of its six columns, and there is no form in this kingdom that anybody could be shown which would say that this county had an opinion.**”

---

**What that Tuesday came to is set out here in pence, and the sum is given after the last of the items and not before them.**

Printing the answer on that office's own printed series: a penny. The clerk's charge for putting a line into a register: fourpence. Printing this county's own copy of that answer: a penny. A foot of candle burned in a room over a weigh-house in a wet week: twopence.

*One penny and fourpence is five pence. Five pence and one more penny is six. Six and twopence is eight, and eight pence is the whole of what a county's answer to a figure of persons came to in four years and a half.*

Total: eight pence. **The thing this office costs a year and the rent a salt merchant asks a month for the pair of rooms are two different prices of two different objects, and neither of them is in the column above, and no one in that building has ever put the two into one sum, and nobody in this county charges for the hire of a room at all.** Fourpence a week out of a private box is a man's pay and it is in no column of this office.

The answer went onto the shelf beside the register, the fair copy of the register, and the sheet that came back from nine counties with a place in it and nothing in the second. Four things are on that shelf and about four people in that lane have counted them and about nine hundred people in that town have not. **She chose the shelf and not the press, and the press can be served with anything by anybody who walks up that lane and can be emptied of it by the same. A day-book on that shelf stands at nine entries of a thing that was not asked and was not opened on that Tuesday, and it is not going to get a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.**

---

**And a man of thirty-one with a satchel will take it up that lane on the Friday in the ordinary way, and he has carried about two thousand sheets of that series in six years to about two thousand doors, and three times in six years in three different places he has said out loud, in the voice a man uses for a price, that he has never once known what was in the four places on a sheet, and it was not asked of him about a sheet with three blanks in it and it will not be said by him, and he reads none of it and reads none of anything.**

Nobody walked any part of that Tuesday with anybody. About nine people went down that lane and about nine men went home from a yard on a street four hundred yards up the county's own road where a board stands four feet off the ground and a press at the end of that yard cannot be served with anything, and a man of about fifty-five turned three bars down and up nine times and went back to a hut three hundred yards down that road, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went up that road on his own with a stick he does not need and has never once put down.

**And the keeper of records of a body corporate in this county and a woman of thirty-eight in a chamber in a town two hundred miles off are two hundred miles apart, and neither of them is the instrument the other one works for, and there is nothing in either of those two offices that could carry one word of this morning the two hundred miles to the other, and neither of the two women has ever stood in the other's room, and about four people in this lane have said since that two women have each spent about nine years asking a room to show what was asked, and that about nine people in four counties have heard one of them say it and about four have heard the other, and one of them is in a county of about four thousand people and one of them is in a building of four floors and seven presses, and the two of them were each refused in a different kind of building on the same morning eleven days apart by a different person with a reason of his own, and about four thousand people live in this town and about nine of them were in that lane at the second hour this morning, and the tide was under the wharf at the north end of that market and went back at about the third hour, and a long room over that wharf stood open all morning with nobody in it and a bench in it that sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week, and underneath the stair at the north end of that market there is a sheet about nine inches by six with a figure at the top of it and nine marks under it, and it is nine, and nobody in this county has ever been asked to move it.**
