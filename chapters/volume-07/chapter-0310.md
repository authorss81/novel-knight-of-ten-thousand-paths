# Chapter 0310

**The fifth day of Frostmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the fourth hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour and the eighth hour, and two rooms over a weigh-house at the end of a lane at the north end of a market in a town of about four thousand people on a river, that market being four hundred yards long, and a yard on a street four hundred yards up the county's own road, and a reach off a lane at the other end of that market with about forty men on it, and a hut at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards down that road, and a ladder standing against a wall in a lane about a mile off, and nobody in that market knows that a county was entered in a column that morning and that nobody in that county was asked about it.**

The market ran from the second hour until about the seventh, and salt stood at the end of the channel road for the better part of an hour with nobody under it, and a keeper wrote one line in the book of that road's up and down, and it has nine lines in it this year and it has never once been the guild's book.

**“There is no room at the far end of it,**” a man said to a carter at the end of the channel road.

“**There is a wharf,**” the first man said, “**and about two hundred feet of it.**”

The reach had about forty men on it and the road was open over it and the salt was over the first bay, and the man on the second bay is fifty and a half and has a bar he is not using, and about nine men were in a yard on that county's own road four hundred yards up it, with a bench-board in it and a press in a room at the back of that yard, out of which nothing can be served, and a man of about fifty-five was in a hut at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards down that road turning the bars down and up and saying four words to whoever was going through, which he has been saying for nineteen years and which nobody in that county has ever asked him about, and the four words are not written down anywhere and are not going to be.

**And a ladder was standing against a wall in a lane about a mile off, and it had been standing there since a week in the middle of that autumn, and nobody in that town knows it is there, and the man of thirty-two with a limp saw it on his way back up the lane and looked at it for about as long as it takes to look at nine feet of nothing and did not stop and about nine feet of it is about the length of a man with a limp's reach plus a hand.**

---

**And at about the fourth hour the keeper of records took a sheet out of a press in a room with a door on a lane and read it, and the whole of what she did with it took about a minute and a half.**

It is a sheet of that office's own printed series. Six spaces, five heads, the sixth the same width as the other five with nothing at the top of it, and the heads are that office's own, and four of the six are ruled for a figure and the third is for a person and it takes him by his trade and not by his name, and **there is no place on the whole of that sheet, in any column, in any line, at the head or the foot, where the name of a person goes.**

The first column has a place in it, written the way that office writes a place, which is a set of market towns, and the second column has a figure in it standing under a head that says *Date*.

**And what is in that second column is a count of people, and it is more people than there are in that town, and it is about nine hundred more than the woman holding the sheet would have said, and she knows the size of that town the way everybody in a town of four thousand knows it, which is badly, and by three or four hundred either way.**

The sheet says that a return is wanted, and a return in the plain way of that office is the same series again with a place in the first column and a figure in the second. The first one of these came up that lane in a cart in the autumn and is on the shelf with the register and it gave a day by which cause might be shown to an office two hundred miles off, and **there are about four days of that day left and nobody in that town knows that there is a day, and nobody in that county has been asked to show cause and is not going to be asked, and a thing nobody has been asked to show cause against cannot be shown to have been answered.**

The second one came up the same lane in a courier's bag and it asks for nothing at all. It wants a return, and a return is a figure, and there is a figure against this county that is about nine hundred more than the number of people in it, and a county cannot get a smaller figure by arguing with a number.

“**A ladder has been standing in a lane a mile off since the autumn,**” the second copyist said. “**Nobody has asked about it.**”

“**I have not walked there,**” the keeper of records said.

“**Then what do we do,**” said the second copyist, who had come down at about the fourth hour because there was a sheet of paper on the table again.

“**Nothing,**” the keeper of records said, and she said it once, and she did not explain it the second time, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine has now said that word in that room four times in about half a year and has given the reason for it three of them and that the reason is the same reason every time, and that a body corporate which has been asked five questions by an office two hundred miles off has answered none of them and has not asked anybody to, **and that the whole of what this office has ever been able to do about a figure is nothing, and that it has been doing it for four years and a half, and that there is a book for it, and that the book is in that room and nobody can be served out of it.**

---

**And then a carter of about thirty-four came up that lane at about the seventh hour with a waggon of his own behind him and asked what a cart costs to take a load two hundred miles, and there is no rate for it in that county and there never has been one, and the ordinary way that room answers a question it cannot answer is with a piece of paper.**

He had a load of lime out of a yard four hundred yards up the road and a man had told him it would go cheaper in a county with a press in it, and there is no press in that county and there is no rate in that county and there is no office in that county, and he came up a lane to the one door in that town with a number cut into it and asked.

“**What does a cart cost to two hundred miles,**” the carter said.

And the keeper of records did not tell him that she did not know, and she did not tell him that there was no such thing, and she took a sheet off the shelf at about the seventh hour and gave it to him, and the sheet is a sheet of that office's own printed series with a head over the first column and a head over the second, and a man takes a piece of paper.

“**Fill that in,**” she said, “**and it will go in the ordinary post.**”

“**And what do I do with it,**” the carter said.

“**Post it,**” the keeper of records said, and went back to the shelf.

**And a carter of about thirty-four took a form of a printed series out of a room over a weigh-house in a town of about four thousand people because he asked what a cart cost, and he put it in his coat, and nobody in that market knows that the only rate anybody has ever offered anybody in that county is a piece of paper with a head on it, and that the form travels in a bag on an ordinary round, and that a man who asks a price and is given a column will fill in the column, because that is what a column is for and because a man cannot ask the same question twice in a lane.**

And a form that travels is a form that can be asked about in about four months by a man in a town two hundred miles off who is not a carter and has never heard of that county and will not go and will not ask, and nine people in that lane have worked out that a county which cannot answer a question about a cart has answered it, and that the answer is going two hundred miles off in a bag on a round a man makes every week, and that nobody in that county will be told what it said.

---

**And that Thursday came to an account, and every item in it is added in pence on the page before the sum is given and not after.**

A sheet of the office's own printed series for this county's own copy of one sheet is a penny. A clerk's fee for entering a sheet is fourpence. A sheet of the same series for the copy of the minute on the back of it is a penny. A foot of candle burned in a room over a weigh-house in a wet week is twopence.

*A penny and fourpence is fivepence. Fivepence and a penny is sixpence. Sixpence and twopence is eight pence.*

Total: eight pence. A room is not in that column and there is no rate in this county for one, and the fourpence a year that this office pays and the two shillings a month that a salt merchant charges for each of these two rooms are two prices of two different things and neither of them is in that column, and nobody in that building has added one of them to another of them, and a total of eight pence is all that Thursday came to.

---

**And the day-book of that office stands at nine entries of a thing that was not asked, and it is the only book in that building nobody can be served out of, and it has been in that building for four years and a half and it is not going to get a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.**

A sheet with a set of market towns in the first column and a figure of persons in the second under a head that says *Date* went onto the shelf with the register and not into the press, and the shelf is the shelf and not the press, and any person at all who walks up that lane can put anything into a press in that room, and about four days were left on a day nobody in that county knows there is, and the county cannot refuse a figure and cannot check one and has not been asked to show cause, and the answer to all three of those is the same word and the instrument for the word is a day-book nine entries long that nothing in this kingdom can be served out of.

---

Nobody walked any part of that last Thursday with anybody. About four hundred people went home off that market, the tide went back at about the third hour and had been under the wharf since the tenth hour of the day before, about forty men went home from a reach, about nine men went home from a yard, a man of about fifty-five turned three bars down and up nine times and went home to a hut three hundred yards down a road, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went up that county's own road alone, on a stick he does not need.

**And in a lane about a mile off a ladder is standing against a wall with nothing under it and nothing leaning on it, and it has been standing there since a week in the middle of that autumn, and everybody in that market went home on the last day of that month not knowing it, and that lane is about a mile from the town and the reach is four hundred yards off the market and the yard is four hundred yards up the road and the barrier hut is three hundred yards down it, and about four thousand people live in that town and about four hundred of them were in a street this morning, and it has two crossings in it and the county says the two of them are four hundred yards apart, and the bell a hundred yards up that road went at the fourth hour and a half, the way it goes every week of the year, and everybody in that market went home not knowing that a county they live in has been put in a column with a figure in it about nine hundred more than the town would have said, and that nobody was asked about it, and that the only record of what this county did about it is nine lines in a book a man keeps in a lane and that nobody can be served out of, and there is a form in a carter's coat going two hundred miles in a bag on an ordinary round with a head over the first column and a head over the second and nowhere on the whole of it the name of a person.**
