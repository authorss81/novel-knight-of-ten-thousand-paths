# Chapter 0324

**The first day of Goatmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people in it, and a press room on the ground floor, and one floor up a room with a stove in it that has not been lit since the weather turned and a single window nine feet off a wall, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a copy of a roll of about four hundred people came up the stairs of that building at the second hour that morning and went into a press that is for returns, and a thing that goes into that press is a thing that went in there.**

The bundle came in the ordinary post with a mill and some oil and a lot of other things, and it came up the stairs of that building in a man's arm and not on a handcart, because it was a roll of about nine hundred lines folded in four and folded in four again and it is not a heavy thing and it goes up a stair on its own weight. It had a strip of paper round it and the strip said a place in it, written in a set of market towns the way that office writes a place, and there was no head on the strip and there was no form on it and it was not one of the four hundred a month that office prints.

**And a man of about thirty who keeps that room's books and has been in that building nine years and carries a district from one quarter to the next came out of that room at about the second hour with a handcart and then put the cart back against the wall again, which took him about nine seconds, and he did not ask what the bundle was, and nobody in that building has ever asked him about anything he has carried, and about four people on that floor have worked out since that a man who can carry a district from one quarter to the next in about four seconds spent nine seconds deciding not to push a handcart with a bundle on it and has not been asked about the nine seconds by anybody.**

“**That is not one of ours,**” he said, and he said it to the man at the table and not to anybody else, and he said it in the voice a man uses for a thing that is going to be somebody else's problem.

“**It is not one of ours,**” the woman of about thirty said, from the window, without looking round, “**and it is going into the press with the rest, because the press is where the things that are not ours go, and about four hundred a month come out of it and nine of them come back and eight of the nine have a figure in them and one has a space left empty with four lines beside it, and all nine go in because a return is a return.**”

---

**And she read it. That is the whole of the discovery and it took about four seconds.**

It is a roll copied by a man out of his own half of a thing he keeps, in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, and it is ruled in three narrow columns, and a day goes in the first, a name in the second, and one of three marks in the third, a gate, a market or a cross, and there is nothing else on it and there never has been, and about four hundred people are on it in about nine hundred lines, and about nine of those lines are for people in a generation that has never once been up the low end of that valley, and there is a gap in the first column of nine ruled days with nothing in it, and the ruling of that gap is in a different hand from the ruling of the rest of the page and is older.

“**That is a roll of a place,**” she said, at about the fourth second.

“**That is a roll of a place,**” the man of about thirty said, at about the fourth second, and the two of them said it at the same time and neither of them had said it before.

And then she put it down on the table under the window, face up, and did not enter it, and the reason she did not enter it is the plainest reason in that building and it is a shape of book.

**The book under her arm takes one form as one line and is ruled nine inches by six, and it has about eleven lines in it in about nine years, and all eleven are in her own hand, and a room writes that book and not a press. It stands on no printed series of that office, it is entered in no register, it has never been near the returns press, nobody two floors up has ever asked to see it in six years, and it is the only thing in that building anybody could be shown.**

“**There is no line in that book for a copy,**” she said. “**There is no line in that book for a book, and there is no line in it for a thing that came in the ordinary post, and there has not been one since I came into this room six years ago, and I have looked, and looking took me about a minute and a half, and nobody two floors up has ever asked me what is in it and I have never told them.**”

“**Then put it in,**” the man of about thirty said. “**A thing that is not entered is a thing that did not happen.**”

“**A thing that is not entered is a thing that did not happen,**” she said, “**and a thing that is entered is a thing that happened, and a copy is a thing that happened four hundred miles off, and if I put that roll on that page then I have said that a valley in another place is a return, and the book under your arm is the only thing in this building anybody could be shown, and once a thing is in it, it is in it.**”

---

**And a press is a press, and there is no line in that building for what a press is for, and about four hundred things a month go into the returns press and about nine of them come back out of it in about four weeks, and everything else in that drawer is still in that drawer when the building closes at the seventh hour, and nobody in that building has ever emptied that drawer.**

“**Can it go back,**” the woman said, at about the fourth hour.

“**It went up the ordinary post from a place four hundred miles off,**” the man of about thirty said. “**There is no back. A thing in this drawer has a way of leaving this building about once every four weeks and it is not by anybody's hand, and I have never once seen a thing go out of that drawer in the ordinary way, and if you want to know what goes in it you have to be the woman who reads the table, and the woman who reads the table is standing in front of the window with a book under her arm.**”

“**Then I have read the table,**” she said, “**and I have read a roll of nine hundred lines off it in about four seconds, and I have found a gap in it, and there is no line in that book for any of the three.**”

**And it went into the returns press, because a press is a press, and the man of about thirty put it in himself and shut the drawer and did not write anything about it anywhere, and about two hundred of the four hundred a month that go out of that building a year are put in by that man and about nine hundred people in that town do not know what is in that drawer.**

**A man of fifty stood at a stone on the ground floor for that whole morning and none of it reached him. Thirty-one years at that press, eleven thousand forms through his hands in that time, and not one person in that building has ever wanted to know what stands in the four spaces on any of them, and he has gone thirty-one working days without a word about anything, and nine of them have counted that.**

The strip of paper came off the roll in the drawer, in the ordinary way, when a drawer is shut on something with a strip round it, and it fell on the floor of the returns press room and it was a strip with a place in it and no head on it, and a woman of about thirty picked it up and read it and put it in her pocket, and about four people on that floor have said since that a woman put a strip of paper in her pocket in a press room, and that she has it still, and that it is not on any form and it is not in any book and there is no line in that office for a piece of paper a person has put in her pocket.

---

**And the whole of what that morning came to is set out here, and every item is a penny figure and the sum is given after the last of them and not before.**

Two sheets of that office's own printed series at a penny the sheet, which is two pence. The clerk's charge for entering one line in a book, fourpence. A quill and an ounce of ink, a penny. A sheet of clean waste paper to line the drawer of a press, twopence.

*A penny and a penny is twopence. Twopence and fourpence is sixpence. Sixpence and a penny is sevenpence, and sevenpence and twopence is nine pence, and nine pence is the whole of what that morning came to.*

Total: nine pence. **A rate for a room is not in that column and there is no rate in this town for one. The rent a salt merchant charges for two rooms over a weigh-house in a county two hundred miles off is two shillings a month for each of the pair of them, and what a small office pays for a door and a number cut into it is fourpence a year, and those are two prices of two different objects and neither of them is in the column above and nobody in this building has ever put one of them into a sum with the other of them, and a wage paid out of a private box to a man on a roll is in no column of this office and may not become one.**

---

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. It was a market day in a provincial town and about nine hundred people were on their feet in it, and not one of them knew where in that building anything was standing. A tray went round the passages of that building between the third hour and the fourth and came back for nobody, and a man of sixty took it out again and said the thing he says on that floor, which he has said for twenty-two years, and nobody in that building has ever put it to him that the thing is not the same on every round, and it is not. The presses on the second floor started at the first hour and went on to the fifth. A man with a satchel went down to a cart at the sixth hour with nine sheets of the office's own printed series and read none of them and reads none of anything.

**And a copy of a roll of about four hundred people in about nine hundred lines, with a gap of nine ruled days in it that one man ruled and left empty and that another man has filled in a hand that is not the first man's, is in a drawer in a building of four floors and seven presses in a provincial town, and it is not a return and it is not in the office's own series and it is not in the returns book, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that it has arrived, and the only record in that building that anybody could be shown is a book ruled nine inches by six with about eleven lines in it and a person at the front of every one of them, and the copy is not in that book, and the copy is in four hundred miles of country and a book in a building is not a copy of a copy, and nobody in that building has worked out which of the two is the one anybody could be shown.**
