# Chapter 0340

**The fifth day of Mudmonth, YR 317, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people on the floors of it, and one floor up from a press room there is a room with a stove in it that has not been lit since the weather turned and a single window nine feet off a bare wall, and about four hundred sheets a month go across one table under that window and are read at waist height, and in a town of about four thousand people on a river two hundred miles off a crossing four hundred yards up that county's own road carried nobody that day.**

Mudmonth is the worst month for roads in that kingdom and there was water standing in the low end of that province and about four hundred sheets a month still went across one table under one window and were read at waist height by one woman, and the weather had turned for the worse about a fortnight before and the light in that room had got worse with it.

**And the stove has not been lit and nobody has asked her to light it, and a woman of thirty is going to spend a winter in a room where the light comes up off the boards and she writes the only record anybody in that building makes by hand, and that is the whole of the cost of this block and it is not a person and it is not a city and it is a hand.**

---

**And she made that book herself, out of nine inches by six, and it has eleven lines in it in nine years and she has put all eleven of them there with her own hand, and no press in that building has ever printed a line of it, and it answers to no series of that office and stands in no register of that office, and if anybody in four floors had to put one thing in front of a stranger tomorrow it would be that, and about nine hundred people in that town could not say whose writing it was.**

**And about four hundred people in that town have been shown it never and nine hundred do not know that it exists, and nobody two floors up has ever asked to see it in six years, and four people in that building have said that a woman of thirty has been writing a book in a room with a cold stove in it for nine years that nobody has asked to read, and that a record nobody has asked to read is the only kind of record in that building that is not on a press, and that the reason it is on no press is that a press cannot make a line about a person.**

**And at about the second hour she said the thing out loud in that room, once, to two people, and neither of them answered it and neither of them is going to.**

“**I write eleven lines in nine years and I cannot read my own hand at this table in the middle of a winter, and I want a second copy, and I have wanted it for about two years.**”

It is thirty-eight words and it foots at thirty-eight, and the woman of about thirty said it in the voice a person uses for a price, and four people in that building have said that a woman of thirty said that sentence out loud in a room at the second hour of a Friday for the first time in about two years and got nothing, and that the sentence is the truest thing anybody on that floor has said in six years and that four hundred people in that town do not know she said it.

“**A copy of what,**” the man of about thirty said.

“**The one under my arm. Nine inches by six, one of your forms to a line, made by me, and on none of your series.**”

“**A copy of that is a second book and a second book is a decision,**” the man of about thirty said, and two people in that building have said that a man of thirty told a woman of thirty that the copy she wants is a decision in about eleven words, and that he is right, and that being right is not a kindness, and that the woman of thirty said **I know** in two words and went back to the table and was not thanked and was not told she was right.


**And two more things were said in that room that morning and neither of them was about a book.**

“**The tray has been round,**” the man of about thirty said, from the stair.

“**It goes round,**” the woman of about thirty said.

“**It has been round since you came in,**” the man of about thirty said, and about two people in that building have said since that a man of thirty and a woman of thirty said six words to each other about a tray on the last morning of a month, and that neither of them has ever been told by anybody that this is the whole of what they say to each other, and that a man who has said it four times in six years and has never once asked whether anybody wanted it.

“**Is that a finding,**” Osten Varel said, without looking up.

“**It is a tray,**” the man of about thirty said, and went up the stair, and about four people in that building have said since that a man of thirty-one asked a question about a tray and was told what a tray is, and that the question was not the question he asked.

---
Nobody in that room improved on it. The man of about thirty went up that stair. Osten Varel ruled a line four inches short of a right margin and did not look up, and four people in that building have said that a man of thirty-one heard a woman of thirty say a true thing about a book and carried on ruling, and that a man who copies lines all day and has never been asked what a book is for is the only person in that room who could have said anything useful about it.

---

**And at about the fourth hour a man of thirty-one carried the ninth line onto a new sheet, and that is the fifth turn of the year, and two people in that building have said that a man of thirty-one has now carried that line five times and has ruled every one of them correct and has looked at the figure standing at the end of it once, and that the one time was a Thursday in this year and it was a woman asking him whether he had looked at it, and he said no.**

The line is straight. The rule is the width the rule is. The place at the head of it is a room over something in a town of about four thousand people and it has been there five turns, and the figure in the second column has changed twice, and the figure in the fifth column has changed twice, and the head over the second column says *Date* and the head over the fifth says *How often*, and there is no place on the whole of that sheet where the name of a person goes, and a man of thirty-one has carried that line five times and has not been asked by anybody in that building which of the two he would rather be, and the sheet went out of that building on the Friday after and it goes round nine counties.

**And a man of about thirty carried nine districts forward that day in about four seconds each, and the ninth of them was the one he read out loud to a room on a Sunday a fortnight before, and he carried it, and four people in that building have said that a man of thirty has carried that thing five times now, asked one question about it in a room, got nothing, and not asked a second, and that a man who has asked one question in nine years and got nothing is a man who has worked out what an answer costs and is not going to pay it twice.**

**And at the ground floor a man of about fifty stood at a stone and set a page and did not look up, and there is a drawer at the end of that room with a word in it that has lain there for two years, and he gave it the length of a count of four about a month ago and left it shut and said nothing whatever, and nobody in that building has taken it out and nobody is going to and about two people in that building have noticed the cold stove two doors from the long chamber and have not connected it to a man.**

Nobody in that town kept anybody company on that Friday. Four hundred sheets went across the table under the one window, and the woman who read them read them at waist height because the light comes up off the boards, and about nine hundred people outside that building did not know that. Down at the bottom of the stair the presses on the floor above were going from the first hour to the fifth, and at the fourth hour a tray came out of a passage empty and a man of about sixty carried it back down the corridor saying the one thing he says, and nobody in that building has ever asked him whether it is the same thing every time, and it is not.

---

**And two hundred miles off, on the same day, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, the market was shut and a tide went back at about the third hour and drew the stones clean, and a crossing four hundred yards up that county's own road carried nobody, as it has carried nobody for four years, and nobody in that county has ever called it anything.**

There is no owner and no money and no third seat and no glass in the sill, and the road over it is a county road and it is the only road out of that town to the north, and about nine people crossed that market that morning and none of them went up that road. Nobody in that county has dug at it and nobody is going to, and the one person in that county who ever went near what is under it cannot get up a bank and is four hundred miles from being able to try and is not going to try.

**And under the fourth step of an outside stair at the north end of that market there is a slip of paper about nine inches by six with a figure at the head of it and nine marks under that figure that are not even, and it is nine, and it said nine before that Friday and it says nine now, and a count of askings is not a price and is not a count of persons and is added to nothing, and a book in a room forty feet up that lane, a book nothing in this kingdom can be served out of, stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked and is not going to get a tenth, and a press in the corner of that room has been standing open for six weeks and a half and a figure of persons standing against that county went back down that lane in a bag five days ago.**

On that Friday nobody in that town had anybody else to walk with. Two hundred of the four thousand went out of their houses and the rest stayed in. Nine men came in off a mile of fields at the wet hour. A mill turned at the low end. Five days earlier a bag had gone out of a lane in a hand that is not the office's, and by that Friday the bag was somewhere on a road and about four thousand people in that town did not know it had ever been in the town at all.

**And about nine people went up the county's own road that morning and none of them went as far as the crossing, and two people in that lane have said that a road which four thousand people use and nobody has any reason to go up is still a road, and that about nine people go up it on a Friday and come back down it and that is the whole of the traffic on it in this county.**

**And at the end of that road, past a yard, there is a press in a room that belongs to a yard and cannot be served with anything by anybody, and there is a bundle in it that has been there for years with nothing written on the outside of it, and nobody in that county has asked what is in it and nobody is going to, and about two people in that lane have said that a county of about four thousand people now has one press that anybody at all can open and one press that nobody at all can open, and that the two of them are two hundred yards apart, and that nobody in that county has ever put those two facts near each other.**

**And a county of about four thousand people has answered a figure of persons standing against it with nothing, and has been asked nothing since, and has now handed a paper back, and has a page under a stair that says nine, and a day-book on a shelf that says nine, and a crossing four hundred yards up its own road that carries nobody and has carried nobody for four years, and about four thousand people live in that town and the last of them went in about the eighth hour.**
