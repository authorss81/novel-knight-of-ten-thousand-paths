# Chapter 0332

**The first day of Thawmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people on the floors of it, and two floors above the room with the stove in it there is a long chamber with a long table in it and a record on that table whose head carries a date in the spring, and the date has gone, and about nine hundred people in that town went out that morning without knowing that a woman had gone up two flights of stairs for the first time in six years and come down again.**

That town trades on two days in the week and this was one of them, and about nine hundred people were standing in it by the second hour, and not one of the nine hundred came within a hundred yards of that building or went up its stairs.

**That chamber is the same shape as the room with the stove in it and it is not the same room.** One flight of stairs separates the first floor from the second and one more separates the second from the third, and a woman who works at a table under a window on the first floor and a man who reads a record on a long table on the third floor do the same trade and keep two different habits, and there is nothing in the whole of that office which carries a thing from one of those rooms to the other, and about four people in that building have worked that out separately in about two years and have never said it to each other. The woman has been in that building six years and has been up those stairs twice.

She went up them at the fourth hour with the roll from the Friday under her arm. It had been standing against the wall under that window for three days, and a thing that came off a shelf two floors up goes back onto the shelf it came off, and about four people in that building have said since that a woman carried two hundred and forty lines up two flights of stairs to put a roll back on a shelf and that is the whole of her reason for having gone up.

---

**And the chamber was not empty, and the one person in it is a clerk of that office who has a bad wrist and does his work with the good hand, and nobody on that floor can say how long he has been at that table.**

He is at the far end of it from the door. At the near end there is a record with a head standing over it, and the head says that a schedule is to be complete before a date, and the date is in the spring, and the date has gone. Beside it is a second sheet with about two hundred and thirty lines ruled on it and every one of the lines empty, and two hundred and thirty is the number of lines there is left, and it was two hundred and thirty in the spring.

**And about nine people in that building have read that head since the weather turned, and four of them have worked out that the date on it has gone, and not one of them has said so to anybody, and there is no column in that building for a head that is out of date, and a head is the one part of a form a person cannot correct without making the form a different form.**

The nib went down onto the stone and round and round and then he lifted it and looked at it and did it again, and then a third time, and about four people on that floor have clocked all three, and not one of them has said a word to any of the others who clocked them, and a man who has sharpened a pen three times before the fourth hour has taken in something about that floor and is not going to be the one to say what.

---

“**Is this the shelf it came off,**” the woman of about thirty said, from nine feet off the near end of that table.

“**It is,**” the clerk said, and did not stop writing.

“**Then I will put it on it.**”

“**You may,**” the clerk said, and two people on that floor have said since that a man with a bad wrist gave a stranger permission to put two hundred and forty lines back on a shelf in four words without putting his pen down, and that the four words were not a courtesy and were not an instruction, and that a man who will not put a pen down for two floors of his own building is a man who has settled what his morning is.

It took about nine seconds. The shelf is nine feet off the wall behind the long table and the roll went onto it with the head of it square with the front of the shelf, and about two people in that building have said since that a woman squared a roll against a shelf nobody looks at and that is the trade and not a decision.

**And then she did not go out, and there is a thing about that, and two people in that building have said since that a woman went up two flights of stairs to put a roll back on a shelf and stood in that chamber nine minutes longer than the putting of it took, and that the chamber is the only room in that building anybody has ever been able to carry a fact up to, and that she came down with it and has not put it anywhere.**

---

“**What happens to a schedule when the date on it has gone,**” she said.

“**It goes to somebody,**” the clerk said.

“**Somebody who is not in this building.**”

“**Then it goes to somebody who is not in this building,**” the clerk said, and four people in that building have said since that a man with a bad wrist agreed with a stranger that a form goes to a person instead of expiring, and that neither of them said anything else about it, and that nine people in that building have read that head about nine times since the weather turned and have got a different answer out of it from that one.

**And the man who would have taken it is not in that building. There is a room two doors along that corridor with a stove in it, and the stove has been cold about two months, and the room's owner has not set foot in the building since a Friday in the autumn before last, and not one person in four floors has put the question of where he went to anybody, and four people have clocked the cold stove and two of them have never once put it together with a man.**

“**He is not here,**” the woman of about thirty said.

“**He is not here,**” the clerk said.

“**Then who has it.**”

“**A thing that has gone out of the ordinary way has not stopped going, and there is no column on the whole of that series for the difference between those two,**” the clerk said, and sharpened the pen for the third time, and four people in that building have said since that a clerk with a bad wrist said the true thing about a form that had gone out of the ordinary way in about nine seconds and then went on with a sheet of two hundred and thirty empty lines in front of him and nothing to copy.

---

**And the corridor outside that chamber is nine feet wide and has two doors on it, and one of them is the door the clerk came in by and the other one is shut, and behind the other one is a room with a stove in it, and nobody in that building has opened that door in about two months.**

She stood in the corridor about a minute. There is a bench in that corridor that people put coats on and there are about nine coats on it in the winter, and the stove behind the shut door is four feet off that bench, and about four people in that building have said since that a stove four feet off a bench with nine coats on it is a stove that could be lit by anybody who came down that corridor, and that nobody in that building has come down that corridor for the sake of a stove in about two months, and that the reason is not the stove.

**And that is the whole of what she got out of going up, and it is two things and neither of them is a thing she can use, and about two people in that building have said since that a woman of thirty went up two flights of stairs on a market Saturday with two hundred and forty lines under her arm and came back down with a shut door and a date in the spring and nothing whatever that anybody could enter anywhere.**

She had a roll that told her a room in a town four hundred miles off was in a column with a figure against it in two of six spaces, and a man on a third floor who could have said where those figures came from, and a bench in a corridor that four people in that building could have lit a stove beside. **And the three of those are in three different rooms and the three of them are about one thing, which is a place, and there is no line in the whole of that office that will take a place and two figures and a shut door in one entry, and about four people in that building have worked that out in about two years and have never once been in a room with each other to say it.**

---

**And then she said the thing she has never said to anybody in that building in six years, and it is about the two rooms and not about the room she is standing in, and the man with the bad wrist let her get all the way through it before he answered.**

“**I work in a room where a person can be asked a thing,**” she said, “**and I have carried a roll up two flights of stairs this morning because a man said it came off a shelf, and you have been in a chamber for nine years where a person can be told a thing and not asked one, and those two of us have been in one building the whole of that time and about nine people on that building have worked out that we do not meet, and I have been up here twice in six years and I counted them both times.**”

“**I know you have,**” the clerk said.

“**Then you know the number.**”

“**I know the number and I have not counted it and it is not in a column and it is not going to be,**” the clerk said, and about four people in that building have said since that a man with a bad wrist let a stranger say the true thing about his own floor in about nine seconds and then said the truer thing about himself in about nine more, and that a man who has not counted the number of times a woman has come into his chamber has decided not to know it, and that deciding not to know is a thing a person can do with a hand and cannot do with a form.

**And that is the whole of the discovery, and it is two rooms in one building, and nobody in either of them has ever put them in one sentence, and about four people in that town two hundred miles off from the county a figure of persons is standing against have never heard of either room.**

She came down those stairs at the sixth hour and went back to the table under the window, and the room with the stove in it was exactly as she had left it, and about four hundred people in that town had gone home off a market in the meantime and five hundred had not gone anywhere.

About nine hundred people stood in that market on that Saturday and not one of them came within a hundred yards of that building. Nobody inside it went out of it with anybody. The presses on the middle floor began before the market opened and stopped about an hour after it shut. At the bottom of the stair, where anybody coming down has to pass, a man of about fifty set pages and did not turn round for one woman in six years, and she went up past him and came down again and he set pages, and about four people on that floor have wondered about that and about none of them has ever said it to him.

**And a roll of about two hundred and forty lines is on a shelf nine feet off a wall in a chamber two floors above the room with the stove in it, and nine of those lines are one room in a town of about four thousand people, and a man at the far end of that table has read the head of that record nine times since the weather turned and has never once said what is standing on it, and the date went in the spring, and the two hundred and thirty empty lines in front of him are the same two hundred and thirty that were empty in the spring, and there is nobody in that building who can put those two facts in a column together, and there is nobody in that county who has been asked.**
