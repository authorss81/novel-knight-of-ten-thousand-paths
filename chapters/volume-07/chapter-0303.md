# Chapter 0303

**The fourth day of Wolfmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the seventh hour, and a room over a cross four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, in a valley, with about nine people on benches and about thirty in the room, and twenty-nine of those benches bolted to the floor in four rows, a number cut into the end of every one of them, with a hundred and forty cut into the end of that one at the back of the right-hand row, and about four hundred people in that valley of whom about ninety are known to somebody who has written them down twice and all of them are not known to any instrument at all.**

The room wanted a number. That was the whole of the business in front of the nine that morning and it had been in front of them for about eleven days, and a room over a cross in a valley of four hundred people wants a number about four times in a year, and the four things it wants numbers for are the water and the wood and the two spans of the bridge and the burial ground at the top of the lane, and about nine people in that room have said since that a room which has to ask a person for a figure is a room and a room which has a figure is a bench, and a bench is not sitting in that room this morning, the twenty-nine of them are bolted down in four rows and there is an aisle four feet wide between the two middle ones that has gone three generations without being stood in.

The bridge was the reason. The woman of thirty-six who keeps two hundred and forty feet of stone over the water at the low end had said that the second span wanted forty feet of stone taken out of it and put in where the channel had moved, and the room had asked her for a figure of what the stone would cost and she had asked them for a figure of how many people there were, and the room had no figure of how many people there were, and there is a thing in that valley that the figure could have come out of and it is a foot long and it is not a number.

---

The roll is the only record any of them has of who is in that valley, and it is kept by a man of about fifty whose name is Marek Ollen, and it is one long sheet folded in four, ruled in three columns, and the three of them are a day, a name, and a mark that is a gate or a market or a cross, and there is nothing else on it and there never has been, and about four hundred people stand on that sheet in about nine hundred lines.

“**There is no figure in it,**” Marek Ollen said, before anybody had asked him, because he had been asked twice in eleven days and he had known what the third one would be.

“**Then we cannot use it,**” a man said.

“**You can use it,**” Marek Ollen said. “**It is the only thing in four hundred miles that knows the answer and it cannot be made to say it out loud in one line, and to make it say it out loud in one line a man has to decide which of nine hundred lines is a person here now and which of them is a person who was here, and that is not a thing a man does in a room with nine people in it in front of thirty.**”

“**It is a thing you could do.**”

“**It is a thing I could do in about nine years,**” Marek Ollen said, and nobody improved on that, and about two of the thirty in that room have worked out that a man who keeps a roll of everybody told a room that the roll could be turned into a number in about nine years and that he had about nine years of it in a drawer in this building, and that the drawer is in this building and the room is over a cross and the two of them have been about four feet apart for the whole of that time.

---

**And the reason a man who has kept a roll for nineteen years cannot turn it into a number in a morning is a second roll, and the second roll is a copy of the first, and the copy is nine years old and is kept because the first one gets wet at the low end every time the water comes up.**

He had made the copy himself. Once a season, out of the first one, at the back of that room, on the trestle, with a rule and a straight edge and about four days of the season given to it, and he had done that about thirty-six times, and the two of them sat in the same box on the same shelf within a hand's width of each other and about four people in that valley had known about the second one for most of those nine years and about two hundred of that generation had not.

And he had wanted somebody to ask him which of the two was right ever since the first season he made the copy, and nobody had ever asked him, and about four people in that valley have said since that the only reason a man of about fifty in that valley had gone on keeping two books instead of one for nine years is that a room could not be got to say out loud that it wanted a figure, and that a man who is not asked a question cannot answer it and keeps making his copy to be ready for the day somebody does.

“**I have written down everybody twice and once was enough and now I have to know which of the two I was right about,**” Marek Ollen said, and it is twenty-four words, and it is all he has said out loud about that box in nine years, and he said it to a man with a limp and not to the room, and he has not said it again in that valley and is not going to.

And nobody said anything to it. A woman of thirty-five could have said something to it and was not in that room, and about four people in that valley have said since that a man said a true thing about two books in a room with nine people in it and got nine faces and no answer, and that the true thing is about two books and not about any of the nine, and that a room in which a true thing gets nine faces and no answer is a room in which a true thing will be said again by somebody.

---

They put the two side by side on the trestle, and the trestle was under the book and the book went under the trestle, and the two rolls lay open on it for about four minutes with about nine people and about thirty watching.

**And they were the same, and they were not the same, and the not the same was in nine places a season and the same places were always the same, and that is what nine years of a man's own hand does.**

The nine places were days. In the first roll there were nine days ruled across in the day column with nothing at all in them, and the ruling was Marek Ollen's and it was old enough in one of them to have a thumbprint in the lime of the ruling where somebody leaned on the table, and in the copy those same nine days were not ruled and were not empty. **They were full.** The copy had names in them and a mark against each and the copy had been made out of the first one, and a man who copies a sheet does not invent nine days, and a man who copies a sheet does not leave nine days out, and one of those two things had happened and nobody in that room could say which.

“**I ruled those,**” Marek Ollen said. “**I ruled them and I put nothing in them. I have done that about four times in nine years and it is in the first one and not in the second and I do not know which of the two of us did that.**”

“**Which one is the real one.**”

“**That is what I have been asking this room for about nine years,**” Marek Ollen said, “**and this room is the reason I have two books.**”

---

**And there is a third thing in that room that morning, and it is the thing nobody put on the table, and it is the thing the two books would have told anybody who had known to look for it.**

A roll of who is in a valley has a day and a name and a mark. A woman of about thirty with a slate came to the top of that lane that morning with a line to enter and a question to ask, and the question was about a name, and the name was on the roll in about four places and had been on it for about eleven years.

“**It stays on,**” Sefa Tamas said. “**I want it to stay on and I am not asking for anything else. It is a name and it is on the sheet and I do not want it off the sheet. That is the whole of it.**”

And she said that to the roll and not to Marek Ollen, and she said it with the chalk against the slate and her hand on the edge of the sheet, and about nine people in that room had heard a want said in that valley and about two hundred of the ninth generation at the back of it had not heard one and were not going to, and a woman who carries a slate for eleven years can say a want to a piece of paper without anybody in the room having to answer her, and there are four hundred people in that valley and about nine of them have ever said a want out loud in that building.

**And nobody entered the forgetting, and the reason is not a decision and has never been a decision, and there is no column on that sheet for it and there never has been, and a roll that has a day and a name and a mark has no place to put down that a woman asked a man in a yard for the name of her own son and could not get it.**

The man of fifty-eight could have said so. He is fifty-eight and he has been in that valley all his life and about two hundred people of the ninth generation have come down off that hill to that room in about eleven years and he has spoken to nine of them, and the two of the nine he spoke to could not tell him who they were, and he wrote neither of them down, and the roll in that box has about nine hundred lines in it and there is nothing in any of them that says that a man of fifty-eight did not know who two people were.

The one thing about it that anybody in that room said was said by the man of fifty, and he said it to the sheet.

“**It does not go in,**” he said. “**A person is on this sheet or a person is not on this sheet and there is no third thing and there is not going to be a third thing, and if I put one of these in it I have to take something out, and I am not going to take a name out of four hundred people to make room for a thing nobody has a word for in this valley.**”

And nobody improved on that either, and two people in that valley have never worked out that the keeper of the only record in four hundred miles said the true thing about a third thing to a sheet of paper in front of nine people, and that the true thing is that there are two states a person can be in and one of them is on a sheet, and that every one of them is in the one that is not and about nine of them have known it for about a year.

---

**Nobody walked any part of that day with him. The water came up at the low end and the mill went on and about two hundred feet of wharf had nothing against them, and a market on two hundred yards of street stood down for the day because that town trades four days out of seven and this was not one of them, and a glass works at the low end had nine men in it and a bread oven at the top of the lane went through its second batch, and the whole of that valley went about a morning that had cost nine days of somebody's life to make a copy of, and not one of them knows that two books are lying open on a trestle in a room over a cross with nine people and about thirty standing round them, and the nine days in one of them are ruled and empty and the same nine days in the other one are full, and there is no instrument in that valley that could tell a person which of the two of those two books is the one that happened.**

**And the asking is not on either of them. There is a roll of who is in a valley and there has never been a roll of who is known to whom, and nobody in that valley can be told which of the two they would have to be asked for, and neither of those two is a thing anybody in that valley has ever set out to keep.**
