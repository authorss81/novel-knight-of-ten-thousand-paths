# Chapter 0327

**The fifteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people in it, and a press room on the ground floor at the end of a passage, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in that press room for about a quarter of an hour on that morning and had a question he has carried since an earlier year and did not ask it, and that nobody in that building has asked that man anything in thirty-one years and nobody is going to.**

He came up that lane on the sixth of that month and it is two hundred miles from the county's own road to that town and he is a man who cannot get up a bank, and he did it in four days and slept in two barns and one night against a wall, and he has a stick he does not need and has never once put down, and about four people in that town have seen a man with a limp come off a road at the second hour on a market day and go into a building, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that the man at the stone on the ground floor of it has been in that room for thirty-one years.

**At the fourth hour that man turned his eyes to a drawer on the wall at the end of that room. There is a word in it. He gave it the length of a count of four, kept his hands where they were, left it shut, and said nothing whatever.**

Nobody in that building has taken it out in the two years it has lain at the end of that wall. It came out of a case and went onto a form once, in the second month of a year now two years back, on a list that went to nine counties, and nobody has asked for it a second time and nobody is going to, and what anybody in that building could say about it if anybody asked is that it is in a drawer.

**And that boy was nineteen when he set it, and the metal in that drawer was cast a long time before he was born, and he has not stood at that stone since the second month of a year now two years back, and about four people in that building have noticed that nobody has ever gone after him to ask where he went.**

Type is cast, and a cast sort is older than the hand that picks it up, and the one in that drawer is a piece of metal that was in a fount before a boy of nineteen was born. About two people in that building have worked out since that a man at a stone thinks about that drawer about twice a year and has never said one word about it out loud, and that the thinking and the not-saying are half an hour apart and that both of them are his.

He is not at the stone. He went up a floor in the world in the second month of a year now two years back and has not come down since, and the bench he used to stand at in that room has been standing empty for about two years, and two people in that building have seen the empty bench and two of them have put it beside the drawer and neither of them has ever mentioned it.

**And that boy put one question about that drawer in that room once, and he got three sentences back, and four people in that building have worked out since that a boy of nineteen who asked whether a word was a real word, and was answered that it is a word, has been handed the correct answer and has heard something else standing inside it, and that the only man in a press room whose living turns on the difference between those two things is a boy doing his time at the stone. He had eleven weeks to serve before the next sitting then and he wanted to be a journeyman, and nobody answered him about the eleven weeks and nobody answered him about the word, and he is not at the stone.**

“**Do you want the second one,**” a man said, at the fourth hour.

“**Leave it,**” the man of fifty said.

And about two people in that building have said since that a man has said leave it twice in two years to four different people and that the four of them took it for an instruction about a drawer and it was not one, and that what it is about is a thing nobody in that building has ever said out loud, and that a man who says leave it twice is a man who has thought about it more than twice.

---

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp was in that press room from about the fourth hour to the fourth hour and a quarter, and he had a question, and it is the question that the whole of that building has been built out of for six years without anybody saying it out loud, and he did not ask it.**

It is a small question and there is no figure in it and no column in it, and eleven thousand forms have gone through that man's hands in thirty-one years and nobody in that building has ever wanted to know what stands in the four spaces on one of them, and nobody has ever wanted to know whether he reads a filled form down to the bottom, and that is the one thing his trade asks of him and the one thing no office in this kingdom has ever asked of anybody.

He has waited two years to be asked. Four people in four counties have worked out since that a man waited two years to be thanked for a true thing about a column and was thanked once, in a margin, in about four seconds, in a hand that was not the office's, and that the thanking turned the true thing into a finding, and that a man who has been thanked once for a true thing about a column is the most dangerous man in a press room and that nobody in that building has worked out why.

“**You have been in this building before,**” a man said, at the passage end, to nobody in particular.

“**Twice,**” the man with the limp said.

**And a man of thirty came down that stair at a quarter past the fourth hour for about nine seconds and a half, the way he comes down that stair, and he said a thing about a tray and went back up before the sentence was finished, and four people in that building have been saying since that a man who carries a district from one quarter to the next in about four seconds has asked four times in six years for the tray to stop going round that passage and that nobody in that building has ever told him it is not going to, and that on that day he came down and said the tray sentence and did not say the other thing, and that nine of them in that building have not worked out which of the two things he did not say.**

“**It has been round again,**” he said.

“**It goes round,**” the man with the limp said.

And that is all that was said to him, and he went up, and about two people on that floor have said since that a stranger in the press room below agreed with a man about a tray and did not ask him anything else, and that a man who says the tray has been round and a man who says it goes round have between them the whole of what anybody in that building has ever said about it.


The man of fifty did not look up. He has not looked up at anybody who came down that stair for a year and the last time anybody noticed it he said nothing about it either, and there is no version of a man of fifty at a stone in that room in which he says one word, and nine people in that building have noticed that and four of them think about it twice a year.

**And the man with the limp stood about nine feet off that stone for about a quarter of an hour with the question in his mouth and did not ask it, and then he said it to the room instead, which is not the same thing and he knew it was not the same thing while he was saying it.**

“**What is in the four places,**” he said, to a press room, at about a quarter past the fourth hour.

And nine men in that room are pressmen and clerks and a man of thirty who keeps the books one floor up, and four of the nine heard it, and the man of fifty at the stone heard all of it, and nobody answered him, and about two people in that building have worked out since that a stranger came two hundred miles to a press room and put a question to a room instead of to the one man in it who could have answered it, and that the room did not answer it and the man at the stone was not asked again and is not going to be, and that a man who has been not-saying a thing for two years has now found out that being not-asked is the same condition as being not-asked and that it costs the same.

**And that is the cost of it, and nobody in that building has written it down and nobody is going to: the one person in thirty-one years who had the standing to ask that man what is in the four places on one of his forms was standing about nine feet off his stone at about the fourth hour on a market day and did not ask him, and he had come two hundred miles to do it, and the reason he did not ask is not a reason anybody in that press room knows and is never going to be told by him, and the two of them are the only two people in that building who know that the question was there and both of them have gone on with about four hundred forms a month between them and neither of them has said one word about it since.**

---

**And eight of every nine sheets of that series have nothing standing in the space in front of the name of the place, and about four hundred of them go out of that building every month into nine counties and four hundred and nine parish rolls, and about four hundred of them come back, and one of the nine that went out in the second month of the year before last has something in that space that was not struck again in the second month of the year before last and is not going to be struck again, and about nine hundred people in that town will not find out about it from anybody, and there is no line on the whole of that series, in any column, in any line, at the head or at the foot, for the name of the person whose hand it was.**

And that hand is not that press's. It writes smaller than a hand of fifty writes and steadier than a hand of nineteen writes, and two people in that town have said since that a hand sitting between those two has been taught to fill in a form, and that some two hundred people in that building fill in forms every day for offices that are not this one, and that not one of them has ever set foot in that press room, and that nobody has worked out whose it is and that nobody is going to.

**And at about the sixth hour the man with the limp was in the passage with a man of about sixty who runs a tray into it and out of it and has done for twenty-two years and has never once been asked whether anybody in the building wanted it.**

“**It has been round four times,**” the man of about sixty said, “**and nobody has wanted it.**”

“**Then it is a good tray,**” the man with the limp said.

And the man of about sixty said the one sentence he says on that floor and has said for twenty-two years, and about four people in that building have been saying since that nobody has ever asked him whether it is the same sentence every time, and it is not, and that a man who is agreed with about a tray by a stranger is not a man who has been thanked for anything and knows it.

---

Nobody walked any part of that day with him. About nine hundred people went through an ordinary market day in a provincial town. A tray went into a passage at the third hour and was back at the fourth with nothing on it for anybody, and a man of sixty took it out again and said the thing he says on that floor and has said for twenty-two years, and about four hundred of that office's own printed series went out of that building in the ordinary post, and a man of thirty-one with a satchel took that day's sheets down to a cart at the sixth hour and read none of them and reads none of anything, and a copy of a roll of four hundred people that came up those stairs in the ordinary post two weeks before is still in the returns press in a drawer that is not emptied, and nobody in that building has opened that drawer either.
