# Chapter 0305

**The fourteenth day of Wolfmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, and a long room at the top of a lane in a city of about four hundred people four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, with eleven chairs down the long side of a long table and eleven chairs down the other one and a clerk at the near end of it, and about two hundred yards of street below that lane with a market standing in it, and about four hundred people in that city who do not know that a roll of everybody in it was copied that afternoon and put into a press in a building nobody in it has been inside.**

The room sits four days out of seven and it has sat that often for as long as anybody in it has been alive, and it has four things on a table on any morning it sits, and about nine people take a chair out of twenty-two, and the eleven on each long side is so that a man who comes in late can sit where he likes and not have anybody get up.

**And the clerk shuts the ink pot nine times a week, and that is the only speed in that room, and a thing that has not been written down in that room has not happened in that room, and the clerk has been the clerk of that room for about eleven years and shuts it nine times a week whether or not anything has been decided, and about nine people in that city have worked out that he shuts it more often than the room can possibly need it, and about four of them have said so out loud to him and he has not answered any of the four.**

The first thing on that table was a matter about the second span of the bridge and it took nine minutes, and the second was a matter about a byre at the top of the water and it took four, and about two hundred people on the street below that lane did a market while nine people in a room at the top of it got through two things in a morning, and nobody in that city has ever worked out whether the room is slow or the street is fast.

---

**And the third thing on that table was a copy, and it was proposed by a man of about sixty who keeps the roll of the crossing, and he proposed it in the ordinary way and his reason was his own and it was not about the Crown.**

“**My half and Marek's half do not agree,**” the man of about sixty said. “**They have not agreed for about nine years and there is no way in four hundred miles of country to find out which of the two of us is right about a day in a season. I want a third book that neither of us wrote, so that where the two of us differ somebody who is not us can see it.**”

“**Who would hold it,**” a woman said.

“**A man at a stone in a town two hundred miles off holds about eleven thousand of them a year and has never once been asked what is in the four places,**” the man of about sixty said, “**and he would hold a third one and he would not read it.**”

And about two of the nine in that room have said since that a man of about sixty asked a room for a copy of a record and gave the reason that the man who would keep it would not read it, and that the reason is correct and that it is the reason nobody in that valley has ever given for anything, and that a room which is told the truth about its own record by the man who is responsible for it is a room in which the truth is already in the room before the vote.

**And a man of about fifty who keeps the roll of the valley said the fear, and the fear is correct, and it is about a copy and not about the Crown, and nobody in that city knows it was said.**

“**Here is what a copy does,**” Marek Ollen said. “**It does not get anything out of the sheet and it does not put anything in. It takes what is on there and it takes it away from the people who are on there, and after about two years the sheet is what anybody in that valley knows about the two of us, and the two of us are still here. That is not a thing a form does to people. That is a thing people do with a form.**”

“**Nobody has said that would happen,**” a man said.

“**I have not said it would happen,**” Marek Ollen said. “**I have said what a copy does. It is the only thing on that table with a use in it and the use is that it can be acted on, and the second thing about it is that when it can be acted on it is acted on, and there is nobody in that town who is going to be asked.**”

And the clerk wrote both of those down and shut the ink pot about four minutes later, and about four people in that room have said since that a man said a true thing about a copy in a room that was about to vote on a copy and did not say it once, and that a true thing said before a vote is a thing the room can hear once and then do what it was going to do.

---

**And then the woman of about thirty with the slate said the thing, and it is all she said that afternoon, and nobody improved on it and nobody has said it since.**

“**A copy of a person is a person who was there,**” Sefa Tamas said.

Nobody in that room gave a figure for it and nobody has since, and a sentence that has no figure against it is the only kind of sentence that cannot be argued down on the ground of its length.

The room did not take it as a finding. It took it as a thing one woman said, and about two people in that room have worked out that a woman who has carried a slate for eleven years and who has wanted one thing off a piece of paper for about a year stood up in a room where a copy was being voted on and said the true thing about it in the plain way, and that she said it to the table and not to the man of about fifty, and that about two hundred people in the ninth generation in that valley have never been told what she said.

**And the room split, and it was five and four, and the clerk wrote the two numbers in the margin of the minute on the same line under one head of his own invention, and shut the ink pot, and did not enter a reason, and four people in that city have said since that a room of about nine people in a city of about four hundred people took its first recorded vote in about eleven years and it was about itself.**

---

**And a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work came to that room in the middle of it and got nothing, and two people in that city have worked out since that he was the only person in it who came with a thing he wanted and that a room which has a copy in front of it will hear about a copy and nothing else, and that a man with sixteen men and no work has been in that room about four times in a year and has got nothing out of it about four times.**

“**Four of them have nowhere to work this season,**” the man of thirty-four said. “**And I am not asking for anything for me. I am asking for a thing for the four of them and I have asked for it four times and there is a book in this building with nine hundred lines in it and not one of the nine hundred is a line about a man with nowhere to work.**”

“**That is not what this book is,**” Marek Ollen said.

“**I know what it is,**” the man of thirty-four said, and sat down, and was not asked anything else, and the clerk wrote the whole of it down and shut the ink pot, and about four people in that room have said since that a man said the truest sentence anybody has said in that room in about eleven years and the truest sentence in a room is the one that gets no answer at all.

And the clerk read that part of the minute back once, at the end, and only once, and the reading took about a minute and a quarter, and nobody in that room asked for it again and it was not offered again.

---

**Nobody walked any part of that day with him.**

**And the four who voted against it went out of that room at the eighth hour and about two hundred of the ninth generation were on the street below, and none of the four said anything to anybody about the vote, and the vote is the only thing that room has ever recorded about itself and nobody in that city knows that it happened.**

**And nobody in that city can call that room. It sits four days out of seven because four days out of seven is what it has always done, and a person who wants a thing put in front of it goes up a lane and waits on a step and is put in front of it after the next thing, and about nine people in that valley have asked for a room in their own time and got it in about nine weeks on average, and about four hundred of them never got it and about two hundred of the ninth generation have never asked.**

**And the copy was made anyway, and the room did not make it, and nothing that the room did that afternoon stopped it.**

The man of about sixty had asked for a third book. The room had split five and four against it, and the four included the woman with the slate, and a decision of a room in that city does not reach a man's own book, because a book a man keeps is not a thing nine people in a room have anything to do with, and about four people in that valley have worked out that a room which has voted about a copy has no more authority over the copy than a market has over what a man grows in his own ground.

He made it between the fifth hour and the eighth hour, on a trestle, with a rule and a straight edge, out of his own half and not out of the keeper's, and it took about two days and it was good work and about four people in that valley have said that a man of about sixty made the best copy anybody in that valley has ever seen and that this is the whole of the trouble with it.

And he put it into a press in a room over a cross, and the press has a door and a bar and no shelf anybody looks at, and it went in at the eighth hour of a market day, and the whole of that city went home from a market that day not knowing that there were two books in that building and not one.

**And that is the cost of it, and it is not a discovery and it is not a decision anybody took, and it is this: a copy made out of one half of a valley's record and set in a press will be held against the other half, and where the two differ the copy will be the one anybody can pick up, and in nine places a season the two differ, and in nine places a season the two differ in a way that a copy cannot show anybody, because the difference is nine ruled days that one man ruled and left empty and that a man of about sixty has since put nine names into in a hand that is not the first man's.**

And the man of fifty ruled those nine days and put nothing in them and has said so out loud once, in a room, to a man with a limp, and nobody in that valley can be shown the difference between a man writing nine days and a man not writing them, and about nine of them know that both of those are in one building four feet apart, **and the sheet in the press is the one that will be picked up, and it is in a room over a cross, and there is no column on either of the two of them for which of them was written by the man whose name is not on either.**
