# Chapter 0304

**The ninth day of Wolfmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, and a mill at the low end of the water in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, and about two hundred feet of wharf above it, and a made road about four hundred yards long with a standing finish on the end of it, and about nine feet of grass and the two stones that hold the finish at the end of that road, and about four hundred people in that valley of whom about nine know that anything was said at a glass works that morning.**

The mill is at the low end of the water and the man at the wheel has not left that wheel since before the water came, and he came out into the cold for about four minutes on an evening in the last month of the last year for no reason anybody has ever been able to find out, and he has done it about twice since and each time for about four minutes, and about four hundred people in that valley have noticed the cold coming out of that door about four times and about nine of them have said out loud that the man at the mill is a man who comes out.

**“Is he out,**” a boy at the mill door said.

“**He is at his wheel,**” a man said.

The water was down and the second span of the bridge was out of use and the woman keeps the second span and has two hundred and forty feet of stone on it, of which forty feet is in a yard at the low end in nine pieces, and the tide of that river is nothing like the tide under a wharf in a county two hundred miles off and nobody there has ever compared the two.

---

**And the road is four hundred yards long and it goes nowhere, and it is the only straight thing in that valley.**

It was taken out of the slope by hand in a wet summer by a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work, and it has a crown on it about the width of a scythe's back and the crown holds the rain on one side and sends it down the other into a ditch that was dug for it at the same time by the same sixteen men, and the ditch has never once been out of the bank in three years. It comes off the lane at the top of the valley and goes four hundred yards down the side of the hill and stops.

At the end of it there is a standing finish, and the standing finish is a thing a man builds when he intends the road to go further and is a thing about two feet high and about four feet wide, and there are two stones in it and the two stones are squared and dry-laid and nothing is set in them but lime, and the lime has gone hard in three winters and it is holding. There is about nine feet of grass on the far side of the finish and then the slope, and the slope goes down about two hundred feet to the river, and there is a bridge at the bottom of it.

**Nobody in that valley has ever asked that road whether it goes anywhere, and it does not, and there is no law about it and no office and no second roll anywhere in four hundred miles with a column for a road, and about four people in that valley have said since that a road dug by the people who use it is the only road in the valley and that about nine men out of sixteen had nowhere to work that season and that a man with sixteen men and no work and a wet season and a hill in front of him is a man who will make a road.**

“**It goes down to the water,**” the man of thirty-four said, when somebody asked him what it was for, and two people have worked out since that a man of thirty-four answered a question about four hundred yards of his own road with a sentence that is true and is not the question, and that nobody asked him the question a second time.

---

**And there are three gates into that valley and about four hundred people come in and go out of them every day, and the gate at the low end is the one a boat comes through, and the gate at the top of the water is the one the second hundred of that generation comes down on a market morning four days out of seven, and the third is a gap in a hedge that a boy goes through with a barrow.**

The crossing above the wharf is a flat stone with a sill in it and about half the trade of that valley comes over it in a season, and a man of about sixty keeps the roll of everything that comes over it, and the number of crossings in a season is a number a man can get by standing at a gate with a slate, and nine people in that valley have worked out that the number of crossings is the one figure in that place that anybody can check, and that it is a figure about carts and not about people, and that the two have never been put in the same column in that valley and there is no column in that valley.

---

**The word for it was said once, in a glass works at the low end of the water, at the second hour, and it was said by a man with his hands in a cooling box, and it was not said again that day and it has not been said since by anybody in that valley.**

The glass works has nine men in it and two hundred yards of street between it and the wharf, and the making of glass is a trade with about four words in it that mean a thing going wrong that cannot be seen from outside, and one of those four words is used about a thing that has gone cloudy all the way through while the surface is still good, and there is no more to it than that and no man in that works has ever been asked to say more.

“**My wife's mother has it,**” a man said at the second hour, to about two people who were standing at a box. “**She has had it a year. And I can tell you what it is by the look of it and I can tell you what causes it, and the two of them are not the same thing, and that is the whole of what a works knows.**”

“**Say the word for it,**” a woman said, and she was about thirty and she had a slate under her arm.

“**It is a bloom,**” the man said.

“**And can it be polished out,**” the woman said.

“**No,**” the man said, and went back to his box, and did not say anything else about it, and did not say it again that day, and nobody in that works has ever said it to anybody since, and every one of them is living in a thing that nine men in a glass works can name and nobody else can.

**And nothing whatever was explained by it. A word in a trade is a word in a trade, and about four people in that valley have said since that a man with his hands in a cooling box named a thing that has been going on in that valley for about a year, and that a man who can name a thing is about nine miles from the end of it and the other nine miles are the road between here and the county the word came from, and there is no road.**

---

The woman with the slate lost a name at the fourth hour that day, in a yard, in front of two people, and she had it back inside a minute and a quarter.

She was sent for by a man whose boy had broken a hand and could not remember which hand, and the man wanted the boy's name written at the top of a board so that the two men at the mill would know whose hand it was, and he had no slate and the woman with the slate was at the top of the lane. And she came down, and the man said the name, and she wrote the first two letters of it and then stopped with the chalk on the board.

“**Say it,**” the man said.

And she could not, and about a minute and a quarter went by, and the man waited, and about two hundred feet below the yard the mill went on, and then she said it and it was right and it was the name of her own son and she wrote it at the top of the board in the plain way and gave the chalk back.

“**You have it,**” the woman said.

The man did not notice that anything had happened. He took the board and went down to the mill and about four of the two hundred people in that valley who were in that yard had noticed a minute and a quarter of a woman not knowing a name, and about nine of the two hundred had noticed it before, and about four had noticed it twice.

**And nobody in that valley asked her about it, then or since, and nobody asked her whether the third one was going to be different, and two people in that valley have worked out since that a woman of about thirty who carries a slate has not been able to get a name for about a minute and a quarter on a day in that month, and that the man she was writing it for did not notice, and that a man who does not notice a minute and a quarter is a man who is not going to be asked about it and is not going to ask.**

---

**And at the seventh hour, at the top of the lane, a man of about sixty who keeps the roll of the crossing filled in nine ruled days that had been empty in a roll of everybody, and nobody asked him to.**

He is the second keeper. The first keeper keeps who is in the valley and the second keeper keeps who came over the water, and the two rolls have been kept side by side in two rooms for about nine years and have never been put on the same table, and neither of the two men has ever asked the other for a day of the other's half, and everybody in that valley is on one or the other of the two and about nine are on both.

He had nine days of names in his own half that the town roll had ruled across and left empty, the days were not the same nine days in any two seasons and about half of them were not the same nine days as the ones the first keeper ruled, and he had seen that ruling about four times in nine years on an open sheet lying on a trestle in that room with a man standing at the side of it who never turned it round, and a man with two halves of a valley's record in two boxes in two rooms four hundred yards apart has about nine times in a season the chance of finding out whether the two agree, and about nine people in that valley have worked out that nobody ever took it.

“**Nine days,**” the man of about sixty said, to nobody, and went on down the lane.

He took it at the seventh hour of the second day he was at that gate, and he did it in about four minutes, and he wrote nine names into nine ruled days in a roll that belongs to a room over a cross and to a man of about fifty who was not in the room and has not been told.

**And a roll with a gap in it is a roll that can be filled in, and that valley found that out on an evening in that month without anybody deciding that they had found it out, and the finding is not about a man and is not about a fever and is about a piece of paper, and nobody in that valley has ever been able to put it back the way it was, because there is no column on that sheet for a name that got there by accident and no column for one that got there on purpose, and the two of those are the same line in the same hand and nobody in that valley can tell them apart and never will.**

---

**Nobody walked any part of that day with him. The mill went on at the low end of the water, and a man who has not left his wheel since before the water came did not leave it, and nine feet of grass stood at the end of a road that a man of thirty-four dug with sixteen men and no work and that does not go anywhere, and a boy of about nine went up that road at the eighth hour with a barrow and did not stop at the finish and carried on past it as he does four days out of seven, and everybody in that valley went to bed that night with a roll of it that had been fuller in the evening than it was in the morning, and not one of them knew it, and about nine of them could have said what was on it and would not have been believed.**
