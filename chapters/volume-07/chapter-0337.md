# Chapter 0337

**The twenty-fifth day of Thawmonth, YR 317, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where a market four hundred yards long is shut, and a lane off the north end of that market has a wagon arch over it and a weigh-house at the end of it with two rooms above the weigh-house and a door at the top of the stair with a number cut into it and a year of a deed cut in under that number, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that nine people walked up that lane in one morning of that Tuesday because a press in the corner of a room in that building has been standing open for five weeks and a half.**

The tide had been under the boards of the lower of those two rooms since the tenth hour of the day before and it drew off the stone at about the third, and the boards were damp on the inside and nobody sweeps the lower floor because there is nowhere to sweep it to. A scale under that room has not been used since the fourth of a month. The door at the top of the stair is the only door in that town with a number cut into it.

**And the press in the corner of the upper room has been standing open since a Monday in the month before last, and nobody has shut it, and the reason she gave out loud in that room a month ago is that a shut thing in there can only be broken and an open thing can be looked in, and two people in that town have worked out that a press has been open five weeks and a half in a county that has an office in it and that is the longest anything in that county has been left in a state on purpose.**

A sheet of that office's own printed series went out of that room in the ordinary post about two months before with a place in the first column and a trade in the third and nothing whatever in the second or the fourth or the fifth or the sixth, and it is in the ordinary post still, and it will come back when it comes back, and nobody in that county has asked about it and nobody is going to.

---

**And about nine people came up that lane between the second hour and the fourth hour of that Tuesday, and about four of the nine were somebody else's business.**

**And the ninth of the nine was a piece of a deed with one corner gone, and a man of about sixty had carried it up that lane in his own hand because he had found it in a wall in the month before last, and he would not leave it and he would not say whose it was, and two people in that lane have worked out that a man of sixty stood in a room over a weigh-house holding a piece of a hundred-and-something-year-old deed and would not say where he had found it or whose it was, and that the keeper of records did not ask him either of those questions, and that about four thousand people in that town do not know that the only piece of a deed in that county came out of a wall.**

**And a body corporate is a thing that can be sued and cannot sue, and a piece of a deed with a corner off it is neither of those things, and there is no column on the whole of that office's printed series for a thing that cannot be named and cannot be produced, and about two people in that lane have worked out that the ninth of the nine things in that press is the only one of the nine that this office could not have refused if anybody had asked it to, because refusing a piece of a deed requires knowing what the deed says and about two hundred yards of that wall is gone.**

That is the whole of what a press that is standing open does to a lane. Anybody at all can walk up that lane and put anything into that press, and anybody at all can take anything out of it again, and about nine people in that lane had worked that out in about four years and a half and two of them had been wrong about it, and the truth of it is that a press in the corner of a room is a hole in that county and it has been a hole since the Monday five weeks before when the keeper of records stood at it and looked into it and did not shut it.

**And the keeper of records looked at all nine things, and she entered none of them, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of twenty-nine looked at nine things in about two hours on a Tuesday and entered none of them and that this is the correct work of that office and not a refusal, because a register with eight entries in it is a register with a finding in it somewhere and a body corporate with a door and a number can be sued over a finding and cannot be sued over a question.**

A thing about a hedge at a parish boundary, folded in four. A page torn off a child's slate, with a sum on it and no name. A receipt from a mill two days up the river for a waggon of lime. A sheet of that office's own printed series, blank, with a corner of it wet. A letter to somebody in a town two hundred miles off that has never been opened and is not going to be. A bill for work done on a door. A dog collar. A piece of a deed with one corner gone. And a ninth thing, which was about two hundred years old and which a man of about sixty said he had found in a wall and would not leave.

She looked at each of them for as long as it takes to look at a thing and she put nine of them on the table, and then she put them back in the press, and about two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of twenty-nine put nine things back into a press in the order she took them out, and that the order is the only record that they were ever in the room, and that there is no line in that county's book for the order things go back in.

---

**And the register stands at seven entries and the day-book stands at nine, and neither of them was opened on that Tuesday, and the shelf beside them carries four things, and about two people in that lane have counted those four and about three thousand nine hundred and ninety-six people in that town have not.**

The four are a contested sheet with a figure of persons standing against this county under a head that says a day, a fair copy of the register carrying the second copyist's trade at the foot of it and not her name, a sheet that came back up that lane with a place in it and nothing at all in the second, and the answer itself. The fifth is in the press and is a copy of the contested one and nobody in that county can produce the hand that put it there.

“**What is this office for now,**” the second copyist said, at about the fourth hour.

She is about thirty and has been in that room six years. There is a paper on that shelf giving her the third post in the day-book. It has been on that shelf since before she came into the room and the space at the foot of it where a name goes is still empty, and if this county ever signed one thing in four years and a half it would be that paper.

**And the keeper of records answered her, and that answer is the whole of what was said in that room that day about what the office is for, and no one in the lane heard it and no one has improved on it since.**

“**A county that has answered a figure with nothing has to go on doing the ordinary work of a county, and the ordinary work of a county is to be there when somebody walks up a lane, and there is no line in this kingdom for what you do with a thing after you have looked at it.**”

It is fifty-eight words and it foots at fifty-eight, and the second copyist did not improve on it and has not improved on it and about two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of twenty-nine told a woman of thirty what a body corporate is for in about thirteen seconds and that the answer is about the ninth thing in a press and not about a register and not about a day-book and not about the four on that shelf.


**And the second copyist asked the second question of that Tuesday at about the fifth hour, and it was about the thing on the table and not about the office, and nobody answered it either.**

“**What was the fourth of the nine,**” she said. “**The one with a corner gone.**”

“**A piece of a deed,**” the keeper of records said.

“**Whose.**”

“**He did not say and I did not ask him,**” the keeper of records said, and about two people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine was asked a question she could have answered in about nine words and answered it in eleven, and that what she gave was a reason for her not knowing rather than the thing, and that the reason for her not knowing is a thing a second copyist has wanted for six years and a woman of twenty-nine has never once asked her for.

---
“**Then we are a room,**” the second copyist said.

“**We are a room,**” the keeper of records said, and about nine people in that lane have worked out that a woman of twenty-nine agreed in four words that a body corporate is a room, and that a body corporate which has spent four years and a half refusing to be a body corporate has become the one place in that county anybody can walk up to and put something in, and that the two of those are the same event and only one of them was chosen.

**And the day-book on that shelf is the only book in that building that nothing in this kingdom can be served out of, and it was not opened on that Tuesday, and it stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and it is not going to get a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if one came, and the keeper of records did not ask the second copyist anything else and the second copyist did not ask her anything else, and not-asking in that room is not a courtesy and is not an occasion and has never been counted by anybody in that county, including the woman who keeps the day-book of it.**

---

**And the second copyist has wanted a line in a book with her own name on it since the summer before last, and she has said once out loud in a kitchen that she would refuse it and does not want it, and that those two are one thing, and she has not said it in that room and is not going to, and the warrant for the third post in that day-book is on the shelf six feet from where the two of them were sitting and has been for six years and has nobody's name at the foot of it.**

She asked the question about what the office was for. She did not ask the question about the warrant and she did not ask the question about the four things on the shelf and she did not ask the question about the answer that went out in the ordinary post two months before, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of thirty who has wanted a line with her own name on it since the summer before last has been in a room with the person who could give her one for six years and has not asked for it, and that the reason is not modesty and about two of those four have worked out that the reason is that a person who is refused a thing in a room stops being a person who can be asked anything in that room.

“**The letter is not going to be opened,**” the keeper of records said, at about the sixth hour, picking up the sixth of the nine things.

“**I did not say it was.**”

“**I know you did not. I am telling you where it is going back to,**” the keeper of records said, and put it in the press on top, and about two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of twenty-nine explained where a letter goes back to in about nine words to a woman who had not asked her anything, and that explaining where a thing goes is the only kind of answer that room has ever given anybody, and that about four thousand people live in that town and two of them are the only ones who know where a letter in a press goes.

No one in that town had anybody else to walk with on that Tuesday. Nine people came down the lane and four of the nine were not there for anything to do with the room at the end of it. The mill at the low end turned the way it turns. Two hundred people were out of their houses. Forty men were on a reach four hundred yards down a lane off that market, and nine were in a yard four hundred yards the other way, and not one of those forty and not one of those nine knew that nine things had gone into a press in a room forty feet above a weigh-house.

**And the two prices of the same two rooms over that weigh-house are the salt merchant's two shillings a month for each of them and the fourpence a year that this office pays for the door with the number cut into it, and they are two prices of two different objects, and nobody in that building has put them in one sum in four years and a half, and the fourpence a year is not in any column and is not a rate and may not become one, and a wage of fourpence a week paid out of a private box to a man who holds a vote and not an office is a wage and is in no column of that office either.**

**And about nine hundred people in a town two hundred miles off do not know that a press in a county of about four thousand people has been standing open for five weeks and a half and that about nine people have walked up a lane and put nine things into it, and a woman in that lane knows, and the county has answered a figure of persons standing against it with nothing and has now been asked nothing since, and nothing is still the whole of what that office can do.**
