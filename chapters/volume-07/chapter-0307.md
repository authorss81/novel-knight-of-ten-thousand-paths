# Chapter 0307

**The twenty-third day of Wolfmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour, and a lane at the north end of that county's market, which is four hundred yards long and stands in a town of about four thousand people on a river, with a wagon arch in that lane and a door on that lane and two rooms over a weigh-house at the end of it, and a yard on a street four hundred yards up the county's own road, and nobody in that market knows that a man came up that lane at about the second hour with a sheet in a bag and put it on a table and went out again.**

**A courier is not a carrier, and the whole of the difference between the two of them is in what a man is allowed to ask either of them.** A carrier can be asked what a thing costs to a place and will answer in the voice a man uses for a price. A courier has a bag and a place to put it in and no round at all and there is no figure in him anybody can ask for, and the two of them have been in that county four times between them in about nine years, have never met and are not going to.

He came up that road in the middle of a market morning and went past about four hundred people without slowing down, and the bag he was carrying had a number of an office on it and nothing at all against a person, and nobody in that town found out what he was.

**The number on that bag belongs to a building two hundred miles off in a provincial town, and this county has nothing such a building could write to. No writ, no seal, nothing of its own with a door on it. What it has is a number cut into a door, and anything can be put through that door by anybody who walks up the lane, and a courier is the one visitor who needs none of the rest of it, because he is not coming to a court. He is coming to a door.**

---

**He came the length of that lane and stopped under the wagon arch at the one door in it that has anything cut into it, and went in, and put a sheet down on a table, and said the sheet was for whoever keeps that room and that it had been in his bag seven days. The keeper is twenty-nine and has held the office four years and a half on a deed of thirteen words, and she lifted the sheet off his hand where it lay and did not read it on her feet.**

**And she did not ask him for a name, and he did not give her one, and that is the ordinary way of that room and not a courtesy and not a rule, and a body corporate with a door and a number cut into it can be served with anything at all by anybody who walks up a lane and there is nothing in that office that a visitor can be entered against.**

“**Who is it from,**” she said.

“**It is for you,**” the courier said.

“**That is not what I asked and you are not going to answer it,**” she said, and the courier went out again and was not asked anything else, and nobody in that town knew he had come.

And about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine asked a man with a bag a question he could not answer and told him so before he had answered it, and that about nine years of that office have been spent finding out that a county of about four thousand people can be found by anybody who wants it and can be written to by anybody who has an address and can be told what a thing is by nobody at all.

---

The weigh-house takes the tide up through its boards in a wet week, and that was a wet week, and the boards were damp on the inside and there was about an inch of water on the floor of the lower room that nobody had swept out because there was nowhere to sweep it to, and a scale under that room has not been used since the fourth of a month.

**And the sheet says that a return is wanted, and it is a sheet of that office's own printed series, and it is ruled in six spaces with a head over five of the six, and the heads are that office's own, and four of the six will take a figure and the fifth will take a man by what he does and not by what he is called, and nowhere on the whole of it is there the name of a person.**

**The first of the six is the object column and it has a set of market towns in it, written the way that office writes a place,** and the second column has a figure in it and the head over the second column says *Date*, and **that figure is the whole of what came up that lane, and there is nothing on the paper in that room that a reader could set it against, and there is nothing in the office two hundred miles off that sent it that could set it against either.**

And at the foot of it, in the ordinary way, in the office's own hand, it says that a return is wanted, and that a return is a sheet of the same series with a place in the first column and a figure in the second, and that about four hundred of them go out of that building every month into nine counties, and **there is no line on it that a person can be named in and no column in that office that a county can send an answer in, and a county cannot be shown a form.**

“**Then what do we do,**” said a voice from the stair.

And it was the second copyist, who is about thirty and has been in that room for six years and is on a warrant for a third post in the day-book that is not signed, and she had come down at about the seventh hour for a sheet of paper and had stopped on the third step.

“**Nothing,**” the keeper of records said, and she said it the way she says it, which is once, and she did not explain it the second time because there was nobody to explain it to that had not heard the first.

“**You have said that before.**”

“**I have said it before and I will say it again and there is no part of this that has moved,**” the keeper of records said, and then she said the part of it that is the office's own, in the plain way, to a woman on a stair.

“**A return is a sheet of the same series with a place in the first column and a figure in the second, and there is a set of market towns in that first column and they are the set of market towns this county is in, and there is nothing in this building that knows what the figure under *Date* is a count of, and there is nothing in that building either, and the two of those offices are two hundred miles apart and neither of them has asked us anything.**”

And the second copyist went up the three steps and got her paper and did not ask anything else, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman of twenty-nine told a woman of thirty in her own building the whole of what had come up the lane that morning in about ninety seconds and the second woman went up a stair with a sheet of paper in her hand and did not say a word about it, and that the not-asking is not a courtesy and is the ordinary habit of a room.

---

“**The tide is under the boards of the lower room,**” the second copyist said.

“**I know it is,**” the keeper of records said.

**And a woman of about thirty with a pail went up that lane at about the fifth hour and stopped at nine doors and said one thing at each of them, and at every one of the nine she moved on before the knuckle had finished ringing in the frame, and those are all the doors in that lane and nobody in that lane has ever asked her why she says things at doors.**

At the door forty feet from a wagon arch she said that a man in a plain coat had come up the lane that morning and had not stopped in the market, and at the next door she said that the tide was under the boards of the lower room and had been since the middle of the week, and at the third she said that the market would be on again on the Thursday and that there would be no salt in it before the seventh hour.

And nobody in that lane has ever asked her where she gets the nine things, and about two people in that lane have worked out that a woman who says one thing at nine doors and does not wait is the only person in that county who says the same sentence to nine houses about the same morning, and that nobody in that town knows that the nine sentences are the same sentence with a thing put in front of it, and that the things are a courier, a tide and a market, and that a person who is told three things in a lane on a market morning is told one thing.

---

**And a second keeper of twenty-six with a key came into that room at about the fifth hour and went straight through it and did not stop at the table, and the third post in the day-book is hers and the warrant for it is not signed, and the two women in that room have never once spoken to each other about either of those two things and are not going to.**

The number cut into that door has been in it for four years and a half and there is a year of a deed cut in under it in a smaller hand, and about four hundred people in that town have walked past that door and about nine of them can read the number, and the number is a number of a body corporate and not a number of a county and not a number of a room, and there is no figure anywhere in that kingdom that can be put against it and no office two hundred miles off that will take one.

A sweep goes up that lane at about the sixth hour and goes back down it at about the eighth, and about nine of them are in that lane on a market morning, and about four hundred people in that town go through a lane in a market morning and about nine of them are somebody else's business, and that ratio has not been worked out by anybody in that county and is not going to be.

---

**And the office pays fourpence a year. It has paid fourpence a year for four years and a half and that is the whole of what it has ever cost that county to be a thing with a door and a number on it, and the two rooms those two women work in are the salt merchant's and he charges two shillings a month for each of them, and those are two prices of two different things and neither of them is in the other's column, and no clerk in that building has ever added one of them to the other of them and is not going to.**

A courier is not entered anywhere either. A man who comes up a lane with a bag and puts a sheet on a table and goes out again is not in the day-book and is not in the register and is not on the fair copy nineteen years old on that wall, and a press in that room can be served with anything at all by anybody who walks up that lane, and the sheet went into that press at about the seventh hour because that is what the press in that room is for, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine put a form into a press she could be served with and that the form in it is one this county cannot answer, and that a press that can be served with is a press that anybody can take a thing out of.

---

Nobody walked any part of that Saturday with anybody. About four hundred people went home from a market, a waggon of salt that is not the guild's stood at the end of it about an hour and went back down the channel road, about nine men went home from a yard on a street four hundred yards up that road, where a bench-board is standing four feet off the ground and there is a press in a room at the back of that yard that nothing can be served out of, a bell a hundred yards up that road went at the fourth hour and a half as it does, and everybody in that town went home on a market morning knowing that a thing had come up a lane and had gone into a press and that **the only record that office keeps of anybody who has ever come up that lane is a day-book that nothing in the world can be served out of, and it stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and there is not going to be a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.**
