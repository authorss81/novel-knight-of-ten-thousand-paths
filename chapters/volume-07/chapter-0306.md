# Chapter 0306

**The nineteenth day of Wolfmonth, YR 316, a Tuesday, and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour and the ninth hour, and two hundred feet of wharf at the low end of the water in that city, which is about four hundred people and is four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, and a glass works and a bread oven and about two hundred yards of street between them with a market that stands on it four days out of seven, and three gates, and about four hundred people in that city who do not know that a sheet of a printed series left that valley in the ordinary post that evening with a place in the first column of it and a figure in the second.**

**“Is it going out,**” a man on the wharf said.

“**It is going out and back,**” the man of about sixty said, and did not look up from the rope he was coiling.

The wharf is two hundred feet and there are four boats on it and a fifth that is not on it most weeks, and the crossing above it is a flat stone with a sill in it that a cart can go over when the water is down and cannot go over when it is up, and about half the trade of that valley comes over that sill in a season and the other half comes over a bridge two hundred feet down the water with a woman keeping it.

**A bell of nine stone hangs over the street at the low gate and it goes at the fourth hour, and nobody in that city has been able to account for it, and nobody in that city has ever been asked about it and nobody in that city is going to be asked.**

It is not in the market's book and it is not in the room's book and it is not in the box at the low gate, and every one of the four hundred people in that valley has heard it about as many times in a year as they have heard the water come up, and it is rung by whoever is nearest to it, which has been a different person about nine times in a year, and the arrangement has not been written down and has not been talked about and is not a rule. There is no answer in that city to the question of what it is for, and a bell that nobody in a place can account for is a bell that nobody in that place can be asked about, and about four people in that valley have said since that a thing nobody can be asked about is a thing every person in the valley can answer for, and that nobody in that valley has ever put those two facts together, and the bell went at the fourth hour that morning and nobody looked up.

---

Nine men in the glass works, and the making of glass is a trade in which about four words mean a thing going wrong that cannot be seen from outside, and two hundred yards up the lane a bread oven that goes through two batches a day and about nine loaves to a batch in a valley of about four hundred people, and about four hundred people eat about the amount nine loaves feeds four times over and the rest of it goes to the steadings up the water, and about two hundred of the ninth generation are the ones who are fed by it and have never once been down at the low end of the water.

**“I know you,**” a man said at the fourth door.

“**You do,**” the woman with the slate said, and wrote nothing down about it.

And there are about nine doors on two hundred yards of that street, and on that morning about nine people were asked, at about nine doors, the ordinary question that a person is asked at a door, and about nine of them answered it in the ordinary way, and about two of the nine took about a minute and a quarter over the answering of it and neither of the two mentioned it, and about four people in that valley have said since that a minute and a quarter at a door is a thing a street does not remark on, and that about two hundred of the ninth generation came down off that hill in the last year and are still coming down, and that a street where about two of nine people pause is a street where about two of nine people paused all last year as well, and that nobody in that city has ever been asked to count them.

---

**And at the seventh hour a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work said a thing to a stranger in a yard at the top of the lane, and it was the only time he has said it, and the stranger did not answer it.**

“**You are on the roll,**” Sefa Tamas said.

“**Since the first day,**” the man of thirty-four said.

He had been in that yard about four hours and he had not said anything to anybody in it, and the stranger was a man of about thirty-two with a limp who had been in that valley about three weeks and had been asked nothing by anybody, and the two of them had been in the same yard with about nine other people and had not said one word to each other until that.

“**A road is a thing you dig and a road that goes somewhere is a different road and I dug this one and it goes nowhere and I am not going to be asked what for,**” the man of thirty-four said.

It is thirty-six words, and it is all he has said out loud in that yard in about a year, and about four people in that valley have said since that a man with sixteen men and no work said a true thing about a road four hundred yards long to a stranger who had been in the valley three weeks, and that the stranger did not answer it, and that a man who says a true thing to a stranger and gets nothing back has found out the ordinary way that there is no instrument in four hundred miles of country for what he said.

**And the man with the limp did not answer it, and nine people in that valley have worked out that a man who is in a place to be asked what a road is for was in a yard at the top of a lane and did not answer, and that not answering is the correct response to a thing that is not a question, and that nobody in that city knows a stranger was on that hill that week at all.**

---

**And the sheet went out at the ninth hour, and it went out because of a column, and nobody in that city knew that it had ever arrived.**

It had come into the valley in the ordinary post about two months before, in a bundle of about nine things for the mill and the oven and the two ferries, and it had stood in the box at the low gate with a strip of paper round it since the day it came, and a strip of paper round a thing in a box is a thing that has not been opened by anybody, and every person in that valley had seen it every day for two months and about nine of them had picked it up and put it down again.

It is a sheet of a printed series. It is ruled in six spaces and five of the six have a head over them, and the heads are the office's own, and about four of the six will take a figure and the fifth will take a person by trade and not by name, and **nowhere on the whole of the sheet, in any column, in any line, at the head or the foot, is there the name of a person.**

The first column is the object column and it takes a place, and a place goes in it the way that office puts a place in, which is a set of market towns, and the second column takes a figure and the head over the second column says *Date*, and the third and the fourth and the fifth took whatever a person had and nobody in that valley had any of the four of them, and the sixth space is the same width as the other five and has nothing at the top of it.

“**Is that yours,**” a boy said.

“**It is nobody's,**” the man of about sixty said, and picked it up out of the box at the low gate on an ordinary morning in that month at the seventh hour, and read it standing up, and it took him about four minutes, and about nine men in that valley saw a man of about sixty standing at a gate reading a piece of paper and none of them asked him what it was and none of them has since.

“**What is it,**” the boy said.

“**Paper,**” the man of about sixty said.

He did not know what office it came from. He knew that it was a printed series and that about four hundred of them go out of a building in a town two hundred miles off into nine counties every month, and he did not know the town and had never asked and about four people in that valley have said since that a man of about sixty filled in a form he could not name for an office he had never heard of because it had a column in it with a head over it and nothing on the sheet that would have stopped him.

**And what he put in it was this: the name of the valley in the first column in the office's own way of writing a place, and a figure in the second column, and the head over the second column says *Date*.**

The figure was the last line of his own roll. A roll of who is in a valley has nine hundred lines in it and a name at the end of every one, and a man with a column in front of him that wants a figure will take the last line off it, and four hundred and four is what the last line of that roll says, and a roll is not a count and nobody in that valley counted anybody in order to put four hundred and four in a column, and about nine people in that valley knew at the time that nobody had counted anything and about four hundred of them did not and still do not know that there is a figure of them in a column two hundred miles off.

And he copied the roll as well. It was in a press in a room over a cross with a door and a bar and no shelf anybody looks at, and he had made it himself and it was the best copy anybody in that valley has ever seen, and he rolled it and put it in the same bag with the sheet, because a man who is going to send a figure is going to send the thing the figure came from, and about four people in that valley have said since that a man of about sixty did the ordinary thing twice in about four minutes and neither of the two things was in his hands that morning and both of them are in a bag on an ordinary round.

---

Nobody walked any part of that day with anybody.

A carrier came up the lane at the ninth hour with a bag and a rate that is not printed in that valley.

“**For the county,**” the carrier said, to a door, and nobody asked him which county.

He did not knock at any door, and nobody in that city knew that a bag had gone down the lane that night with a roll of nine hundred lines of everybody in it rolled up inside it and a figure of four hundred and four against a set of market towns in the first two columns of a printed sheet, and there is no line on the sheet for the nine hundred names and no column on the sheet anywhere for the name of a person, and the carrier read none of it and reads none of anything.

And the roll at home is still on its trestle in the room over the cross, and the man of fifty-eight did not open it that evening, and a press in the same building with a door and a bar and no shelf anybody looks at was empty, and every one of them went to bed that night in a place that had just been entered in a column two hundred miles from the nearest town anybody in it can name, under a head that says *Date*, by a man who was not asked and did not know and did it because there was a column in front of him and about two hundred of the ninth generation in that place are the only people in it who have never once been asked whether they are all right.
