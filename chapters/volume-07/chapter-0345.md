# Chapter 0345

**The eleventh day of Greenmonth, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour and the seventh hour, in a provincial town of about nine hundred people two hundred miles off a county of about four thousand people on a river, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people on the floors of it, and a floor above the press room there is a room with a stove in it that has not been lit since the weather turned and one window nine feet off a bare wall, and about four hundred sheets a month go across the one table under that window and are read at waist height, because the light in that room comes up off the boards and not down off the sky, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a set of forty sheets came up the stairs of that building in the ordinary post ten days ago and is in a drawer that nobody empties.**

That town stands about nine hundred people in it and every Saturday it is all out in a market by the second hour, and the building is a hundred yards off the middle of it and nobody in the market goes near it. The presses on the middle floor were going before the stalls came out and stopped an hour after the stalls went in. A wooden tray with a low lip went into a passage at the third hour and came out at the fourth with two sheets on it and a man of about sixty carried it back down the corridor saying the one thing he says on that floor, and nobody has ever asked him whether it is the same thing every time, and it is not, and about two hundred people in that building have said since that a sentence said six thousand six hundred times without being asked is a thing that has become a form.

**And about four hundred sheets a month go across one table under one window and are read at waist height, because the light in that room comes up off the boards and not down off the sky, and a woman of thirty reads every one of them that way and a woman of thirty-three is at the fourth column of them.**

About four of the six take a figure. The third takes a person by that person's trade and not by that person's name, and nowhere on the whole of it there is the name of a person. The person column is the third and the fifth is *How often*, and the fourth is headed *Keeper* and takes a person the same way the third does, and the head on it has been the same head for two years.

---

**And a set of about forty sheets came up the stairs of that building in the ordinary post ten days before that Saturday, in a bundle with a strip of paper round it and a place written on the strip in a set of market towns, and a man of about thirty carried it down with a handcart and put it into the returns press, and the returns press is a drawer with a handle, and nobody in that building empties that drawer on any day anybody keeps.**

It is the fourth county. The sheets came out of a room in a county that has four market towns in a district and about nine hundred people, and the room they came out of had four hundred of that office's own printed series in a frame on a wall, and about ninety of them came out of it wet along one edge, and about four hundred did not come out of it at all, and there is no list of either number anywhere and there is no form in that county on which either could be entered.

Nobody in that building has ever asked that county what the sheets were. Nobody in that county has ever been asked where the sheets went. A sheet of that office's own printed series is a penny a sheet at a counter and there is no counter in that building and has not been one since before the oldest person in it came to work.

**And the forty of them were not complete, and the incompleteness is not a fault and is what they are, and about two people in four counties have worked out since that a set of forty sheets out of four hundred is a tenth of a thing and that the other nine tenths are in nine other places in four counties and that nobody in any of the four has ever told anybody in the other three what the forty were for.**

---

**And a man of about thirty brought the forty out of the drawer at the fourth hour and put them on that table in one stack and said one thing to the room about them, and then he went up the stair, and that one thing is the whole of what was said about them in that room for two hours.**

“**The fourth column on the top nine of these wants copying out and there is nobody on this floor who can do it but her,**” he said, and went up.

Osten Varel was nine feet off with a straightedge in his hand. He has been in that building seven years and he carries a line, and about two people in that building have said since that a man who rules a line for a living watched a man ask a woman to copy a keeper column and did not look up from the straightedge and was not asked, and that the ninth of those nine is a line about a room in a county about four hundred miles from that table and he has ruled it five times and has not asked anybody what the keepers are.

**And a woman of thirty-three copied the fourth column out of the top nine of about forty sheets in about a minute and a quarter apiece, without looking up, and she did it nine times in about nine minutes, and nobody in that room thanked her, and nobody asked her why, and about nine people on that floor have been saying since that a woman who has filled that column about two hundred times a season for eleven years copied nine of them out for a man who did not say thank you and did not say why, and that she did it without looking up because a hand that has done a thing about two thousand times does not need to look at it.**

The third column on those nine is a trade and the fourth column on those nine is a trade and they are not the same trade, and where a trade is standing against a room on one sheet and a different trade is standing against the same room on another sheet, the copy she made has both of them in it in her own hand and no name anywhere, and about two people in that town have worked out since that a woman of thirty-three wrote out nine keepers in a room with a cold stove in it and that the nine of them are the only keepers anybody in that town could produce in one hand, and that about nine hundred people in that town have never seen one of them and that there is no column in four floors for the person who could say what the nine are.

**And nobody in that building has ever thanked her and about two people on that floor have worked out since that a woman of thirty-three has not been thanked for that column in eleven years and that a man who says thank you for nine minutes of work has not existed in that building, and that the not-saying is not unkindness and that nobody on that floor has ever worked out what it is.**

---

**And the ninth sheet of the forty was not of that series, and it was a copy of a book about nine inches by six with eleven lines in it in nine years, made by hand, and it went up the stairs of that building in a bundle with a strip of paper round it, and a man of about thirty had made it and has not told anybody that he made it and has not been asked.**

A woman of about thirty made that one herself, six years ago, and there have been eleven lines in it the whole time and every one of them is in her own writing, and no press in that building has ever printed a line of it and it answers to no series that office prints and stands in no register that office keeps, and nobody two floors up has asked to see it in six years, and it is the only thing in that building anybody could put in front of a stranger.

**And she said out loud in a room on the last morning of Mudmonth that she wanted a second copy of it, and a man of about thirty told her that a second book is a decision, and nobody answered her and nobody thanked her and nobody told her she was right, and the stove in that room has not been lit since the weather turned and the light comes up off the boards and a hand cannot read its own writing at that table in the middle of a winter.**

And the copy exists, and it is in a bundle, and it came up the stairs with forty sheets of another county's business, and about two people in that building have worked out since that it got itself into a bundle because a man was making a bundle and put a thing in it that had no business being in it, and that the man is not a villain and is not anybody's man and is a man of thirty who carries a district from one quarter to the next in about four seconds and put a piece of paper in a bundle because it was on the table.

**And she saw it at the fourth hour on that Saturday, on the table, under her own hands, in the middle of copying nine keepers, and she did not stop, and she did not look up, and she did not say anything about it to anybody then or afterwards, and about nine people on that floor clocked her hands and about four of the nine clocked her face and about none of the four has ever asked her about either.**

---

**And at the sixth hour the forty went back into the drawer and the drawer is not emptied on any day anybody keeps, and about two people in that building have said since that a set of about forty sheets out of a county four hundred miles off is in a drawer in a press on the ground floor of a building of four floors and that a drawer that is not emptied is a room and that nobody in that building has worked out which of the two it is and neither has anybody in the four counties the other three of them are in.**

“**Who holds it,**” Osten Varel said, at the sixth hour, without looking up.

“**You do,**” the man of about thirty said, from the stair. “**You are the only one on this floor who could put a hand on that drawer in the dark. I have not entered it anywhere and I am not going to and there is no column in four floors for a man who holds a drawer.**”

“**I have never held a drawer in my life.**”

“**Then you have held one since about the fourth hour this afternoon and you are the only one of the four of us on this floor who can be shown it, and that is all a holder is, and I did not make a rule and there is no rule.**”

And about four people in four counties have been saying since that a man of thirty-one who rules a line for a living was told in a room that he holds a drawer and said he had never held a drawer in his life and was given the reason he now holds one, and that the reason is that he could be shown it, and that a record which can be produced by one holder is a column, and that a record which cannot be produced by anybody is a shelf, and that about four people in four counties have been saying since that the thing on the table is neither and that nobody in four counties has a word for it.

**And the woman of thirty-three is one of nine people in four counties who holds about forty sheets by name, and nobody asked her whether she would, and she did not say that she would, and the thing she has wanted since the month she was twenty-two is nine words long and it has not got one word more in it than it had then, and it is not written down anywhere in that building, and a leaf in a book on the second shelf behind her is turned face down and was not turned over on that Saturday and is not going to be.**

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. About nine hundred people went into a market and about two hundred of that town did not go anywhere, and nine men were in a yard with a bench-board, and a man of about fifty-five turned three bars down and up at a barrier, and a bench of nine in a county two hundred miles off sat in a market morning on that Saturday and was asked nothing, and the count on a page under the fourth step of the outside stair of that room said nine before that Saturday and said nine after it.

**And a woman of thirty-three went home from that building at the seventh hour with nine keepers copied out in her own hand and a set of about forty sheets in a drawer she is the only one of four floors who could put a hand on in the dark, and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her why she did either of those things and nobody told her she was right, and the second copy of a book of eleven lines in nine years is in a drawer in the same building and she is the only person on that floor who knows that it is, and about two people in that building have been saying since that a woman has been given the one thing she asked a room for and did not ask for it and has not thanked anybody and is not going to, and that about nine hundred people in that town do not know any of it and that about four hundred sheets a month go across that table and read at waist height and not one of them in ten years has been a copy of anything anybody in that building made with their own hands.**
