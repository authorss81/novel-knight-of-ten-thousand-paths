# Chapter 0316

**The first day of Hearthmonth, YR 316, a Tuesday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, and the four days from that Tuesday to the Friday, and a room above a cross at the top of a lane, in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, where twenty-nine benches are bolted down in four rows with a number cut in the end of every one, and the number cut in the end of the last bench of the right-hand row at the back is a hundred and forty, and four feet of aisle between the two middle rows has not been stood in by three generations going, and about nine people are on those benches and about thirty are in the room and about two hundred of a generation that has never once come down off that hill are standing at the back, and four hundred people live in that valley and nine of them know that a book was copied in that room over four days.**

The room over a cross is a room and not a bench, and the twenty-nine benches bolted down in four rows are the room, and a thing can be asked a second time in a room and cannot be asked a second time in a bench, and the only one of those twenty-nine that anybody in that valley thinks of as a bench is the one at the back of the right-hand row with a hundred and forty cut in the end of it, and nobody in that valley has ever sat at that one for a vote.

**And about nine people in that valley have said since that the only way anybody in this place can tell a room from a bench is to wait for a thing to be asked twice, and that a room which has never been asked anything twice is a bench that has not been noticed, and that about four hundred people have been coming up that lane for about a hundred and forty years and have not asked anything twice.**

---

**And a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work had a trestle brought into the corner at the back of that room on the first morning and had been copying the winter book on it for four days, and Marek Ollen, who keeps the roll of who is in that valley, was in the room for about an hour on the second of the four days, and four of the thirty had come in to look at the copy, and two hundred at the back had not.**

Forty leaves, tied with a piece of tarred hemp, and nine spreads, one to a generation. A man of fifty-eight keeps it, and his father's before him kept it, and his father's father's before that. Marek Ollen has kept that roll for nineteen years, is a different man from the keeper of the winter book, and has not been asked about the copy and is not going to be. There is a finding in it. Nobody in that room said anything about where the finding is or how many there are, and nobody asked, and the man of thirty-four did not copy the head of the first spread out loud to anybody and did not look at it while he was working, and the four days he spent on that book are four days out of which about four hundred people in that valley spent an afternoon in a room with a man making a copy of the only book any of them will ever be in twice.

He made it in the ordinary way, with a rule and a straight edge and about nine lines to a page, and the copy is good work, and about two people in that valley have said since that a man who does not keep a book has copied it better than the hand that wrote half of it, and that the hand that wrote the other half of it is in this room and is fifty-eight and did not watch him do it and has not been asked to.

**And the man of fifty-eight was in that room for about two hours on the first of the four days and about half an hour on the last, and he did not stand at the trestle, and he did not ask what the man of thirty-four was doing, and nobody in that room told him, and about two people in that valley have worked out that the keeper of the only book four hundred people will ever be written down in did not ask a man of thirty-four what he was doing with it, and that the reason he did not ask is that nobody in that room has ever asked the keeper of that book anything.**

“**You have got the nine spreads,**” a man said, on the second morning.

“**I have got the nine spreads,**” the man of thirty-four said.

“**It is a copy of somebody's book.**”

“**It is a copy of the only one there is,**” the man of thirty-four said, and did not look up, “**and the difference between a copy and a second book is that a second book is a thing somebody has decided to make and a copy is a thing somebody has had to make, and I have had to make it, because about nine of my sixteen have nowhere to work this season and there is one thing in this valley that is work and it is about forty leaves.**”

---

**And the reason a man with sixteen men and no work and a hill in front of him makes a copy of a book of nine spreads is a rule of that valley and not a decision, and the rule is about two hundred years old and nobody in that valley can remember who made it and it is not written down anywhere.**

The rule is that a book of nine spreads is the only record any of them has, and a record of that kind that is in one room and gets wet is a record that is gone, and there is a water at the low end of that valley that comes up to the floor of that room about twice in a generation, and a man of fifty-eight has never once lost a leaf and has not been asked how he manages it and there is nothing in that room that is not above the floor.

**And a copy made by a man who does not keep the book is not a second book, because a second book is one a person has decided to make, and about two people in that valley have said since that the difference between a copy and a second book is that a second book is a decision and a copy is four days of a wet season, and that a man with sixteen men and no work does not make decisions in a wet season, he makes copies.**

So the copy went into the press at the end of that room on the fourth morning, and the press has a door and a bar and no shelf anybody looks at, and the original went back under the trestle against the wall on a board of its own, where it had lain for nineteen years before that trestle was carried in, and the two of them are in the same room within about six feet of each other and there is no line in the room and no line in either of the two books that says which of them is the one that happened.

---

**And the woman with the slate was in that room for about an hour on the third of the four days and she carried no slate and asked for nothing, and her name is Sefa Tamas and she has carried one for eleven years.**

She carries one in the ordinary way. It is a foot long with a string on it and a stick of chalk tied to the string, and she has carried it for eleven years, and she had it under her arm at the top of that lane that morning and she did not take it out, and about four people in that room have noticed that and about nine have not, and nobody in that room asked her about it and nobody has asked her about anything since the day she said a want to a piece of paper in that building.

“**You will want a line at the foot of it,**” a man said, at about the fifth hour, to the man of thirty-four.

“**There is no line at the foot of a book of nine spreads,**” the man of thirty-four said. “**That is the whole of what is wrong with it and I am not going to put one there in pencil in about four days' work and have it be a tenth thing in a room where the only other nine things are in a book about four feet from me.**”

“**Then how will anybody know you made it.**”

“**They will not,**” the man of thirty-four said.

**And about four people in that valley have said since that a man with sixteen men and no work copied the only book four hundred people will ever be written down in and refused, in the ordinary way, to sign his own copy of it, and that a man who will not sign a copy of a book is a man who has understood something about copies that about four people in that valley understood before he did and about two hundred did not.**

---

**The man of fifty-eight was in that room on the last of the four days for about half an hour, and the bar was already across the press, and he did not go to the press and did not go to the trestle, and about four of the thirty worked out that there are two books in that room and that the one under the trestle is the one he has brought up that lane himself every year since he was seventeen, and the two hundred at the back did not.**

Fifty-eight, and every day of his life has been spent in that valley. In about eleven years the ninth generation has come down off that hill to that room to the number of about two hundred, and he has spoken to nine of them, and when he asked two of the nine who they were they could not tell him, and he wrote down neither answer nor question.

**And he is not coming back, and about four hundred people in that valley are not told why, and nobody in that valley is going to be told why, and there is no instrument in that valley that could carry the reason if it were ever given to anybody, and the reason was never given to anybody and there is no column in that room for a road that was gone down and came back from and there never has been.**

Nobody walked any part of those four days with anybody. The mill went on at the low end of the water and a man at that wheel did not leave it, and about nine feet of grass stood at the end of a road about four hundred yards long that a man of thirty-four dug in a wet summer and that goes nowhere, and a boy of about nine went up that road four days out of seven with a barrow and did not stop at the standing finish and carried on past it, and the market at the low end of the water stood down for two of the four days and went about on the other two, and a glass works had nine men in it and a bread oven at the top of the lane went through two batches a day.

**And there are two books of nine spreads in a room over a cross four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, and one of them was made by the man who keeps it and one of them was made by a man who does not, and the man who keeps it is fifty-eight and is not coming back and does not know that there are two, and about four hundred people in that valley keep a memory of a man who walked up a road for nine days and read that book out loud in that room in front of about nine people and about thirty and about two hundred at the back, and nobody in that valley has ever asked him where he is and nobody is going to, and a copy of what he read out loud is in a press six feet from where the original lies, and there is no line in that room that says which of the two of them is the one that happened, and there is not going to be one.**
