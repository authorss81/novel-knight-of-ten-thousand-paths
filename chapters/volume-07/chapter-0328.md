# Chapter 0328

**The nineteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, with a market four hundred yards long that is shut, a lane off the north end of it with a wagon arch in it, and at the end of that lane a weigh-house with two rooms above it and a door at the top of the stair with a number cut into it and a year of a deed cut in under the number, and about nine hundred people in the town two hundred miles off a press room who do not know what a woman of twenty-nine did at about the fourth hour of that Monday, and neither does anybody in that town.**

Two rooms over a weigh-house, and the two of them are the salt merchant's and not the office's, and the salt merchant charges two shillings a month for each of them and the office pays fourpence a year for a door and a number and a room, and those are two prices of two different objects and nobody in that building has ever put one of them into a sum with the other of them and nobody is going to. The weigh-house takes the tide up through its boards in a wet week and it had been dry for about four days, and the door at the top of the stair is the only one in that town with a number cut into it and about four thousand people live in that town and two of them know why.

**And the answer that went out of that room on the fifteenth of a month two hundred miles off is in the ordinary post somewhere, and it will come back about two months from now, and nobody in that county has been asked anything about it and nobody is going to be, and there is no day against it anywhere in this kingdom and nobody has written one down.**

---

**And the register stands at seven entries, all of them questions, none of them a finding, and the day-book stands at nine entries of a thing that was not asked, and neither of those two books was opened on that Monday, and a shelf beside them carries four things, and a press in the corner carries a fifth, and the fifth is the whole of that chapter.**

The four things are a contested sheet with a figure of persons against this county standing under a head that says a day, a fair copy of the register made in the middle of that month by a woman of about thirty in that room which carries her trade at the foot of it and not her name, a sheet that came back up that lane in the ordinary post with a place in it and nothing at all in the second, and the answer itself, going round nine counties. About four people in that lane have counted those four and about nine hundred people in that town have not.

The press has a door and a bar and it is in the corner of that room and it can be served with anything by anybody who walks up that lane, and it can be emptied of anything by the same, and it has not been opened in about four years and a half, and about two people in that lane have worked out since that a body corporate that has spent four years and a half refusing to be a body corporate has kept one press in the room it works in and has never once looked in it, and that a press is the only instrument in that room that works and cannot be checked, and that the keeper of records put the fair copy on the shelf and not in the press, and that a person who puts the thing she made on a shelf and not in a press has already worked out what a press is for and has not written it down anywhere.

“**You have never opened it,**” the second copyist said, at about the fourth hour.

“**Not once,**” the keeper of records said.

“**Then you do not know what is in it.**”

“**I know what is in it,**” the keeper of records said, “**which is about four years and a half of things that people walked up this lane and put in, and about four of them in four years and a half were about this office, and about four people in this lane have counted that number and nobody has counted the other one.**”

---

**And a man came up that lane at the second hour with a folded paper in his hand and put it into that press and said nothing and went out again, and the keeper of records was at the table and did not look up and did not ask him what it was, and about two people in that lane have said since that a press in that room is used every day by people who are not that office and that the office has never once counted what goes into it and never once looked, and that a body corporate that has answered a figure it cannot refuse with nothing for four years and a half has a press in the corner of its room that anybody at all may put anything into, and that this is the fourth year and a half and the only office in that county that has anything anybody else can use.**

**And she opened it, and the whole of what was in it took about a minute and a half, and the second copyist of about thirty stood at the table and looked at it and did not touch anything.**

There is a bundle of papers about a hedge at a parish boundary that nobody in that county has opened since it was put there. There is a piece of a letter on the back of a sheet about a wall. There is a printed thing with a figure on it that is about two years old. And at the bottom of that press, under all of it, folded once, is a sheet of that office's own printed series, and it is a copy of a thing that is on the shelf six feet away, and it is the contested one.

**And nobody in that county can produce the hand that put it there, and the courier who came up this lane with it did not give a name and was not asked for one, and it went into a press because a press is a press, and about two people in that lane have worked out since that a figure of persons standing against this county under a head that says a day has been in that room twice over since the autumn and that one of the two of them can be carried out of that room by any person at all without saying a word to anybody, and that the county cannot refuse the figure and cannot check the figure and has never been asked to show cause against it, and that of the two copies the one on a shelf is the one nobody can get out of the room.**

“**That is a copy of the thing on the shelf,**” the second copyist said.

“**It is,**” the keeper of records said.

“**Who made it.**”

“**I do not know,**” the keeper of records said, “**and I have been in this room four years and a half and there are two people in this county who could have made it and one of them is standing in front of me with her arms folded and the other one is me, and I have never made it, and I am not going to say that I know that. I am going to say that it is in there and that I cannot check how it got there, and those are two different sentences and only one of them is about this office.**”

And about two people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine opened a press for the first time in four years and a half and found a thing in it that she cannot account for and refused to account for it, and that a county which has answered a figure it cannot refuse with nothing for four years and a half has now looked in the one place in that room where a thing can go without anybody asking, and has found that a thing went there anyway, and that the day-book on the shelf is the only book in that building that can hold the fact that she looked and found it and is not going to get a tenth.

---

**And at about the seventh hour the keeper of records read that register out loud in the ordinary voice, the way a person reads a list she has read before, and the reading took about a minute and a quarter, and nobody in that room asked for it and nobody was offered it again.**

Seven entries, and every one of them a question, and not one of them a finding, and the fifth is about four months old and the sixth and the seventh are both dated in that same month. She read them the way a person reads a list she has read before and not as a thing she is presenting, and about two people in that lane have worked out since that a woman of twenty-nine has read that register aloud in that room for the first time in four years and a half and that nobody asked her to and nobody has asked her to and the reading is the only thing that office has ever done in that room that was not asked for.

“**Seven,**” the second copyist said.

“**Seven,**” the keeper of records said, “**and every one of them is a question and none of them is a finding, and if you want to know what this office has done in four years and a half the answer is on that shelf in two words and a press and neither of them can be shown to anybody.**”

---

**And here is that Monday in pence, item by item, and the total is not spoken until the last of them is written out.**

A candle, a penny. A cloth for the stone of a press, a penny. A sheet of that office's own printed series for this county's own answer, a penny. The clerk's charge for entering a sheet in a register, fourpence.

*The candle is a penny and the cloth is a penny and the sheet is a penny, and three pennies is threepence, and the clerk's charge is fourpence, and threepence and fourpence is seven pence.*

Total: seven pence. **The fourpence a year that this office pays for the door and the number is not in that column and has never been in a column, and the two shillings a month the salt merchant charges for each of these two rooms is not in that column either, and nobody in this building has added the three of them together in four years and a half and nobody is going to.**

---

Nobody walked any part of that Monday with anybody. About nine people went down that lane and about four of the nine were somebody else's business, a mill turned at the low end of the town, about two hundred people were out of their houses and about nine hundred of that town did not leave them, and a woman of about thirty went up that lane in the fourth hour with a pail in her hand and said one thing at each of nine doors and was gone before the noise of the knuckle had finished in the frame, and the keeper of records in the room forty feet away did not hear her, has not asked her where she gets the nine things, and did not ask her on that Monday.

**And a day-book on that shelf stands at nine entries of a thing that was not asked, and it was not opened on that Monday and no tenth is coming, and a tenth would not be a present if it arrived, and a press in the corner of that room is open for the first time in about four years and a half with a copy of a figure standing in it, and there is no form in this kingdom that anybody could be shown which would say what a county does with an instrument that is working and that it cannot check, and the answer this county has given to everything for four years and a half is still one word, and about two people in that lane have said since that the answer is still one word and that the press is open now, and that a woman who has spent four years and a half finding out what a county cannot do has found one thing it cannot do, which is close a press, and that about four thousand people live in that town and the two of them are the only ones who know it and one of the two is not in that room.**
