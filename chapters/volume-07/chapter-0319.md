# Chapter 0319

**The thirteenth day of Hearthmonth, YR 316, a Sunday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour. Nobody is trading. In a town of about four thousand people on a river, a street four hundred yards long has about nine people in it, a lane off the north end of it has a wagon arch in it and a door at the end of it over a weigh-house, a field a mile off holds nine men, and about two hundred of the four thousand are out of their houses. Nobody in that town knows that a day has gone.**

The market was shut and nine people crossed four hundred yards of it at the second hour and nine more at the fourth. The reach was going and it had about nine men on it and not the forty it has on a working day, and a man of fifty and a half was on the second bay putting one finish over another with a bar in his hand he is not using, and the tide was under the wharf and had been since the tenth hour of the day before and goes back at about the third.

**And on the fifth day of a month in that same year there was a day by which cause might be shown to an office two hundred miles off, and about four days of it were left on that morning, and nobody in that town knew that there was a day, and nobody in that county was asked to show cause and was not going to be asked, and that day is gone.**

It went the way a day goes. It was the ninth of that month and now it is the thirteenth and there has been a market on it and a market before it and a tide under the wharf twice and a waggon of salt up the channel road and back down it, and four thousand people in that town have done four thousand ordinary things in four days and not one of them was showing cause to anybody, because there is no writ in that county and no seal and no office in that county at all and the only office in that county is a room with a door on a lane and a number cut into it and a year of a deed cut in under the number.

**And two people in that county know that the day was there, and neither of them has ever said so to the other, and one of the two is twenty-nine and keeps a day-book that nothing in this kingdom can be served out of, and the other is about thirty and has a pail.**

---

**The woman with the pail went up that lane at about the fourth hour, said one thing at each of nine doors, and at all nine of them was gone before the noise of the knuckle had finished in the frame, and those nine are every door in that lane.**

She has said one thing at those nine doors for about a year and the things are the same sentence with a different thing put in front of it, and the things have been a courier, a tide, a market, salt off the first bay, a waggon gone back down the channel road, a room over a wharf open all morning, and a ladder standing in a lane about a mile off that nobody has asked about, and on that Sunday the thing in front of it was a day.

“**There was a day,**” she said, at the first door.

“**Was there,**” a woman said, from behind a door that was not opened.

“**It was the ninth,**” the woman with the pail said, and moved on before the knuckle had finished ringing in the frame.

And at the second door she said it, and at the third, and at the fourth, and at the fifth, and at the sixth, and at the seventh, and at the eighth, and the sentence is the same sentence with a different thing in front of it and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman who has said one thing at nine doors for about a year said a day at nine doors on a Sunday and did not wait at any of them, and that the day is one that nobody in that county had been asked anything about.

**And at the ninth door a man asked her where she had heard it, and that had not happened at any door in a year.**

He is about forty and he keeps no book of anything and he is the only man in that lane who has ever asked her a second question, and he asked it in the ordinary way, with the door about a foot open, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of about thirty who says one thing at nine doors and moves on was asked a question at the ninth door on a Sunday morning and gave an answer that was not an answer and went on.

“**Where did you hear that,**” the man said.

“**I heard it where anybody hears a thing,**” the woman with the pail said.

“**That is not a place.**”

“**No,**” she said, and moved on before the knuckle had finished ringing in the frame, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman who has said the same sentence at nine doors for a year gave a false answer at the ninth one and a true one at the other eight, and that the true one is that she does not know, and that she does not know because the only person in that county who knows is in a room forty feet from a wagon arch and has not told her and is not going to.

“**It was the ninth of the month,**” a man said, from a window above the lane, after she had gone past.

“**It was,**” she said, from about nine feet further on, and did not stop.

**And the keeper of records does not know where the woman with the pail gets the nine things, and has not asked, and about four people in that lane have said since that a woman of twenty-nine has not asked a question of a woman of about thirty in four years and a half and that neither of them has ever given the other a reason.**

---

**And a county of about four thousand people has been in a form since the last day of last year and has answered with nothing, and the instrument for nothing is a day-book that stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and it is not going to get a tenth, and if a tenth came it would not be a present.**

That is what the second year of it is. A market on a Saturday and a market on a Thursday, four hundred yards long both times, four hundred people in the street on each of them, and a tide under the wharf from about the tenth hour to the third, and nine men on a reach on a Sunday and about forty on a working day, and nine men in a yard on a street four hundred yards up the county's own road, and a boy of fifteen copying a blank that nobody has told him about, and a man of about sixty packing lime against a heap with a lid on it who is the only man in that yard not paid out of it, and three bars down and up nine times at a barrier three hundred yards down that road by a man of about fifty-five who says four words and has for nineteen years.

**And a sheet on a shelf in a room with a door on a lane with a figure of persons against this county in the second column under a head that says a day, and about four days of that day were left on the fifth of that month, and four thousand people in that town went about four days and not one of them showed cause, and there is no form in this kingdom that anybody could be shown which would say that a county had answered.**

---

**A man of thirty-two with a limp stood on the deck of that reach at about the fourth hour on that Sunday with no book, and it is the first Sunday in about two years he has been in that county and not counted anything, and nobody in that county knows that.**

About forty mornings a month in a book that has no number on it and is not in any office and cannot be produced by anybody and is not on a form, and about four hundred mornings a year, and he has done it about forty mornings a month on a Monday and on a Tuesday and on a Wednesday and on a Thursday and on a Friday, and **he has not done it on a Sunday in about two years and this was the first Sunday of that and he stood on a deck for about two hours with his hand flat on the timber and counted nothing, and the palm of that hand was warm at about the fourth hour as it is about the fourth hour in that county every day of the year, and about four people in that town have seen that hand laid flat and not one of them has ever said a word about it.**

That man looked at the hand for about four seconds and then at the water going under the first bay and then at nothing at all, and about two people in that yard have said since that a man who has been laying a finish over a finish for a fortnight noticed which hand a stranger was not counting with, and that he has never asked anybody anything about his own work and has never once asked about a hand.

“**You are not writing,**” he said, at about the fifth hour.

“**It is not a working day,**” the man with the limp said.

“**It is a day,**” the man said, and went on with the bar he is not using, and neither of them said anything else that day, and about two people in that yard have worked out that two men standing on a deck on a Sunday with nothing in their hands said about nine words to each other and that nine of the words were about the day and not one of them was about the work.

---

Nobody walked any part of that Sunday with anybody. Two hundred people went out of their houses and about nine hundred of that town did not, nine men came in from a field a mile off at the wet hour, a mill turned at the low end the way it turns, and water that had been under the boards of a lower room over a weigh-house since the tenth hour of the day before went back at about the third hour, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went up a road that belongs to the county by himself on a stick he does not need and has not put down.

**And the day-book in that room over the weigh-house was not opened on that Sunday and does not get a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came, and a woman with a pail has now said out loud at nine doors in a lane in this county that there was a day and has not said it to the room, and the room has not said it to her, and about four hundred people in that town have had a day go past without being asked anything about it, and nobody in that county has worked out yet that the two people in it who knew there was a day are four hundred yards apart and have never spoken about it, and about four people in that lane have.**
