# Chapter 0339

**The third day of Mudmonth, YR 317, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where a reach is four hundred yards down a lane off a market four hundred yards long, a yard is four hundred yards up the county's own road, and a barrier of three bars is three hundred yards further up that road again, and about forty men are on that reach and about nine are in that yard and about nine people went down that lane, and about four thousand people in that town do not know any of it.**

The market was shut and it shuts on a Wednesday. The tide was under the wharf and goes back at about the third. A waggon of salt came up the channel road about the seventh hour and stood at the end of it with nobody under the tailboard and went back down it, and the man who keeps the book of that road's up and down put one line in it, and there is no figure anywhere in what he keeps and no form it could be produced on.

**And the reach was going, and it has four timber bays, and the fourth of them was put up last summer and took three days of pointing and has not appeared on an account since, and the road is open over it and about forty men are on it whatever else the county is happening.**

The arches and the pointing on the second bay are the work of a man of fifty and a half, and the laying of the timber is not, and he has been putting a second finish over a first one for a fortnight with a bar in his hand he is not using. There is a joint on the parapet of that bay a foot and a half short, and nobody in that county has measured it and nobody is going to.

“**You are not using that,**” a man on the first bay said.

“**I am not using that,**” the man of fifty and a half said, and four people in that county have known that a man who has been putting a finish over a finish for a fortnight has been asked once what the bar in his hand is for and answered it truthfully and not been asked a second time, and that a reach does not stop for a market and does not stop for a quiet season either.

**And a man of forty-seven was on the third bay with a barrow and he was not asked anything by anybody on that reach all day, and he has not been asked a sixth time, and four people in that county have said that a man on the roll with a vote and not an office, paid fourpence a week out of a private box, spent a Wednesday on a third bay and was not asked one thing, and that this is now the ordinary outcome and the ordinary outcome is the whole of what happened to him.**

---


**And a man of about nineteen asked a question of that yard at about the fifth hour and got an answer of about four words, and the answer was about the weather, and the weather was not the question.**

“**Is there timber coming,**” the man of about nineteen said.

“**There is timber coming,**” a man said.

“**Is there timber coming for this,**” the man of about nineteen said, and pointed at the empty hod with the other hand, and about four people in that lane have said since that a man of about nineteen put a second question to a man in that yard and was given the first answer again, and that a yard that answers a second question with the first answer has answered it, and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out which of the two of them that is.

---
**And four hundred yards up the county's own road there is a yard with nine men in it, and a board on legs standing four feet off the ground with about nine feet of it along, and a press at the back of that yard that answers to the yard and to no office in this kingdom and that nobody has ever served a thing out of.**

A boy of fifteen stands at that board and copies. He copies the number and he copies the space under the number, and nobody on that yard has ever told him what goes in the space and nobody is going to, and four people in that county have said that a boy of fifteen has been copying an emptiness eleven times a week for about a year, and that an emptiness is a thing a person learns to do as carefully as a thing, and that nobody in that yard has worked out whether he knows it is empty.

**And behind that yard there is one room, and a press stands in it, and the press is the yard's and not any office's, and there is no writ in this kingdom that could get anything out of it, and a bundle has been lying inside that press for years with nothing on the outside of it except what the yard's own book already says.**

The rule of that yard is that nothing belonging to a man goes in that yard's book and only a thing set. A bundle with nothing on the outside of it is therefore either a thing set or a thing somebody put in there, and four people in that lane have said that a press in a yard nobody can serve is the only press in that county nobody can look into, and that the press over a weigh-house four hundred yards down that market has been standing open for six weeks and a half, and that in two hundred yards of road there are two presses and one can be opened by anybody and one cannot, and that nobody in that county has ever put those two facts near each other.

Nobody in that yard has asked what is in that bundle and nobody is going to, and the boy of fifteen at that board does not copy anything about it because nothing about it is on the yard's book, and two people in that lane have said that a boy of fifteen copies everything he is given and knows nothing about what he is not given, and that this is the safest way anybody in this county has of keeping a thing.

A man of about nineteen is at the back of that yard with an empty hod and has been for about four months, and he has an elder sister of twenty-two that he has not spoken of in a year, and two people in that lane have said that they live about nine hundred yards apart, and that a man of about nineteen is not waiting to be asked and has not been asked and is not going to be.

**And a man of about sixty is packing lime against a heap with a lid on it, with his back to the yard the whole morning, and he is the only man in that yard who is not paid out of it, and he knows a thing about a warp in a lid, and there is nobody in that yard he could say it to who would not want to know why he knows it.**

---


**And the boy of fifteen at that board said the thing he says at the end of a day about four times a week, and nobody on that yard has ever asked him why he says it, and about two people in that lane have said since that a boy of fifteen has been saying the same four words to nine men at the end of a day for about a year and that he says them to a board and not to the men.**

“**That is all of it,**” he said.

“**That is not all of it,**” a man said.

“**That is all of it today,**” the boy said, and copied on, and about four people in that county have said since that a boy of fifteen has added a word to a thing he says every day and that the word is *today*, and that a person who adds a day to a thing has not changed the thing and has changed what the thing is about, and that about nine hundred people in a town two hundred miles off have no idea any of that happened.

---
**And three hundred yards further up that road there are three bars and a hut and a man of about fifty-five who has been in that hut nineteen years and turns those bars down and up for anybody who goes through.**

**And a bar is a bar and not a gate, and there is no form on that barrier and no book at that hut and nothing in that hut but a stool and a lamp that is not lit at that hour and about nine tools on a wall for a barrier that anybody at all can go round at either end in about nine seconds.**

About four people in that lane have said that a man has been turning three bars down and up for nineteen years at a place that anybody can walk round, and that the walking round costs nine seconds, and that the man has never once said that to anybody, and that the county has never once worked out that the only thing that barrier does is stand in the middle of a road for no reason anybody in it can name, and that about four thousand people have gone through it in nineteen years instead of nine seconds and not one of them has thought about the nine seconds.

**And a carter of thirty went up that road and down it again that afternoon with a waggon of his own and would not mind a toll and is not going to say so to anybody either, and two people in that lane have said that a man with a waggon and a barrow has said two things out loud in two days to nobody this month, and that the two of them were about a horse, and that four thousand people in that town do not know a man in that county has offered to be paid and has not been taken up.**

Three bars, and they lift two waggons abreast and never three, and the centre bar has stood at two since before that hut was built. A carter brought one up that would take the pair and not the odd one and wanted the middle bar dropped for a day, and the man in the hut gave him the answer he gives, and the carter came back at it with another question, and the man gave him the same answer a second time, and the waggon went back the way it came without going through at all.

**And nobody in that county has ever asked him where the words come from, and four people in that lane have said that a man who says four words nine times a day has said them about eight thousand times, and that nobody has ever asked him where they came from and nobody is going to, and that about four thousand people have gone through that barrier and not one of them has thought about where they came from either.**

**And about forty mornings a month in a book with no number on it, not in any office, producible by nobody and on no form, and about four hundred mornings a year, and a man of thirty-two with a limp has done that for about two years, and he has a stick he does not need and has never once put down.**

**And a man of thirty-two came out onto the deck of that reach at about the fourth hour with a stick in his hand that he does not need and has never once put down, and there was nothing in his other hand but his own arithmetic, and he counted load points off the timber into it the way a man counts anything he has counted before, and four people in that town have watched him do it and nine hundred people in that town do not know he is standing on that deck at all, and the palm of that left hand was warm at about the fourth hour, which it is about the fourth hour in that county every day of the year, and four people have seen that hand laid flat on cold timber and not one of them has ever said one word to him about it.**

---

**And the whole of what that Wednesday came to is set out here in pennies, and the total is spoken only after the last of them is written out.**

A fathom of string and a stick of sealing wax, twopence. A foot of candle, twopence. A quill, a penny. A sheet of that office's own printed series, a penny. The clerk's charge for entering one sheet in a register, fourpence. A broom for that yard, a penny. A pot of oil for that hut, a penny. A sheet of clean waste paper, a penny.

*The twopence and the twopence is fourpence, the quill with that is five, the sheet with that is six, the charge with that is ten, the broom with that is eleven, the oil with that is twelve, and the waste paper with that is thirteen pence, and thirteen pence is what that Wednesday came to.*

Total: thirteen pence. **What this office pays a year is not in that column, and the rent a salt merchant asks a month for the pair of rooms over that weigh-house is not in that column, and the two of them are two prices of two different objects and nobody in that building has ever added them together.** The fourpence a week paid out of a private box to a man with a barrow on a third bay is a wage and is in no column of that office and may not become one, and lime and string and oil and a broom are the ordinary prices of a working yard and are not the prices of anything anybody has asked about.

---

Nobody walked a part of that Wednesday with anybody. About nine people went down that lane and about four of the nine were somebody else's business, about forty men came off that reach with the road open over it, about nine men came out of that yard, a man of about fifty-five turned three bars down and up nine times and went back to his hut, and a man of thirty-two came back up that road on his own, alone, carrying a stick he has no need of and has never once let go of.

**And about forty men were on four timber bays in a town of about four thousand people on a Wednesday that is not a market day, and a boy of fifteen was copying an emptiness about nine feet along a board four feet off the ground, and a man of about nineteen was holding a barrow with nothing in it, and a man of about sixty was packing lime with his back to a yard, and a man of about fifty-five was turning three bars down and up nine times, and a clerk of about sixty was copying a fair copy in a long room over a wharf, and a woman of twenty-nine was looking at a press in a room with a door on a lane, and a man of thirty-two with a limp was counting load points into a book that does not exist, and four thousand people in that town know none of it and were not asked and are not going to be, and that is the whole of the ordinary work that everything in that county stands on, and there is no column in that county for any of it and there never has been.**
