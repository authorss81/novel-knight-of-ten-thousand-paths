# Chapter 0342

**The eleventh day of Mudmonth, YR 317, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a county that has four market towns in a district and about nine hundred people, in the room behind a shop on the north side of the biggest of those four markets, where there is a press and a frame of about four hundred pigeonholes in it and about two hundred sheets of that office's own printed series on a board in nine piles, and about nine hundred people in that county do not know that the second round of that series went out of that room at the sixth hour of that Thursday with a man in a satchel, and that he had been told something about that room in a room he has been in once, and that he cannot name a person in it.**

**The season turns in about nine days.** That is not a figure anybody in that county keeps. It is what a man says about a season, and a man who carries paper for a living says it about nine days before it turns because the ground goes and a cart that has been out on a bad road is a cart that is not there, and about four people in that county have worked out since that a round of about four hundred and fifty sheets is a thing that has to be finished before a season and not before a day, and that nobody in that county has ever asked the man who says so why.

The second round was made up on the Saturday before. It is on a board in nine piles in that room and it is the two short of the first round, because a woman of about fifty counted it and the count is four hundred and forty-eight and she does not round it and there is nowhere in that county to enter the two.

**And at about the second hour of that Thursday a man with a satchel came into that room and put the satchel on the bench against the wall and started on the piles, and he was the same man who was in that yard a fortnight ago asking a shopkeeper a question, and nobody in that building has said to him that he was there before and he has not said it himself.**

He is about thirty. He sorts into the satchel the way a man sorts into a bag he has sorted into a hundred times, and he does not read them, and about two hundred people in that county have worked out since that a man who has carried two thousand sheets of that series in six years to two thousand doors has never once known what was in the four places and has said so out loud three times in six years in three different places, and that nine hundred people in that county have heard none of the three.

The woman of about fifty was at the board. She did not stop counting and she did not look up, and a man who sorts a bag beside a woman who counts a board for nine minutes in a room is a man in a room and not a man in a conversation, and the two of them had not said a word to each other in that room since the last round went out.

“**You are two short,**” she said.

“**I know I am two short.**”

“**I have not said how I know.**”

“**There is a figure on the corner of the board and the figure is yours and it has been yours every round for nineteen years,**” the man with the satchel said, “**and I have carried two rounds out of this room and both of them were the number on your corner, and I did not know that was a number and thought it was a trade.**”

And the woman of about fifty did not look up, and about two people in that room have worked out since that a man told a woman that he had thought a figure for nineteen years was a trade, and that she has written one in the corner of a board in her own hand since before he came into the building, and that neither of them said anything about it and that the man went back to the satchel.

---

**And at about the fourth hour he stopped and stood with a sheet in his hand and said the thing he has wanted said, and he said it once, and it is the whole of what he said in that room on that day that was not a trade.**

“**I have nine days before the season turns and I want the round done inside them, and I am not going to be in that room when it turns.**”

It is twenty-nine words and it foots at twenty-nine, and the woman of about fifty came to four hundred and forty-eight into the ninth pile while he said it and did not look up and did not answer it, and about nine people in that county have worked out since that a man who carries paper said twenty-nine words in a room in a county of nine hundred people and asked for a thing nobody in that county can give him, which is an ordinary thing to want and is not a thing any of nine hundred people in that county can do anything about.

**And the man of about forty came out of the shop part at the fourth hour and was at the end of the board, and the man with the satchel did not stop sorting, and the man of about forty did not ask him anything, because he has never asked anybody anything in four years and has not begun, and about two people in that county have been saying since that a shopkeeper and a carrier stood four feet apart in a room for nine minutes and neither spoke, and that it is the ordinary way of two men who are both working.**

“**There is a lamp in that room,**” the man of about forty said, at the end of it.

“**There is a lamp in that room,**” the man with the satchel said.

“**I bring it in at the fourth hour in the winter and I take it out again at the seventh and it has been in here every winter I have kept this shop,**” the man of about forty said, “**and I have never once had anybody tell me what a lamp in a room full of paper is.**”

**And that is the whole of what either of them said about it, and the man with the satchel went back to the satchel and the man of about forty went back to the counter, and two people in that county have worked out since that a man was told about a lamp in a room and answered the sentence he was given and did not say the other thing, and that the other thing was a question, and that a man cannot ask about a room he has been in once without saying that he has been in it.**

---

**And he said the other part of it at the sixth hour, when the bag was tied, and he said it to the two of them together and it is about a room he has been in once and is not this room and there is a man in it that he cannot put a shape on.**

“**There is a room with a table in it and a door and one window in another building in this district,**” he said. “**I was in it once. A man at the table told me the room behind this shop is a hazard. He did not say what the hazard was and I did not ask him and I have gone over it nine times since and there is nothing in it I can put in a sentence.**”

“**That is not the same room,**” the woman of about fifty said.

“**It is not the same room. It is a room I have been in once, in a building with a door, and the man at the table told me about this one, and I have not seen him since the last month of the year before last and he is not in that building now and nobody in this county has asked me where he went and I am not going to.**”

“**Then you have a man and a room and no name,**” the man of about forty said.

“**I have a man and a room and no name. And the man had been told by a man, and that one by a man, and that one by a man, and I have not been in the room the last of them said it in and I do not know whether there is anybody standing in it.**”

It is seventy-six words and it foots at seventy-six, and about four people in four counties have worked out since that a man told a shopkeeper and a woman of about fifty that a thing he had been told had come to him through three men and that the fourth of the four is a person he has never met in a room he has never been in, and that the chain is four long and that no person in this county has ever been told the end of it and that he has not gone looking for the end of it and has not said that he wants to.

**And nobody asked him one question about any of that, and about four people in that county have said since that a man put four hours of the most honest thing anybody has said in that building in four years into a room with two people in it and both of them let it lie there, and that one of them is a woman who has not looked up in nineteen years and the other is a man who has not asked a question in four, and that neither of them is a person anybody in that county has ever known to be unkind and that is the whole of what the room did.**

---

**And at the sixth hour the bag went out of that yard on a barrow that a boy of about seventeen pushed up the street, and it had four hundred and forty-eight sheets in it and the two were not in it and nobody entered the two, and the man with the satchel went with it as far as the end of the street and turned round and came back and put the satchel down against the bench against the wall where he had put it at the second hour, and the round goes round nine counties and will be back in about a month and there is no list of it in this county and there never has been.**

“**You will be back in a month,**” the man of about forty said.

“**I will be back in a month and the season will have turned and I will want it finished before that,**” the man with the satchel said, “**and I have said that twice in this room today and I am not going to say it a third time and nobody in this county has to do anything about it.**”

And he said the last of it to the board and not to either of them.

“**There is one thing I cannot do with a thing I have been told,**” he said. “**I cannot go and ask whether it is true. A man who asks whether a room is a hazard is a man who has admitted that he has been told about the room, and the man who told me is not in that building and has not been for about four months, and if I go and ask anybody in this county then the next person to be told anything about that room will be somebody else, and I do not know that they will be told less.**”

**And that is the whole of the cost of that day, and it is one man not asking one question, and he did not ask it, and he has not asked it since and is not going to, and about two people in that county have been saying since that a man who was told a thing in a room he has been in once has spent six years carrying paper into and out of the room it was about and has never once found out whether it is true, and that the reason he has not found out is the true one and not a coward's, and that a county of nine hundred people has no way at all of telling the difference between the two, and that nobody in that county has been asked.**

---

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. Two hundred yards of market carried about four hundred people and about five hundred more did not come out, the mill two days up the water went round, a bench-board four feet off the ground in a yard had nine men at it, three bars on the road above that street went down and up nine times, and a bench of nine across that street did not sit, because it sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week and that was a Thursday.

**And a room over a byre across that street stood open from the second hour to the seventh with nobody in it and nothing in front of it, and under the fourth step of the outside stair of that room there is a slip of paper about nine inches by six, and there are nine marks on it under a figure, and it said nine before that Thursday and it said nine after it, and nobody in that hall has ever entered that count on a form, and in a room forty feet up that lane in a county two hundred miles off there is a book about nine inches by six that stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and nobody is going to put a tenth in it, and a tenth would not be a present if it came.**

**And a bag of four hundred and forty-eight sheets of that office's own printed series went out of a shop in a county of about nine hundred people on a Thursday in the sixth hour, and nine counties got it in the ordinary way and nine hundred people in each of them will read it or not read it, and nobody in any of the nine has ever been told that there was a figure written in the corner of a board in a shop in a room where a lamp is brought in at the fourth hour in the winter and taken out again at the seventh, and that a man was in that room and said the truest thing anybody in that county has said in four years and two people in it let it lie there.**
