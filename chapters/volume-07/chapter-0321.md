# Chapter 0321

**The sixteenth day of Hearthmonth, YR 316, a Wednesday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, with a mill at the low end of the water, two hundred feet of wharf above the mill, a glass works and a bread oven and two hundred yards of street between them, nine men in the glass works, and about four hundred people in that valley, of whom about nine have wanted a thing since before a season turned and have not said so out loud in a room.**

The water came up at the low end of that valley twice a day and the mill took it and the wharf above the mill did nine things a day, and the market stood on two hundred yards of street four days out of seven, and the day before was the ninth of those four, and this was the first of the three after it, and the bread oven at the top of the lane went through two batches and about nine loaves to a batch. A boy of about nine went up the made road with a barrow four days out of seven and did not stop at the standing finish on the end of it and carried on past it, and about two hundred of a generation that has never once been up the low end of that valley came down off the hill behind them in the last year and are still coming down.

**And behind the glass works there was a trough with a lid on it, and the lid was a board and the board had a piece across it, and the trough was filled about four months ago on nine days by nine men, and about four people in that valley have worked out since that a trough behind a works with a lid on it is a thing nine men do and not a thing one man does, and that nobody in that valley was told about any of the nine days and nobody has asked.**

Nine men make glass in that shed and the trade they work in has about four words in it for a fault nobody standing outside the shed could ever see, and one of those four is the word those men use for a piece that has nothing whatever wrong with it that can be seen from outside, and that is the whole of what the word is and nobody in that works has ever been asked to take it any further than that.

---

**And the woman with the slate was at the top of the lane at the fifth hour and did not take it out, and she has carried one for eleven years and has not put a name on anything in that room since a day in the autumn when a man said a thing out loud that was not his and she wrote one line and turned the slate over against her hip and would not show it to anybody.**

“**I have not written it,**” she said, to nobody.

“**You are the only one here who could,**” a man said.

“**I have not written it and I am not going to write it in a room with two hundred people standing at the back of it,**” she said. “**If I put it on this slate then it is on it for every house in that valley until the end of the year, and not one of those houses has been asked anything.**”

Four people in that room have said since that a woman who has carried a slate for eleven years stood in a room with a covered vessel standing in it and did not write the vessel down, and that the one person in that valley who could have written it down is the one person whose hand cannot be served out of afterwards.

---

**It was named once. A man with his hands in a cooling box named it, in that shed, in the second hour of that morning, and nobody there used the word again that day, and no one in that valley has used it since.**

“**Say the word for it,**” a man said.

“**A clear,**” the man with his hands in the box said.

“**That is a word for a piece,**” the man said.

“**It is a word for a piece,**” the man with his hands in the box said, “**and it is the word we use for a piece and it is the word for the trough, and I did not make it and neither of the nine of us did and we filled it on nine days and I am not going to say what is in it, because if I say what is in it then a man in a county four hundred miles off will write it down in a place where it is a different thing entirely, and that is not a thing I am going to do with a word.**”

And he went back to his box, and about two people in that works have worked out since that a man with his hands in a cooling box named a thing that has stood at the low end of that water for four months and then declined to say one word more about it, and that a man who names a thing is about nine miles from the end of it and the other nine miles are the road between here and a county nobody keeps, and there is no road.

**And nothing whatever was explained by it. There is a trough behind a works with a lid on it and a word for it in the mouth of nine men, and the word is a word those men already used about glass, and about four people in that valley have said since that a cure in a valley of about four hundred people has a trade name and no other name, and that a thing with only a trade name cannot be argued over in a room, because the only people in that valley who know what the word means are nine men in a shed and not one of them has ever been asked to say more.**

---

**And about nine people in that valley had wanted it for about four months, and not one of them had said so out loud in a room, and the reason they had not is the plainest reason in the world and it is that each of them found out separately.**

A man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work worked it out in about a month and told nobody. They had come down to the low gate one at a time, and they had come on nine different days spread over about four months, and every one of them had stood at that gate and asked after the trough in the voice a person uses for a thing they are asking the price of, and not one of them had asked in front of a second person, and not one of them had asked whether anybody else had asked, and when the man of thirty-four put the question in a yard at the top of the lane, in about a month, four of them said yes in about four seconds each and the rest said nothing at all, and about two people in that valley have worked out since that a man with sixteen men and no work found out about nine people's secret in a yard by asking one question in the ordinary way, and that asking the question cost him nothing, and that the cost was that four people in that valley now know that other people in that valley had wanted it, and there is no room in that valley in which that can be said twice.

“**You could have asked me,**” a man said, in that yard, in about a month.

“**I have asked nobody anything since a road,**” the man of thirty-four said, “**and I dug it and it goes nowhere, and I am not going to pretend that a question over a trough is a different sort of question from the one I have been asking with my hands for a year.**”

---

**And a man of forty came up that lane at the sixth hour and stood at the mouth of it and looked at the covered vessel from nine feet off for as long as it takes to look at nine feet of nothing, and he is one of the nine on those benches and has not said a word in that room in two years.**

“**I have had some,**” he said, in a room with two hundred people at the back of it, and he said it in the voice a man uses when he is telling somebody the price of a thing, and about two hundred people at the back of that room heard it and about nine of them understood what it was and those nine had known for about four months and none of them had said.

“**Say the rest of it,**” the man of thirty-four said.

“**The rest of it is for the room,**” the man of forty said, “**and a room gets it when it asks, and this room has not asked me anything since the aisle was still used.**”

Four people in that valley have said since that a man of forty said four sentences in a room at the top of a lane and three of them were a price and one of them was a condition, and that the condition is the first anybody in that valley has ever put on a thing in that room, and that a room which has never asked anybody anything cannot be made to ask by nine men with a board across a trough.

---

**And the mill went on at the low end of the water, and a man at that wheel had not left it once in about two years and did not leave it that day, and he has had two measures out of the trough and about two people at the low end of that water know it and about four hundred people in that valley do not.**

He is a man at a wheel and he has been at that wheel for longer than anybody in that valley has been counting, and he does not get down. He did not come up the lane on that day and nobody expected him to and nobody asked him whether he wanted the thing in the trough, and the asking was not a thing anybody in that valley had organised, and about two people in that valley have said since that a man who cannot leave a wheel is the only person in a place of about four hundred people who cannot be asked a question by anybody, and that a room which decides things by asking everybody has decided already without the one man in it who would have had a different view.

---

**And at about the eighth hour a boy with a barrow carried it up the lane from the low gate to the top, in a covered vessel with a board across it, and the boy was not asked anything and asked nothing and carried on the way he always carries on.**

That room is not a bench. The twenty-nine benches bolted down in four rows are the room, and about nine people were on those benches and about thirty were standing in that room and about two hundred were at the back of it, and the four feet of floor between the two middle rows was empty, as it has been for about three generations, and the two hundred at the back had come up a lane to look at a thing in a room and about nine of them did not know what the thing was.

And it was set down on the boards at the mouth of the aisle.

**And the aisle is four feet wide and it goes three generations without being stood in, and nothing has been set down in it either in about a hundred and forty years, and about four people in that valley have said since that a covered vessel went onto the boards at the mouth of a four-foot aisle in front of nine people on benches and about thirty standing in a room and about two hundred at the back, and that the number cut into the end of the one bench at the back of the right-hand row is old, and is not a count of people, and that nobody in that valley has ever said out loud what it is a count of, and that a thing set down in front of a room is a thing the room has been asked about and a thing in a column is a thing the column has been asked about, and that those are two different sentences and about four people in that valley have worked out that they have only ever needed the first one.**

Nobody walked any part of that day with anybody. The mill went on, the wharf did about nine things, a market stood down for the third day running and the oven went through two batches, and about two hundred people went down off that hill, and nine men in a shed at the low end of the water went home at the dark hour, and about four hundred people in that valley went through an ordinary evening not knowing that a thing with a lid on it was standing in an aisle four feet wide at the top of a lane, and that the aisle had not been used since before any of them was born, and that the word for it had been said once in a shed and is the word those nine men use for a piece of glass with nothing wrong in it that can be seen from outside, and nobody in that valley has asked the nine men what is in the trough and nobody in that valley is going to.

**And there is no line in that room for what a person wanted before a morning and had not said, and about nine people in that valley wanted it before that morning and had not said it to each other, and the room found out the way it finds out everything, which is by somebody saying it out loud.**
