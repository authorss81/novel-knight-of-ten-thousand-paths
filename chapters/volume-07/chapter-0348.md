# Chapter 0348

**The first day of Longlight, YR 317, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river in a county two hundred miles off a provincial town of about nine hundred people, at a lock on that river with a room over it and a counter in it and a book on the counter, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a woman of thirty-eight came two hundred miles to that room in the fourth hour and was refused in it a second time by a person she had never met, in a room that is not a chamber and is not a long room over a wharf and is not a room in a building of four floors, and that the refusal was entered.**

A lock is not an office and the room over it is not a chamber and nobody in that room is a member of anything. There is a counter about nine feet long with a hatch in it, and a book on the counter, and that one is the book of that lock and it has lain on that counter since before the town had a market four hundred yards long, and it takes what goes up, what comes down, what a man paid and the hour, and about four people in that town have said since that it is the only book in this county that anybody can be shown on demand and that nobody has ever asked to be shown one.

**And a woman of about sixty keeps it, and has kept it for about thirty-one years, and she is not a keeper of records and has never been one, and there is no column in it for what anybody asked.**

The tide was under the wharf at the north end of that market and goes back at about the third hour. That market is four hundred yards long and on a Friday it is shut and about nine people go down the lane off its north end. A cart of salt came up the channel road the evening before and was at the end of it with nobody under the tailboard and went back down it, and a man who keeps a book of that road's comings and goings wrote one line about it, and there is no figure in what he keeps and no form it could be produced on.

**And in a field a mile off that town a boundary oath-stone stands in a hedge with a figure cut into the face of it that is twenty years old, and it is read and renewed once in nineteen years, and the last time was the last time, and the next one is nineteen years off that one and is not in this month and is not in this year, and the clerk of about sixty who keeps the minute of that reading keeps two kinds of minute and is not going to keep a third, and about four people in that county have said since that a stone with a figure in it that is twenty years old is the only object in that county that is not asking anybody for anything, and that four thousand people in that town do not know it is there.**

**And about forty men were on the timber bays four hundred yards down a lane off that market with the road open over them, and nine were in a yard four hundred yards the other way, and three bars on the county's own road above that street were turned down and up nine times by a man in a hut, and not one of those three places is a hundred yards from that lock and not one of them knows what is written in a book on a counter over it.**

---

**And she came up that lane at the fourth hour on her own and she had come two hundred miles and she was not in that room for more than a quarter of an hour, and about two people in that town have said since that a woman of thirty-eight stood at a counter over a lock in a town of four thousand people and asked a woman of about sixty for a line in a book, that the asking took four minutes, and that the whole of it is in a book on a counter and that four thousand people in that town cannot be shown it.**

She has asked for nine years for a record to show what was asked. She said it once, in nineteen sentences, standing, in a chamber in another building in a provincial town, and a witness performed the best possible version of her want in front of her in that room and it did not work, and she entered that room as a room in the minute anyway, and the finding at the end of that proceeding is a figure. She was refused a ninth time on the last day of the year before last, in a long room over a wharf in this county, by a bench of nine, in eleven words with a reason in it, and nobody thanked her for asking and nobody thanked her for being answered and nobody in that room told her she was right.

**And the second refusal cost her something the first one did not, and what it cost was a line in a book that anybody in that county can walk in and ask to read.**

“**I want a line in it,**” she said. “**Not a finding. Not what was decided. A line that says what was asked, and the day it was asked, and that it was asked and refused.**”

“**No,**” the woman of about sixty said.

“**You have not asked me why.**”

“**No. I have kept it for about thirty-one years and in that time I have had about four thousand people come to this counter and not one of them has ever asked me to put in it what they came to ask me. They ask me what the toll is. They ask me what the pound is. They ask me whether the water is up. Nobody has ever come to that counter and asked me to keep what they said, and if I do it for one then I have to do it for the rest of them, and about four thousand people live in this town and about nine hundred of them come to this counter in a year.**”

“**Then the line is not for everybody. It is for me.**”

“**It is not for anybody or it is for everybody, and there is no third thing, and I am not going to invent a third thing in a book that has been on this counter for thirty-one years.**”

---

**And the water under that lock was the ordinary water. It came up the cut twice a day and went down twice a day, and about four boats went through that lock in a week, and about nine of that town make their living off the water between that lock and the wharf at the north end of the market, and a man of about forty was a lock hand and had been one for about twenty-two years and worked the gate with a bar and did not look up at anybody who came to that counter with anything except a coin, and about two people in that town have been saying since that a man who will not look up at a woman of thirty-eight from the Crown's office is doing the ordinary work of his trade in the ordinary way, and that nobody on that reach or in that yard or at that barrier has ever been asked about a woman of thirty-eight, and that none of the three of them would have known what to do.**

**And the woman of about sixty entered it, and the entering is the cost, and she entered it correctly and in the ordinary way and about two people in that town have worked out since that a woman of about sixty wrote three lines in a lock book in about nine seconds and that all three of them are true and that the first of them is the one nobody in that county can check.**

Three lines. The day. That somebody stood at that counter and asked for a line in the book on it. That it was refused.

“**You have not put what I asked for,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**No.**”

“**Then the third line is not what it says it is. The third line says that somebody asked for a line. It does not say what the line was.**”

“**It does not,**” the woman of about sixty said, “**and I am not going to write a column for it and I am not going to spoil thirty-one years of a book to hold one thing, and you can read that page any day of the week and any person in this town can read it, and I have not written your name on it because I do not know your name and I have not asked and I am not going to ask, and if I had written your name on it then about four thousand people in this town could be shown the name of a woman of the Crown's office standing at a counter over a lock, and that is not a thing this county does.**”

**And the first refusal was entered in a room over a wharf in this county about a year ago by a clerk of about sixty in his own hand under a line that said the bench did not vote, and it is on a minute that a stranger cannot be shown and that nobody in that county has ever asked to see, and the ninth mark under eight on a page about nine inches by six under the fourth step of that room's outside stair is the only record of it in this kingdom, and nobody has ever entered that count on a form.**

**And this one is in a book on a counter that anybody can walk in and read, and about two people in that town have been saying since that the first one was hidden and this one is not, and that a woman of thirty-eight has now been refused twice and both times correctly, and that the second one is the more expensive of the two by a long way and that the difference between the two is that the first one was a page under a stair and this one is a page in a book, and that nobody thanked her for the first asking and nobody has thanked her for the second and nobody in either room told her that being right about it is what she is and it is not.**

---

**And the door at the top of that stair, forty feet back up the lane from the counter, has a number cut into it and a year of a deed cut in under the number, and this office pays fourpence a year for it, and the two rooms over the weigh-house beside it are two shillings a month each to the man who lets them, and that lock book on that counter has a column for a lock and a column for a toll and neither of those two figures is in either of those two prices, and about four thousand people in that town have been in and out of that lane for nine years and have never once had the two of them put side by side and neither has anybody who keeps a book about it.**

**And a press in the corner of that room at the top of that stair has been standing open for fourteen weeks and a half and nobody has shut it, and four things are on a shelf six feet from it and a fifth is in the press, and the register on the table is at seven entries and every one of them is a question and not one of them is a finding, and the day-book beside it is at nine entries of a thing nobody asked and is not going to get a tenth, and two people in that lane have been saying since that a woman of twenty-nine left it open on purpose over a holiday and that it has been open every working day since, and that a county of four thousand people has two presses in it now, two hundred yards apart, and that one of them can be opened by anybody who walks up that lane and that the other one cannot be opened by anybody at all, and that nobody in that county has ever set the two of them side by side and nobody is going to.**

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. About nine people went down that lane and about four of the nine were somebody else's business, and about two hundred people in that town were out of their houses and about three thousand eight hundred were not, and the tide went back at about the third hour and drew the stones of that market clean for about two feet on each side.

**And a woman of thirty-eight walked back down a lane on her own at about the fifth hour and got on a cart at the end of it and went two hundred miles, and about two people in that town have said since that a woman came two hundred miles and was refused in a quarter of an hour and did not say one word about the first refusal and did not thank anybody and was not thanked, and that the page on that counter says a person asked for a line on that day and it does not say what the line was, and that a woman of about sixty who has kept a book for about thirty-one years has now been the second person in this county to tell her no, and that the first of those was nine people sitting on a bench and the second of them was one woman at a counter, and that neither of them told her she was wrong and that about four thousand people in that town do not know that either of those two things happened and that the nine under that fourth stair is nine and said nine before that Friday and said nine after it.**
