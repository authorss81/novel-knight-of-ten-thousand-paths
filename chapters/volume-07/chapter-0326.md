# Chapter 0326

**The tenth day of Goatmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a yard at the top of a lane in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, where a man of thirty-four has sixteen men and no work, and the market was standing on two hundred yards of street at the low end of that water and about two hundred people were on it, and about nine of the sixteen men in that yard heard a thing at about the second hour that was said by a man with a limp about a woman he had met once, and not one of them has ever been up the water to the steading where she lives.**

The yard is a yard and not a bench. There are sixteen men in it and no work for any of them since the water turned, and nine of them are in it on any morning and the rest are wherever the rest of a valley of about four hundred people is, and there is a bar, and there is a stack of boards at the back of it that is about two years old and has been rained on, and a man of fifty and a half would have known in about four seconds whether that stack was worth moving and is four hundred miles off and was never asked.

He was in that yard at about the fourth hour. He had been up the water on his own business and had come down the lane at about the sixth the evening before, and he stood nine feet off the man with the limp and said a thing about a woman and went back to his boards, and about two people in that valley have been saying since that a man of thirty-four stood nine feet off a stranger and said one sentence and then went to work, and that the stranger did not follow him and did not ask him what he meant by four days, and that of the two of them the man of thirty-four is the one who came and the man with the limp is the one who let him.

---

**And at the second hour a man of twenty-five in that yard asked the stranger a question, in the ordinary way, because that is what a yard does with a man who is in it, and the man with the limp answered it, and what he answered was not his.**

“**You have been up the water,**” the man of twenty-five said. “**Did you see the man who went in at the third span yesterday.**”

“**He was not cold,**” the man with the limp said.

And nine men in that yard stopped what they were doing, and the man of thirty-four stopped what he was doing, and the sentence stood in the air of that yard for four seconds, and it was a true sentence and it was about a man at the third span and it had nothing whatever to do with the man at the third span.

**What it was about was a woman who died in the winter before last in a valley of about four hundred people, and the fire at the low end of the water that had not been a fire, and a name that went and came back an hour later, and a woman of about thirty who said on a wall at the fourth hour five days ago that a person handed the same thing twice has not had the first of them either, and that the second of those is not the first of those and does not count.**

“**He is not my cold,**” the man with the limp said, four seconds later, and nine men in that yard have said since that a stranger in their yard said the word cold about a woman he had met once and then said the word was not his, and that the man of twenty-five asked the second question in about nine seconds and it was the only question anybody in that yard had asked him since he came up that road and it was a different question from the first one, and that a yard in a valley of about four hundred people is a place where a thing said in it goes four hundred miles because nobody there has ever heard of the woman it is about.

“**Whose cold,**” the man of twenty-five said.

“**I do not know,**” the man with the limp said, and did not say anything else, and went and stood at the back of that yard about nine feet off the boards with his left palm flat on the top of a stack of boards two years old, and the palm was warm at about the fourth hour in that county every day of a year and nine men in that yard could have put their hands on that stack and would not have noticed anything about it, and did not.

---

**And at about the fourth hour the man of thirty-four put a bar down across two boards and left it there and came about nine feet off the man with the limp, and what he said took about a minute and a quarter.**

“**I am going to say a thing and then I am going to go back to the boards,**” he said. “**I have watched you do that twice. The first was on the second morning you were in this valley, in this yard, when somebody asked you what you did before you were a man with a stick and you answered with a woman's hand. The second was five days ago at a gate up the water and I was at the second gate with a cart and I did not hear a word of it and I did not need to, because I have seen sixteen men do that in about a year and it looks the same every time, and it looked the same on you.**”

“**How many is twice,**” the man with the limp said.

“**Twice,**” the man of thirty-four said. “**And the second one was worse and you did not know it was the second one, and if I do not say this to you today then you will do a third one and believe it is the first, and I have watched three men in my life do the first one twice and not know, and one of them was me, and he did it again nine years later in a different yard and it was worse then and he was standing next to it.**”

---

**And a man of about twenty-five in that yard went back to the boards at the seventh hour and did not say one word to anybody about the word cold, and about four people in that yard have worked out since that a man of twenty-five heard a stranger answer a question about a man who went in at a third span with a sentence about a woman and has not mentioned it to one of the fifteen men he works beside, and that he will be at that span in four days, and that nine men in a yard in a valley of about four hundred people are now carrying a woman of about thirty who is alive and does not know any of them and has not been told and will not be.**

---

**And the man with the limp did not say that he was right and did not say that he was wrong, and he asked one question, and it is the only question in nine days that anybody in that valley has put to him and it is not about a road or a cross or a roll or a lamp or anything that has a name in it.**

“**Why is the second one worse,**” he said.

And a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work who dug a road four hundred yards long that goes nowhere and has never once been asked what it is for took nine seconds over that, and about two people in that yard have said since that a man who has not been asked about a road in a year was asked a question about himself by the man with the stick and answered it in nine seconds and that the answer cost him something he has not got a name for.

“**The first one you find out,**” he said. “**You say a woman's thing and then you say it is not yours and the people who are in the room can go home. Nobody is left holding anything. This morning nine men in a yard have got a woman out of that yard in their heads and they do not know her and they cannot put her down anywhere, and one of them is twenty-five and he will be at the third span in a few days and he will think about the word cold for a year, and four of the nine will think about it for nine years, and I have watched that happen to about four men in my life and I am not going to watch it happen to a fifth one in a yard where I have to work in the morning.**”

“**What would you have me do,**” the man with the limp said.

“**I would have you ask somebody what is yours before you answer the next man,**” the man of thirty-four said, “**and I would have you ask it out loud, in front of the people in the room, and I would have you accept that they will not answer it, because I have tried it and they do not answer it and the not-answering is the whole of what a room can do.**”

And then he said the other thing, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and it is the last thing he said in that yard that day and he has not said it since and is not going to.

“**I have sixteen men and no work, and I dug a road that goes nowhere, and about nine people in this valley have taken a thing that works and about four hundred have not and nobody was stopped, and a man who does not know whose grief he is carrying is the last thing this valley needs this year, and I have not asked you for anything, and I have watched you for nine days, and that is all of it.**”

---

**And at about the seventh hour he was still in that yard, and he did not ask anybody what was his, and nobody in that yard said anything to him, and about two people in that valley have been saying since that a man was told to ask a room a question out loud in front of the people in it and did not, and that the not-asking is the first half of the answer and that he will be in this valley again and the other half is still open and nobody in that valley is going to be the one who closes it.**

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. The mill went on at the low end of the water and the man at that wheel did not get down, and about two hundred people were on two hundred yards of market street, the oven went through two batches, a boy of about nine went up a made road past the standing finish with a barrow, a man of forty walked down to the low gate and back up the lane with nothing, and about four hundred people in that valley went through their day not knowing that a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work told a man with a limp that he had watched him do a thing twice and that the second time was worse, and that the man with the limp asked one question about why, and that the answer was that nine men in a yard now have a woman they have never met and cannot put down anywhere, and that neither man said one word about it to anybody else and there is no line in that valley on which either of those two things can be put down and got back out of, and about two people in that yard have said since that a man who came up a road of nine days and asked a woman to correct him and a woman told him she did not believe him and then a man who had known him for nine days told him the number, and that the number is two, and that a man told a number is not the same thing as a man told the truth, and that nobody in that valley is going to find out which of the two he has, and that the woman up the water was right about one half of it and will never be told about the other half.
