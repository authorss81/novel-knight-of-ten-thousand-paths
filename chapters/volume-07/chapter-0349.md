# Chapter 0349

**The sixteenth day of Longlight, YR 317, a Saturday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the fourth hour and a half and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river in a county two hundred miles off a provincial town of about nine hundred people, where a market four hundred yards long is standing with about four hundred people in it, and a bell of nine stone stands a hundred yards up that county's own road, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that a month has gone by in four counties in which nothing at all has been entered.**

The market stood at the second hour and four hundred people were in it out of four thousand, and three thousand six hundred were not out of their houses, and the tide went back at about the third hour, and the stones of that market came clean for about two feet either side of the middle of them. A cart of salt came up the channel road and was at the end of it a while with nobody under the tailboard and then went back down it, and a man who keeps a book of that road's comings and goings wrote one line about it and shut the book.

**And a month has gone by in four counties in which nothing at all has been entered, and that is the first thing anybody has found out this year that nobody had gone looking for, and two people in that town have said since that a month with nothing entered in it is not a quiet season and is the other thing, and that the two of those are the same size and neither of them is in anybody's ledger.**

Nothing entered. About four hundred sheets a month go out of a building of four floors in a provincial town into nine counties and about four hundred come back, and nine of every nine that come back are entered in a press because a return is a return. And for four weeks now about nine of the four hundred have come back to that building and nine of the nine have not been entered, and nobody in that building has entered a line in four weeks, and the tray still goes into the passage at the third hour and comes out at the fourth, and it has gone round about six thousand six hundred times in twenty-two years and has never once been asked whether anybody wanted it.

**And about nine hundred sheets of that series go round nine counties in a year in two rounds from a county that has four market towns in a district and about nine hundred people, and a frame of about four hundred pigeonholes in a room behind a shop used to be made up into a round in a day and a half by a woman of about fifty, and that room is not a room any more, and the second round of the year went out of a county of nine hundred people nine weeks before this and has not come back and nobody in that county has asked about it and there is no form in that county on which the asking could be entered.**

And so: nothing entered. The register in a room over a weigh-house is at seven entries, all of them questions, and not one of them is a finding. The day-book beside it is at nine and does not get a tenth. Nine sets of forty sheets are held in four counties by nine people and not one of the nine has entered a line about it. A clerk of about sixty who has kept a minute for nineteen years has written nothing in it since the last time a bench sat. And a man of about fifty-five turns three bars at a barrier that anybody at all can go round at either end in nine seconds and has not written that down in nineteen years either.

**And about nine people in four counties have said since that the whole of what is happening in four counties at once is not a schedule that has stopped, because a schedule that has stopped is a thing somebody decided, and nobody has decided anything, and about four hundred people in four counties have gone four weeks without being asked a single question, and that is not a thing anybody set out to do.**

---

**And the rule that came out of it is the only rule anybody made in a year in four counties and nobody made it, and it is one sentence, and it is said once, at about the fourth hour of that Saturday, by a woman of about fifty who has about ninety sheets in a room over a byre in a county two hundred miles off, and it is in no register and it is in no minute and there is no form for it in this kingdom.**

“**Each of the nine is held by one person and everybody in the four counties knows which person, and that is the whole of it, and nobody made it and nobody wrote it down and there is no form for it, and I am the ninth and I have not asked anybody to say it out loud and I am not going to be asked why I have it.**”

It is sixty-nine words and it foots at sixty-nine, and about nine people in four counties have said since that a woman of about fifty said the first rule of this year out loud once, in a room over a byre, in about eleven seconds, and that nobody improved on it, and that a rule that one person has said out loud in one room and that four counties know about is not a rule at all in the sense this kingdom uses the word, and that it is the first thing anybody in four counties has agreed on since before any of them can remember and that nothing was minuted.

**And two people asked about it in the room and neither of them got a rule out of it, and one of them got a person, and about nine people in four counties have been saying since that a rule which is nine sentences long can be heard once in a room over a byre and can be asked about twice and can be written down by nobody, and that is the correct outcome and not a defect in it.**

“**Is that a rule,**” the man of about forty said, from the door.

“**It is a sentence,**” the woman of about fifty said. “**A rule has somebody behind it. There is nobody behind that one and there is not going to be, and if there were I should want to know who, and I should want to be told where the sentence would be kept, and I have asked you and you have not told me where it would be kept, because there is nowhere.**”

“**Then what do we call it,**” the man of about forty said.

“**I have never once in nineteen years had to call anything anything,**” she said, “**and I have said that sentence out loud once and I am not going to say it a second time, and you have your ninety sheets and I have said what they are and there is nothing else about them that anybody in this county is going to be told this year.**”

And about nine people in four counties have worked out since that a woman of about fifty said the whole of what a rule is in a room in about sixty-four words and that the sixty-fourth word was *year*, and that a county of about nine hundred people has a rule in it now and has had it for about half an hour, and that nobody in that county will be able to point at it in nine years and that this is the correct outcome and about two hundred people in that county have worked that out and nobody has said so out loud.

**And what is at the other end of it is a trade and not a name, and a man of thirty-two with a limp took a leaf out of the inside pocket of a coat at about the fourth hour of that Saturday and looked at the seventh line on it, and it is the seventh time he has looked at it, and the seventh line says *a person*, and a trade is not a person and a person is not a trade, and he folded the leaf in three and put it back and did not finish it and has not finished it.**

Nobody in that county knows that he did that and nobody in that county is going to, and about two hundred people in four counties have worked out since that a man has now got an answer to a question he has carried for two years and that the answer is the kind of thing a person is, and that a person is not the same thing as a person, and that a page with nine lines on it and an hour against each of them is the only record in four counties with a person at the front of every one of its lines, and that it has not been filled in since a week in which a man of about thirty read nine lines up to himself and asked one question and got nothing.

---

**And the bell went at the fourth hour and a half of that Saturday, a hundred yards up that county's own road, and it went at the fourth hour and a half the way it has gone every day, and nobody in that county can be asked who keeps it and there is no answer in that county and nobody is going to be able to give one.**

It is nine stone of green-black bronze with a band of forty-one rivets in it, and it goes on a line pulled together on the count of four, and the two people on that line are an apprentice of the steward's who has had the job two years and three months and is still not good at it, and a carpenter who is a hand on that yard and not a keeper of anything. Neither of the two is paid by the guild out of that bell and neither belongs to anything, and the arrangement has been a boy and a carpenter since the ninth of a month in the year before last, and it is not written down anywhere in that county and is not a rule and is not going to be.

The boy has a bar he is not using and the carpenter has a bar he is not using either, and about two people in that county have worked out since that the count of four is the arrangement and is not a rule and is not written down anywhere in that county and is not going to be.

“**Two,**” the boy said, on the third pull, which is a thing he has done about four times in two years and calls the count himself when nobody else has said it.

“**Two,**” the carpenter said.

“**That is two,**” the boy said, “**and I have said two and the line has not gone and I am going to say two again,**” and about two people in that county have been saying since that a boy has been the second voice on that count for two years and three months and that he did not learn it from anybody and that nobody has ever asked him where he got it and that four thousand people in that town have heard two thousand of those and not one of them has ever wondered.

About four thousand people in that town have heard it about four hundred times this year, and nine hundred of them have heard it and not gone out of their houses, and nobody in that town has ever once asked who keeps it, and two people in that county have been saying since that nobody in it can be asked a question about that bell, and that a thing which cannot be asked about is a thing everybody in the county can answer for, and that four hundred people in that town have never worked that out and are not going to.

**And it went at the fourth hour and a half on that Saturday in a month in which nothing had been entered anywhere in four counties, and it went on a market morning, and it will go on the next one and the one after that, and about nine people in that county have said since that a month of silence and a bell going every day at the fourth hour and a half are two facts and that nobody in that county has ever put them in the same sentence, and that this is the whole of what a bell is for.**

---

**And that Saturday came to twenty-one pence, and here is the twenty-one, and the items are given in no order and they are all in this county and none of them is a rate.**

There are ten items, and four of them are a penny apiece, which is a candle, a wick, a quill, and a sheet of clean waste paper. One is a fathom of string at threepence. Three are twopence apiece, which is a stick of sealing wax, a pot of oil for a lamp, and a leather apron. And two are fourpence apiece, which is what it costs to put a line in a book and what it costs to put a line in a second book, and those two are the only things anybody in that county has charged anybody for, and everything else in the list is what a person spends their own money on.

*Four and three and six and eight, and that is twenty-one, and the twenty-one pence is the whole of that Saturday.*

Total: twenty-one pence. **A sheet of that office's own printed series is a penny a sheet and about four hundred of them go out of a building two hundred miles off in a month and nobody in that building has entered a line in four weeks, and a penny a sheet is not a rate and a month of nothing entered is not a saving and neither of those two is in any column. What a man on that roll is paid out of a private box is his wage and does not belong in any column of that office and is never going to become one, and the fourpence a year that a small office pays for a door with a number cut into it is not in the twenty-one above and the two shillings a month that the salt merchant asks for each of two rooms over a weigh-house is not in it either, and those are two prices of two different objects and nobody in that county has added one of them to the other of them in four years and a half.**

---

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. About four hundred people were in four hundred yards of market and three thousand six hundred did not leave their houses, forty men came off a reach with the road open over it, nine men came out of a yard, a man of about fifty-five turned three bars down and up nine times and went back to a hut, and a man of thirty-two with a limp walked up that county's own road on his own at the fourth hour and was at the end of it and came back down it.

**And a page about nine inches by six under the fourth step of an outside stair at the north end of that market said nine before that Saturday and said nine after it, and nobody in that hall has ever entered that count on a form, and a day-book on a shelf forty feet up a lane stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and nobody is going to put a tenth in it, and about nine people on that floor have never opened it on any day anybody keeps, and a press in the corner of that room has been standing open for sixteen weeks and a half with a copy of a figure in it, and a county of about four thousand people has answered a figure of persons standing against it with nothing for about a year and has now been asked nothing for four weeks along with three other counties, and nobody in any of the four knows whether that is because there is nothing or because the thing has stopped going, and a bell went at the fourth hour and a half a hundred yards up that county's own road, and it is not a schedule that is late and it is not a schedule that has stopped, and about four thousand people in that town live in it and are not in it.**
