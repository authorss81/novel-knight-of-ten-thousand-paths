# Chapter 0343

**The twenty-first day of Mudmonth, YR 317, a Sunday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour and the seventh hour, in a county that has four market towns in a district and about nine hundred people, in a low building on the north side of the biggest of those four markets, and behind its shop counter there is a room with a press in it and a wall frame of about four hundred pigeonholes, and about four hundred sheets of that office's own printed series was in that frame and on a board at the fourth hour of that Sunday, and about nine hundred people in that county do not know that a man carrying one sheet of that series went into that room at the fourth hour and that the room burned between the fourth hour and the seventh.**

A Sunday in that county is a Sunday. Four market towns are four streets with the doors shut, about two hundred of nine hundred people are out of their houses and the rest are in them, the mill at the low end of that water went on as a mill goes, nine men were inside a yard and did not go out into it, a man of about fifty-five was at three bars on a road above that street where anybody at all can go round at either end in nine seconds, and a bench of nine on the other side of that street did not sit, because it sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week.

**And that room was not empty on that Sunday, and there was a reason for it, and the reason was a frame with about four hundred pigeonholes in it that had been filled again on the Friday and the Saturday after the second round went out on the Thursday before.**

About nine hundred sheets of that office's own printed series go round nine counties in a year in two rounds, and the two of those are made up in that room out of the frame, and between one round and the next the frame is filled again from nine counties in the ordinary way, and it stood at about four hundred on that Sunday morning and had stood at about four hundred the week before and will stand at about four hundred the week after, and there is no figure in that county for how many have passed through that frame since the shop was a shop, and about two people in that county have worked out since that a frame of holes in a wall is not a record of anything and is the only thing about that series in that county that anybody outside it can be shown.

---

**And a woman of about fifty was in that room at the second hour with an oil can because the board has been oiled once a month since before she came into it, and a man of about forty was in it with a lamp under his arm because the lamp was not taken out on the Thursday when it should have been, and a man with a satchel came in at the third hour with one sheet, and none of the three said a word to the others for the first quarter of an hour.**

“**It should have gone out on Thursday,**” the man of about forty said, and put it on the bench by the press.

“**It is not lit,**” the man with the satchel said.

“**It is not lit and it is not out and there is a difference and I have been keeping that building for four years and I know the difference.**”

And a woman of about fifty oiled a board for about nine minutes and did not look up and said nothing, and about two people in that county have worked out since that three people were in a room with about four hundred sheets in it on a Sunday and that two of them were about their own business and the third had come about his, and that nobody in that county has ever worked out why a man who said he would be back in a month came back in six days.

**And he came back in six days with one sheet in the satchel, and he had not been asked for it and had not been told to bring it and had found it under a bench in a building in a town two hundred miles off, and he does not know whose it is and nobody has asked him and about two hundred people in that county have worked out since that a man who carries paper for a living went and got a sheet out from under a bench and brought it back to a room he had been told was a hazard, and that he had done that on a Sunday and not told anybody he was going, and that a man who does a thing like that is not a man who is anybody's man.**

He put the sheet into the frame at the fourth hour. It went into a hole with a number cut over it and that is the whole of what putting a sheet into a frame is, and he did it in four seconds and it was the last thing he did in that room that day.

---

**And the man with the satchel said the one thing he had come into that room to say, and he said it to a woman oiling a board, and she did not stop, and it is the last sentence a person said in that room.**

“**I have been in this room once before and I stood nine feet off that frame and I did not know then that there was a lamp in it in the winter,**” he said. “**I have come back today because there was a sheet under a bench in a town two hundred miles off and I thought I would bring it and then go, and I am aware that that is a reason a man gives a woman with an oil can.**”

The woman of about fifty oiled the board and did not look up and said nothing, and about two hundred people in that county have worked out since that a man told a woman why he had come into a room and she went on with what she was doing, and that the woman was right, and that a man being right about that is the cheapest thing available to a person in a room.

“**It is not a reason,**” the man of about forty said, from the counter. “**There is no list and there is no order and you came because you had a sheet in your hand. If you had not had it in your hand you would not have come and neither of those two facts is a reason and there is nowhere in this county to enter either of them.**”

“**I know there is not,**” the man with the satchel said. “**I have been coming to this room for about six years and there has never been anywhere to enter anything and it is the only room in this county I have ever been in that is like that and I did not know that until about half an hour ago.**”

**And then the light went, which it does in that country in Mudmonth between the fourth hour and the fifth, and the woman of about fifty said the sentence a person says at that hour in that month, and the man of about forty went to the lamp, and the man with the satchel went to the door.**

The room had one window nine feet off a bare wall and the light in it comes up off the boards and not down off the sky, and the lamp was the only other light in it and it had been standing on the bench by the press since the fourth hour and it was not lit.

**And the sheet was on the bench.** It was on the bench at the corner nearest the door, where a man who is in a hurry puts a thing down when his hands are full, and the lamp was on the bench at the corner nearest the press, and the bench is nine feet long and it is one bench and it is against the wall and there is nowhere else in that room to put a thing.

There was no decision in it. There is no hand in that county and there is no word in that county and there is nothing in the two of those that will ever be found, and about four people in that county have worked out since that a man who was told in a room he had been in once that a room was a hazard walked into that room on a Sunday and put a sheet down on a bench and went out through a door nine feet away, and that the sheet and the lamp were on the same bench and the bench is nine feet long and there was one of them, and that the whole of what happened in that room between the fourth hour and the seventh hour of that Sunday is four feet of paper and a lamp that was not lit, and that nobody in that county has ever found a fourth foot anywhere in the building.

The man of about forty had the lamp in his hand two seconds after the woman of about fifty had said her sentence, and he had it out of the room and down the shop part before anything showed, and the man with the satchel was in the yard.

**And in the street, at about the sixth hour, four people asked the man of about forty four questions and he answered none of them, and about two hundred people in that street saw it and about none of them knew what the four questions were.**

“**What was in the frame,**” a man said.

“**Paper.**”

“**How much of it.**”

“**About four hundred and I have not got a figure for the year and there is no figure for the year in this county and there never was.**”

“**And the shop.**”

“**The shop is a shop. It sells nails, string, twine, sealing wax, a sheet of clean waste paper and a quill, and I have kept it for four years, and there is no list of the stock in it either and there has not been one since before I came.**”

**And that is the whole of what a county of about nine hundred people had to ask a man about a room that stopped being a room, and about two hundred people in that street have been saying since that four questions is what four thousand people would have asked if there had been four thousand of them, and that the man answered the first one and the third one properly and the second and the fourth he answered with a sentence that was true and that he is the man who keeps the room and not the man who keeps the count.**

**And the woman of about fifty went back into that room once, after the yard was safe, because the board was in it, and she went back in for a board and not for anything else, and the board is a plank of four feet with a figure in the corner of it in her own hand written before the man of about forty came to that building, and she carried it out, and it is the whole of what came out of that room that has ever been worth anything to that county.**

---

**And it burned between the fourth hour and the seventh hour, and a building that burned is a building that burned and nobody in that county has called it anything, and four hundred sheets of that office's own printed series went with it, and a press went with it, and a frame of about four hundred pigeonholes went with it, and a shop that sells nails, string, twine, sealing wax, a sheet of clean waste paper and a quill went with it, and the counter went with it, and about nine hundred people in that county went on living in that county and two hundred of them came out of their houses in the fourth hour, put a foot in that street, and went back in.**

It took nine hours to come down of itself and four people put water on it until the eighth hour and then stopped, and about two people in that lane have worked out since that four people with water on a press room for about two hours is a county that has done the ordinary thing and that the ordinary thing did not make any difference and that about nine hundred people in that county was in that street in the fifth hour and about four hundred of them did not go out at all and never knew.

A boy of seventeen was in the street two hundred yards off with a barrow and came and was sent home. A man of about fifty-five walked down from his barrier at the sixth hour and was in that street until the eighth and said nothing to anybody, and about two people in that county have said since that a man who turns three bars at a place anybody can go round in nine seconds walked two miles to stand in a street with smoke in it and said nothing, and that nobody has asked him why and nobody is going to.

The man with the satchel did not go back to that building on that day or any day after, and he has never been in that street since except to walk along it, and about two people in that county have been saying since that a man who set a fire in a room walked past the place it happened in for four years afterwards and nobody in that county ever said a word to him about it, and that the reason is not that nobody knows and that the reason is that a man who was told a thing in a room he has been in once cannot now be asked whether he was in it, because there is no room to ask him about and the whole of what he could be asked has burned in.

**And a woman of about fifty who has counted four hundred and fifty sheets into nine piles for nineteen years was in that street at the seventh hour holding a plank of four feet with a figure in the corner of it in her own hand, and there was nothing to put the figure on in any building in that county, and two hundred people in that street saw her holding it and nine of them asked her what it was and two of those nine asked her where it came from and she answered neither, and she has not been asked since and is not going to be.**

---

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. About two hundred people went out of their houses and seven hundred did not, and a mill turned two days up the river the way a mill turns, and nine men came out of a yard at the wet hour and went back in, and a man of about fifty-five was in a street for about two hours and then walked back up to three bars, and the man of about forty was in a street at the seventh hour and then went two miles off and slept there and has not been in that street since.

**And about nine hundred people in that county went through a Sunday in which a room behind a shop on the north side of the biggest of their four markets stopped being a room, and nobody in that county was asked anything about it and nobody has been asked since, and nine hundred sheets of that office's own printed series go round nine counties in a year in two rounds and about four hundred of them was in a frame on that Sunday morning, and there is no list of them and there never was, and a man who was told in a room he had been in once that that room was a hazard is alive and is about thirty and has said nothing to anybody, and the four of them who told him are not named in this county and the last of the four is in a room that nobody in this county has been in.**
