# Chapter 0336

**The twenty-first day of Thawmonth, YR 317, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where a market four hundred yards long is shut and about nine people go down the lane off the north end of it, and at the north end of that market there is a long room over a wharf with one bench in it that sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that the room stood open all day on that Friday with one man in it and nobody asked that man anything.**

The tide was under that wharf and had been since the tenth hour of the day before and it goes back at about the third. The market was shut and it shuts on a Friday. About nine people went down that lane and four of the nine were somebody else's business, a mill turned at the low end of the town the way a mill turns, and about two hundred people in that town were out of their houses and about nine hundred of the four thousand were not.

**And the one bench in that room is used on Saturdays and on no other day of the seven, which means that on a Friday the room is a room with a door that is not shut and nobody in it, and a man of about sixty is in it on a Friday because the fair copies get done then, and a man in that room in that lane have worked out that the only room in that county where nine people could be asked anything has now gone about a year without being asked anything, and that what it is open on a Friday for is copying.**

Nineteen years is how long he has been the man who writes down what that room does, and nothing pays him but the chest. In all that time the minute in front of him has had one line entered on it about a thing that did not happen, and he entered it by entering that the bench did not vote, and he wrote the reason underneath it with his own pen, and the form he writes on carries no column anywhere for the number of times anything in this county has been asked anything, and he has not gone looking for one.

The room is a long one. There is a long table down the middle of it with a bench on each side, and under the window there is a chest with two marks on it, and neither mark has shifted since the ninth of a month in the year before last, and they are the only numbers in that room and are not a sum. There is a subscription book on a shelf with four names in it, of which two are the same man written twice. There is a fair copy open in front of him and it is a copy of a minute of four years ago.

**And the two marks in that chest are not a sum and are not a price, and they are the two sums that body corporate has ever been given by anybody, and they have not moved, and about two people in that lane have worked out that a chest under a window with two marks in it that have not moved in a year and a half is the only instrument in that county that has got a number right and nobody has ever asked it a question.**

**And the subscription book is a book somebody decided to start about nine years ago and the four names in it are four subscriptions and there are three men, and about two people in that lane have worked out that the fourth name is the third man's second time, and that a man can put his name down twice for the same thing, and that that book is not a minute and cannot be got out of and has never been got out of, and that the clerk of that room has never once asked the man about it and is not going to.**

**And between the long table and the wall there is a strip of bare boards about the width of two men, and a bench runs along it with its back to everything, and the four feet of it have been walked past by about nine people in nineteen years and not one of the nine has ever stood in them and asked what they are kept clear for.**

**And about nine people in that county have noticed those four feet in nineteen years, and about four of the nine have noticed them twice, and about two of those four have worked out that a room which has been asked nine things in nineteen years has never once had a person in it who was not on the bench, and that the four feet are the part of that room where a person would stand who was not on the bench, and that in about a hundred and forty years the only person who ever stood in them was a man who was not asked.**

There is a mark on the boards in the corner of them and it is not a mark anybody put there on purpose and four people in that lane have looked at it and not one of them has said what it is, and there is no line in that county's own book for a mark in a floor, and the bench along the wall has its back to the room and has had it for nineteen years and was put there by a man who has been dead for eleven of them.

---

**And a waggon of salt came up the channel road at the second hour and stood at the end of it with nobody under the tailboard and went back down it about the fourth, and the man who keeps the book of what goes up that road and what comes down it wrote one line, and there is no figure anywhere in the book he keeps and no form in this county that would let anybody produce it, and nine people on that floor in that county have worked out that a county of about four thousand people keeps a book with a line in it and cannot produce it and cannot refuse it and that those are two different things and only one of them is anybody's business.**

---

**And at about the fourth hour the man of forty-seven came up the outside stair and stood in that four feet of floor for about as long as it takes to look at nine feet of grass, and nobody in that room asked him a single thing.**

He is on the roll with a vote and not an office and is not on a trade. He is paid fourpence a week out of a private box and not out of the chest, which is a wage and is in no column of anybody's. He has not used his vote since the day he was elected and did not use it then. He has been the witness at two ends of two crossings in a year, and he has said the same four things five times in a year, and the four things are not written down anywhere in that county and are not going to be.

“**Sit down,**” the clerk said, without looking up.

“**I will stand,**” the man of forty-seven said, and about four people in that lane have worked out that a man who has been asked nothing at every one of the nine sittings he has sat in was told to sit down in an empty room on a Friday by a man who was not asking him anything either, and that the two of them said about nine words to each other and that eight of them were about where a man was going to stand.

**And a man of forty-seven stood in those four feet of floor for about two hours, and nobody in that county has ever worked out what a man does in four feet of floor for two hours, and the two of them are the facts there are: a man stood in the part of that room where a person stands who was not asked, and the room did not ask him, and he stood there for the copying to finish.**

**And then nothing happened in that room for about two hours, and about two hundred people in that town were out of their houses during them and not one of them was in that room, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane had forty men on it and a yard four hundred yards up the county's own road had nine, and neither of those is in that room and neither of them knows it is not.**

---


**And a man came up the outside stair at about the sixth hour with a tally under his arm and asked whether the room was sitting, and the clerk of about sixty told him it was not, and the man went down again, and about four people in that lane have said since that the reeve of that county comes up that lane about once a month the year round and has done for nine years and does not vote on the guild's own matter and has not for nine years, and that on that Friday she came up a stair, asked one question, got one answer, and went down again without giving a reason for asking.**

“**Is it sitting,**” she said.

“**It is not sitting,**” the clerk said.

“**Then I will come back on Saturday,**” she said.

“**You may,**” the clerk said, and about two people in that lane have said since that a man of about sixty told a woman who has been coming up that lane for nine years that she may come back on Saturday, in four words, in the voice of a man granting a request he did not hear, and that she has never once asked him for anything and that he has never once waited for her to.

---
**And then somebody asked the clerk of about sixty a question, and it was the wrong one, and the answer he had ready went unused.**

“**Is the copy of the minute from four years back the one that went in the file,**” a man said, at the seventh hour, from the door.

“**It is the copy,**” the clerk said.

“**Then there are two of them now.**”

“**There are two of them now,**” the clerk said, and went on with the copying, and about two people in that lane have worked out that a clerk of about sixty who has had an answer ready for nineteen years was asked about a fair copy at the seventh hour on a Friday and gave the answer to the question he was asked instead of the one he had, and that the answer he had is about a page about nine inches by six under the fourth step of the outside stair of that room, and that nobody in that county has ever wanted it and nobody is going to.

**And the page is under the fourth step of that outside stair and it is about nine inches by six and there is a figure at the head of it in a hand that does not write figures very well, and nine marks under that figure that are not even and about four of them heavier than the other four, and it is nine, and it has been nine since the last day of the year before last, and nobody in that hall has ever entered that count on a form, and what the count is of is how many times that room has been asked something, and a number of that kind is not money and is not people and is added to nothing anybody keeps.** **Four hundred miles away, in a room with benches bolted to the floor, somebody cut a hundred and forty into the end of one of them, and what that one counts is how often anybody has ever stood in an aisle to be asked, and the two figures are not the same figure, nobody has ever added them together, and nobody is going to.**

---

**And here is that Friday, item by item, in pennies, and the sum is not said until the last of them is on the line.**

A candle for the table, a penny. A cloth for the stone of a press, a penny. A fathom of string for a bundle that has not been tied since the spring, twopence. A sheet of that office's own printed series, a penny. The charge for entering it in a register, fourpence. A second candle, because that room has one window and it does not face it, twopence.

*One penny with one penny is two, the fathom of string with that is four, the sheet with that is five, the charge with that is nine, and the second candle with that is eleven pence, and eleven pence is what that Friday came to.*

Total: eleven pence. **Two prices of two different objects sit in this county and neither of them is in the eleven pence. One of them is what the salt merchant asks every month for each of the two rooms over that weigh-house, and the other is the fourpence a year this office pays for the door with a number cut into it, and no clerk in that building has ever added one of them to the other of them in four years and a half and nobody is going to.** A wage of fourpence a week paid out of a private box to a man standing in four feet of floor is a wage and is in no column of that office and may not become one.

---

On that Friday nobody in that county kept anybody company. Nine people went down that lane and four of the nine were passing through on other people's business. Forty men came off a reach with the road open over it. Nine men came out of a yard. A man turned three bars down and up nine times at a barrier and walked back to his hut. And a man of thirty-two with a limp was not in that county that day and has not been in it for about six weeks, and about four thousand people in that town know neither of those things and are not going to be told either of them.

**And a room over a wharf stood open all day with one man in it and nobody asked him anything, and the count under the fourth step of that outside stair is nine and did not move, and a day-book on a shelf in a room forty feet up that lane stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked and is not going to get a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came, and a body corporate in this county has answered a figure of persons standing against it with nothing for four years and a half, and nothing is the whole of what that office has ever been able to do, and about four thousand people in that town do not know that any of it is standing.**
