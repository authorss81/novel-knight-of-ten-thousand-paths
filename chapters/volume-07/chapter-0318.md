# Chapter 0318

**The eleventh day of Hearthmonth, YR 316, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a provincial town, and a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people in it, and a press room on the ground floor, and one floor up a room with a stove in it that is not lit and a single window that looks at a wall nine feet off, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know there is a room one floor up in that building, and that four hundred forms a month are read in it at waist height under a window by light off the floor.**

That room is the only one in the building with a window that anybody works in, and there is nothing on the wall nine feet off it, and the light comes up off the boards and not down off the sky, and about four hundred of a series are read under that window every month at waist height by whoever clears the table, and about four of the six columns on every one of them are ruled for a figure and the fifth is for the man who knows, described by what he does and not by what he is called.

**And nowhere on the whole of one of them, in any column, in any line, at the head or the foot, is there the name of a person.**

At about the second hour a man of about fifty was at the stone on the ground floor setting a page, and he has stood at that press for thirty-one years and about eleven thousand forms have gone through his hands in that time, and nobody in that building has ever asked him what is in the four places on one of them, and he did not look up at anybody who came down that stair that morning and said nothing at all about anything, and about two people in that building have worked out that a man of fifty at a stone has said nothing at all about anything every working day for thirty-one years and about nine of them have noticed.

---

**And a woman of about thirty has been in that room six years and she is a clerk of that office's returns and there is one book in that building that she enters herself and about eleven lines in it in nine years.**

Nine inches by six, and it takes one form as one line. All eleven lines are in her own hand, and a room writes that book and not a press. Under the table leg there is a plate, and over that a second plate, and over that a third, and nobody in this building has ever unscrewed the top one to find out what is under it, and four people in this building have said since that there have been three plates on that leg for as long as anybody can remember and there has never been a fourth.

“**Eleven in all the time there has been a book,**” she said, to nobody, at about the fourth hour, with the book under her arm.

“**It is a busy month,**” said a man at the table.

“**It is a Friday in the middle of a month and it is not a busy month, and eleven in all the time there has been one is a figure and not a rate,**” the woman of about thirty said, “**and I have said that sentence out loud in this room about four times in six years and about four people in this building have heard it and I have never once written it anywhere.**”

**And there is no line in the office's own printed series that will take a thing like that, and there has never been one, and the only thing she ever did about it was write it on that series once, in the ordinary way, about four years ago, in the second column under a head that says a day, and the office sent back a figure.**

“**I asked for a second book,**” she said.

“**You asked for a figure,**” the man at the table said.

“**I asked for a second book and the only paper I had was that sheet, and I put the asking in the only column on it that would take a thing, and the column took it and turned it into a number, and the number came back to me in about two months on a sheet of the same series with nothing in the first column at all,**” the woman of about thirty said, “**and I have not asked on that paper since and I am not going to.**”

“**Then what are you asking on.**”

“**I am not asking.**”

“**You asked for four days ago.**”

“**I asked for four days ago and I got a figure and the figure came back to me on a sheet with nothing in the first column, and I have thought about that figure for two years and every one of the about nine figures I have been sent is a figure and none of them was an answer to anything I asked,**” the woman of about thirty said, “**and I have not asked on that paper since and I am not going to, and if I ever do you may stop me.**”

“**I would not know how.**”

“**No,**” she said, “**and that is the second reason.**”

And that is the whole of it, and she has wanted a second copy of a book she has written in herself for six years, and the reason she wants it is that she cannot read her own hand in that room in the middle of a winter, because the stove in it has been cold since the weather turned and the light in it comes up off the floor and there is one table and about four hundred sheets a month go across that table and she is the one who enters them, and **a copy of a book is a thing a person can hand to somebody else to read, and that is the whole of the reason and it has nothing to do with the machine and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that a woman of thirty wants a second book.**

---

**And at about the sixth hour she stood at that window with the book under her arm and did about four seconds of arithmetic, and those four seconds are the whole of the discovery.**

About four hundred forms a month are read in that room at waist height, and about four of the six columns on each of them are ruled for a figure, and the fifth takes a man by his trade, and the sixth has nothing over it and nothing has ever gone in it, and nowhere on the whole of one of them is there the name of a person.

**And the book under her arm has a person at the front of every one of its eleven lines, and it is ruled nine inches by six, and it is not a sheet of that office's own printed series, and it was not made in that building, and nobody two floors up has ever asked to see it in six years, and there is no line in that office for a book.**

“**That is the only one,**” she said, to nobody, at about the sixth hour, and about two people in that building have worked out that a woman of thirty stood at a window with a book under her arm and worked out that the only record anybody in that building makes by hand is the only record in it that has a person at the front of a line, and that she has been right about that for about two years and has told nobody and was not going to tell anybody, and that a person can be entered into a line in that building without a press and without a series and without a column with a head over it, and that the only way anybody found that out was a woman counting four seconds at a window on a Friday afternoon in the middle of a month.

And a man of about thirty who keeps that room's books came down at about the sixth hour and a half for about nine seconds, said that a tray had been round the passage and was back with nothing on it for anybody on that floor, and went up again before he had finished the sentence, and he has been in that building about nine years and has asked four times in six years for the tray to stop going round that passage, and nobody has ever told him it is not going to, and about two people in that building have worked out that a man who carries a district from one quarter to the next in about four seconds cannot get anybody to stop a tray, and that those are the two things he does and about nine hundred people in that town do not know he does either.

“**The wall nine feet off that window,**” said a man from the stair, at about the sixth hour.

“**Is coming down,**” the woman of about thirty said.

“**It is not coming down.**”

“**No,**” she said. “**It has been there about as long as this room and about four people in this building think about it twice a year and one of the four is me. But about four hundred sheets a month go across that table and one woman reads them at waist height, and the light in here comes up off the floor, and my own hand is not a thing I can read in the middle of a winter, and that is the whole of what I am going to be asked about and it is the reason I wanted a second book and not the reason I said.**”

“**Then the light is the thing.**”

“**The light is the thing,**” the woman of about thirty said, “**and the second book would have been the answer to the light, and the second book is on the office's own printed series and the office's own printed series has four of six columns ruled for a figure and one for a man by his trade and one with nothing over it, and I put a book in the second of the four in a minute and a quarter and I got a number back two months later, and every one of the about nine of those numbers is a number and none of them has ever been a book.**”

---

**Nine sheets of that series left the ground floor of that building at about the sixth hour and went down to a cart in a satchel, and the man with the satchel read none of them and reads none of anything. About four hundred of that series go out into nine counties in a month and about four hundred come back. Eight of the nine that went out on that Friday had nothing in the space in front of the name of the place, and one of them had something in it in a hand that is not the press's hand, and nobody in that building knows whose hand it is and nobody in that building is going to find out, and the sort for that word was struck once in the second month of the year before last and is not going to be struck again, and a boy of nineteen who set it once is not in that press room.**

Nobody walked any part of that Friday with anybody. Nine hundred people went about an ordinary day in that building, four floors and seven presses and about forty people in it. A tray went into a passage at the third hour and was back at the fourth with nothing on it for anybody. About four hundred sheets went across one table under one window that looks at a wall nine feet off it, and were read at waist height, and four of the six columns on every one of them are ruled for a figure.

**And a book ruled nine inches by six went up that stair under a woman's arm with a person at the front of each of its eleven lines, and it is the only book in that building with a person at the front of a line, and it is not on the office's own printed series and it is not in any register and it is not in the returns press and there is nothing about nine hundred people in that town that says it exists, and nobody in that building has asked the woman of thirty to show it to anybody in six years and she has not shown it to anybody, and about two people in that building have said since that the answer to what that office's sheets are for is a room one floor up with a cold stove in it and about eleven lines in it in all the time there has been a book, and that nobody two floors up has ever been up those stairs to be told.**
