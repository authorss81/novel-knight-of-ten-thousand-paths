# Chapter 0313

**The sixteenth day of Frostmonth, YR 316, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, and a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people in it in a provincial town, and a church at the end of a street four hundred yards from the front of it with a vestry behind the choir, and about nine hundred people in that town who do not know that a woman of thirty-eight was refused in a vestry on that Monday morning.**

Something near four hundred of that office's forms go out of that building every month, into nine counties, against four hundred and nine parish rolls. Every one of them is ruled in six spaces, and five of the six have a head over them in the office's own setting, and the sixth is as wide as any of the other five with nothing at all above it. About four of the six are for figures, one is for the man who knows and takes him by his trade, and every one of them is read in that building at waist height under a window by light off the floor. **There is nowhere on the whole of one of them for the name of a person.**

**A woman of thirty-eight has been in that building nine years. What she has at the end of every one of the nine is a figure, and a figure fits in one hand and can be put down on a table in front of anybody at all.**

She is of the office that convened a proceeding in a chamber in this town in the second month of the year. She has a want and she has had it for nine years and she said it once, standing, in nineteen sentences, in a month early in the spring, in front of about nine people. She is not going to say it again in that building and is not going to say it in a different one.

**And in the spring a witness performed the best possible version of what she wants in front of her in a chamber, in eleven sentences, and it did not work, and the space after the eleventh stroke in the margin of that minute is ruled and empty, and about two people in that town have said since that the best version of a want anybody could build was said out loud in a room she was sitting in and nothing came of it, and that is the whole of what nine years has come to and she came up that stair anyway.**

At about the second hour she went down the stair past the press room on the ground floor and a man of about fifty was at the stone setting a page, and he did not look up and she did not stop and neither of them said one word, and about four people in that building have noticed that the two of them are in the same passage twice a month and have never once spoken in it.

She came up that stair first, out of a chamber on the third floor with no window in it and a long table down the middle of it and a board at the end of it with two folders on it a foot apart, and there is a register on that table and it is nineteen years old and it takes a proceeding as a line and there are nine proceedings in it and **every one of the nine is a figure, and not one of the nine is a question, and there is no line in that register for what anybody was asked and there never has been one.** She had been in that chamber for about two hours that morning and had not been asked anything in it and had not said anything in it, and about nine people in that building have said since that a woman of thirty-eight spends about two hours in a room on the third floor twice a month and comes out of it with nothing in her hand, and that the register on that table is the only book in the building that says what that office has done in nineteen years and it says it in figures.

---

**And at about the fourth hour she went down a street four hundred yards and into a church and through to a vestry behind the choir, and a man of about fifty was in that vestry with a minute book open on a table.**

The minute book is eleven years old and he has kept it eleven years and he is a churchwarden of that parish and not an office of anything, and a churchwarden in this kingdom keeps a minute and signs the rate and is answerable to about four hundred people who stand in that church on a Sunday and about nine hundred who live in the parish and do not.

“**You are the woman who was in the chamber,**” he said.

“**I am.**”

“**Then you will want to know that the last thing anybody asked this parish has been in that book for four years,**” he said, and turned the book round on the table so that it faced her. It is a fair copy of a fair copy and the hand in it is not his, and about four entries in it are questions and none of them is a finding, and **the whole of the reason a person can be shown those four is that this church is read out once a year in about four hundred pairs of eyes and there is no line in any of them for a person.**

“**I want a thing entered as a thing asked,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

He did not answer that for about a minute and a half. He turned back to the quarter-day and put his thumb on a line in it, and about four people in that vestry would say afterwards that a man of fifty with his thumb on a line in a rate book is a man thinking about the parish's money and not about a woman from a building four hundred yards up a street.

“**No,**” he said.

**And he gave a reason that was his own and it was not about her, and about nine people in that church have said since that a man who has kept a minute eleven years refused a woman of thirty-eight in about two minutes and that the reason he gave was a rate and not a record.**

“**A question in that book is a question out here,**” he said. “**That is read out in this church once a year and about four hundred people stand in this room and hear every word of it, and a thing that is a question in a book is a thing the parish cannot answer, and a parish that cannot answer a thing is a parish that is asked it again. And the rate for this parish is settled at the quarter-day and I have lent this parish against it and it comes back to me on that morning, and I am not going to have a woman I have never met unsettle a quarter-day.**”

Nobody improved on it. She did not put her want down a second time and he did not give the rate a second time, and about four people in that vestry have said since that two people, one in a building and one in a church four hundred yards off, each gave a single reason and neither of them ever said the other's out loud.

---

**And then he asked her a question, and that is what the second refusal cost, and it is not a figure.**

“**You have a sheet of that series in your hand,**” the churchwarden said. “**The man with the bag brought one to this vestry in the autumn and it is in that drawer and I have not done anything with it, and I will tell you what is on it. There is a place in the first column and there is a figure in the second under a head that says a day.**”

“**I know what is on it,**” the woman of thirty-eight said.

“**Then tell me what the figure is a count of.**”

And she could not. There was about four seconds in that vestry in which a woman who has spent nine years asking rooms to record what was asked of her was asked the ordinary question by a man with his thumb on a rate book, and the ordinary question is the only question anybody in this kingdom has ever asked about a figure on that printed series. It has been asked three times in about four years and it has been answered once, and the answer was that the figure was the last line of a man's own roll, and that answer is in a county two hundred miles off this one and the man who gave it is not in this room.

**And she did not answer it, and he did not press her, and about nine people in that town have said since that the one question anybody in this kingdom has ever asked a person carrying a sheet of that series was asked in a vestry by accident on a Monday morning, and that the woman who had spent nine years asking rooms to show what was asked could not answer it, and that nobody in that vestry has worked out yet that the two facts are about the same thing.**

---

**And being correct is the most expensive thing available to a person in a chamber, and it is more expensive in a vestry, and nobody in that vestry told her that she was right.**

He entered the refusal in that minute book in his own hand, in about four seconds, in the ordinary way, in the middle of a page, and nobody asked him to read it again and she was not asked to stay and see it written and she did not.

“**It is the same line with a person at the front of it,**” she said, at the door, and he said he did not know what that meant and she said it was nothing and went out.

And at about the seventh hour a man of about thirty came down a stair in that building for about nine seconds to say that a tray had been round that passage and had come back with nothing on it for anybody on the floor he was on, and was up the stair again before the sentence was finished, and a man of about sixty took the tray back out and said the one sentence he says on that floor and has said for twenty-two years, and about two people in that building have worked out that the woman of thirty-eight went out of the front door at about the seventh hour, went up the street, and did not come back into that building that day, and about nine hundred people in that town went home knowing nothing about any of it.

**And the keeper of records of a body corporate in a fen county two hundred miles from that building is not told any of it, and is not the instrument this woman works for, and is not going to be, and there is no paper in either of those two offices that would carry a word of this morning to the other one, and nobody in either of them has ever been in the other's room.**

Nobody walked any part of that Monday with anybody. Nine hundred people went about an ordinary day in a building of four floors and seven presses. The presses on the second floor began at the first hour and were still going at the fifth. A tray went into a passage at the third hour and was back at the fourth with nothing on it for anybody.

**And in a church four hundred yards from the front of that building a man of about fifty has shut a minute book and gone out into a churchyard with about nine people in it on a Monday morning, and about four hundred people stood in that church last Sunday and about nine hundred live in the parish and about four hundred of the nine hundred do not know there is a parish at all, and one of the two of them has been refused the only thing she has asked for in nine years and the other of the two did not know it had happened until she stood in front of him, and neither of them has said a word about the other's morning, and there is a drawer in that vestry with a sheet of a printed series in it that nobody has ever done anything with, and the figure under the head that says a day is still a figure, and nobody in that church has ever been asked what it is a count of, and nobody in that county was told that a woman came two hundred miles to ask for it to be put in a minute and was told no in a vestry in about two minutes by a man whose quarter-day is in the same book.**
