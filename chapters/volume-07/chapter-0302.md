# Chapter 0302

**The twenty-ninth day of Embermonth, YR 316, a Wednesday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the second hour after the light and the fourth hour after the light, and a room over a cross in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, with twenty-nine benches bolted to the floor in four rows and a number cut into the end of each one and a hundred and forty cut into the end of the one at the back of the right-hand row, and an aisle four feet wide between the two middle rows that has gone three generations without being used, and about nine people on those benches, and about thirty in the room, and about two hundred of a generation that has never once left that valley at the back, and nobody in that valley knows that a stranger was entered on a roll in that building that week.**

Nobody in that valley counts days the way a county does. The year there is four seasons and the seasons are not the same length twice, and a thing that happened is *last week* or *a while back* or *the winter before the water came*, and a man who came up a road nine days once and read a book out loud in that room is remembered as *the man who read the book*, and nobody in that valley has ever said how long it took him to come and it is the one fact about him that four hundred people could not give if they were asked.

The room is over a cross and the cross is at the top of a lane and the lane runs down to two hundred feet of wharf, and there are three gates into that valley and about two hundred yards of street with a market on it four days out of seven, and about four hundred people live in eleven steadings and four hamlets and the town at the low end of the water, and the highest of the steadings is a two hours' walk up from the wharf and two hundred of that generation have never once come down it.

---

Nine people were on the benches and about thirty were in the room and the two hundred were at the back standing along the wall in four deep, and they were there because a room over a cross in that valley is a thing you stand in.

The man of fifty-eight was at the far end with the book under a trestle and the trestle against the wall, and the book is about forty leaves tied with a piece of tarred hemp and there are nine spreads in it, one to a generation. He put his hand flat on the cover about once during the sitting and left it there about four seconds and nobody in that room was in a hurry about it and neither was he.

There is one finding in that book and it was entered at the head of the first spread on a line the man of fifty-eight ruled himself, and it is the first finding in that book in a hundred and forty years, and it has not been entered a second time and it was not asked about that morning, and about nine people in that room have worked out over about two years that it is not going to be asked about again and about nine of them have never worked it out at all.

The matter in front of the room was a channel. A man at the low end of the water wanted forty feet of stone put in the channel above the mill to take the water off his side, and the mill was on the other side of it, and the two of them had been at it since before the water came, and the room had it over four days and would have it over a fifth, and nine people in that room had heard the same forty feet described four times and two of those descriptions were the same words in the same order.

“**It is not the same forty feet,**” a man said, for the second time.

“**It is the same forty feet and it is in a different place,**” the man of thirty-four said, “**and those are two different things to argue about and nobody in this room has ever separated them.**”

And that was most of what a room over a cross did that morning, and about two hundred people at the back of it had heard four arguments about forty feet of stone in about as long as it takes to boil a kettle, and a room in which a thing can be asked a second time is not a bench, and there is one in that room and it is the twenty-nine benches bolted to the floor and not the nine people on them.

---

A man of thirty-two with a limp had a chair at the side of the room with a back to it and he had been in that valley a week and had been in the room over the cross twice and had been asked nothing on either occasion, and about four of the thirty had worked out that he was a stranger and about four had worked out where he had come from and about nine had worked out neither.

The man of thirty-four stood up at the side of the aisle and waited for the forty feet to be put to one side, and then he asked his question, and he asked it in the front of the room, and about thirty people heard it.

“**Say what your end of it is like now,**” he said. “**You have come four hundred miles and you have been here a week and none of us knows anything about it and about two hundred of the ninth generation are standing at the back of this room. Say it so that they can hear it.**”

And the man of thirty-two answered in about a second and a half, and he answered it in a voice that was not his own, and what he said was not about the end of a road.

“**There is a bridge with about two hundred and forty feet of stone on it and the parapet was done in one summer by about nine men and I have not got up a bank since a day in the middle of a long month and I cannot and I am not going to, and I have sixteen men and four of them have nowhere to work this season and one of the sixteen is nine years old, and I dug four hundred yards of road with a crown on it in a wet summer and it has a standing finish on the end of it and there is about nine feet of grass at the end of that finish and nothing else, and I have never once been asked whether it goes anywhere, and there is no law about it and no office and nobody in that valley has ever called it anything but a road.**”

About thirty people in that room heard all of it. Nobody made a sound. A hundred and forty is cut into the end of the bench at the back of the right-hand row and about nine inches of that aisle had not been stood in for three generations and a man had just said the whole of what four hundred people in that valley had been carrying for a year in 157 words in a room with a board ceiling and no window in it.

**And he knew it in about a second, and four of the thirty noticed that something had happened, and about twenty-six did not.**

“**I am not right,**” he said. “**And I would like somebody to tell me what I have taken.**”

---

Two people heard that sentence in that room, and about nine more heard the words and not the sentence, and the two of them had been in that valley a long time and one of them was about thirty-four and the other was about fifty.

“**That is not yours,**” the man of thirty-four said. “**That is mine. I have not said it in this room and I said it in a yard in the plain way to a boy who did not stop, and you have said it here in a voice that is not yours and you said it better than I say it, and about four of them have worked out that you have not been in this valley a week and have already said a thing of mine in this room.**”

And then he said the second of the two things, which was the one the man with the limp has been given instead of the first.

“**And you do not know when you do it,**” the man of thirty-four said, “**and that is the part of it that will have to be given to you by somebody else, and I am not going to be the one who stands in this room and do that for you twice.**”

And the man with the limp turned to the man of about fifty who keeps the roll, and the man of about fifty was standing at the side of the trestle with the corner of the folded sheet under his thumb, and he had been in the room for the whole of it and had not moved.

“**You keep the roll,**” the man with the limp said. “**You keep it for four hundred people. Tell me which of the two things I said just now is the true one.**”

“**No,**” Marek Ollen said.

“**You are the man who writes it down.**”

“**I am the man who writes down what a person tells me at a door,**” Marek Ollen said, “**and I have never been asked which of two accounts of a person are right and I am not going to start with you, and there are about four hundred people on that sheet and not one of them has ever been asked me either.**”

And he turned the corner of the sheet back down and put his hand flat on it and left it there about four seconds, and two hundred people at the back of that room had been watching his hands and not his face, and a man who keeps a roll of everybody in a place has about four hundred chances in a year to be made into something and he has been made into nothing so far and he was not going to begin on a week in which a stranger had come in out of the weather and put a grief of his into a room with a board ceiling over it.

And about four people in that valley have said since that two men in a room over a cross were asked the same question on the same morning and one of them answered it and one of them would not, and that the one who would not was the man who writes the names down, **and that a man who writes names down is the last person in any room who can be asked which of two accounts of a man are true, and that nobody in that valley had ever worked that out and it had been standing in the room with them for about nine years.**

---

The room emptied at the fourth hour after the light the way it empties, and the book went under the trestle and the trestle went back against the wall, and the man of thirty-four went out to his sixteen, and the man of fifty-eight stayed where he was, and at the mill a man came out into the cold for about four minutes anyway and went back in.

**Nobody walked any part of that day with him, and the man who had said the thing at the seventh hour and the man who had said the other thing at the seventh hour went out of that room by different doors and neither of them said goodnight to anybody.**

**And the woman of about thirty with the slate was at the top of that lane with about nine other people going in and out of the door, and she had a line to enter and she entered it, and it went in the day column with a mark against it that is a gate, and there was nothing to enter about him under any of the other two columns and she did not invent one.**

Nobody asked him to stay. Nobody asked him to go. Nobody in that valley has ever asked a person that and it is not a courtesy and it is not the ordinary way of a room, it is that the roll has a column for a day and a name and a mark and no column at all for the day a person leaves, and a roll that has a line for coming in and no line for going out is a roll that a man can be in for as long as the man who keeps it leaves the line alone.

And four people in that valley have said since that a man came up a road that four hundred people had given up waiting on and was entered in the only record any of them will ever be in inside a week of arriving, and that nobody in that valley asked him about it and nobody in that valley will ever ask him about it, and that the roll has no line for the day he goes and that a man who goes out of a roll is a man who has been in it, and that there is no form in four hundred miles of country that a valley can send in order to be told that he has left.
