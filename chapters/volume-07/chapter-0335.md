# Chapter 0335

**The sixteenth day of Thawmonth, YR 317, a Sunday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, in a provincial town, in a building of four floors and seven presses with about forty people on the floors of it on a working day and about four of them in it on that Sunday, and one floor up from a press room there is a room with a stove in it that has not been lit and a single window nine feet off a bare wall, and about two hundred people in that town went out of their houses and seven hundred did not, and not one of the seven hundred knows that a man read nine lines out loud to himself on that Sunday and asked one question and got nothing.**

A Sunday is a day that building does nothing. The presses stand. The tray does not go into the passage, because the man of about sixty who carries it has not been in on a Sunday in twenty-two years and nobody has ever asked him why not and nobody is going to. About four hundred sheets that went out of that building on the Friday before are not coming back until about a month from now and the table under that window is clear.

**And three people were in that room, and the woman of about thirty was one of them and the man of about thirty was another, and Osten Varel was the third, and about four people in that building have been saying since that three people in a room on a Sunday is a different kind of room from forty people in a room on a Tuesday and that nobody in that building has ever found out which one the room is for.**

---

**And at about the fourth hour on that Sunday the man of about thirty read nine lines up to himself, out loud, at that table, in the ordinary voice, and that is what a man does to a line he has carried, and nobody had asked him to.**

He has kept that room's books for nine years. He carries a district: a set of market towns, written at the head of a sheet at the turn of a quarter, in about four seconds, nine times a quarter, and he has never been asked to do it a tenth time in front of anybody. He does not carry the line. The line is carried by the man of thirty-one nine feet off him, and the two of them have worked in that room for nine years and seven years and have never once put the two jobs in one sentence.

He read the ninth of the nine first, because it is the one that comes last, and the ninth is not a set of market towns.

“**The same weigh-house, the same nine entries, the north end of that market, and about four thousand people on that river,**” he said, to the table.

Nobody answered that either. The woman of about thirty was at the table and Osten Varel was nine feet off with a straightedge in his hand, and a man at that stone in that building have been saying since that a man of thirty read nine rooms out loud in a room on a Sunday to two people who work in the same room and that neither of them said that he had read anything.

“**That is a room,**” he said. “**That is nine of the same room and it is not a place and it is written in the column places go in.**”

“**It is what came back,**” the woman of about thirty said.

“**It did not come back. Nothing came back on Friday. It is on a shelf.**”

“**Then it is on a shelf,**” the woman of about thirty said, and about two people in that building have been saying since that a man of thirty said nine rooms out loud and was told that it was on a shelf, and that the answer was true and was not the answer to the question he had not yet asked.

---

**And he asked the question at about the fourth hour and a half, and it took him about nine seconds, and it is the only question anybody in that room has asked in six years, and nobody answered it.**

“**Which of the two is it meant to be,**” the man of about thirty said. “**A place that we are counting, or a room that somebody is in.**”

And about nine people in that building have been saying since that a man of thirty asked the true question about the first column of a printed series in a room with two other people in it on a Sunday, and that it is the truest question anybody on that floor has asked in six years, and that it took him nine seconds to ask and that nobody in that room could have answered it and that one of the two of them has been in that building nine years.

The woman of about thirty did not answer it. She said a thing that was not an answer and said so at the time, which nobody in that room had expected, and about two people in that building have been saying since that a woman of thirty was asked a question she could have answered in four seconds and said instead that she could not, and that the not-answering was the correct response and that about nine people on that floor have thought since that if she had answered it the room would have had a finding in it.

“**I could put my finger on the column,**” she said. “**You did not ask me for the column. I worked out what you had asked while you were still standing there saying it.**”

“**I know that,**” the man of about thirty said.

Osten Varel did not answer it. He had the ninth line in front of him on the sheet and a straightedge in his hand, and he had carried that line four times across four turns of the year, and about two people in that building have been saying since that the one man in that room who could have said whether the ninth line is a place or a room is the man who rules it, and that a man who rules a line does not get asked what is at the end of it, and that nobody in that room has ever asked him and nobody is going to.

---

**And he read the heads up as well, at that table, in the ordinary voice, and a man who reads the heads of a sheet up before he reads the entries has not decided anything about the sheet and has done it nine times a quarter for nine years.**

They are that office's own heads. The first is *Object* and it takes a place. The second says *Date* and it takes a figure. The third is *Person having knowledge* and it takes a person by his trade and not by that person's name. The fourth is *Keeper* and it takes a person the same way the third does. The fifth says *How often* and that head is that office's own. The sixth is the same width as the other five and there is nothing set over it at all.

**About four of the six are ruled for a figure. There is nowhere on the whole of one of those sheets, in any column, in any line, at the head or the foot, where the name of a person goes.**

“**Nine people in a hundred years could have wanted a name,**” the man of about thirty said, and two people in that yard in that building have worked out that a man of thirty said that sentence in a room on a Sunday and was not disagreeing with anything and was not agreeing with anything either, and that a sheet with six columns and five heads has been going round nine counties for a year with a place at one end of it and a figure at the other and no way at all of standing in the middle of it.

---

**And the fourth column had a keeper against the ninth line, and the keeper was a trade, and it was the same trade as the keeper against the other eight lines in that quarter, and about two people in that building have worked out that a man of thirty read a keeper out loud to a room on a Sunday and did not know what the trade was and neither did the two other people in the room, and that the office has about nine hundred people in a town outside it and there is no column anywhere in four floors for the person who would.**

---

**And at about the fifth hour he noticed that the tray had not been, and said so, and that is the fifth thing that was said in that room on that Sunday and the only one of the five that was about a thing anybody in that room had asked for.**

“**It has not been round,**” he said.

“**It does not go round on a Sunday,**” Osten Varel said, and about two people in that building have worked out that a man of thirty-one told a man of thirty on a Sunday morning that a tray does not go round on a Sunday, and that the man of thirty has been in that building nine years and has asked four times in six years for the tray to stop going round and has never once been told that it does not go round on a Sunday, and that both of those are about the same tray and that nobody in four floors has ever put them in one sentence.

“**I know it does not,**” the man of about thirty said.

“**Then you have asked me for a thing that has not happened on about a hundred and forty days a year for six years,**” Osten Varel said, and about two people in that building have worked out that a man of thirty-one worked out in about nine seconds that a man of thirty has been asking for a thing that does not happen on a Sunday or a Thursday or a Friday, and that he worked it out on a Sunday of all days, and that the man of thirty did not answer him and has not answered him since and is not going to.

---

**And then he put the sheet down, and that is the whole of the cost of that Sunday, and nobody saw it happen except three people and one of them was not looking.**

He has asked four times in six years for the tray to stop going round that passage and nobody in that building has ever told him it is not going to. He carries a district nine times a quarter. And nobody in that building, in nine years, has ever asked him which of the two he would rather be doing, and there is no column on the whole of that office's printed series for what a man would rather be doing, and the two of them are not a matter of preference and are a matter of which of the two gets written down, and about nine people in that building have worked out that a man of thirty spent nine years carrying a place while the place stopped being a place and nobody in four floors ever put him down as the man who carried it.

**And two people in that lane in that room have worked out that the woman of about thirty wanted a second copy of a book of eleven lines in that room in about the fourth hour of that Sunday, and did not ask, and has not asked since and is not going to, and that a person who wants a copy of a book and does not ask for it in a room with two men in it is a person who has decided that a room is not the place to ask and that deciding that is the most expensive thing available to a person in a building.**


Nobody walked any part of that Sunday with anybody. About two hundred people went out of their houses in that town and seven hundred did not, and a mill went at the low end of it the way a mill goes, and a woman in that lane in that building stood in three rooms and did not go into one another's.

**And a sheet with nine of the same room in the first column and a figure against them in the second and something else against them in the fifth is on a table under a window in a room with a cold stove in it, and the man who carries that district has read the nine lines out loud on a Sunday to two people and asked them one true question and got nothing, and one of those two people has been in that building nine years and one of them has been in it seven, and neither of them has ever been asked which of the two things they do they would rather be, and there is no column in that building for it, and about nine hundred people in that town do not know that any of it happened.**
