# Chapter 0311

**The sixth day of Frostmonth, YR 316, a Friday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, and a lane off the north end of a market four hundred yards long, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where a wagon arch stands over the lane and a door with a number cut into it stands at the end of the lane over a weigh-house, and about nine people in a street that is shut today, and nobody in that town knows that a thing was set down in the upper of those two rooms on that Friday morning that is going to be the sixth question in a book four years and a half old.**

The tide had been under the boards of the lower room since about the fourth hour of the day before and had been there about thirty hours, and the boards were damp on the inside and there was about the depth of a thumb of water on the floor down there that nobody had swept out because there was nowhere to sweep it to. A scale under that room has not been used since the fourth of a month. A press stands against the far wall of the upper room with a long shelf over it, and the press can be served with anything at all by anybody who walks up that lane, and the shelf has about four hundred documents in it in bundles tied with string, and a fair copy and a working about four inches apart on a bench under the window. A second press four hundred yards up the county's own road belongs to a yard and not to an office, and nothing can be served out of that one by anybody, and a bundle has been lying in it for years with nothing on the outside of it that is not on that yard's book.

Among the bundles on that shelf there is a register with five entries in it. All five are questions, not one of them is a finding, every one of them opens with a set of market towns put down the way that office puts a place, and the fifth carries a date about four months old. Nobody in that office has read any of it out loud to anybody in four years and a half.

**Beside it is a day-book with nine entries in it, every one of them a thing that was not asked, and no hand in this kingdom can be served out of that book, and there is not going to be a tenth entry in it, and a tenth would not be a present if it turned up.**

The keeper of records is twenty-nine. She has held the office four years and a half on a deed of thirteen words, *for the keeping of what this county cannot be shown it has lost*, and the office pays fourpence a year for it, and the number of that body corporate is cut into the door of that room with a year of a deed cut in under the number in a smaller hand.

---

**At about the second hour on that Friday she began a thing that was finished by the fourth hour, and the whole of it is a sheet of a printed series that she took off her own shelf.**

There is no other series. About four hundred of that series go out of a building in a town about two hundred miles off into nine counties every month, and about four hundred of them come back in a month, and about four of the nine counties in it are counties this office has never had a letter from, and a thing written on anything else in this kingdom is a thing that stays where it is written. That is the whole of the reason and there is no other reason and she gave it out loud to nobody because there was nobody in the room.

It carries six spaces, and five of them have a head standing over them in that office's own setting. Four of the six are for figures. The fifth is for the man who knows, given as his trade and not as his name. **There is no place on the whole of that sheet, in any of the six, in any line of them, at the top or the bottom, where the name of a person goes.**

Number one is the object column and a place goes into it, and a place in that office is a set of market towns. Number two is for a figure and the word above number two is *Date*. Number three takes the person who knows by his trade. Number four says *Keeper*. Number five says *How often*, and that head is the office's own. Number six is as wide as any of the other five and nothing at all is set over it.

She writes what the thing was for in the first column, in the plain way, and then she stops for about a minute and a half with the pen down, and then she writes a figure of persons in the second column.

**And the head over the second column says *Date*, and she knew that when she sent it.**

There is no column on that series that will take a day. The second column takes a figure and calls it a date, and that is what the second column is for, and a question has to have a day on it or it is not a record of anything, and the only place on the whole of that sheet where a thing can be dated is the place where that office puts a count of people.

“**A question that carries no day is not a question,**” said the keeper of records, to nobody in particular, at about the second hour and a half.

“**It is a question,**” said a voice from the stair.

“**It is a question with nowhere on it to put the day it was asked,**” she said, “**and the only column on the whole of that sheet that will take a day is the one that says *Date* over a figure, and about four hundred of them go out of a building two hundred miles off into nine counties a month and every one of them has a count of something in the second column, and the office that reads them has never once asked what any of the counts are a count of.**”

And she put her own trade in the fifth column, because that is the only column on the whole of that printed series that will take a person at all, and it will take him by what he does and not by what he is called. She wrote *keeper of records* in it, and about four hundred people who would have been able to say whether that was a person or an office were not asked.

**And the first mistake in it is in the first line of it, and it is the second column, and it went out of that room in the ordinary post in a bag with about four hundred others of the same thing in it, and nobody in that county was told that it had gone.**

---

**And at about the third hour and a half the reeve came up that lane and put a tally on the table, and the reeve is the elder sister of the magistrate and does not vote on the guild's own matter and has not for nine years.**

She comes up that lane twice a week about the year round and she had come up it about ninety times in nine years, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman with a right to vote on almost everything in that county who has not voted on one thing in nine years is known at the market cross by a trade.

“**What is that,**” she said.

“**A proposal,**” the keeper of records said, “**that this office keep a copy of every question put to this county, and that anybody who asks one be given a copy of it. That is the whole of it.**”

The reeve read it standing up, about four minutes, the whole way down both times and once more at the end of it, and there is no line on the whole of that sheet that tells a reader where the front of it stops.

“**Whose question is it,**” the reeve said.

“**It is not mine. It is a thing that was asked of this county in the spring in a chamber two hundred miles off and it was not entered as a thing asked.**”

“**Then somebody asked it and you are answering for them.**”

“**I am asking for a copy.**”

“**A copy of a question,**” the reeve said, and put it flat on the table with the heel of her hand, “**is a question that can be produced.**”

And then she said the whole of the refusal, and it was short, and about nine people in that lane heard it and about four hundred in that town did not, and it is the reason and not a reason and she has not improved on it since and nobody has improved on it for her.

“**No,**” the reeve said. “**This is a body corporate and it has spent four years and a half refusing to be one, and the first thing that makes a county a thing anybody can act on is a copy of what it has been asked, and I will not have this room be the place it happens in. And I do not vote on the guild's own matter and this is the guild's own matter and I have not voted on it this morning either.**”

Nobody in that room improved on that. The keeper of records did not ask her a second question and the reeve did not give the reason a second time, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman who has not voted on one thing in nine years came up that lane about ninety times in nine years and used the whole of what she had on the ninety-first.

“**You will not be sorry of it,**” the reeve said, at the stair.

“**I have not said I wanted it,**” the keeper of records said.

“**No,**” the reeve said. “**You have not said a great many things in four years and a half and that is the whole of what is wrong with that room and it is not wrong with the room, it is wrong with what has to be done in it.**”

And then she was gone up the lane with her tally under her arm, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman who has not voted on one thing in nine years told a woman of twenty-nine in about eleven seconds that the trouble was not the room and was what a room has to be put to, and that neither of them has ever mentioned it again.

---

**And the refusal went into the register, and the register took it the way the register takes everything, which is as a question, and no other line in that book was open to it.**

The fifth of those five entries is dated and about four months old. The sixth is dated that Friday and opens with a set of market towns put down the way that office puts a place, and it says that a body corporate was asked for a copy of what it is asked and did not give it, and it stands there as a question, and the reason it stands there as a question is the only reason there is for a question to stand anywhere: a body corporate with a door and a number can be sued over a finding and cannot be sued over a question.

**A register with six entries in it and no finding in it is the only kind of book in this kingdom that cannot be produced against anybody, and there are two of the kind in that room, and there is a press against the wall that anybody can be served with anything at all.**

---

**A Friday costs what a Friday costs, and it is set out in pence below and the sum comes after the last of the items rather than before it.**

One sheet off that office's own press, for the proposal: a penny. The clerk's fee for entering one line in a register: fourpence. A fathom of string and a stick of sealing wax to tie this county's own copy with, because a book anybody can be served out of has to be tied: twopence. A foot of candle in a room over a weigh-house in a wet week: twopence.

*A penny and fourpence is fivepence. Fivepence and twopence is seven. Seven and twopence is nine pence.*

Total: nine pence, and two people in that lane have worked out that a county of about four thousand people has spent about four and a half years refusing to be a thing anybody can act on, and has just paid nine pence to go on being asked. **What this office pays a year, and what a salt merchant asks a month for the pair of rooms, are two different prices of two different objects. Neither of them stands in the column above and no clerk in that building has ever put the two into one sum.** A room is not a fee and there is no rate in this county for one, and a wage paid out of a private box is a wage and is in no column of this office.

---

**About nine people in that lane between the sixth hour and the eighth are travelling it because it is their work, and about four hundred people in that town go through a lane on an ordinary day without any work in it at all, and nobody in this county has ever done the sum on the two and nobody is going to.**

A waggon of lime came up the lane at about the seventh hour and stopped at the weigh-house and could not be weighed, and the scale under that room has not been used since the fourth of a month, and the carter of it stood at that door for about nine minutes and then took the waggon down the lane again, and two people in that lane have worked out that a man drove two hundred yards to a door with a number cut into it and a year of a deed cut in under the number and could not be weighed, and that there is no figure in that county for the weighing of a waggon and there never has been and nobody has ever asked.

---
The second copyist came down about four steps at about the fifth hour and stood there.

Six years she has been in that room and she is thirty, or thereabouts, and a warrant naming her to a third post in that day-book has been on the shelf for six years with nobody's name at the foot of it, and if this county ever signed anything it would be that. What she wants out of it is her own name written at the end of a line, and she has wanted that since the summer before last, and she has told a kitchen, once, that she would refuse it if it came and that she does not want it, and that those two are the same thing and not two things.

“**You put a figure under a head that says a day,**” she said.

“**I put the only date on that sheet where a date goes,**” the keeper of records said.

“**And nobody is going to read it as a date.**”

“**No,**” the keeper of records said, “**and that is why I have said it out loud in the room, and it is the first time in four years and a half that I have said anything about a sheet in this room and it will be the last.**”

The second copyist took her paper back up the four steps and said nothing else on her way. In the same hour a second keeper of twenty-six came through that room with a key in her pocket and did not stop, and the third post in the day-book is hers by a warrant nobody has signed, and the two women in that office have never in their lives discussed either of those two things with each other and are not going to.

**Four people in that lane have worked out since that a woman of twenty-nine put the whole of her morning into about forty seconds of talk to a woman of thirty, and the second of them went up a stair holding a sheet of paper and did not say one word back, and not-asking in that room is not a courtesy, it is what a room does.**

---

**Nobody walked any part of that Friday with anybody.** The market was shut and about nine people were in that lane in it, and the tide went back at about the third hour having been under the wharf since the tenth hour of the day before, about forty men came off a reach with the road open over it, about nine came out of a yard on that county's own road four hundred yards up, where a board stands on legs four feet off the ground and a press at the back cannot be served with anything, and a man of thirty-two with a limp went up that road by himself on a stick he does not need and has not put down.

**And a register in a room with a door on a lane has six entries in it, and every one of the six is a question, and a body corporate with a door and a number cut into it and a year of a deed cut in under the number has been asked six things in four years and a half and has answered none of them, and about four thousand people live in this town and about four hundred of them will be in that street on Saturday morning, and the office that would keep a copy of what this county is asked is the office that cannot answer any of the six questions it has, and there is no line in that register for a refusal and there never has been one, and two people in that lane have worked out that a woman wrote down a true thing in the wrong column on purpose on a Friday morning in a wet week and sent it two hundred miles in the ordinary post, and that she will do it again in about a month because there is nothing else to write it on.**
