# Chapter 0323

**The twenty-sixth day of Hearthmonth, YR 316, a Saturday, and not a market day in a county with a market, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the seventh hour. The room is over a cross at the top of a lane in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map, and it has twenty-nine benches in it bolted down in four rows, and the one at the back of the right-hand row has a hundred and forty cut into the end of it, and about four hundred people are in that valley, of whom about nine walked out of that room a different way than they came into it on the fifth evening after a room had said no to a thing in eleven words.**

The market was standing on two hundred yards of street that morning and it had been standing two days out of the three before it, and behind it about two hundred of a generation that has never once come down to the low end of that valley came off the hill and went back up it again, and the mill went on and the wharf did nine things and the oven went through two batches, and a boy of about nine went up the made road with a barrow four days out of seven and did not stop where it ends.

Nobody walked any part of that day with anybody.

---

**And the nine men in the shed at the low end of the water had been asked about the trough about four times in the five days since that room said no, and not one of them had given an answer, and the reason they did not give an answer is that nobody in that valley had asked them anything they could answer with.**

A room in a valley four hundred miles from any county that is on a map can say no to nine men in a shed and the nine men in the shed cannot appeal it to anybody, because there is no office in that valley and no seat and no writ and no place to put a thing that a room has said no to, and the nine of them had gone on making a trade word and filling a trough and putting a lid on it and had not spilled it and had not carried it away, and about two people in that shed have been saying since that a thing refused by a room is not refused by anybody, and that a man who is told no by nine people and not told no by the man who made the thing is in the ordinary way of a trade, and that the only difference is that the trough was at the low end of the water when the room said it.

“**You are going to keep filling it,**” Marek Ollen said to one of them on the second morning of those five days, in the ordinary way, at a gate.

“**Nobody has told us to stop,**” the man with his hands in a cooling box said. “**A room said something and a room is not a man I pour for.**”

---

**Hoben Ryle went down to the low gate at the second hour with a covered vessel and came back up the lane with it at about the fifth hour, and nine men in a shed at the low end of the water filled it for him and said nothing and counted nothing, and about two people in that valley have said since that a man of forty carried a measure up a hill on the fifth evening after a room had said no to it, and that a man can carry a thing uphill that a room has refused and nobody on the way up or the way down has any business stopping him.**

Nobody in that valley stopped him. There is no law in that valley about it and there is no office and no clerk and no seat and nothing at the top of that lane but a door and twenty-nine benches and about thirty people standing in the room, and about two hundred people at the back of the room, and about four people in that room who had known for about four months what was in a covered vessel and had said so out loud in a room five days before.

“**I am not going to stop anybody in this room,**” the man of thirty-four said. He was standing in the four feet of floor between the two middle rows with his back to the aisle and he said it once and he said it at the height a man says a thing at when he has thought about it. “**I have sixteen men and no work and I have wanted that for a month. If you want to say I stood here and let it happen then say it to me now and not to the valley, because the valley is not in this room and I am.**”

And about two people in that room have worked out since that a man with sixteen men and no work stood in a four-foot aisle and gave a room a thing to be angry at instead of a rule, and that a rule in that room would have held and a thing to be angry at will not, and that the second of those two is what about two hundred people at the back of that room went home with.

---

**And the vessel went onto the boards at the mouth of the aisle and about nine people in that room took some out of it, one after another, in the ordinary way, and nobody watched to see who, and nobody took a second measure, and nobody said a word while it was going on.**

It took a long time and it was not dramatic. A woman at a steading up the water went first, because she had asked in the fourth hour five days before and had been answered twice and had not been thanked for asking. Then the man who keeps the roll of the crossing. Then a girl of about seventeen who was at the back and came to the front for it and went back to the back. Then two people at an oven. Then the man of forty, last, and he did not fill his own measure and he was given one.

**And four of the nine people who had wanted it for about four months and had said so out loud in a room five days before were in that line, and five of the nine were not, and the room did not know which was which and did not keep it, and there is no line in that room for what a person took out of a vessel, and the only record that it happened at all is that about four people at the low end of that water know that a measure has gone out of a trough twice.**

**And the woman of twenty-eight who had asked the question in the fourth hour five days before was fourth in that line, and nobody asked her afterwards whether her mother had her back, and she has not said, and she has not been asked again, and about two people in that valley have said since that the one person in that room who came with a question nobody had asked her has not been given the answer and is not going to be, and that a woman of twenty-eight in a valley of about four hundred people cannot go to a person and say my mother forgot me on the second day of that week, and that nobody in that valley has worked out that this is the first cost of a thing that works and the only one anybody in that valley can describe.**

Nobody stopped anybody. A man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work stood in the four feet of floor and let nine people in front of him do a thing his own room had refused five days before, and a man of about fifty who keeps the roll of that valley stood at the side of it and did not move, and about two hundred people at the back of that room saw the whole of it and about two hundred people at the back of that room had not come for it.

---

**And a woman of thirty-four was at a steading up the water when it happened and was told at the fourth hour at the bread oven, and she said nothing at all and she did not come up that lane that day and she did not come up it on any day after it.**

She is the one they send for. Four hundred miles of lane, up and down, about nine times a season since the fever came into that valley, and she is the one who goes when a name goes, and about nine times in about four months people have asked her for the thing that stops it and she has said no about nine times.

About two people in that valley have worked out since that the one person in that valley who goes to nine doors in a season to tell a person that a name has gone from them was not at the top of a lane on the evening that nine people took a thing, and that there was nowhere in that valley to write down that she had not come, and that she came up that lane eleven days later, on her own business, and did not go into the room at all.

---

**Marek Ollen, who is about fifty and keeps the roll of that valley, went down to the low gate after the room emptied and asked the man at the trough how many measures had gone out of it since the lid went on, and the man at the trough said that he did not count and that nobody had ever asked him to and that he was not going to start.**

Marek Ollen's roll is one long sheet, creased twice, and it carries three narrow columns and nothing else. A day goes in the first. A name goes in the second. The third takes one of three marks and there are only three, a gate, a market, a cross, and a person is put at a gate or at a market or at a cross and is not put anywhere else, and about four hundred people in that valley stand in about nine hundred lines of it, and about nine of those lines were written this season and about two hundred of them were written before most of the ninth generation came down off that hill.

“**I have not got a column for it,**” Marek Ollen said, at the low gate, to a man with his hands in a cooling box.

“**I have not got a figure for it either,**” the man said. “**I have got a trough and nine men and a lid and no count, and you are the first person who has ever come down here and asked me a question, and I am not going to answer it with a number I do not have.**”

And that is the whole of what the roll of a valley of about four hundred people knows about the evening, which is nothing, and Marek Ollen wrote nothing down that night and has not written anything down since, and about two people in that valley have said since that a man who keeps the only record anybody in that valley has of who is in it stood at a gate and asked for a figure and was told there is not one and did not ask for anything else, and that a roll which cannot be produced by anybody in four hundred miles of country has now been told that it is incomplete and has no way of being anything else.

---

**And that room has not asked anybody a second thing, and the eleven words are still standing in it, and about nine people in that valley have taken a thing that works, and the rest of that valley has not, and nobody has worked out how many the rest of it is and there is no line in that valley on which a person could work it out and put it down.**

**And the ninth generation came down off that hill over the following season and got smaller, and the man of fifty's roll went on being filled in at the third fold in the ordinary way by a man who asks a man at a trough questions that cannot be answered, and about two hundred people at the back of that room went home that evening having watched nine people do the thing their room had refused, and not one of them said one word to anybody about it, and about two people in that valley have said since that a community which has decided to keep its painful memories has members in it who have decided otherwise and that the second fact is not a betrayal of the first one and is not a correction of it either, and that nobody in that valley has worked out what to call the two of them together and about four hundred people there are going to have to go on living in the room where both of them are true.**
