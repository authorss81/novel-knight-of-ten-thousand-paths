# Chapter 0350

**The twenty-fifth day of Longlight, YR 317, a Monday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the fourth hour and a half and the seventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people on a river, where a market four hundred yards long is shut and the county trades on a Saturday and on a Thursday and on nothing else in the week, and a bell of nine stone stands a hundred yards up that county's own road, and four thousand people in that town do not know that nine people in four counties are the ones a thing can be shown to, and that a month has gone by in which nothing at all was entered.**

A Monday in that town is a day the town does not trade and it is not a day the town stops. About two hundred people were out of their houses and three thousand eight hundred were in them, nine people went down the lane off the north end of that market and four of the nine were somebody else's business, and nine people crossed the four hundred yards of it between the second hour and the fourth, and nobody stood in it.

**The tide was under the wharf at the north end of that market and went back at about the third hour, and for two feet on each side of it the stones came clean. Salt came up the channel road in a cart in the sixth hour, halted at the end of that road, and went back down it before the wet hour, and a man who keeps a book of that road's comings and goings wrote one line about it. There is no figure in what he keeps and no form in this kingdom it could be produced on, and two people in that town have said since that a county of four thousand people keeps a book with a line in it and cannot show it and cannot refuse it, and that those are two things and only one of them is anybody's business.**

And the whole of what stood in that street that Monday was four hundred yards of stone set in clay with a rope and a bar at one end of it, and about nine hundred stones in it that a person can name, and nobody has ever tried, and a bar at the end of a street is a thing a market has got and not a thing anybody stands at.

---

**And the road was open over four timber bays four hundred yards down a lane off that market and about forty men were on it, and a reach does not stop for a market and does not stop for a quiet season either, and a man of fifty and a half had spent a fortnight laying one finish over another on the second bay with a bar in his hand he was not using, and on the parapet of that bay there is a joint about a foot and a half short of where a joint ought to be, and nobody in this county has put a rule on it and nobody is going to.**

There is a point under about half an inch of lime in the third course of the first bay that is about nine inches long and has not moved, and it is not a figure of persons and it is not a count of anything anybody keeps, and about two people in that county have looked at it and about none of them has ever said what it is and nobody is going to, and about nine people went up that county's own road that Monday and none of them went as far as the reach.

**Nine men were in a yard four hundred yards the other way, and a board about nine feet along stood on legs four feet off the ground in it, and a boy of fifteen was at that board copying. He copies the number and he copies the space under the number. Nobody on that yard has ever told him what goes in the space and nobody is going to, and two people in that county have said since that a boy of fifteen has been copying a space under a number eleven times a week for about a year, and that a space is as hard to copy as a figure once a hand has learned it, and that nobody in that yard has worked out whether he knows there is nothing in it.**

At the back of that yard a man of about nineteen held an empty hod and had held it for four months, and with his back to the yard all morning a man of about sixty packed lime against a heap that has a lid on it, and he is the only man in that yard who is not paid out of it and nobody on that yard has ever asked him about the lid. Three hundred yards above that yard a hut stands beside three bars, and a man of about fifty-five has been inside the hut nineteen years putting the bars down and up for whoever goes through, and a bar is a bar and not a gate, and there is no form on that barrier and no book at that hut and a hut on a road in this county holds a stool, a lamp and nine tools and nothing else anybody has ever put in it.

**And the boy of fifteen said the thing he says at the end of a day, and he says it to the board and not to the men, and about two people in that county have said since that a boy of fifteen has been saying the same four words to nine men four days a week for about a year and that on that Monday he added a day to it and that a person who adds a day to a thing has not changed the thing and has changed what the thing is about.**

“**That is all of it,**” the boy said.

“**That is not all of it.**”

“**That is the whole of the day,**” the boy said, and went on copying, and about four hundred people in that county have worked out since that a boy of fifteen has added a day to a thing he says every day, and that a person who adds a day to a thing has not changed the thing and has changed what the thing is about, and that nobody in that yard was asked about it.

**And the man of about sixty with the lime said one thing to that yard at about the sixth hour and nine men did not take it up, and it is the only thing he has said there in a year. Every one of the other eight is paid out of that yard and he is not. He knows what a warp in a lid does, and he knows it from where he came from, and there is nobody among those nine he could hand that to without being asked where he came from, and nobody on that yard has asked him and nobody is going to.**

“**It will hold until the frost and not a day past it,**” he said, to nobody, at about the sixth hour.

“**You have not said that to anybody in a year,**” the man of about nineteen said, from the back of the yard with the empty hod.

“**No,**” the man of about sixty said, and went on packing lime, and about two people in that county have been saying since that a man who packs lime out of a private box has said a true thing about a lid out loud once in a year to a man of about nineteen who was not asking and that neither of them has mentioned it since and that the lid is the only thing in that yard that is going to be wrong in about a month and that about nine men in that yard are not going to be told.

**And at all nine of them she was off down the lane again before the last of the knocking had died in the frame, and she says one thing at each of nine doors and has for about a year and she tells the door and not the room, and the room is forty feet behind the ninth of them and she has not been in it in four years and a half and is not going to be.**

---

“**He went up the road on Friday with a waggon of his own and came back without it,**” the woman with the pail said, at the first door, “**and he was not stopped at the bars and nobody has asked him why he went up there.**”

“**On Friday,**” a woman said, from behind a door that was not opened.

“**He did, and he came back the same road, and that is the whole of what I know about it,**” the woman with the pail said, and moved on, and about two people in that lane have worked out since that a woman with a pail has told nine doors one thing in the last year and has not told one person the same thing twice, and that what she tells them is about a waggon and a road and a man and that the room forty feet behind the ninth of those doors is a room with a register in it at seven questions and not one finding, and that she has not been in that room in four years and a half and is not going to.

**And under the fourth step of an outside stair at the north end of that market there is a slip of paper about nine inches by six, and a figure stands at the head of it and nine marks stand under that figure, and the marks are not even and about four of them are heavier than the other four, and it said nine before that Monday and it said nine after it. Nobody in that hall has ever put that figure on a form. About nine people in four counties have been saying since that a piece of paper under a step and a plank of four feet with a figure in the corner of it are between them the whole of what four counties have written down in a year, and that neither of the two can be produced by anybody who is not standing next to it.**

A day-book on a shelf in a room over a weigh-house forty feet up that lane stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and it is not going to get a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came. A register on the same table carries seven entries, and not one of the seven is a finding, and all seven of them are questions. Four things are on a shelf six feet from a press and a fifth is in the press, and the press has been standing open for eighteen weeks and nobody has shut it. Two people in that lane have been saying since that a county of about four thousand people owns two presses now, two hundred yards apart, and that one of them stands open in a room any passer-by can walk into while the other one answers to a yard and not to an office, and that nobody in this county has ever put the two of them in the same sentence.

**And a woman of thirty-three in a provincial town two hundred miles off is at a column at the fourth hour of that Monday with a stove in her room that has not been lit since the weather turned, and a set of forty sheets is in a drawer on the ground floor of that building that nobody empties on any day anybody keeps, and she is the only one of four floors who could put a hand on that drawer in the dark, and nobody in that building has thanked her for that and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to, and the thing she has wanted since the month she was twenty-two has not got one word more in it than it had then, and nobody in that building has ever asked her to say it out loud, and it is not written down anywhere.**

And a man of about thirty-one in that building ruled a line four inches short of a right margin at about the fourth hour of that Monday, and it was the ninth line, and it was correct, and the figure standing at the end of it is not the figure that was standing at the end of it a month ago, and he did not look at it, and about two people in that building have said since that a man who has ruled that line five times has never once been asked what is at the end of it and that the answer to that question is a room in a county of about nine hundred people two hundred miles off and that the room is not a room any more, and that the line is still the correct length and the figure is still against it, and that is the whole of what a line is for.

---

**And a bell of nine stone with a band of forty-one rivets in it went at the fourth hour and a half of that Monday, a hundred yards up that county's own road, on a day the county does not trade, and it went at the fourth hour and a half the way it goes, and it is rung by a boy who has been the steward's apprentice for about two years and three months and is still not good at it and by a carpenter who is a hand and not a keeper, and neither of the two of them is paid by the guild and neither of the two of them is a member of anything, and there is no answer in that county to the question of who keeps it and there never has been and four thousand people in that town have never once asked, and it does not stop, and nothing in this year or the next has anything to do with it.**

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. About two hundred of that town were out of their houses and about three thousand eight hundred were not, forty men were on a reach with the road open over it, nine were in a yard, the hut on the road above took its man back inside it at about the eighth hour, and at the north end of that market a man of forty-seven stood in four feet of floor for about two hours, and that is now the ordinary outcome of every day he spends, and what he says about twice a year is written down nowhere at all in that county.

**And a man of thirty-two with a limp stood at the end of that road about a hundred yards off that bell at about the fourth hour and a half and did not go up, and about two people in that town have been saying since that a man who has come to that bell four times has never once been asked about it and has never once asked, and that the two of those are the same thing and are not the same thing, and that about four thousand people in that town do not know either.**

**And about four people in that county have worked out since that a copy is a thing you can make on a day nobody is looking, and that this county looks at about four hundred people on a Saturday and two hundred on a Thursday and at nobody on a Monday, and that nine sets of paper in four counties were arranged around a day like this one, and that nobody here was asked whether that was a good idea.** **And the four hundred yards of it stood empty from the second hour to the seventh on a day this county does not trade, and about nine people crossed it, and a bell a hundred yards up that county's own road went at the fourth hour and a half and went on going, and about four thousand people live in that town and about nine of the four thousand are the ones a thing can be shown to, and a street four hundred yards long is still a street on a Monday.**
