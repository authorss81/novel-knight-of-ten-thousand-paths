# Chapter 0315

**The twenty-sixth day of Frostmonth, YR 316, a Thursday, and a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the sixth hour, and a street four hundred yards long in a town of about four thousand people on a river, and four hundred people standing in it, and a long room over a wharf at the north end of that market standing open with one man in it, and a hut at a barrier of three bars three hundred yards up the county's own road, and about four hundred people in that market who do not know that the only record anybody in this county keeps of the nine things a bench of nine has been asked in nineteen years is a page about nine inches by six under a stair at the north end of that market, and that the man who keeps it has said out loud this morning that a copy of it would be the end of it.**

It opened at the second hour and it does not close till about the seventh. A waggon of salt stood at the mouth of the channel road for the better part of an hour with nobody under the tailboard, and the man who keeps the account of that road's up and down put one line in for it. Nine lines have gone into that book this year and this is one of the nine.

**And the bench of nine that sits in that long room sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week, and a Thursday is a day that room stands open in with nobody in it, and a man of about sixty is in it on a Thursday because that is when the copying is done.**

He has kept that minute nineteen years. He is paid out of the chest and out of nothing else, and he has entered the absence of a vote in that minute once, and he entered the reason for it under the absence in his own hand, and no line in that minute was ever ruled for how many times a thing has happened in it, and he has not gone looking for one, and he has an answer ready and four thousand people in that town do not know that he has one and nobody in that county has ever wanted it.

He was in that room at about the second hour with a fair copy of a minute of nineteen years ago open on the long table and a second fair copy of a minute of four years ago, and a chest under that window with two marks in it, put where they are and not moved since the ninth of a month in the year before last, and four bare feet of floor between that table and the wall with a bench along it and its back to the room.

---

**And at about the fourth hour he said the thing, and it is the true thing about a copy, and he is a man who has counted, and nobody improved on it and nobody has improved on it since.**

“**I want to say a thing and then I want to go back to the copying,**” he said, to about four men who had come up the outside stair on the way to the wharf and had gone out again, “**and I am not going to say it again, and if any of you have come back up those steps to hear it I will not say it twice.**”

“**We have not come back,**” a man said.

“**Then I will say it,**” the clerk said.

**And here is the fear, and it is correct, and it is about a copy and it is not about the Crown and it is not about a person.**

“**A copy of a count is a count of the copy,**” the clerk of about sixty said. “**Not a count of the asking. I have kept that minute nineteen years and I have been in that hall for every one of the nine things it was asked, and I have put nine marks on a page under that stair with my own hand in about two seconds each, and that page is the only place in this kingdom where the number nine and the word asking are in the same four inches of paper. If anybody in this county ever made a copy of that page, then in about two years the copy would be the page, and the copy says nine, and the page under the stair is under a stair, and there is no form in this kingdom anybody could be shown that would say which of the two of them was the asking.**”

“**Nobody has asked for a copy of it,**” a man said.

“**Nobody has asked for a copy of it because nobody has ever been told that it is there,**” the clerk said, “**and I have not told about nine people that it is there, and one of the nine is a man of about thirty-two with a limp who came up those steps on the Wednesday before last and stood in that four feet of floor and looked at the bottom of that stair for about as long as it takes to count nine things twice, and he did not go down it, and I did not tell him what was down it, and he did not ask, and I have not told anybody and I am not going to.**”

“**You could have told him.**”

“**I could have told him and he would have gone down those steps,**” the clerk said, “**and a man who has been down them is a man who knows, and a man who knows is a man who can be asked whether the nine is nine, and I have spent nineteen years being a man nobody in this county can ask a second question.**”

“**That is a reason for keeping a thing and it is not a reason for a count.**”

“**It is the only reason there is,**” the clerk of about sixty said, “**and I have had it since the first of the nine and I have not said it out loud before and I am not going to say it out loud again.**”

Nobody in that room improved on that. About four men had been in the room and about four went out of it and about four hundred people in that market did not hear it, and about two people in that lane have said since that a clerk of about sixty who has kept a minute for nineteen years said the true thing about a copy of a count in about ninety seconds to four men who were on their way to a wharf and then went back to copying, and that the truest sentence anybody has said in that room in nineteen years is about a page under a stair that nobody has ever entered on a form.

**And the count is nine. It is nine this morning, and it was nine on the Wednesday before last, and it was nine on the last day of last year, and nobody in that county has been asked to move it, and nobody in that county is going to be, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.**

---

**And a clerk of about sixty with an answer ready and a minute with no line in it is one of four, and about two people in that lane have said since that there are four people in this kingdom that anybody knows of who have a thing they could write in and have not written in, and that the four of them are in four counties about as far apart as this kingdom is wide, and that two of the four are in rooms two hundred miles apart and have never heard of each other, and that there is no paper in either of those rooms that would carry a word of the other one, and that one of the two is a woman of thirty-eight who has asked nine rooms in nine years for a thing to be entered as a thing asked, and that the other one of the two is a man of about sixty sitting in a room over a wharf with a fair copy in front of him and no way at all of saying what he has not written.**

He is not going to be told about her and he is not going to tell her, and two people in that lane have worked out that the two of them asked the same kind of room the same kind of question nine years apart and one of them is a keeper of a register in a county of about four thousand people and the other one is a woman with a figure at the end of every one of nine things, and that there is no line on the whole of either of their papers that would take the name of the other one.

“**There is a woman two hundred miles off,**” the clerk of about sixty said, at about the fifth hour, to the copying, “**who has been asking a room to show what was asked for nine years. I know that because a thing came down that lane in the spring with a copy of a minute on the back of it and a man in a plain coat read it out in a chamber and there was a woman in that chamber who had been in that building nine years.**”

“**Have you ever sent her anything,**” a man said.

“**No,**” the clerk said.

“**Then you do not know whether there is a woman.**”

“**I know there is a woman because a minute came down that lane and a minute does not come down a lane for nobody,**” the clerk said, and went back to the copying, and four people in that lane have said since that a clerk of about sixty who has an answer ready for everything said the one thing he had not got an answer for, and that a man who has spent nineteen years not being asked a question cannot be got to start by being asked one about a woman.

---

**And at about the sixth hour a man of about fifty-five came up that road from the barrier of three bars with a carter and a waggon behind him that would not go through, and he stood in that long room for about ten minutes and nobody asked him anything.**

He has been in that hut nineteen years and he turns those bars down and up for anybody who goes through and he has said the same four words to every one of them and about four thousand people in that town have gone through that barrier and not one of them has ever asked him where they come from.

The waggon would go through two at a time and not one and there is a bar on that barrier that is set for two and has been set for two since before the man came to it, and the carter wanted to know whether the man would take the middle one out for a day, and the man of about fifty-five said his four words, and the carter asked him a second question, and the man said the four words again, and the carter took his waggon back down the road and did not go through.

**And a clerk of about sixty stood in that room with his thumb on the edge of a fair copy and did not ask him where the four words come from, and the four words are not written down anywhere in this county and are not going to be, and the clerk had an answer ready about a minute and did not use it, and about two people in that lane have said since that a man with a thumb on a page under a stair was in the same room for about ten minutes with a man who says four words a day at a barrier and neither of them asked the other one a single question.**

And then the man of about fifty-five went back down the road and the clerk went back to the copying, and four hundred people went home off that market, and the tide went back at about the third hour, and the man who writes down what comes up that road and what goes down it did not write a second line.

Nobody walked any part of that Thursday with anybody. About forty men went home from a reach with the road open over it, about nine men went home from a yard on a street four hundred yards up that road, a man of thirty-two with a limp went up that road by himself, on a stick he does not need and has never once put down, and a man of about fifty-five turned three bars down and up nine times that morning before he came up the road at all and went back to his hut and turned them nine more times after the waggon had gone back down.

**And a page about nine inches by six under a stair at the north end of a market four hundred yards long says nine, and it says it in a hand that does not write figures very well, and there is no second of it in this county and no second of it in this kingdom, and the whole of why there is not is that nobody has ever been told it is there, and about four hundred people walked past that stair that afternoon and not one of them knew, and a clerk of about sixty who has known for nineteen years went home to a room over a wharf and did not write down one word of what he had said at the fourth hour, and about four thousand people live in this town and not one of them has ever been asked to move anything, and nobody in that county is going to.**
