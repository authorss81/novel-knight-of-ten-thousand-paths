# Chapter 0344

**The first day of Greenmonth, YR 317, a Wednesday, and not a market day, and the second hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, in a county that has four market towns in a district and about nine hundred people, in the room over a byre across the street from where a shop stood until ten days ago, where there is a long table and one bench that sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week and a clerk of about sixty who keeps a minute and a chest with two marks in it under a window that have not moved since the ninth of a month in the year before last, and about nine hundred people in that county do not know that nine sets of paper were counted out on the floor of that room in two hours, and that a clerk who has kept that room's minute for nineteen years did not open the minute once while they lay there.**

What came out of that shop on the north side of that street was carried out by about four people over two days and it is worth naming because the whole of what came out of that shop was those things and nothing else, and the rest of what happened to it was about what did not.

The shop part came out whole, because the shop part is one room deep and the fire was in the room behind it, and the counter came out and the stock came out and the lamp came out and a bench came out with the lamp standing on it. A drawer under the counter came out with about ninety sheets of that office's own printed series in it, wet along one edge and dry along the other, and those ninety are the only paper of that series that came out of that building.

A plank of about four feet came out in a woman's hand on the Sunday, and there is a figure in the corner of it in her own hand, and there was nothing to put the figure on and there is nothing to put it on now.

**And about four hundred sheets of that office's own printed series went with the room, and a press went with it, and a frame of about four hundred pigeonholes went with it, and there has never been a list of any of them in that county and there is not one now, and about two hundred people in that county have been saying since that a man can lose four hundred pieces of paper and a county cannot say what they were, and that the county is not the party that lost them and has no way of asking the party that did.**

---

**And about nine hundred sheets of that series go round nine counties in a year in two rounds, and that has been the way of it since before the shop was a shop, and every one of the nine counties has been keeping what came to it.**

It is a pigeonhole in a wall, or a shelf in a room, or a box in a press, and it is the county's own, and it holds the county's own portion of what came round, and about four hundred of a year go to each of the nine counties over two rounds, and nobody in any of the nine has ever sent a sheet back, and nobody in any of the nine has ever asked the other eight what they had, and about four hundred people in four counties have worked out since that a thing which is in nine places and has been for years and has never once been joined up is not nine copies of a thing and is not one thing in nine places, and that there is no word in any of those four counties for what it is, and that the nearest thing anybody in any of them has is a pigeonhole.

**And the nine counties are not the same size and the nine sets are not the same thing, and that is the discovery of that Wednesday, and nobody in any of the four counties had worked it out and nobody has worked it out since.**

A county of about nine hundred people in a district of four market towns held the frame. A fen county of about four thousand people on a river held a shelf. A province with a town of about nine hundred people and a building of four floors in it held a drawer in a press that nobody empties on any day anybody keeps. A county two days up the river with a mill in it held a sack.

**And the ninth of the nine is not the one with the most in it and it is not the one anybody would pick, and the man who made the decision about the order on that Wednesday could not tell whether the ninth was more important than the fourth, and he said so in the room, and he did not pretend to.**

---

He came into that room at about the second hour on his own, and about two hundred people in that county have worked out since that a man of thirty-two with a limp came into a room over a byre ten days after a shop burned and was at the end of a long table and did not sit down, and that a clerk of about sixty who has kept that minute for nineteen years offered him a stool and he did not take it and the clerk did not ask him again.

A woman of about fifty was there with the plank. A man of about forty was there who has not been in that room in four years except to buy something at the shop that is not there, and who has walked past it every morning since the fire and has not gone in, and who came into that room at the fourth hour because he was asked to and did not want to be.

Nine sets were counted out on the floor of that room between the second hour and the fourth hour by four people, and about two of the four were saying figures out loud and about two were saying nothing at all.

“**That is not complete,**” a woman said.

“**None of them is complete,**” the man of thirty-two said.

“**You have not looked.**”

“**I have not looked and I am not going to, and I am telling you that in a room with four people in it because it is the useful thing to say, and the reason it is useful is that everybody in this county has been saying for nine days that somebody ought to know what was in that frame, and the truest answer anybody can give to that is that nobody will know, and that about four hundred sheets have gone and about four hundred have come out of the pigeonholes of four other counties over two years and nobody in any of the four has been in the room the sheets came out of.**”

---

**And the decision was made at about the seventh hour of that Wednesday and it was a decision about an order and not about a thing, and the man who made it was not the man of about forty and was not the clerk of about sixty and was the one nobody in that county had ever seen in it before.**

The order is: which of the nine counties to send a man to first, because the nine sets hold nine different portions of a series of about nine hundred a year, and no two of them hold the same portion, and the only way to find out what the whole of it was is to start where the most of it is and work outward, and there is no more and no less.

“**You have four counts on that floor,**” the clerk of about sixty said, “**and about two hundred and forty lines against a handful, and a handful, and about ninety, and a figure of about two hundred written in a hand that is not anybody's in this room, and you are going to tell four people in this county which of those four is the important one.**”

“**I am going to tell them which one we go to first,**” the man of thirty-two said, “**and I do not know whether the ninth is more important than the fourth and I have not pretended to know, and the two of those are different questions and the first one has an answer and the second one does not.**”

“**Then you have decided on a number.**”

“**I have decided on an order and it is the fourth, and the reason I have decided on the fourth is not that it is the most important one. It is that a man in the fourth of the nine has had a sheet in his hand every working day for about six years and has read none of them, and he is the only one of the nine who has ever asked anybody a question about what a sheet is for, and that man asked it into a room and got nothing, and he will ask it again and he will get nothing again, and about two people in that county have said since that the first place to send a man is the one place where somebody has already tried.**”

And about two people in that county have been saying since that a man of thirty-two with a limp was at the end of a long table in a room over a byre and gave a reason for an order that had nothing to do with the order, and that the reason was true, and that nobody in that room asked him where he had got it, and that he did not say.

**And then he was asked the question that is the whole of that Wednesday, and he answered it, and it is the only answer he gave in that room.**

“**Which of the nine is more important,**” the woman of about fifty said, from the plank.

“**I do not know and I am not going to tell you one way or the other, and there is no form in this county on which I could write it down and there is no form in the four others either, and the honest thing to say in a room where four people are holding up pieces of paper is that a piece of paper does not say how important it is. The frame said four hundred. The frame did not say what the four hundred were, and about four hundred of them are in this room on the floor, and the ninth is a count of about two hundred in a hand I cannot place and I do not know what it is and I am not going to find out by asking you.**”

**And nobody in that room said that he was right, and nobody thanked him for the order, and about four people in four counties have been saying since that a man was in a room and said out loud that he could not tell which of nine pieces of paper mattered most, and that a man who says that out loud in a room is doing a thing nobody in four counties has ever seen done in one, and that he paid for it in a room where nobody would have known what to do with an answer either way.**

---

**And that Wednesday is set out here in pennies, and the total is on the line after the last item.**

A quill, a penny. A candle, a penny. A wick, a penny. A pot of oil for a lamp, twopence. A sheet of that office's own printed series, blank, to make the count on, a penny. A leather apron, twopence. A fathom of string, threepence. The charge for entering one line in a book, fourpence.

*Three of those are pennies and that is threepence, and the apron and the oil are twopence each and that is fourpence, so threepence and fourpence is sevenpence, and the string is threepence, so sevenpence and threepence is tenpence, and the charge is fourpence, and tenpence and fourpence is fourteen pence, and fourteen pence is the whole of that Wednesday.*

Total: fourteen pence. **A blank of that office's own printed series is a penny a sheet at any counter that sells them and this room is not a counter and there has been no counter in it since before the shop across the street was a shop. What a small office gives for a door with a number cut into it is in no column of this one, and what the salt merchant asks for each of two rooms over a weigh-house two hundred miles off is in no column of this one either, and those are two prices of two different objects and nobody in that county has ever added one of them to the other of them. A wage paid out of a private box to a man who put water on a room that was burning for two hours in the last week of Mudmonth is a wage and is in no column of that office and may not become one.**

---

Nobody walked a part of that day with anybody. About two hundred people were out of their houses on a day that county does not trade and seven hundred were indoors, the mill two days up the water went on turning, nine men stood at a bench-board four feet off the ground in a yard, and three bars on the road above that street were put down and put up all day by a man nobody there has ever asked a question, and a bench of nine in that room did not sit, and there is a piece of paper about nine inches by six under the fourth step of the outside stair of it, and it said nine before that Wednesday and it said nine after it.

**And nine sets of paper were counted out on the floor of a room over a byre in a county of about nine hundred people on the first day of a month, and the four counties that hold them are four counties and not five and not one, and the nine of them are not complete and none of them has been joined to any of the others, and about four hundred sheets that went into a frame in a room behind a shop in the last week of Mudmonth are not in any of the nine and never will be, and about two hundred people in that county know that nine sets were counted out on the floor of a room over a byre and about seven hundred do not, and the clerk of about sixty did not enter it in the minute he has kept for nineteen years because he has not been asked to and there is no line in that minute for what nine sets of paper is, and about nine hundred people in that county do not know any of it.**
