# Chapter 0704

**Thawmonth the fifth, YR 322, a Thursday and a market morning four days off in this market town with no market here and no market on this made road, the fourth hour at the head of the made road where a five-bar gate stands across a lane that goes up over the shoulder of the hill, a man of thirty-two with a stick standing at the mouth of that lane, and a man shutting a gate.**

Thawmonth is the month the ground comes up and the roads go down, and the four days between this market town and this gate is a made road that has carried one man a day for four and a half years and about nine hundred people who live along it, and on the fifth of Thawmonth it was in the worst state it is in all year at about the fourth hour, and nine people in nine counties have said since that it is not the same road in Thawmonth and five have said that it is the same road with more of the year on it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the gate at the head of that lane is a five-bar gate with a bar in it and the bar goes in on the lane side and not on the field side, and a man who shuts a gate on the lane side is shutting it against the road and not against himself.

Nobody thanked him and he came the four days on the made road that morning and it took him about five hours and a half longer than it took him at the middle of Harvestmonth, and the road was in the state a made road is in in Thawmonth at about the fourth hour, which is to say about nine feet of it is soft on the high side and about four feet of it is standing water in the ruts on the low side, and nine people in nine counties have said since that four and a half hours is not a long time and that a man who has walked one road for four and a half years knows exactly how long that road is in every month and that this is the only thing about it that has not changed.

Nobody thanked him and there are eleven houses on that made road and about nine hundred people living along it and every one of those nine hundred people knows which of them goes out on it at the sixth hour, because there has been one man doing it since the first of Longlight and a road of eleven houses has a very short memory for anything else.

Nobody thanked him and he came up the last rise at about the fourth hour and the wall was on his left and the byre was at the low end of it, and about nine people in nine counties know that this is the last built thing on a made road for about nine miles, and about four have said that a man who walks four days to a wall does not do it for the wall, and five have said that he does not know what he does it for and that this has been the shape of it since the first of Longlight.

Nobody thanked him and the man was about forty-one and he keeps a drove and a byre and about nine hundred yards of wall at the head of this made road, and nobody in nine counties buys anything off him except about four households that buy a beast in the autumn, and his name is Orme Skell.

Nobody thanked him and there had been a drove of about nine beasts in the fold behind him and the byre was built of stone at the low end of about nine hundred yards of wall, and the wall is not old and is not a fortification and is nine feet of good stone laid by a man who could lay stone, and four people in nine counties have said that nine hundred yards of good wall with a drove behind it is the only thing in nine counties that has been built on purpose since the middle of Longlight and five have said that a wall is not a thing anybody looks at.

Nobody thanked anybody and Orme Skell had seen this man coming every morning for about eleven days and had not said anything to him and had not been asked for anything, and on this Thursday afternoon he shut the gate and put the bar in and turned round.

Nobody thanked him and the nine beasts are about two years old and they go out on Thursdays, and they go out through a gap at the low end of that wall about nine feet wide which is not a gate and has never been one, and it is four feet high and it is the width of a drove, and a gap in a wall that is made for a drove is about nine feet wide forever and cannot be made narrower without taking the wall out and cannot be made wider without letting a cart in.

Nobody thanked him and the drove goes up that lane and goes about nine hundred yards and stops, because about nine hundred yards up it there is a field with a stone wall round it the same height as the wall at the bottom, and the beasts go in there and stand, and nine people in nine counties know that the beasts go up nine hundred yards and about five have ever followed them.

Nobody said anything and four people in nine counties have said since that a drove that walks nine hundred yards up an unmade lane every Thursday for about eleven years is the reason that lane is a lane, and five have said that the beasts go because they are beasts and would go up a cart track or a stream or nothing at all, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked him and the byre holds about nine beasts and hay for about nine beasts and nothing else, and the wall is nine hundred yards because nine hundred yards is where the land stops being his, and four people in nine counties have said that he built more wall than he needed by about four hundred yards and that the extra four hundred yards is where the beasts stand.

Then the man of thirty-two said a thing out loud, in the open, at the mouth of a lane, and about one person heard it and nobody answered it.

“**Then I am not going to ask you twice.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in nine counties have said since that the second of those two things is the one the county has, and about four have said that the first of them is a thing a man says when he is about to ask something and five have said that it is a thing a man says when he has already decided not to ask it.

Nobody thanked anybody and he did not ask it, and the reason he did not ask it is the only thing in this chapter that belongs to him, and it is in his own trade, and he gave it out loud on a wet Thursday afternoon at the head of a lane to about one person.

“**I have asked nine hundred girders the same question twice. You do not ask a man who keeps beasts about a road twice, because the first answer is the whole of what he has, and a second question makes it a thing he has been asked to produce, and then it is a thing with a use in it, and I have watched a thing with a use in it come down a road and take a crossing with it.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in nine counties had heard a man who works with his hands give the reason he is not going to use a man who works with his hands, and five had heard a man be careful, and nobody thanked him.

Nobody thanked him and Orme Skell had his hand on the bar of that gate when the man of thirty-two said it and he did not take the hand off and he did not say anything for about as long as it takes to turn a cart.

Nobody thanked anybody and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man of forty-one at a gate was the only person in nine counties who could have said whether that was an insult and did not say anything, and about four have said that he did not understand it and five have said that he understood it exactly and that a man who keeps beasts knows what happens to a thing that has been asked to produce.

Then the drove went out through a gap at the low end of the wall, and it went out because it was Thursday and it is a drove and it goes out on Thursdays, and the beasts are not let out on a lane and are not let out on the made road and go along the hedged head of nine hundred yards of wall to a field above the lane, and about nine people in nine counties know that this happens on Thursdays and about five have been up the lane to watch it.

Nobody thanked him and Orme Skell shut the gap and came back and put his hand on the bar again.

Nobody thanked anybody and then he opened the gate, and he did it on his own account, and he gave no reason that anybody in nine counties has been able to repeat, and about four people have said he did it because the bar had been bent about the ninth hour and five have said he did it because he wanted to see whether anybody would come up a lane in Thawmonth.

Nobody said anything and nine people in nine counties have said since that the second of those two reasons is the true one and that nobody will ever be able to prove it and that about four people in nine counties have said that a man who has said ten words in his life at a gate does not start explaining at the eleventh.

Nobody thanked anybody and the bar came out and the gate came back about four feet and the lane was on the other side of it, and it is not a made road and it never has been, and it is about nine feet wide at the head and it goes up over the shoulder of the hill at about a rise in nine, and there is about two miles of it and about four people in this county have been up it.

Nobody thanked him and nobody has ever been able to establish what is at the top of that shoulder, and that is not because it is a mystery, and there has never been any need in this county for anybody to go and see, and the two of those sentences have been true for about eleven years, and a man who keeps beasts at the bottom of it has never been asked to make them one or the other.

Then he said the thing about the gate, and he said it in fourteen words, and nobody thanked him for it.

“**A gate nobody has looked at is not a gate, it is a story.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in nine counties had heard a man of forty-one say out loud that eleven years of a shut gate was a story and not a gate, and five had heard fourteen words, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time anybody in nine counties has said out loud that the bar has been in for about eleven years, and that the man who put it in said the number himself and not the number that somebody else has been saying.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody thanked Orme Skell and nine people in nine counties have said since that the number has been in this county as a fact for about four and a half years, because a gate that has been shut since the middle of Longlight has been shut for about four and a half years in the telling, and that on the fifth of Thawmonth a man who keeps beasts corrected it to about eleven years and nobody argued with him because it was his gate.

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two stood in the mouth of that lane for about as long as it takes to turn a cart and looked up it, and the lane at that point is about nine feet wide with a bank on the high side that has not been cut back since the middle of Harvestmonth and a drop on the low side into about nine hundred yards of pasture that belongs to nobody in particular, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man standing at the mouth of a lane looking up it can see about nine hundred yards of it and no more, and that the rest of the lane turns twice before it goes over the shoulder.

Nobody thanked him and he could see the field with the stone wall round it at the end of what he could see, because the field is at about nine hundred yards and it is level and the beasts were standing in it in the middle of the afternoon, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the only part of any of it that a man can see from a road, and five have said that the man of thirty-two looked at nine beasts standing in a field and not at a lane, and that this is what he came four days for and has come four days for every day since the first of Longlight.

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two did not go up the lane, and the gate was open and the lane was there and about two miles of it went up the shoulder of the hill with the drove in front of it.

Nobody thanked him and nine people in nine counties have said since that a gate stood open for about as long as it takes to shut a cart and the man who shut it did not stand in it, and about four have said that a man who opens a gate and does not use it is a man making sure that nobody comes up it, and five have said that a man who opens a gate and does not use it is a man who is entitled to open it and has nothing to go up, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Then the gate came shut and the bar went in on the lane side, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of the fifth of Thawmonth at the head of that made road, and about four of them have said that a man who has waited four and a half years to be asked one question and then opens a gate and shuts it again has told the man who asked it everything there is to tell, and five have said that he has told him where to come back and not what is there.

Nobody thanked him and the drove came down the lane at about the fifth hour and went through the gap and the gate was shut behind it and the bar went in, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this happens every Thursday and has happened every Thursday for about eleven years and that the gate is not used for the drove and is not used for anything else and has not been used for anything else in about eleven years.

Nobody thanked him and then the light went off the coping on that wall before the light went off the sky, and he was on the made road at about the fifth hour with four days in front of him and the frost coming back up out of the ground, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man who walks a made road in Thawmonth in the afternoon walks into the worst of it at about the fifth hour, and that this is the only thing about Thawmonth that anybody in nine counties can rely on.

Nobody thanked anybody and the bar went in and the man of thirty-two went on up the made road on his own and got back into that market town at about the eighth hour, and about four people in that market town noticed the coat and about five did not, and about nine people asked him at some point in that evening where he had been, and about four of them got an answer and about five did not, and one of the five was a man who asked it twice

And the man who asked it twice was a man of about thirty-four with a satchel, and he asked it out loud, in the open, at the boards in a market street four days off with about nine people in it and about five not, and nobody stopped him.

“**Where does it go,**” he said, “**and who is at the other end of it?**”

Nobody said anything and Orme Skell was four days away and nobody had sent anybody and about four people in that market street had heard two questions that nobody in nine counties has an answer to and five had heard a man who finds things out., and nobody asked him anything at all twice.

**And the cost of that Thursday is a bar that has been in for about eleven years being out of a gate for about as long as it takes to shut a cart, and a lane that two miles of this county has never been up, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a waxed thread at 10, a pot of tallow at 13, a crate at 15, a gate shoe at 16, a hand bar at 17, a hurdle at 18, a barrow wheel at 22, a cresset at 23, a hand adze at 25, and a barrow at 27.** Ten, thirteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, twenty-two, twenty-three, twenty-five and twenty-seven is one hundred and eighty-six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and eighty-six pence is three marks and forty-two over.** About nine hundred yards of good stone wall with a drove behind it and a five-bar gate in it has been standing at the head of that made road for about eleven years with the bar on the lane side, and on the fifth of Thawmonth YR 322 the bar was out of it for about as long as it takes to shut a cart, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the second time in about four and a half years that a thing at the head of that made road has been opened by nobody on his own account and closed by nobody on his own account, and five have said that the first one was a watercourse.
