# Chapter 0710

**Mudmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 322, a Friday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, four days out at the head of a made road where about nine yards of a wall is down and a man is putting stone in it on his own account, and a man of thirty-two with a stick.**

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine yards of the nine hundred yards of wall at the head of that made road came down on the twenty-eighth of Thawmonth and the water took the foot of it out on the second of Mudmonth, and nine people in nine counties know that this and five have seen the gap.

Nobody thanked him and the wall is nine feet of good stone laid by a man who could lay stone, and the stones of it came down in about nine pieces, and nine of them are whole and nine of them are in the mud at the bottom of the gap, and the rest are about nine yards downstream in about nine inches of water.

Nobody thanked anybody and the stones are the same stone as the rest of it because it was all taken off that hill, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the only thing about that wall that is convenient and that a man who built nine hundred yards of it out of one hill has about nine hundred yards of it lying about in the weather, and four of them have said that this is the answer to the question of why it is nine feet high and five have said that this is the answer to nothing and is nine feet of stone on a hill.

Nobody thanked him and the gap is four feet high at its lowest point because the stones below it have not washed out, and a gap four feet high is a gap that a man can get through on his hands and knees and that a drove cannot, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is now the fifth thing about that wall that nobody has ever been able to say out loud, and five have said that it is a hole in a wall and that is the whole of it.

Nobody thanked him and the wall was nine feet high and about nine hundred yards long and he laid it himself over about eleven years, and it is the only thing in nine counties that anybody has ever built without being asked what for, and nobody has ever asked him what it is for and he has never said.

The gap is on the high side of the lane, which is the only part of that gate anybody in nine counties can see from anywhere, because it is visible from about four hundred yards down the made road. The stones that came out of it are about nine pieces and nine of them are whole and they are lying in the mud at the bottom of the gap where the water put them, and the water has been going over them for twenty-three days.

Nobody said anything and this county has spent about four and a half years on a thing it could not see and has now got two things it can see, and neither of them can be acted on. A wall at nine miles is four hundred people pointing at it on a Saturday. A gap at the end of a drove road is nine people pointing at it, and the difference is a number and not a kind.

Nobody thanked him and the bar he was carrying was about four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it, and it weighs about sixty pounds with the stones, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man with a bad leg should not be on the end of a bar and five have said that a man with a bad leg on the end of a bar is worth one man without one, and that this is the arithmetic of every wall in every county and that nobody has ever put it in a book.

Then the man who keeps it, on his own account, at about the seventh hour on the morning of the twenty-fifth.

Nobody thanked him and he was in the gap with about nine stones of the nine hundred yards' worth laid out on the high side of it in a line, and he had about nine hundred yards of wall to lay and about nine yards to rebuild and about nine days since it went down, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man who keeps nine hundred yards of wall and has about nine yards of it down at the wrong end is a man with about nine weeks of work in front of him, and five have said that this is the whole of what a wall is and that nobody in nine counties wants it until it is down.

Nobody thanked him and he had not asked anybody for the stone and nobody had brought him any, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the stone at the head of that made road is quarried about two miles off on the low side and that a man with nine yards to rebuild and nine days gone would have to walk two miles for nine loads and carry them back on his own, and four of them have said that this is what he has been doing since the second of Mudmonth and five have said that this is what he will be doing until the end of Mudmonth.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody in nine counties knows that he has been doing it alone, and the reason nobody knows is that nobody has been up that road in Mudmonth except one man, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the whole of what happened between the second and the twenty-fifth of Mudmonth at the head of that made road was one man and about nine loads and about nine yards, and that it was not written down and that nobody asked.

Then the fifth person came, at about the tenth hour, on foot, with a stick in his right hand, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that a man has come up that made road at a time when somebody else was already working on it, and that about four of them have said that the last time was the middle of Longlight and that there was a wain on it.

Nobody thanked him and he did not ask to help and he was not asked, and he put his stick down against the wall on the high side and picked up the end of a bar with two stones on it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a bar with two stones on it is about four feet and about sixty pounds and that a man with a bad leg should not be on the end of it, and five have said that a man with a bad leg on the end of a bar is worth one man without one and that this is the arithmetic of every wall in every county.

Nobody said anything and Orme Skell looked at him and did not stop and did not thank him, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the only question anybody asked on that wall in about an hour was whether the bar was wanted, and that it was, and that this is not a fact anybody has ever been able to check

And he said it out loud, once, on a wall, in the mud, to about one person.

“**Is the bar wanted?**”

Nobody said anything and Orme Skell said one word back and the word was yes, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the entire conversation two men had at that wall on the twenty-fifth of Mudmonth, and that it was in their trades, and that neither of them has ever repeated it., and they carried for about an hour, and nine people in nine counties have said since that two men who have never said a word to each other carried nine yards of wall about four times over in about an hour and neither of them said anything at all, and that about four of them believe they said something and about five do not.

Nobody thanked him and about half an hour into it, at about the eleventh hour, the man of about forty-one said one thing out loud, in the open, on his own account, to about one person, and it was sixteen words, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the second thing he has said in about four and a half years and that both of them were said at this gate.

“**You can have the bar from the second yard on, there, and not off this end.**”

Nobody said anything and nobody thanked him and the man of thirty-two changed ends without saying anything, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of what was said between two men at that wall in about an hour and that a man who keeps nine hundred yards of wall has said one more word this year than he said in the eleven years before it, and that about four of them think the word was about the wall and about five think it was about the lane.

Nobody thanked him and the hour was about as long as it takes to turn a cart four times over and then they stopped, and the bar went down against the wall and about nine yards of it was not rebuilt but was about nine yards further along than it was on the twenty-eighth of Thawmonth, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a wall rebuilt nine yards in twenty days is a rate of about two days a yard and that this is about half the rate the wall was built at and that nobody has ever worked out why.

Then the gate, which has been shut since the sixth of Thawmonth and is shut.

Nobody thanked him and the bar went in on the lane side and four people in nine counties have said since that a five-bar gate with a bar in it, shut against a lane that is under nine inches of water, is a gate that is shut against nothing, and five have said that it is a gate that is shut against a lane, and that those two sentences have been true for about eleven years and that this is the first time anybody has ever had to choose between them.

Nobody thanked anybody and the drove went out on the Thursday as it goes out every Thursday and went up nine hundred yards and stood in the field with the stone wall round it, and nobody went with it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the drove has gone up that lane alone about nine times since the second of Mudmonth and that this has never happened in about eleven years, and five have said that a drove does not mind and about four have said that the beasts know the way.

Nobody thanked him and then the man of thirty-two went on up the made road on his own with his stick in his right hand and got back into that market town at about the eighth hour, and nine people in nine counties have said since that he did not stop at the ninth mile stone on the way back and did not stop at the eleventh mile stone either, and five have said that a man who has carried a bar for an hour at the head of a four-day road does not stop at two stones on the way home.

Nobody thanked anybody and the lane is under and the wall has nine yards of gap in it and the gate is barred and the drove goes up it alone on Thursdays, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of the twenty-fifth of Mudmonth at the head of that made road, and four of them have said that it is the first time in about four and a half years that two men have been at that gate together and that neither of them said anything about the lane, and five have said that the lane was not what they were doing.

Nobody thanked him and nobody wrote any of it down, and there is no page in any room in nine counties with a bar and an hour and sixteen words on it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is now the fourth and a half year in a row in which the two most important things anybody did in this county happened on a road with nobody on it, and that about four of them have said that the last of those things was a bridge going down in nine minutes and five have said that the last of them was a cloth going over a bench.

Nobody thanked anybody and then the question, because a chapter that ends on a question is not a chapter that ends on anything, and it is said here because nobody in nine counties has said it and there is no register to put it under.

Nobody thanked him and the question is about a drove road, and it is in his own trade and it is not a question about a path and it is not a question about a wall.

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two asked it out loud, in the open, to about one person, on a Friday afternoon in Mudmonth at the head of a made road, four days from a market town, and nobody answered it and he did not ask it twice.

“**Somebody with no standing in this county and no trade to lose has to go up that lane in Hearthmonth, and the whole of what I have to decide before then is whether I am that person or whether I am the man who keeps finding out what other people decide.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the sixth time in about four and a half years that a man has said out loud that he is the man who finds out what other people decide, and four of them have said that this is the first time he has said it as a question about himself and five have said that it is not a question at all and is a man making a note to himself on a wall in the mud.

Nobody thanked him and nobody answered it and nobody was asked anything else, and about nine yards of a wall went forward about four feet in about an hour and a gate went into a bar on the lane side and a man of about forty-one went up to his byre and did not say why.

**And the cost of that Friday is nine yards of a wall, an hour of a bar, and about nine days of a man's ordinary year that nobody in nine counties will ever hear about, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
