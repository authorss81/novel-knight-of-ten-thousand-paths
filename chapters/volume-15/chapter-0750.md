# Chapter 0750

**Frostmonth the twenty-fifth, YR 322, a Sunday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, the ninth hour in that market street with nine people in it and four of them at the boards, and a man of thirty-two who has found out where the oldest road in this county goes and is not going to be told anything about who holds the wall above it.**

A Sunday in that market town of about two thousand people is not a market morning, and nine people were in that street by the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of Frostmonth and four of them were at the boards with nothing on them and five were not, and the bolt on that room over the weigh-house went on from the Saturday and nobody went up the four hundred pace lane that holds it.

Nobody thanked anybody and this is the last day of this month and Hearthmonth the first is six days off and about nine hundred yards of dead grass sits nine hundred paces above a bar of ash that has been in a gate since Mudmonth, and nobody in nine counties is going to be told any more about either of those two things than they have been told already.

Then four days off, and about nine hundred people along that made road did the ordinary part of a Sunday, and the ordinary part is that nothing about a Sunday is visible on a road.

Nobody said anything and a drove went up that lane four days off on the Thursday before, which was the twenty-second of Frostmonth, at about the sixth hour, and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it, and that is thirty-eight drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth, and four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap is about a yard long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side and one man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for two hundred and sixty-three days, and the last yard is about nine feet of a course and he has not started it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The brood in a walled yard half a mile below that wall is five frames where there were nine on the night of the eighth of Thawmonth, and that night is two hundred and eighty-seven days back, and a brood frame comes back in about four weeks in a year like this one, and the fifth frame has been all brood since the twenty-seventh of Wolfmonth and no sixth has gone in, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a young man of about nineteen has said out loud twice that he is not going to make more bees than the bees he has got and five have said that nobody has ever asked him whether he meant any of it.

Nobody thanked him and the weigh-house weighed one load of lime and nothing else that day, and the man who keeps it said out loud, to four people in and five out, that Frostmonth is twenty-five days old and about two hundred and fifty sheets have come past that floor in it, and that about four hundred and nine parish rolls come past his floor in a good month, and that nobody has ever asked him what a sheet weighs.

Nobody said anything and the inn at the high end had four beds empty that morning and put no board out on the made street, and the woman of about fifty-one who keeps that house came out into it at about the fifth hour and stood nine feet off the boards and would not come nearer, and a chandler nine doors down put a cloth over a bench rather than write anything at all on it.

Nobody said anything and the near wheel of a cart came off at the low end of that street and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes and nobody stopped walking, and two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped, and four people in nine counties have said since that those two women have argued about that bolt on nine mornings in four and a half years and five have said that they have never once been told what the bolt was worth.

The room over that weigh-house stood bolted from noon on the last Saturday and the copyist of about twenty-six did not come down those thirteen stairs at all that day, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room put a second lamp at the foot of them on the Friday before and had it lit at the fourth hour, and it is the second lamp in that room and it is on thirteen stairs and there was no lamp on them before, and four people in that lane had worked out that she paid for it out of fourpence a year and five had not, and nobody has asked her why and nobody is going to.

Nobody said anything and there has been a second thing four days off, and nobody in nine counties knows about it and it is not about the wall and it is not about the scar. On the made road between the seventh mile stone and the ninth there were four stones that had come out of line and two of them were lying in the drain and the ninth has been cleaned with a bit of stick by one man for about four and a half years, and about the middle of Harvestmonth a man of thirty-two with a bar and a lever and no mortar put two of them back, one about two hundred yards back from where it had been and one about nine feet, and the second of the two has not walked out of line since, and nobody in nine counties has asked him and nobody has been told.

Nobody thanked anybody and that is the whole of what two people here have built this winter, and neither of them built it for the other and neither of them has said one word to the other about anything in four and a half years, and they have been in one building and not in one room more than once in four and a half years, and about four people in nine counties have said since that two people four hundred paces apart have each made one thing this winter that would still be there if the other one had never come to that town and five have said that nobody has ever asked them about it and about four have said that this is not a thing anybody has ever asked about and that is the reason it has lasted four and a half years.

The nine stones at the end of the nine-foot line in the grass are about the height of a man's knee and about as wide as two hands and the tops of them are all at the same height, and nobody in nine counties has found out why, and about nine households keep beasts on the flat past them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years, and nobody has ever asked any of the nine about it and nobody is going to.

Four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it has been across that lane gate since Mudmonth, and it is the bar of a man of forty-one and nobody in nine counties has asked him to put it there or to take it out, and the drove has gone up past it through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of wall on every Thursday since the second of Mudmonth and has never once gone through that gate, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the gate is not the road and five have said that nobody has ever asked the man who bars it what the gate is for.

On that east rise the broom has been off for about nine days and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing where it stands, and a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years and about four people in nine counties have said since that the nine hundred yards a young man of about nineteen left standing are the same nine hundred yards that are dead in the grass above them and five have said that a man who cuts a line and leaves the end of it is the only man in nine counties who has ever both made a thing and not made a thing and about four have said that nobody has ever asked him which of the two he meant.

The far bank is four days south of that market town and about four hundred people live across nine hundred yards of water at the top of a tide and about four hundred at the bottom, and a hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway every Monday afternoon in the winter and the twenty-fifth of Frostmonth was a Sunday and the lamp was not put there, and the hand bell on the gate post above that coping is about the size of a fist and is rung when the water is over the causeway, and the water was not over it, and nobody in that market street has ever asked a person at a gate at the near end of that water anything at all.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the owners of those two miles are nine households and not one of them has ever asked anybody anything about anybody who walks past its gate, and about four people in nine counties have said since that one of those nine has about nine hundred yards of dead grass nine hundred yards above his own wall and five have said that nobody has ever asked the two of them about it.

Nobody said anything and the ninth mile stone was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before and the clean ring at the foot of it is about a foot wide and about half an inch deep, and nobody has ever watched him do it and nine people in nine counties know that he does it and five have ever seen it, and the eleventh mile stone about four hundred yards past it has about nine hundred names cut into the side of it going back about two hundred years and nobody in nine counties can read more than about nine of them.

Then the part of that Sunday that was said out loud, and three things in it were, and all three of them were about who is going to hold nine hundred yards of wall, and nobody agreed with anybody.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of about fifty-two said it out loud, in the open, in that market street, at about the ninth hour, to nine people, in his own trade, and it was 58 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**A wall is held by the man who repairs it and I am the man who repairs it and I have been asking that office for nine years to pay me for the nine hundred feet of kerb and nobody has ever answered me and I am not going to hold nine hundred yards of wall for nothing.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a roadkeeper price nine hundred yards of wall out loud and five had heard 58 words, and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him, and four people in nine counties have said since that the first answer anybody here has given to who is going to hold that wall came from the only man in nine counties who has ever said what a thing costs and five have said that a man who is not paid is not holding it and about four have said that a man of forty-one has not been paid either and nobody has ever said so out loud.

Nobody thanked him and the young man of about nineteen said it out loud, in the open, in that market street, at about the ninth hour, to nine people, in his own trade, and it was 43 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**The bees are not going to hold it and I am not going to ask them and about nine hundred yards of my broom is standing dead nine hundred yards above a wall and nobody has asked me what to do with it.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a young man of about nineteen refuse on behalf of nine hives and five had heard 43 words, and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him, and about four people in nine counties have said since that two answers in one street in about as long as it takes to turn a cart are further apart than two hundred yards of wall and five have said that the roadkeeper wants to be paid and the beekeeper wants to be asked and about four have said that neither of them mentioned the man of forty-one and five have said that neither of them was asked to.

Nobody said anything and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head was at the high end of those boards on that Sunday and said nothing at all, and the man of about thirty-four with a satchel was there and said nothing at all, and the man of thirty-two with a stick was at the sea end of that market street with his stick in his right hand and his left hand hanging at his side where anybody could see it and he said nothing at all, and about four people in that street had worked out inside about nine seconds that three men who can hold something have stood in one street on a Sunday and not one of them has said a word and five had not.

Nobody said anything and then a person at the third door of that market street asked him one thing out loud at about the tenth hour, kindly, and nobody stopped it, and it was not about a road and not about a wall.

“**Are you going to be all right?**”

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two with a stick answered it out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 52 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**I have a bad leg and I have lost the use of one ear and there are nine hundred yards of grass four days off that will not come back and nine counties cannot hold any of it, and that is the answer and I am not going to say it twice.**”

Nobody said anything and nobody said anything back to him and nobody thanked him, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside about nine seconds that a man has told four hundred people on a Sunday morning what he has lost and that he has not asked anybody for anything and five had not, and about four people in nine counties have said since that nobody in nine counties has consoled him and nobody has thanked him and nobody has said one word to him about the part of him that was going to come home on the fourth of Rainmonth and five have said that this county has never had anybody in it lose a thing and say nothing about it.

Nobody thanked anybody and here is the whole of what this county has on the twenty-fifth of Frostmonth, and there is nothing after this page about it. Nine hundred yards of an old road is in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand. Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand either. One sheet of paper with one line on it, one leaf with one thumb on it, and two printed notices with two names at the foot of them are in an office four days off and nobody in nine counties can read them. Nine hundred yards of dead grass is on the back of a board in a cart shed and about nine people in nine counties can point at it. And nothing about any of that is in anybody's hand.

Nobody said anything and that origin is answered, and the answer is a boundary and a refusal, and the refusal is nine hundred years old and it is not anybody's, and there is no body and there was never a body and nobody came down any road with one, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the oldest road in this county does not go anywhere and that it stopped where the boundary began and five have said that a road that never went anywhere was never a road and about four have said that it is nine hundred yards of kerb under the grass since before anybody in this county was keeping anything.

Nobody said anything and both of the refusals stand and the acceptance stands with them, and he said it out loud in a market street on the twenty-eighth of Fallowmonth in forty-five words and that is the whole of what he has done about either of them and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and that was a hundred and one days and eighty-nine days ago, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the acceptance has not been argued with in three months and five have said that three months is longer than anybody in this county has gone without asking twice about anything.

Nobody thanked him and the question that is left in this county is not where a road goes and it is who is going to hold nine hundred yards of wall that nine hundred yards of dead grass sits above, and it is a worse question than the one this began on, and nobody in nine counties is going to be told the answer to that either, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a road nobody could see was a question four hundred people could repeat and five have said that a wall four hundred people have stood in front of is a question they cannot repeat because there is only one of it and nobody has been asked to hold it.

Nobody said anything and three things are standing in this county at the end of it that nobody here will carry. A place on a road that is shutting its own end before Hearthmonth because it has said so on a leaf with one thumb on it. A wall that is drawn on the back of a board instead of hidden behind about nine hundred yards of nine-foot grass. And a piece of paper from an office four hundred miles off that asks this county for the name of a person and a copy of a drawing, and that this county has been unable to answer since the first of Goatmonth and that nobody has asked it again.

Nobody said anything and on that Sunday the light went off the west bank first and found the upper edge of those boards about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and the lamp at the high end of them had been standing lit since the fourth hour and went out with the light because there was nothing left off the wet slate of them to need it, and nobody in that street has ever turned it out early, and five of the nine people in that street went out of it into about five trades and four stayed at the boards.
