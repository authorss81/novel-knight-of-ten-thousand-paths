# Chapter 0738

**Embermonth the nineteenth, YR 322, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, the room over the weigh-house open, and a badge-man asking one question after about four and a half years of not asking one.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and four hundred people were in that street by the second hour of the nineteenth of Embermonth and two hundred of them stopped what they were doing, and the frost came off the ruts about an hour after that, and about four people in nine counties had worked out that the fourth person in this county to be refused has not worked out what he is going to do about it and five had not.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine hundred yards of an old road has been in about four hundred mouths since the thirteenth of Rainmonth, and that is ninety-five days, and two men have said no out loud in two places since the fourteenth of Fallowmonth, and that is thirty-five days and twenty-three days, and nobody in nine counties has been thanked for either of them and nobody has argued with either of them.

Those boards have been repainted twice and are the colour of a wet slate, and the cloth on the bench at the low end of them has been on it since the middle of Hearthmonth, and the lamp at the high end of them is lit at the fourth hour on every day of the week and has been for about four and a half years, and nobody in that street has ever turned it out early.

Then the ordinary half of that Thursday, which is nine tenths of every day anybody in nine counties has ever had, and the drove goes out four days off at about the sixth hour as it goes out on every Thursday of the year.

Nobody said anything and nine beasts went out through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of stone wall at about the sixth hour on the nineteenth of Embermonth and went up about nine hundred yards of that lane and stood in the field with the stone wall round it, and nobody went with them, and that is about twenty-nine drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap is about a yard long and about four feet high, and one man has been carrying stone two miles in his own hands for a hundred and ninety-seven days, and the last yard is about nine feet of a course and he has not started it and nobody has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The brood in a walled yard half a mile below that wall is four frames where there were nine on the night of the eighth of Thawmonth, and that night is two hundred and twenty-one days back, and a brood frame comes back in about four weeks in a year like this one, and about four people in nine counties have said since that four frames is the same figure the hives stood at on the first of Harvestmonth and five have said that it has not moved in about nine weeks and that not moving is the ordinary condition of a figure nobody in nine counties has looked at.

Nobody thanked him and the broom on that east rise is nine acres of standing dead about nine hundred yards off and about two hundred yards above the gap in that wall, and the fifth frame in that yard is not there yet and is not going to be hurried, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a young man who has said out loud that he is not going to make more bees than the bees he has got has then waited about nine days for a frame that has not come and five have said that waiting is not making more bees.

Nobody said anything and the man of about thirty-four with a satchel was at the high end of those boards and said nothing in that street for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and he has said nine things out loud in this county in about four and a half years and said the ninth of them on the thirteenth of Rainmonth and has refused since that day to say anything twice, and nobody asked him and nobody sent for him, and four people in that market street had worked out that a man who has said nine things and has now said nothing for ninety-five days is a man waiting to see whether anybody does anything about the ninth and five had worked out that he has run out and four had said that both of those are the same thing.

The weigh-house weighed two loads of lime and a load of wool that morning, and the man who keeps it said out loud, to four people in and five out, that about four hundred and nine parish rolls come past his floor in a good month, that Embermonth is nineteen days old and about a hundred and ninety sheets have come past that floor in it, and that the man who keeps that floor has never been asked what a sheet weighs and is not going to be.

Nobody thanked anybody and the inn at the high end had four beds full that morning and no price on any board, because nothing has moved a price in that street since the middle of Harvestmonth, and the woman of about fifty-one who keeps that house came out into it at the fifth hour and stopped nine feet short of those boards and would not come nearer, and nine doors down a chandler covered a bench with a cloth rather than write anything at all on it.

The ordinary part of that market morning is that four hundred people each put a thing down and pick it up again, and a man with a barrow of lime went the length of that street nine times and unloaded at four doors and loaded at two, and nobody stopped walking at any part of it, and the near wheel of a cart came off at the low end and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes, and two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped.

Nobody said anything and the room over that weigh-house was open because it is a Thursday and a room over a weigh-house is open five days in seven, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs at the seventh hour and went back up, and four people in that lane saw her come down and five did not, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and the day-book stands at eleven entries and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

Then the seventh hour, and it is at the high end of those boards, and about four and a half years of not asking anything ends in it.

Nobody thanked him and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head came out of that lane off the weigh-house at a slow walk with the bar against his leg and its strap over his shoulder, and nobody in that street hurried him and nobody hurried anything else, and he stopped four feet short of those boards and laid his hand flat on the top rail, and there was a skin of white in that rail at about the seventh hour, and about nine people in that street have said since that a man who puts a hand on the thing he is about to speak at is about to speak carefully.

Nobody said anything and he said it out loud, in the open, to about four hundred people and to about nine of them, in his own trade, and it was a question, and it was about a decision and not about a road, and it was 113 words, and nobody stopped it.

“**I have three clauses in nine hands with no copy of them and I have said in this street four times that a paper does not do a thing to a road, and I have never asked you one question in about four and a half years. Four hundred people in this street have heard nine things and nobody in nine counties is holding any one of them. You are the only man in nine counties who has said out loud in the open that he is not going to argue, and you said that in this street on the twenty-eighth of Fallowmonth. Are you going to be the one who holds it?**”

Nobody said anything and two hundred people in that market street had heard a badge-man ask a question about four and a half years of not asking one and about nine of them had stopped what they were doing while he said it and five had not, and nobody stopped it and nobody answered it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that the first question that badge-man has ever asked anybody in this county is about holding something and five have said that a man who has a word in nine hands that nobody has ever heard him use has spent four and a half years waiting for a question about holding it.

Nobody thanked anybody and then the man of thirty-two with a stick stood at the sea end of that market street with his stick in his right hand and his left hand hanging at his side where anybody could see it, and he did not put it behind him and he did not close it, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside about nine seconds that his left hand was open and five had worked out that he had come here with it open, and nobody in that street had ever noticed that hand before this morning and nobody noticed it now.

Nobody said anything and he said it out loud, in the open, to about four hundred people, in his own trade, and it was 53 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**No. I am not going to hold it and I am not going to give it to anybody, and there is a thing at my left hand and I am not going to use it, and I am not going to say what it is and you are not going to ask me.**”

Nobody said anything and the whole of that street had heard a man of thirty-two say no out loud to a question he had been asked, and about four people in that street had understood every word of the fifty-three and about five had understood none of them, and nobody argued with him and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him a second question, and about four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that a man in this county has said no out loud to a question he was asked and five have said that the first thing he did with a question was refuse it.

Nobody thanked him and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head did not ask him again, and said one thing back out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 27 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**Then that is a decision and I am not going to put it in nine hands and there is nowhere in nine counties I could put it.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in that market street had heard a badge-man say out loud that there is nowhere in nine counties a decision of that kind could be put and five had heard twenty-seven words, and nobody argued with either of the two of them and about four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that two men in this county have said one thing each out loud in the same street and agreed with each other and five have said that they did not agree and that one of them said he was not going to put it anywhere and that is the whole of the agreement.

Nobody said anything and here is what that morning cost and it is not a thing anybody in nine counties has a name for. The thing at that left hand was still there when he said the forty-four words and it is still there this evening and it was warm at about the fourth hour of the morning of this day and it will be warm again in about four days, and saying no to the question did not take it off him and did not shut it and did not put it anywhere, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who says no to the only person who has standing to ask him has not given anything up and five have said that he has given away the only thing he had, which was that nobody knew.

Nobody thanked anybody and that is the second half of it and it is a new act and not the one on the shoulder. On the shoulder he told nobody and nobody asked and nobody noticed the palm, and this morning somebody with standing asked him a question in a market street and he answered it out loud with the hand where anybody could see it and did not put one word of explanation on it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who tells nobody gets asked nothing and that a man who tells one person on purpose gets asked that one question and five have said that the second is the worse of the two and that he chose it.

Nobody said anything and nobody in nine counties has consoled him and nobody in nine counties has said a word to him about it except the man who asked him the question, and nobody thanked him, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man of thirty-two has now been refused twice in the open and has given one thing away in the open and has not been thanked for either and five have said that this county has never had anybody do all three in one month and that nobody here has worked out what it is for.

Nobody said anything and the part of that man of thirty-two which was going to come home to him on the fourth day of Rainmonth is not going to come home, and the reason it is not going to come home is a shoulder on the fourteenth of Fallowmonth and a street four days off on the twenty-sixth of that month, and there is no page in this county that says so and nobody in nine counties has said so out loud, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has been told no twice by two people with standing has been told the true thing about himself by two people who do not know anything about him, and five have said that this is the cheapest way in nine counties to be told anything at all and about four have said that it is not cheap.

Then the rest of that morning, which is nine tenths of it and is nobody's business.

Nobody thanked anybody and the light came off the west bank first and off those boards about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and the lamp at the high end of them was lit at the fourth hour and stood lit until the market was gone, and nobody in that street has ever turned it out early, and about four hundred people went out of that market street into about nine trades and about nine people stayed at the boards.

Nobody said anything and about nine hundred yards of an old road is in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand, and two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand either, and one sheet of paper is in an office four days off, and one thing at a left hand is in one man's head, and about four people in nine counties have said since that this county has four things and four places and that none of the four places can take any of the four things and five have said that a man's head is not one of the four places anybody has counted.

Nobody thanked him and nothing about any of it is in a document, and there is no page in any room in nine counties with the nineteenth of Embermonth on it, and the register in the room over that weigh-house holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and four people in nine counties have said since that a badge-man who has just declined to put a decision into his own nine hands has left a room four hundred paces up a lane with one fewer thing in it than he went in with, and five have said that is the first thing anybody has ever declined to write down in this county.

**And the cost of that Thursday is one question asked after about four and a half years and one answer that gave nothing away except the fact of a thing on a hand, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
