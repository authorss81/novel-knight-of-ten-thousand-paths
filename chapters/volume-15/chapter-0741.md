# Chapter 0741

**Embermonth the twenty-sixth, YR 322, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, the room over the weigh-house open, and a light on nine hundred yards of a grass line at the fourth hour that one man saw and told nobody about.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and four hundred people were in that street by the second hour of the twenty-sixth of Embermonth and two hundred of them stopped what they were doing, and the frost came off the ruts about an hour after that, and four people in nine counties had worked out that a month is nearly done and five had not.

Then four days off, and about nine hundred people along that made road did the ordinary part of a Thursday, and the ordinary part is that nothing about a Thursday is visible on a road.

Nobody said anything and nine beasts went out through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of stone wall at about the sixth hour and went up nine hundred yards of that lane and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with them, and that is about thirty drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth and about four hundred people in that market street have never once watched one.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap is about a yard long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side and one man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for two hundred and four days, and the last yard is about nine feet of a course and he has not started it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The coping along the top of that wall is about nine inches wide and there is a lamp on it that is not lit, and the wall is nine feet high and about nine hundred yards long and it is the only thing anybody in this county has ever built without being asked what it is for, and the man who laid it has never said what it is for and nobody has ever asked him, and about four people in nine counties know that the man who cleans the ninth mile stone can see the whole length of it from the made road and five have never stood where he stands.

The brood in a walled yard half a mile below that wall is five frames where there were nine on the night of the eighth of Thawmonth, and that night is two hundred and twenty-eight days back, and a brood frame comes back in about four weeks in a year like this one, and the fifth came in on the twentieth of Embermonth and is not all brood yet and is not going to be hurried, and four people in nine counties have said since that five frames is the first figure anybody four days off has given this year that is half of what it was and five have said that half is a figure and not a finding.

That yard is about four rods by six behind a stone wall about four feet high and nine hives stand in it, and the ground inside it is beaten flat by nine pairs of boots going over the same twenty feet every morning since the first of Fallowmonth, and the man who cleans the ninth mile stone has never been inside it and the young man who keeps those hives has never been at that stone, and neither of those two men has ever heard the other one mentioned, and about four people in nine counties have said since that two men four hundred yards apart with a wall between them are the ordinary condition of this county and five have said that it is not ordinary anywhere else.

On that east rise the broom was cut for the first time on the morning of the twenty-sixth and about a day of it is done, and about nine days of it is the whole of it, and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide will be left standing when he has finished, and a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide along that shoulder has not closed over, and four people in nine counties have said since that a young man of about nineteen who cut nine days of nine acres in four days has left the last nine hundred yards standing on purpose and five have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked him which nine hundred yards.

Those nine stones stand about the height of a man's knee and about as wide as two hands and the tops of them are all at the same height, and nobody in nine counties has found out why, and the man of thirty-two cleaned about four hundred yards of the grass off the top of two of them with a bit of stick on the fourteenth of Fallowmonth and has not been back up that lane since, and nobody has asked him why he does it.

Nobody said anything and the bar at the head of that made road is in on the lane side and has been in since Mudmonth and it is four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it, and the drove goes up through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of wall and not through the gate, and the man who bars it is forty-one and keeps a drove and a byre and nine hundred yards of wall and has been asked one question about a road in eleven years, and nobody asked him a second one on the twenty-sixth of Embermonth and about nine people in nine counties have said since that nobody is going to either.

Then the weigh-house, which is the only floor in nine counties where about four hundred and nine sheets a month are put down by one man, and it is the only floor in nine counties where a figure is kept out loud.

Nobody thanked him and the weigh-house weighed two loads of lime and a load of wool that morning, and the man who keeps it said out loud, to four people in and five out, and it was 37 words, and nobody thanked him.

“**Embermonth is twenty-six days old and about two hundred and sixty sheets have come past that floor in it and a man has not asked me what a sheet weighs in about four and a half years.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market town had worked out inside about nine seconds that a man who counts sheets for four and a half years and has never been asked what one weighs is a man who has been waiting to be asked, and five had worked out nothing, and four people in nine counties have said since that about two hundred and sixty sheets in twenty-six days is about ten a day and that ten a day is about three hundred in a month of thirty and five have said that three hundred is not four hundred and nine and that four hundred and nine is a month that floor has not had since the middle of a Harvestmonth.

The inn at the high end had four beds full that morning and put no price out on any board, because nothing has moved a price in that street since the middle of Harvestmonth, and the woman of about fifty-one who keeps that house came out into it at about the fifth hour and stopped nine feet short of those boards and would not come nearer to them, and a chandler nine doors down put a cloth over a bench rather than write anything at all on it.

Nobody said anything and a man with a barrow of lime went the length of that street four times and unloaded at one door and loaded at two, and nobody stopped walking at any part of it, and the near wheel of a cart came off at the low end and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes, and two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped.

The room over that weigh-house was open because it is a Thursday and it stands shut behind its own door on every morning of the week the market is not in that street, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs at the seventh hour and went back up, and four people in that lane saw her come down and five did not, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled, and none of that has moved in about four and a half years.

The two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of that hill are a mile of stone on the high side and a mile of orchard on the low side, and about nine hundred trees went out of blossom in the second week of Fallowmonth and the fruit came down into four hundred yards of grass and was still in it in the spring, and the owners are nine households and not one of them has ever asked anybody anything about anybody who walks past its gate, and nobody in nine counties has ever picked one up.

Then the fourth hour of the twenty-sixth of Embermonth, and it is not in that market street and nobody was told about it and there was nobody to tell.

Nobody said anything and at about the fourth hour of that morning, four days off, at the head of that made road and before the light, the top of that shoulder lit up for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over, and it lit along the line and not across it, and it went from the ninth of the nine stones at the top down about nine hundred yards of the nine-foot line in the grass and stopped where the kerbed road begins, and it was not a fire and there was nothing burning on it afterwards and there is nothing on it now.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of thirty-two with a stick was standing at the head of that made road at about the fourth hour of that morning because he cleans the ninth mile stone on a Tuesday and a Saturday and is up before the light, and he saw all nine hundred yards of it from about four hundred paces off at the low end of that road and he did not go up, and he has not gone up there since the fourteenth of Fallowmonth, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man who has said out loud that he is not going up a lane is a man who does not go up it, and five have said that a man who sees nine hundred yards of light on a hill at four in the morning is a man who has been up before the light about nine hundred times and has not seen anything on any of the other of them.

Nobody said anything and he did not go up and he did not go for anybody and he came in off four days of made road on that Thursday with a stick in his right hand and said nothing to anybody about it until the Sunday of the next week, and four people in nine counties have said since that a fact one man saw at the fourth hour of a morning is one man's fact and five have said that this county has known that since about the middle of Longlight the year before.

Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and neither of them has been argued with, and they were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth, and that is forty-two days and thirty days, and nobody in nine counties has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and four people in nine counties have said since that the acceptance he made in a market street on the twenty-eighth of that month is the whole of what he has done about either of them and five have said that thirty days of not arguing with a thing is longer than anybody in this county has gone without asking twice.

One sheet of paper with one line on it has been in an office four days off for thirty days, and the line on it is that a man said a thing out loud in a market street, and nobody in nine counties has asked for it and nobody in nine counties could read it if they asked, and four people in that market town had worked out inside nine seconds that the road office four days off has got one thing in it that it cannot use and five had worked out nothing, and that one thing is the sheet, and nobody in that market street has read it.

The ninth mile stone on that made road is about the height of a knee and was cleaned with a bit of stick on the Tuesday before, and the ring of mud at the foot of it has been gone since the first week of Mudmonth and there is a clean ring in the ground where it stood, and four people in nine counties have said since that a clean ring at the foot of a stone is the only mark a man of thirty-two has ever left on a road and five have said that it is a mark and not a record.

Nobody said anything and then somebody at the third door of that market street asked him one thing out loud at about the ninth hour, kindly, and nobody stopped it, and it was not about a road and not about a wall.

“**Have you been up the hill?**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a question asked about whether a man has been up a hill and five had heard it asked kindly, and nobody stopped it and the man of thirty-two with a stick answered it out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 32 words, and nobody thanked him.

“**I have been on that made road every day since the twenty-sixth of Mudmonth and I have not been up that hill since the fourteenth of Fallowmonth and I am not going.**”

Nobody said anything and nobody said anything back to him and nobody thanked him, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man with a stick and a bad leg has told a market street that he is not going up a hill he has been looking at for about four and a half years and five have said that there is one hill four days off and about nine households past nine stones and that a market street is the wrong place to say it, and four have said that it is the only place in nine counties where four hundred people hear the same sentence at the same time.

The sea is four days south of that market town and about four hundred people live on a far bank across nine hundred yards of water at the top of a tide and about four hundred at the bottom, and a hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway every Monday afternoon in the winter and the twenty-sixth of Embermonth was not a Monday and the lamp was not put there, and nobody in that market street has ever asked a person at a gate at the near end of that water anything at all.

Nobody said anything and here is the whole of what this county has on the twenty-sixth of Embermonth. About nine hundred yards of an old road is in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand. Two refusals are in about four hundred mouths and in nobody's hand either. One sheet of paper is in an office four days off. One thing at a left hand is in one man's head and he has not said out loud that he is never going to use it. And one light on a hilltop four days off is in one man's eyes and he has told nobody, and it is nine days after he said in a market street that he was not going up that hill.

Nobody thanked anybody and nothing about any of it is in a document, and there is no page in any room in nine counties with the twenty-sixth of Embermonth on it, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the fifth time a man of thirty-two has worked a thing out and not said it and five have said that the first was the first day of a year and the second was the twenty-second of Longlight and the third was the twenty-fifth of Harvestmonth and that the fourth was yesterday.
