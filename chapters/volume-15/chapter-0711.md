# Chapter 0711

**Mudmonth the twenty-sixth, YR 322, a Saturday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, the room over the weigh-house open, and forty-eight days since a frost on a night nobody in nine counties was told about.**

A Saturday in that market town of about two thousand people is the loudest thing in nine counties, and about four hundred people are in that street by the second hour and about two hundred of them stop what they are doing, and the proportion has not moved since the first of Embermonth, and nine people in nine counties have said since that it is the only figure anybody in this county can check twice a week and has never once been written down.

Nobody thanked anybody and the mud was on every road in that town by the sixth hour and there has been mud on them every morning since the twenty-fourth of Thawmonth, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come past the floor of that weigh-house in a good month and in Mudmonth they come past it wet.

Nobody thanked him and the weigh-house weighed two loads of lime and a load of wool and the man who keeps it said out loud, to four people in and five out, that a cart that has been in the low side of a road carries about nine times its own weight of road out of it in about four loads, and that nobody in nine counties has ever asked him for that figure, and four people in that market street had heard him say it four times since the first of Mudmonth and about five had heard it twice.

Nobody said anything and the mud was not the mud of a wet winter, which is a thing that goes into the ground and stays there, and this was the mud of a thaw, which is a thing that comes up out of the ground in about nine days and goes away in about nine more, and four people in nine counties have said since that a road in the first week of Mudmonth is worse than a road in the worst of Frostmonth and about five have said that this is because the ground under a road in Frostmonth is frozen and a road on frozen ground is a road standing on itself.

Nobody thanked anybody and the frost came out of that ground again on the Sunday night and it had been in the ruts at the low end of that market street by the seventh hour on the Monday morning, and it went to powder under a heel, and two men were unloading boards off a cart and breathing out of their mouths and not talking, because talking at that hour on a Saturday costs a man about nine seconds of work he does not get back.

Then the ordinary half, which is nine tenths of every day anybody in nine counties has ever had, and which is the whole of this morning until about the fourth hour.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of thirty-two had come in off that made road at about the fourth hour the night before and had not gone out again, and the four days of that made road are hard at the two ends and soft in the middle, and it took him about nine hours longer in the last week of Thawmonth than it had at the middle of Harvestmonth, and about four people in that market town had seen the coat and about five had not.

Nobody thanked him and the two days out and the two days back are the whole of what a man who walks that road has, and about nine hundred people live along those four days and every one of them knows which house he comes out of, and four of them have offered him a bed since the middle of Frostmonth and about five have not, and nine people in nine counties have said since that he has not taken one.

Nobody said anything and he cleaned the ninth mile stone on the Saturday morning before he came in, as he has cleaned it about nine hundred times since the middle of Harvestmonth, and the stone had about four inches of mud round the foot of it and the mud had gone into the ground and left a ring, and nine people in nine counties know that he cleans that stone and about five have ever watched him do it.

Nobody thanked him and the market street is about nine hundred paces from the gate at the low end of it to the inn at the high end and it runs east and west, and the bank to the west of that town is higher than the street, which is the only thing anybody has ever been able to say about the light in it, and that the light goes out of that street before the light goes out of the sky.

Then the hives, which are four days off at the head of that made road and half a mile below the wall, and which are a Tuesday and a Saturday in every week of the year.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine hives stand in a yard about four rods by six behind a stone wall about four feet high, and most of them are straw skeps on a beam about a hand high and two of them are frame hives about nine feet apart on the same beam, and the young man of about nineteen who keeps them counted the brood in nine frames in the middle of Frostmonth and in the middle of Longlight and did not count it on the eighth of Thawmonth because the frost came on that night.

Nobody thanked him and nine frames of brood went in a frost on the night of the eighth of Thawmonth and it is the twenty-sixth of Mudmonth and that is forty-eight days, and a brood frame comes back in about four weeks in a year like this one and so there is one frame in those two hives where there were nine, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the only number in nine counties that anybody can check and that the young man it belongs to has nobody in nine counties to give it to.

Nobody said anything and about four of those nine people have done the sum and about five have not, and the sum is one frame and twenty days, and there is no figure anywhere in nine counties for what a young man of nineteen eats in the nine days a year when nine frames do not come back, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of what a year is in a trade and that nobody has ever been asked to hear it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the drove at the head of that made road went out on the Thursday as it goes out every Thursday, which was the twenty-fourth of Mudmonth, and went up about nine hundred yards of that lane and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this has happened about nine times since the second of Mudmonth and that it has never happened in about eleven years before that, and about four have said that a drove does not mind and about five have said that the beasts know the way.

Nobody thanked him and about nine yards of the nine hundred yards of wall at the head of that made road came down on the twenty-eighth of Thawmonth and the water took the foot of it out on the second of Mudmonth, and it has been down for about thirty days, and nine people in nine counties know that a man has been putting stone in it alone since the second of Mudmonth and about five have ever asked him where the stone came from.

Nobody said anything and the stone is quarried about two miles off on the low side, and a man with nine yards to rebuild and thirty days gone would have to walk two miles for about nine loads and carry them back on his own, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is what he has been doing every day since the second of Mudmonth and five have said that this is what he will be doing until the end of Mudmonth.

Nobody thanked anybody and the bar he carries is about four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it and it weighs about sixty pounds with the stones, and nobody in nine counties has ever asked whether a man should be on the end of a bar and nine people in nine counties have said that a man with a bad leg is worth one man without one and that this is the arithmetic of every wall in every county and that nobody has ever put it in a book.

Then the room over that weigh-house, which is open because it is a Saturday and a room over a weigh-house is open five days in seven.

Nobody thanked anybody and the room is up thirteen stairs off a lane four streets up that market street, and the lane is about four hundred paces from that street, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the stairs at the seventh hour with a leaf in her hand and went back up, and about four people in that lane saw her come down and about five did not.

Nobody thanked him and the man of thirty-two went up those thirteen stairs at about the fourth hour of that morning, which is the first time anybody in nine counties has seen him in that room in about four and a half years, and there were two people in it, and neither of them said anything to him and he said nothing to either of them, and he stood in it about as long as it takes to turn a cart and looked at a press standing open with no date on it and came down.

Nobody said anything and four people in that lane had worked out that a man who goes past a door four hundred paces about nine hundred times has gone up the stairs once, and about five had not noticed him go up at all, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the most careful piece of silence anybody in this county has produced in about four and a half years and that nobody involved in it has ever described it as anything.

Nobody thanked anybody and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them, and the fourth ruled head over a place where a finding goes is empty, and no fifth head is ruled, and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the three series stand and are not derived from one another, and none of that was touched on the twenty-sixth of Mudmonth and none of it was touched while he stood in the room.

Nobody said anything and nobody thanked him and about four hundred and nine sheets came past that room in the last month across three series and a roll of hands is not a sheet and does not come past the floor of a weigh-house, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time anybody has noticed that the practice of that room has a hole in the shape of a person.

Then the thing that this whole month turns on, and it is said in a trade and not in a street.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of about fifty-two who keeps four miles of made road under contract for about nineteen years came in at about the fifth hour and said one thing out loud, in the open, to about nine people in four trades, and he said it in his trade and not in anybody's, and it was thirty-six words.

“**The shoulder came up on the nineteenth and the lane is under to the second gate, and there has been nine days this month when a man could have stood up there and not found out.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a roadkeeper give a date for the ground and five had heard nine days, and nobody stopped him and nobody asked him a second question, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the second date anybody in this county has given for anything since the first of Mudmonth and that the first one was a date of Hearthmonth given by the same man.

Nobody thanked him and the shoulder is above the lane and about two miles from the head of that made road and it is about nine hundred yards wide at the top, and a young man of about nineteen stood on it on the twelfth of Thawmonth and found about nine hundred yards of road with kerbs on both sides and then about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide in the grass and nine stones set across the end of it at the same height.

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had worked out inside about nine seconds that the ground a young man was standing on in the middle of Thawmonth came up on the nineteenth of Mudmonth and about five had not, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that two people in this county have stood on the same day on the same piece of ground and neither of them knew it.

Nobody thanked anybody and there were nine days between the nineteenth of Mudmonth and the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth, and on all nine of them the ground on that shoulder was firm enough to walk on and there was no way of getting to it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of what a road is, and about five have said that this is the whole of what this county is.

Nobody said anything and somebody in that market street asked him out loud, kindly, at about the sixth hour, whether anybody had tried to get to it in those nine days, and about two hundred people in that street heard the question and nine people in nine counties have said since that it is the first question anybody has asked about that shoulder since the nineteenth of Thawmonth.

“**Then has anybody walked on it?**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a man ask the second question anybody has asked about that shoulder in about nine days and five had not, and the man who keeps four miles of made road said that nobody had and that he had not and that he had given the date because a date is a thing a roadkeeper gives and not a thing anybody walks on, and nobody said anything else and nobody asked him a second question.

Nobody thanked him and the water came up in that lane at the head of that made road on the twenty-fourth of Thawmonth and has been under since, and a gate at the end of a lane that is under is not shut and is not open and is the third thing, and a man of about thirty-eight who puts boards on the inside of gates said that out loud in this street on the first of Mudmonth and nobody has asked him what the third thing is called.

Nobody said anything and then the middle of that morning happened the way the middle of every morning in that street happens, and there is no account of it in any book and there could not be, because it is about nine trades and about nine hundred people and nobody watching.

The near wheel of a cart came off at the low end and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes and nobody stopped walking. A boy of about eleven came down the lane from the weigh-house with a pail and went back up it with the pail full, and nobody said anything to him at either end of it. Two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped, which is how that argument ends nine times in ten. The frost came off the ruts by the ninth hour and the light came up the side of the weigh-house and found the upper edge of the boards, and the boards were the colour of a wet slate and had been since the middle of Harvestmonth.

Nobody thanked anybody and the market went on, and two hundred people went back to nine trades, and the two hundred are not the same two hundred every Saturday, and nine people stayed at the boards, and nobody wrote any of it down.

Nobody thanked anybody and the four hundred people in that street have heard four things said out loud since the twelfth of Thawmonth, and four of them believe them and five have never spoken to any of the other four, and nine people in nine counties have said since that about four hundred people hearing a thing and not acting on it is not a thing that can be put back the way a thing that has not been said can.

Nobody said anything and the cloth over the bench at the low end of those boards was on it and nothing lifted it and nobody asked what was under it, and the lamp at the high end of the boards was lit at the fourth hour and stood lit until the market was gone, and about nine people in that market town have said since that the lamp is the only thing in nine counties that has happened on every day for about four and a half years without anybody having decided that it should.

**And the cost of that Saturday is one frame of brood where there were nine, thirty days of a wall with nine yards of it down, and about nine days this month when a man could have stood on a shoulder and not found out, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a tallow dip at 3, a hurdle hoop at 7, a peg at 10, a wall peg at 12, a hurdle at 15, a pot of tallow at 16, a hand bar at 18, a straw skirt at 19, a barrow wheel at 24, and a barrow at 28.** Three, seven, ten, twelve, fifteen, sixteen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty-four and twenty-eight is one hundred and fifty-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and fifty-two pence is three marks and eight over.** The forty-eight days between a frost on the night of the eighth of Thawmonth and a market morning in Mudmonth are the whole of what one young man's year has been worth in YR 322, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a figure that comes out of a bad year as a loss and never as a price is the ordinary way of every trade in every county, and about five have said that this county has decided not to notice it about nine trades.
