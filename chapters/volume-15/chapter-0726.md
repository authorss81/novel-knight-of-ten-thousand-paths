# Chapter 0726

**Rainmonth the eighth, YR 322, a Monday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, four days on a shoulder above a drove road with nine acres of broom on the east rise four hundred yards up, and a thing that happened on the fourth day at about the fourth hour that nobody on that shoulder was told about.**

Nobody thanked anybody and the shoulder is above the lane at the head of that made road and two miles above the wall, and it is nine hundred yards wide at the top and it is grass and it is not level and there is a rise in it going east, and nine people in nine counties know that and five have ever stood on it, and the ground on it came up on the nineteenth of Mudmonth and for nine days in that month it was firm enough to walk on and there was no way of getting to it.

Nobody said anything and there were two people on it, and they are not a party and there is no head of them and there is no paper of them, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the first time in four and a half years that two people have been left on a hilltop in this county was two people who went up with a drove on a Thursday morning and did not come down with it.

Nobody thanked him and the man of thirty-two with a stick went up that lane with the drove on the fourth of Rainmonth because it goes up on Thursdays, and the drove came back down at about the fifth hour of that Thursday and he did not go with it, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in eleven years that anybody has gone up that lane and not come down with the drove and five have said that it is the first time in about eleven years that anybody has stayed on that shoulder at night.

Nobody said anything and the young man of about nineteen went up with that drove on the fourth of Rainmonth with about nine yards of tarred twine and a sickle and about nine feet of new waxed cord and about four hundred yards of a lane past the drove's nine hundred yards, and about four people in that market town had worked out that he went past the field and about five had not, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a person who has to walk about nine hundred yards of a drove road to get to his own errand is still on his own errand.

Then the broom, and it is on, and it is about nine acres on the east rise.

Nobody thanked anybody and a broom comes on a hill about the fourth of Rainmonth and it is about nine feet high where it is highest and about two feet where the wind has been at it, and it is the only broom within a day's walk of a walled yard of nine hives, and about nine people in nine counties know that and about five have ever cut it, and four people in nine counties have said since that a broom that comes on a hill every year and is cut by nobody has been coming on that hill for longer than anything else anybody in nine counties has ever watched.

Nobody said anything and it is cut with a sickle and not with a scythe, because a scythe takes about nine paces to turn and a man with four bundles of twine on his shoulder cannot turn nine paces, and four people in nine counties have said since that a sickle is a tool for about one acre and five have said that a broom of nine acres cut with a sickle takes about nine days and that this man has four.

Nobody thanked him and about four days of that weather were clear and cold with the wind off the low side and about nine hundred yards of it, and on the sixth and seventh the wind dropped at about the eighth hour and nine people in nine counties have said since that a clear day is the only day a sound carries nine hundred yards of hill and that a shoulder is the highest ground for about nine miles in any direction.

Then the finding, and it is said out loud on a shoulder in a trade and not in a street, and it is said in his own order and not anybody's.

Nobody thanked him and the young man of about nineteen stopped cutting at about the seventh hour on the fourth day and sat down on a stone about nine yards back from the first of nine stones and said the first of three things out loud, in the open, to one person, in his own trade, and it was 69 words.

“**There is about nine hundred yards of kerbed road on this shoulder and then about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide in the grass and then nine stones at the end of it, and I walked all of it in Thawmonth and I walked all of it again this week and it is where I said it was and it is not nine yards different anywhere.**”

Nobody said anything and about one person on that shoulder had heard a young man give a figure for a road he has now walked twice and nine people in nine counties have said since that the first time anybody checked anything anybody in this county has ever said out loud in the open was in Thawmonth, and four have said that the second time was on the fourth of Rainmonth and five have said that nobody has checked the third thing anybody in this county has ever said out loud.

Nobody thanked him and the man of thirty-two did not repeat the figures back and did not write them down and said one word, and the word was how, and the young man of about nineteen said the second of three things out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 72 words.

“**The line is nine feet wide because the east rise is cut along it once a year and a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years. I have seen two other nine-foot lines in this county and one of them was gone in two years and one of them was gone in five, and that is how I know it is being kept and not left.**”

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two put his hand flat on the top of the first of the nine stones and it was not loose and said one word, and the word was who, and the young man of about nineteen said the third of three things out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 78 words.

“**The nine households past those nine stones keep a rota of that flat that has been kept about nine years and has not been broken once in the nine years, and the grass on the road is cut with the flat and not as a road, and nobody else cuts any of it, and the kerb is older than the cutting and I cannot tell you how much older because there is nobody in nine counties to ask.**”

Nothing in that was said out loud twice and the man of thirty-two put his hand flat on the top of the first of the nine stones after it and it was not loose, and four people in nine counties have said since that nine households have been keeping nine hundred yards of an old road going for about nine years without a head on it and without a paper and that it is the longest anything anybody in nine counties has ever heard of being kept, and five have said that a household which keeps beasts cuts grass to its own boundary and that the boundary is nine stones and that the road is whatever the boundary happens to be on.

Nobody thanked him and then a question was asked out loud on that shoulder at about the eighth hour of the fourth day by the man of thirty-two and nobody stopped it, and nobody answered it.

“**Then what is it for?**”

Nobody said anything and about one person on that shoulder had heard a man ask what a road is for and the young man of about nineteen said one thing back and it was in his own trade and it was 34 words.

“**It is not for anything. It is a boundary first and it is nine hundred yards of it, and whatever it was before that nobody in nine counties knows and nobody has asked me.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that anybody has said out loud on that shoulder that a road has no reason, and about four people have said that a road with no reason is not a road and five have said that a road with no reason is a road and that the two sentences cannot both be said in the same breath by the same person.

Then the fourth hour of the fourth day, and it is the whole of what happened at it, and nobody on that shoulder was told about it and he told nobody.

Nobody said anything and on the fourth day, at about the fourth hour, the wind had dropped again and about nine hundred yards of that shoulder went out flat and blue to the low side with nothing on it, and the left palm of the man of thirty-two was warm, and it was the warmth it has about four days in seven since the first of Embermonth, and it was not a heat in the hand and it was not a heat in the air.

Nobody said anything and there was a thing at that palm, and the thing was not a road and it was not a door and it was not a name, and what it was was the ninth hour of a night about as far back as a man of thirty-two can remember being, and it was there the whole of about as long as it takes to turn a cart and it was not going anywhere while it was there.

Nobody said anything and he did not close his hand and he did not open it further and he did not put it behind him, and he looked at the ground at his own feet, and there is about nine inches of that grass in front of a boot on that shoulder in Rainmonth and about nine inches of stone under it, and he looked at both of them for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and about nine hundred yards off up the east rise the sickle went into the broom and came out again and went in again.

Nobody said anything and it was a transfer and he witnessed it and did not conduct it, and there is no other set of words for a thing like that in nine counties, and nobody on that shoulder used them and nobody anywhere has asked him for any, and the young man of about nineteen did not look up, and the wind did not get up again until about the fifth hour, and by then there was nothing at that palm that either of them would have been able to say had gone.

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two did not say a word about it that afternoon or the next morning or on the way down, and about as long as it takes to turn a cart after the fourth hour he cut about nine inches off a stalk of broom with the sickle the young man had left lying on a stone and put it inside his coat, and nobody asked him what he was doing and he did not say.

Nobody thanked him and four people in nine counties have said since that a man who says things out loud in a market street for a living has two accounts and one of them is not his, and about five have said that he has said nine things out loud in about four and a half years and that not one of them was a thing he had done, and about four people have said that a man who keeps two accounts is a man who has decided which one he is in and about five have said that he has not decided and that the fact that he keeps two is the whole of the not having decided.

Nobody said anything and the drove came back down that lane at about the fifth hour of the fourth of Rainmonth and about nine hundred yards of it went past below them going down, and four people in nine counties have said since that two men heard nine beasts come down a drove road nine hundred yards below a shoulder and neither of them went down to see and five have said that a person who has been on a shoulder since the sixth hour has a bad leg and four have said that a drove knows its own way down.

Nobody thanked him and from the top of that shoulder a person can see nine acres of broom going brown on the east rise, about nine hundred yards of kerbed road going away toward the low side, about nine hundred yards of a line in the grass going the other way, the coping on a wall nine hundred yards below with a lamp on it that is not lit, and the sea four days south of that, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man who stands on that shoulder long enough stops seeing any of it and five have said that is the ordinary condition of a hilltop.

Nobody said anything and about four days of sleep on a stone on a hilltop is four nights in which every one of the two of them was awake twice in the night and neither of them said anything about it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man who sleeps on a stone on a hill does not sleep and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the ordinary account of it and about five have said that a person who has been on a hill for nine hours wakes at the third hour and at the ninth and that this is a fact about the ground and not about the stones.

Nobody said anything and about nine inches of a stalk of broom came down two miles of unmade road inside the right sleeve of a coat that has salt in it, and nobody in nine counties has seen it, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a thing that is found is not a thing that is used until somebody has carried it off the hill in his own hand, and about five have said that a stalk of broom nine inches long is not a road and is the first object anybody in this county has taken off that shoulder that was not a broom.

Nobody thanked anybody and the ordinary half of four days on a shoulder is nine tenths of it, and there is no wall on that shoulder and no tree on it and nothing to get in behind, and the wind comes off the low side about nine hundred yards and goes over the east rise and has nothing on it to stop against.

Nobody said anything and they did not light a fire, and nine people in nine counties have said since that two people who have been on a hilltop for four nights in Rainmonth and have not lit a fire is the second strangest thing this county has produced in about four and a half years, and four have said the reason is that there is nothing on that shoulder to burn that is not somebody's and five have said the reason is that nobody wanted to be the first one to put smoke on it.

Nobody thanked him and they carried water up from the field with the stone wall round it, which is about nine hundred yards below them and about two hundred yards to the low side, and one of them went for it every morning of the four days and came back with two pails, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the only labour anybody in nine counties has ever recorded about that shoulder and that it went in no document at all.

Nobody said anything and the frost on the mornings of the fifth, the sixth and the seventh was about a sixteenth of an inch on the tops of the nine stones and about an inch on the grass either side of them, and the man of thirty-two cleaned about four hundred yards of the grass off the top of two of the nine with a bit of stick on the second morning because a stone under nine inches of grass is not a stone anybody can see, and nobody thanked him and nine people in nine counties have said since that the second time anybody has cleaned one of those stones it was the same man and five have said that nobody has ever asked him why he does it.

Nobody said anything and he did not put a foot past the ninth of the nine stones and he did not go down the line in the grass towards them past the fourth, and nothing on the other side of them was seen by anybody on this side of them, and four people in nine counties have said since that three times in about four and a half years a man has stood at that end of that line and not gone over it and five have said that this is the first time one of them has stayed four nights.

Nobody thanked him and the four bundles were tied on the seventh evening about nine hands across in one of them and about nine feet of twine round each one, and they weigh about as much as a man wants to carry two miles on an unmade road with a bad leg, and four people in nine counties have said since that four bundles is the most a man can carry down that lane and five have said that a man who has cut nine acres with a sickle for four days has about nine days of it left on that hill.

Nobody thanked anybody and then the thing about using it, which is the second half of that finding, and it is twelve words.

Nobody thanked him and the young man of about nineteen said it out loud on that shoulder at about the eighth hour of the seventh day, in the open, to one person, in his own trade, and it was 12 words.

“**A thing that is found is not a thing that is used.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that anybody in this county has said out loud that there is a difference between a road that has been found and a road that is kept, and five have said that this county has been finding things for about four and a half years and has not kept one, and nobody thanked him and nobody said it back to him.

Nobody thanked him and the light came off the low side of that shoulder at about the seventh hour on the seventh day and the wind came across about nine hundred yards of it from the low side, and there is nothing to see from the top of a shoulder at the seventh hour in Rainmonth except nine acres of a broom going brown and about nine hundred yards of a road going away toward the low side.

Nobody said anything and nobody crossed the nine stones and nothing on the other side of them was seen by anybody on this side of them, and four bundles of broom came down two miles of unmade road on the eighth day of Rainmonth behind one young man and one man of thirty-two with a stick, and four people in nine counties have said since that four bundles is the whole of what about four and a half years has produced off that hill for anybody in nine counties and five have said that four bundles is the first thing anybody has ever taken off it.

**And the cost of those four days is a transfer that was witnessed and not conducted, about nine acres of a broom still standing on an east rise, and four bundles going down two miles of lane, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
