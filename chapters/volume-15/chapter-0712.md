# Chapter 0712

**Mudmonth the twenty-ninth, YR 322, a Tuesday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, four days out at the head of that made road where the lane came out of the water the day before and about nine yards of a wall is down, and a man of thirty-two with a stick at the mouth of a lane.**

A Tuesday is not a market morning and the boards in that market street stood in an empty street with nothing on them, and about four people came to them at about the ninth hour four days off and about five did not, and the badge-man was not there and nobody sent for him.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of thirty-two went out on that made road at about the sixth hour and walked the four days, and it took him about four hours longer in Mudmonth than it had at the middle of Harvestmonth, and the four days of that road are hard at the two ends and soft in the middle, and the hard part at this end is the causeway and the soft part is the two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of the hill.

Nobody thanked him and there are eleven houses on that made road and about nine hundred people living along it, and every one of those nine hundred people knows which of them goes out on it at the sixth hour, because there has been one man doing it since the first of Longlight and a road of eleven houses has a short memory for anything else.

Nobody said anything and he came off that causeway at the seventh hour and turned round and went at the wall-foot, and the two miles of wall and orchard on that made road are a stone wall on the high side for about a mile and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the orchard is about nine hundred trees and the owners are about nine households and not one of them has ever asked him anything about it.

Nobody thanked him and the orchard was in blossom and the mud under the wall on the high side of it was about four inches deep at the wall-foot and about nine inches in the ruts, and the frost came up out of the ground at about the eleventh hour the way it does, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a man who walks one road every morning for four and a half years knows what that road smells like in every month and that nobody has ever asked him.

Nobody said anything and the light was off the coping on that causeway before the light was off the sky, and the lamp at that gate is put on the coping at the low side every Monday afternoon in the winter and was not put there on a Tuesday, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the only lamp on this made road and that it is lit whether anybody is there or not.

Then the second gate from the top of that made road, and the woman who keeps it, and she was in her own gateway doing her own work on her own account.

Nobody thanked anybody and her gate is shut and it is the second one from the top of that road and she is about thirty-nine and she has not kept a horse in about nine years and has not wanted one, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a gate keeper who keeps a horse is a gate keeper who can be asked to go somewhere, and about five have said that she has said that in two market streets since the first of Hearthmonth and about four have said she has said it nowhere at all.

Nobody thanked her and she came in on foot on the Tuesday because the top of that road is four miles from that market town and she has not kept a horse in about nine years, and she was taking water up to a house that has no other water and she took two pails and put them down at the gate and did not put them down inside it, because a pail inside a gateway is a thing that has to be carried out again.

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two went by at about the third hour and she said the thing out loud, in the open, on her own account, to about one person, and it was forty-two words, and she said it in her trade and not in anybody's.

“**A gate is shut against a lane and a lane is a road, and I have kept a gate shut against that lane for as long as I have kept it and the water was never in it once until this year.**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in nine counties had heard a gate keeper say that a gate is a thing done to a road and five had heard forty-two words, and nobody stopped her and nobody asked her a second question, and nine people in nine counties have said since that she has said no four times in a market street this month and that this is the fifth time and that the fifth time was not a no.

Nobody thanked her and then he went on up the made road and got to the head of it at about the eleventh hour, and the wall was on his left and the byre was at the low end of it, and about nine yards of that wall was down where it had come down on the twenty-eighth of Thawmonth, which is about thirty-one days ago, and the water took the foot of it out on the second of Mudmonth and has been at the bottom of the gap ever since.

Nobody thanked him and the gap is four feet high at its lowest point because the stones below it have not washed out, and a gap four feet high is a gap a man can get through on his hands and knees and a drove cannot, and nine people in nine counties know that the only opening anybody has ever made in that nine hundred yards of wall in about eleven years is four feet high and about nine feet wide and is the width of a drove.

Nobody said anything and the stones that came out of the gap are about nine pieces and nine of them are whole, and they are lying in the mud at the bottom of the gap where the water put them, and the water has been going over them for about twenty-nine days, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of what is wrong at the head of that made road and that it has been the whole of it since the twenty-eighth of Thawmonth.

Then the lane, which came out of the water the day before.

Nobody thanked anybody and the lane is about nine feet wide at the head and it is not a made road and never has been, and there is about two miles of it and it goes up over the shoulder of the hill at about a rise in nine, and the water came up in it on the twenty-fourth of Thawmonth and went out of it on the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth, and that is thirty-four days, and nine people in nine counties know that the lane goes under about twice a year and about five have ever seen it do it.

Nobody said anything and four people in nine counties have said since that a gate at the end of a lane that is under is not shut and is not open and is the third thing, and that a man of about thirty-eight who puts boards on the inside of gates said so out loud in a market street on the first of Mudmonth and nobody has asked him what the third thing is called.

Nobody thanked him and the lane came out of the water on the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth and the gate at the head of it is shut, and a shut gate at the end of a lane that is not under is the ordinary state of a gate and there is nothing in it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the third thing stopped being the third thing at about the fourth hour on the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth and that nobody in nine counties noticed.

Nobody said anything and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the second time in about four and a half years that a thing this county said out loud has quietly stopped being true, and that the first one was a rule about shutting roads, and that neither of them was noticed by anybody who said it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the bar went in on the lane side on the Thursday morning after the water went out and it is in now, and four people in nine counties have said since that a man puts a bar back in on the side it goes in on and does not think about it, and about five have said that a man who has barred a gate on the lane side for about eleven years thinks about it.

Nobody said anything and the drove went out on the Thursday as it goes out every Thursday, which was the twenty-fourth of Mudmonth, while the lane still had nine inches of water in it, and it went up about nine hundred yards and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down, and nobody went with it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that nine inches of standing water is nothing to a beast and that this is the only fact about that lane that nine counties and one drove would agree on.

Then the hives, which are half a mile below that wall on the low side and off a lane of their own that is not made, and it is a Tuesday and the young man of about nineteen does his Tuesday.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine hives stand in that yard and most of them are straw skeps on a beam about a hand high and two of them are frame hives, and the bees in them had been flying about four feet off the ground all morning and going out and coming back with nothing, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that bees fly low when there is nothing out and fly high when there is, and that this is a thing a man can tell about a hive about two days before anybody else in nine counties can.

Nobody thanked him and there is one frame of brood in those two hives where there were nine on the night before the eighth of Thawmonth, and a brood frame comes back in about four weeks in a year like this one, and about four people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of what a man of about nineteen knows this year and about five have said that he has not said it to anybody and that nobody in nine counties has asked him.

Nobody said anything and he cut no comb that Tuesday and made no frame and walked to the ridge about a mile and a quarter at about a rise in seven and back and got the water, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that a young man carrying water up a hill two trips on a Tuesday and two on a Saturday has about nine thousand other things to do and that nobody in nine counties knows any of them and that this is not a complaint.

Nobody thanked anybody and then came the wall, and the man who keeps it, and he does not explain anything.

Nobody thanked anybody and he was in the gap with about nine stones of the nine hundred yards' worth laid out on the high side of it in a line, and he had not asked anybody for stone and nobody had brought him any, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man with nine yards to rebuild and about thirty-one days gone would have to walk two miles for about nine loads and carry them back on his own, and that this is what he has done every day since the second of Mudmonth.

Nobody thanked him and he was asked nothing, and nothing was said to him, and the man of thirty-two stood at the low end of about nine yards of wall that is nine feet high and did not put his hand on it, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the second time in about four and a half years that two men have been at that wall together and that the first time was nine days ago and sixteen words were said, and that nothing has been said since.

Nobody said anything and four people in nine counties have said since that the first question anybody asked at that gate was asked on the twenty-sixth of Goatmonth and was not asked twice on the fifth of Thawmonth and has not been asked since, and about five have said that a man of thirty-two has now been at the mouth of that lane four times since the twenty-sixth of Goatmonth and has asked nothing on any of them, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of about forty-one laid a stone and looked at it and laid another one and did not look at the man of thirty-two, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man who has been asked one question in about eleven years and had the gate opened for about as long as it takes to shut a cart has nothing to add and has never had anything to add, and that this is the whole of what a wall is for.

Nobody said anything and a boy of about eleven who keeps goats on the low side of that lane was at the gap with nine goats and a stick, because nine goats will go through a gap four feet high if the gap is about nine feet wide, and he asked a question out loud, in the open, on his own account, and nobody stopped him.

“**Has it gone down yet?**”

Nobody said anything and about four people in that lane had heard a boy of about eleven ask a question about water and five had heard a boy of about eleven who keeps goats ask a question he has no business asking, and he was told that it had, on the day before, and that it had been in since the twenty-fourth of Thawmonth, and that he said he knew and asked because the man who keeps the goats always asks, and nobody thanked him for it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the light came up off the coping on that wall before the light went off the sky, and the man of thirty-two went on up the made road on his own with his stick in his right hand and got back into that market town at about the eighth hour, and four people in that market town noticed the coat and about five did not.

Nobody thanked him and he did not go up that lane, and there has never been any need in this county for anybody to go and see, and four people in nine counties have said since that a lane which came out of the water the day before is the best week of the year to walk up and about five have said that the water was nine inches deep on the Thursday and the drove went through it and a man with a bad leg would not have.

Nobody said anything and the shoulder came up on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, which is ten days ago, and the ground on it has been firm every day since, and nobody has been on it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is now the tenth day of a shoulder that nine counties can walk on and nobody has walked on, and about five have said that this is not a thing anybody decides.

Nobody thanked anybody and the room over that weigh-house was shut behind its own door on that Tuesday because a Tuesday shuts at noon on the Saturday and does not open again until the seventh hour on the Monday, and four people in that market town had noticed the bolt and about five had not, and nobody sent for anybody and nobody was sent for.

Nobody said anything and then the ordinary half of a Tuesday in Mudmonth happened four days off, and about nine hundred people along that made road did the ordinary part of it, and the ordinary part is that nothing about a Tuesday is visible on a road.

Nobody thanked anybody and the bar was in and the drove had gone up and come back and about nine yards of a wall went forward about four feet, and a man stood at the mouth of a lane that is not under for the first time in thirty-four days and looked up nine hundred yards of it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the whole of the twenty-ninth of Mudmonth at the head of that made road is a man looking up a lane and not going up it, and four of them have said that he will and five have said that he will not.

Nobody thanked anybody and the water in that lane went out on the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth and nobody in nine counties has written that down, and there is no page in any room in this county with the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth on it, and four people in nine counties have said since that a lane coming out of a flood is a thing that happens about twice a year at nine miles off a market town and that this county has never once put it in a document.

**And the cost of that Tuesday is thirty-four days of water in a lane, thirty-one days of nine yards of wall, and a shoulder that has been firm for ten days with nobody on it, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a nail at 4, a walling spade at 6, a bar staple at 10, a gate pin at 12, a hurdle rail at 15, a hand bar at 17, a coping stone at 18, a bight of rope at 21, a gate shoe at 22, and a hurdle at 28.** Four, six, ten, twelve, fifteen, seventeen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-two and twenty-eight is one hundred and fifty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and fifty-three pence is three marks and nine over.** The shoulder at the head of that made road came up on the nineteenth of Mudmonth and the lane at its foot came out of the water on the twenty-eighth, and about nine days passed in between in which the ground was firm and there was no way of getting to it, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the only time in about four and a half years that this county has lost nine days by having nothing to do, and about five have said that it is not lost and is put by.
