# Chapter 0749

**Frostmonth the fifteenth, YR 322, a Thursday and a market morning, the second hour in the market street at the boards with about four hundred people in it and about two hundred of them stopped, the room over the weigh-house open, a printed sheet come down the made road from an office four hundred miles off asking this county one question it cannot answer, and a badge-man asking a second one in the same street and getting nothing.**

A Thursday in that market town of about two thousand people is a market morning, and four hundred people were in that street by the second hour of the fifteenth of Frostmonth and two hundred of them stopped what they were doing, and the frost came off the ruts an hour after that, and four people in nine counties had worked out that a second cart had come up that made road this month and five had not.

Nobody thanked anybody and that second cart came up four days of made road on the Tuesday before with paper in it and stood outside the Crown's road office at the low end of that street for one day and went on up the made road at the sixth hour with nine sacks of wool in it, and four people in that market street had worked out that this is the third cart in about six weeks to come up that made road with paper in it and five had worked out nothing at all.

A set of boards has been standing at the low end of that street since the middle of Longlight the year before and has been repainted twice and is the colour of a wet slate, and there is a cloth on the bench at the low end of it that has been there since the middle of Hearthmonth and nobody has lifted it and nobody has asked what is under it, and the lamp at the high end goes on at the fourth hour on every day of the week on which the light has gone off the wet slate and nobody in that street has ever turned it out early.

On that east rise the broom has been off for about nine days and about nine hundred yards of a line nine feet wide is left standing where it stands, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a young man of about nineteen cut nine acres in nine days and left nine hundred yards of it and that the nine hundred yards are the same nine hundred yards that are dead in the grass now and five have said that he did not know that and that nobody has told him and about four have said that nobody in nine counties is going to tell him before somebody has to.

The nine stones at the end of that line are about the height of a man's knee and about as wide as two hands and the tops of them are all at the same height, and nobody in nine counties has found out why, and nine households keep beasts on the flat past them and the rota of that flat has been kept about nine years and no third household came up between the eighth of Rainmonth and the seventeenth and nobody asked it for a reason.

Four feet of ash with two stones on the end of it has been across that lane gate since Mudmonth, and it is the bar of a man of forty-one and nobody in nine counties has asked him to put it there or to take it out, and the drove has gone up past it through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of wall every Thursday since the second of Mudmonth and not one beast has gone through that gate in eleven years, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a badge-man has just asked in a market street four hundred miles off who is going to hold nine hundred yards of wall and that the only person in nine counties holding anything at that head of road is a man of forty-one who bars a gate on a Thursday morning and five have said that he is holding nine hundred yards of wall and that nobody asked him to.

The far bank is four days south of that market town and about four hundred people live across nine hundred yards of water at the top of a tide and about four hundred at the bottom, and a hand lamp goes on the coping at the low side of that causeway every Monday afternoon in the winter and the fifteenth of Frostmonth was a Thursday and the lamp was not put there, and nobody in that market street has ever asked a person at a gate at the near end of that water anything at all.

Then four days off and about nine hundred people along that made road did the ordinary part of a Thursday.

Nobody said anything and nine beasts went out through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of stone wall at about the sixth hour and went up nine hundred yards of that lane and stood in the field with the stone wall round it and came back down at about the fifth hour, and nobody went with them, and that is thirty-seven drove to go up that lane since the second of Mudmonth and nobody has ever written any of them down.

Eight of the nine yards of wall at the head of that made road are back in and the gap is about a yard long and about four feet high, and the stone is quarried two miles off on the low side and one man has been carrying it two miles in his own hands for two hundred and fifty-three days, and the last yard is nine feet of a course and he has not started it, and nobody in nine counties has asked him when he will and nobody is going to.

The brood in a walled yard half a mile below that wall is five frames where there were nine on the night of the eighth of Thawmonth, and that night is two hundred and seventy-seven days back, and the fifth frame has been all brood since the twenty-seventh of Wolfmonth and no sixth has gone in, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a brood frame comes back in about four weeks in a year like this one and that five has now stood for about eight weeks and five have said that a figure that stands still is a figure and about four have said that nobody in nine counties has ever asked the young man who keeps them what he is going to do about it.

Nobody thanked him and the weigh-house weighed two loads of lime and a load of wool that morning and about nine hundred feet of new kerb went past its floor, and the man who keeps it said out loud, to four people in and five out, that Frostmonth is fifteen days old and about a hundred and fifty sheets have come past that floor in it, and that about four hundred and nine parish rolls come past his floor in a good month, and that nobody has ever asked him what a sheet weighs.

Nobody said anything and the inn at the high end was full that morning from about the fourth hour, four beds and four more on the floor above them, and no price went out on any board in that street, and the woman of about fifty-one who keeps that house came out into it at about the fifth hour and stopped nine feet short of those boards and would not come nearer, and a chandler nine doors down put a cloth over a bench rather than write anything at all on it.

Nobody said anything and the near wheel of a cart came off at the low end and the carter got it up again with a bar in about nine minutes and nobody stopped walking, and two women at the third door argued about a bolt of cloth for about four minutes and then went into the shop and came out with it wrapped, and a man with a barrow of lime went the length of that street nine times and unloaded at four doors and loaded at two.

The room over that weigh-house was open because it is a Thursday, and the copyist of about twenty-six came down the thirteen stairs at the seventh hour with a printed sheet under her arm and went back up with it, and four people in that lane saw her come down and five did not, and the register in that room holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none and the day-book stands at eleven entries, and the fourth ruled head is empty and no fifth head is ruled.

Nobody thanked anybody and that scar is nine feet wide and about nine hundred yards long and it is twenty days old, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a line in grass that is not cut closes over in about three years and that this one is dead inside its own width and will not close at all and five have said that nobody in nine counties is going to be told which of those two is true and about four have said that it does not matter which is true because nobody can do anything about either.

Then the part of that Thursday that was said out loud, and two things in it were, and the second of them was a question and nobody answered it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the copyist of about twenty-six came down those thirteen stairs again at about the ninth hour with the printed sheet in her hand and stood at the high end of those boards and read out of it in the open, to about four hundred people, and it was 21 words, and nobody stopped her.

“**Under the Marshal's hand, Edric Hunn, at the office four hundred miles off, and this county is asked to answer it.**”

Nobody said anything and two hundred people in that market street had heard that name read out at those boards for the second time in four weeks and nine of them had heard it twice and five had not, and nobody stopped her and nobody thanked her, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a county can be asked something by a piece of paper four hundred miles off and cannot answer it and that the asking took about eleven words and five have said that the answer would take about as much and that nobody in nine counties has anybody to write it.

Nobody said anything and the whole of what that printed sheet asks for is two things, and the two are the name of the person who holds a certain wall on a certain made road, and a copy of any drawing anybody in that county has made of what is above it, and nobody in nine counties has answered it and nobody in nine counties is going to, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside about nine seconds that this county has been unable to answer anything since the first of Goatmonth and five had worked out that the first of Goatmonth is when the thing that answers could not be called any more and about four have said that a county that cannot call a hearing cannot answer a letter and that this county has not been able to call one since the first of Goatmonth and five have said that it has been unable to answer anything since the same day.

Nobody thanked him and then the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head came out of that lane off the weigh-house at a slow walk with the bar against his leg and its strap over his shoulder, and nobody in that street hurried him and nobody hurried anything else, and he stopped four feet short of those boards and laid his hand flat on the top rail, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him, and he said it out loud, in the open, to about four hundred people, in his own trade, and it was 73 words, and it was the second question he has asked anybody in this county in about four and a half years.

“**There is a man of forty-one at the head of that road with nine hundred yards of wall and about eleven years of a gate in it and he has never been asked what it is for and he has never said. There is nine hundred yards of dead grass nine hundred paces above his bar and about nine people can point at it on a board. Who is going to hold it.**”

Nobody said anything and about four hundred people in that market street had heard a badge-man ask for the second time in about four and a half years about holding something and neither of the two has been about a road, and about nine of them stopped what they were doing and five did not, and nobody stopped it, and nobody answered it, and four people in nine counties have said since that a question asked in a market street of about four hundred people on a Thursday morning about who is going to hold nine hundred yards of wall is a question this county cannot answer and five have said that it is a worse question than the one about where a road goes and about four have said that the road was a thing nobody could see and the wall is a thing four hundred people have stood in front of.

The man of about thirty-four with a satchel was at the high end of those boards on that Thursday and said nothing in that street for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and he has said nine things out loud in this county in about four and a half years and said the ninth of them on the thirteenth of Rainmonth and has refused since that day to say anything twice, and nobody asked him and nobody sent for him, and four people in that market street had worked out that a hundred and fifty-two days is longer than the nine days it took the last finding to get nine hundred yards into four hundred mouths and five had not worked that out and will not.

The two refusals in this county were given on the fourteenth and the twenty-sixth of Fallowmonth and neither has been argued with, and that is ninety-one days and seventy-nine days, and nobody has asked either of those two men anything since and nobody is going to, and about four people in nine counties have said since that a badge-man asking a second question about holding something is not a third refusal and five have said that it is not a refusal of anybody and that nobody asked him anything.

Nobody thanked him and a man of about fifty-two came into that market street at about the fourth hour with about nine feet of a new kerb standing against a shop wall and he did not put the back of his board up and nobody asked him to, and about four people in that market street had worked out that the first drawing of anything anybody in this county ever made has been on the back of a board in a cart shed for about a month and five had not, and four have said since that a man who drew nine hundred yards and then took it out of the rain and did not draw it again has done the whole of what this county has managed to do with a finding and five have said that what a county has managed to do with a finding is put it out of the rain.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of about fifty-eight with a badge and no head stood at those boards for about as long as it takes to turn a cart twice over after he had said it and nobody said anything and nobody thanked him and he did not say it again and he went back up the four hundred pace lane off the weigh-house on his own, and about four people in that market street had worked out that a badge-man who asks a question and gets nothing does not ask it twice and five had worked out nothing.

Nobody said anything and then a question came into that market street at about the tenth hour from a person at the third door who wanted to know one thing, and nobody stopped it, and it was asked kindly, and it was not about a road.

“**Is there an answer to that?**”

Nobody said anything and the man of thirty-two with a stick answered it out loud, in the open, in his own trade, and it was 49 words, and nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“**No and I am not going to give one, and four people in this street have worked out that the badge-man has asked two questions in four and a half years and that both of them have been about holding something and neither of them was about a road.**”

Nobody said anything and nobody said anything back to him and nobody thanked him, and about four people in that market street had worked out inside about nine seconds that a man who has been refused twice in the open in this county and who answered a badge-man no on the nineteenth of Embermonth has now told four hundred people that there is no answer to the second question the badge-man has ever asked and five had not, and nobody asked him about the first one and nobody asked him about either again.

**And the cost of that Thursday is one printed sheet four hundred miles off asking a question this county cannot answer, and a second question in the same street with nobody in it, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
