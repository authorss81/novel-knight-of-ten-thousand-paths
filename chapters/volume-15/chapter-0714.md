# Chapter 0714

**Longlight the seventh, YR 322, a Friday and not a market morning with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the hearing not sitting and no market in that street, two days out on a drove road and up over the shoulder of a hill, six people on a shoulder with no head and no paper.**

Two days out is the whole of what there is between that market town and that shoulder, and there is no third way, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the only road in nine counties that a man can walk in a fortnight and arrive at the end of it with a load he started with.

Nobody thanked anybody and the five went out at about the sixth hour on the fifth of Longlight and arrived at about the fourth hour on the seventh, and the man of thirty-two went out with them because he walks that made road every morning and has never once gone up the lane at its head, and four people in nine counties have said since that he is the sixth and five have said that he is not one of the five, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked him and the first day of those two is the ordinary day of a made road and there are eleven houses on it and about nine hundred people living along it, and the two miles of wall and orchard between the eleventh mile stone and the top of the hill are a stone wall on the high side for about a mile and a mile of orchard on the low side, and the orchard is about nine hundred trees and the owners are about nine households and not one of them has ever asked anybody anything about the six people walking past its gate.

Nobody thanked them and the causeway is about nine hundred paces long and about nine feet wide at the crown with a low parapet on the seaward side that a cart cannot get over and that a man can lean on with his whole weight, and the hand lamp on the coping at the low side of it is put there every Monday afternoon in the winter and was not put there on a Friday, and the hand bell on the gate post above it is about the size of a fist and is rung when the water is over the causeway, and the water was four hundred yards wide and about an hour and a half off it at the hour they were on it.

Nobody thanked him and they went through the gap at the low end of about nine hundred yards of stone wall, and the gap is four feet high at its lowest point and about nine feet wide and is the width of a drove, and about nine yards of that wall is down where it came down on the twenty-eighth of Thawmonth and it is not filled.

Nobody thanked anybody and a man of about forty-one stood at the head of the gap and did not help and did not watch them go and went back in, and nobody said anything to him and he said nothing, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the fourth time in about four and a half years that a man has stood at the head of that gap while somebody went through it and that three of those four were a drove.

Nobody said anything and the frost was out of the low side of that made road on the fifth of Longlight and about four days after the water came out of the lane, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a road in Longlight carries about three quarters of what it carries in Frostmonth and five have said that the difference between three quarters and a quarter is nine days of mud and nothing else.

Nobody thanked him and the second of the two days is the hard part at the far end and about four hours of it, and the frost came out of the ruts on the low side of that road at about the fourth hour and by the ninth the four inches of standing water that was in them on the twenty-eighth of Mudmonth was gone, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that the difference between a made road in Mudmonth and a made road in Longlight is that in Longlight you can see where the ruts were.

Nobody thanked him and the lane is about nine feet wide at the head and it is not a made road and it never has been, and there is about two miles of it and it goes up over the shoulder of the hill at about a rise in nine, and the first nine hundred yards of it is what the beasts use and it is the surface of a field that has had stock on it since before anybody in this county can account for.

Nobody thanked anybody and the field with the stone wall round it is at the end of those nine hundred yards and it is level and the wall round it is the same height as the wall at the bottom, and it was empty on the seventh of Longlight, because the drove goes out on Thursdays and came back on the fourth, and nine people in nine counties know that and five have ever looked.

Nobody said anything and then the lane turns, and it turns twice before the shoulder, and the first turn is about four hundred yards past the field and is a turn to the left round about nine yards of bank, and the second turn is about six hundred yards past that and is a turn to the right round a stone of about the size of a cartwheel that is in the middle of the lane because it has always been in the middle of the lane.

Nobody thanked him and four people in nine counties have said since that a lane that goes over a shoulder and turns twice is a lane that was made for something that was not going in a straight line, and five have said that stock does not care and will take a turn without a rut and that these two sentences have been true for about eleven years and that the six people walking up it on the seventh of Longlight are the first anybody in nine counties has ever counted.

Nobody thanked anybody and the broom is on the east rise above the shoulder and it is not on, and it comes on about the fourth of Rainmonth every year, and nine people in nine counties know that it is the only broom within a day's walk and five have ever cut it, and four people in nine counties have said since that a young man of about nineteen went up here three weeks early for it in Thawmonth and got nothing and said he would not come up again until the fourth of Rainmonth.

Nobody thanked anybody and the six of them were a long way apart from each other on that shoulder within the first half hour, because about nine people in nine counties have said since that there is no use in six people standing together on a flat bit of grass and about five have said that this is the only thing about a party that is not an army.

Nobody said anything and the man of about thirty-four with a satchel asked out loud, in the open, at about the fifth hour, how many people in nine counties had ever stood at the far end of that line, and about four people on that shoulder looked at him and about five did not, and nobody answered it and he did not ask it twice.

“**How many people in nine counties have ever stood at that end of it?**”

Nobody said anything and about four people on that shoulder had worked out the answer inside about nine seconds and five had not, and the answer was one, and the one was the man who asked it, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first question anybody in nine counties has ever asked about a road that is not about a road, and about five have said that it is the first question anybody has asked anywhere in this county for about four and a half years that nobody could answer.

Then the shoulder, which is the top of the four miles of made road and about nine hundred yards wide.

Nobody thanked anybody and the shoulder is grass and it is not level and there is a rise in it going east, and about nine hundred yards wide at the top, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a shoulder is not a hilltop in this county but a place where a hill has a flat bit on it because somebody needed a flat bit.

Nobody thanked him and the man of about fifty-two who keeps four miles of made road under contract for about nineteen years stood in the middle of it and said one thing out loud, in the open, to about five people, in his own trade, and it was forty-two words.

“**Four miles of made road come off that shoulder and this is the top of it, and I have laid stone on the low side of a hill for about nineteen years and I have never once laid stone on the flat.**”

Nobody said anything and four people on that shoulder had heard a roadkeeper say that he had come to the flat end of his own road and five had heard forty-two words, and nobody said anything back and nobody thanked him, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first thing anybody in nine counties has said at the top of that shoulder and that it was said in a trade and not about the stones.

Nobody thanked anybody and on that flat bit there was about nine hundred yards of road, and it was kerbed on both sides, and it was about nine feet wide, and it went across the shoulder in a line from the low side to the high side, and it was the same stone as the wall at the bottom of the lane, and four people on that shoulder had looked at the kerb for about as long as it takes to turn a cart and about five had not looked at it at all.

Nobody said anything and four people in nine counties have said since that a road across the top of a hill that goes from one side of it to the other is not a road anybody built to go anywhere, and five have said that a road that goes between two places is a road and that both of the places are under the grass, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked him and then the kerb stopped, and where it stopped the road did not narrow and did not break up, it went on as a line about nine feet wide in the grass, and there is about nine hundred yards of that line to where the grass comes over it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a person can stand at the far end of the line and look back at nine hundred yards of kerb and see every yard of it.

Nobody thanked anybody and six people walked the whole of that line on the seventh of Longlight and nobody went past the end of it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that six people walked nine hundred yards of a line in the grass that one person had described in about nine minutes in a market street and had got no broom for, and four people have said that this is the first time anybody has checked anything anybody in this county has ever said out loud.

And then the stones themselves, which stand about nine hundred yards past the place where the kerbs stop, across the far end of the line in the grass.

Nobody thanked anyone and they are set across the line and there are nine of them and they are about the height of a man's knee and about as wide as two hands and they are set at the same height along the top of them, and nine people in nine counties have said since that nine stones set at the same height along the top of them is not a field boundary and is not a gate and is not a mark anybody in nine counties makes.

Nobody said anything and about four of the six people on that shoulder had put a hand flat on the top of the first of the nine and about five had not, and the stones are the same stone as the kerb, and one of the six put his hand on the first of them and it was not loose, and nine people in nine counties have said since that four of those hands were the hands of people who put things up and one of them was the hand of a man who has never laid a stone in his life.

Nobody said anything and then the man of about thirty-four with a satchel asked the man of thirty-two a question out loud, in the open, on that shoulder, in his own trade, and nobody stopped him.

“**Then you are the sixth, and you are the only one of the six who has no trade to put at risk, and I keep a gate and a gate is a thing that can be put at risk, so I would like to know what your trade is.**”

Nobody said anything and he said it out loud, in the open, to one person, on that shoulder, and it was twenty-seven words, and he did not improve it.

“**I walk that road. It is four days and I have walked it every morning since the first of Longlight and there is nothing else in it.**”

Nobody said anything and four people on that shoulder had heard a man answer a question about himself in the open on a hilltop and five had heard twenty-seven words, and nine people in nine counties have said since that a man who walks four days of made road every morning has a trade and that it is the only trade in nine counties that nobody can be put at a gate with.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody went past the ninth of the nine stones, and nobody crossed anything, and nobody in nine counties has ever seen anybody go past them, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that six people have now stood at the far end of nine hundred yards of a line in the grass and not one of them has put a foot past a stone, and that this is the first time in about four and a half years that anybody in this county has stood at the end of a thing and not gone over it.

Nobody thanked anybody and then they stayed on that shoulder, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time anybody has stayed on it and that nobody has ever stayed on it, and that there is no wall on it and no tree on it and nothing to get in behind, and four people in nine counties have said that this is the reason nobody has ever stayed on it and five have said that it is because there is no reason.

Nobody thanked them and the man of about fifty-two who keeps four miles of made road under contract walked the whole of the nine hundred yards of kerb twice and neither time said anything about it, and the man of about thirty-eight who puts boards on the inside of gates went to the fourth of the nine stones and stood there about as long as it takes to turn a cart and came back, and the man of about twenty-seven who keeps eels in weirs about two days north of the causeway went to the high side and looked east and stayed there, and the man of about thirty-four with a satchel wrote nothing down because he had nothing to write it on.

Nobody thanked her and the woman of about thirty-nine who keeps the second gate from the top of that made road sat down on the ninth of the nine stones with her back to the east rise and looked back down the line at nine hundred yards of kerb, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first thing anybody has done on those stones that was not touching them.

Nobody said anything and past the nine stones the ground goes on flat and low and there is a fence line and there are beasts on the other side of it, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first description of anything on the other side of those stones that anybody in nine counties has ever had and that it is twenty-three words long.

Nobody thanked anybody and the light came off the low side of that shoulder at about the sixth hour and the wind came across about nine hundred yards of it from the low side and there was nothing on the shoulder to stop it, and four people in nine counties have said since that the wind on that shoulder is the reason the drove goes up nine hundred yards and stops at the wall and does not go over it.

Nobody said anything and the six of them were on that shoulder with no head among them and no paper, and about nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that six people in this county have been together anywhere for a reason that is written out in the mouths of six of them and in nothing else.

Nobody thanked anybody and nothing about any of it is in a document, and there is no page in any room in nine counties with six trades and nine stones on it, and the register in the room over that weigh-house holds ten questions with no finding under any of them and gains none, and the day-book stands at eleven entries.

Nobody said anything and the broom will be on about the fourth of Rainmonth, which is about twenty-seven days off, and six people are on a shoulder with about six weeks of weather in front of them and about nine hundred yards of wall at the bottom of it, and nine people in nine counties have said since that no person in nine counties has ever gone up a hill for one thing and come down with another.

Nobody thanked anybody and the man of thirty-two put his stick down against the ninth of the nine stones and stood on that shoulder until about the fourth hour and looked back at about nine hundred yards of kerb going away from him toward the low side, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the only time in about four and a half years that a man has stood at that end of that line and looked back down it, and five have said that a man who looks back down a road is a man thinking about being up it again.

**And the cost of those two days is two pairs of feet and six trades and about nine hundred yards of kerb that four people looked at and five did not, are not in it and none of them has a price.**
