# Chapter 0703

**Thawmonth the first, YR 322, a Sunday and not a market morning and the first of a month and the first day of a new year, with the room over the weigh-house shut behind its own door and the bolt still on from the Saturday, and about four people in a market street, and nothing at the top of it.**

The first of a month in this county comes whether or not anybody is in the street for it, and the first of Thawmonth came on a Sunday, and there is no market on a Sunday, and this is the second of the twelves since the first of Goatmonth on which nothing at all has happened at it.

Nobody thanked anybody and about ten firsts of a month come in the ten months from here, and about two of them have a market on them, and the other eight come into a street with nobody in it, and that is the shape of this county's calendar and it is not anybody's doing and nobody has ever drawn it.

Nobody thanked anybody and about four people came to those boards at about the ninth hour on the first day of the year and about five did not, and the four were not the same four who came on the first of Goatmonth, and the badge-man was not there.

Nobody thanked him and nobody sent for him and nobody sent anybody to him, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first first of a month in about four and a half years on which the man who built the thing was not at it, and about four of them have said that a man who says nothing for two hours is a man doing a thing and a man who is not there is a man who has stopped, and five have said that he said on the first of Goatmonth that nothing happens on the first of the next month and that a man who says a thing like that has to be the first one who keeps it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the four who came were four trades and they had not come together, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the whole of what a first of a month is in this county now, which is four people from four trades who come at the ninth hour on a day with no market on it, and that about four of them have never once spoken to the other three.

Nobody thanked anybody and the four were a woman of about thirty-nine who keeps the second gate from the top of the made road and who had come in on foot, a man of about fifty-one who keeps four beds at the top of that street and who came out of his own door and stood about nine feet off and did not come closer, a man of about thirty-four with a satchel who came from the road and stood at the high end, and a person of about nineteen with nothing at all who stood at about the ninth pace from the low end and did not move for the whole hour.

Nobody said anything and nine people in nine counties have said since that the four have never asked each other what they are doing there, and that on the first of Hearthmonth there were about nine and on the first of Goatmonth there were about nine and different nine, and that the number has been four or nine or five all year and that nobody in nine counties has ever agreed which is the real number and that this is now the fourth year of not agreeing.

Nobody said anything and nobody said anything at those boards for about nine minutes, and about four people in that market street have said since that nine minutes is not long and that it is the longest anybody in nine counties has stood in an empty street on a day with nothing on it in about four and a half years, and five have said that the first of Goatmonth had two hours and that nobody has stood for two hours since.

Nobody thanked anybody and the boards were standing in that market street with nothing on them, and nobody took them down, and they had been repainted twice since the middle of Longlight and are the colour of a wet slate, and the rail along the top of them has frost in it at about the seventh hour on a morning when the ground has been frozen since the middle of the night.

Nobody thanked him and nine people in nine counties have said since that the boards are the only thing in this county that nobody has ever agreed about and nobody has ever taken away, and about four have said that this is because a set of boards cannot be instructed and five have said that it is because nobody who could take them down would be able to say what the taking was for.

Then the fifth person came, at about the tenth hour, and it was the man of thirty-two with his stick in his right hand, and he came fifth and stood at the low end of them.

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had worked out within about nine seconds that the man who has gone out on that made road every morning since the first of Longlight had come fifth to a thing that does not happen, and five had not noticed him arrive at all.

Nobody thanked him and he had a question, and he did not put it anywhere, and the reason he did not put it anywhere is worth saying plainly because nine people in nine counties have said since that it is the whole of what happened on the first day of the year.

Nobody thanked him and there was no register to put it in and no bench to say it at and no man with a badge to say it to and no copyist to write it down.

Nobody said anything and the room over the weigh-house has a register in it that holds ten questions and no finding under any of them, and a fourth ruled head over a place where a finding goes that has been empty for about twelve months, and nobody has ruled a fifth head, and the woman who keeps that room has said out loud in three months and in two rooms that she is not going to rule one and that neither is anybody else. The day-book in that room stands at eleven entries. The three series stand and are not derived from one another. None of that was touched on the first of a year and none of it is touched on the first of any month and four people in nine counties have said since that a thing which is true on twelve days a year and is not moved on any of them is the only kind of thing this county has.

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a man decide not to ask a question and five had heard a man arrive.

Nobody thanked him and he said one thing out loud in the open, and it was eighteen words, and four people heard all eighteen and about five heard four of them, and it was the only thing anybody said at those boards on the first day of the year.

“**I have not got anybody to ask this and I am not going to go and find one.**”

Nobody said anything and four people in that market street had heard a man of thirty-two admit to a thing in the open on a Sunday, and five had heard a man be careful, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that the man who goes has said out loud in a market street that there is nobody to ask, and that before the first of Goatmonth there was always somebody to ask, and that what stopped was not the asking and was the answer.

Then the ordinary half of a Sunday in Thawmonth happened, and it is nine tenths of every day anybody in nine counties has ever had, and it happened in about nine hundred paces of street and in about eleven lanes off it.

Nobody thanked anybody and the smithy at the fourth door was banked and shut and had been since the middle of Frostmonth, and the yard behind the inn had about nine loads of lime stacked against the north wall under a tarpaulin that had begun to go at one corner in the middle of Longlight and had not been mended, and about four people in that market town have said that the tarpaulin has been going at that corner for about eleven weeks and five have said that a man who has a tarpaulin going at a corner for eleven weeks is a man who cannot buy a new one.

Nobody thanked anybody and about two hundred people in that market town went to their own front doors at about the tenth hour because a Sunday in Thawmonth is when a market town goes to its own front doors, and about nine hundred people along a made road four days off did the ordinary part of the first day of a year, and the ordinary part is that nothing about the first day of a year is visible on a road.

Nobody thanked anyone and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room was not in that street and was not in her lane, and nobody sent for her and nobody sent anybody to her, and the copyist of about twenty-six was not there either.

Nobody said anything and four people in that market town had noticed the bolt still on the door from the Saturday and five had noticed nothing at all, and it was the second night running the bolt was on, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the room was shut on the Saturday because a Saturday shuts at noon and this is the second night and nobody has opened it since, and that a room which stays shut when nobody is asking it to shut has stopped being a thing that is shut and become a thing that is closed.

Nobody thanked him and the lane that weigh-house is on is four hundred paces from a door in a lane four streets up the market street, and nine people in nine counties have said since that four hundred paces is about as far as anything in this county is from anything else, and that somebody worked that out on purpose a very long time ago.

Nobody said anything and about four people in that market street have said since that the man of thirty-two has gone past that lane about nine hundred times since the first of Longlight and that on the first day of a year, with the street empty and nothing to stand at, he walked down it about nine paces and turned round at the thirteenth pace, and five have said that he walked the whole of it to the door and did not knock and did not go in, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked him and nobody knocked on that door on the first day of a year and nobody asked after the woman who keeps that room, and four people in nine counties have said since that this is the first first of a month in about four and a half years on which nobody in nine counties has said her name in the open, and five have said that she is in a room over a weigh-house and that a room over a weigh-house is shut on a Sunday.

And this is the whole of that lane, because he did not knock and everybody in nine counties has talked about it since and nobody has it right.

It is about nine hundred paces from the market street to the door and the lane is straight for about four hundred of them and then it bends round the back of a yard where a man keeps goats, and four people in nine counties have said since that the bend is the only thing in that lane that is not a straight run and that a lane that bent at about four hundred paces was bent on purpose or was bent by a pond that is not there now.

Nobody thanked him and he went down it at about the eleventh hour on a Sunday morning in the first month of a year, and he went down it because the street was empty and there was nothing else to do and not because he had decided to, and four people in nine counties have said that this is the honest account and five have said that a man does not walk four hundred paces down a lane on a Sunday morning for no reason at all.

Nobody thanked him and at about the thirteenth pace from the market street he stopped, and he stopped because the gutter on the low side of that lane had about four inches of standing water in it in it, frozen, with about nine days of grit in the ice, and he stood and looked at it for about as long as it takes to turn a cart.

Nobody said anything and nobody has ever established what he was looking at, and four people in nine counties have said since that he was looking at the water because water is what he looks at, and five have said that he was looking at about nine days of grit in ice in a gutter in a lane and that a man who has been the one who goes for four and a half years will stop on a Sunday for nine days of grit, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked him and then he went on to the door, which is a plank door with a latch and a hasp and nothing else, and the hasp is on the inside, and the paint on it is about eleven years old and has gone the colour of a wet slate in the same way as those boards, and nine people in nine counties have said since that whoever painted that door used the same pot as the man who painted the boards and that this is the only known connection between that room and that street.

Nobody thanked him and he did not knock and he did not put his hand on it, and he stood on the step for about as long as it takes to turn a cart, and the step is stone and is about nine inches high and has a wear in the middle of it that is about the width of a foot, and four people in nine counties have said that a wear in the middle of a doorstep is made by a person going in and coming out and not by a person standing on it, and five have said that a wear in the middle of a doorstep is made by a person standing on it for a long time, and that those two things are nine years apart at least.

Nobody said anything and he went back up the lane at about the eleventh hour and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the fourth and a half year in a row that a man has stood on that step in daylight on his own account, and that on three of those occasions it was raining.

Nobody thanked him and nothing in that lane is in any document, and there is no page in any room in nine counties with anything about a step in it, and four and a half years of a lane four hundred paces long has never once been written down by anybody, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the single most careful piece of record-keeping in this county and that it is being done by a man who is not a keeper of records and would not know what to do with a page.

Then a person in that market street asked the man of thirty-two a question out loud, and it was asked kindly and about nine people heard it, and it was the fourth question anybody had asked anybody at those boards in about nine minutes.

“**Then what do you do about it?**”

Nobody said anything and he said fourteen words back to it, and four people in that street heard all fourteen and five heard a man answer a question in the open.

“**I go up the lane on Thursday and I do not ask him twice.**”

Nobody said anything and nine people in that market street had heard a man of thirty-two name a gate and a day and a number in one sentence and had not worked out that he had said out loud the whole of his own plan, and four of them had worked out that he had and five had worked out that a man who says when and where and how many times before he has done a thing is a man who is going to do it, and that neither of those two sets has ever spoken to the other.

Nobody thanked him and nobody argued with him and nobody asked him a second question, and nine people in nine counties have said since that this is the first time in about four and a half years that a man in that street has said a thing about a lane and not been asked for anything else, and that about four of them believe he will go and about five believe the lane is two miles of unmade road above a hill in Thawmonth and that they have not decided between those two.

Nobody thanked anybody and about nine people in that market street kept standing there until about the eleventh hour and about five went home at the tenth, and the four trades went their four ways and nobody said goodbye to anybody, and the man of thirty-two was the last of them and stood there until the light had gone off the wet slate of those boards.

Nobody thanked anybody and the lamp at the high end of the boards was lit at the fourth hour by a man who does that on a Sunday as he does on any other day, and nine people in that market town have said since that the lamp is the only thing in nine counties that has happened on every single day for about four and a half years without anybody having decided that it should.

Nobody thanked anybody and the room over that weigh-house was still bolted at about the seventh hour and the door was shut and the boards stood in an empty market street with nothing on them, and nine people in nine counties have said since that the first of Thawmonth YR 322 is the first day of the year in which nothing about a road in this county was decided, and about four of them have said that the last four and a half years were full of days on which nothing was decided, and five have said that a day which decides nothing in the middle of a year is a day that costs, and a day which decides nothing on the first day of a year is a day that is going to cost.

**And the cost of the first day of the year is a question with nowhere to go and eighteen words said out loud in an empty street and nobody sent for anybody, are not in it and none of them has a price.**

**a nail at 11, a tallow dip at 12, a sheet of parchment at 13, a hurdle at 15, a tarred line at 16, a hoop at 18, a strap of hide at 21, a skep at 23, a wall peg at 27, and a bolster at 28.** Eleven, twelve, thirteen, fifteen, sixteen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-three, twenty-seven and twenty-eight is one hundred and eighty-four pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and eighty-four pence is three marks and forty over.** About nine people in that market street stood at a set of boards with nothing on them on the first day of a new year and about five did not come, and nobody has written down that any of them was there, and four people in nine counties have said since that the register in that room would take it in about four seconds if anybody asked her to and five have said that nobody is going to ask her.
