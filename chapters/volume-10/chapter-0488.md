# Chapter 0488

**The twenty-ninth day of Greenmonth, YR 319, a Tuesday and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the seventh hour, on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, four hundred yards up the sand from the landward end, at the back of the eleventh of eleven houses and then at the ninth of eleven, and there is no month in anybody's mouth on that shore and no day of the week, and a widow of about seventy lives in the eleventh house at twenty pence a day and a man with nine hundred acres inland holds a lease on the ninth, and both of them have had about two hundred days of the water off long enough and the whole of what it cost the two of them is not in the same kind of thing.**

## One

**A season on that sand is the days the water is off long enough; a wagon year is about two hundred days, and the man of about fifty-three with a wagon is the only man on that shore who counts them out loud, and nobody else has ever asked him what a season is, and he has said it once, and what he said was that a season is about two hundred days and a man can make about two hundred crossings in a year and does.**

The widow at the eleventh house does not have a season. She has days, and she has twenty pence a day, and she has had them for longer than anybody on that sand has been asking her anything.

“**I have not got a wagon,**” she said, to a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow who had come four hundred yards up the sand to ask her a question she had not been asked in about nine years. “**I have got a boat and a stove and twenty pence a day and I have got the days, and I could not tell you how many days there are in a year and neither could he,**” and she nodded at the wagon on the sand a hundred yards below, “**and he could not tell you what a season is either, he has just got a number for it and he thinks I do not know that.**”

**And that is seventy words in two turns of one exchange, of which the first is six and the second is sixty-four printed in two spans of thirty-seven and twenty-seven, and the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow did not thank her and the widow did not want to be thanked, and four people on that sand heard the whole of it and nine people heard the first six words.**

## Two

**And the cost of a season to that household, in money, is about two hundred days at twenty pence a day, and two hundred days at twenty pence a day is four thousand pence, and that is the figure and it is not in any column anybody keeps and it is not the number anybody has been carrying about for two months, which is a different number about a different length of time, and the two are not added and have never been on the same line.**

Four thousand pence is what it costs the eleventh house to be there for a season.

**And that is not the same as what a permanent connection would take out of that household, and the two must not be confused, and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow was the one who said so out loud at about the fourth hour of that Wednesday and then said she was not going to say any more about it, because she had said it once, and nobody had thanked her for the once, and she was not going to say it a second time in order to be thanked for the first.**

## Three

**And the cost of a season to the household at the ninth house is not money and cannot be put in a column, and the man who holds the lease there has never worked it out and has said that he is not going to.**

He has about nine hundred acres inland and a lease on a house on a road that has no line on it, and for nine weeks he did not buy, and the ninth house stood still, and now it buys again at nine pence a sack as it has for eleven years.

“**You have not lost nine hundred pence,**” the man of about fifty-three with a wagon said to him at about the seventh hour, and the man with nine hundred acres said that he had worked that out and had not been told it and had not asked.

“**Then here is the other half and I am not going to say it twice,**” the man with nine hundred acres said, before the man with the wagon could say anything. “**Nine weeks of not buying cost me two carts up the made road and four days of a boy's legs each way and a question I asked a stranger on a sand, and the question was whether I would buy off a wagon that comes twice a day, and I said I would, and I said it in front of nine people, and I had said it every week for nineteen years and I had never once had to say it out loud before. That is what a season cost this house. It is not pence. I cannot give it to anybody in pence and if anybody puts it in a column I will take the column out.**”

The man of about fifty-three with a wagon said one word on that tide and it was printed.

“**Right,**” he said.

**And that is one word in one turn of one speech, and a hundred and forty words were said in the three turns of the exchange before it, of which the first is seven, the second is fifteen, and the third is a hundred and eighteen printed in one span, and the man with nine hundred acres inland has not said the rest of it to anybody and has not been thanked for it and did not want to be.**

## Four

**And the two figures sit on the same stretch of sand and they are not in the same column and they are not added, and one of them is money and one of them is not, and there is no rate in nine counties that converts a boy's two legs up a made road and four days back into pence on that foreshore, and a rate that nobody can check is a rate that works, and this is not one of them.**

A carter of about fifty stood forty yards off and heard the last hundred and eighteen words of the third turn and nine people heard the whole of it and four of the nine were going to carry it and one of those four is the man of about fifty-three with a wagon.

**Aren Kest was four hundred yards down the sand at the seventh hour and heard the whole of the first of those two turns and none of the second, and he wrote the two figures on the back of a handcart docket afterwards at somebody else's fire, which is not a keeper of anything, and he did not say either figure out loud to anybody that day.**

**The woman of about thirty-four with a barrow wrote nothing down that day. She has four columns in a fold in a coat and has not joined any two of them up, and a widow of about seventy at the eleventh house has never kept one and is not going to start, and a man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses has heard about half of it and has told nobody and has said that he is not interested, which is the first thing he has said about anybody in about a month.**

## Five

The cost of that Wednesday is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow's, and it is the fourth one she has kept, and two hundred days and twenty pence a day and four thousand pence are not in it, and two carts and four days of a boy's legs are not in it, and a lease and about nine hundred acres are not in it.

**a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a fourth loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, a tallow candle at two, a second tin of oil at two, a second room at six, a bed at four, a handcart for the day at four, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a second broom at five, a pound of rope at six, a bucket of nails at three, a pair of mitts at two, a bucket of tar at eight, a second waxed cord at four, a second inkwell at three, a second pound of candles at four, a second basket at five, a second brush at two, a second knife at two, a second night's lodging at the fourth house at two, a second handcart for the day at four and a second pail of nails at three.** One, one, one, one, four, three, three, two, two, six, four, four, one, one, three, five, six, three, two, eight, four, three, four, five, two, two, two, four, four and three is ninety-four pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and ninety-four pence is a mark and forty-six over**, and a cart-load of shell at six pence in the only book in the trade and nine pence in the mouths of three ports are not in it and are never added in any register including a notebook, and three sacks a trip at nine pence a sack is not in it and is a price the ninth house has paid for eleven years, and a chain and a rod and a wall and a deed are not in it.

**And a widow of about seventy at the eleventh house lives at twenty pence a day and has about two hundred days of it in a season, which is four thousand pence, and a man with nine hundred acres inland at the ninth house has paid two carts and four days of a boy's legs for the same about two hundred days, and neither of the two figures was said to the other and neither of them was put in a column and the second one cannot be, and the wall at a hundred paces from the landward end took the water over it twice that day as it has twice a day since the four days it took to lay, and the tide came up at about the ninth hour and put about two hundred paces of the seaward half under and left the eleventh house and the ninth house and the four of them that were standing in on dry sand above it, and it was off again before four in the morning and the fire behind the fourth house was in both of those.**
