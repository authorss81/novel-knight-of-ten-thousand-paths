# Chapter 0468

**The twenty-seventh day of Goatmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour, at the ninth of eleven houses on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, and there is no month in anybody's mouth on that shore and no day of the week, and a man of about twenty-nine who has asked two women what they want in about four days is standing at a salt wagon with a man of about fifty-three who has driven it down that sand for nineteen years, and neither of them is keeping anything and neither of them has been asked to.**

## One

**Tobin Kernick has driven a salt wagon between the port four days up and that shore for nineteen years, and he goes down on the ebb and stands about two hours and comes back on the flood, and nobody sets him a time and nobody has ever set him one and he could not tell you who to ask.**

He was at the ninth of the eleven, which is the one house on that sand with a lease on it, because the man with nine hundred acres inland has been buying salt off him for eleven years and stands in the doorway while it is tipped.

The man of about twenty-nine had been four days on that sand and had asked two women and had been told that both of them wanted something, and he had four days of walking in him to spend and no fifth place to go.

**The wagon is a broad-wheeled thing that has been off and on that sand so long that the near rims have been cut down twice, and it went down on the ebb and stood at the ninth of the eleven for about two hours, and the tipping took about nine minutes and the man with nine hundred acres stood in the doorway the whole of it and did not offer a hand and the carter did not ask for one, and about nine people watched the nine minutes from about forty yards and about four of them are the kind of person who will carry what was said afterwards.**

## Two

He asked the ordinary question a person asks a man about his work, which is how long he had done it, and got nineteen years, and then asked the other one, which is what he stops at.

And the man of about fifty-three said something that was not an answer to either question and has not been thought about by anybody in nine counties.

“**I stop at four of them,**” he said. “**A pan at the fourth house and a fire behind it that never goes out. A lease at the ninth. A widow with the last of a boat at the eleventh who buys four sacks and will not sell them back to me. And a house at the seventh that a man stands in, and about four of us will not stop at, and I will not tell you why and you did not ask me.**”

## Three

**And then he said the thing that is the sixth answer, and he said it to a man who had come four days to ask a question, and there were about nine people within about nine feet of it.**

“**Nobody stopped at any of them on purpose. I stop at four of them because there is something to stop for, and the other seven are empty because there is a thing about a house with nobody in it that a house with a man in the doorway has not got. And the whole of your road is about nine hundred people and eleven houses and about four hours out of twelve, and it has been like that nineteen years and I would put money on it staying like that until I am dead.**”

“**So do not ask me who keeps it. Nobody keeps it. There is a pan at the fourth house and that is a trade, and there is a man at the ninth and that is a lease, and the seven are the way out of the weather and they are the way out of the weather because nobody is standing in them, and that is the whole of the arrangement and not one person on this shore agreed to it.**”

## Four

**And that is a hundred and seventy-four words in two turns of one speech, and nobody in nine counties had proposed either of them, and the man of about twenty-nine stood at the ninth of eleven houses and understood four things at once and one thing he did not.**

The four were wrong, and he had heard all four of them wrong inside a month. **A man in a fold: a thing is kept by not being moved, and the water moves it twice a day. A man at a bench: a thing is kept by a turn of a year, and there is no year on that sand. A woman in a township: a thing is kept by a name anybody can read out loud, and there is no name on it and there never has been one while anybody on that sand has been alive. And a dale with a hurdle: a thing is kept by nothing at all, and nothing is not a refusal.**

The sixth was not a keeping either. It was an account of where four people's work happened to stop, and about nine hundred people had been getting across that sand for nineteen years because that is where it stopped, and the man of about fifty-three was fifty-three and slept in his wagon and had nowhere between the port and that shore to stable a horse.

And he said, before the man of about twenty-nine could ask him anything else, that if anybody ever came down that sand and asked him to keep anything at all he would say no, because a man who cannot put a horse down is not a keeper of anything and never will be.

**And then he said one more thing, at the fifth hour, with the wagon empty and the horses already in the water, and it was the only thing he said all day that was not a fact about his own work.**

“**There is a man two counties up who has been asking me since about a year ago whether I will take a house on that sand and answer for it, and I have said no to him about nine times and I will say no to him again, and I am telling you that because you are the first person in four days who has asked me a question and then stood there and waited for the answer, and because about nine people on this shore have heard about him and about two hundred have not and the rest of them have never been asked.**”

**And that is a hundred and five words in one turn of one speech, and it is the second time in about a year that man of about fifty-three has said no to a stranger about a house, and it is the first time he has said it twice, and there was nobody within about nine feet of him who had a form and nobody within about nine feet who wanted one.**

## Five

The cost of that Monday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's and it is the eighth and he has eight of them in a fold in the inside of a coat and has still not added them up, because adding them up would be a total against a road and he does not have a road yet.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a third pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, a third pound of wood at three, two tallow candles at two, a quart of small beer at two, a room at six, a bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a pot of cheese at six, a handcart for two days at four, a handcart for the four days back at four, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a pair of boots mended at five, a second pair of boots mended at six.** One, one, one, four, four, four, three, three, three, two, two, six, four, four, six, four, four, one, one, one, three, five and six is seventy-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and seventy-three pence is a mark and twenty-five over**, and nineteen years of a salt wagon is not in it and is not a figure of men, and the four houses and the seven houses are not in it and are not added to eleven in a column and never are.

**And a man of about fifty-three who has driven a salt wagon down two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand for nineteen years said that nobody keeps that road and that about nine hundred people have been getting across it for nineteen years because four people's work happens to stop at four of eleven houses, and about nine people heard him and about four of them are the kind of person who will carry it, and a woman of about fifty-one at the seaward end did not hear him and was not told and does not want to be told, and eleven paces of stone with a second footing course on it stands at a hundred paces from the landward end and takes the tide twice a day exactly as a post does.**
