# Chapter 0485

**The eleventh day of Greenmonth, YR 319, a Friday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the tenth hour, on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and broken shell, at the landward end and about a hundred paces in from it, and a surveyor of about forty-four with a chain has run his chain from the landward face of eleven paces of stone about two feet high to the door of the eleventh of eleven houses and got four hundred yards, and the eleventh house is the one a widow of about seventy lives in with the last of a boat, and the chain came back slack in the middle twice and the man of about forty-four wrote four hundred yards on a page with no head on it anyway.**

## One

**Four hundred yards is a distance and it is the only thing in two months that anybody has measured between the wall and anything, and it took the two of them about four hours on a Friday because the tide was in half of the time and a chain cannot be pulled through water a man is standing in.**

The wall is eleven paces long and about two feet high and it stands at a hundred paces from the landward end, and it has a second footing course nine inches lower, and about four inches of sand is banked against its far side.

The eleventh house is four hundred yards from the landward face of the wall.

**And between those two things there is sand, and shell, and about two hundred paces of it that goes under at about the ninth hour, and there is no paper anywhere in nine counties that joins a wall of stone to a house with a widow in it, and there is no paper anywhere that joins the wall to any of the other ten either, and the man of about forty-four did not go and look for one and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet has never said that one exists.**

## Two

**And what the wall is not is the thing four people on that sand said out loud between the sixth hour and the tenth hour, and what it is turns out to be nothing anybody has been able to name against it in two months.**

A wall of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end of a road that nobody owns is not illegal. It is not a boundary, because a boundary is a line and this is a line of stone about eleven paces long in a place where the sand turns in about nine places and none of the nine is a boundary anybody keeps.

**It is not a toll, it is not a gate, it is not a rate, and it is not a keeper; it has not stopped a cart in the two months it has been there, and every one of those five is a thing somebody could have said and nobody has said, and one man of about fifty-three with a wagon has said all five of them to himself in four minutes on nine trips and to nobody else at all.**

A boy of about seventeen with a rod wrote four hundred yards on a page and then turned the page over and wrote nothing else on it, and when the man of about forty-four came back and looked at it he did not ask what the boy had written on the other side and the boy did not offer.

“**That is a long way!**” the boy said.

“**It is a number,**” the man of about forty-four said. “**It does not mean that anything is between them. It means there is a number between them, and I wrote it down, and that is all it is for.**”

**And that is thirty-eight words in two turns of one exchange, of which the first is five and the second is thirty-three printed in two spans of four and twenty-nine, and they are the only two things the man of about forty-four said to anybody that tide, and he said both of them about a hundred yards from the water and the wind took about a third of the first one.**

## Three

**And the widow at the eleventh house is not on this page and does not know that a chain was run to her door on a Friday, and she is the person in that house and has been for about nine years, and she buys four sacks off the wagon and will not sell them back to it, and nine people on that sand know that and about four of them have said it out loud and she has said it once.**

A man of about thirty-three with a barrow and a load of glass in it came up the sand at about the seventh hour and put the barrow down at the eleventh house because he had done it nine times before, and the widow was not in and he left it and went on.

**Aren Kest was about two hundred paces seaward of the wall for the whole of that Friday with a stick he is not leaning on, and he did not go up to the eleventh house and did not go into the ninth and did not stand at the fourth, and nobody asked him to, and he was not there for the chain and the chain did not wait for him.**

**He did not know about the chain. He has not been told and was not asked and the four hundred yards has not been given to anybody in that house, and the fourth head of a form with three heads filled in is empty and stays empty and is not connected to a wall or a house or a woman of about seventy by any paper at all.**

A man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses was on the sand at about the eighth hour and said one thing about the four hundred yards and it was that he had known the distance would be about that and had not wanted it written down.

“**It was four hundred yards before there was a wall,**” he said, “**and it is four hundred yards now, and the wall has not moved the distance, and the only thing the wall has done is made the distance a thing you can write on a page.**”

**And that is forty-five words in one turn of one speech printed in two spans of ten and thirty-five, and a man of about forty-one said it standing in a doorway nine feet from a chain that had gone slack twice, and four people heard it and one of them was a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow who has not been thanked for saying anything in two months and was not thanked for this either and has said so.**

## Four

**And on the same tide, about two hundred paces landward of the wall, four of the eleven houses had somebody's work happening in them and seven did not, and that had nothing to do with the wall and nobody on that sand put the two in the same breath.**

A pan at the fourth house and a fire behind it that has not been out in forty-one years. A lease at the ninth, and the man who holds it standing at an off wheel. A widow with the last of a boat at the eleventh, with four sacks of salt in the boat and a barrow-load of glass on her step. And a man standing in the doorway of the seventh whom four of us will not stop at.

**That is four of eleven and seven of eleven and the two are never added to eleven in a column, and the four of them are where a sick person can be taken, and about nine hundred people found that out two months ago without anybody putting it in a column, and about four of them are in it now and about nine hundred are somewhere on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand that is there about four hours out of twelve.**

The man of about forty-four packed his chain at about the tenth hour and the boy of about seventeen with a rod coiled the second one and neither of them went to the eleventh house and neither of them went to the ninth house and neither of them went to the fourth house.

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet is not on that sand today and was last there on a day nobody on it can name, and nobody has asked him why he comes when he comes and he has not said, and nobody on that sand has put his name on anything.**

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow's, and it is the second one she has written down in two months, and she wrote it on the back of a handcart docket with a nail on about the eleventh hour, and four hundred yards and eleven paces of stone and four of eleven houses are not in it and are not added to one another in any order.

**a new wheel for the barrow at nine, a coil of rope at six, a length of tarred line at four, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a bundle of wood at three, a second bundle of wood at three, a night's lodging at the fourth house at two, a brush at two, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a pound of nails at three, a pair of mitts at two, a pound of candles at four, a tin of oil at two, a second room with a fire in it at eight, a quart of small beer at two, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a knife at two, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one and a third loaf at one.** Nine, six, four, four, four, three, three, two, two, five, eight, three, two, four, two, eight, two, two, two, one, one, three, two, one, one and one is eighty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-five pence is a mark and thirty-seven over**, and a chain is not in it and a rod is not in it and neither is rented on that shore, and four hundred yards is not in it and is a number and not a figure of men, and a form with three heads filled in and a fourth head empty is not in it and is in a room over a weigh-house two hundred miles off and a press there stands open with no date anybody will give.

**And a chain was run on a Friday from the landward face of eleven paces of stone to the door of the eleventh of eleven houses and came to four hundred yards, and it came back slack in the middle twice, and the man of about forty-four wrote it on a page with no head on it and turned the page over and the boy of about seventeen with a rod wrote nothing else on that side, and a widow of about seventy at that house was not in and was not told and bought four sacks off a wagon at nine pence a sack and will not sell them back, and the wall took the water over it twice that day as it has twice a day since the four days it took to lay, and the wall was under for two hours of the night with the sea on both sides of it and no man on the sand, and the sand was off it again before four in the morning, and about nine hundred people came over the top of it on the following tide as they had come over the top of it on the one before that.**
