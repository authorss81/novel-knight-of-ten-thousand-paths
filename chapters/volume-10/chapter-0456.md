# Chapter 0456

**The ninth ebb from a door in a salt-store and the fourth hour after the water went out, on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, and there is no month on that shore and there is no day of the week on that shore and there has been neither for about nine years, and eleven houses stand along the two miles and about nine hundred people use the road and four of the eleven have a person in them who answers for it and seven do not, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is standing at about the two hundredth pace with a woman of about fifty-one and a woman of about sixty-three who is not the same woman and has not come down to the sand in about nine years.**

## One

**The pan is a foot and a half across and it is set in brick at the landward end behind the fourth house, and the fire under it has not been out since a month in a year she does not give, and the woman who owns the fire is sixty-three and the fire is forty-one years old and both of those are true.**

Halla Wray had been coming down to the sand when she was twenty-two and had not come down to it in about nine years, and what she did instead was sit on a low wall at the back of the fourth house with a stick in her hand and a bowl of brine beside her, and the wall is about four feet high and it faces the wrong way for the water and it faces the light.

The man of thirty-two with a limp went up to the wall because a woman of about fifty-one said he would want to.

**Nobody introduced them, and the woman of about fifty-one was not in that conversation, and the two of them talked for about four minutes about a pan and a fire and a quantity of salt, and the woman of about fifty-one did not use the word keeper and the man of thirty-two with a limp did not use it either and the conversation was entirely about the work.**

## Two

She told him what the pan did in a season, and he asked her the ordinary question that a man asks a person about a job, which is what a good season looks like, and she answered it and then answered a second one that he had not asked.

“**A good season is a season where the water is off before the wind gets up and on again before the boats want in,**” she said. “**That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it since before I had the pan. And you have asked me what a good season looks like, and you have got the same answer you would have got off any of the four, and there is nothing in that answer about me.**”

She turned the bowl over on the wall and the crust came off it in one piece.

“**I have been at this fire forty-one years,**” she said, “**and I have been asked twice. Once by a man who wanted to buy the pan and once by a clerk who wanted a figure. And in forty-one years I have not been asked one single thing about what I want, and that is not a complaint and I have never said it out loud to anybody, and I am saying it now to a man who came here on somebody else's road with somebody else's letter.**”

**And that is a hundred and sixty-five words in two turns of two speeches and it is the whole of what she said to him, and he did not ask her a second question and about six people on that foreshore heard the whole of it because there is no other conversation on a foreshore at low water.**

## Three

**And a woman of about fifty-one is standing about two hundred paces off with a piece of driftwood and twenty-two notches in the edge of it, and she knows he is there, and he has not come over, and the two of them have not spoken in about twenty-two years and not one of the nine hundred people on that foreshore can say why.**

The reason is this, and it is the whole of the want of a woman of about fifty-one, and it is spoken once on that foreshore in twenty-two years: the four houses answer for themselves and the seven do not, and the four and the seven have never been in the same room, and the four have asked the seven for nothing in twenty-two years because the moment the seven ask the four for something the seven are eight, and the four are still four, and then there is a count.

“**That is the whole of it and I have never said it and I am not going to say it to him,**” she said, to nobody, at the ebb before the light, **and about nine people heard her say it and about four of the nine are the kind of person who will carry it to the four, and none of the four was on that foreshore that day.**

## Four

The man of thirty-two with a limp did the work that day because there was work and because nobody told him not to, which is the arrangement on a foreshore and has been since before the four.

He carried shell. About nine cart-loads a day off four boats, and the boats are flat-bottomed and go out on the ebb and come in on the flood, and he carried from the slip to the pans about forty yards, nine times, and about six times he carried two.

**And on the fourth of the nine trips the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow, on the far side of the sand, said a word to him that she uses for a person who is not from the coast and has stopped doing a thing wrong, and he said the word back to her in the accent of that coast, and about nine people heard him say it and about four of them were not surprised and one of those four was the woman of about sixty-three at her wall who has never once in forty-one years been surprised by a thing on that foreshore and has never said so.**

A woman of about fifty-one at the seaward end watched him do it and wrote nothing down and has not written anything down in about twenty-two years and there is nowhere on that foreshore to write it.

## Five

The cost of that day is a column and it is his and the money on it is the money of a man who has not been paid in coin for nine days and has been fed twice a day by nine people who did not agree about him and did not keep a count of it.

**four loaves at a penny, two pounds of cheese at four, three pounds of wood at three, four tallow candles at two, two quarts of small beer at two, a bushel of oats at four, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a sheet of paper at one.** One, one, one, one, four, four, three, three, three, two, two, two, two, two, two, four, five, eight and one is fifty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and fifty-one pence is a mark and three over**, and about nine cart-loads of shell and forty-one years of a fire and a figure of nine hundred people on two miles of sand are not in it and are not added to anything, and the four houses and the seven are not in it and are not a figure of men.

**And a woman of sixty-three with a pan and a fire that has not gone out since she was twenty-two said a hundred and sixty-five words to a man who came on somebody else's road, and a woman of about fifty-one at the seaward end said a thing out loud to nobody at the ebb before the light, and about nine people heard it and about four of them are the kind of person who will carry it to the four, and the four were not on that foreshore, and the water came in at about the ninth hour and went over about two hundred paces of the eleven houses' front doors and seven houses on the seaward half are shut when the water is in and four of the eleven are open and the four are the four that have a person in them who answers for it.**
