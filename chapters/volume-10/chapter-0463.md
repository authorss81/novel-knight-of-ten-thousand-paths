# Chapter 0463

**The fourth day of Goatmonth, YR 318, a Saturday and a market morning, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour, in a room over a door on a lane in a fen town of about four thousand people, where a bench of nine men sits on Saturdays and on nothing else in the week, and has been asked nine things in about nineteen years, and a man of about twenty-nine has been sitting at a factor's table under that room for a fortnight and came down the stairs at the second hour this morning with a folded sheet he did not put on the rail.**

## One

**The bench is nine men and it is not a court and it does not have a chair, and it has a board and a rail and about nine feet of floor in front of the rail, and a man with a business stands in the nine feet and not at the rail, and about nine hundred people are in the street below and the bench does not sit because of them and has not for about nineteen years.**

The man who keeps the minutes is about fifty-eight and has kept them a year and is not the clerk of anything and has never been, and the minutes go round.

**He is not paid for it, which is the point of it, and about two hundred people in that town know that he is not paid for it and about nine hundred have never worked it out.**

**A clerk of that bench is a year of a man's life and not an office, and about eighteen men in that county have been the clerk of it before him and about eleven of them did not want to be and not one of the eleven said so in the book, because the book is for what was agreed.**

He is on his second year and has four months left of it and everybody in that room knows it and nobody has said it this morning.

**The rail runs the length of the wall and there are marks cut into the underside of it about every four feet, and about nine men have cut those marks and about four of them cut two, and the marks run back about nineteen years and no further, and nobody knows why they stop, and the man of about fifty-eight has been asked twice and has said twice that he does not know.**

## Two

The man of about twenty-nine did not put the sheet on the rail. **He stood in the nine feet and asked the room a question that was not a thing the room had ever been asked, and about four men in that room turned round and about five did not.**

He said he was looking for a way to keep a thing together that somebody's father had not kept and somebody's son was not going to keep, and he said that the thing was not in that county and was not nine miles off it either, and that he had been to three places that had not been able to help him and had come here fourth.

And then he said the thing that decided the morning, which was that the place he was asking about was about four days off any made road that anybody keeps up and about four days off a coast where the water goes out twice a day, and that he had four days of walking in him in one direction and four in the other and no more than that.

A man of about forty in that room said that a road under water twice a day could not be a bench's business and that the bench had nine things in about nineteen years and was not going to get a tenth for a thing nobody in nine counties could name, and the man of about fifty-eight let him finish and then answered him in about nine sentences without once looking at the board.

**About four men in that room had not known before that morning that a turn ends on a day somebody else fixed, and about four of them have said since then that they did know and had not thought about it in those terms, and about two hundred people in that town know which four months of which year the man of about fifty-eight is the clerk of it and have never once said so to him.**

Nobody in that room improved on him and the man of about fifty-eight put his pen down.

## Three

“**I will tell you what a turn is,**” the man of about fifty-eight said, “**and then you can go and find out that it is not the same thing as yours.**”

“**A turn is a year. It starts when it starts and it ends when it ends and a date is fixed at both ends about four months before anybody knows who the next one is. I do not know whether I will be asked to do a third and neither does anybody else in this room, and that is the good part, and I would not give the good part up for a fortnight's pay.**”

He stopped there and about four men in the room agreed with him and about nine hundred people went past in the street and the bench did not sit because of them.

**Then the man of about twenty-nine asked him the second question, which was what a turn is for, and the man of about fifty-eight said that a turn is for making sure that a thing cannot be held by whoever happens to want it, and that the reason a turn beats a keeper is not that a turn is a better man and it is that a turn ends, and that about eleven men in that county had found that out at about four months' notice.**

And then he was asked the third question, which was whether a turn could be put on a thing that was there four hours out of twelve, and he said that a turn is a year and that a year is a thing that goes round, and that the thing he had been given was not going round and would not go round while he was in it, and that a man who is handed the first year of one of those is not a clerk of it and is something else, and he did not say what.

## Four

And then he said the other half of it, and it is the half that is the finding, and he said it without being asked and he said it to the man of about twenty-nine and not to the room.

“**Your road is there about four hours out of every twelve and it is there twice a day, and the question anybody will ask it is the question of whether a man is standing in it. I answer that question every week for four hours and then I am a different man for the rest of the week and he answers it.**”

**And that is sixty-two words in the last of three turns of one speech, and the whole of what he said that morning is a hundred and sixty-two words in the three of them, and he counted them on the board in front of him in about four seconds and did not read them out, and the man of about twenty-nine did not write them down and has thought about them on four days of walking since.**

## Five

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's and it is the third of them and he has stopped folding them in four and has started folding them in two.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a tallow candle at two, three quarts of small beer at two, a room at six, a bed at four.** One, one, one, four, three, two, two, two, two, six and four is twenty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and twenty-eight pence is over half of one by four**, and a bench that has been asked nine things in about nineteen years is not in it and is not a ninth asking and there is no tenth coming, and about nine hundred people in the street below are not in it and are not a figure of men.

**And a man of about fifty-eight put a pen down in a room over a door on a lane in a fen town of about four thousand people and said that his year would end on a day somebody else had fixed four months before he knew who would stand in the nine feet after it, and a man of about twenty-nine stood in the nine feet and heard it and did not put his folded sheet on the rail, and about nine hundred people in that street got a market morning done underneath a room that has been asked nine things in about nineteen years, and the man of about twenty-nine put his folded sheet back inside his coat on the stairs and did not say one word about it to anybody.**
