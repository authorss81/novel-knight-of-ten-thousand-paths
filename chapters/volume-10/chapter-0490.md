# Chapter 0490

**The first day of Longlight, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and the ninth hour and the eleventh hour and the second hour of the afternoon, on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, at a hundred paces from the landward end, and there is no month in anybody's mouth on that shore and no day of the week, and eleven paces of stone about two feet high with a second footing course nine inches lower on it are taking the water over them for the second time that day, and four hundred yards of sand runs from the landward face of that wall to the door of the eleventh of eleven houses and nobody has ever written a line on any part of it.**

## One

**The wall has taken the water over it twice a day every day since the four days it took to lay, and nobody on that sand has ever counted how many times and the counting of it is not anybody's job, and on this Thursday at about the ninth hour it did it again with a man of about thirty-two with a limp standing nine paces off it on the seaward side, and he had not been asked to stand there and was not asked anything while he stood there.**

The water came up over the near course first and then over the far one and then over both together, and it did not go over it any faster than it has gone over it since the month before last, and about four inches of sand is banked against the far side of it and about four inches has been banked there in four months.

**And eleven paces of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end of a road that has no line on it is not a boundary; it is not a toll, it is not a gate, it is not a rate, and it is not a keeper; it has never stopped a cart, and nobody in nine counties has named a way it is against anything, and a surveyor of about forty-four with a chain who could have named four ways of it in four minutes the day he ran it did not name any of them, because a chain finds out how far a thing is and does not find out whether it ought to be there.**

## Two

**And four hundred yards of sand runs from the landward face of that wall to the door of the eleventh of eleven houses, and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow walked all of it at about the eleventh hour and came back along the tide line, and she is the only person on that shore who has walked the whole of it in either direction since the chain was run, and she has not written a line on it and there is no line on it.**

She stopped twice. The first time was at the place where a chain had gone slack in the middle three weeks back and had been left lying flat on the wet sand and had not been rolled up, and there is nothing there now and there was nothing there then except the place where it had been.

“**Four hundred yards,**” she said, to a man of about forty-one with a basket who had come up the sand behind her from the boat land and had not asked her what she was doing.

“**I know what it is,**” the man of about forty-one said. “**I have carried nine baskets over that sand this month and I never once thought about the number.**”

“**I did not think about it either,**” she said, “**and I have thought about it every day for three weeks, and that is the whole of what I have got, and I am not going to write it down, and you are not going to tell anybody I walked it.**”

**And that is seventy-four words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is three, the second is five, the third is eighteen, and the fourth is forty-eight printed in two spans of seven and forty-one, and the man of about forty-one with a basket has not said a word about it since and has put nine baskets up a slipway about an hour later, and neither of them was thanked and neither of them wanted to be.**

## Three

**And the wall is four hundred yards from a house with a widow in it, and the house is four hundred yards from a chain that was run and not rolled up, and the wall is at a hundred paces from a landward end where about nine hundred people come off four hundred yards of quay, and between those four points on that day there was a market morning in a town of about two thousand people four days up a made road, and a form with three heads filled in and a fourth head empty, and a deed with one ruled signature at the foot of it, and neither paper mentions the other one and no person is named on either of them except an officer who is not the answerable one.**

Nobody has filled the fourth head. Nobody has said that anybody has filled it in somewhere else. Nobody has taken up an offer of one sentence about that wall made in about a hundred and ninety words in three turns on that coast four days up a made road, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet has been on that sand six times and has never once been asked to carry it and has never once said that he would if somebody asked, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp offered it first and has not offered it twice.

**And a man of about fifty-three with a wagon went down on the ebb, stood two hours, and came back on the flood, and about nine hundred people went across both ways of him, and he sent no boat back empty and told nobody why, and the ninth of the eleven bought three sacks at nine pence a sack and had bought them at nine pence a sack for eleven years.**

## Four

**And about the second hour of the afternoon the water went off the wall for the second time that day and went back at about four hundred paces and left about two hundred paces of the seaward half dry for about four hours out of twelve, and eleven paces of stone stood on it with a second footing course nine inches lower and about four inches of sand banked against the far side, and four hundred yards of sand between that wall and the eleventh of eleven houses had no line on it, and the man of about thirty-two with a limp walked back up the sand toward the landward end on his own with a stick he is not leaning on and got up a bank about four seconds slower than a man of thirty-two with two good legs, and nobody on that sand said anything about it, because on a foreshore you do not say anything about how a man gets up off the shingle.**

A man of about fifty with a cart was at the landward end and did not see him go and was not told. A woman of about twenty-seven with a basket was at the fourth house and did not see him go and was not told. A man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses saw him go and told nobody.

**And the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow was on the sand at about the second hour of the afternoon with her barrow turned over and her hands in her pockets and nobody had asked her to do anything, and the form with three heads filled in and a fourth head empty is in a room over a weigh-house two hundred miles off and is not empty because anybody emptied it, and there is no paper anywhere in nine counties that joins a wall of eleven paces of stone to a house with a widow in it four hundred yards away. There never was going to be one.**

## Five

The cost of that Thursday is a column and it is Aren Kest's, and it is the fifth one he has kept and he does not know what any of the five is for, and sixty-four words and nine weeks and nine hundred pence and four hundred yards and about nine hundred paces are not in it and cannot go in it.

**five loaves at five, a pound of cheese at four, four pounds of wood at three, two tallow candles at two, three quarts of small beer at two, a room at six, a bed at four, a second bed at four, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a pound of rope at six, three sheets of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a second quill and ink at three, a pound of nails at three, a pair of gloves at two, a waxed cord at three, an inkwell at three, a pound of candles at four, a handcart for the day at four, a basket at five, a brush at two, a knife at two, a bushel of oats at four, a second night's bed at four and a second room with a fire in it at eight.** Five, four, three, two, two, six, four, four, five, eight, six, one, three, three, three, two, three, three, four, four, five, two, two, four, four and eight is a hundred pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred pence is two marks and four over**, and eleven paces of stone about two feet high and a second footing course nine inches lower and about four inches of sand banked against the far side in four months are not in it and are not a figure of men, and four of eleven houses and seven of eleven houses are not in it and are never added to eleven in a column, and a road that is not a toll road has no rate printed on it anywhere in nine counties and none was printed here.

**And at about the second hour of the afternoon the water went off eleven paces of stone for the second time that day and left four hundred yards of sand between that wall and the eleventh of eleven houses dry on the tide line, and there is no line on any part of it, and the wall is still there and the road still works and nobody has won, and four hundred yards of sand is not a gap in anything, it is four hundred yards, and the water came in at about the ninth hour and went over about two hundred paces of the seaward half and went back at about the fourth hour of the morning with the wind still against it.**
