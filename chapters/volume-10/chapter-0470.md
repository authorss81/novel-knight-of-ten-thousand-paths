# Chapter 0470

**The first day of Thawmonth, YR 319, a Friday and not a market day, and the third hour and the sixth hour and the ninth hour, on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, and the county does not trade on a first of a month, and about nine people at the seaward end had come in off the boats that morning for a cart that was not coming, and there is no month in anybody's mouth on that shore and no day of the week, and a man of about twenty-nine who came four days up a made road with a folded sheet and has been on that sand eight days is putting the sheet away for the last time.**

## One

**A first of a month is not a day anybody trades on, and the reason is a cart, and on a first of a month the cart is four days up a made road going the other way with what it has gathered in it, and about nine hundred people on that sand found out at about the third hour that nobody was coming.**

The four boats went out on the ebb and came in on the flood and about nine cart-loads of shell came off them and went into four pans, and the seventh house, which is one of the seven that nobody is in, had nine people in it by the fourth hour and about nine more by the sixth, and there is no form in nine counties on which any of that is a thing anybody could be told about.

**What about nine hundred people on two miles of hard sand do on a day when nothing is coming and nothing is going is what they did on every other day, and that is the whole of the finding and it took nineteen years and one salt wagon to arrive at, and it cannot be written down, because a column wants a head and this has never had one.**

## Two

Nobody on that foreshore noticed that anything had changed, because nothing on that foreshore is counted in anything that changes.

**A man of about thirty-four with a sheet has been on that sand four times since the middle of a month in Wolfmonth, and there is nothing in the four of them that says why he comes when he comes, and nobody has asked him and he has not said, and the wall of eleven paces at a hundred paces from the landward end took the water over it twice that day exactly as it has twice a day since the four days it took to lay.**

**The water is off about four hours out of every twelve and the year on that shore is counted in springs and in notches, and a woman of about fifty-one at the seaward end has been cutting one of them into a piece of driftwood for about twenty-two years, and there are twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it with the twenty-second not cut, and it goes in when the water goes off a mark that has not been off in twenty-two years.**

The water did not go off that mark. It has not gone off that mark since a year in which a woman got it wrong twice on a wagon and the wagon went into a channel and there was nobody on it, and nobody on that sand knows that a year has turned today and nobody on that sand has a word for today that means it.

## Three

The man of about twenty-nine asked a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow what the day was, at about the ninth hour, in the ordinary way a man asks a thing he does not know.

And she said that there was a word for it in three counties where he had come from and a different set of words for it where he was standing, and that the one she would use was the number of times the water had been off and on since the boats went out that morning, and that if a man wanted the number he could stand there and count it, and that she was not going to do it for him.

## Four

**And then she said the thing that is the last thing anybody said on that sand that day, and she said it to a man with a folded sheet in his hand, and she did not know what was in the sheet and has not asked and is not going to.**

“**You have been here eight days and you asked two women what they want and a man with a wagon what he stops at,**” she said, “**and nobody has asked the eleven houses anything, and that is not a criticism, and it is the first thing you did right.**”

And the man of about twenty-nine said that he had thought about asking them, and that he had got as far as standing about nine feet off the ninth of the eleven with a sheet in his hand and a man of about fifty-three telling him about a pan and a lease and a widow, and that he had understood the whole of it in about a minute and had not asked, and that he was not going back to do it, and that was the reason and not a reason about houses.

And she said that she knew, and that about four of the nine people standing about nine feet off had known it at the same moment and about five had not, and that nobody on that sand had ever asked eleven houses anything and it was not going to begin with a man off a made road.

And she said that a house cannot be asked anything and that he had better not try it, and that he should go up the made road in the morning and take his four days over that road with him, and that if he wrote any of it down on the way he should not read it back in a room with a table in it.

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's and it is the ninth and it is the smallest of the nine and the reason it is the smallest is that nothing came down that road and nothing came up it except him.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a tallow candle at two, a room at six, a bed at four, three days' bread at three, a pot of cheese at six, a handcart for the four days up at four, a sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a second sheet of paper at one, a second night's lodging at the made road at four, a third night's lodging at the made road at four, a loaf for the road at one, a second loaf for the road at one.** One, one, four, three, two, six, four, three, six, four, one, three, five, eight, one, four, four, one and one is sixty-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and sixty-two pence is a mark and fourteen over**, and eleven houses and about nine hundred people are not in it and are not a figure of men, and a year turning is not in it and is not a thing anybody on that shore can put a column against.

**And the year turned on a Friday on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and broken shell and nothing on it knew, and a piece of driftwood in a post at the seaward end has twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it with the twenty-second not cut, and it goes in when the water goes off a mark that has not been off in twenty-two years, and the water did not go off that mark, and about nine cart-loads of shell came off four boats into four pans, and the fire behind the fourth house has not been out since a month in a year a woman of sixty-three does not give, and nobody on that foreshore has said a month in about nine years and nobody has said a day of the week, and there is no day here for a year to turn on.**
