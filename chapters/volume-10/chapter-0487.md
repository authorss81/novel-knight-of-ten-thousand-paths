# Chapter 0487

**The third ebb after the one where a man of about thirty-four with a sheet was asked why he comes when he comes, and the second hour after the water went out, on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, at the landward end and about two hundred paces short of it, where four of the eleven houses have a person in them who answers for it and seven do not and the seven are the ones that are used, and eleven paces of stone about two feet high are at a hundred paces from the landward end with four men and a boy of about seventeen with a rod and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow and a load of glass in it all on the same sand at the same hour, and there is no month on that shore and no day of the week on it and there has been neither for about nine years.**

## One

**Two things were working on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand at the third hour after the water went out, in the best month of the year, and neither of them knew the other was there, and about nine hundred people were on the sand at the same time doing a third thing that belongs to neither of them.**

The first thing was four of the eleven houses. A pan at the fourth house and a fire behind it that has not been out in forty-one years, and the woman of sixty-three at the back of it with her stick and her bowl of brine. A lease at the ninth, and a man standing at an off wheel with a wagon that had been on that sand four times a week for nineteen years. A widow of about seventy at the eleventh house with the last of a boat, and four sacks of salt in it. And a man standing in the doorway of the seventh whom four of us will not stop at.

The second thing was eleven paces of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end, and four men with a crowbar and a shovel and a sledge and a barrow, and a boy of about seventeen with a rod, and a chain that had gone slack twice twelve days back and was being laid out again along a line about forty yards long that did not follow any of the nine places the sand turns in.

**Four of eleven and seven of eleven and the two are never added to eleven in a column, and eleven paces of stone is not in the same column as either of them, and about nine hundred people is a third figure and is not a figure of men, and nobody on that sand had ever put the three of them on one line and nobody was going to.**

## Two

**And about nine hundred people crossed that sand between the second hour and the sixth hour, and a yard keeps its count in days and a road keeps its count in tides and a path community keeps its count in crossings open and weeks shut, and the three of those were not added on that sand by anybody and the only person who would have added them is about two hundred miles off in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, and is not on that sand and does not know it.**

The man of about fifty-three with a wagon counted nine cart-loads of shell off four boats into four pans and sent no boat back empty and told nobody why. The woman of about thirty-four with a barrow was at the ninth house at about the fourth hour with nine hundred paces of hard mud in her boots and an armful of wood on her shoulder and she put it down at the fourth house and did not stay.

**And the man of about thirty-three with a barrow and a load of glass in it was on the same sand at the same hour going the other way with a boat to load, and he went past four of the eleven houses and past the eleven paces of stone and past the four men and past the boy with the rod, and four people on that sand followed him with their eyes, because about seven weeks back there was a second man on that sand with nothing in his hands, and they have not stopped doing it since.**

## Three

**And the only conversation between the two instruments that tide was about a barrow — there was no other, and nine people were within earshot of it and none of them was asked to choose between the two things it was about, and it was between a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow, and both of them were doing their jobs at the time, and neither of them put the other one's job in his mouth.**

“**You will not get up that slipway with that on,**” the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said, pointing at the load with the crowbar and not at the man.

“**I have got up it four times this month with that on,**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said, “**and it is nine hundred paces of sand between that slipway and a door and about two hundred of them are under at the ninth hour, and I would rather have the two hundred up now than the nine hundred later, and you would too if you had four loads and a boat that floats.**”

“**I have a wall,**” the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said.

“**You have a wall,**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said, “**and I have a boat, and neither of us is going to be asked about the other one, and that is not an arrangement, it is just two men on sand.**”

**And that is a hundred and sixteen words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is ten, the second is sixty-seven printed in two spans of twelve and fifty-five, the third is four, and the fourth is thirty-five printed in two spans of four and thirty-one, and nine people heard it, and the boy of about seventeen with the rod wrote none of it down, and four of the nine say the man of about thirty-three with a barrow was right and four say that the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar had a wall and a boat is not the same size of thing.**

## Four

**And what happened between the sixth hour and the eleventh hour on that sand was that both of the instruments went on, and neither of them stopped, and about nine hundred people went home along the other one, and nothing that happened that day had anything to do with the fourth head of a form with three heads filled in, which is empty, and which is in a room over a weigh-house two hundred miles off where a press stands open with no date anybody will give.**

A pan at the fourth house and a fire behind it that has not been out in forty-one years. A lease at the ninth. A widow at the eleventh with the last of a boat. A man in the doorway of the seventh.

Eleven paces of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end, with a second footing course nine inches lower and about four inches of sand banked against its far side.

**A chain laid out flat on the wet sand about forty yards long with a slack in the middle of it. Nine cart-loads of shell off four boats into four pans. And a piece of driftwood about the length of a forearm and two inches through, wedged in a driven post five inches square at the seaward end, with twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it, one for each year, the twenty-second not cut, and it goes in when the water goes off a mark that has not been off in twenty-two years, and the water did not go off that mark on the first of a month in this year and did not go off it on the seventeenth day of this month either, and the twenty-second notch is not cut.**

Nobody made a list. There is no list and there was no meeting and there is no room on that sand, and about nine hundred people did what they did because eleven houses are there and four of them are standing in, and eleven paces of stone is there because four men and a barge put it there over four days, and nobody has ever said out loud that those two facts are in the same sentence.

**And Aren Kest was about two hundred paces seaward of the wall at about the fifth hour with a stick he is not leaning on and saw both of them at once, and he is not a keeper of either, and he said one sentence about them to a man of about twenty-three who rows one of the four boats, and the sentence was about the slack in the middle of the chain, and the man of about twenty-three said that the slack was the only honest part of the morning.**

## Five

The cost of that tide is a column and it is the man of about thirty-three with a barrow's, and it is the third one he has kept and he has three in a fold in a coat and has not joined any two of them up, and a boat of glass and nine cart-loads of shell and four of eleven houses are not in it and are not a figure of men.

**a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a mallet at three, a second mallet at three, a farrier at three, a quill and ink at three, a second quill and ink at three, a pound of nails at three, a second pound of nails at three, a broom at five, a second broom at five, a basket at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a second bucket of tar at eight, a handcart for the day at four, a second handcart for the day at four, a room at six, a bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a third night's bed at four, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a bushel of oats at four, a second bushel of oats at four, a quart of small beer at two, a second quart of small beer at two, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a pound of candles at four, a second pound of candles at four, a pair of mitts at two, a second pair of gloves at two, a knife at two, a second knife at two, a brush at two, a waxed cord at four, a tin of oil at two, a second tin of oil at two, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, a second bucket of wood at three, a fourth sheet of paper at one, a second bread at one and a second loaf at one.** One, one, one, three, three, three, three, three, three, three, five, five, five, eight, eight, four, four, six, four, four, four, four, four, four, four, two, two, two, two, four, four, two, two, two, two, two, four, two, two, three, three, three, one, one and one is a hundred and forty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and forty-three pence is two marks and forty-seven over**, and about nine hundred people and a yard and a road and a path community are not in it and are not added to one another in any order, and eleven paces of stone and four of eleven houses and seven of eleven houses are not in it and are never added to eleven in a column, and a chain is not in it and is not rented on that shore.

**And on the same tide a pan went on at the fourth house and a lease went on at the ninth and a widow at the eleventh went on with the last of a boat and a man stood in the doorway of the seventh, and at a hundred paces from the landward end four men and a boy of about seventeen with a rod went on with eleven paces of stone, and about nine hundred people went across both of them and did not stop on either, and about the ninth hour the sea came in under both of them at once, over about two hundred paces of the seaward half, and neither a pan at the fourth house nor a footing course nine inches lower was moved by it, and it was off the sand again before four in the morning.**
