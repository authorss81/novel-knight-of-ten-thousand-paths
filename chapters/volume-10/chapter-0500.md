# Chapter 0500

**The twenty-fifth day of Rainmonth, YR 319, a Tuesday and not a market day, and the first hour and the fourth hour and the tenth hour, in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane about two hundred miles off a coast, and then in a kitchen, and the twenty-second day of a month carried at thirty in this year is a Tuesday and the twenty-fifth is a Tuesday and both of them are not market days, and nothing is decided and nothing is won and nothing is torn down.**

## One

**What happened on that Tuesday is that a man of thirty-two with a limp went back to a kitchen and put three things in it in the wrong places on purpose, and about nine thousand paces of a town he has not lived in since he was nine years old were in the room with him, and he was not sure of the room and he placed it anyway, and the twenty-second notch is not cut, and the wall is a hundred paces from the landward end of two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and it is still standing and is still four hundred yards from that end and four hundred yards is four hundred yards.**

The kitchen is at the back of a house of two rooms and a lean-to in a street four hundred yards long with a made road off it, in a town about two thousand people, four days up a made road from a coast where eleven houses stand along about two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand, and the man who owns it is not in it and has not been in it since a month carried at thirty in the year before this one.

Three things went into that kitchen. A bowl with the rim chipped out of it, which went on the shelf above the pan instead of inside the press, and a hook with a piece of horse-brass on it that went on the third nail from the left instead of the fifth, and a stool that went under the window instead of against the wall, and the window does not shut and never did and the shutter is nailed.

**And he was not sure of the room and he placed it anyway, and the reason he placed it is that the bowl and the hook and the stool are his and the room is not, and the three of them are on a shelf in a room two hundred miles off and the kitchen is not, and about four of the nine people he has told about the three things have asked him which room it was and about four of the nine have not asked him and about one of the four who did ask said *I did not think you were the sort that had a kitchen* and about three of the four did not say anything at all.**

The chipped bowl is at the back of a shelf over a pan in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane about two hundred miles off that coast, and the hook with the horse-brass on it is on the third nail from the left of a board in the same room, and the stool is in a corner of the same room, and about nine hundred pence of rent in this year is not in either of those places and is not converted into one of them.

## Two

**And the habits of a full ordinary year in Merefen no longer fit his days, and the reason is not the coast and the reason is not the wall and the reason is that he cannot put a month to a place, and about four hundred people in Merefen know that he came back and about nine of those four hundred have been told by him that he is back and about four hundred and thirty-nine people in a town of about two thousand people four days up a made road have not been told anything at all, and the two figures are not added.**

A merchant's bench sits on Saturdays. A market is on Thursdays and Saturdays and not on a Sunday. A man of thirty-two with a limp has been on four roads in a fortnight and has slept in a room in a town of about four thousand people in the fen county and in two ports and in a room over a weigh-house and on a beach, and none of those places keeps its count the way the bench's town does, and there was a year in Merefen when the same man could have told you in a moment what day it was from the light on Weir Street and the tide at the Old Toll and what was due at the guild bench on the fourth, and he cannot do that now and has not been able to do it since about the ninth of a month carried at thirty in the year before this one, and he has not told anybody that, and it is not on any sheet.

**And the arithmetic of it is not a mystery and is not a debt and is not anybody's doing, and it is the ordinary arithmetic of a man who has two counts of one life and the two do not agree, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room and about a hundred and four go out in one band on a fixed day and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come in a month, and none of the three is derived from another, and none of the three has anything to do with a chipped bowl on a shelf over a pan.**

A woman of about thirty-one with a pen was in that room and is a different person from the woman who keeps it and is not a keeper of anything and nobody in that room is one. What she said was six words, and she said them while writing and did not look up.

“**You are back two days early.**”

“**I am back on a Tuesday,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said, “**and I have not been able to tell anybody which day of the week a thing happened on since about the ninth of a month in the year before this one, and I have got very good at not saying so, and you are the first person I have said it to and you did not put your pen down.**”

“**I did not put my pen down because you said it to me and not to the bench,**” the woman of about thirty-one said, “**and the bench is not the place for it and I have a piece to finish by the fourth.**”

**And that is a hundred and nine words in three turns of one exchange, of which the first is six, the second is printed in two spans of six and sixty, and the third is thirty-seven printed in two spans of eighteen and nineteen, and the woman of about thirty-one was not thanked and did not ask to be and went back to her piece and it was finished on the fourth and the fourth was a Saturday and a market day and the day's book in that room has nine entries of a thing nobody asked and this is not the tenth.**

## Three

**And the wall is a hundred paces from the landward end of two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand where a river runs into the sea, and it is eleven paces of stone in four days on sand that is under water twice a day, and nobody has begun to take it down and nobody is standing looking at it, because it is four days up a made road and nobody in this room can see it, and about four people on that coast are not looking at it this morning and about nine people on that coast are, and neither of those figures is a measurement of anything.**

The deed for it is a real deed. It is in a hand that is not a clerk's hand and it has a seal on it that is a seal, and it was drawn properly, and it wants a signature, and there is no signature on it, and nobody who lives on that sand has signed it and nobody who owns that sand could, because the sand is not owned by anybody. Two of the eleven houses are damaged and two are not, and eleven people put them right in about nine days, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was the sixth one over and not the first and is not the reason they stand, and about four people on that sand would tell you that the wall is the only thing anybody has built there in about nineteen years, and about nine of them would tell you that it stands a hundred paces from the landward end, and a hundred paces is a hundred paces.

**And nobody who has read it wins the argument about it, and the deed is not called good by anybody and is not called bad by anybody, and those are two facts about nine counties and not about a law.**

**And that is fifty-four words in three turns of one exchange that did not happen, of which the first is seven, the second is forty-six, and the third is one, and neither of them knew the other had said anything, and nobody has been told, and a wall a hundred paces from the landward end of a sand and a notch in a post four hundred yards off it are two facts and neither of them has been joined to the other and are not joined here.**

“**There is a stone on that beach,**” a woman of about sixty-three said, at about the fourth hour, in a fourth house of eleven on a coast about two hundred miles off, “**and there is a notch in a post at the other end of it, and one of them is four hundred yards from the other and about four of us have been trying for a year to work out which of the two is the coast's.**”

“**Neither,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said, from two hundred miles off, in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, and it is printed that the two of them were in the same room and they were not, and that is the only answer given in this matter by two people who were not in a room, and neither of them knew the other had said anything and neither of them has been told.

## Four

**And five paths are open, and there is no keeper on any of them, and the man of thirty-two with a limp is not one of them and has not said so out loud and will not be asked, and about four people know that there are five and about nine people know that there are at least three, and the number five is not printed in a room and is not on a sheet and is not the coast's and is not Merefen's.**

A man of about thirty-four from the port four days off has one and is not a keeper of it. A woman of about thirty-four with a barrow has one and is on no roll. A mason of about sixty-eight at a single arch over a beck nine miles off a yard of four lime kilns has one and has asked the far side of it about four hundred times. A woman of about fifty at a roadhouse nine miles on from the kilns has kept a column with no head on it for thirty-one years and has one. A woman of about fifty-one at a post with twenty-two notches cut in the edge of a piece of driftwood has one and did not ask for it and the twenty-second is not cut.

None of the five has met another of the five. About four of the five have heard of none of the other four. Two of the five would not describe what they hold to anybody who asked, and one of those two has described it in about nine words to a stranger who did not carry the answer anywhere, and the answer is still nine miles off from the room where it would be wanted.

**And the six of them are on five roads and one of the five is a beach, and about four hundred people in Merefen and about two thousand in the town of about two thousand people four days up a made road and about nine hundred on the sand and about eleven hundred on no roll at all are not added to one another anywhere anybody has written them down, and the word of the paper that starts all of it is not printed here, and it is not printed because nobody on five roads has ever been asked to say it in the same place twice.**

## Five

The cost of that Tuesday is a column and it is the man of thirty-four from the port four days off's, and it is the sixth one he has carried up and down a made road and he has never joined two of them, and four hundred yards is not in it.

**a night's berth in the fourth house at two, a second night at two, a load of wood at three, a second load of wood at three, candles for a week at two, a second week's candles at two, flour for a week at four, a second week's flour at four, a side of bacon at nine, a second side of bacon at nine, cheese for a week at four, a second week's cheese at four, an axe handle at two, a second axe handle at two, a whetstone at one, a second whetstone at one, oil for a lamp at two, a second lamp's oil at two, a pail of tar at eight, a second pail of tar at eight, a week's washing at four, a second week's washing at four, a sheet of writing paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a quill at two, a second quill at two, a bootlace at one, a second bootlace at one, a night's straw at one, a second night's straw at one, a farthing at one, a second farthing at one, a week of a room at nine, a second week of a room at nine, a week of fuel at three, a second week of fuel at three, a night's bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a broom at five, a second broom at five, a pot of tallow at two, and a farthing at one.**Two, two, three, three, two, two, four, four, nine, nine, four, four, two, two, one, one, two, two, eight, eight, four, four, one, one, two, two, one, one, one, one, one, one, nine, nine, three, three, four, four, five, five, two and one is one hundred and thirty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and thirty-nine pence is two marks and forty-three over**, and a chipped bowl is not in it, and a hook with a piece of horse-brass on it is not in it, and a stool is not in it, and a kitchen is not in it, and eleven houses and eleven paces of stone and five paths and one difference of three are not in it, and twenty-two notches are not in it and the twenty-second is not cut.

**And on the twenty-fifth day of Rainmonth in this year a man of thirty-two with a limp stood in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane about two hundred miles off a coast and did not open anything and did not close anything and did not sign anything, and in a kitchen about nine thousand paces from a room he has not lived in since he was nine years old he put a chipped bowl on a shelf and a hook on the third nail and a stool under a window that does not shut, and he was not sure of the room and he placed it anyway, and a wall a hundred paces from the landward end of two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand is still standing and four hundred yards is still four hundred yards, and five paths are open and none of them has a keeper, and he cannot say which of his days belong to Merefen and has stopped trying to say it out loud.**
