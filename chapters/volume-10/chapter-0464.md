# Chapter 0464

**The thirteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the tenth hour, in a township of about nine hundred people two days off a made road, where a market runs on two days in seven and this is not one of them and there is nothing in the street to say it is, in a meeting room over a store that a woman of about sixty-three opened for him at the second hour after he asked her at the door, where there is a chair on a platform and a hat on a shelf and both of them are about a hundred and forty years old and neither of them is locked.**

## One

**A chair on a platform and a hat on a shelf and a book in which what was agreed is written, and the room has had all three for about a hundred and forty years, and it has used them for a pound, a common oven, a set of nets, the repair of a water trough, and a list of other things that is a list of about nine over four generations.**

The way it works is that anybody may put a name in the hat, and anybody may come and look at the names in the hat before the names are read, and about nine people out of about nine hundred put a name in every year, and each of the nine has the thing for a month, and the chair goes round about nine times a year and about a hundred and forty times in a hundred and forty years, and it has never once had anybody's name against it for longer than a month.

That is the whole of the arrangement and it is a good arrangement and about nine people in nine counties have said so in those words.

**The chair is on a platform about nine inches high with two steps up to it, and the platform was built for a chair that was higher than the one on it, and the bolt on that door has not been drawn in about nine years, and the woman of about sixty-three keeps the key in her apron and has never once not had it.**

## Two

**He asked her for two days and she said he could have the room and not the two days, and he took the room, and she did not ask him what for and he did not say.**

A band of about a hundred and four had gone out on the fixed day and come back on the second day before and one sheet out of the band had come into that room, and the woman of about sixty-three had read it and put it in the box on the shelf and not in the book. **It came from a county two hundred miles off and it has a date at the top of it and no head on it and something written on the other side that nobody in that township has read out, and the woman of about sixty-three knows what is on it and does not know what to do with it and has not asked anybody and is not going to.**

The man of about twenty-nine read it standing up in about nine seconds and put it back in the box on the shelf.

**He asked her who else in that township could read it and she said that about four people could and that about two of the four had asked her what came in the band and about two had not, and that the box on that shelf has been the same box for nineteen years and has gone back further than that and that nobody has ever counted what is in it, and that about nine hundred sheets a month come into that county from a room two hundred miles off and about a hundred and four of them come in one band on a fixed day and the two are not the same number and neither of them is added to the other.**

## Three

He asked her the question he had asked in two other places by then, and he asked it in the ordinary way, standing in the room with his back to the door.

“**What happens if a thing is left and nobody knows who is holding it.**”

And the woman of about sixty-three said that anybody may come and look at the names in the hat, and that any of about nine hundred people in that township can walk in there and read a name out loud.

And the man of about twenty-nine said nothing for about four seconds, and then he said that a name that can be read out loud from a shelf is a name that can be found, and that both of the other places he had been to had been about a name that could not be found and that this one was about a name that could.

## Four

“**A hat is the right answer for a pound,**” she said, “**and a common oven, and a set of nets, and the repair of a water trough, and it is right for those four things because nobody minds being found about a pound.**”

“**Your thing is not a pound. Your thing is a road that about nine hundred people have to know about twice a day, and the moment one name goes into a hat on this floor then every one of the nine hundred can say who to send for, and about four days later there will be a man on that sand who was sent for and he will not be the man who was there.**”

**And she said that she has kept that room for nineteen years and has never once been asked to put a road in it, and that she would not know where to begin and that the book is for what was agreed and not for what it cost and that there is a difference between those two things that has taken her nineteen years.**

And then she said the last thing of that morning, and she said it to the door and not to him, and about two hundred people in that township had no idea it had been said.

“**A hat is how a township says that it does not mind. That is all a hat is. And there is not one pound or one oven or one set of nets in this room that anybody would mind being found about, and there is not one thing you are describing that anybody would mind being found about either, and that is the trouble and it took me nineteen years not to be able to say it.**”

## Five

The cost of those two days is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's and it is the fourth of them and he has begun to write them on one side of a sheet and has not joined them up.

**a room at six, a lamp at two, a sheet of paper at one, a pound of wood at three, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, four quarts of small beer at four, a pound of cheese at four, two tallow candles at three, a day's bed at four, a second day's bed at four, a basket at five, a handcart for the two days at four, a quill and ink at three, a second sheet of paper at one.** Six, two, one, three, one, one, four, four, three, three, four, four, five, four, three and one is forty-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and forty-nine pence is a mark and one over**, and a chair going round about nine times a year is not in it and is not a figure of men, and about nine hundred people in a township of about nine hundred people is not in it and is not a column and is nobody's business.

**And a man of about twenty-nine stood in a room over a store with his back to the door and was told by a woman of about sixty-three that a hat is the right answer for a pound and a common oven and a set of nets and a trough, and was not told it was the right answer for a road, and put a sheet with a date on it and no head on it back into a box on a shelf where it had been for about two weeks, and about two hundred people in that township got an ordinary Monday done without knowing any of it, and one of the nine names put into that hat this year had been in it for three weeks and was about a week from the end of it and had not been asked.**
