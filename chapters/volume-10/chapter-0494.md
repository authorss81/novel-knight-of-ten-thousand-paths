# Chapter 0494

**The thirteenth day of Rainmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, the first ebb after the night the water came over the landward face of eleven paces of stone and took about four inches of shingle out from under four houses, and the second hour after the water went out, on a sand two miles and about nine hundred paces long with a river in it where it meets the sea, and where no mouth on that shore has said a month or a day of the week in about nine years, and four of the eleven houses have lost the ground under the seaward wall of them and none of the four is lost.**

## One

**A footing of stone that has been taking the sea over it twice a day for two months does not fail at the stone. The stone is fine. What fails is the shingle the stone is standing in, and a shingle bank is about four inches of what the sea has been putting there for as long as there has been a sea, and about four inches of that went out from under four houses in one night, and the sea put about four inches back in the same night and it went back in a different place.**

The four are the fourth and the seventh and the ninth and the eleventh. They are four of eleven, and seven of the eleven are empty and the seven are the ones that are used, and about nine hundred people found that out in the first month without anybody putting it in a column.

The fourth house has a pan at the back of it and a fire behind the pan that has not been out in forty-one years, and a woman of sixty-three who was up at the fourth hour and had the fire in and had two armfuls of wood on it by the time about nine hundred people came off the boats.

The seventh house has a man standing in the doorway of it, and he was standing in the doorway of it at the second hour with the door open behind him, looking at a gap about nine inches wide where there had not been a gap on the previous tide.

The ninth house has a lease on it and a man holding the lease came out onto his own step, which he had not done in nine weeks.

The eleventh house is four hundred yards up the sand and has a widow of about seventy in it with the last of a boat and four sacks of salt in the boat, and she was not in and the boat was not in the water.

**And no house was lost, and no person was hurt, and a wall of eleven paces of stone about two feet high is still a hundred paces from the landward end and four hundred yards from the door of the eleventh of eleven, and no paper in nine counties joins those two things and nobody has named a way the wall is against anything.**

## Two

**And about two hours after the water went out there were eleven people on that sand who had not agreed to be there, and the first one over was the man of about thirty-three with a barrow and a load of glass in it, and he was not the first one over because the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar was, and the crowbar man had been there since the water was still on the ground because he had been told nothing and had come anyway.**

Nobody called them. Nobody paid them. They did it in about nine days, and at the end of the nine days four of the eleven houses had the ground back under the seaward wall of them, packed in with shingle off the flat a hundred yards seaward, in courses, and the courses were not level and about four of the eleven said so and nobody minded.

Aren Kest was the sixth one over. He carried a basket of shingle for about four days and about nine people took shingle off him and about nine did not, and he was not the first one over and was not the reason any of the four houses was got back, and at about the seventh hour on the fourth day a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said so to a man of about thirty-one with a barrow of sand and not to him, and he was thirty feet off and heard it.

“**He is the sixth,**” the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said.

“**I can count,**” the man of about thirty-one with a barrow of sand said.

“**Then you will have noticed that he has not once been told where to put it.**”

“**I have noticed that,**” the man of about thirty-one said, “**and I have decided that a man of thirty-two who cannot get up off the shingle without about four seconds is not going to be the man who tells a boy of seventeen where to put a basket of shingle, and that is a decision about the boy and not about the man.**”

“**That is four things you have noticed,**” the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said, “**and you have said one of them out loud, and I am not going to ask for the other three.**”

**And that is a hundred and seven words in five turns of one exchange, of which the first is four, the second is three, the third is sixteen, the fourth is fifty-seven printed in two spans of four and fifty-three, and the fifth is twenty-seven printed in two spans of seven and twenty, and the man of about thirty-one with a barrow of sand made a decision about a boy and said so and did not say what it was about, and the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow did not ask for the other three things, and neither of them said the name of the man of about thirty-two with a limp in that exchange, and it is the eleventh house's gap about nine inches wide and not the sand that is being talked about.**

## Three

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet came down on the second tide after the night and stood nine feet off the fourth house and wrote four things on a sheet with no head on it, and the four things were how wide the gap is at each of the four houses, and how deep the shingle that came out of it would fill a barrow, and how many tides it takes the sea to put a bank back, and how long the four houses have been standing on that bank.**

He did not measure the sea and he did not measure the bank and he wrote down a number for all three of them, and the numbers were his, and about four people who could have counted the gap said afterwards that two of the four numbers were wrong and neither of them was the width.

**And what nobody on that sand said to him, on that tide or on any of the nine that followed, was that he should not have come, and what nobody on that sand said to him either was thank you, and he came six times in all before the four houses were got back and he was the seventh one over on the last of the nine days and he did not say he was the seventh.**

“**You have written that the sea puts a bank back in nine tides,**” a woman of about sixty-three said to him, from the doorway of the fourth house, at about the fifth hour on the seventh day, and she had a stick in one hand and had been standing in that doorway since the fourth hour and the fire behind the pan was in and had been in for all forty-one years.

“**I have,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said.

“**It is eleven,**” the woman of sixty-three said.

“**It is nine on your side and eleven on mine,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, “**and I have written nine, and if you want it changed I will change it, and if you want to write eleven on the sheet yourself you may do that and I will not stop you.**”

The woman of sixty-three looked at the sheet for about as long as it takes a man to say four and then she went back inside and shut the door about a foot and a half, which is how that door has been shut on that house for about nine years.

**And that is sixty-four words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is thirteen, the second is two, the third is three, and the fourth is printed in two spans of ten and the rest of the forty-six, and the woman of sixty-three has never once in forty-one years been asked what she wants and did not say it on that tide and has not said it since, and the man of about thirty-four with a sheet offered her his sheet and she did not take it and shut the door to a foot and a half.**

## Four

**And nine days after the water went out, four of the eleven houses had the ground back under them in courses that were not level, and about nine hundred people crossed that sand on the first tide after it and stopped at none of the four, and four of the eleven houses and seven of the eleven houses are two figures and are never added to eleven in a column, and eleven people is a third figure and is not a figure of houses and is not added to either of them.**

The man of about fifty-three with a wagon came down on the ebb and stood about two hours and came back on the flood and this time he stopped about forty yards short of the wall, which he has not done in nineteen years, and stood there the two hours.

The man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses was not asked to mend his own house and was in his doorway the whole of the nine days and came out twice, and on the second time he came out he stood about nine feet off and watched and said nothing at all to anybody, which is the first thing anybody has heard him not say.

**And the sea went over the seaward half of that sand at about the ninth hour and took about two hundred paces of it, and it went over eleven paces of stone a hundred paces from the landward end at the same hour, and it was off both again before four in the morning, and on the next flood the gap at the fourth house was four inches again and at the ninth it was not, and the woman of sixty-three has not said which of the two she thinks will happen again.**

## Five

The cost of those nine days is a column and it is the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow's, and it is the fifth one she has kept and it is in a fold in a coat and she has not joined any two of them up, and eleven people and four houses and seven empty houses are not in it.

**a new sill at four, a second new sill at four, a beam of fir at eight, a second beam of fir at eight, a third beam of fir at eight, a pound of nails at three, a second pound of nails at three, a third pound of nails at three, a wedge at two, a second wedge at two, a third wedge at two, a chock at one, a second chock at one, a pound of rope at six, a tarred line at four, a bucket of tar at eight, a pound of candles at four, a tallow candle at two, a loaf of bread at one, a second loaf of bread at one, a third loaf of bread at one, a fourth loaf of bread at one, a fifth loaf of bread at one, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a third pound of cheese at four, a night's lodging at the fourth house at two, a second night's lodging at the fourth house at two, a third night's lodging at the fourth house at two, a bushel of oats at four, a broom at five, a brush at two, a knife at two, a handcart for the day at four, and a second handcart for the day at four.** Four, four, eight, eight, eight, three, three, three, two, two, two, one, one, six, four, eight, four, two, one, one, one, one, one, four, four, four, two, two, two, four, five, two, two, four and four is one hundred and seventeen pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and seventeen pence is two marks and twenty-one over**, and nine days of somebody else's shingle is not in it, and nine tides and eleven tides are not in it and are two numbers a man of about thirty-four with a sheet wrote on a sheet with no head on it, and four thousand pence at the eleventh house and two carts at the ninth are not in it and are never added.

**And four of the eleven houses lost the ground under the seaward wall of them in one night of the middle of Rainmonth in this year and none of the four was lost, and eleven people put it back in courses that were not level in about nine days without anybody calling them and without anybody paying them, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp was the sixth one over and carried a basket and was not the reason any of it worked, and a man of about fifty-three with a wagon stopped forty yards short of a wall of eleven paces of stone for the first time in nineteen years, and the sea took about two hundred paces of the seaward half at about the ninth hour and was off it again before four in the morning.**
