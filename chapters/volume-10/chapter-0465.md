# Chapter 0465

**The nineteenth day of Goatmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, at a hurdle across a lane nine miles off a made road, in a dale of about nine hundred people and a stream and two hundred acres of grazing and a chapel that was rebuilt in the year the old mill stopped, where a man of forty-four who farms the near side puts the hurdle across twice a day and takes it back and has never had a lock on it and has never wanted one, and a man of about twenty-nine has walked nine miles of lane to stand on the wrong side of it.**

## One

**The dale is about nine hundred people and nothing has been asked of anybody in it in about forty years, and the reason is a sentence of nine words that a man of forty-four said standing at the hurdle without being asked to say it.**

“**A man with a form stayed here four years.**”

He said it before he had had his breakfast and then he put the hurdle back the other way and went to his breakfast, and about nine hundred people in that dale have had forty years of a morning that begins with a hurdle going over and four of them know the whole of the nine words and the other about nine hundred and six have never asked.

**The lane runs four miles with a stream beside it and a footbridge at the bottom of it and two hundred acres of grazing coming down to it on the near side, and about nine hundred people in that dale get an ordinary Sunday done in it, and the lane sends no cart up it, and the reason the lane sends no cart up it is the nine words, and nobody has ever gone to find out whether that is the whole of the reason.**

## Two

The man of about twenty-nine stood on the wrong side of the hurdle and said his question across it, and the man of forty-four came back and stood on his own side of it and did not take it down, and a hurdle and two hundred yards of lane and nine people at a cottage door were between the two of them and none of the nine came over.

He said that he had been to three places and that a man in a fold had told him that a thing is kept by not being touched, and a man at a bench in a fen town had told him that a thing is kept by a turn of a year, and a woman in a township had told him that a thing is kept by a name anybody can read out loud.

**And then he told the man of forty-four about a room over a weigh-house in a port four days off any coast, where a woman of forty-four lets it by the day and goes home at the eighth hour and does not draw the bolt, and he said that a room nobody can be found about is not a place to go and is the same fault as the other three with the front taken off it.**

And then he asked whether nothing was going to be the answer, and he asked it as a question and not as a claim, and nine people at a cottage door heard him ask it and four of them came nine paces into the lane.

## Three

**And the man of forty-four said no, and he said it without any pride in it, and what he said in the next four minutes is the whole of what this dale has to give and it is about not having a gate.**

“**That is not what I have got and I would not sell it to you if I had it.**”

“**A place with no gate is not a refusal. A refusal is a thing a person does and says out loud and somebody hears. Nothing is a thing a place has not got. I have never said no to anybody in this dale and neither has anybody else in it, and if you go down that lane tomorrow and ask nine hundred people here whether they will answer for your road, every one of them will tell you there is nobody here to ask.**”

## Four

And then he said the half of it that the man of about twenty-nine carried four days down a made road and did not put in a book.

“**I put a hurdle across twice a day and I take it back and I do it before I have had my breakfast, and if you asked me why I would tell you it is a habit and not a keeping. My father did it and I have never once asked him why and he is nine years dead and cannot tell me now. You could put your road in here tomorrow and the whole of what this dale would give you is a man putting a hurdle across a lane and not knowing why.**”

**And that is ninety-five words in one turn of one speech, and the man of about twenty-nine wrote none of it down, and four people in that dale heard all of it and two hundred yards off the lane a woman who was pulling a swede stopped with her hands full and did not come any nearer.**

**He asked the man of about twenty-four at the cottage door, on his way out, what the dale thought about all that, and the man of about twenty-four said that the dale does not think about it, and that thinking about it is the first thing that goes when you stop being asked, and that he had asked his father once, about nine years ago, and that his father had not known then either.**

**And then the man of forty-four did a thing nobody asked him to do, which was to walk the man of about twenty-nine back down the lane to the point where the hurdle is, and he walked it at the pace of a man going to do something and not at the pace of a man going for pleasure, and nine people came out of doors along that lane to find out what was happening and four of them went back in before they got to the hurdle.**

## Five

The cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's and it is the fifth of them and it is the last one he keeps in the fen country, before he goes and finds out what he has been asking about all along.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, two tallow candles at two, four quarts of small beer at four, a room at six, a bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a third night's bed at four, a day's bread at two, a pot of cheese at six, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a hire of a horse for the nine miles at nine, a loaf for the road at one, a pound of cheese for the road at four, four quarts of small beer for the road at four, a pair of boots mended at five.** One, one, one, four, four, three, three, two, two, four, six, four, four, four, two, six, one, one, three, nine, one, four, four and five is seventy-nine pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and seventy-nine pence is a mark and thirty-one over**, and a dale of about nine hundred people and forty years of not being asked for anything is not in it and is not a figure of men, and three places that were all wrong is not in it and is not a column and is nobody's business.

**And a man of about twenty-nine stood on the wrong side of a hurdle across a lane nine miles off a made road and was told by a man of forty-four that nothing is not a refusal and that the whole of what nine hundred people could give him is a habit and not a keeping, and he wrote none of it down, and four days later he was standing on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and broken shell with about forty days of walking behind him and four more in front of him and a piece of driftwood with twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it at the seaward end.**
