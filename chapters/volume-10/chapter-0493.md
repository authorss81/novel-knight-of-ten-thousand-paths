# Chapter 0493

**The twenty-sixth day of Longlight, YR 319, a Monday and not a market day, four days before the last of a month carried at thirty, and the third hour and the seventh hour and the eleventh hour, a hundred paces off the landward end of that sand, where nobody has said a month or a day of the week in about nine years, and where eleven paces of stone with a footing course nine inches below the top of it have been under the water twice in that day, and where about four inches of sand has banked itself against the far side of the stone in four months, and where four men are doing on that stone the thing a road office does.**

## One

**A road office that has put eleven paces of stone a hundred paces from the landward end of a road that is under water twice a day has about nine things left to do with that stone, and eight of them are about the stone, and the ninth is about the ground the stone is standing on, and the ninth is the one that takes a crowbar.**

That is what four men were doing at about the third hour. A man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar, a man of about twenty-six with a shovel, a man of about fifty-one with a sledge and a man of about thirty-one with a barrow of sand. They were not attacking anything. They were shoring the landward face of eleven paces of stone that had been taking the sea over it twice a day since a month in the middle of the year, and shoring is what a road office does to a footing it has put in a place where the water comes, and nobody has ever asked the office why the footing is there.

A man of about thirty-four with a sheet was on that sand and had been on it four days out of the last nine, and he had a board and a sheet on it and had written nothing on the sheet, and nobody has asked him why he comes when he comes and he has not said.

**And the wall is not a boundary, not a toll, not a gate, not a rate and not a keeper; it has never stopped a cart in the two months it has been there, and no man in nine counties has named a way it is against anything, and a surveyor of about forty-four with a chain could have named four ways of it in about four minutes the day he ran it and named none of them.**

“**You are putting timber against the wrong face,**” a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said, at about the seventh hour, to the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar.

“**I am putting timber against the face the water comes off,**” the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said.

“**That is the same face.**”

“**It is the same face,**” he said, “**and you are asking me a question about a wall and I am answering it about a wall, and neither of us is being difficult.**”

“**I am not being difficult,**” she said. “**I am saying that a man with a sheet is standing nine feet off you writing nothing, and that is a thing I have noticed six times.**”

“**Then it is a thing you have noticed six times,**” the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said, “**and I have not noticed it once, and both of those are correct, and I would take the timber off this face if there were a reason and I have not got one.**”

**And that is a hundred and twenty-nine words in seven turns of one exchange, of which the first is eight, the second is eleven, the third is five, the fourth is thirty printed in two spans of five and twenty-five, the fifth is five, the sixth is twenty-seven, and the seventh is forty-three printed in two spans of ten and thirty-three, and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar is not a knave and is not a villain and is a man who has been shoring stone in bad water for about twenty-six years and has never once been asked what a wall is for, and the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow has noticed the man with the sheet six times and has asked about it once and is not going to ask again.**

## Two

**And a boy of about seventeen with a rod was on that sand with a notebook with no head on any page, and he kept that day's column on the back of a rod-measuring sheet with a stub of pencil out of a shed, and the column is the timber: a crowbar and a shovel and a sledge and a barrow of sand, and gloves, and nails, and two lengths of rope, and four nights of a bed at a room over a chandler's shop four days up a made road, and about nine of the things in it are the same nine things the four men were using on the stone.**

The boy wrote it down at about the seventh hour and turned the sheet over and wrote nothing on the other side, which he has done after every column he has kept since a month in Mudmonth. When the man of about thirty-four with a sheet came and looked at it he did not ask what was on the other side and the boy did not offer.

“**That is a lot of timber,**” the man of about thirty-four with a sheet said, at about the eleventh hour, reading it upside down from nine feet off.

“**It is a lot of timber,**” the boy said.

“**And you have written down that it is a lot of timber.**”

“**I have written down what it is,**” the boy said. “**I have not written down that it is a lot. That is your word. I do not put your words in my book, or I would have to keep yours and mine, and then somebody would come along and take one out and I would not know which.**”

**And that is thirty-two words in one turn of one speech printed in two spans of five and twenty-seven, and about four people heard it and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar did not, and the man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses has said the same thing out loud about nine times in about two months and has not said it to a crowbar once.**

## Three

**And about four hundred yards up that sand a widow of about seventy was not in and does not know that eleven paces of stone is being shored a hundred paces from the landward end, and four hundred yards is the only distance between that wall and that door that anybody has ever measured, and it is the only distance that has ever been chained.**

She buys four sacks off a wagon and will not sell them back to it. She lives at twenty pence a day, and about two hundred days of the water being off long enough is a season to that household, and a season at twenty pence a day is four thousand pence, and the house next door along paid two carts up a made road and four days of a boy's legs each way for the same about two hundred days and will not put it in a column, and the two figures are on the same stretch of sand and they are not added and have never been on the same line.

A man of about fifty-three with a wagon came down on the ebb and stood about two hours and came back on the flood and did not stop at the eleven paces of stone at all, because a wagon does not go a hundred paces up a sand for a stone, and about nine hundred people went across both ways of him.

**And a man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses was on that sand at about the seventh hour and said one thing, and it was that the timber is on the right face, and he said it to nobody and about four people heard it and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar did not.**

“**It is the right face,**” the man of about forty-one said, to the wind, “**and I have said that out loud about nine times in about two months and about four of the nine were to people who were not asking.**”

## Four

**And nobody on that shore has said a day of the week in about nine years and nobody has said a month either, and on the twenty-sixth of a month carried at thirty, four days before the last day of it, four men put timber against the sea side of eleven paces of stone that a man with a sheet has been coming to look at six times without being asked why, and a surveyor of about forty-four with a chain was not on that sand and a boy of about seventeen with a rod was, and about nine hundred people crossed the sand between the third hour and the seventh hour and stopped on the stone and on four houses at nobody in particular, because about four of the eleven houses have somebody's work happening in them and seven do not and nobody made a list.**

**And the water came over the top course at about the ninth hour and lay on it for about two hours and went over about two hundred paces of the seaward half, and it was off the stone again before four in the morning, and the timber was still against it, and about four inches of sand is banked against the far side of it in four months and about four inches is going to be banked against the timber in another four, and nobody has named a way any of that is against anything.**

## Five

The cost of that Monday is a column and it is the boy of about seventeen's, and it is the fourth he has ever kept and it is on the back of a rod-measuring sheet with a pencil stub out of a shed, and eleven paces of stone and a second footing course nine inches lower are not in it.

**a crowbar at nine, a shovel at nine, a sledge at nine, a barrow of sand at nine, a pair of gloves at two, a second pair of gloves at two, a pound of nails at three, a second pound of nails at three, a rope at six, a second rope at six, a lamp oil at two, a second lamp oil at two, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a stop for a boot at three, a second stop for a boot at three, a farrier at three, a second farrier at three, a night's bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a third night's bed at four, a fourth night's bed at four, a room at six, a second room at six, a third room at six, a bushel of oats at four, a bottle of tar at eight, a sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a broom at five, and a pail of water at two.** Nine, nine, nine, nine, two, two, three, three, six, six, two, two, one, one, one, four, four, three, three, three, three, four, four, four, four, six, six, six, four, eight, one, three, five and two is one hundred and forty-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and forty-two pence is two marks and forty-six over**, and a chain is not in it and a rod is not in it and neither is rented for money on that shore, and four hundred yards is not in it and is a number and not a figure of men, and four thousand pence and two carts are not in it and are two prices for the same length of time at two houses and are never added.

**And a wall of eleven paces about two feet high with a second footing course nine inches lower took the water over it for the second time on the twenty-sixth day of a month carried at thirty in this year, four days before that month ended, and four men shored the face the water comes off with about nine things' worth of timber and a barrow of sand, and none of the four said what the wall is for and none of the four has been asked, and a boy of about seventeen wrote down what the timber cost and did not write down that it was a lot, and about four hundred yards up that sand a widow of about seventy was not in and was not told, and the sea laid about four inches of sand against the far side of eleven paces of stone in four months and laid it against the timber in one tide, and the sand was off it again before four in the morning.**
