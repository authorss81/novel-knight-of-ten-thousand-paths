# Chapter 0458

**The twenty-first day of Frostmonth, YR 318, a Friday and not a market day, and the seventh hour and the tenth hour and the first hour of the afternoon, on a foreshore where the water goes out twice a day, at about a hundred paces from the landward end, where there is a wall of stone eleven paces long and about two feet high, and a surveyor of about forty-four is standing at the landward end of it with a chain and a boy of about seventeen with a rod, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet is not on the foreshore and was last there on the ninth of this month, and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow is on the sand and is not a keeper of anything.**

## One

**A survey of a foreshore is not a hard thing to do and it took about four days, and the reason it took four days is that the tide is in the way twice a day and a chain cannot be pulled through water that a man is standing in.**

The surveyor of about forty-four had a boy of about seventeen with a rod and about nine days of a notebook with no head on any page, which is unusual and which nobody on that foreshore has asked about and which is not a form and is not a column.

He came to the landward end in the middle of a month in Wolfmonth and stayed about nine days, and the wall went in over four days before the end of that month, and the stones came out of a barge and there were four men on them and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet stood on the shingle about nine paces off and did not speak to any of the four.

**What is on the foreshore at about the seventh hour is this. The wall. A notebook. A boy with a rod. A barge with the stone out of it. And a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow, standing at the wall, not blocking it and not on the other side of it.**

## Two

The surveyor asked her three questions and she answered all three and one of the answers was about the houses and not about the wall.

“**How many go along here,**” he said.

“**Nine hundred, near enough, and it is nine hundred in a week and it is about nine hundred in the wrong part of the day, and if you want a figure of men then a man of about thirty-four with a sheet has got one and he got it off me on his first visit, which was a good while back, and I gave it to him because he had a chain and I did not want a second visit.**”

“**And the houses.**”

“**Eleven. Four of them have a person in them. Seven of them do not.**”

“**And you are one of the four.**”

“**I am one of the seven,**” she said. “**I have a barrow and I am not anybody's keeper and I have never been asked and if you put that down as the keeper of a house I will say so on the sand and there will be about nine people here in a minute and they will back me and not because of me.**”

**And that is the whole of the exchange and it took about four minutes and the surveyor wrote two of the three answers down and did not write the third one and the boy of about seventeen with the rod copied both of the two and did not ask about the third.**

## Three

**And what came back to a town of about two thousand, four days off that foreshore, about a week after the wall went in, was a piece of paper with a seal on it that wants four things, and the four things are the three heads of a form that is about forty years old and a fourth that is newer.**

A place. A figure of men. A trade. And, at the foot, in a second hand, the name of the person who will be answerable for the road between the two ends.

**Nobody in that town is going to fill the fourth one in. That is not a decision anybody took. It is what happens when a piece of paper comes to an office that has three heads cut into it and a fourth head cut into it by a man who wanted four, and the office does the three and the fourth sits there, and a clerk of about nineteen in his second year looks at the fourth head and asks the one question nobody has asked.**

The question is not a good question and it is the ordinary question and it is asked in about four counties a week and it is the question that has no answer on any form in this kingdom: **who is to answer for it.**

## Four

A man of thirty-four with a sheet came back to that foreshore on the second day of a month nobody gives and he had the wall and the survey and a piece of paper with three heads filled in, and he did not bring a fourth.

**He stood on the landward side of the wall for about nine minutes and the woman with the barrow stood on the seaward side of it and neither of them said one word, and the wall was between them and the water came in over both of their feet at about the ninth hour, and about nine people on that foreshore watched two people stand on opposite sides of eleven paces of stone with the sea coming up and about four of the nine had an opinion and about none of the nine had said it out loud.**

What the man of about thirty-four with a sheet does with a wall and three filled heads and an empty fourth head is written down anywhere in either of the two weeks in which it happened, and he is not on any list, and there is no form in this kingdom on which a man is entered as the man who decided.

**And the surveyor of about forty-four took his chain off the sand at about the eighth hour and said, to nobody, that he had been asked for a figure of men and had been given one by a man who did not have it, and that is a thing a surveyor says on a beach once and does not say twice and he has not said it since and will not.**

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is the surveyor's and it is the only column in about four days that anybody kept, and the reason it was kept is that a wall of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end of a road that is under water twice a day is a thing an office has to be able to put a figure against, and a figure is nine pounds of stone and four men's time and about four days.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, a third pound of wood at three, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a quart of small beer at two, a bushel of oats at four, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a handcart hired for the day at four, a room at six, a second room with a fire in it at eight.** One, one, four, four, three, three, three, two, two, two, four, five, eight, four, six and eight is sixty pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and sixty pence is a mark and twelve over**, and a barge of stone is not in it and is four days of four men, and a figure of men is not in it and there is no figure of men in it, and about nine hundred people on two miles of hard sand are not in it and went home along it.

**And a wall of stone eleven paces long and about two feet high stands at a hundred paces from the landward end of a road that is there twice a day, and it was not there nine weeks ago, and it took four days and nine pounds of stone and a boy's rod and a chain, and a piece of paper came away from it with three heads filled in and a fourth head empty, and the fourth head is going to be the whole of the next four months, and nobody on that foreshore has written any of it down, which is not a decision anybody took and is not going to be, and the water came in at about the second hour of the afternoon and went over the wall and went over about two hundred paces of the seaward half and went back at about the eighth hour of the morning with the wind still against it.**
