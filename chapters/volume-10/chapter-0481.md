# Chapter 0481

**The twentieth day of Mudmonth, YR 319, a Friday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour, on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, at about a hundred paces from the landward end, where eleven paces of stone about two feet high have taken the water over them twice a day every day since the four days it took to lay, and a surveyor of about forty-four with a chain and a boy of about seventeen with a rod have come down that sand with four more men, and Aren Kest is standing in the middle of all of it as the guest of a man of about fifty-three with a wagon and is not a party to any part of it.**

## One

**A wall of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end of a road that is under water twice a day is a thing an office has to be able to say where it is, and saying where a thing is on a beach is the one job a chain has and the only job it has ever had.**

A chain is a hundred links and a ring at each end and a handle. It answers a single question. It says how far it is from the ring at this end to the ring at the other end, along whatever the two men holding it walk over.

**The sand turns in about nine places, and none of the nine is a boundary anybody keeps, and the ninth of them is three black stones a storm put there in a month nobody now living was born in, and the surveyor of about forty-four has come down to find out how many of the nine he can chain across without the chain lying.**

He has been on the sand four days out of nine. He has a notebook with no head on any page, which is unusual, and four people on that foreshore have noticed it and not one of them has asked him about it.

**And the sand does not stay where it was put, and so a chain run straight across one of the nine turns comes up with a longer answer than the ground under it, and he knows that, and he has been walking round the turns instead of across them, and that is why he is on the ninth day and not the fourth.**

## Two

The four men with him are not on the chain and are not on the notebook and are not there about the wall.

**A man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar, who has done four jobs in four counties and is the only man within four days of that shore who can cut a socket true in a kerb. A man of about twenty-six with a shovel, who is the son of the man who works the slipway at the landward end. A man of about fifty-one with a sledge. And a man of about thirty-one with a barrow of sand, who is not on the sand at all after the first hour and is filling a gap about two hundred paces seaward of the wall where the last tide took a foot out of the top course.**

Not one of the four is a knave and not one of them is a liar and not one of them has ever taken anything off that foreshore that was not his to take. They are four men doing what a road office does, which is to keep a footing in wet sand by putting other wet sand against it, and the reason they are there is that a stone wall on a beach has to be looked at and a man who looks at one has to bring somebody to shift the stones that have moved.

**A man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said one sentence at the second hour and did not say another until the sixth, and the sentence was that the far end had gone and the near end had not, and the boy of about seventeen with the rod wrote both halves of it down and did not ask which end was which.**

## Three

**Aren Kest is on that sand because a man of about fifty-three with a wagon said come down and stand where I can see you, and he said that he would, and he has been there about an hour and a half and has not been asked for anything by anybody.**

He has a stick in his right hand that he is not leaning on. He stands on a rise of shell nine feet above the wet, which is the only place on that stretch where a man of thirty-two with a limp can stand for an hour and a half and not be moved by it, and he moved to it on the second hour and has not moved since.

**The chain came over the sand he was standing on twice and the boy of about seventeen with the rod set the end of it within four feet of his boot both times, and neither time did anybody say anything to Aren Kest about it, and both times he was the only person on that sand who was not holding anything.**

He had been nineteen days on that coast. He had come down the made road four days and had walked the length of the sand twice and had been on the sand at the fourth hour of the morning nine times, which the man of about thirty-three with a barrow had noticed and a man of about twenty-three who rows one of the four boats had also noticed and neither of them had said so to him.

**And on this morning a man of about fifty-three with a wagon had come down with a load, had put it at the fourth of the eleven houses, had come back up, and had said come down and stand where I can see you, and Aren Kest had done it, and that is the whole of what he has done on that sand this morning and the whole of what he has done on that coast in nineteen days.**

## Four

**And the question the boy of about seventeen with the rod asked at the sixth hour is the one question about a chain that has been asked on that sand in about nineteen years, and it was asked to a man of about twenty-six with a shovel, and the man of about twenty-six with a shovel answered it because he did not think it was a hard one.**

“**What is it for,**” the boy said. “**I can see where the wall is. I do not need to know how far it is from here to there.**”

“**It is for the far one,**” the man of about twenty-six with a shovel said. “**The wall is not the thing. The wall is where somebody put stone. The thing is the hundred paces, and the hundred paces is a number, and a number has to be got somehow, and the only way anybody has ever got one is to walk it and count it and that is what that is for.**”

“**Then what is the rod for?**”

“**The rod is for whether the wall is straight across and whether the bottom of it is level, and the two of them are not the same question and a man who tries to do both with one of them gets one of them wrong.**”

**And that is a hundred and thirty-nine words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is twenty-five printed in two spans of four and twenty-one, the second is sixty-three printed in two spans of six and fifty-seven, the third is six, and the fourth is forty-five, and all four are printed, and the man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses was nine feet off and heard the whole of it and has said that it is the first time in nineteen years anybody on that sand has said out loud what a chain is for.**

Aren Kest heard it too. He did not say anything about it and nobody looked at him while it was being said.

**And then the man of about thirty-four with a sheet was not on that sand and was last there on a day nobody on it can name, and the surveyor of about forty-four wrote the boy's question in the notebook with no head on it, on the left-hand page, and the answer on the right, and neither page has anything above it.**

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is the surveyor's, and it is the second one he has kept in about nine days and he has not joined either of them up.

**a tallow candle at two, a loaf at one, a pound of wood at three, a second loaf at one, a quart of small beer at two, a pound of cheese at four, a room at six, a bed at four, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a pound of nails at three, a pound of rope at six, a handcart for the day at four, a broom at five, a pound of candles at four, a pair of gloves at two, a bushel of oats at four, a brush at two, an inkwell at three, a basket at five, a second night's bed at four, a second bushel of oats at four, a waxed cord at four, a knife at two, a second quill and ink at three, a pair of mitts at two, a second pound of candles at four, a hook at one.** Two, one, three, one, two, four, six, four, one, one, one, three, three, six, four, five, four, two, four, two, three, five, four, four, four, two, three, two, four and one is ninety-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and ninety-one pence is a mark and forty-three over**, and a sand that turns in about nine places is not in it, and eleven paces of stone about two feet high and a second footing course nine inches lower are not in it, and four men with a crowbar and a shovel and a sledge and a barrow are not in it and are not a figure of men, and about nine hundred people on two miles and about nine hundred paces are not in it and went home along it.

**And eleven paces of stone stood at a hundred paces from the landward end and took the water over it twice that day as it has twice a day since the four days it took to lay, and a chain came over the sand a man of thirty-two with a limp was standing on twice and stopped within four feet of his boot both times, and neither time was he asked for anything, and a boy of about seventeen with a rod wrote down what a chain is for and a man of about twenty-six with a shovel told him, and about four inches of sand is banked against the far side of that wall, and the water came in over that wall at about the ninth hour, went over about two hundred paces of the seaward half, and did not go back until the fourth hour of the morning, and the wind was still against it when it did.**
