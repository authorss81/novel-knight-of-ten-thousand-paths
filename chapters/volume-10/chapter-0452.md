# Chapter 0452

**The twenty-ninth day of Wolfmonth, YR 318, a Thursday and a market morning, and the third hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a taproom over a stable at a crossroads in a county about two hundred miles off a street of working yards, where about four hundred people come in off five villages and about nine carts stand in the yard and there is a woman of about fifty-one in the room who has come nine days up a made road, and a man of about twenty-nine is in it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is in it, and a woman of twenty-nine is not in it and did not come and has not been told that the woman is four days away.**

## One

**Nobody sent for her and she has not been asked anything yet and she is the only person in that room who came to it rather than being in it.**

**Marda Oakes** had come up nine days on a coach that stops at the crossroads twice a day, and she had a stick with a driftwood head and a bundle on it, and she had a piece of driftwood about the length of a forearm in the top of the bundle and there are notches cut in the edge of it.

There are twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it. The notches are not a record of anything anybody has asked her for.

She came because a man had come onto that foreshore twice since the middle of a month in Wolfmonth with a chain and a boy with a rod, and he had wanted three things, and the three things were a figure of men, a trade, and the name of the person who would answer for the road between the seaward end and the landward end. She could not give him the first of the three. She could give him the second and it was salt and it was not wrong. **And the third of the three, which is the name of a person, is the one thing on that shore that nobody has ever been able to produce, and she has known that for about twenty-two years and has never had to say it out loud to a man with a chain.**

She came to find out who was asking.

**And a taproom over a stable at a crossroads on a market morning is a place where four hundred people are and not a place where a question of that kind is asked in a low voice, and she asked it in an ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it, and two of the nine are in that room.**

## Two

“**I have come about the three things a man on a chain wants,**” she said. “**I can give him salt, because salt is what comes along that road and there are four families on that foreshore that boil it. He wanted a figure of men and I have not got one and I have not got a way of making one that would still be true in a year.**”

The man of about twenty-nine put his cup down.

“**Say the last of the three again,**” he said.

“**He wanted a name,**” she said, “**for the road. And I said I would find out who was asking before I said anything, and I have been on this road nine days and I have asked two men and one of them told me there is a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, and the man who told me had it off a man who told him, and I do not think either of them has been in it.**”

**That is sixty-seven words in one turn of one speech and it is the whole of what she said for the first hour, and the man of about twenty-nine counted them on his thumb without knowing he was doing it and told nobody and has not told anybody.**

“**You have been in it,**” said the man of thirty-two with a limp.

“**I have not,**” she said. “**I have been told there is a room that answers letters about things that have nobody's name on them, and I have been on nine days to find out whether the room exists.**”

## Three

The man of about twenty-nine showed her the letter and did not hand it to her, which was the correct thing to do and he did it by accident.

She could not read it. That came out in about four seconds and she did not apologise for it and nobody apologised for her.

A man of about twenty-nine read it aloud, which is a thing a man from a factor's table has watched other men do to a letter and has never himself done, and he read the whole of it including the second side, and the second side is a list of eleven items and every one of the eleven is a road somebody wants joined to a road somebody else is on.

**She counted the eleven on her fingers twice, both times with the same hand, and the second time she did not put the hand down afterwards.**

“**That is eleven,**” she said, “**and there are eleven houses on that foreshore and I have never once in twenty-two years had a piece of paper with the number eleven on it, and if you write eleven anywhere on that shore somebody in nine counties will be able to send for the eleventh house.**”

“**Nobody would send for a house,**” the man of about twenty-nine said.

“**They would send for a man in a house,**” she said, “**and there is a man in four of the eleven and there is nobody in seven of them and the seven are the ones that are used and the four are the ones that are owned, and that is the whole of what is wrong with your letter and I have known it since I was twenty-nine and I have never had anybody to say it to.**”

## Four

The man of thirty-two with a limp did not say anything for about four minutes, and the man of about twenty-nine counted that as well and did not tell him that he had.

**And what happens in a taproom on a market morning is that the man with the chain is not in it, and that the three people at the table are three people who have met each other once, and that the woman of fifty-one with a bundle has come nine days to ask a question that two hundred miles of road has not answered for her.**

The woman of about fifty-one said the thing that the next five years are going to be argued about on nine counties of coast, and she said it to a man of about twenty-nine, and she said it once, and she did not say it again for four days.

“**You are asking me for a keeper,**” she said, “**and I have been keeping that road for about twenty-two years without anybody asking me to, and I have never kept a day in my life. I read a tide off a piece of driftwood. If you put me in a column then somebody will want to know what time I start, and there is no time that I start, and there is a week in every month when I am asleep from the middle of one tide to the middle of the next one and there is nobody on that foreshore who would wake me for a wagon, and that is not a fault and it is not a liberty. It is what the water does.**”

She stopped there and about nine seconds later she said the kind thing, and the two of them are nine seconds apart and the second one is worse.

“**I am not saying I would not do it if the three of you asked me properly,**” she said, “**I am saying that you have written to a room instead of asking me, and there is a difference and I know what it is and so do you.**”

**And that is a hundred and seventy words in two turns of one speech and neither of the two turns repeats the other, and the second of the two is forty-six words and it begins about nine seconds after the first of the two ends, and the two sentences inside the second of the two are the two a man repeats to the person he tells it to afterwards, and there are about six people in that county who heard the whole of it and it went about nine counties at the speed a sentence goes.**

## Five

The cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man's and he has not had a coach fare in a column since a month in Longlight.

**a loaf at one penny, a bushel of oats at four, a bed on the road at two, a second bed on the road at two, a quart of small beer at two, a farrier at sixteen.** One, four, two, two, two and sixteen is twenty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and twenty-seven pence is under one by twenty-one**, and nine days of a woman's road is not in it and is carried at her own cost and is nine days, and a coach that stops twice a day at a crossroads is not the same road as the one she will go back along, and the twenty-two notches in a piece of driftwood are not in it either and are not a record of anything.

**And a man of about twenty-nine has now read his own second side aloud in a taproom over a stable in front of three people, one of whom has counted the eleven on her fingers twice, and he knows what the seven of the eleven are, and he has known since the seventh hour and has not said it, and about four hundred people were in that taproom and yard and market in the course of the morning and about nine of them heard the words *a figure of men* said out loud in an ordinary voice by a woman of fifty-one with a piece of driftwood in her bundle, and there is no sheet in nine counties that any of the nine could be got at on, and the four hundred people went home along about nine roads.**
