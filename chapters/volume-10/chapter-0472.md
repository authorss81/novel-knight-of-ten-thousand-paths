# Chapter 0472

**The seventh day of Thawmonth, YR 319, a Thursday and a market morning, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the ninth hour, at the back gate of a salt factor's yard in a market town of about two thousand people four days off a coast where a river runs into the sea, where nine people are standing in the yard waiting to be hired to load a boat and have been standing there since before the light, and a man of about twenty-nine who came up a made road four days with eight folded sheets has been in that town four days, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp has been in it five and has not said a word to anybody in the yard and is standing at the wall with a stick he is not leaning on.**

## One

**Loading is paid at six pence a day in that town and has been for as long as the man of about twenty-four who is hiring can remember, and the nine people in that yard know the figure better than the man hiring does, and the reason they know it is that it is the only figure in the trade that anybody has ever said out loud in a street.**

The man of about twenty-nine had been four days in that town with a question and had got, by the fourth day, nine pence a cart-load out of three ports and six pence a cart-load out of a book, and the three pence between them had been sitting in him for two days doing nothing.

He asked the man of about twenty-four to let him do the arithmetic in the yard, and the man of about twenty-four said he could do whatever he liked in the yard as long as it did not stop the loading, and then went and stood at the other end of it, which is the last thing a man in a hurry does for a stranger and the reason the stranger got the whole of it done.

## Two

**Nine cart-loads of shell a day off four boats into four pans, and about two hundred days a year when the water is off long enough, and three pence a cart-load between what a book says and what a man will pay for it, and that is the whole of the arithmetic and it takes nine seconds to say and about two years to be believed.**

He said the sum out loud in the yard, once, and then said it again because the man of about twenty-four had not moved.

“**Nine cart-loads a day at three pence, and about two hundred days, is five thousand four hundred pence a year,**” he said. “**And I have got that out of two numbers that nobody has ever put on the same sheet, and one of them is a price and the other one is a habit, and the difference between them is the whole of what a person on a shore is being paid and neither of them knows it.**”

**And that is seventy-six words in one turn of one speech printed in two spans, of which the first is twenty and the second is fifty-six, and nine people were standing in that yard when he said it and four of them were hired men with a boat to load before noon — and three of the nine have said since then that they heard a man count, and two of those three have said since then that what they heard was a man who had been counting for a long time.**

## Three

And then he said the part that was not arithmetic, and he said it to the yard and not to the man of about twenty-four, and he said it because he could not ask a question about a price without putting the other end of it on the ground.

“**There is a widow at the eleventh house on that sand with the last of a boat, and I did not ask her anything, and a man of about fifty-three who has driven a salt wagon down there for nineteen years asked her once and she told him what she lives on, and it is twenty pence a day.**”

“**Two hundred and seventy days is nine months. Two hundred and seventy days at twenty pence a day is five thousand four hundred pence. And that is what I have just said out loud in this yard about a cart-load of shell, and the two numbers are the same number, and I have been carrying a request for a permanent connection for a month and the whole of what that request would take out of the eleventh house on that coast is nine months of her winter, and it would not be taken by a man with a chain. It would be taken by a page.**”

**And that is a hundred and sixty-five words in two turns of one speech, of which the first is fifty-nine and the second is a hundred and six, and the yard did not go quiet, because a yard at the second hour of a market morning is the loudest place in that town, and four of the nine have said since then that they heard the first number and three of them have said since then that they did not hear the second, and the man of about twenty-four heard both and has not said what he thought of either.**

## Four

**And Aren Kest said the only thing he said in that yard, at about the fifth hour, to the man of about twenty-nine and not to the man of about twenty-four, and he had been in that town five days and had not opened his mouth in it once.**

“**You have found the cost of it,**” he said, “**and I am the man who asked for it, and I want to say the other half of it before you do, and then I am going to stop talking.**”

“**The cost of it is not the shell and it is not the widow and it is not three pence a cart-load. The cost of it is that there will be a page. A page is a thing with a hand on it, and the hand turns the leaves, and the leaves go in and come out, and a shore that has been keeping its own count in its own heads for twenty-two years has to start telling it to somebody; and it will tell it to a man with a book, and the man with a book will be the man who decides what a cart-load of shell is worth. Not on purpose. Because that is what a book is for.**”

**And that is a hundred and fifty-nine words in two turns of one speech, of which the first is thirty-seven and the second is a hundred and twenty-two, and it is the fourth time in about seven months that a man of thirty-two with a limp has said a thing out loud that he had been holding, and it is the first time he has said it before anybody else.**

The man of about twenty-nine did not answer him and wrote nothing down, which was the first thing in five days that the man of about thirty-two with a limp had got out of him, and the man of about twenty-four at the far end of the yard said that the loading was held up and that if the arithmetic was finished he would take the boat, and neither of the two men in the middle of the yard had noticed either of them.

## Five

The cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's and it is the second of them, and he has two of them folded separately in the inside of a coat and has still not joined them up, and a page is not a column and is not in this one.

**a handcart for the day at four pence, a basket at five, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a tallow candle at two, a room at six, a bed at four, a second night's bed at four, a quart of small beer at two, a second quart of small beer at two, a third quart of small beer at two, a fourth quart of small beer at two, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a fourth sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a second quill and ink at three, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a pound of rope at six, a waxed cord at four, a pound of nails at three, a pair of gloves at two, a second pair of gloves at two, a night's lodging at the made road at four, a handcart for the four days at four, a second handcart for the four days at four, a pound of candles at four, an inkwell at three.** Four, five, one, one, one, four, four, three, two, six, four, four, two, two, two, two, one, one, one, one, three, three, five, eight, six, four, three, two, two, four, four, four, four and three is one hundred and six pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and six pence is two marks and ten over**, and two figures of about nine hundred people are not in it, and five thousand four hundred pence a year is not in it and is not a wage anybody is paid, and nine months of a winter is not in it and is not a figure of men, and a page is not in it.

**And a man of about twenty-nine stood at the back gate of a salt factor's yard and said the same number twice in nine seconds in front of nine people who were waiting to be hired, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp said the half of it that was not arithmetic and then stopped talking, and a man of about twenty-four took his boat and went out of the yard and the nine people went with him, and three pence a cart-load is not a toll and is not a rate and nobody in that yard put it on anything, and about nine hundred people on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand went on getting across it four hours out of twelve, and a piece of driftwood at the seaward end has twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it, one for each year, the twenty-second not cut, and the water did not go off that mark on the first of Thawmonth and did not go off it since.**
