# Chapter 0453

**The third day of Frostmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the eighth hour of the morning and the eleventh hour and the second hour of the afternoon, on a foreshore where a river runs into the sea and the sea comes up over the road about twice a day, in a county four days off a crossroads where about four hundred people come in on a Thursday, and there are eleven houses along two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is on it, and a woman of about fifty-one is on it, and a man of about twenty-nine is on it and has been on it since the fourth hour.**

## One

**The road is there now and it will not be there again until the water goes out at about the eighth hour of the morning, and it comes in at about the second hour of the afternoon, and that is twice a day, and every one of the eleven houses knows it and not one of them has written it down.**

It is hard sand and broken shell, about two miles and about nine hundred paces at the lowest, and a loaded wagon can go one way at the bottom of the water and cannot come back the same tide. A barrow can go either way. A man on foot can go at any hour and gets his boots filled and says nothing about it.

**There is no line on it. A man who wants to know where it begins stands at the seaward end at low water and walks until the sand turns, and the sand turns in about nine places and none of the nine is a boundary anybody keeps, and the ninth of them is a line of three black stones that a storm put there in a month nobody now living can name.**

About nine hundred people use it. Four of the eleven houses belong to somebody and seven belong to nobody, and the seven are the ones that are used.

## Two

The woman of about fifty-one walked it with them and did not lead, and when the man of about twenty-nine asked her which end was the beginning she pointed at the landward end and said that was the end the cart came from.

“**I did not say the beginning,**” she said. “**I said the end the cart comes from. There is a beginning of it at about the eighth hour of the morning and another one at about the second hour of the afternoon and they are not in the same place, and that is not a trick and it is not a saying.**”

She got the stick out of the bundle and turned the driftwood over and showed him the edge of it, and there were twenty-two notches in it, and he counted them twice and got the same number and she said he was the first person in about nine years to count them and get it right.

**And then she said the thing that nine counties are going to argue about for five years, and she said it in the tone of a woman reading a number off a post.**

“**Twenty-two notches, one for each year, and the twenty-second is not cut yet, and it goes in when the water is off a mark on that post that has not been off in twenty-two years, and it was not off in the year my husband died and I got it wrong twice on a wagon and the wagon went into the channel, and nobody was on it, and I have not cut that notch and I am not going to, and that is the whole of the reason and it is not a tradition and it is not an office and nobody elected me.**”

## Three

**And a man of about thirty-four with a sheet was on that foreshore twice since the middle of a month in Wolfmonth, and the second time was on the last day of that month, and there is no connection between those two things and this day and nobody has looked for one.**

What came off the foreshore is a stone wall.

There is one footing course laid in wet sand at about a hundred paces from the landward end, and the stones are set in a line and are about a foot and a half long, and there is mortar in the joints and the mortar has cured, and the wall is eleven paces long and about two feet high and it is not a ruin and it is not old.

**A wall takes the tide twice a day like a post and it takes it exactly the same way, and at the fourth hour of the afternoon the sea comes over the top of it and goes back, and at the eighth hour of the morning it is dry and there is sand against the far side of it about four inches deep, and in four months that sand will be nine inches and in a year it will be a bank, and nobody has to do anything to that wall for it to stop being in the way.**

Nobody on that foreshore is unhappy about it and nobody has said a word about it, because a wall of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end is a wall at a hundred paces from the landward end, and the seven houses that belong to nobody are all of them past it.

## Four

The man of thirty-two with a limp got down onto the sand about four times and the third time he was not asked and he did not have to be.

**He has not got up a bank since the seventh day of a month in Longlight and he is not going to, and getting up off a wet sand is about four seconds longer than sitting down, and a woman of about fifty-one watched him do it twice and said nothing about it and then, on the third, said the only thing anybody said to him on that foreshore all day.**

“**You are looking at it the way a man looks at a sill,**” she said. “**I have watched about nine men do that and one of them was a mason and he was looking at whether the stones were bedded right. You are not going to tell me what you are looking for, because you have not decided, and I have got a road to open in about four hours and you are stood on it.**”

He said that was fair and stood off the sand.

**And it is the whole of what passed between them on the first day and about six people on that foreshore noticed the two of them standing about nine paces apart and not talking, and there is no word for what the two of them are that either of them has used in front of the other, and there has not been one since before either of them was on that sand, and it is not going to be given one on a beach.**

## Five

The cost of that Monday is a column and it is his and he has kept one on about four days in seven since a room in a township of about nine hundred people asked him to leave it.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a bushel of oats at four, a pound of wood at three, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a quart of small beer at two, a sheet of paper at one, a farrier at sixteen.** One, one, four, three, two, two, two, one and sixteen is thirty-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and thirty-two pence is under one by sixteen**, and a night in nine of the eleven houses is a penny or nothing and four of the nine took him and five did not and the five were not asked twice, and a man's bed on that foreshore is a bench in a room that is let by the night and the room has no keeper and the room is not a room anybody can be sent to.

**And there are two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand between a landward end and a seaward end and about nine hundred people on it and a wall of stone eleven paces long at a hundred paces from the landward end that a woman of about fifty-one walked past four times without looking at, and a man of about twenty-nine stood in the middle of the sand at about the seventh hour and did not write down the name of the foreshore because he has nowhere to write it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp got up off wet sand four times and was told about a sill by somebody who had watched nine men do it and one of them was a mason, and at the fourth hour of the afternoon the sea came over the wall and went back.**
