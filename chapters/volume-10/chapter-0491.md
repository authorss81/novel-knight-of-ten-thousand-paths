# Chapter 0491

**The eighth day of Longlight, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and broken shell where a river runs into the sea, at the landward end and about a hundred paces in from it, where there is no month in anybody's mouth on that shore and no day of the week, and about nine hundred people are on a sand that is there about four hours out of twelve and has been there about four hours out of twelve every tide since the middle of a month in Wolfmonth, and the last of four clauses about a wagon is being said out loud at two ends of that sand and cannot be said at the third.**

## One

**A wagon came off the sand at the ninth of eleven houses at about the fifth hour with a near wheel in the channel and it stood there while the water came up round the axle, and the man nearest it was a man of about thirty-three with a barrow and a load of glass in it, and he put the barrow down, and he got the wheel up, and it took him about as long as it takes a man to say nine, and about four hundred people watched him do it from about two hundred yards and about nine of them were close enough to have helped and did not.**

Nobody called him. Nobody paid him. And then, when he had the wagon up on the flat and the horses stood and the water had gone out of the channel, nobody asked him how it went.

**That is the fourth of four clauses, and it is the only one of the four that is a sentence about what happens afterwards, and it is the hardest of the four to carry, and on this tide it was carried at two ends of two miles and about nine hundred paces of sand and could not be carried at the third.**

“**How long have you been doing that?**” a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said to a man of about twenty-four who had been standing nine feet off with a rope he had not used.

“**Since I could walk,**” the man of about twenty-four said.

“**And how long has nobody asked you?**”

The man of about twenty-four thought about it for about as long as it takes a man to say four.

“**Always,**” he said. “**Nobody has ever asked me that. I would not know the answer to put in a book, because it is not one thing, it is about four hundred times.**”

**And that is forty-eight words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is seven, the second is four, the third is seven, and the fourth is thirty printed in two spans of one and twenty-nine, and the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said the first and the third without stopping and the man of about twenty-four said the second in four words and the fourth in two spans with an attribution between them, and nobody thanked her for asking and she did not want to be thanked, and the man of about twenty-four has not been asked it a second time and has not told anybody the four hundred.**

## Two

**And at about the eighth hour a woman of about fifty-eight with a slate came down the sand with four other people and a handcart, and one of the four was a man of about nineteen at a bar who was on that sand for the first time in his life and who said afterwards that he had thought there would be a line.**

There is no line. The sand turns in about nine places and none of the nine is a boundary anybody keeps and the ninth of them is three black stones a storm put there in a month nobody now living was born in, and about four hundred people crossed those three stones that tide and not one of them went round them.

“**Is this the road?**” the man of about nineteen said.

“**It is while the water is off it,**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said, and he was still standing nine feet off a wagon that was not his own, and he had not picked his barrow up.

“**And when the water is on it?**”

“**Then it is a place with water on it,**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said. “**That is the whole of what I know about it and I have been on it about nine years and I have never once been asked and I have never once had to say.**”

**And that is sixty-two words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is four, the second is eight, the third is seven, and the fourth is printed in two spans of nine and the rest of the forty-three, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow has a wagon in a channel nine feet off him that is not his and did not say a word about it, and the man of about nineteen at a bar was on that sand for the first time in his life and has been back nine times since and has not asked for a line.**

## Three

**And a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow was at the landward end at about the second hour saying the fourth clause out loud to anybody who asked, which is the only thing a room at the landward end is for, and she said it eleven times that tide and about four people asked and seven did not.**

The sentence is **nobody asks you how it went.**

“**Who says it at your end?**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said, at the fourth hour.

“**My room says it,**” she said, “**and it says the other three first, and it says them in the order it says them, and if you want the fourth you get the first three as well, and about four people want that and about nine do not.**”

“**And the room at the seaward end?**”

“**Says all four. Says them faster than mine does and has been saying them about nineteen years and has never once said the four to a room, because there is no room at that end, there is a woman and a post.**”

“**And the port four days off?**”

The woman of about thirty-four with a barrow stopped, which she does perhaps four times a year, and looked at the water for about as long as it takes a man to say nine.

“**It did not refuse,**” she said, “**and nobody there has said no to anything about this road in my hearing, and they cannot say it because a weigh-house is a room where about nine hundred people are paid twice in seven days, and the sentence says *nobody is paid*, and a man standing in that room cannot say four clauses out loud without making three of them a lie.**”

**And that is a hundred and seventy-three words in five turns of one exchange, of which the first is six, the second is printed in two spans of four and forty-one, the third is forty-nine printed in two spans of seven and forty-two, the fourth is six, and the fifth is printed in two spans of four and sixty-three, and the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow has said the half of it before, about four months ago, to a different man, and has not improved on it and does not want to, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow heard all of it and has not repeated any of it to anybody, and the port four days off is a port of about nine hundred people with four hundred yards of quay and a slipway and it is still standing there.**

## Four

**And about nine hundred people were on that sand between the second hour and the sixth hour and the sand did what it has done every tide since the middle of a month in Wolfmonth, which is to be there about four hours out of twelve, and for about eight hours out of twelve the seaward half of it is under about two hundred paces of water, and nobody on that sand put a figure on any of it and about four of the nine hundred would have said the wrong one if anybody had asked.**

Nine cart-loads of shell went off four boats into four pans. A woman of about fifty-one was at the seaward end at about the fourth hour, and what she had in her two hands was a forearm's length of driftwood and no thicker than a man's thumb, and it was wedged in a square driven post and she read the post and went away, and the twenty-second notch is not cut and the water has not been off that mark in twenty-two years and it did not go off it on the eighth of a month carried at thirty in this year.

A man of about fifty-three with a wagon came down on the ebb, stood about two hours, and came back on the flood, and about nine hundred people went across both ways of him and about nine of them counted his sacks out loud for him and he sent no boat back empty and told nobody why.

**Aren Kest was four hundred yards up the sand at the fifth hour and at the eighth hour and was the hundred and fourth person over that sand that day, and the hundred and fourth is not the first, and he was asked nothing, and he said nothing that anybody repeated.**

## Five

The cost of that tide is a column and it is the man of about fifty-three's, and it is the third one he has written in nineteen years and it is on the back of a handcart docket with a nail, and about nine hundred people and eleven houses and four clauses are not in it.

**a new broom at five, a pound of rope at six, tar and shoeing at six, a night's lodging at the fourth house at two, a second night's lodging at the fourth house at two, a quill and ink at three, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a pound of candles at four, a handcart for the day at four, a farrier for the near horse at three, a stop for a hoof at three, a bushel of oats at four, a second bushel of oats at four, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a fourth loaf at one, a bucket of tar at eight, a pair of mitts at two, a second pair of mitts at two, a waxed cord at four, a hook at three, an inkwell at three, a basket at five, a broom at five, a second broom at five, a pail of nails at three, a bucket of lime at six, a jar of grease at four, a saddle strap at three, and a second saddle strap at three.** Five, six, six, two, two, three, one, one, two, two, four, four, three, three, four, four, four, four, one, one, one, one, eight, two, two, four, three, three, five, five, five, three, six, four, three and three is one hundred and twenty pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and twenty pence is two marks and twenty-four over**, and four clauses are not in it and are not a rate, and nobody is paid in it and nobody was paid, and about nine hundred people on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand are not in it and are not a figure of men, and three black stones a storm put there in a month nobody living was born in are not in it.

**And a wagon stood in a channel at the ninth of eleven houses on the eighth day of a month carried at thirty in this year with a near wheel in the water, and a man of about thirty-three with a barrow and a load of glass in it put the barrow down and got the wheel up in about as long as it takes a man to say nine, and about four hundred people watched and about nine of them were close enough to have helped, and nobody called him and nobody paid him and nobody asked him how it went, and at the other end of that sand a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said the sentence about that out loud eleven times, and at the port four days off a man in a weigh-house cannot say it and has not said he cannot, and the water came over the seaward half at about the ninth hour and took about two hundred paces of it, and it was off the sand again before four in the morning and about nine hundred people were on it again on the tide after that.**
