# Chapter 0479

**The fourteenth day of Mudmonth, YR 319, a Saturday and a market day, and the third hour and the seventh hour, at the seaward end of a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, and there is no month in anybody's mouth on that shore and no day of the week, and Aren Kest is standing at the ninth of eleven houses with a stick he is not leaning on doing an addition out loud, and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow has come up nine hundred paces to tell him what his addition is for.**

## One

**He has been doing an addition for two days and it is not about money and it has nothing to do with the sheet of nine inches by six that is in the inside pocket of his coat and that he has not shown anybody.**

What he is adding is the days.

He said that the ninth of the eleven had not bought salt or shell for nine weeks, and that a wagon year is about two hundred days and is twenty-seven pence a day the wrong way, and he said the figures out loud on that sand the way a man reads a line off a board.

“**Nine weeks is about a sixth of a year, and a wagon year is five thousand four hundred pence the wrong way, and a sixth of five thousand four hundred is nine hundred,**” he said, “**and nine hundred pence is forty-five days of the winter of a woman at the eleventh house, at twenty pence a day. That is the sum and that is all of it.**”

**And that is sixty-five words in one turn of one speech printed in two spans of thirty-three and thirty-two, and four people on that sand heard it and one of them had heard the other half of it nine days before, and the piece of driftwood in the post at the seaward end has twenty-two notches cut in the edge of it, one for each year, the twenty-second not cut, and it goes in when the water goes off a mark that has not been off in twenty-two years, and the water did not go off that mark on the first of Thawmonth and has not gone off it since.**

## Two

**And then the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow told him the other half, and she did not ask him for it and she had walked nine hundred paces in the mud to give it away.**

“**You have been on this sand nine days,**” she said, “**and you have not asked me a single question since the sixth hour of the day you came down, and I have come up nine hundred paces to tell you that you are the reason the ninth house has not bought for nine weeks, and I am not going to be gentle about it because there is nobody else here to be gentle to.**”

“**I have not said anything to the ninth house.**”

“**You said a thing at the landward end about a man coming in the spring,**” she said, “**and you said it in a voice that came from about forty miles away, and a carter heard it and the carter is in that house four days in ten. And then a man of about thirty-four with a sheet came down this sand a fifth time with a different man, and about four of us saw it, and the ninth house has a man in it who is a careful man. He stopped buying to find out whether the sand would still be here when the spring came.**”

## Three

**And that is a hundred and eighty-five words in three turns of one exchange, of which the first is seventy-two printed in two spans of eight and sixty-four, the second is nine, and the third is a hundred and four printed in two spans of fifteen and eighty-nine, and the whole of the exchange was printed on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long with nine people inside nine feet of it and a wind off the water.**

Aren Kest did not say that it was not his fault, and he did not say that he had been careful, and he did not say that a man who speaks once on a sand is not a keeper of anything.

**What he said was that he had said a sentence about eleven paces of stone and a figure of men on a sheet of paper with a fourth head on it that nobody had filled in, and that it had been sixty-four words, and that he had counted them on the made road in about four days, and that he had not thought once in nine days that a sentence could do that, and that he had been wrong about that and would not be arguing.**

“**I said it about a wall,**” he said. “**I said it four days up a made road and I said it to one man and it was four days up a made road. I did not say it to the ninth house and I did not know there was a ninth house.**”

## Four

**And the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said the thing that was not about him, and it is the reason any of this is the shape it is in, and she said it in ten words and then did not say it again.**

“**A man who talks about a keeper is a keeper,**” she said.

“**Is the fourth head still empty?**” she said.

“**It is,**” he said.

“**Good,**” she said, “**and I am not going to be thanked for this and you are not going to be either, and I would have said that in about four days if you had not said it out loud first.**”

**And that is fifty-six words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is ten, the second is six, the third is two, and the fourth is thirty-eight printed in two spans of one and thirty-seven, and the ten words are the whole of what nineteen years of a road nobody owns has come to and the woman who said them has not been thanked for them and has not been thanked since, and the fourth head is empty and is still empty.**

**And four people on that sand heard the whole of it, and a man of about fifty-three with a wagon was at the seventh of the eleven and did not hear it and was not told, and the woman of about fifty-one at the seaward end was not told and does not want to be told, and a woman of sixty-three at the back of the fourth house was not told, and Aren Kest has not written any of it down and has not asked anybody to.**

## Five

The cost of that Saturday is a column and it is Aren Kest's, and he wrote it standing up against a post with a nail, and it is the second one he has kept and he does not know what it is for, and sixty-four words and nine weeks and nine hundred pence are not in it and cannot go in it.

**five loaves at one penny, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, four pounds of wood at three, two tallow candles at two, three quarts of small beer at two, a room at six, a bed at four, a handcart for the four days at four, a broom at five, a second broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a second bucket of tar at eight, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a second quill and ink at three, a pound of nails at three, a pair of gloves at two, a second pair of gloves at two, a waxed cord at three, an inkwell at three, and a pound of candles at four.** One, four, four, three, two, two, six, four, four, five, five, eight, eight, one, one, one, three, three, three, two, two, three, three and four is eighty-two pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and eighty-two pence is a mark and thirty-four over**, and a man of thirty-two with a limp, sixty-four words, nine weeks, nine hundred pence and about nine hundred people are not in it, and none of them is a figure of men, and the fourth head of anything is not in it and is still empty.

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp was told by a woman who is not a knight that he had been the reason a wagon had not run for nine weeks, and he did not argue, and he got nothing for it, and he is not the keeper of the ninth house or the seventh or the sand, and about nine hundred people went on getting across two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand four hours out of twelve, and the water came in at about the ninth hour and went over about two hundred paces of the seaward half and went back at about the fourth hour of the morning with the wind still against it.**
