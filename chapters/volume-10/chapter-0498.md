# Chapter 0498

**The twenty-third day of Rainmonth, YR 319, a Sunday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the tenth hour, on that sand, at the landward end and a hundred paces in from it, and then at the fourth of eleven houses and at the ninth and at the eleventh, and there is no month in anybody's mouth on that shore and no day of the week, and a thing has been standing up for about two days with eleven houses in it and no keeper and no crown and no office and no register of persons, and a rule about the tide in it, and no fixed day.**

## One

**What happened is that about nine hundred people carried on crossing a road of hard sand that is there about four hours out of twelve, and the four of eleven houses with somebody's work happening in them were open, and that is a thing that has been true for about nineteen years, and the only thing that is new is that three rooms have each said it out loud, once, on their own tide, in their own words, and have not said it together and have not fixed a day to say it again.**

There is no meeting. There was no room. Nobody called anybody. A woman of about thirty-four who carried a sheet down that sand and up a made road put the sheet on a flat stone at the landward end at about the second hour on the twenty-first of a month carried at thirty and went away, and the sheet is still there, and about four hundred people have read it and about nine hundred have walked past it.

**And what is on it is a place, and a figure of houses, and a trade, and a word in a fourth head, and the fourth head is not a name and the third head is salt and glass and grain and preserved fruit and four boats, and none of the four is a person and no person is anywhere on it.**

“**Is somebody keeping this?**” a woman of about twenty-nine with a basket said, at about the sixth hour, at the fourth of eleven houses, to nobody, and about four people heard her and about nine did not.

“**No,**” a woman of about sixty-three said, from the back of the fourth house, where she had been since the fourth hour with a bowl of brine in her hand and a fire behind her that has not been out in forty-one years.

“**Then who opens it when it shuts?**”

“**It shuts when the water comes,**” the woman of sixty-three said, “**and it opens when the water goes, and neither of those is a person, and I have been sitting behind a pan for forty-one years and I have not opened anything and I have not shut anything and I would like that written down somewhere and I have just said it to about four people on a sand and that will have to do.**”

“**And the sheet?**” the woman of about twenty-nine with a basket said.

“**The sheet is a sheet,**” the woman of sixty-three said. “**It is not a keeper and it is not a door and it is not a lock. It is a sheet on a stone, and it will be a sheet on a stone in about four months, and if the water takes it then it is gone and the four of us who said the four things out loud will still have said them.**”

**And that is a hundred and fifty-four words in seven turns of one exchange, of which the first is four, the second is one, the third is seven, the fourth is seventy printed in two spans of six and sixty-four, the fifth is three, the sixth is five, and the seventh is sixty-four, and the woman of sixty-three has been sitting behind a pan for forty-one years and has now said a thing out loud about opening and shutting a thing for the first time in that time, and she is not thanked and did not ask to be and the woman of about twenty-nine with a basket did not thank her.**

## Two

**And the rule about the tide is the only rule there is, and it can be said in thirty-four words, and about four people said those thirty-four words on that sand that tide and about nine hundred people crossed the sand without hearing any of them, and the thirty-four words are not on the sheet and they are on about four mouths in four counties.**

The rule is that **the water is the only thing that decides anything on that coast, and it decides twice a day, and it is not paid, and it does not attend anything, and it cannot be asked.**

“**Then it is not a rule,**” a man of about thirty-three with a barrow said, at about the seventh hour, at the ninth of eleven houses, where a wagon was standing with its near rim cut down twice and a man holding a lease was in a doorway. “**A rule is a thing that a person can break.**”

“**Then it is not a rule,**” the woman of thirty-four who carried the sheet said, who had come down that sand a second time in about two days for a reason she has not given, “**and it is the only thing on this coast that is not a rule, and about nine hundred people have been living by it for about nineteen years, and if you would like a thing on a sheet that a person can break then you will want a person, and I have come down here a second time to tell you what happens next and it is the last time I come down.**”

“**What happens next?**” the man of about thirty-three with a barrow said.

“**Next,**” the woman of thirty-four who carried the sheet said, “**is that the three rooms each say what they have said, on their own tides, in their own words, and nobody ever says it in the same place twice, and there is no day, and if a person wants to know what the coast's terms are they can go and stand on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and wait about four hours out of twelve and read a sheet on a stone, and if the sheet is gone they can ask four people and about four of those four will not remember it exactly and that is the arrangement.**”

**And that is two hundred and three words in six turns of one exchange, of which the first is six, the second is ten, the third is six, the fourth is seventy-three, the fifth is three, and the sixth is a hundred and five printed in two spans of one and a hundred and four, and the man of about thirty-three with a barrow put the first six words on the flat in front of the ninth of eleven houses and about four people heard them and about nine hundred crossed and the two figures are not added, and the woman of thirty-four who carried the sheet has been down that sand twice in about two days and has said she will not come a third time and nobody has asked her to.**

## Three

**And what the man of about thirty-four from the port four days off did with two letters written on a sheet with no head on it, and what the man of about thirty-two with a limp did about nine sentences he said in a salt-store, and what the three rooms do about a form with a fourth head empty in a market town four days up, are three different facts and none of them is the same fact and about nine people on that sand between the second hour and the tenth hour said a version of that out loud in about four minutes.**

A man of about fifty-three with a wagon said that a thing with four of eleven houses in it and no person in it is not a thing that can be done by accident, and that it was done by accident, and that both of those are true and about four people heard him say it and about four did not.

A woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said that it is a thing that was done by a person and that the person has a name and that the name is on about four mouths in four counties and is on a sheet on a stone and is in about four mouths, and that it is not on any form and that if it were on a form there would be a hand that filled it in.

A man of about forty-one who stands in the doorway of the seventh of the eleven houses said nothing, and he has said nothing on that sand for about two months, and about nine people have noticed.

“**Do not put it on a form,**” a man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said, at about the ninth hour, from nine hundred yards off, and he was not asked and he has not been asked and he came nine hundred yards on a sand that is there about four hours out of twelve to say seven words.

“**What if somebody comes with one?**” the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said.

“**Then they will have a form with a fourth head on it and a name in the fourth head, and the name will be of somebody, and the somebody will be a person, and about four of us will find out within about four days and none of us will be able to say where it was filled in,**” the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar said, “**and that is what a form is. A form is a thing that finds out.**”

**And that is eighty-seven words in three turns of one exchange, of which the first is seven, the second is six, and the third is seventy-four printed in two spans of fifty-nine and fifteen, and about four people heard the third turn entire and about nine heard the first, and the man of about thirty-eight with a crowbar has been shoring the landward face of eleven paces of stone for about three weeks and has never been asked what the wall is for and said the sentence about a form to nobody and has not repeated it.**

## Four

**And there is no keeper and no crown and no office and no register of persons, and nobody has written anybody's name on anything on that coast, and the four of eleven houses that have somebody's work happening in them and the seven that do not are two figures and are never added to eleven in a column, and the sheet on the stone at the landward end has four heads on it and none of the four is a person.**

A press in a room over a weigh-house about two hundred miles up a made road stands open and nobody will give a date for it. Seven things are written in that room's register and not one of the seven is a finding, and all seven are questions. Nine things went into the day's book and nobody asked for any of them. That office sends out about nine hundred sheets a month, and about a hundred and four of them travel together on the fixed day, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls arrive, and none of those three series is the sum of another or is turned into another, and about eleven hundred people are on no roll and no sheet and no list and nothing in this year has put them on anything and a thing with eleven houses in it on a foreshore is not that list and does not become that list.

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp was about two hundred paces seaward of the sheet on the stone at about the sixth hour on the twenty-third of a month carried at thirty in this year, and it was his first day on that sand in about five days and he came down the made road on the morning of it, and he read it and he did not touch it and he was not asked to and he did not offer, and he is not the reason it is there, and about four people on that sand would tell you that a man of thirty-two read a sheet on a stone and did not touch it, and that is the whole of what he did.**

## Five

The cost of that Sunday is a column and it is the woman of sixty-three's, and it is the first one she has ever kept, and she kept it on the back of a fish slat with a nail, and eleven houses and four of eleven and seven of eleven are not in it.

**a room for a night at the back at six, a second room for a night at six, a third room for a night at six, a pallet at four, a second pallet at four, a third pallet at four, faggots for the pan at three, a second bundle of faggots at three, a third bundle of faggots at three, a rushlight at two, a second rushlight at two, a pound of candles at four, bread at one, a second bread at one, a third bread at one, a fourth bread at one, a pound of bacon at four, a second pound of bacon at four, a quarter of salt at four, a second quarter of salt at four, a pair of sheets washed at four, a second pair of sheets washed at four, a scrubbing brush at five, a second scrubbing brush at five, a barrel of brine for the pan at eight, a coil of line at six, a nail for the sheet at three, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a stub of chalk at three, a second stub of chalk at three, a pail of water at two, a second pail of water at two, a wick at five, a bundle of kindling at four, a second bundle of kindling at four, a tinderbox at three, a pair of scissors at two, a second pair of scissors at two, a mending basket at four, a second mending basket at four, a farthing at two, a second farthing at two, a third farthing at two, and a last nail at three.** Six, six, six, four, four, four, three, three, three, two, two, four, one, one, one, one, four, four, four, four, four, four, five, five, eight, six, three, one, one, three, three, two, two, five, four, four, three, two, two, four, four, two, two, two and three is one hundred and fifty-one pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and fifty-one pence is three marks and seven over**, and eleven houses are not in it and are never added to four of eleven and seven of eleven in a column, and a word in a fourth head is not in it and is not a name, and four clauses about a wagon are not in it and are not a rate, and about four hours out of twelve is not in it.

**And on the twenty-third day of Rainmonth in this year there was a thing on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand with eleven houses in it and no keeper and no crown and no office and no register of persons and a rule about the tide in it and no fixed day, and it was made of a sheet on a stone at the landward end and thirty-four words said by about four mouths in four counties, and a woman of sixty-three at the back of the fourth of eleven houses said out loud for the first time in forty-one years that she has not opened anything and has not shut anything, and a man of about fifty-three with a wagon said it was done by accident and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said it was done by a person and neither of them was thanked, and a man of about thirty-two with a limp read the sheet and did not touch it, and the water came over the seaward half at about the ninth hour and took about two hundred paces of it and was off the sand again before four in the morning.**
