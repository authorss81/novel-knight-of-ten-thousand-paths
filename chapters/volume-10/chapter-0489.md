# Chapter 0489

**The first day of Longlight, YR 319, a Thursday and a market day, and the third hour and the seventh hour and the tenth hour, on a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, at the seaward end and about two hundred paces short of it, where a month carried at thirty has begun at midnight and nobody on that sand has noticed a month begin in about nine years, and a market runs on two days in seven in a town of about two thousand people four days up a made road and this is one of them, and about nine hundred people are about to go across the best month of the year on sand that is there about four hours out of twelve and find out that nothing has changed.**

## One

**Longlight is the month in which boundaries are sworn, and on the first of it, in a field about four miles off a made road in a fen county, a man with a mallet and a chisel puts a figure into a hedge-stone and nine people stand in a wet field and hear it read out, and that is what a month is for in four counties, and on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand there is nothing to swear because there is nothing there that anybody keeps.**

The sand turns in about nine places. None of the nine is a boundary anybody keeps.

The ninth of them is three black stones a storm put there in a month nobody now living was born in, and four people on that sand have known about those three stones for about twenty years and about nine hundred had never heard of them, and the three of them are not a boundary and are not going to be sworn this month or any other month and nobody is going to carve anything into them.

**And a boundary oath-stone four miles off a made road is missing, and has been missing since about the first of a month in a year a woman of about thirty-two with a limp did not notice, and no second swearing has been set for it, and nobody in nine counties has sent anybody to look for it, and a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long that is under water twice a day has nothing to do with it and is not going to be told about it.**

## Two

**And what about nine hundred people do on the first of a month in the best month of the year on a road that has no line on it is exactly what they did yesterday and exactly what they will do tomorrow, and about four of them had worked that out before the third hour and about nine hundred worked it out between the third hour and the seventh hour without anybody saying a word.**

Aren Kest came down on the first working tide of the month and went back up the made road at the eleventh hour with nine hundred paces of hard sand in his boots and nothing in his hands, and he was the twentieth person over that sand that day, and the twentieth is not the first, and he did not say so to anybody.

Nine cart-loads of shell went off four boats into four pans on the ebb and the man of about fifty-three with a wagon counted every one of them and sent no boat back empty and told nobody why, and he was on that sand at the third hour and at the seventh hour and nine people saw him do the count both times and about nine hundred were on the sand at the same time and neither of the two facts touched the other.

A woman of about fifty-one was at the seaward end at about the fourth hour with a piece of driftwood about the length of a forearm and two inches through, wedged in a driven post five inches square, and she looked at the mark on that post for about as long as it takes a man to say nine, and she went away, and the twenty-second notch is not cut.

**And the water did not go off that mark on the first of a month in this year and it did not go off it on the twenty-ninth day of the month before, and it has not gone off it in twenty-two years, and the twenty-two notches in the edge of that driftwood are years and not tides and there is no other figure for them and nobody has ever asked her for one.**

## Three

**And the only thing anybody said out loud on that sand about the first of a month in that year was said by a woman of about twenty-seven with a basket at about the seventh hour, and it was fourteen words, and it was about a stone, and four people heard it and none of the nine hundred heard it.**

“**It is the first of a month and there is nothing on here to swear,**” she said, “**and that is the third time I have said it and I only say it because it is the first and I have nothing else to do with the first.**”

“**You said it the first time to a man with a chain,**” a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said.

“**I said it the first time to nobody and a man with a chain heard me,**” the woman of about twenty-seven with a basket said, “**and the second time I said it to a man who was not here, and the third time I am saying it to you, and you are the fourth person I have said it to, and none of the four of you has done anything about it, and I am not asking.**”

**And that is a hundred and twenty-five words in three turns of one exchange, of which the first is forty-five printed in two spans of fifteen and thirty, the second is twelve, and the third is sixty-eight printed in two spans of sixteen and the rest of the sixty-eight, the last of those two spans being a figure that is not set down, and the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow is not going to be thanked for having been the fourth person and has not been thanked for being the first, and neither of them has said her want and neither of them was thanked, and a woman of sixty-three at the back of the fourth house was not told and did not ask and put a fifth armful of wood on a fire that has not been out in forty-one years.**

## Four

**And eleven paces of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end took the water over it twice on the first of a month in that year, exactly as it has twice a day since the four days it took to lay, and four hundred yards of sand between that wall and the eleventh of eleven houses has not got a line on it, and the chain that was run across that sand on a Friday went slack in the middle twice and was not rolled up and is not on that sand now.**

Nobody has put a name in the fourth head of a form with three heads filled in, and nobody has said that anybody has put one in anywhere else, and nobody has taken up an offer of one sentence about the wall that was made in about a hundred and ninety words in three turns on a sand four days up a made road, and there is no list and no roll and no meeting and no room on that foreshore and there never has been.

**And a surveyor of about forty-four with a chain was not on that sand on the first of a month in that year, and a boy of about seventeen with a rod was not on it either, and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet was not on it and has not been asked why he comes when he comes and has not said, and none of those three absences is a decision anybody took and none of them is scheduled.**

## Five

The cost of that Thursday is a column and it is the woman of about twenty-seven's, and it is the first one she has ever kept and she kept it on the back of a fish slat with a nail, and fourteen words about a stone are not in it and cannot go in it.

**a second broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a pound of rope at six, a second night's bed at four, a waxed cord at four, a pound of candles at four, a second pound of candles at four, a room at six, a pair of gloves at two, a handcart for the day at four, a basket at five, a brush at two, a knife at two, a tin of oil at two, a second tin of oil at two, a mallet at three, a second mallet at three, a farrier at three, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, an inkwell at three, a second quill and ink at three, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a third sheet of paper at one, a bed at four, a bushel of oats at four, a second room with a fire in it at eight, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a quart of small beer at two, a second quart of small beer at two, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a pound of nails at three, a broom at five, a hook at three.** Five, eight, six, four, four, four, four, six, two, four, five, two, two, two, two, three, three, three, three, three, three, three, one, one, one, four, four, eight, two, two, two, two, four, four, three, five and three is a hundred and twenty-seven pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and a hundred and twenty-seven pence is two marks and thirty-one over**, and twenty-two notches and a mark that has not been off in twenty-two years are not in it and are not tides, and about nine hundred people on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand are not in it and are not a figure of men, and a first of a month and a fourth of eleven and seven of eleven are not in it and are never added to eleven in a column.

**And a month carried at thirty began on that coast at midnight and about nine hundred people went across the best month of the year on sand that was there about four hours out of twelve and found out that nothing had changed, and eleven paces of stone at a hundred paces from the landward end took the water over it twice, and four hundred yards of sand between that wall and the eleventh of eleven houses still has no line on it, and there was nothing on two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand to swear this month and there is not going to be anything to swear next month, and the sea came in at about the ninth hour and went over about two hundred paces of the seaward half and did not go off the mark on the post at the seaward end, and it was off the sand again before four in the morning, and the twenty-second notch is not cut.**
