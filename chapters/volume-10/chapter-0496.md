# Chapter 0496

**The twenty-first day of Rainmonth, YR 319, a Friday and not a market day, and the second hour before light and the sixth hour and the eleventh hour, in one low room whose beams come down to about four feet above a stone floor and which has a single door, behind a weigh-house at the landward end of a foreshore two miles and about nine hundred paces long where a river runs into the sea, in a port of about nine hundred people, where there is no month in anybody's mouth and no day of the week, and nine people are in that building and a man of about thirty-two with a limp is one of them and he was asked the question at about the sixth hour and he answered it at about the sixth hour and it took him nine sentences.**

## One

**The question was whether his road and their history can be made into one account, and it was asked by the woman of about thirty-four who carried the sheet four days earlier and came back up the made road and down again on a day she was not paid for, and about nine people in that building heard it, and three of them had wanted to ask it for about four days and had not.**

Aren Kest had a stick he was not leaning on. He had been in that building twice before in eleven days and had sat on a bundle of rope both times, and he was on the bundle this time, and about four feet of headroom is not a place to stand up in if you are deciding something.

“**Can your road and our history be one account?**” the woman of about thirty-four who carried the sheet said.

He did not answer for about as long as it takes a man to say nine. The man of about thirty-four from the port four days off counted it and told two people afterwards that it was nine seconds and that he counted them twice because he did not believe the first nine.

“**No,**” Aren Kest said, and he said it in the accent of that shore, and he knew he was saying it in that accent, and about four of the nine people in that building heard a man from about four hundred miles off say one word in a voice that about four hundred people on a coast speak every day and that nobody in that room had heard him use before.

“**Say it again,**” the man of about thirty-four from the port four days off said, not unkindly.

“**I will say it again,**” Aren Kest said, “**and it will be in the same voice, and I want to say that first so that nobody has to wonder about it afterwards.**”

**And that is forty-two words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is nine, the second is one, the third is three, and the fourth is twenty-nine printed in two spans of five and twenty-four, and the man of about thirty-four from the port four days off asked for it twice and got the same word twice and did not ask a third time, and about four of the nine people in that building noticed the voice and about five of the nine did not and the two groups did not overlap at all.**

## Two

**And then he said the reason, and the reason is not a principle, and it is nine sentences, and about nine people in a building with a stone floor heard every one of them and about four of the nine wrote nothing down and about four of the nine did not have a thing to write with.**

“**I will tell you why, and it is not a principle, and I have had about four days to get it down to nine sentences and it is nine and I have not cut one of them for length.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**Your history is twenty-two notches cut in the edge of a piece of driftwood, and the twenty-second is not cut, and that count is in years and it is not in tides and it is not in days.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**Nobody has ever asked you to put it in days and nobody is going to.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**My history is a road with a beginning, and the beginning is a day, and a day is a thing I can be found on.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**There are about nine hundred sheets a month going out of a room two hundred miles up a made road with a date at the top of every one of them, and about a hundred and four of those go out in one band on a fixed day, and those are two counts and I am not going to add them here.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**If those two are made into one account then one of them has to be on a day and the other one has to be on a year, and a man reading that account is going to count the notches as if they were days because he has a date in his hand.**”

“**Go on.**”

“**The moment one account has a keeper the keeper can be asked, and the asking is a hand on a door, and there is a thing behind that door about a woman of fifty-one and a post of driftwood, and you have spent twenty-two years making a thing nobody can be got at.**”

“**I am about to hand it over in a sheet of paper because I would like to know what happened in the year you were sixteen.**”

“**I do not know what happened in the year you were sixteen and neither do you, and that is not a reason to keep it, it is the whole of the reason.**”

“**So no, and the twenty-second notch is not cut and it will not be cut by me, and I have not come down this sand to have a no written down anywhere.**”

“**Then why are you here?**” the man of about sixty with a slipway said.

“**Because the tide was out and I am on it,**” Aren Kest said, “**and because I would rather say no to nine people in a room than have nine people find it out on a beach in about four months, and that is the last of the nine sentences and I have said all nine.**”

**And that is four hundred and forty-four words in eighteen turns of one exchange, of which the first is thirty-nine and is the preamble and is not one of the nine, the nine reasons are thirty-eight, fifteen, twenty-five, sixty-two, fifty-three, fifty-three, twenty-six, thirty-two and thirty-two in that order, there are six two-word turns of *go on* between the first six of them and none between the last three, the seventeenth turn is a question of five words, and the eighteenth is printed in two spans of ten and forty-two, and every one of the nine can be counted on one hand and none of the nine is a principle, and about nine people in that building heard the whole of it and four of the nine would tell you tomorrow that it was the best thing they have heard a man say about a road, and four of the nine would tell you tomorrow that it was a man protecting a woman of fifty-one who never asked him to, and both of those are the same sentence pointed in two directions and neither of the four has read anything about her in four hundred years of records in nine counties.**

## Three

**And the woman of about thirty-four who carried the sheet did not argue with him, and the man of about thirty-four from the port four days off did not argue with him, and the man of about sixty with a slipway said one thing about it at about the tenth hour and it was not about the argument.**

“**The notches are not mine,**” the woman of about thirty-four who carried the sheet said, at about the sixth hour. “**I have never seen that post. I have carried a sheet down a sand and up a made road and I have said a word out loud that a woman of fifty-one says every day of her life and I did not know I was carrying it until a man of thirty-eight said five words nine hundred yards off. If your no is about that woman then your no is about a thing you have not met, and I have not met her either, and I have been the carrier of a coast's terms for four days and I would like to know what I am carrying.**”

“**You are carrying a word,**” Aren Kest said, “**and the word is right, and it is the right word, and I have heard it in that voice and out of it and both of those are true, and if I had not said no in that voice you would have had no way at all of knowing that the word and the voice belong to the same people.**”

The woman of about thirty-four who carried the sheet looked at him for about as long as it takes a man to say four.

“**That is the first true thing anybody has said in that building today,**” she said, “**and I do not thank people, and you are not owed anything, and I am going to say the rest of it out loud once so that it is in the room.**”

**And that is two hundred and twenty-two words in four turns of one exchange, of which the first is five, the second is a hundred and seven, the third is sixty-five printed in two spans of five and sixty, and the fourth is forty-five printed in two spans of thirteen and thirty-two, and the woman of about thirty-four who carried the sheet has not been thanked and did not say thank you and said five words that were not a thank you, and about four of the nine people in that building heard all of it and about nine have repeated the five words since without repeating the hundred and seven that came before them.**

## Four

**And what the no cost is not on that page and is not in that building and is about four days up a made road and nine hundred paces down a sand, and it is not the thing anybody in that room thought it was, and about nine of the people on that coast will find out about the no between the twenty-first and the twenty-fifth of a month carried at thirty and about four of them will say something about it and about five will not.**

The press in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane two hundred miles up the made road stands open with no date anybody will give. The register in that room has seven entries and every one of them is a question and none of them is a finding. The day's book has nine entries of a thing nobody asked. The drawer about eleven inches deep is not open. That room sends out about nine hundred sheets a month, and it ties up about a hundred and four of them on the fixed day, and about four hundred and nine parish rolls come in at it, and the three of those answer to nothing and are not made into one another, and about eleven hundred people are on no roll and no sheet and no list and nothing in this year has put them on anything.

**And nobody in that room two hundred miles up the made road knows that a man of thirty-two with a limp said no in a salt-store at the landward end of a foreshore in the accent of that shore on the twenty-first day of Rainmonth in this year, and a woman of twenty-nine in that room said a sentence in an accent about four hundred miles from any town she has worked in and did not notice that she had done it, and has not noticed yet, and the narration has said so once and is not going to say it again.**

## Five

The cost of that Friday is a column and it is Aren Kest's, and it is the sixth one he has kept and it is the first one he has written standing up, and a no in nine sentences is not in it and four days of a made road and a day of a boat are not in it.

**a handcart for the four days down at four, a handcart for the four days back at four, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a fourth loaf at one, a fifth loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a second pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, a second pound of wood at three, a room at six, a bed at four, a second bed at four, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a pound of candles at four, a sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a pound of rope at six, a basket at five, a brush at two, a knife at two, a bushel of oats at four, a second bushel of oats at four, a tin of oil at two, a second tin of oil at two, a pair of gloves at two, a second pair of gloves at two, an inkwell at three, a waxed cord at four, a third night's bed at four, a fourth night's bed at four, a fifth night's bed at four, a sixth night's bed at four, a seventh night's bed at four, a second room at six, and a second room with a fire in it at eight.** Four, four, one, one, one, one, one, four, four, three, three, six, four, four, two, two, four, one, three, five, eight, six, five, two, two, four, four, two, two, two, two, three, four, four, four, four, four, four, six and eight is one hundred and thirty-eight pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and thirty-eight pence is two marks and forty-two over**, and twenty-two notches are not in it and are years and not tides, and a form with three heads filled in and a fourth head empty is not in it and is in a room two hundred miles up a made road, and a road that is not a toll road has no rate printed on it anywhere in nine counties and none was printed here, and a foreshore is not a road.

**And a man of about thirty-two with a limp was asked in a salt-store at the landward end of two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand at about the sixth hour on the twenty-first day of Rainmonth in this year whether his road and their history can be made into one account, and he said no, in nine sentences, in the accent of that shore, and he knew he was saying it in that accent, and the woman of about thirty-four who carried the sheet looked at him for about as long as it takes a man to say four and then said five words that were not a thank you, and the door was shut at the ninth hour and the water was over about two hundred paces of the seaward half from the sixth hour and it was off the sand again before four in the morning, and the twenty-second notch is not cut.**
