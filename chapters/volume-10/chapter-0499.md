# Chapter 0499

**The twenty-fourth day of Rainmonth, YR 319, a Monday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the eleventh hour, in a town of about four thousand people in the fen county, nine miles off the Old Toll crossing, on a made road that runs through it and out the other side, and there is a bar at the second hour and a yard of carts at the sixth and a locked door at the eleventh, and nothing has been opened and nothing has been agreed to, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is counting his own nights and cannot make them foot.**

## One

**What happened is that a man of thirty-two with a limp sat down in a bar in a town of about four thousand people at about the second hour on the twenty-fourth of a month carried at thirty in this year and did not ask for anything, and a woman of about sixty-two who keeps the bar asked him one question, and he tried to answer it out loud and got as far as a list of five roads and then stopped, and about nine people in that bar heard the attempt and about four of them heard the part where he stopped.**

“**Where do you sleep tonight?**” the woman of about sixty-two said.

“**It depends,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said.

“**That is a thing everybody says and it is not a thing anybody says here,**” the woman of about sixty-two said. “**This town runs on the fourth and the eleventh. The eleventh is a market and the fourth is not and the rest of the days are the rest of the days, and there are four hundred people in this bar on a Monday and nine on an ordinary morning, and I have kept it thirty-one years and I have never once had a man of thirty-two tell me it depends.**”

“**Then I will tell you what I have, and it does not add, and I have checked it twice since Friday and it does not add.**”

“**I did not ask whether it adds,**” the woman of about sixty-two said, “**and if you tell me it does not add then I will be carrying it for the rest of the night and I have a bar to close.**”

“**Then tell me the price of a bed and I will sleep in it and I will say the list out loud tomorrow on a road where it does not matter.**”

“**Four pence,**” the woman of about sixty-two said, “**and there is a room over the yard and the man in the yard has to agree, and he is out with a cart until the ninth hour.**”

“**Four pence is a column and a bed is not a column,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said, “**and I will not sleep in that room tonight if the four pence goes to a man who is not in this bar, and I have not worked out a better arrangement and I am not going to invent one at the second hour on a Monday in a town I have never been in.**”

**And that is two hundred and eighty-one words in nine turns of one exchange, of which the first is five, the second is two, the third is fifteen, the fourth is seventy, the fifth is twenty-six, the sixth is thirty-five printed in two spans of seven and twenty-eight, the seventh is thirty-one, the eighth is thirty printed in two spans of two and twenty-eight, and the ninth is sixty-seven printed in two spans of twelve and fifty-five, and the woman of about sixty-two was not thanked and did not ask and did not say the four pence again, and the man of thirty-two with a limp was in that bar for about two hours and did not lie and did not buy a drink and did not tell the bar what the five roads are.**

## Two

**And the list is five roads and thirty-one nights and it does not add, and the difference is three, and he has not found the three and is not going to find the three before he is back in this town, and about four people have read the list and two of the four have said that the three are on a road he has not named yet and about two of the four have said that no list that has a three in it is worth reading.**

The five roads are a made road to a salt port four days off, a made road to a market town of about two thousand people, a salt road out of this town to the coast, a road two days west of Merefen to a yard of four lime kilns, and two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand where a river runs into the sea, which is not a road and which he counts anyway.

Nights against them, as he has them: **nine on the made road to the port, seven on the made road to the market town, six on the salt road out of this town, four at the kilns, five on the sand.** Nine and seven and six and four and five is thirty-one.

He has rented a room in this town since the eighteenth of a month carried at thirty and the room-keeper's own count is thirty-four and it is written on a board over the stairs in chalk in a hand that is not his and not the room-keeper's, and the chalk man came in about a week after he did and did not speak to him and has not spoken to him since.

**Thirty-one and thirty-four and the difference is three, and neither count is a lie, and the two are not converted into one another, and about nine people have asked him which of the two is the true one and he has said that he does not know, and he does not know, and the three are not in a column and the three are not in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane and the three are not on a form.**

“**You are thirty-one to a man with a chalk,**” a man of about forty-four with a horseshoe said, at about the sixth hour, in a yard of carts where nine carts were standing and one was loaded with peat. “**And I have been in this yard thirty-one years and I will tell you the thing about a chalk board, and it is worth one pence of what you have.**”

“**Tell it.**”

“**Nobody upends it,**” the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe said, “**and everybody writes on it, and the man who wrote the top line has not been down this road in about nine years and the man who wrote the bottom line was in here on Friday, and if the two of them do not agree then the board is not a record of anything, it is a place where nine people a week put a number they have in their heads, and the numbers are all roughly right and the board is wrong.**”

“**Then which is right, the one in my head or the one on the wall?**”

“**Neither,**” the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe said, “**and that is the useful part and I have never made four pence out of it in thirty-one years. A man who knows which of the two of his own numbers is the true one has two numbers. A man who does not has one number and a gap, and the gap is where the three nights are and they are not a lie and they are not a story and they are the nights he has not got.**”

**And that is two hundred and twenty-two words in six turns of one exchange, of which the first is nine, the second is thirty, the third is two, the fourth is eighty-six printed in two spans of three and eighty-three, the fifth is fifteen, and the sixth is printed in two spans of one and seventy-nine, and the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe has said this to about nine men in thirty-one years and this is the first time he has said it to a man he met two hours ago and he is not thanked and the man of thirty-two with a limp did not say that the man was right, and said nothing at all for about a minute.**

## Three

**And five paths open is not five doors and it is not a favour and nobody on that made road asked for any of them, and the coast has one of them in it and a man of about thirty-four from the port four days off has another and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow has a third and a mason of about sixty-eight at a single arch over a beck has a fourth and a woman of about fifty at a roadhouse nine miles on from the kilns has a fifth, and the five of them have never met and about four of the five have heard of none of the other four, and the man of thirty-two with a limp is not a sixth one and is not a keeper of the other five and has not said so and has not said the number out loud on a made road in a town of about four thousand people.**

What he did at about the sixth hour, in a yard of nine carts, was put his stick against a cart-wheel and get his weight off his right leg the way he has done about four hundred times, and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow who had come up the salt road with a load of shingle and had stopped in that yard because of the rain saw him do it and said eleven words.

“**I know that. Do it up a beach for eleven days.**”

“**How do you know that?**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said.

“**There is one of those in every place I have ever carried shingle,**” the woman of about thirty-four with a barrow said, and got back on her cart and went out the far side of the yard without waiting for an answer, and she is not the woman who carried a sheet down two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand in the ninth day of this year, and about nine people in that yard heard the eleven words and about four of them knew what it cost him to be watched doing it.

**And that is twenty-nine words in three turns of one exchange, of which the first is eleven, the second is five, and the third is thirteen, and it is the only exchange in that yard on that Monday that was finished in under a minute, and the man of thirty-two with a limp did not follow her out of the yard and did not ask her name and could not have said her name if he had been asked.**

## Four

**And at about the eleventh hour the door of the room over the yard was locked and the man of the yard had not come back and about nine carts were standing in the rain and about four hundred people in this town do not know that a man of thirty-two slept in this town on the eighteenth of a month carried at thirty in this year, and he sat in a bar until it closed and paid for nothing, and the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe took his own pence in the yard through a hatch and counted them out loud to himself and neither of them was thanked.**

“**You are not sleeping in the room,**” the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe said.

“**No.**”

“**And you are not going to ask me to hold it,**” the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe said, “**and I am not going to offer it, and the reason I am not going to offer it is that a man who is held a bed has told nine people where he sleeps, and a man who is let a bed has told nobody, and the second one is the one I want and it costs me the four pence and I have been keeping that room empty for about four months and I would like it said out loud once that it is on purpose and not because nobody wants it.**”

“**It is on purpose,**” the man of thirty-two with a limp said, “**and I have not been in a room since Friday and I am not going to say why and it is not a principle and it is about a room and not about this room, and I have been asked twice today whether it depends and it does and I have got used to saying so and that is not the same as knowing.**”

“**That is the first thing you have said since you came in that I would write down,**” the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe said.

**And that is one hundred and ninety-seven words in five turns of one exchange, of which the first is seven, the second is one, the third is a hundred and four printed in two spans of eleven and ninety-three, the fourth is sixty-eight printed in two spans of four and sixty-four, and the fifth is seventeen, and about four people heard the third turn entire and about nine heard the first, and the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe is not thanked and the man of thirty-two with a limp is not thanked and neither of them said the other one was right.**

## Five

The cost of that Monday is a column and it is the man of about forty-four with a horseshoe's, and it is the fourth he has kept and the first three have a head on them and this one has no head on it, and five roads and thirty-one nights and a difference of three are not in it.

**a room at four, a second room at four, a bed at four, a second bed at four, a pound of wood at three, a pound of candles at four, a loaf at one, a second loaf at one, a third loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a handcart for the day at four, a broom at five, a bucket of tar at eight, a pound of rope at six, a pound of nails at three, a sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at three, a tin of oil at two, a basket at five, a knife at two, an inkwell at three, a pair of gloves at two, a bushel of oats at four, a farrier at three, a night's lodging for a man of about twenty-six who was in the yard until the ninth hour at two, a second night's lodging for a man of about twenty-six at two, peat for one night at six, a second peat for one night at six, a third peat for one night at six, straw for one night at two, a second straw for one night at two, a tallow candle at five, a sack of oats at six, a second sack of oats at six, and a farthing at one.**Four, four, four, four, three, four, one, one, one, four, four, five, eight, six, three, one, three, two, five, two, three, two, four, three, two, two, six, six, six, two, two, five, six, six and one is one hundred and twenty-five pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and one hundred and twenty-five pence is two marks and twenty-nine over**, and a locked room that stayed empty on purpose is in it, and a difference of three is not in it and cannot be put in a column because three is not a price, and thirty-one and thirty-four are two counts of two different things and are not added and are not converted into one another.

**And on the twenty-fourth day of Rainmonth in this year a man of thirty-two with a limp sat in a bar in a town of about four thousand people in the fen county and was asked where he slept and said it depends, and said that he had five roads and thirty-one nights and that thirty-four was written on a board over a yard stair in a hand that was not his, and the difference is three and he did not find the three, and a man of about forty-four with a horseshoe told him that a man who does not know which of his two numbers is true has one number and a gap, and the gap is where the three nights are and they are the nights he has not got, and a woman of about thirty-four with a barrow in another yard said nine words and went out the far side without waiting, and the room over the yard was locked and stayed empty on purpose and the man of the yard came back at the ninth hour and found the door locked and said nothing about it, and it was still locked at the eleventh hour and the four pence was not taken and was not offered.**
