# Chapter 0451

**The twenty-sixth day of Wolfmonth, YR 318, a Monday and not a market day, and the second hour and the sixth hour and the tenth hour, in one room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane, in a county about two hundred miles off a street of working yards, where a press has stood open since a day nobody in that building will name and nobody shuts, and where about nine hundred sheets a month go out of that room into nine counties, and a woman of twenty-nine is in it, and a man of about twenty-nine is in it, and a man of thirty-two with a limp is in it and came into it four days ago and has a sheet of nothing at his elbow with a rule on it.**

## One

**The letter came in with a cart and not with a person, and it is folded twice and sealed with a lump of something brown, and it is addressed to a keeper of records in a county about nine counties off, and the address is correct, and it has been eleven days on the road.**

It is nine inches by six when it is open.

A woman of twenty-nine has a room with a door on a lane and a deed of thirteen words and fourpence a year, and she has had the room four years and a half, and about nine hundred sheets a month go out of it into nine counties with a head on every one of them, and a hundred and four go out on the fixed Saturday in a band of a hundred and eight with four taken out of it. She read the letter twice on the sixth hour of the Monday and once on the seventh and she did not send for anybody on the sixth hour, which is the correct hour and is not the hour anybody would guess.

She sent for him on the seventh.

**And what the letter asks for, in nine words on the first side and a list on the second, is a keeper.**

Not a road. Not a trade. Not a licence and not a warrant and not money. A keeper, for a connection, between three roads that have never been joined and whose owners are three assemblies on three shores and who have never met.

## Two

The man of about twenty-nine had come up in a cart he hired at a crossroads and had been in the room for two days and had not once asked what a return was.

**Rusen Ball** had a factor's table in a fen town of about four thousand people and a coat with the cuffs gone at the right wrist and nine days on a road behind him, and he had asked for the room over the weigh-house because it is the only room in nine counties that answers letters about things that have nobody's name on them, and he had got that right in nine days and nobody told him how.

“**I am not asking for a road,**” he said. “**I have got three of them. I am asking for the man who holds the middle of them, and there is not one, and there have not been three, and the three of us have been writing to each other for about two years with a cart each way and about nine days on the middle road.**”

The woman of twenty-nine did not look up.

“**Then you want a middle,**” she said, “**and you have come to a room with a door on a lane and fourpence a year to ask a woman to make one out of paper.**”

“**I want a name in a column,**” he said, “**and I have not got one, and the form will not take anything else.**”

## Three

**The form is three heads cut about forty years ago in a room two counties off by a man who is not alive, and every one of the fifty or sixty of them in this kingdom is the same three heads in the same order, and the woman of twenty-nine has sent about nine hundred a month for four years and a half and could put the order in her sleep.**

A place. A figure of men. A trade.

She said it out loud because he had not said it and because saying it out loud in a room is cheaper than saying it to a clerk in a corridor.

“**A place,**” she said. “**Then a figure of men. Then a trade. And I want you to sit there and tell me which of the three of those a connection between three roads is, and I want you to take as long as you like, because I have got about nine hundred of these a month and I have never once been asked that.**”

Rusen Ball looked at the second side of the letter for a while.

“**It is a place,**” he said. “**It is the foreshore at the seaward end. It is two miles and about nine hundred paces of hard sand and there are eleven houses on it and about nine hundred people go along it.**”

“**Then it has a place,**” she said, “**and about nine hundred people, which is a figure of men or near enough for a man who wants it to be, and a trade, which is salt. And in about nine minutes you have got a form filled in and a thing that is on a column, and in about four years it will be the only sentence anybody in nine counties can say about that foreshore.**”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

“**I have not got nine hundred people,**” he said. “**I have got nine hundred people on a road that is under water twice a day and a clerk on a shore who has told me twice that nine hundred is a figure of households and he will not sign a figure of households on a line that says men.**”

## Four

**And the man of thirty-two with a limp said that he would take it, and it is twenty-eight words in one turn of one speech, and it is the second time in about six months that he has offered to be the person a thing is got at, and the first time was about a word in a song.**

“**I will hold it,**” he said. “**I am on no list in nine counties. I have been to nine places in nine counties and I can go to a tenth.**”

The woman of twenty-nine put her pen down, which she does not do.

“**You have said that before,**” she said, “**about a thing that was the name of a road, and you were asked to leave a room in a township of about nine hundred people about a month ago, and I know that because the cart brought it and I did not ask.**”

He did not answer that.

“**And the reason you cannot hold this one is not the same as the reason you could not hold that one,**” she said, “**and I want it said out loud in a room, because you said it out loud about the other and four people believed you, and this is a worse shape.**”

“**The request wants a place,**” he said, “**and what it wants to hold is between three places, and a man who stands in the middle of three places is a man who can be sent for to all three. That is the whole of it. I would not be a keeper. I would be the middle of a column, and the column would have a name on it, and the name would be mine, and there are about nine hundred people on a foreshore who would then be describable by the fact that a man stood between them.**”

“**And the man of about twenty-nine would go back to a factor's table with a name,**” she said.

“**He would,**” he said.

## Five

The cost of that Monday is a column and it is hers and it is the shortest one she has kept in about four years and a half.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a sheet of paper at one, a second sheet of paper at one, a quill and ink at seven, a tallow candle at two, a second tallow candle at two, a fire at three, a second fire at three, a broom at three.** One, one, one, one, seven, two, two, three, three and three is twenty-four pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and twenty-four pence is under one by twenty-four**, and the room is hers and the fire is hers and the broom is hers and a cart's fare of four pence went to a man of about twenty-nine out of his own money and is not in it.

**And a man of about twenty-nine sat in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane and asked for a keeper for a connection between three roads, and a woman of twenty-nine told him what a form is in a handful of sentences and did not fill one in, and a man of thirty-two with a limp offered to hold a thing for the second time in about six months and was told the reason out loud, and the reason was that a man who stands in the middle of three places is a man who can be sent for to all three, and nobody in that room offered a third thing, and the second side of that letter is still on the bench in a county about two hundred miles off a street of working yards, and the brown lump on the seal has not been broken open again and will not be.**
