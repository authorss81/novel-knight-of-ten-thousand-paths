# Chapter 0461

**The fourteenth day of Hearthmonth, YR 318, a Sunday and not a market day, and the second hour and the fifth hour and the eighth hour, in a room over a weigh-house in a port of nine hundred people four days off a foreshore where the water goes out twice a day, where the room is let by the day and the woman of forty-four who keeps it goes home at the eighth hour and the bolt is not drawn, and nine people are in the room and six of them walked four days to be in it.**

## One

**The room is about nine feet by fourteen and it has a table in it with the top lifted off and four stools and a lamp on the sill that is not lit, and it is over a weigh-house because a weigh-house is the only kind of room in a port of nine hundred people that anybody lets by the day.**

There are nine people in it and they are not a body and there is no chair. **Three of them came four days up a made road off a foreshore, and three more came four days the same way and by a different lane, and the other three live within four hundred yards of the foot of the stairs, and five of the nine are standing, because there are four stools and a table with the top lifted off and nobody in that room has ever asked for a fifth stool.**

And the man of about twenty-nine who came nine days with a letter in a coat did not come into that room and was not asked to and had gone up the made road two days before with his own business to do.

**There is a window in that room with four panes and one of them is cracked from the corner and has been cracked since before any of the nine people in it was born, and nobody has had it mended, and the woman of forty-four says it lets the wind off the flame in the lamp and that is why it stays.**

## Two

**They have met each other twice in forty years, and both of the times can be named, and neither of them happened in this room.**

The first was forty-one years ago, when six of them walked to a barn nine miles off and sat in it for an afternoon about a boat that did not come in, and the second was eleven years ago for a wreck at the seaward end, where nine men pulled a hull up a beach that is there twice a day and four of those nine men are standing in this room.

Nobody arranged this one. **Nine people had heard about it and four of them had told two others each, and that is the whole of how nine people came to be in one room, and there is no list in nine counties that says who told whom.**

The man of sixty from the landward end said the second time was the shorter of the two, and that everybody present remembered it wrong by about a year in the same direction, which was the only thing any of them said about either occasion that anybody else improved on.

The man of about twenty-seven with four boats was at neither of them, because at the first he was not born and at the second he was sixteen and on the other side of the water.

“**I was not at the barn,**” he said. “**I have told people I was. I was not.**”

Four of the nine laughed and five did not, and that was the closest that room came all day to anything like a meeting.

## Three

**The road was not on the agenda and there is no agenda, and nobody put it there, and the reason nobody put it there is that there is nobody in this port to put it to and no room that stands often enough for a thing to be put to it twice.**

They talked for about three hours about six things. A boat that came in wrong on a flood and put her bilge across the slip. The price of a load of shell and whether it ought to be the price of a load of shell. A boy of nineteen on the quay below who writes the slipway's book and cannot read the words in it. The eight days a year that the made road above is shut. A woman of sixty-three who keeps a room two days off that road and takes what the room is taken for out of a box on the shelf and has never put it in the book. And a fire behind a fourth house four days down the sand that has not been out since a month in a year nobody on that shore will name.

Then the man of sixty with the slipway at the landward end said the thing that all nine of them went round and said in nine different ways over three hours, which was a wagon.

**He said a loaded wagon comes off the sand at the wrong hour and stands in the channel with a near wheel in the water, and everybody within half a mile of that sand knows about it inside a minute.**

“**How long has that been true?**” the man of about thirty-four asked him.

“**Since I was a boy.**”

“**And what do you do about it?**”

“**Nothing,**” the man of sixty said. “**That is what I am telling you. Nothing is what we do about it.**”

**And the boy of nineteen on the quay below said that the wagon in the channel is not what anybody in that room is frightened of, and that what they are frightened of is the man who comes down the made road afterwards with a sheet and a chain, and that nobody in that room told him he was wrong and that four of them had thought the same thing for years and had not put it the way he put it.**

“**You are nineteen,**” the man of about twenty-seven said.

“**I am,**” the boy said. “**I write the book and I cannot read it. That is the whole of what I know and it is enough.**”

And then they agreed on one thing and no more.

The man of sixty put it as a question, because he put everything as a question.

“**Say the wagon is in the channel and the tide is on it. Who goes?**”

“**Whoever is nearest it,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**And who calls you?**”

Nobody answered him.

“**And who pays you?**”

Still nothing. The lamp on the sill stayed unlit and the cracked pane let the wind across it.

“**And after,**” the man of sixty said, “**do they ask you how it went?**”

“**No,**” the man of about thirty-four said.

“**Then that is what we have agreed,**” the man of sixty said, “**and I would say it plain, because nobody else in this room is going to.**”

That took about four minutes and it is the only thing agreed in this room in three hours, and four of the nine have said since then that it is not an agreement and four have said since then that it is the whole of one, and nobody has said the other half out loud.

## Four

**And one of the answers, which was not put to the road at all because it was the room they were standing in, is a room that is let by the day whose keeper goes home.**

The woman of forty-four had opened it at the second hour and had taken the fourpence for it out of a box on the shelf and had not written the fourpence down anywhere, and at the eighth hour she put a key on the table and said she had a fire to see to and went down the stairs.

“**I am going,**” she said, in the doorway, to nobody.

“**Aye,**” said the man of sixty, and let her go.

Nobody stopped her. Nobody asked her to stay. **Nine people in that port have watched her do it and not one of them has ever asked her why, and the reason nobody has asked is that everybody already knows and there is nothing in it that a question would improve.**

The man of thirty-four who is a carter and lives four hundred yards off said that the room has been let by the day for as long as he can remember and that he has stood in it nine times in his life and has never once known who to ask for it, and that he has always assumed it was the same nine people, and that it is not, and that this is the first year of his life that anybody has asked him who to ask for.

And the boy of nineteen said that the bolt is not drawn every day and that the woman of forty-four draws it about four times in a year, and that the four times are the four times somebody has wanted the room for something that was not a day.

A room that is let by the day and whose keeper goes home is a room that nobody can be found about. **That is not a fault in the woman and it is the arrangement, and the arrangement is right for a room and wrong for a road, and the reason it is wrong for a road is that a road is asked about twice a day and this room is asked about nine times a year.**

## Five

The cost of that day is a column and it is the man of about twenty-nine's and he keeps it on the back of a sheet folded in four at a factor's table on a made road, and the sheet is not in a book and is not a copy of anything.

**a loaf at one penny, a second loaf at one, a pound of cheese at four, a pound of wood at three, two tallow candles at two, a room at six, a bed at four.** One, one, four, three, two, two, six and four is twenty-three pence. **A mark is forty-eight pence and twenty-three pence is under half of one by one**, and six people walking four days to a room over a weigh-house is not in it and is not a figure of men, and a road that is under water twice a day is not in it and a foreshore is not a road.

**And nine people sat in a room over a weigh-house for three hours and agreed on four clauses about a wagon in a channel and on nothing else at all, and the woman of forty-four who kept the room put a key on the table at the eighth hour and went down the stairs and the bolt was not drawn behind her, and four hundred yards off a man of about thirty-four with a sheet was not in that port and has not been in it since the middle of a month in Wolfmonth, and eleven paces of stone with a second footing course on it stands at a hundred paces from the landward end of a road that takes the tide twice a day, and nine hundred people use that road, and for eight hours out of every twelve the seaward half of it is under about two hundred paces of water, and for about four hours out of every twelve the whole of it is hard sand and broken shell.**
