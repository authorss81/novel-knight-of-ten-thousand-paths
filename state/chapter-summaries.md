## Volume 07 — Batch 0001 — Chapters 301–310, "The Room That Is Listed"

**The block.** Main world and path time, day 390, the twenty-fifth of Embermonth, YR 316, a Saturday and a market day and the volume's first day and Volume 06's last day, to day 430, the fifth of Frostmonth, YR 316, a Thursday and a market day. Five market mornings printed. **23,778 words in ten chapters.** The frame is counted from `outline/volume-07.md` §17 and not from the prompt, and all ten datelines are exact. **A path is on the page for the first time in two volumes, and it is the Ninefold Winter, and it is a valley four hundred miles off any county that is on a map, and it has about four hundred people in it and a woman with a slate and a keeper of a roll, and it is a different place from the city four hundred miles off behind the reader, and nothing in this block is sent to that city.**

### The ten chapters

**Ch 301 · day 390, the twenty-fifth of Embermonth, a Saturday and a market day, about the fifth hour · the fen county, and a path.** The bell goes at the fourth hour and a half and nobody in that market looks up, and a waggon of salt stands at the end of the channel road for an hour, and a reach four hundred yards down a lane has about forty men on it, and a yard four hundred yards up that county's own road has nine men and a bench-board and a press nothing can be served out of. **A man of thirty-two with a limp asks a man of about nineteen with an empty hod to stand at one end of a road for about four minutes, and tells him the whole of what he is witnessing, which is that a man said a date and an end, and the man of about nineteen asks no second question.** The road opens, and it is not made with a hand on anything and nothing was broken. **On the far side there is a valley, and the first person he meets is a woman of about thirty with a slate, and he answers her ordinary question with somebody else's grief in about a second and a half — a book read out in an aisle, a line ruled across it in a hundred and forty years, about two hundred people who have never left the valley, a leg he cannot get up a bank with, and a road with nobody at his shoulder — and he knows in about a second that it is not his.** He says, before anybody has asked him: *I am not right, and I would like somebody to tell me what I have taken.* **She says nobody is going to tell him that and that he will have to ask a man and then believe him.** **The man who keeps the roll is behind the door and he is about fifty and his name is Marek Ollen and he has kept it since he was thirty-one, and the roll is a long sheet folded in four with a day and a name and a mark, and there is no column for anything else and there never has been one. He tells Aren that the answer to the seventh line is a trade and not a name, and that the line wants the other thing.** He puts his finger on nine ruled days with nothing in them. **Change:** a man has been entered in the only record a valley of four hundred people will ever be in, inside a week of arriving, in a roll with a column for a day and a name and a mark and no column for the day a person goes. **Ends:** *I do not know* — the first time anybody in that valley has been told in words that the nine days are not known, and it was told to a stranger on the day he came.

**Ch 302 · day 394, the twenty-ninth of Embermonth, a Wednesday, and not a market day in a county with a market · the room over a cross.** Nine people on twenty-nine benches bolted to the floor, about thirty in the room, about two hundred of a generation that has never once left the valley at the back, and an aisle four feet wide that has gone three generations without being used. He says the sentence again. **Two men hear it. The man of thirty-four corrects him — that is his, and it is said better than he says it, and the part of it that will have to be given to him by somebody else is that he does not know when he does it — and the man of about fifty refuses, in eleven words' worth of plainness, to say which of two accounts of a man are right, because about four hundred people on his sheet have never been asked him either.** **Change:** he is entered in the day column with a mark that is a gate, and there is nothing to enter about him under either of the other two columns and nobody invented anything. **Ends:** a roll that has a line for coming in and no line for going out is a roll a man can be in for as long as the man who keeps it leaves the line alone.

**Ch 303 · day 399, the fourth of Wolfmonth, a Monday, and not a market day in a county with a market · the same room over a cross, the seventh hour.** **The room wants a number and the only thing in four hundred miles that knows the answer is a roll, and a roll cannot be made to say its answer out loud in one line without a man deciding which of nine hundred lines is a person here now.** There is a second roll, and the second roll is a copy of the first, made by the keeper once a season for about nine years, and the two differ in nine places a season and the difference is nine ruled days. **His want is said once, to a man with a limp and not to the room, and it foots at twenty-four words: *I have written down everybody twice and once was enough and now I have to know which of the two I was right about.* Nine faces and no answer.** The woman with the slate says her want to the roll and not to a person, and no figure is printed on it. **And the third thing is the thing nobody puts on the table: the forgetting is not entered, and the reason is not a decision, and there is no column for it and there never has been, and a man of fifty-eight spoke to nine of about two hundred people of that generation in about eleven years and could not tell two of them who they were and wrote neither of them down.** **Change:** a keeper of a roll says the true thing about a third thing to a sheet of paper in front of nine people, and the true thing is that a person is on a sheet or a person is not on a sheet and there is no third thing. **Ends:** there is a roll of who is in a valley and there has never been a roll of who is known to whom, and nobody in that city can be told which of the two they would have to be asked for.

**Ch 304 · day 404, the ninth of Wolfmonth, a Saturday, and not a market day in a county with a market · the mill, the gates, the glass works, the road, the lane.** A man at a mill wheel has not left it since before the water came and has come out into the cold four times for about four minutes each. **The road is four hundred yards long, made by hand in a wet summer by a man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work, it has a crown and a ditch and a standing finish on the end of it, and it is the only straight thing in that valley and it goes nowhere, and nobody has ever asked it whether it goes anywhere.** There are three gates, and the number of crossings in a season is the one figure in that place anybody can check, and it is a figure about carts. **The word for the forgetting is said once, in a glass works, by a man with his hands in a cooling box, and it is a trade word and it is not explained and it has not been said again.** The woman with the slate loses a name in a yard at the fourth hour and has it back inside about a minute and a quarter, and the man she was writing it for does not notice. **And at the seventh hour a man of about sixty who keeps the roll of the crossing fills in nine ruled days that had been empty in the roll of everybody, and nobody asked him to, and he took about four minutes.** **Change:** **a roll with a gap in it is a roll that can be filled in, and that valley found that out on an evening in that month without anybody deciding that they had found it out, and the finding is about a piece of paper and not about a fever.** **Ends:** there is no column on that sheet for a name that got there by accident and none for one that got there on purpose, and the two are the same line in the same hand and nobody in that valley can tell them apart and never will.

**Ch 305 · day 409, the fourteenth of Wolfmonth, a Thursday and a market day · a long room at the top of a lane, eleven chairs down each long side, a clerk whose ink pot is shut nine times a week.** The room sits four days out of seven and nine people take a chair out of twenty-two and the clerk's ink pot is the only speed in the room. **A man of about sixty proposes a third book, out of his own half of the record, to be sent to a man at a stone two hundred miles off who will not read it, and his reason is his own and it is correct.** **The keeper of the roll says the fear, and the fear is correct, and it is about a copy and not about the Crown: a copy does not get anything out of the sheet and does not put anything in, and after about two years the sheet is what anybody in that valley knows about the two of them, and the two of them are still there.** The clerk reads that part of the minute back once and only once. **A woman of about thirty with the slate says the thing the volume is about, in eleven words, and no figure is printed on it: *A copy of a person is a person who was there.*** Nobody improved on it. **The room splits five and four, and the clerk writes both numbers on one line under a head of his own invention, and does not enter a reason, and that is the only thing that room has ever recorded about itself.** A man of thirty-four comes in with sixteen men and no work and gets nothing, and the true sentence anybody has said in that room in eleven years gets no answer at all. **Change:** the copy is made anyway, out of his own half, and goes into a press in a room over a cross, and the room's vote does not reach a man's own book. **Ends:** the sheet in the press will be the one that gets picked up, and it is in a room over a cross, and there is no column on either of the two of them for which of them was written by the man whose name is not on either.

**Ch 306 · day 414, the nineteenth of Wolfmonth, a Tuesday, and not a market day · the wharf, the glass works, the lane of nine doors, the box at the low gate, and the ordinary post.** A bell of nine stone goes at the fourth hour over the street at the low gate and nobody in that city has been able to account for it and nobody is going to be asked, and it is rung by whoever is nearest. **On about two hundred yards of street with about nine doors, about nine people are asked the ordinary question and about two of the nine take about a minute and a quarter over it and neither of the two mentions it.** **A man of thirty-four with sixteen men and no work says the volume's fear out loud to a stranger in a yard, once, and it foots at thirty-six words, and the stranger does not answer it.** And at the seventh hour **a man of about sixty picks a sheet out of a box at a gate where it has stood for two months in the ordinary post, does not know what office it came from, and fills in the first column with a place and the second column with a figure under a head that says *Date*, and the figure is four hundred and four because that is the last line of his own roll and a roll is not a count.** He copies his own half of the record as well and puts it in the same bag, because a man who is going to send a figure is going to send the thing the figure came from. **Change:** the copy and the sheet go out on an ordinary round at the ninth hour. **Ends:** nobody in that city knew it had ever arrived, and about two hundred of the ninth generation in that place are the only people in it who have never once been asked whether they are all right.

**Ch 307 · day 418, the twenty-third of Wolfmonth, a Saturday and a market day · a lane, a wagon arch, a door on a lane, two rooms over a weigh-house.** A courier is not a carrier and the whole difference between them is in what a man is allowed to ask either. **He comes the length of the lane, stops at the one door in it with anything cut into it, and puts a sheet on a table, and the sheet says a return is wanted, and it is a sheet of that office's own printed series with a set of market towns in the first column and a figure of persons in the second under a head that says *Date*, and the figure is about nine hundred more than the keeper would have said, and nowhere on the whole of it is the name of a person.** The keeper of records does not ask him for a name and he gives none, and when she asks who it is from he says it is for her and she tells him she did not ask that. **A second copyist of about thirty comes down three steps and says then what do we do, and the answer is nothing, and the reason is given in the plain way once and is about two hundred miles and a figure nobody knows the count of.** A woman of about thirty with a pail says one thing at each of nine doors and moves on before the knuckle has finished ringing in the frame, and the three things she says that morning are a courier, a tide and a market. A second keeper of twenty-six comes through the room and does not stop at the table. **The office pays fourpence a year and the two rooms are the salt merchant's at two shillings a month each, and the two are named together and not added.** **Change:** the sheet goes into a press that anybody can be served with, in a room whose only record of anybody who has ever come up that lane is a day-book nothing can be served out of. **Ends:** it stands at nine entries of a thing nobody asked, and there is not going to be a tenth, and a tenth would not be a gift if it came.
