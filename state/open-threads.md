# Open Threads

## Initial story threads

1. **The cracked toll bell:** Why does the Bellweather anchor respond to Aren, and what does its first crossing cost him? The local mystery pays off in Volume 1 — a damaged anchor that still remembers refusing a severance writ, a permanent roadmark, the remembered pitch of one verse, nine lost days, and three days of fever. The bell's provenance and its two-pence purchase stay live for the whole series, because at Chapter 900 it is silent under his hand.
2. **The living Bellweather path:** Bellweather-Living has its own people, debt system, and refusal to be restored by the main world. Established in Volume 1 through the guild, the toll houses, and the Hushwater ferry cooperative, and carried forward as a recurring relationship rather than a one-volume exhibit.
3. **The official Sundering account:** Who authorized the cull, what did Aren actually know, and why was his disgrace manufactured? Edric's name is put on paper in Volume 1's midpoint, by the toll houses rather than by the guild. The full Council context, the post-Sundering inquiry, and the cost of Aren's own silence are spread through Volumes 2, 6, 11, and 18.
4. **The Gatehouse's consent rule:** The archive was built to prevent forced historical selection, but the Council altered it. The First Witness is revealed in Volumes 11–15; Volume 01 may only show its *effect* — a private-keeper clause being applied to Aren in a provincial letter — and must not name the mechanism.
5. **Ilyra Kest's crossing:** Aren’s mother used the Gatehouse to carry him from a sealed path. Her route is answered by the final volume; her exact survival is intentionally unknown and is untouched in Volume 01 except as the verse whose pitch he loses.
6. **Aren and Mara:** Their relationship begins as evidence custody versus intrusion. Volume 1 stages it as a fight over a filing system, with her winning the terms of his only evidence. It develops through disagreement and changed decisions, never instant attraction or dependence.
7. **Aren's command flaw:** Volume 1 stages his first failure of the pattern inside the path (Chapters 6 and 41–47): a solo Bind that puts the force into the pier and hurts a man, because he is the best reader of the load path in the room. Volume 1's smaller version is volunteering for a cost nobody asked him to pay, in Chapter 7.
8. **Roads without Aren:** Established in Volume 1 by the guild bench, the path's stewards, and the refusal to be grateful. The limited channel that opens at the volume's close is run by a Merefen steward and a Bellweather-Living steward with one independent witness each; Aren witnesses and translates. Volume 18 can therefore remove him without removing the network.
9. **The named dead:** Ferris Oat, Hanna Vey, Corin Slate, Bess Alder, Anwen Rusk, and Bryn Rusk exist as records, not as figures. They are the reason the case has a moral weight the Crown cannot dismiss. Jos Rusk, who survives and claims for them, is a different person and must stay distinct from Pei Tarrow; both first appear in Batch 0002, never together.
10. **Hushwater's silence:** The drowned hamlet's name in older records hints that the Bellweather-Living path was known to the archive system before the cull. Volume 01 gives the hint its physical form — Hushwater Reach, a living hamlet on a channel that is drowned in the main world, with a crossing book in a living hand — and explains nothing. The cooperative counts hands and hearths, not souls, so its nearest estimate is about **sixty** and nobody can be more definite; the erased village of the same name is **eighty**, and the two figures must never be printed together or compared on the page. This stays a hint until Volume 09.

## Answers to the questions the outline phase had to settle

- **What exactly is Aren doing, and for whom, in the first three chapters before any crossing occurs?** He is holding a road at flood stage for a town that cannot pay him properly, in the cheapest labor available, and he is doing it because it is the only problem he is allowed to have. The first three chapters are one working day argued over by four parties with money at stake, his own bond, and the bell he should have sold.
- **What makes him ring the bell on the anniversary storm rather than any other night?** Three reasons, all concrete: the storm is the only night the river is loud enough to cover the sound of a working; the town's custom puts him among the ferrymen ringing for the drowned, so one man with a bar is nobody's story; and the Archive's deadline gives him a night he privately resents. The cracked bell is the hinge of a refusal made in a storm on that date, and it answers the same weather. **Prose note:** the Archive deadline is now the **second of Greenmonth**, the province's renewal date, and the nineteenth is the human date. Both deadlines point at the same week and only the first one matters to a filing system.
- **How does Mara first become involved, given that she works above a salt merchant and Aren works a bridge?** She needs a sworn tradesman's account of what the bell is attached to, because the Archive cannot record an anchor on a clerk's word, and the bell is the only surviving object she can put in front of a magistrate. She finds him through the Old Toll Bridge's parish repair marks, by asking the ferrymen who signed the sill. She offers custody and a claim number in exchange for a promise not to ring it — undated, and he agrees to the whole of it. He does not tell her the bell rings by itself, and Chapter 4 is therefore a broken promise rather than a technicality.
- **Which of the seven villages gives Volume 01 its most immediate human stake?** **Hushwater.** It is the only one with a living claimant in Merefen, and its crossing book is the document that can be checked against a ferryman's memory. Jos Rusk is the first claim Mara personally refutes, in Batch 0002, and he is wrong about the cause and does not want to be argued with. Alder Cross carries the second stake, through Bess Alder's parish list, which is what splits the Crown's four thousand back into two hundred dead who can be named. Volume 01 keeps the two figures on separate pages and never prints a combined total.

## Threads opened by Chapters 1–10

1. **The seizure was prepared in advance, and so was the second letter.** The bridge guild's removal notice is dated the eleventh, the morning it reaches Aren. Rell's sealed order is dated the **sixteenth**, before the storm and before the anniversary, and a second letter dated the twenty-ninth — reaching the trust, not the Archive — again calls Aren the road-keeper of Merefen and summons him to the provincial town on the ninth of Greenmonth. The seal is the same hand-laid-on-a-book device on all three. Somebody in the marshal's office expected an answer from that bell, and expected two rounds of it. Volume 01 shows the dates and never explains them.
2. **The private-keeper clause.** Two provincial documents now name Aren the road-keeper of Merefen and the holder of the Bellweather hinge, and Lysa Fenn has searched the town's charter, the county's list of appointments, and the crown's own schedule of provincial offices and found no such office. The authority for the title is a hole, and a hole in a chain of paper is the plant for Volumes 02–03.
3. **The toll houses' northern trade.** On the page now: a toll-house steward in the guild hall using a foreign stranger as an argument, forty-one toll-houses, the three northern ones shut eleven years, and a promise that a register page might be had "in a year" from a man who has brought something to trade. That is the road into the toll-house counting room and the volume's midpoint.
4. **The outer ring of the second pier.** Eight inches, not growing this month, a new cutwater unaffordable, and a bench order to mark it twice a year. Carried from Chapter 7 to the volume climax, where the only way through is a public promise the debt cannot pay for.
5. **The seal match.** The wax on the path's counterfoil and the wax on Merefen's storehouse are the same press and the same hand, and Lysa has put that in a record in her own hand because it is the one fact in the file that does not depend on anybody's memory. This must survive to Volume 11's trial record.
6. **The register.** Named in the guild hall as the document that carries the signature the counterfoil does not, held by the toll houses. The index showing a second severance authorization in the same hand, four years later, in an unnamed county, is the door into Volume 02 and has not yet been seen.
7. **Whose name is on the eleven-year-old salvage filing?** Lysa Fenn was at this table and made a point of saying the bell filing and that filing are not the same document. She has still not said what the 301 filing covered, and the story still has not said.
8. **The bell's return.** Under a Crown seal in a watch storehouse by the fish weir, on a merchant's bell, in a town that is not the crown's. The town reclaims its own iron in Block 5; what the new band and the dull red sealing cost on the far side is a Block 5 question and not a Chapter 50 answer.
9. **Anchor-glass, four marks, a fortnight.** A slip nailed in a hut on the Old Toll road, money before cutting. The sill is the anchor and it has been used once. Until that glass exists, there is no second crossing, and the next phase's resource problem is a wages problem.
10. **The verse.** The remembered pitch of one verse of Ilyra's river song is gone, taken by the bell's crushed note, and he will keep finding the hole. Untouched by the batch except as a wound; her route and her fate remain Volume 09 and Volume 18 business.
11. **The path is running without him.** Six days in, a crossing booked at the seventh hour, a crack being measured twice a year, a bench that has asked for a promise to return, and no road to carry a word. Batch 0002 must show Bellweather-Living moving on its own, not waiting.

## Questions Batch 0002 inherits

- Four days to the **second of Greenmonth**, when the province renews its finding that nothing survives, and three to the **hearing of the fourth** on the sealed seizure order.
- The first surviving Hushwater claimant has a claim the keeper will personally refute, and he is alive in Merefen and does not want to be argued with.
- The watch captain's sergeant has to answer in writing for what the watch did, and answering is what costs him.
- The registrar who holds the Crown's own Bellweather entries has a price for a ledger that should not exist, and has already been paid once by the keeper of this office.
- A glasswright in Merefen sells honest anchor-glass at a price Aren resents.
- Nobody can find the road back, and the man who went there is the only man anybody would ask.

## Final-volume questions

- Can the Gatehouse be opened to thousands without making one person's memory the lock?
- Can path communities share roads while retaining the right to refuse contact?
- Will Aren surrender the private power that makes him useful?
