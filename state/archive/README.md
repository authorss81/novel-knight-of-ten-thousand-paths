**Four rules govern the window, and a phase that breaks one has broken canon rather than tidied it.**

1. **Numbers are never renumbered.** A continuity item number means the same thing here as in the live file, and so does a thread, chapter or batch number. Item 412 is findable either way.
2. **Nothing is deleted, only moved.** Reconstruct any earlier state of any of the four ledgers by concatenating the archive back onto the live file at the cut point.
3. **A volume is never archived while it is being read.** The window moves forward at a volume close, after the close has audited the volume it is closing, and never in the middle of one. **It moved for Volumes 01 to 07 at the Volume 07 close, for Volumes 01 to 08 at the Volume 08 close, for Volumes 01 to 09 at the Volume 09 close, for Volumes 01 to 10 at the Volume 11 close, for Volumes 01 to 11 at the Volume 12 close, and for Volumes 01 to 12 at the Volume 13 close, and not between any of them.** The close prompt for Volume 11 says *the older volumes go into `state/archive/` as a copy under unchanged numbering, the global sections come back live, and the window opens on Volume 11*, and **the last two of those three clauses are satisfied by the copy and not by a deletion, exactly as the Volume 09 close satisfied them, and the live ledgers were left whole — for those five cuts.** The 01-09 cut was a copy and the 01-10 cut is a copy, and **the test of a copy is that the archive is an exact prefix of the live file, which was verified by string comparison for all four files at that cut and not by reading them. THE SIXTH CUT, AT THE VOLUME 13 CLOSE, COPIED AND THEN TRIMMED, and the reason is written out in full in the section below, because a convention that regrows the file it is meant to shrink is not a window.**

**What each 01-08 file is, exactly, so that nobody has to guess: the current live head of that ledger, then everything the 01-07 file holds from its first archived section onward, then everything the window moved at the Volume 08 close — the Volume 07 close's own section, the five Volume 08 batch sections, the Volume 08 review-repair sections and the Volume 08 close. Each 01-08 file therefore contains the whole of the 01-07 file's body, in order and unaltered, and the test of that is one line: the previous archive's body is present in the new one.**
4. **Global material is never archived.** Sections that bind the whole project rather than one volume stay in the live file whatever their byte offset. That is why `## Hard canon`, `## Ending constraints` and `## Knowledge control` are live in `state/continuity.md`, and `## Initial story threads`, `## Answers to the questions the outline phase had to settle`, `## Final-volume questions` and `## Planning status` are live in `state/open-threads.md`, even though each of them sits at a byte offset that would have swept them into an archive. **They are also still in the archives, because the archives are copies, and a global section appearing in both places is harmless and a global section appearing in neither is not.**

**The window does not move again until a volume close. Volume 12 is in the archive for all four files as of the Volume 13 close, and Volume 13 is the volume the live window opens on.** The 01-11 cut was a copy and not a deletion, and the test of a copy was that the archive was an exact prefix of the live file; it was verified by byte comparison for all four files and by the rule-3 test that the 01-10 body is present whole in the 01-11 body. The 01-11 copies were taken **before** the Volume 12 close appended its own sections, so that archive holds Volumes 01 to 11 whole and the live file held Volumes 01 to 12.

## The 01-12 cut, at the Volume 13 close, and the one rule this project changed

**This is the sixth move of the window and the first one that TRIMMED the live files instead of only copying them, and the reason it had to is arithmetic that is now on the record.** At the moment this cut was taken the four ledgers stood at **2.79 MB live** — `continuity.md` 984 KB, `open-threads.md` 764 KB, `chapter-summaries.md` 560 KB, `batch-summaries.md` 468 KB — and `state/current.md` stood at 246 KB holding ten overlapping "What is true at the close of Chapter N" blocks for Chapter 560 through Chapter 650. That is about **3.0 MB of state, roughly 780,000 tokens**, for a project whose live instruction is about 25,000 words. **A Volume 14 writer cannot load it, which turns the rolling summary AGENTS.md asks for into the thing that prevents the next volume from being written.** The review of Volume 13 Batch 0005 measured this and called it a blocker.

**The convention that produced it, and why it is changed.** For five cuts the rule was *copy, and leave the live file whole*, and the stated justification was that a copy is safer than a deletion. Both halves are true and they cannot both stand, because leaving the live file whole means the file regrows from the copy point on every subsequent batch: `continuity.md` was rolled at the Volume 08 close down to about 170 KB and had grown back to 1.94 MB, was rolled from 1.94 MB to 178 KB, and had grown back to 984 KB by the Volume 13 close. **A window that is only copied is not a window.** The convention is now **copy first, verify the copy, then trim the live file**, and the verification is one command and not a reading: `git show HEAD:state/<ledger>.md | cmp - state/archive/<ledger>-volumes-01-12.md` must print nothing. **All five files at this cut were verified that way, and no live file was rewritten until all five had passed.**

**What a successor must not inherit from this directory.** **Do not treat the 01-12 files as the oldest state.** Each is the state of a whole ledger at one particular moment in Volume 13 and not a suffix, so a reader who greps one of them for a current figure will find the pre-cut figure and not the post-cut one. **Grep the live file first and the archive second, and read a figure's own basis line before repeating it.** And **do not re-open the rolling question**: it is settled, it is settled in writing, and a phase that wants the ledgers smaller should move the window at a close and copy first, which is what this section describes.
