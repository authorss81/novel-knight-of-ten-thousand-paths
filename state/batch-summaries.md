# Batch Summaries

One entry per completed batch, newest last. This is the durable home the quality gate needs: `logs/` is gitignored, so a batch summary written only into a phase log leaves no record for the next writer.

Rules for an entry:

- Write it after the chapters are finished, not before, and do not pad it to a length. Six to ten lines is usually right.
- Say what the batch **changed**, not what it was about: the new situation at the end, the cost paid, the promise made or broken, and the one question the next batch inherits.
- Record any canon the batch invented that later batches must obey, and put the durable part in `state/continuity.md` rather than leaving it only here.
- The one-line `Last batch summary` field in `state/current.md` names the most recent entry in this file.

## How to read this file, and the rolling window it now sits in

**This file is a rolling window. It holds the whole of the volume currently being written, one entry per completed batch, newest last, and nothing older. Everything older is in `state/archive/batch-summaries-volumes-01-06.md`, verbatim, with every entry exactly as it was here.** As of the review of Batch 0004 this file was **1.03 MB**, and it is now about 100 KB. **Nothing is deleted, only moved, and nothing is renumbered, and a volume is never archived while a close is reading it, so the window moves forward at the Volume 07 close and not before.**

**The live window begins at *Volume 07 — Batch 0001 — Chapters 301–310*. The entries for Volumes 01 to 06, including all five volume closes and every review-repair entry, are in the archive under the same headings.**

**A batch summary is the durable record of a batch, and `logs/` is gitignored, so a summary written only into a phase log leaves no record at all for the next writer.** That is why this file exists and why it is not to be trimmed for length. What may be trimmed is the *age* of an entry, and only by moving it to the archive, never by deleting it.

A salt waggon at the end of a road with nobody under the tailboard is the fen county's furniture and it appeared four times in this block before a repair pass took it down to one, and a clerk reading a slip of paper under a fourth step appeared three times and a man turning three bars appeared six times. The frame-against-frame clause is about the first kind and the raw-token clause is about the second and the third kind needs both, and the honest report is that a block should write a list of its own furniture before it starts and not after.**
