# Batch Summaries

One entry per completed batch, newest last. This is the durable home the quality gate needs: `logs/` is gitignored, so a batch summary written only into a phase log leaves no record for the next writer.

Rules for an entry:

- Write it after the chapters are finished, not before, and do not pad it to a length. Six to ten lines is usually right.
- Say what the batch **changed**, not what it was about: the new situation at the end, the cost paid, the promise made or broken, and the one question the next batch inherits.
- Record any canon the batch invented that later batches must obey, and put the durable part in `state/continuity.md` rather than leaving it only here.
- The one-line `Last batch summary` field in `state/current.md` names the most recent entry in this file.

## Volume 09, Batch 0001 — Chapters 401 to 410, "Nine Words and a Road"

**The block in one paragraph.** Volume 09 opens on a song of nine lines sung in parts by four people at a lime-kiln draw on the beat of a bar coming down on a rim, and eight of the nine lines are practices and the ninth is the name of a road, and the ninth is held. In ten days the protagonist meets four of the people who hold one of the eight, finds that none of them has ever heard of the song and that the practices are everywhere, finds that the ninth word cannot be said for free because a road has nobody in it and therefore nothing to be asked about — and then loses it anyway, in a market, on a Saturday, because a man with a barrow settled an argument about a load of stone by singing all nine lines and about nine people heard the ninth inside nine feet. The plan he made in the first week — eight copies in eight places, none of them in a room, none of them in a column headed *who* — is executed in the last chapter by a sheet-cart that carries about a hundred and four sheets a month into nine counties, arranged by a keeper of records who will not produce the list of nine rooms she named four places from, and witnessed by him. **Three of the eight have already refused the paper in writing and are doing the thing anyway, and the ninth copy is under a stone on a bench in a shed nine feet by seven, in front of a man of about nineteen who can count a barrow of bricks and cannot read it and has not asked anybody to.**

**What the block changed.** The protagonist states in about forty words, standing up, in Chapter 404, that he will hold the ninth copy, and is agreed with in nine minutes, and then the decision is taken out of his hands inside the same chapter by the reason he gave: a man of thirty-two with a limp is on more lists than any other man in nine counties, and about four hundred people once went without a road for four months because a clerk wrote a man's trade in a column. **The relationship work of the volume is done here and not in a scene: the partner does the work, the man with a limp is the witness, and the page does not explain it and does not admire it.** And the book's spine is set: a name that is said is not a name that can be asked about, and the moment nine people have sung it in a market it is nine people and not a road.

**What the block cost.** About forty people heard the ninth word in a market and about nine of them properly; a man of about thirty wrote it on the back of his own hand and put a bar of soap over it in the evening; a boy of seventeen with four hundred entries in a book and a woman of about fifty with about three hundred and seventy figures on four feet of board found nine marks in common and one that neither can read; a man of about forty-one is going to break a hundred-year practice in a fold of ground and has told nobody; a mason of about sixty-eight has been saying a road's name to strangers for forty years and did not know it was a fuse; and the keeper of records did not answer the one question that came back from a road, for about a minute and a half, which is the only time in nine days that anybody in that building was not doing what they were doing.

## Volume 09, Batch 0001 — review repair, applied after the batch

**A review was run over the ten chapters and returned forty findings, seven of them canon-level, and all seven are repaired.** The two that would have cost Batch 0002 the volume: **the man of thirty-three with a barrow had been made to know a ninth line in a room where the ninth line is ruled and empty**, and **one sheet had three incompatible identities** — the bench-board sheet of eight words, the bar's sheet of nine figures, and a drawer in a market town. The ninth line now has a cause on the page: the first eight he has had for four years from the kilns, and the ninth is his own, from a road his father used to send him along. And the distribution is now legible as eight copies in eight places with **three of the eight nowhere at all**, refused by three people in three chapters.

**Also repaired: two inherited figures wrong by a factor of forty and a factor of ten (nine carts in a week for nine carts in four hours; four hundred hearing the song for forty); nine speech-length figures all stated low in the same direction, now nine of nine footed; a board that carried about fourteen thousand figures on four feet, now about three hundred and seventy at nine to the inch; a sheet-count, a quire-count, a day-count and a week-count that did not carry; a roadhouse keeper who was a man in one chapter and a woman in the next; a boy of seventeen played twice as a man of about twenty-four; two men in one room facing opposite ways; and a rule that the plan broke before it was written, which Chapter 407 now says out loud.**
