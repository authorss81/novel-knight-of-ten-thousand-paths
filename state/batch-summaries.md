# Batch Summaries

One entry per completed batch, newest last. This is the durable home the quality gate needs: `logs/` is gitignored, so a batch summary written only into a phase log leaves no record for the next writer.

Rules for an entry:

- Write it after the chapters are finished, not before, and do not pad it to a length. Six to ten lines is usually right.
- Say what the batch **changed**, not what it was about: the new situation at the end, the cost paid, the promise made or broken, and the one question the next batch inherits.
- Record any canon the batch invented that later batches must obey, and put the durable part in `state/continuity.md` rather than leaving it only here.
- The one-line `Last batch summary` field in `state/current.md` names the most recent entry in this file.

## Batch 0001 — Volume 01, Chapters 1–10, "The Nineteenth of Mudmonth"

**Landed.** Chapters 1–10 in `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0001.md` to `chapter-0010.md`, four digits, **40,105 words** (40,075 excluding the title lines), ten complete scenes, POVs Aren ×8, Mara ×1 (Ch 3), Tovan ×1 (Ch 9). Two Gatehouse lines in the whole batch, one at the ringing (Ch 4) and one at the return (Ch 8), as budgeted. **Per chapter:** 3,129 / 3,276 / 3,615 / 3,202 / 3,589 / 3,597 / 5,413 / 4,134 / 3,893 / 6,257. Ch 7 and Ch 10 run long and are the two to watch in review.

**What the batch changed.** Aren bought a cracked bell for two pence, signed a hold and then a six-mark guild bond putting it in his own name, broke an undated promise to ring nothing, crossed on the night of the eighteenth, was gone nine days instead of the three the road-crews' saying allows, and came home to an empty cradle under a Crown seal, a closed road, a lost market, and a fever. He spent the only thing he brought back — the writ's **counterfoil** — in the Bellweather Claims Archive on the thirtieth, with no condition and no copy, together with a sworn account in his own hand that names his own eleven-year silence and both of his omissions. Stage 0, marked, unqualified.

**The state at the end of the batch.**
- **Old Toll bell:** in the watch storehouse by the fish weir, under a Crown seal, **taken off the abutment at first light on the nineteenth of Mudmonth** by Rell's watch, four men and a cart, on an order dated the **sixteenth** and delivered on the evening of the eighteenth. The cradle on the near abutment has been empty **eight days** when he returns on the twenty-seventh, and the bell has now been gone sixteen. Nobody in Merefen can produce the office that authorised it.
- **The Old Toll sill:** usable once and marked. A pale, warm, dry cross the size of two hands where the packing was lifted and set down out of line. **Anchor-glass is now the gate:** two Steppe discs, four marks the pair, money before cutting, ready in a fortnight. No stitch is prepared and none can be until that is paid for.
- **Aren's guild bond:** six marks against a tool-chest worth three and a half, witnessed by Tovan, standing off the guild's removal notice of the eleventh until the sitting on the twenty-fourth — a sitting that will now never happen.
- **The counterfoil:** custody entry and a claim number in the Archive, dated the thirtieth of Mudmonth, three days before the province's renewal, entered under the seal and *beneath* Aren's account at Lysa Fenn's order. Ferris Oat's brine weights are **held and cited, not filed.**
- **Rell's watch book:** a statement entered the twenty-seventh, in the sergeant's hand, including the first public appearance of the phrase *road-keeper of Merefen* and *holder of the Bellweather hinge*. Rell wrote it down not believing it and said so in the room.
- **Lysa Fenn:** a sealed order is not yet decided. The hearing on the seizure is set for the **fourth of Greenmonth**, in the salt-loft room, watch captain under oath, the bridge guild's notice of the eleventh in front of her. She has read the county's schedule of provincial offices and found no keeper's office, and has said the honest answer to the seizure is *yes, it was lawful, and it took three days from the day written on the paper to the morning the bell came off the abutment, and somebody must answer for it.* **Three days is the figure, not eight and not nine** — eight is how long the cradle stood empty when Aren got back, and nine is how long he was gone.
- **What Tovan has refused:** the road, the secret, and being the man holding the span. He has also refused, in Aren's hearing, to be the one who gives a statement about the removal — and he has already been asked for one.
- **The next crossing's bill:** four marks he does not have, and a glasswright in Merefen's batch-two slot. The old Toll sill is the anchor and it is spent.

**Canon the batch invented that later batches must obey.**
1. **The province's renewal is dated for the second of Greenmonth**, not the anniversary. The nineteenth is a Sunday and the Archive is shut on it. A claim entered before the second is a claim of the old year; after it, a claim under a finding that nothing survives. This is the volume's administrative clock.
2. **The "three days" is the road crews' saying**, not a Gatehouse panel: three days to open an old road and three to come back. Volume 01's two-line Gatehouse budget is spent, and the saying is the only warning Aren gets. The drift is therefore six days on the first crossing.
3. **Aren crossed in the clothes he left in**, and the field notes rode in the breast pocket of that same coat. They came back with him. The coat is the same coat and it is the reason the account in Ch 10 can be specific.
4. **His bar went through with him** and came back; it is lying on the Old Toll setts where he set it down in the fourth second of the return.
5. **The Bellweather fever was real** in Bellweather-Living: two hundred and eleven dead of the low quarter in Mudmonth, YR 310, kept in a local ledger, and the number is given to Aren by **the ferryman on the north bank**, not by anybody else. Also on the page: **a woman on Ferry Lane who keeps her shutters shut**, and a man at the tanner's who has not been out in daylight in three years. **The woman on the gallery is a third person and not the Ferry Lane woman** — she is the one who asks *How would you know?* and who says nobody here has ever wanted to see a stranger's hand. Three separate women in Ch 5; do not merge them. The Crown's fever account is a true thing carrying a false one. Held as the moral complication of the whole case.
6. **The third bell** is the crossing-closed bell, rung from the crown at night, once and long. It cracked on the third stroke on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, and that ringing *is* the hinge. The bell is in the path's founder's yard on two oak trundles under a tarred cloth, unrecast.
7. **The path's bell-founder's yard, the toll-house steward's paper, the bench-clerk, the arch's second pier, and the outer ring's eight-inch crack** are all now on the page. The second pier's ring stone is sound; the mortar behind it was raked out from inside by water coming up under the arch. There is no repair that does not begin with the arch out of the water for a season. That is the climax, planted.
8. **Aren's fee in the town ledger: eleven marks and four pence**, itemised in Ch 10 to six lines, of which eleven days' crew pay fell to Tovan and not to the trust. Tovan's own tally in Ch 9 (oak 1s 4d, lime 1s 6d, stone and cartage 2 marks, nails 8d) is the material line of the same column, at two marks three and sixpence, and the two columns agree. It is the smallest number in the book and it is not the point.
9. **Mara's roll of the dead is still unbuilt** and its rule is now stated in her own head: a roll built out of Ferris Oat's ledger must be entered as a ledger, in its own order, with the weights standing beside the names, and never as a speech. Do not spend that in Volume 01.
10. **Hushwater Reach is on the page in Chapter 7**: the crossing book's second page, eleven crossings and nine names, a cooperative that counts **ferry hands and hearths and not souls**, and a best estimate of **near sixty** offered by three assessors and a man from the abbey and defended as a defect of the counting. The erased Hushwater in the main world is eighty. No character compares the two and no chapter may.

## The question Chapter 11 opens on

Chapter 10 closes on the **thirtieth of Mudmonth**, so the day map for the next batch is the only calendar that counts. **The first of Greenmonth is tomorrow**, Chapter 11. **The second of Greenmonth** is the day the province renews its finding that nothing survives, and everything in the file becomes a year weaker — that is the day after tomorrow from the thirtieth, not four days out. **The hearing of the fourth** on the sealed seizure is two days after the renewal, and three days after Chapter 11 opens. Underneath all of it: a bell under a Crown seal that a magistrate can find lawful and cannot un-find; four marks for two discs of Steppe glass that a man with a shilling a day does not have, against a sill that will not carry a second road until it is mended with them; and **the four marks are Tovan's, not Aren's** — see the handoff note below. The path is on its eleventh day without him and has sent nothing, because a path does not write letters and a man at the first stage has no door.

**Two things the handoff must not get wrong.** **The four marks belong to Tovan.** Ch 9 gives them to him twice ("He could carry four marks. He had four marks"), Ch 10 restates it from Aren's side ("a brother who had four marks and a bridge to finish"), and Ch 20's card is explicit that Tovan has the money and will not be asked. Aren has a shilling a day, eleven days of unpaid wages, a bond for six against a chest worth three and a half, and about a mark and a quarter. Nobody may hand the batch's central scarce resource from one brother to the other. **And the bell is one bell, and the page says so now.** Ch 7: Aren states it flatly to the bench — nine stone, split lip to shoulder, the founder's mark on the shoulder is theirs, read in a Merefen scrap yard before he ever came up the north steps; it is under a tarred cloth on two trundles in Bellweather's yard and on an iron cradle on a bridge abutment in Merefen, and he rang it there, and both are true. The Crown's clearance entry in Merefen reads *unclassified salvage* with no city and no river on it, entered in **YR 302, the year after the writ**. That is the volume's hinge and a reader is allowed to hold exactly one belief about it.



## Batch 0002 — Volume 01, Chapters 11–20, "What the Fever Cost Everyone Else"

**Landed.** Chapters 11–20 in `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0011.md` to `chapter-0020.md`, ten complete scenes, POVs Aren ×5 (11, 13, 15, 18, 20) / Mara ×3 (12, 14, 19) / Tovan ×1 (17) / Lysa Fenn ×1 (16). **Zero Gatehouse lines, as budgeted** — Volume 01's two were spent on the ringing and the return, and the silence is the batch's pressure. Six names, all pre-established: Jos Rusk, Bram Ottery, Halven, Pei Tarrow, Sarn Oyelaran, Ilvane Quarren. **57,708 words** for the batch, title lines included, **post-repair**. **Per chapter:** 5,878 / 5,183 / 4,250 / 4,429 / 6,710 / 4,634 / 5,762 / 4,182 / 8,610 / 8,070. Ch 19 and Ch 20 run long and are the two to watch in review; Ch 19 carries five scenes. *(The pre-repair figures were 56,071 and 5,831 / 5,196 / 4,229 / 4,413 / 6,321 / 4,620 / 5,759 / 4,012 / 8,491 / 7,199. Ch 17 is shorter because the Tarrow coda was cut and Ch 20 longer because the ninth is now said aloud on the bridge.)*

**What the batch changed.** The Archive stopped keeping records and became a party. A magistrate found the seizure **lawful** and made the *hole* under it a public record in her own name; the guild's trust stays in abeyance; the bell stays under a Crown seal. A surviving Hushwater ferryman had his cause put in dispute in writing, on purpose, to weaken it. A registrar's fee turned out to be a charge for a search, and the ledger it guards is missing from a shelf it is entered on. A **watch sergeant's unasked statement** went into a court record and named the man in the stair who has been selling the fever since the seventeenth — and the statement cost Ottery his stripes and his accrual, and no promotion. A guild clerk served a finding on seven villages **by name** and left the sum line blank. The Old Toll sill was cut for a pin and set with two Steppe discs paid for out of a **debt against a cutting contract**, not a bond, with a maker's account of how the glass will fail and three hands on a dated undertaking. And the second of three crossings this season is spent on paper for the morning of the ninth.

**The state at the end of the batch.**
- **The Old Toll sill:** a seat cut square in a block of the fourth course, two annealed Steppe discs set edge to edge across the scar, a pin and an oak collar, the maker's mark under the near disc, and **Quarren's page on how it will fail.** A scar is a place a thing came through and can come through again: **two is easy, three is not a number a glasswright will sell.** The sill is now an anchor a man made himself with his own hands and his own money.
- **Aren's money:** four marks paid, of which a mark and a quarter was his own and **two marks and three quarters is a wage advance against Tovan's face-stone contract**, at eight pence a day, carried on if the cutting fails, entered on the guild roll as a debt and not a surety, and never in a sentence with Tovan's name in it. He has nothing left. Eleven days of wages still owed by the trust. The bond of the eleventh, six marks against a chest at three and a half, is still on the roll and the bench votes on it on the **ninth of Greenmonth** while its surety is under an arch.
- **The ninth of Greenmonth:** a market day, the bench's sitting, the provincial summons, and a crossing. **He will not answer the summons.** That is a gap in a provincial ledger, and Lysa Fenn said in advance that a man who does not appear has left a gap and a court that notices a gap will sometimes do something about it.
- **The bells' paperwork:** two instruments, no office behind either, and a finding that says so in a magistrate's hand. The Crown's **YR 302 clearance entry is out of place in a bound order, in a hand the same age as its neighbours**, filed unargued with a copyist's attestation. Nobody explains it and nobody may.
- **Waiting:** the missing-anchor ledger opens on the **fifteenth** if the order is returnable then. Lysa is at the Dunmere assizes on the eighth and ninth, so the ninth's bench sits without her.
- **People:** Ottery relieved of the sergeant's post and of nine and three months' accrual, told by Rell in a corridor, and unrewarded. Rusk has a claim number, a cause in dispute, and a wrapper with a tradesman's hand on it. Pei Tarrow is on Tovan's payroll at the yard rate and has said he is not a symbol. Sarn Oyelaran has a warrant for a third post in the day-book and has begun to be needed. Halven has a gap on a shelf and a date on a request.

**Canon the batch invented that later batches must obey.** Items 51–67 in `state/continuity.md`, including: the currency (**one mark is four shillings / 48 pence**, so four marks is 192 pence and sixteen shillings, and eight pence a day is 24 days — `bible/terminology.md` said 8 shillings and has been corrected to match); the seam of the **quarter-inch bench mark**; the **watch-sergeant's statement** and Ottery's cost; the magistrate's finding of the fourth, clause by clause; the **YR 302 out-of-place clearance entry**; Mara's second wall rule, **no combined figure is entered in this office**; and the **anchor-glass pair in Quarren's rack**, already a year annealed, which is why the slip's fortnight is not the governing term.

Ch 15 no longer asserts a second bell (*either of these papers*), Ottery's statement is dated the **second** of Greenmonth instead of the seventh, the invented thirteenth-of-Mudmonth repacking is gone, and **Rell now puts the twenty-seventh to the court and is shown frightened**, which the card required; Ch 13 no longer prints the four thousand beside the villages and no longer says *in March*; Ch 18's three forward-look dates, the report's hour, and the seventeen-of-Mudmonth contradiction are fixed, and **the chapter now carries no System display at all**; Ch 19's YR 302 question now asks the right year, the clerk is *refused* permission to add the villages, the twenty-four-day figure is consistent in both places, the Tarrow coda inventory was cut, and the three missing card beats (the fee bought a date and not the ledger, the missing-anchor book, the order returnable on the fifteenth) are now on the page; **The question Chapter 21 opens on**

The ninth of Greenmonth, and the bench sits before he is under the arch, and the crown's copy of the finding has seven village names in a clerk's hand and a blank line under them, and a road warden's sworn account of a city of four thousand is the only document in the county that says Bellweather is standing. The path on the other side of the door has been running its own business since the day he got off it and has sent nothing, and a man with a cut scar in a sill and two discs of glass in it is going to find out what a prepared return stitch costs when there is nobody standing at the far end of it and a crown's paper is standing at this one.
