## Hard canon

- World: Neyra, a late-medieval river-and-road world. Years count from the **Quiet Reckoning**; the story opens in **YR 312, Mudmonth**.
- Protagonist: Sir Aren Kest, thirty-two, a former Lantern Guard captain and current bridge repairer in Merefen. Estranged from Tovan; still idealizes Edric.
- Starting mystery: the Bellweather Sundering, **YR 301, Mudmonth**, erased Bellweather and seven villages. The story opens on the eleventh anniversary, in the same season. The Crown blames Aren’s failed bridge defense.
- The seven villages: Alder Cross, Saltbrim, Wrenlow, Cobbett, Nine Elms, Stonebarrow, Hushwater.
- Deeper answer: Marshal Edric Hunn and the Lantern Council ordered a forced cull to contain the Unwritten Wound; the order sacrificed a living city.
- Gatehouse rule: a living path cannot be reset or forcibly closed while conscious life exists inside it. Closing a route removes Aren’s access, not the world.
- **Local custody rule (added in bootstrap):** roads are opened by named local custodians, not by Aren. Two consenting custodians with paired anchors can open a road without him, and path communities can open roads to each other without the main world. Aren is a translator and temporary common anchor, never a necessary mechanism. The ending must show roads working without him.
- Path capacity: one at Stage 0/First Stitch, then two, three, five, seven, and ten at later stages. The final Unowned Road stage removes exclusive personal access.
- Path costs: roadmarks, memory loss, path bleed, lost time, failed workings, social obligations, and permanent local consequences. No path is a disposable map.
- Crossing budget: about three attempted crossings per season at any stage; **three days of fever after a return, at Stage 0 and at Stage 1 alike** — the first crossing in Volume 01 pays the same three days — growing to a month at Stage 6. A return stitch is consumed by use.
- Final enemy: Edric Hunn, using the established First Witness protocol to activate the One Road. No new final enemy is planned.

## Canon established by Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10, prose on the page)

32. **The province's renewal is dated for the second of Greenmonth, not the anniversary.** The nineteenth of Mudmonth is a Sunday and the Bellweather Claims Archive is shut on it with the blinds down. A claim entered before the second of Greenmonth is a claim of the old year; anything after it is entered under a finding that nothing survives and is weaker for a year. The nineteenth is human grief and the second is a clerk in the provincial town copying a finding out of the same book he copied out in 301. **This is the volume's administrative clock and Block 2 runs on it.** The exhibit is dated the thirtieth of Mudmonth, three days ahead of it.
33. **The "three days" is how the warning reaches a roadwarden, and the saying is the same rule.** The Gatehouse's warning at the opening is three days; Volume 01's two-line voice budget is spent on the ringing and the return, so what Aren actually receives is the road crews' saying — *three days to open an old road, three days to come back through it, and a man who has not three days to give should not begin.* **The warning, the saying, the nine days and the six-day drift are all unchanged.** Open question 5 ("what did the warning mean by three, and how is it nine?") stays live and is answered in the middle volumes: nine is six path days plus the three the rule allowed for the crossing itself, and the drift is the six.
34. **Aren crossed in the clothes he left in, and the field notes rode in the breast pocket of that same coat.** They came back with him and they are the physical reason Ch 10's account can be specific about eleven years of silence. He has never handed them over and has never lost them. Do not move them to a chest, a box, or a room.
35. **The bar went through with him and came back.** It is the four-foot iron bar from the Old Toll cradle operation; it is his tool and it is the only object he carried into Bellweather-Living. It lies on the Old Toll setts where he set it down in the fourth second of the return.
36. **The Bellweather fever was real inside Bellweather-Living.** Two hundred and eleven dead of the low quarter in Mudmonth, YR 310, kept in a local ledger. **The number is given to Aren by the ferryman on the north bank**, who also names the woman on Ferry Lane who keeps her shutters shut and the man at the tanner's who has not been out in daylight. **The woman on the gallery is a different person** — she is the one who asks *How would you know?* and who says nobody there has ever wanted to see a stranger's hand. Ch 5 has three separate women in it and Chapter 9 once gave the number to the wrong one; keep the ferryman as the source of two hundred and eleven and keep the Ferry Lane woman distinct from the gallery woman. The Crown's fever account is therefore a **true thing carrying a false one**, and this is the moral complication the case is built on. It is not the Unwritten Wound, nobody in the path uses any word like it, and the path's ledger and the Crown's four thousand are two different documents about two different events.
37. **The third bell is the crossing-closed bell, and it is the hinge — and it is one bell, not two.** Rung from the crown at night, once and long, to close a crossing. It cracked on the third stroke on the night of the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, when the Bellweather Bridge Guild refused the severance writ on the deck — so the refusal and the damage are the same event, and the cracked bell is the anchor and the hinge at once. The path's bell stands in the founder's yard on two oak trundles under a tarred cloth, unrecast and unringed for eleven years. **The main world's bell is that same bell:** it was lifted out of the river at Merefen and entered on a Crown clearance roll in **YR 302, the year after the writ**, as *unclassified salvage*, with no city and no river written on it; it was sold at that clearance for two pence on the first of Mudmonth, YR 312; and it stood on the Old Toll cradle until the watch took it. **Chapter 7 adjudicates this on the page**, in Aren's own flat sentence to the bench: nine stone, split lip to shoulder, the founder's mark on the shoulder is theirs and he read it in a Merefen scrap yard before he ever came up the north steps, and it is in two places, and he rang it in one of them, and he cannot make the two agree. **This is settled fact and is not an open mystery, a time-diverge, a ghost, or a twin.** The bell is an anchor, and an anchor may be in more than one place at once; the Crown's blank clearance entry is the proof that the north has had it and does not know where from. Do not stage a second bell, a copy, a replica, or a mistaken identity, and do not let any character explain it inside Volume 01. Nobody in Volume 01 uses the word *hinge* about the bell except the Crown paper.
38. **The path's immediate problem is on the page and is not Aren's to solve.** An eight-inch crack in the **outer ring of the second pier**, river side, that opens in the cold and shuts in the warmth and is not growing. The ring stone is sound; the mortar behind it was raked out from inside by water coming up under the arch. The honest repair is a new cutwater: nine months of the bench's whole capacity against a debt pledged to the ninth of a grandson. The bench has ordered the pier marked twice a year by two men and a clerk. This is the seed of Block 5's failed public oath and must not be resolved before Chapter 41.
39. **The Old Toll sill is spent, and anchor-glass is the gate.** The packing was lifted and set down out of line, and there is a pale, dry, warm cross the size of two hands in the stone where the working struck it from the other side. Two Steppe discs, cut and annealed to bridge pins, **four marks the pair, ready in a fortnight, money before cutting.** No return stitch can be prepared, and no second crossing can be attempted, until that glass is paid for, cut, and set. This is the batch's forward resource and it is a money problem before it is a magic problem.
40. **The town ledger comes to eleven marks and four pence**, itemised in Ch 10 in six lines, of which eleven days' crew pay fell to Tovan and not to the trust. Tovan's own tally in Ch 9 (oak, lime, stone and cartage, nails) is the material line of the same column and the two must continue to agree. It is the smallest number in the book and it is never to be set beside the four thousand.
41. **Mara's roll of the dead is still unbuilt, and its rule is now on the page in her own head:** a roll built out of Ferris Oat's ledger must be entered as a ledger, in its own order, with the brine weights standing beside the names, and never as a speech. A speech can be answered; a ledger can only be checked. **Do not spend this in Volume 01.** The brine weights are held and cited in Batch 0001; the roll is the Chapter 892 payoff.
42. **What the characters do and do not know at the end of Chapter 10.** Aren knows: the city is standing, in debt, in a morning of its own; a writ was refused on the deck; a real fever was used to carry a false one; he was twenty-one and given a partial order and did not reach the inner city; he has been part of the lie for eleven years by saying nothing; a Crown document calls him a keeper and nobody can find the office; and the man who can be traded for glass is named in a slip nailed in a hut on the Old Toll road. Lysa knows the seal is the same on two papers and the office does not exist, and has refused in front of Mara to discuss the eleven-year-old salvage filing she took from him. 43. **Three dates inside Block 0001's first week are fixed by the prose and must not drift.**

## Relationship state at the end of Chapter 10
