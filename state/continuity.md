# Continuity State

## How to read this file, and why the guide is at the top

**This file is a rolling window. It holds the global canon, the whole of the volume currently being written, and nothing older, and everything older is in `state/archive/continuity-volumes-01-08.md`, verbatim, with every item number exactly as it was here.** The window moved for Volumes 01 to 07 at the Volume 07 close and for Volumes 01 to 08 at the Volume 08 close, **and after that cut this file holds the global canon and nothing else, because Volume 09 has not been written yet.** The reason is arithmetic and it is not a preference. As of the review of Batch 0004 this file was **1.94 MB and about 373,000 words, growing about 1,100 words a chapter, which is roughly a third of the manuscript**, and it was being carried whole into every phase, which is the opposite of what AGENTS.md asks a rolling summary to be. It is now about 170,000 characters, and it stays about that size for as long as the window is maintained.

**Three rules make this safe, and a phase that breaks one of them has broken canon rather than tidied it.**

1. **Items are never renumbered.** 1 to 1000 are fixed and are what they were, and the highest of them, item 1000, is the last item of the Volume 08 close. 646 onward were appended by volume, by batch and by repair pass in the order they were established, and that is unchanged. **An item number means the same thing whether you find it here or in the archive**, and the archive carries the same numbers, so *item 412* is findable either way.
2. **Nothing is deleted, only moved.** The archive is a byte-for-byte copy of what was cut, and the four files under `state/archive/` reconstruct any earlier state of any of the four ledgers exactly.
3. **A volume is never archived while it is being read.** The window moves forward at a volume close, after the close has audited the volume, and not before.

**Read this guide, then read the section for the volume you are actually working on, and do not read the whole thing. For the Volume 09 first prompt the useful reading is: this guide, `state/current.md`, `outline/volumes/volume-08-close.md` §§5, 6, 7, 9 and 10, the seventeen threads under *Threads the Volume 08 close hands to Volume 09* in `state/archive/open-threads-volumes-01-08.md`, and items 984 to 1000 in `state/archive/continuity-volumes-01-08.md`. Everything before item 984 is in the archive and is reachable by grep, not by reading.** **The highest item at any time is the newest, and the number is in `state/current.md`.**

**The archive index, so a number can be found without grepping a megabyte.** `state/archive/continuity-volumes-01-08.md` holds, in order and unaltered, everything `continuity-volumes-01-07.md` holds plus all of Volume 08 and the Volume 08 close: the Volume 01 to Volume 05 batch and close sections, the six Volume 06 sections, the Volume 06 close, the Volume 07 batch sections and its close, the five Volume 08 batch sections, their review-repair sections, and the Volume 08 close's own items 984 to 1000. **Items 1 to 645 are Volume 01 to Volume 05 and are fixed. Items 646 to 897 are Volume 06, Volume 07's batches and the Volume 07 close. Items 898 to 983 are the Volume 07 close's tail and the five Volume 08 batch sections. Items 984 to 1000 are the Volume 08 close. The live window is empty and opens on Volume 09.**

**Items that are marked superseded in part are the point of them, and the markers are the point.** Item 746 is a block device ledger whose seven figures did not reproduce on their own stated basis, and item 748 reported a sweep of a block clean when three of the paragraphs it claimed to have repaired were still on the page. Both are left standing as the first printing and both point forward. A writer who needs a Volume 06 Block 4 figure takes it from item 757, and a writer who needs to know what the block believed takes it from 746 and 748 and knows that nine figures in them were wrong.

**One warning that cost a real error. Do not trust a line-number pointer into a chapter file in this ledger.** Every repair pass inserts and removes lines, so a pointer written by one phase is stale by the next one. Item 697 pointed the four lines of the winter book at `chapters/volume-06/chapter-0266.md:39` and the actual positions were 47; after the Batch 0002 review repair they are 45, 47, 49 and 51. **Refer to a passage by its chapter and its opening words instead, and if you must use a line number, re-derive it in the same turn you use it.**

Bootstrap canon is established in `bible/`, `outline/series.md`, and `outline/ending.md`. **Volume 08 is complete and closed; the close record is `outline/volumes/volume-08-close.md` and it is the authority for items 984 to 1000 and for every figure in this project's current state.**

## Hard canon

- World: Neyra, a late-medieval river-and-road world. Years count from the **Quiet Reckoning**; the story opens in **YR 312, Mudmonth**.
- Protagonist: Sir Aren Kest, thirty-two, a former Lantern Guard captain and current bridge repairer in Merefen. Estranged from Tovan; still idealizes Edric.
- Starting mystery: the Bellweather Sundering, **YR 301, Mudmonth**, erased Bellweather and seven villages. The story opens on the eleventh anniversary, in the same season. The Crown blames Aren’s failed bridge defense.
- The seven villages: Alder Cross, Saltbrim, Wrenlow, Cobbett, Nine Elms, Stonebarrow, Hushwater.
- Deeper answer: Marshal Edric Hunn and the Lantern Council ordered a forced cull to contain the Unwritten Wound; the order sacrificed a living city.
- Gatehouse rule: a living path cannot be reset or forcibly closed while conscious life exists inside it. Closing a route removes Aren’s access, not the world.
- **Local custody rule (added in bootstrap):** roads are opened by named local custodians, not by Aren. Two consenting custodians with paired anchors can open a road without him, and path communities can open roads to each other without the main world. Aren is a translator and temporary common anchor, never a necessary mechanism. The ending must show roads working without him.
- Path capacity: one at Stage 0/First Stitch, then two, three, five, seven, and ten at later stages. The final Unowned Road stage removes exclusive personal access.
- Path costs: roadmarks, memory loss, path bleed, lost time, failed workings, social obligations, and permanent local consequences. No path is a disposable map.
- Crossing budget: about three attempted crossings per season at any stage; **three days of fever after a return, at Stage 0 and at Stage 1 alike** — the first crossing in Volume 01 pays the same three days — growing to a month at Stage 6. A return stitch is consumed by use.
- Final enemy: Edric Hunn, using the established First Witness protocol to activate the One Road. No new final enemy is planned.

## Ending constraints

The ending must preserve Bellweather-Living as a living sovereign community, contain but not magically cure the Unwritten Wound, ratify the Charter of Many Roads, keep Aren and Mara’s relationship voluntary and independent, and leave Edric alive for public judgment.

## Knowledge control

`outline/series.md` contains a per-volume table of who knows what. Do not reveal a fact to any character before the volume listed there. This is the primary tool for protecting the central mystery across 900 chapters.

## Canon clarifications recorded in this phase

1. **Name change.** The Culling Order field warden was renamed **Sera Dain → Sabin Dain** to remove a collision with Sera Quen. Use Sabin Dain in all prose and state.
2. **Name change.** The Lantern Council chancellor was renamed **Veyra Senn → Iselde Senn** to remove a collision with Mara Vey.
3. **Cast discipline.** At most six new named characters per batch. Every recurring character needs one want unrelated to Aren. No supporting character is killed for shock.
4. **The bell has provenance.** Crown salvage, sold at a Merefen clearance for two pence, bought by Aren as scrap bronze in the opening Mudmonth. It is a damaged anchor, not a key or a summons.
5. **Mara’s archive is the main-world Bellweather Claims Archive** in Merefen’s salt-loft rooms. It is not the living archive inside Bellweather-Living, which is run by Hollis Dree and refuses to surrender copies.
6. **Relationship policy is decided:** one slow-burn relationship, Aren and Mara Vey. No harem.
7. **Aren’s age arithmetic.** Aren is 32 at the opening and was **21** at the Sundering (born YR 280, apprenticed at twelve, Guard at seventeen, commissioned by brevet at twenty-one). This is deliberate: he was the youngest captain in the Guard, which is why Edric chose him, why the Crown can blame a boy, and why Aren believed his own defence for three years. Do not rewrite the Sundering as a child being made to do it alone. He held a real command and really failed it.
8. **The Rusk families are separated.** Jos Rusk, the Hushwater ferryman, survives and lives in Merefen; his claim is the first one Mara Vey personally refutes, and he is wrong about the cause. His wife **Anwen Rusk** (37, who kept the Hushwater ferry’s crossing book) and daughter **Bryn Rusk** (11) are among the erased and are now named outright instead of being left as “the wife and daughter.” The recurring Merefen ferryman formerly called Pei Rusk is **Pei Tarrow** (52, Hushwater survivor’s son, freight boats on the Old Toll channel). He is no relation to Jos Rusk. Do not stage the two men together, and do not let a bystander call Pei a Rusk.
9. **Hanna Vey and Mara Vey are not related.** The shared surname is deliberate common naming from the Sable river country, not a plot relation, not a mistake, and not a reason to rename either of them later. Let Mara notice the coincidence once, early and coldly, and never return to it. No scene may imply kinship.
10. **Toll figures are locked.** Bellweather city: about **four thousand**. The seven villages: about **seven hundred and twenty** (Alder Cross 200, Saltbrim 90, Wrenlow 60, Cobbett 140, Nine Elms 110, Stonebarrow 40, Hushwater 80). The Crown quotes the city figure alone and folds the villages into it; the Archive keeps the two numbers apart on every page and never prints a combined total. In a book whose moral weight rests on exact arithmetic, a character arguing about the dead must use the number actually being argued about.
11. **Dispatch reality, for the record.** The runner selects the first `workspace/**/PROMPT.md` in sorted order that has no `.done` or `.blocked` marker. `state/phase-ledger.json` and `PHASE_SYSTEM.md` are controller-owned, are never written by the writer, and the ledger is not what dispatch reads — so the only protection against a repeated batch is the prompt files themselves. Consequence: **exactly one prompt may exist per batch.** The duplicate `workspace/phase-002-batch-plan/` prompt, which also asked for Chapters 1–10, was deleted during the phase-000 fix pass. `workspace/volume-01/batch-0001/PROMPT.md` is the single prompt for the first batch and pairs with `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md`. The `.wip-conflict` marker in `workspace/phase-000-bootstrap/` only gates WIP-branch resume and is cleared by the runner on completion; leave it alone.
12. **The named dead are records, not cast.** Ferris Oat, Hanna Vey, Corin Slate, Bess Alder, Anwen Rusk, and Bryn Rusk are read aloud, quoted, filed, and argued over; they do not act on the page and do not count against the six-new-named-characters-per-batch limit. That limit applies to characters who speak, decide, or obstruct. A survivor or claimant who acts is a character and does count: Jos Rusk and Pei Tarrow are both cast, not records.

## Canon clarifications recorded in the Volume 01 outline phase

13. **The Sundering's date is the nineteenth of Mudmonth.** The story opens on the sixth of Mudmonth, YR 312, so the eleventh anniversary falls thirteen days later, and the storm comes up on the night of the eighteenth into the nineteenth. Volume 01 opens on the sixth of Mudmonth and closes in the last week of Fallowmonth, about five months of main-world time, matching the series time ledger.
14. **Aren's aunt is dead.** She raised him after Ilyra's crossing and died of the low-quarter fever three winters before the opening, so Aren has no household, rents one room above a cooper on Weir Street, and nobody at home has to be told where he is. The person who holds his tools and covers for him is Tovan.
15. **The bell's economics and dates are fixed.** Aren buys the bell on the first of Mudmonth, YR 312, at the Crown clearance for two pence, **five days** before Chapter 1 opens on the sixth. Chapter 2 is the **eleventh**, the day the Merefen bridge guild's removal notice reaches him, so the notice is dated the day it arrives — eight days before the anniversary, and before any storm. The guild's standing valuation is **six marks** as scrap bronze, because the crack is worth the difference and the roll is right. In Chapter 2 Aren cannot raise six marks and signs a guild bond for the sum with his tool-chest as surety. He refuses Tovan's honest six-mark offer and takes the bond instead, on purpose: the metal buys nothing, a bell in a yard belongs to somebody else, and a bond puts the bell in his name in front of the guild. He has chosen to be the man answerable for it, and he never says so to anyone. The hold he signs in Chapter 1 is good until the guild's next sitting, **the twenty-fourth** — and nothing happens on the twenty-fourth, because a bench cannot act against a man who is not in front of it and Aren is nine days gone. That is the town's own process for a cracked bell, and the watch's sealed order replaces it three days later, on the twenty-seventh, without anybody in Merefen getting to choose between them. The bond lapses when the watch takes the bell, which is a smaller humiliation than the market days the closed road costs.
16. **Return stitches cannot be improvised for free.** Aren rings the bell without having prepared a return stitch, which is the mistake that shapes the batch: he is stranded for six path days, loses **nine** days of the main-world calendar against a Gatehouse warning of three, and returns on the twenty-seventh to a bell already seized. A working time-diverge warning in this series is wrong by days or weeks, never silently by months, and the drift is stated in the prose as a warning that was wrong.
17. **The path's own answer comes from two institutions.** The Bellweather Bridge Guild kept the **counterfoil** — the stub proving a severance writ was sent to Bellweather-Living on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, refused on the deck of the arch and withdrawn. The **outgoing writ register** naming the signatory is kept by the toll houses, who are the guild's political opponents. The volume midpoint reveal lives in the toll-house counting room, not the guild hall.
18. **Path-side vocabulary is the volume's argument.** Bellweather-Living maintains its crossing through **stewards**; a steward maintains. The corrupted Crown term is **private keeper**, and a provincial document applies it to Aren during Batch 0001. The legitimate word *custodian* is not used in Volume 01. The word "keeper" fitting Aren is a Volume 01–03 hook and is never explained in Volume 01.
19. **Batch 0001's six names are fixed:** Tovan Kest, Mara Vey, Wenna Koll, Lysa Fenn, Captain Rell, Sera Quen — all six already established in `bible/characters.md` with a want, a flaw, and a function, and named in prose for the first time in this batch. Jos Rusk, Bram Ottery, Halven, Pei Tarrow, Sarn Oyelaran, and Ilvane Quarren are Batch 0002. Bram Ottery therefore **does not carry out the bell seizure in Chapters 8–10**: the seizure is Rell's watch, and its sergeant is addressed by office and left unnamed so the six-name cap is not spent on a man who performs one errand. Bram enters by name in Volume 02, when somebody has to answer in writing. Rell's unlawful seizure remains the fact recorded against him. A full batch-by-batch cast plan is in `outline/volume-01.md`.
20. **Batch 0001 uses two POVs plus one.** Aren in eight chapters, Mara in Chapter 3, Tovan in Chapter 9. The path-side institutions in the batch are addressed by office — the bench, the bench-clerk, the hull-master, the toll-house steward — so the six-name cap is not spent on people who only exist to be quoted.
21. **Two Gatehouse lines in the whole first batch,** one at the ringing and one at the return, both plain and both technically true in a way that conceals the moral cost.
22. **Planted in Batch 0001, owed later.** The bell bought for two pence (Chapter 1); the Merefen bridge guild's removal notice **dated the eleventh, the day it arrives** — before the storm and before any crossing (Chapter 2), which pays off when the watch's sealed seizure order turns out to be dated the same week (Chapter 8); Ferris Oat's brine weights, **held and cited but not filed** — the extract Mara trades for his promise (Chapter 3) — with the formal entry of the named roll of the dead reserved for **Chapter 892**, where it is the first document the Charter names; the bell ringing in the anniversary storm (Chapter 4); the empty iron cradle left on the near abutment after the seizure (Chapter 8), which is **not** still empty in Chapter 50 — the town reclaims its own iron during the Block 5 resolution, bands the crack, and the bell returns to the bridge without ever returning to Aren; Aren's deferred field notes and his three years of repeating the official fever account, disclosed in his own hand in Chapter 10, together with the two omissions he files alongside them. These are the Volume 01 setups named in `outline/ending.md`, and the writer must not add a second version of any of them. In particular, the brine weights are a cited authority in Volume 01 and a Chapter 892 payoff; filing them as an exhibit in Chapters 10 or 48 spends the ending's beat early and must not happen.
23. **Volume 01's cast discipline on the Culling Order.** No named member appears in Volume 01. Sabin Dain enters in Volume 2. The phrase "Culling Order" may appear at most once in Volume 01, in a document or a claimant's mouth, with no member attached to it.
24. **Hushwater Reach exists in the path.** Bellweather-Living has a living hamlet on a channel that is drowned in the main world, and its ferry cooperative keeps a crossing book in a living hand with different names in it. The cooperative counts **ferry hands and hearths, not souls**, so nobody can put a number on the hamlet; the best estimate in play is **near sixty**. That is deliberately far from the **eighty** erased at Hushwater in the locked toll figures, because a living hamlet of exactly eighty beside an erased village of exactly eighty is a signpost and this volume is only allowed a hint. No character notices the gap, and no one explains the coincidence in Volume 01. The thread stays held until Volume 09.
25. **The first crossing's calendar is locked, and so is the fever.** Crossing one opens on the night of the eighteenth into the nineteenth of Mudmonth and returns on the **twenty-seventh**; path time inside it is six days. The fever takes **three days — the twenty-seventh, twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth — and is over on the thirtieth**, which is the day Chapter 10 opens. The thirtieth is not a fourth day of illness and must not be called one; write it as the day the fever breaks. Three days is the figure in the crossing budget above and in `outline/volume-01.md`; `outline/ending.md` states no fever length and is not a source for it. The cost does not grow until Stage 6.
26. **The batch's day map is the only calendar the first ten chapters use.** Ch 1 the sixth · Ch 2 the eleventh · Ch 3 the twelfth · Ch 4 the night of the eighteenth into the nineteenth · Ch 5–7 path time with no main-world clock · Ch 8 the twenty-seventh · Ch 9 the twenty-seventh to the thirtieth · Ch 10 the thirtieth. The thirteenth to the eighteenth is a stated cut: no visitor, no document, no arrest, no further bell sound. **The bell stays on its cradle across that cut because of the twenty-fourth hold in item 15, not because a date was missed.** Volume 01's own time budget in `outline/volume-01.md` now lists the same days.
27. **Two guilds, two names, no crossing between them.** "The guild" in the main world is the **Merefen bridge guild**: a town's craft body of bridgewrights and road-wardens holding the Old Toll crossing in municipal trust, keeping the scrap roll the bell's six-mark valuation comes from. The path's institution is always the **Bellweather Bridge Guild**. They never appear in each other's world in Volume 01, and a chapter must not let "the guild" be ambiguous about which one it means.
28. **The promise Aren makes to Mara is undated.** She asks for a promise that the bell will not be rung, and refuses to put a date on it, so Chapter 4 is a broken promise rather than a technicality. Do not restore the earlier wording, which limited the promise to "before the renewal." The batch owns **two omissions, holding three facts**: to Mara, the promise with no date on it and the night he has already chosen; to Tovan, the reason he refused six marks and signed a bond. Both omissions appear in the written account he files in Chapter 10, which is why the second is what makes the first credible.
29. **The Volume 01 drift ceiling.** The first crossing's warning is wrong by six days and the third by three weeks. `outline/series.md` now allows three days to three weeks of uncertainty at return for Volume 01, and three weeks is the ceiling at this scale. Do not drift further in this volume, and do not drift by months.
30. **Chapter files, batch summaries, and review records have fixed homes.** Chapters go to `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0001.md`, four digits, matching the card labels `### Chapter 0001` in `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md` (`PHASE_SYSTEM.md` is the authority and is controller-owned; this entry records the convention where the writer will actually see it). A completed batch's summary is appended to `state/batch-summaries.md`, and the one-line "Last batch summary" field in `state/current.md` points at the most recent entry. Reviewer findings are persisted under `reviews/`, not left in `logs/`, which is gitignored.
31. **Dispatch guards, standing version of item 11.** Do not create a second prompt for a batch that already has one; if a phase's work tempts a new prompt directory, it is a new batch and not a new copy of this one. And **`state/phase-ledger.json` is controller-owned, is never written by the writer, is stale by design, and is not what dispatch reads** — the prompts themselves are the dispatch list, and `AGENTS.md` says to update the ledger while `.opencode/agent/novel-writer.md` forbids it. The runner's own restore list does not include the ledger, so nothing will undo a careless edit; the only protection is the agent rule and the fact that no one reads the field. Leave it alone: a writer who "fixes" it is the one most likely to break the one-prompt-per-batch protection.
