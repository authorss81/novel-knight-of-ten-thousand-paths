## The two findings the last two batches made about their own prose, and what was done

**Batch 0002 substituted a narrator formula for the scenes.** The formula *about four people in that building have said since that* reached seventy-four uses in 23,184 words, quoted dialogue fell to 170 words in ten chapters, and two chapters had none. The review reversed the block's own verdict, and the repair cut the formula from 74 to 25 and `about four people` from 84 to 38 and spent the recovered space on what a room does.

**Batch 0003 took that as the rule and the numbers bear it out.** The narrator reporting clause is at **12** uses and `about four people` at **20**, against Batch 0001's 70 and 68. Quoted dialogue is **2,276 words** across 111 speeches, against Batch 0002's 224 and Batch 0001's about 1,855, and **no chapter in the block is without dialogue.** `about` is 461, at 19.81 per 1,000 words, against Batch 0001's 19.8 and Batch 0002's repaired 24.3. **The lesson to carry: a block with a field, a chamber and a book in it will run a high `about` and that is fine, and a block where `about` is high because the scenes have been replaced by assertions about the scenes is not fine, and the two figures to watch are the reporting clause and the dialogue count, not the `about` count.**

**And the paragraph-scoped sweeps still cannot see the defect that matters.** Batch 0002's bell specification was written out three times and both the duplicate-paragraph sweep and the near-repeat sweep reported the block clean, because both are paragraph-scoped and the defect lived inside a paragraph. **Batch 0003 ran the clause-level comparison and it found seven pairs at 0.60 or above, including one at 1.00 where Chapter 277 reproduced Chapter 259's description of the sealed list word for word over about fifty words, and all seven were cleared.** A Batch 0004 writer should run the clause-level comparison on the named pairs first, before the paragraph sweeps, and should treat a near-zero from a loose shingle basis as no result at all.
