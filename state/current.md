## What Block 3 is, in one paragraph

A boundary oath-stone in a fen county is renewed in a field in the open for the first time in nineteen years, and a clerk of about sixty keeps the second kind of minute he has ever kept, and the minute describes a crossing with no owner and no money, a sill with nothing in it, a dressed block and a bell, and a word in front of the place name is read out loud in that field. Eight days later a sheet of that same series is in the sill of that crossing with a woman's name in a *Keeper* column, and a man of thirty-two with a limp puts a hand on a thing in a county he is standing in, and nobody is asked, and there is no bridgekeeper at his shoulder, and the moment is not written. The cut takes the anchor: forty yards of tarred hemp four feet down are gone, and a bell a hundred yards off goes on being a standing fact of a county that heard nothing. And then the reversal, which is a complication and not a vindication: the thing he has been reading as a record is a list of what was taken, because a document with a keeper in it and no outcome in it is not a record of a journey but a record of custody. His mother is on the eleventh line. She is not saved and not lost and is not resolved. The price is another crossing and it is refused out loud in a yard. And a woman of sixty-six who came in with nobody says, once, in an aisle four feet wide, that a hearing is a column with a roof on it and the roof is the part that does the damage.

## The two findings the last two batches made about their own prose, and what was done

**Batch 0002 substituted a narrator formula for the scenes.** The formula *about four people in that building have said since that* reached seventy-four uses in 23,184 words, quoted dialogue fell to 170 words in ten chapters, and two chapters had none. The review reversed the block's own verdict, and the repair cut the formula from 74 to 25 and `about four people` from 84 to 38 and spent the recovered space on what a room does.

**Batch 0003 took that as the rule and the numbers bear it out.** The narrator reporting clause is at **12** uses and `about four people` at **20**, against Batch 0001's 70 and 68. Quoted dialogue is **2,255 words** across 111 speeches, against Batch 0002's 224 and Batch 0001's about 1,855, and **no chapter in the block is without dialogue.** `about` is 460, at 19.8 per 1,000 words, against Batch 0001's 19.8 and Batch 0002's repaired 24.3. **The lesson to carry: a block with a field, a chamber and a book in it will run a high `about` and that is fine, and a block where `about` is high because the scenes have been replaced by assertions about the scenes is not fine, and the two figures to watch are the reporting clause and the dialogue count, not the `about` count.**

**And the paragraph-scoped sweeps still cannot see the defect that matters.** Batch 0002's bell specification was written out three times and both the duplicate-paragraph sweep and the near-repeat sweep reported the block clean, because both are paragraph-scoped and the defect lived inside a paragraph. **Batch 0003 ran the clause-level comparison and it found seven pairs at 0.60 or above, including one at 1.00 where Chapter 277 reproduced Chapter 259's description of the sealed list word for word over about fifty words, and all seven were cleared.** A Batch 0004 writer should run the clause-level comparison on the named pairs first, before the paragraph sweeps, and should treat a near-zero from a loose shingle basis as no result at all.
