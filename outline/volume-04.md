## 1. Volume identity

Volume 01 asked who has the right to decide about a living city that survived an official cull, and answered *nobody, and here is the sheet with two names on it.* Volume 02 asked what a record owes the person it describes, and answered *a promise is only as wide as the people who have agreed to it, and a hall is not a person.* Volume 03 asked how you keep a promise that has been kept for a hundred and forty years and is killing everybody who keeps it, and answered *with a door, and the door is made of the same material as the thing that was killing you.*

**Volume 04 asks the first question in the book that cannot be answered by a document at all: why do some path inhabitants recognise a man as a carrier rather than as a visitor — and what is a promise nine generations have kept when the ninth generation has never been asked what it agrees to?**

The Ninefold Winter is a valley under a winter that has lasted nine generations. It is physically stable. Nothing is collapsing. The river runs, the stores hold, the births are normal, and **nobody in the valley is in danger in any way a relief could fix.** The trap is political: the ninth generation has been told, from the day it was born, that it is a generation of carriers, and has never been asked whether it agrees, and the whole of the valley's government, economy and marriage custom is built out of an unasked question.

**The engine is a roll.** Nine generations of a valley that has been sending people out for a very long time, and **a list of who went and who came back and who was called a carrier for it, kept in the winter book, and a young man of the ninth generation who is on it and does not know by what hand.** That is the volume's whole plot, and it is the Marches' lesson turned on the protagonist: **in Volume 03 an office put a man in a column he did not fill in and made him answerable for a road he keeps and cannot refuse. In Volume 04 a community put a man in a column he did not fill in and made him the heir to a winter.** The difference between the two is the whole of the volume, and the difference is consent.

**At the centre of it a man who can cross and cannot stop crossing, who has been walking with a stick for months, who has not told one person in the world the order an ordinary morning happened in, and who arrives in a valley where people greet him by a name nobody put him in a column for.**

## 2. Central pressure

Four parties want four different things out of a nine-generation promise, and the volume's engine is that three of them are right.

- **The valley council** wants **the ninth generation to keep the winter.** It has kept it nine times by asking, in public, of people who are still alive, and it has never once been refused, and it has never once been asked whether keeping it is the same thing as agreeing to it. **Iven Sarr**, 58, who keeps the winter book, wants the original oath found and read in the room it was made in, **and he is right about that and wrong about what finding it will do, and no one in the volume may call him a villain.** It is the Marches with the salt taken out and the stone in a warmer place.
- **The ninth generation** wants **not to be a column.** About two hundred people, alive, on a roll, in a valley with no writ to answer and no bell to ring. They have been named carriers since birth by families that had a roll and no other way to say what they were. **Their want is to be asked, and their want is also that the asking not happen in a form, and those two things are in tension and the tension is the volume.**
- **A valley of bridgekeepers** wants **the winter to end and the road to open.** Nine generations of keeping a thing have produced nine generations of people who know how to build a road and have never been allowed to use the skill, and the youngest of them is thirty-four. **Wystan Roole** wants the road and is right that a valley that cannot leave is a valley that cannot argue with anybody about anything.
- **The Office of Provincial Registration** wants **a complete register of carrier obligations.** It has no file on the valley, it has a form with a column headed *Keeper* and a marginal instruction it printed itself, and **it has learned in the last eleven months that a count of persons is lawful, names nobody, needs no court, and cannot be refused without refusing a figure.** **It is an office. It is doing its job. It is the most dangerous thing in this volume and it never appears.**

**The volume question:** *what is a promise that nine generations have kept when the ninth generation has not been asked?*

## 4. Starting power state, and the shape of reaching Threadkeeper

**Stage 2 going in, proved.** Two routes held and the holding of one not the holding of the other; two carryovers of untraceable origin; one message and it must be a list; one object under a pound, two half tiles; a return prepared by two hands, a date, and a witness at each end. **One route closed in writing and not lost.** A permanent road-marked left palm, warm at about the fourth hour and not needed. **A notebook with an ordinary morning in it he can do and cannot put in order. And a limp, and a stick for four hundred yards of road.**

**The decision this volume makes.** `outline/series.md` gives **the Second Door to Volumes 2–3 and Threadkeeper to Volumes 4–6**, and `bible/power-system.md` gives Threadkeeper's ability as three paths, path-lineage reading and a limited forced-connection cut, and its qualification as **one consensual cut made while a local bridgekeeper remains present, and then helping the same community repair the physical scar before the route is declared stable.** `outline/series.md` gives this volume a **sixteen-month path interval, the longest in the manuscript.** Therefore:

- **Stage 3 is reached in Block 5 and not before.** No lineage is read in Blocks 1–4 and no cut is made, attempted, offered as a plan, priced, or described as available by anybody.
- **Three paths held at the close of Volume 04: Bellweather-Living, the Hearth Marches, and the Ninefold Winter valley.** Two to three is the whole of the movement, and **the limit is a capacity and not a queue of disposable maps.**
- **The volume's second power movement is a refusal, and it is a document.** A fourth route is offered, cheaply, that would open for him alone and cost the offering community nothing, and taking it would mean the valley does not have to be paid for. **He refuses it in writing.** It is the second refusal in a document in two volumes, and Dain's is the first, and the two are in different counties and neither party knows the other exists.
- **The cost is not a limp.** Every cut leaves a visible seam in the current world and gives the affected path a lasting memory of him. **The seam in the current world is in a bridge, and that is why it is visible, and it is the first thing in two hundred chapters that a stranger can see and not explain.** **The Many-Handed stage's worsening of the limp belongs to Volume 15 and Volume 04 adds no second bodily cost.** The fever after the return is a week, because the stage earns it.
- **And the Volume 06 row of the failure table is Volume 06's and is not scheduled here.** *A cut made on a false witness: the community's own anchor tears, contact ends, sometimes for good.* **The risk is carried forward as known-and-refused — the refusal planted in Chapter 124 on a date and a community and a forged witness, and unexplained — and no consequence of a false-witness cut is scheduled, forecast with a date, or priced in this volume.**

### 5.2 The Ninefold Winter (the path)

A valley under a winter nine generations old, and the winter is **kept**, not survived. Rolling country on a river the valley calls the Burn, with a market town, about eleven steadings and a scatter of hamlets, **and a road that has not been made in nine generations and is made in this one.**

- **A carrier in this valley is a person who has gone out of it and been named for it, and the naming is the whole of the institution.** There is no magic in the word and no blood in it and the valley does not know that. **The roll of carriers is nine generations deep and it is kept in the winter book and it is the only roll in the valley**, because a valley with a roll of who has gone is a valley that expects people to leave, and a valley that expects people to leave builds a road, **and this valley has not built a road, and the reason it has not built a road is on the roll.**
- **The winter is held.** Not by the promise alone. **The ninth generation's promise is the public half of a thing that is held underneath by a working nine generations old, and the working is straining, and the strain is what the ninth generation can feel and cannot name.** No main-world month name and no weekday of this world is spoken in a valley scene, **and the valley keeps no months at all: it counts winters, and a generation is a winter, and there have been nine.**
- **The winter book** — a stone room over the byre, and a roll nine generations deep, and a hand in it that is not any hand anybody alive can name. **Iven Sarr keeps it and has wanted it read by somebody else for six years.**
- **The ninth generation** — about two hundred people, on the roll from birth, **in a valley with no writ to answer and no bell to ring**, who have been named by families and not by choice, and who have never been asked.
- **The bridgekeepers.** Nine generations of keeping a thing have produced a valley that can build a road and has never been allowed to use the skill. **They have a craft guild of about sixteen men and no work.**

## 6. Major factions

Each has a want that survives Aren leaving the valley. **None is a monster and two are better than he is.**

- **The valley council.** Wants **the ninth generation to keep the winter.** It has kept it nine times by asking in public of people who are still alive and has never been refused, and it has never once been asked whether keeping is the same as agreeing. **It is not a rescue and must not be written as one. It argues about a road.**
- **The ninth generation.** Wants **not to be a column.** About two hundred people, alive, on a roll, in a valley where a person is recorded by being named. **They have never been asked what they swore, and the asking is the only thing they want, and they want it not to be in a form.** Their want is a record and not a wage, and they are the volume's mirror of the Marches' sixty and **the book must not say so.**
- **The bridgekeepers.** About sixteen men who can build a road and have no work and want the winter to end. **They are right that a valley which cannot leave is a valley which cannot argue with anybody about anything.**
- **The Office of Provincial Registration.** Wants **a complete register of carrier obligations.** It has no file on the valley, it has a form with a column headed *Keeper*, and **it has discovered in the last eleven months that a count of persons is lawful, names nobody, needs no court, and cannot be refused without refusing a figure.** **It is an office. It is doing its job.**
- **The winter itself.** Not a faction. **A fact, and the antagonist of the volume, and it is not named by anybody as an antagonist because it is not a person.**

## 7. The mechanics this volume runs on

1. **A road is a line and two hands, a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it.** Carried forward whole from `outline/volumes/volume-03-close.md` §8 item 2. **If the witness is not there the road does not open and it does not fail, and nobody is to say afterwards that it failed.** In Volume 04 this is the engine of the crossing **out** and the engine of the return, because **his return stitch is destroyed and he has to find another living anchor.**
2. **A record is a person who is not present.** Carried forward whole. **A roll of carriers is a claim about two hundred people and it is the only roll in the valley, and a valley with one roll of who has gone is a valley where a name is the whole of a person.**
3. **A column you did not fill in is the disease, and Volume 04's contribution is that a community can do it to itself as efficiently as an office.** **The Marches put a household in a list and entered the danger at the head of the list. The valley put two hundred people in a roll at birth and entered nothing, because a roll of carriers is not a list of anybody to be struck and there is no form on which to enter the danger.** **This is worse and it is the volume's finding.**
4. **A form is a machine for making an office out of a word, and the machine is running, and in this volume it is offered a subject it wants.** The marginal instruction may be printed, quoted in a return, cited in a file and refused about in a gateway. **It may not be explained in a sentence by anybody, and Sabin Dain may not be the person who explains it.**
5. **A count is a list with the names taken off, and the office has learned to prefer the count.** The Crown's instrument in this volume is **a proposed count of carrier obligations in a valley with no writ**, returnable on a named day, **with a space for a figure and no space for a name.** A magistrate answers it with the form's own construction, **the instrument is spent the moment it is answered, and the office's reply is a bigger count.** **No name in a Crown's hand, and there is still not one.**
6. **Asking is the instrument, and the refusal is the result, and the two are the same size.** **The first asking of the ninth generation is refused, and the refusal is the correct answer and it is what protects them, and the second asking is consented to, and the difference between the two askings is what the argument is.** **A refusal recorded against a person is a list. A refusal recorded about a class of persons is a document.** Carried from Volume 03's class-of-persons entry, which was got at a woman's own asking and named nobody.
7. **A promise that can only be kept by breaking something else is a claim, not a record.** Carried forward from Volume 03, where it was arrived at by a ditchman in a shed with a rule on it. **In Volume 04 it is arrived at in the winter book, in a different hand, and by a person with no stake in the Marches at all, which is the test Volume 03 could not give it.**

## 8. Escalation sequence — five blocks, Chapters 151–200

### Block 1 · Chapters 151–160: The Relief

*Main world, the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth to the seventh of Goatmonth, YR 313, and then path time. A document road opened by somebody who is not Aren. A count answered. A man asked to find an oath.*

**Ch 151 · the twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, a Saturday, a market day, the fourth hour · the board under the stair · Aled Rhys.** **The volume's first act is a person who is not Aren using one of the things Volume 03 gave away.** A water-barge hand of twenty-seven from the Hearth Marches, in Merefen on the crew's account and paid, **comes up two flights of a stair and reads a fair copy off a board under the bench, because anybody coming up two flights may read it, and asks for it to be copied.** Nobody has to be asked. ### Block 2 · Chapters 161–170: The Roll

*Path time, the first winter of sixteen. The winter book. The roll. The first asking, and the refusal.*

**Ch 161–163 · the winter book, and a hand nobody can name.** **Iven Sarr**, 58, who has kept the winter book for nineteen years and has wanted it read by somebody else since the first year and has had two readings and one of them lasted a night. **The roll is nine generations deep and it is a list of who went out and who came back and who was called a carrier for it, and the ninth generation is on it from birth, in a hand that is not any hand anybody alive can name, and the hand that wrote the ninth generation's line wrote it before the ninth generation was born.** That is the volume's mystery and it is a mystery about a **hand**, not about a man, and a press-mark proves a book and not a man and a hand is not a press-mark and the volume knows the difference.
**And the mechanism, once, in trade terms, by a person with no stake in the Marches: the winter is kept by a working nine generations old, and the working is not a promise, and a promise cannot hold a thing for nine generations, and this is why the ninth generation is asked to keep it every year — because a promise has to be renewed or it stops, and a working does not, and the renewal is the price of the working being a promise at all.** Nobody explains the mechanism. It is said in the language of a valley that builds roads, and it is said once, and it is not restated.

**Ch 164–166 · the two hands, and the first asking, and the refusal.** **Wystan Roole**, 34, a bridgekeeper of the ninth generation, sixteen men in a craft guild with no work, who wants the road and is right that a valley which cannot leave is a valley which cannot argue with anybody about anything. **Nev Treen**, 66, of the eighth generation, who was asked what she swore and answered, and who is the reason the ninth generation has not been: **because she was asked in a room with a witness and it took nine minutes and it was the worst nine minutes of her life and she has told the ninth generation so, and she told them it was survivable, and she was wrong, and she knows she was wrong.** **And the volume's first asking, which is the asking the whole volume is built on: he asks the ninth generation to be asked what they swore, and they refuse the asking.**
**Not him. The asking.** In about four seconds, from a young man of twenty-two, and the reason is one sentence and it is the sentence the Marches spent fifty chapters earning: **a name in a column is a thing you cannot get out of, and the only person anybody has ever been able to get out of one had written on the back of a leaf, two years before the return, that he did not put his name there.** **And then the second half, and it is the volume's argument: they do not refuse the question. They refuse the form of it. They want the asking and not the form, and nobody in that valley has ever worked out how to want a thing and not have a page, and neither has he.**

**Ch 167–170 · the trade, and the cost of it, and the main world without him.** Ordinary labour in a valley that cannot leave: **stone, timber, a road gang that is not allowed to build a road and builds a byre wall instead, and it is the best wall anybody in the valley has seen and it is the wrong wall.** The first drift figure of the volume, **written in the valley's own register and refused in his own notebook, because the valley keeps no months and he has a number and no frame, which is the second time in the manuscript a character has been unable to convert and the first time he has had the number and not the frame.** **The main world runs on without him and the audience for it is a hearing in a room over a weigh-house on a Sunday, on the ninth of Rainmonth, for a delegation that cannot arrive, which stands, and which somebody has to sit in, and the person who sits in it is not him.**

### Block 3 · Chapters 171–180: The Office in an Older Hand

*Path time, the second and third winters. The record. The question the series handed on. And a second asking, about a different thing.*

**Ch 171–174 · the older administration, and an office and not a man.** The valley's own records, in a room behind the winter book, contain **a hand that is an office's hand and not a person's, in a form nobody in the valley has read for nine generations, and the office is named and the office is the Office of Provincial Registration's own ancestor, and the thing on the form is a count with no name in it, and the count is of carrier obligations, and it is two hundred years old and it was never answered and it was never withdrawn.**
**This is where `outline/series.md`'s Volume 4 escalation line is paid, and this close pays it in an office and not in a name — see §16.** The consequence: **the valley has been keeping a winter that an office asked it to keep, for two hundred years, through nine generations, in a form, and nobody in the valley ever read the form because the form is a form.** And the finding that carries the volume: **an office is the most durable thing in the book. A county can break an oath in public. A family can burn a roll. An office waits.**

**Ch 175–177 · a third generation's house, and what a carrier was for.** The volume's texture block: **a valley with nine generations of a craft and one generation of no work, and a young man of the ninth generation who is a carrier and has never been anywhere.** The mechanism stated once, by a person with no stake in the Marches, in road language, and it is the same mechanism and it is not improved on. **The counterweight: the winter is held by something and the something is straining, and the strain is a thing the ninth generation can feel and cannot name, and a bridgekeeper can feel it in a road, and a bridgekeeper of thirty-four can feel it in a road better than a historian of fifty-eight can feel it in a book.**

**Ch 178–180 · the second asking, and it is consented to.** **The question is asked twice and gets two answers, and the difference is the whole of the volume.** First asked: *what did you swear?* — refused, correctly. Second asked: **the working underneath the winter is straining, and severing it will end the winter in a season, and the valley will be a valley that can leave, and about two hundred people are on a roll that says they are the heirs of a winter and are not, and there is no road and no writ and no bell, and there is a count sitting in a file two hundred miles off, and the count is the only thing in the world that can be produced against anybody here — and the severing has to be consented to, and the only people who can consent to it are the two hundred people on the roll.**

### Block 4 · Chapters 181–190: The Thread

*Path time, the fourth and fifth winters. A return stitch destroyed. A council that debates whether to keep the winter. And the cost of the road in the country he is not in.*

**Ch 181–184 · the stitch goes.** **The first safe crossing destroys a return stitch.** Not dramatically and not on anybody's fault: **a bridge he had prepared as a stitch is taken down by a valley that has decided a road is not a safe thing to leave standing with a stranger's mark on it, and the taking down is done properly and by a bridgekeeper who has explained why, and the mark was on the parapet and the parapet is a public thing.** He is **stranded**, and the stranding is nine weeks, and it is the first time in the manuscript a man is stuck somewhere with no way home, and **the volume does not resolve it with a second road.**

**Ch 185–187 · the council debates whether to keep the winter, and it is the Marches in a warmer place.** **The council is asked to keep the winter and the council is asked what happens if it does not, and the answer is that nobody in the valley can say, because the thing underneath has been held nine generations and there is no book about it that anybody has read.** A vote. **The bench is not a bench and knows it, and the sentence about a subscription is in the minute again, word for word, and is not improved on, and the valley has never heard it before and does not know it is a quotation.**

**Ch 188–190 · the main world, and the cost of a road as a political fact.** **Cut to the north without leaving the path, which is a thing the volume is allowed exactly once and never explains.** A bench of nine is asked to fund a road to a valley four hundred miles off and **the ask is a real ask with a figure on it and the figure is footed in pence on the page, and the bench says no, and the no is correct, and the reason the bench gives is a sentence about a chest with two marks in it that anybody in this manuscript would recognise.** **And the hearing stands empty on a Sunday and is not adjourned and not refused, and a body corporate that can be sued has to decide what to do with a hearing against nobody, and the decision is a document, and the document is the volume's best main-world page.**

### Block 5 · Chapters 191–200: The Seam

*Path time, the sixth winter to the last. The cut, consented to, with a local bridgekeeper present. The scar, repaired by the same community. And the road.*

**Ch 191–194 · the cut.** **The one cut of the volume, made in the open, with a bridgekeeper of the valley standing at his shoulder holding the other end of something, and the nine not-unanimous people named in the book before it is done and not named after.** It is not a feat and it is not a burst of light. **It is a severing of a thing nine generations old, worked the way a bridge is worked, over four days, with the valley doing most of it.**

**Ch 195–197 · the scar is repaired, and the winter ends, and migration begins.** **The qualification is complete and the close of the volume's power business is in it: the same community repairs the physical scar before the route is declared stable, and he helps, and the repair is a road gang in daylight with the road open and about forty men on it and nobody counting load points.** The winter comes down over eleven weeks. **The valley thaws, and about two hundred people begin to leave, and the leaving is the resolution and it is not a happy one and it is not a cure: the first three families to go are the three who said no.**
**And the ninth generation is asked one more time, and this time the asking is not refused, and what they say is not a pledge and is entered as a fact and not as a defeat, and it is four words long.**

**Ch 198–199 · back across, a week of fever, and a document that is not an apology.** **The return is made through another living anchor and not through the stitch, and it takes a week of fever at Stage 3, and somebody else holds his tools and somebody else runs his errands and somebody else tells his family where he is, which is the price and is on the page.** **The refuge route is refused in writing, in a document, dated, with the hour, and it is the second refusal in a document in two volumes and neither party knows the other exists.**
**And the intimacy, and its price.** **A path community has asked for secrecy — the ninth generation asked not to be entered in a form, and that asking was refused and the refusal stands, and what they ask for instead is that nothing be said about them in a county that has a roll — and Mara Vey insists that any public claim include its human cost, and the human cost is a leg, a seam and two hundred people who will be somewhere else in a year, and she makes him say all three in a room, and the saying is not an apology and the breach is not repaired and the two of them are further along a road than they were and they are not walking it together yet and the volume does not pretend otherwise.**

**Ch 200 · the twenty-fifth of Fallowmonth, YR 314, a Monday, not a market day, the fourth hour · the last image.** **Ordinary work. A road with a vote and not an office. A bench that has been asked five times and has said yes four times and has a fifth answer in a minute.** About forty men and a road that is open and pointing that is never finished on the day it is begun. **A bell with forty-one rivets in it, rung by other hands, and the arrangement not his and nothing in the sound his.** **A seam in a stone course on a bridge he is not holding, that he looked at once and did not measure, because a man with a level has told him what a figure turns into.** **And a road up from the water that is four hundred yards long and it is there**, and the last line of the volume is not a sentence about a man and is not a sentence about a valley.

## 9. Volume climax

**Chapters 195–198: the scar, the thaw, and the week of fever.**

The last structural act of the volume is **a refusal accepted in order to protect people, and a cut made with a local bridgekeeper's hands on the other end of it, and then the repair done by the community the cut was made in.** He has the working, the lineage reading, the three routes, and the valley has nine generations of a promise and about two hundred people on a roll that says they are its heirs and are not. **They refused the first asking and consented to the second, and the difference between those two answers is the volume's argument in one page and it is delivered by a young man of twenty-two and a bridgekeeper of thirty-four and not by him.**

**And the cut is made on consent and not on a witness's word, and the Volume 06 row is not scheduled and its consequence is not priced and the risk is carried forward as the thing he said no to in Chapter 124 on a date and a community, unexplained, six volumes ago.**

## 10. Concrete resolution

**Chapters 198–200, and the state at the close of the two hundredth chapter.**

- **The winter ends over eleven weeks and the valley becomes a valley people can leave, and the leaving begins, and the first three families to go are the three who said no.** Nobody in the volume describes this as a rescue and no character may.
- **The ninth generation is asked, and the first asking is refused and the refusal is the correct answer and it is entered in a form that names no person.** The second asking is consented to, with a number against it and a small one. **The roll is not destroyed, not superseded, and not burned**, and the ninth generation's line stays on it, **and what changes is that there is now a second column on the page and the second column is a person's own hand.**

- **A fourth route is refused in writing.** It is the second refusal in a document in two volumes and neither party knows the other exists.
- **The return stitch is destroyed and the return is made through another living anchor, and a return stitch is a thing a place is used for, and the place is a bridge parapet in a valley, and a valley took it down.**
- **The breach is open, and it is further along a road, and it is not repaired.** **No apology, in any form, in a scene, an aside or a document. The not-askings stand at nine and the volume does not enter a tenth, and the reason is entered as a fact and not as a defeat.** **The chosen intimacy happens and it is bought with a leg, a seam, a refusal and a public accounting, and the close's instruction to this volume is that the cost of the intimacy is a thing that has to be paid and not a cure.**

- **The letter for a jar is not answered**, was not answered in the year it was sent, is not answered in this volume, and **the two nine hundred and forty-ones are not printed in one line and nothing is built on them.**
