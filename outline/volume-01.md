# Volume 01 Outline

**Volume:** 01 — The Bellweather-Living Road (Chapters 1–50)
**Dominant pressure:** action / survival, with investigation carried inside it
**Recovery anchor:** the three-day fever and the work it costs other people, Chapters 9–18
**Relationship work:** Aren and Mara negotiate custody instead of trust; Tovan refuses to be his soldier
**Power arc:** Stage 0, the Disgraced Knight, into Stage 1, the First Stitch — qualified by his own work, never awarded
**Main-world elapsed:** the sixth of Mudmonth to the last week of Fallowmonth, YR 312, about five months
**Longest path interval:** six months and twenty days in Bellweather-Living, on the third crossing

## Central pressure

A cracked toll bell that officially does not exist opens a living city on the other side of a state-erased history, and four parties immediately try to decide what it is for.

- **Aren** can cross. He believes that makes him the person who should decide.
- **Edric Hunn's emergency office** wants the anchor sealed, and had the seizure order written *before* the anniversary.
- **Merefen** wants its road open, its market days kept, and its bell returned to being a piece of metal.
- **Bellweather-Living** wants to be left alone, is doing fine, and has never asked to be restored.

The pressure is not "will he find the lost city." He finds it in Chapter 4. The pressure is custody: an anchor nobody owns, surrounded by people who each have a legitimate reason to want it. Every chapter asks who gets to decide, and Aren keeps answering for everybody.

**The volume question:** who has the right to make a decision about a living city that survived an official cull — the man who can cross, the office that culled it, the archive that records it, the town that stands on the road, or the people who live there?

## Starting state

- YR 312, Mudmonth. The sixth day. The eleventh anniversary of the Sundering falls on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, in the same season and the same kind of weather that took Bellweather.
- Aren Kest is thirty-two. He was twenty-one at the Sundering, the youngest captain in the Guard's history, commissioned by brevet. Eleven years later he is a road warden and bridge repairer in Merefen, earning a shilling a day, lodging in a rented room above a cooper on Weir Street. His aunt who raised him died of the low-quarter fever three winters back; he has no household of his own.
- The Sable is in spring flood. Sedge Reach Bridge is on four timber bays and is holding. The Old Toll Bridge upstream still carries the cracked Bellweather bell, hung there because no one ever thought of a better place for it.
- He bought the bell on the first of Mudmonth at the Crown clearance for two pence, as scrap bronze, and told everyone that. A foundry would have charged him forty marks to recast the tools he needs. His brother Tovan wants the metal.
- He has a chalk stub, a notebook of bridge failures, a burn scar on his right forearm, and a pale seam on his left palm that he has never had examined.
- The official account is settled and taught: the fever took Bellweather, and the captain lost the bridge. Ordinary, friendly people repeat it to his face.
- No open path, no stitch, no carryover, no second witness anywhere in his life.

## Starting relationships

- **Aren and Tovan Kest:** estranged and affectionate. Tovan owns the Merefen bridge crew and the yard that keeps the crossings open. He refuses to call Aren "sir" in private. The wound is that Aren left for the Guard and returned a stranger, and that nobody asked Tovan to hold a bridge; only Aren was asked.
- **Aren and Edric Hunn:** eleven years of unexamined devotion. Edric was the last commander who treated him as worth teaching. He still argues with Edric's judgement in his head while defending it out loud.
- **Aren and the Bellweather Claims Archive:** nothing yet. The office sits in two flood-safe rooms over a Merefen salt merchant and keeps at least one set of records outside Crown custody, which makes it useful and politically suspect.
- **Aren and Merefen:** the town trusts the road and distrusts the man. He is paid badly, mocked for the bell, and useful enough that nobody can quite discard him.

## Starting power level

**Stage 0 — the Disgraced Knight.** Bridgecraft, inspection, field command, and journeyman Bind, Warm, and Mend. No path access, no anchor reading beyond a Remember a trained witness could do, no residue capacity, no local custodian anywhere. His qualification is lived: years of inspection, failed repairs, and the testimony of people who have worked beside him.

## Major locations

**Merefen (main world)**

- **Sedge Reach Bridge** — four timber bays, the market town's crossing, the structure the town knows him by, and the first thing he fails to inspect for nine days.
- **The Old Toll Bridge** — narrower, older, out of cart use for two generations. The bell hangs on its near abutment. Its cut-stone sill is Aren's private repair project, the physical anchor for every crossing in this volume, and it is scarred every time he uses it.
- **Weir Street** — working yards along the west channel: the coopers, the foundry, Tovan's yard, the rooms Aren rents.
- **The salt-loft rooms** — the Bellweather Claims Archive, above a man who weighs salt for a living.
- **The Reed Line** — the cut reeds along the old raised road, which the town uses to date floods.

**Bellweather-Living (the path)**

- **The great arch** — the crossing itself, standing, in use, and mortgaged to the toll houses for a century.
- **The north-bank steps and the ferry slips** — where Aren arrives, in the wrong place, with no papers, and is annoyed at.
- **The guild hall and the bench** — the bridge guild, the institution that refused the cull, and the body that answers him.
- **The toll-house counting room** — the hereditary debt books, and the outgoing writ register that names the marshal who signed the order. Held by people who have no reason to give it to a bridge guild or to him.
- **Hushwater Reach** — a living hamlet of about eighty on a channel that is drowned in the main world. Nobody in Volume 01 explains why the name is familiar. Do not draw the conclusion on the page; it is held for Volume 09.

## Major factions

Each of these has a want that survives Aren leaving the room.

- **The Merefen bridge crews and the toll-holders** — the road must earn. The road toll-holder's income fails if the road closes and the ferry carriage's income fails if it opens, which is why the two of them have never needed to agree on anything else. The crews' wages fail if the work stops. A bridge kept for the fourth-day market is worth a month of tolls.
- **The Merefen watch under Captain Rell** — Rell received a sealed emergency order from the marshal's office before the anniversary, believes the bell caused the fever recorded after Bellweather, and wants to prevent another outbreak. He is frightened, thorough, and not cruel.
- **The Bellweather Claims Archive** — restore the names and last known wishes of the erased, and keep the record outside Crown custody. Mara Vey will use people well and grieve neatly.
- **The Lantern Council's emergency office** — treat a forced severing as a public safety measure. It is off-page and signed. Its instrument is paperwork: a sealed order, a provincial renewal line, and the private-keeper clause that will start calling Aren "keeper" in official documents for reasons nobody can yet explain.
- **The Bellweather Bridge Guild** — the survivors' institution. It refused the writ, lost the northern trade, and has been short of hands for eleven years. It wants Aren for his hands and distrusts him for his accent.
- **The toll houses of Bellweather** — hereditary creditors who funded the bridge out of future tolls. Every public decision is expensive to them, so they are the faction that most wants the main world to reopen the northern trade, and the reason contact is politically explosive inside the city.
- **The Hushwater ferry cooperative** — a working crossing, a crossing book in a living hand, and a strong interest in being neither restored nor forgotten.

## The mechanics this volume runs on

1. **The bell is a damaged anchor, not a key or a summons.** It responds to a person who holds a hinge in memory and a reason to ring it. The storm is cover, not cause.
2. **The hinge is a refusal.** The cracked bell remembers the night the bridge guild refused a severance writ on the deck of the arch. The refusal is what makes the road openable, which is why a cull that was supposed to erase the witness produced the only surviving witness.
3. **The roadmark is not Bellweather.** It is Ilyra's route signature, misread by a damaged legacy stitch as a provisional keeper credential. **Nobody in this volume learns why Aren can cross.** He is told nothing, guesses wrong, and the Crown's papers start using a title that fits him for reasons that remain unexplained.
4. **Every crossing is a job with a physical bill.** A prepared return stitch, hours of daylight, a scar on the anchor, three days of fever, and a fixed ration of about three attempts a season. Aren spends two of his three in the first half of the volume and the third at the climax.
5. **He cannot be rescued from the path by himself.** The return at the climax is made by the guild, for the guild's reasons, with two stewards and two witnesses, while he holds a span and is not in charge.

## Escalation sequence

### Block 1 — Chapters 1–10: The nineteenth of Mudmonth

Ordinary work at flood stage, the guild's valuation on the bell, Tovan's claim on the bronze, the Archive's custody demand, the anniversary storm. Aren rings the bell at the ninth hour and crosses. Six days in a city that is alive, in debt, and entirely uninterested in being saved. He repairs a ferry plank the hard way under a ferrywright who will not be grateful, because gratitude is not useful. He cannot leave: he prepared no return stitch, and a return stitch is a thing other people help you make. He comes back nine days later — the Gatehouse promised three — to find the bell gone, the road closed, the market lost, and a fever starting in his own chest. He spends the first piece of evidence in a public record that includes his own complicity. **Changes:** the promise, the mark, the cost, the loss of the verse's pitch, and the first sight of a named office calling him keeper.

### Block 2 — Chapters 11–20: What the fever cost everyone else

Recovery, in other people's accounts. Tovan holds the tools and eats the loss. Lysa Fenn, the magistrate, must decide whether a sealed Council order can seize a private bell in a town that is not the province. Jos Rusk, the surviving Hushwater ferryman, has a claim that Mara personally refutes, and he is wrong about the cause and does not want to be argued with. Pei Tarrow refuses to be a symbol. The Archive discovers a registrar's fee on the one record it needed. The province renews the "no surviving claim" line, and everything filed after the renewal is weaker. The path goes on without Aren for eleven days and sends him nothing, because at Stage 1 he has no way to receive anything. **Changes:** the Archive becomes a party; the bell moves into a watch storehouse; the Old Toll sill is found to be scarred and needs anchor-glass before it can carry another crossing.

### Block 3 — Chapters 21–30: The second crossing and the refusal

The second crossing is nineteen days of path time and six of the main world, and it is the volume's investigation engine. The guild shows him the counterfoil of the writ that was refused: proof that a cull was ordered and refused, with a seal he can match to the seizure order. The guild does not want to be restored. The toll houses do, badly, and use him as their argument. The midpoint reversal lands in two beats: the toll-house counting room produces the outgoing writ register, which names Marshal Edric Hunn as the signatory of the severance of "the Alder Vale crossing and its dependent places" — and then the path refuses to be a case. In the same stretch, Aren admits in writing that he was given a partial order, that he did not reach the inner city, and that for three years he repeated the fever account and kept his own field notes in a coat instead of handing them over. **Changes:** the mystery moves from "what happened" to "who signed"; Aren is no longer the man the record is about; the path's terms for his return become an agreement, not a favour.

### Block 4 — Chapters 31–40: The road shut

The longest quiet stretch in the volume and the one that proves the book's promise. There is no crossing in this block. The Crown declares contact illegal. Rell is pressed from above and stays in post, frightened, correct, and unrepentant. The town eats the cost of a sealed road and a lost market, and blames him. Mara argues for copies held in more than one place while the Archive itself is subpoenaed, petrified, and then used. Edric's first letter arrives and contains no offer, only an accurate summary of everything Aren has done, followed by an instruction to prevent further contact. The path is not a story on the other side of a door: news arrives late and secondhand, and a stranger's report of a moving crack in the outer arch arrives two weeks after the fact through a barge mate who heard it from a toll-house steward. Aren cannot get there in time through any legal route. **Changes:** the absence of a power is felt as a cost, the main world becomes a real place with real losses, and the volume's last move is a decision made without a crossing.

### Block 5 — Chapters 41–50: The oath and the two bridges

The third crossing is the longest in the series so far: six months and twenty days in Bellweather-Living, two months of the main world, with the Gatehouse's warning wrong by weeks. The outer arch's scour hole is found at low water and the honest repair is a nine-month job the city's debt forbids, so the guild is cornered into renewing a public oath it cannot keep. The oath fails, and because the path's oath and the main-world bridge are paired anchors, the failure travels: the Old Toll sill cracks in Merefen on the same night, with nobody in Merefen able to say why. Aren's first answer is the wrong one — a solo Bind on the span, which puts the force into the pier, cracks stone, injures a person, and proves in one scene that competence is not authority. The guild repairs its own pier, renews the oath in a narrower form it can actually keep, and opens the road with two stewards and two witnesses from the other side. The crossing reopens and the collapse is contained as a visible scar, not a repair. In parallel in Merefen, Mara secures a public copy of the writ register page, witnessed, duplicated outside Crown custody, and entered as a claim. Aren returns with the roadmark permanent, three days of fever, the verse's pitch still gone, and a local person on the far side who consented to keep the road visible. **Changes:** Stage 1, the First Stitch, by qualification rather than by reward.

## Midpoint reversal

**Chapters 23–27.** The path proves the city survived, and its citizens do not want the main world to restore them. The toll-house counting room's outgoing writ register names Edric Hunn as the man who signed the severance of "the Alder Vale crossing and its dependent places" on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301. Two consequences turn the book around:

1. **The evidence exonerates and indicts in the same breath.** The order was not a panic and Aren was not the cause of it. The Crown can say out loud that a boy lost a city because a boy losing a city costs nobody a commission.
2. **The living city does not want to be his case.** The guild has its own account of the night, its own dead, its own debt, and no interest in being appended to a Crown record. The most useful witness in the world tells him that a city rescued by the main world would be a city the main world owns.

The midpoint also makes his own silence part of the case: for three years after the Sundering he repeated the fever account in debriefs and kept his field notes, because challenging the inquiry would have cost him the road work he needed. He is not absolved by Edric's signature. He helped make the lie durable, and from here he can no longer build a case he is not also a witness against.

## Volume climax

**Chapters 41–47.** Two bridges, two places, one failure.

In Bellweather-Living the outer arch's scour is exposed at low water. The fix is a new cutwater, and a new cutwater is a nine-month job against a debt that has already pledged thirty years of tolls, so the guild is publicly cornered: renew the oath that the crossing will be held, or hold the crossing and break the oath in front of a city that has watched people keep promises for four generations. The guild is forced into a promise it cannot pay for. When it fails, the paired anchor in the main world fails with it, and the Old Toll sill opens in Merefen while two thousand people are on the arch at Bellweather.

Aren's first move is the old move. He puts his own working into the span, because he can see the load path and the oath and the water and nobody else can act in time. The force goes into the pier exactly as it should, because that is what a Bind does: stone cracks, a pier splinters, a hull-hand and a stranger are hurt, and the fix takes longer than the water gives. He is the reason the working fails, in the same way he was the reason the bridge at Bellweather fell, and this time he is watching himself do it.

The crossing is reopened without him deciding anything. The guild's older members, the toll-house steward, the Hushwater cooperative, and Bellweather's own two stewards set a narrower oath that they can keep in writing and stand the repair in the open. The road opens as a fold in the rain with four sets of hands on the anchor and Aren translating, which is the work he is actually for. In Merefen, the sill holds because Mara has been rebuilding the Archive's copies in three places for forty chapters, so that when the anchor cracks there is a witness who can say what it meant and who was standing there.

The collapse is contained, not repaired. The scar stays in both worlds, and both communities will be arguing about the debt and the oath for the rest of the series.

## Concrete resolution

**Chapters 48–50.**

- Bellweather-Living remains a separate, sovereign community. It is not restored, absorbed, relocated, or reconciled with the main world, and it keeps its own dead.
- A limited channel opens. It is not Aren's road. A Merefen steward and a Bellweather-Living steward, with one independent witness each, renew a scheduled crossing on a named day; the first message across it is a toll dispute about a load of paving stone, not a treaty.
- Aren holds Stage 1, the First Stitch: one open route, one carryover residue, a permanent roadmark, a return stitch that must be prepared before every crossing, and no ability to choose the path's politics.
- The Crown declares the contact illegal in writing, confirms that no Bellweather survivor claims exist, and instructs the watch to prevent further contact. The sealed seizure order is entered into the record as lawful procedure and is never mentioned again by the office that wrote it.
- Edric Hunn offers conditional restoration: rank, back pay, the old office, and a legal shield for the Archive, in exchange for the route and an inspection of Bellweather-Living. He is entirely reasonable and entirely correct that a man with a private road and no constitution is a public danger.
- The Archive files a claim number for the writ register page, keeps a copy outside Crown custody, and enters Ferris Oat's brine weights — thirty-one days of salt weights with the arithmetic intact — as its first exhibit, because a ledger cannot be argued with about how much there was. Four thousand and seven hundred and twenty are never added together on one page.
- Aren pays for all of it in the only currency he has: a road-marked palm, three days of fever he did not choose, the remembered pitch of one verse of his mother's river song, nine days of his own calendar on the first crossing, and a brother who will mend his bridge but will not keep his secret.

## Next-volume question

**What other paths are being sealed, and who is buying the records of their disappearance?**

Three concrete hooks leave the volume. A claimant's form arrives at the Archive about a fever in the eastern counties that has already produced a list of names to be removed from the public roll. A registrar's fee is quoted for the missing-anchor ledger that should not exist. And the writ register's own index shows a second severance authorization in the same hand, from four years later, in a county no one in Merefen can name. Edric's office has also stopped asking Aren to stop; it has started asking who else he has told.

## Final chapter image

The last week of Fallowmonth, low water, bridge season. Sedge Reach holds. The Old Toll bell is back on its cradle with a new iron band and a new crack sealed with dull red oathlight, and it is not Aren's to ring. A Merefen boy, the steward's apprentice, stands on the near abutment with the Bellweather-Living steward on the other side of a scheduled crossing, and they ring it together because that is the arrangement. Aren is on the Sedge Reach deck with a tool bag, counting load points out of habit, when he hears the note go out and the answer come back. His palm is warm. He does not need it, and nothing in the sound is his.

## Continuity traps for this volume

- **The numbers.** Bellweather is about four thousand. The seven villages are about seven hundred and twenty. The Archive never prints a combined total. The Crown quotes only the city.
- **The Rusk families.** Jos Rusk survives in Merefen and is wrong about the cause. Pei Tarrow is not a Rusk, is no relation, and is never staged beside him.
- **Hanna Vey and Mara Vey are not related.** Mara notices it once, coldly, in writing, and never again.
- **The named dead are documents.** Ferris Oat, Hanna Vey, Corin Slate, Bess Alder, Anwen Rusk, and Bryn Rusk are read, quoted, and filed. They do not act and they do not count against the six-name batch cap. Jos Rusk and Pei Tarrow do count.
- **The words.** Path-side locals say steward, and a steward maintains. The corrupted term is "private keeper," and a Crown document will apply it to Aren. "Custodian" stays unused in this volume.
- **The bell.** Bought at the Crown clearance for two pence on the first of Mudmonth, YR 312, as scrap bronze. It is not a key, a spyglass, or a summons, and nothing about it is rare.
- **No path is a reset.** Nobody in Bellweather-Living is a version of a main-world victim, and the living and the erased are never added together.
