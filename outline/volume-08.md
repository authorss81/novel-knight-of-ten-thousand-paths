## 1. Volume identity, and the question this volume is built on

**`outline/series.md`'s Volume 8 central pressure is an annexation, and the close of Volume 07 handed on a rule, and the two are the same room for the fourth volume running.**

The series says: the Crown annexs the Salt Verge to control path anchors, displacing people and starting a trade war. The close says: **about nine sets of about forty sheets are in four counties, none of them complete, each held by one person by name, and everybody in the four counties knows which person, and that is the one new rule anybody made in Volume 07, and nobody made it, and it was said out loud once in sixty-nine words in a room over a byre, and it is in no register and in no minute and there is no form for it in this kingdom.**

Put them together and the volume is this: **a rule that makes a record findable, met by an office that has been finding people by their trades for nineteen years.** The named-holder rule is not broken. It is never tested. It is *used*, by an instrument that does not need a name, and the finding is the whole volume.

**What Volume 07 asked and answered:** *a copy is a person who was there.* **What Volume 08 asks and has to earn:** ***a person who is a keeper is findable, and a rule that says a record has one keeper has made that keeper a thing an office can be asked about, and a rule this good does not survive being anonymous.***

**And the second half, which is the volume's cost.** Volume 07's answer was a *shelf* — a thing nobody can be shown. **A toll is a price, and a price is the only kind of paper everybody obeys, and a crossing with a price on it is not a crossing with a bar on it, it is a crossing with a clerk on it, and the clerk can be anybody with the right trade.** Nobody in this volume has to be a monster. A man with a form asks a woman a fair question about her trade, and she gives him a fair answer, and the result is that a crossing on the flats has a price on it that did not have a price on it, and **the whole of what happened is a question and an answer and neither of them was a lie.**

**What the volume is not.** It is not a volume about an army. **Aren wants a military solution in Block 1 and the volume spends four blocks teaching him that a line of men on a road is a line of men on a road, and the offer of a real army comes from a path in Block 3 and is refused by a person in a yard in Block 3 and is then let go by the man who wanted it in Block 5.** It is not a volume about a villain in a room. **The Chancellor of the Lantern Council is named and is the office, and the marshal is not named and is on the page in Blocks 2, 4 and 5, and the office of the Crown is not a character and is not given a want, and the volume's antagonist is an instrument the same way Volume 07's was a form.** And it is not a volume in which anybody says the word *heroic* about a person on the flats, which is a mistake the volume uses twice.

## 2. Central pressure

**Four parties want four different things, three of the four are right, and the man in the middle is the only one who can pay for it and the only one who cannot have what he wants.**

- **A yard of nine men on Weir Street** wants **bridge work in the season there is bridge work, at a bridge rate.** Fallowmonth through Embermonth is bridge season and it is seven weeks off in Block 1, and **two sides of the flats have offered the same figure to the penny and one of them pays in salt.** A yard that takes a road rate instead of a bridge rate has bought a season.
- **A woman of about fifty on the flats** wants **that a crossing nobody uses still be a crossing.** She has kept one list of nine crossings for nineteen years and one of the nine has not been a crossing since before she came and it is in her book and she has never taken it out. **Her want is about the getting of a crew, not about the Crown, and it is a trade want and it is the volume's first honest one.**
- **A keeper of records in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane** wants **a public account that will survive the next election**, which is what the series row gives her, and **she is the only character in the volume who is asked to do a thing she is good at by people who need it, and she refuses one part of it and the refusal is the volume's relationship work and not an aside.**
- **Aren Kest** wants **to stand on nine crossings with nine men and be the reason they are still crossings.** He cannot, and the reason he cannot is arithmetic and not courage, and **he spends the first three blocks finding that out and the last two blocks learning what to do with a man who is not on a crossing.**

**The volume question, in its operational form:** *a Crown office puts nine crossings and a price on a sheet, and it must put somebody at each crossing to collect the price, and it has for nineteen years been writing down the trades of every person in nine counties in a column headed Keeper, and about nine people in four counties who hold a set of about forty sheets each are keepers of something, and nobody had to break a single rule.*

**The answer the volume gives is that a crossing is not a line — a line has two ends and two hands and a date and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it, and a toll has none of those and needs none of them — and that the refusal of a price is therefore not a political act but a missing person, and that the whole difficulty of the volume is that a person can be asked, and cannot be unasked.**

## 7. The mechanics this volume runs on

1. **A road is a line and two hands, a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it.** Carried forward whole. **In Volume 08 the volume's whole argument is one clause of it: a line has two ends. A toll has one price and a clerk, and a price does not have an end, and a thing without an end cannot be kept by anybody in particular, and therefore it is kept by whoever is nearest.**
2. **A record is a person who is not present.** Carried forward. **In Volume 08 it is the engine of the block turn: the office does not need the record's holder, because it has a column of trades that the record's holder is already in, by her own trade, for doing something else entirely.**
3. **A refusal recorded against a person is a list. A refusal recorded about a class of persons is a document.** Carried forward. **The volume's refusal is refused *for* a person by a person who may refuse it, and the difference between the two is the whole of the reconciliation in Block 5.**
4. **A form is a machine for making an office out of a word, and the word it is offered this time is *keeper*.** The *Keeper* column may be printed as a head, quoted in a return, cited in a file and read out in a hall. **It may not be explained in a sentence by anybody, it may not be given a want, and the marginal instruction's nine words are not printed and are not printed here.**
5. **A count is a list with the names taken off, and the office has learned to prefer the count.** Carried forward. **Volume 08's consequence: a crossing is not a count, and a schedule of crossings is a count, and a toll is a count, and a road is the only one of the three that anybody in this volume actually uses.**
6. **A person can be asked a second time and a column cannot.** Carried forward as the volume's spine. **Volume 08 turns it: a person can be asked a first time, and the second asking is not a second asking because the first one is not spoken of, and a rule that says a person is a keeper is a rule that says a person can be found and asked and found and asked again.**
7. **A promise that can only be kept by breaking something else is a claim, not a record.** Carried forward. **In Volume 08 the promise is made by a man to nine men in a yard in nine words and is not written down, and the yard's book cannot hold it and cannot be served with anything, and a man who is written down in a yard's book is a man who can be called out of it.**
8. **What is under a thing is not mended by the man who dug it.** Spent in an earlier volume. **What Volume 08 spends in its place is the ordinary labour everything stands on: a bar put down and lifted, a tilt cart mended with a plank, a book kept for nineteen years, a bench-board, a lime heap with a lid on it, a salt buyer who converts a figure.**

**And the new mechanic, said once, in trade terms, by a person with no stake, in Block 1, and not improved on:** *a crossing is not a line; a line has two ends and two hands and a date, and the moment a crossing is a line on somebody's paper it is a toll, and a toll is the only kind of paper anybody obeys.*
