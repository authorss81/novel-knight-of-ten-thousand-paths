## 1. Volume identity

Volume 01 asked who has the right to decide about a living city that survived an official cull, and answered *nobody, and here is the sheet with two names on it.* Volume 02 asked what a record owes the person it describes, and answered *a promise is only as wide as the people who have agreed to it, and a hall is not a person.* **Volume 03 asks a third question, and it is the first question in the book that cannot be answered with a document: how do you keep a promise that has been kept for a hundred and forty years and is killing everybody who keeps it?**

The Hearth Marches is farm country organised around household fields, commons, and seasonal oath-stones. A boundary in the Marches is made of water, so the man who holds the ditch and the man who holds the oath have been the same person for a hundred and forty years, and the oath has been renewed in public, on a stone, in a year anybody there can name, by men who are still alive and can be asked what they swore. **They have all been asked, every year, and every year the answer has been the same, and the answer is the reason the soil under four villages is going white.** Keeping an oath about a boundary in the Marches means moving a ditch. It has meant moving a ditch for a hundred and forty years. The boundary is fixed by the stone; the water is fixed by the ditch; and the two cannot both be fixed, so the ditch moves, and every renewal moves it again, and a renewal is a promise, and a promise kept a hundred and forty times is a claim.

**The engine is not a structure failing and it is not a record being bought.** It is a **promise that can only be kept by breaking something else**, in a county that cannot refuse anything because a thing asked for and not given has no word in its own language, and a refusal and a loss are the same word. What happens is that a form's marginal instruction is relied on in a village, and a man is made answerable for a road he keeps and cannot refuse; that a Crown office proposes a count for a border county and a keeper of records in a fen has to decide whether a figure about a place is a record; that a ferryman of a bridge guild takes a contract in a county four hundred miles off and goes inside it on the county's own calendar; and that the river changes course.

**At the centre of it a man who can cross and has begun to count the people he has, and who is sent to a place he may not watch the stone in, discovers that the thing which outlasts him is a woman with a gauge column nobody has ever asked to see, a man who wants his tile lifted and not thrown away, a bench that argues about a boundary, and a hand who will not be his replacement.** And a bell that is not a bell, on a coping, four hundred miles off, on the other end of a road that has just been closed in writing by the only people who have the right to close it.

## 2. Central pressure

Four parties want four different things out of an oath, and the volume's engine is that all four of them are reasonable.

- **The Marches council** wants **the stone to stand.** It is a bench of about eleven that argues about a boundary the way a county bench argues about a road, it has no writ to answer and no bell to ring, and it has held the same boundary for a hundred and forty years. **Delya Munn**, 55, who renews it, wants the stone to stand, and her reason is a grandmother's name, and **she is right about the history and wrong about the soil and no one in the volume may call her a villain.** It is the Bellweather Sundering with a hedge on the front of it and neither half of that is said out loud.
- **The Marches bridge guild** wants **the water to be somebody's job.** It knows the waterworks better than the main world, it has the only men who can re-cut a ditch, and it has been refused a wage twice by a landlord and once by a bench. **Coll Ferrow**, 60, will accept a stranger's labour on terms and states the terms first, and the terms are better than anything the north has brought.
- **A road warden of the Merefen bridge guild** wants **to be in a room where the load path is visible and somebody can be asked to stop him.** He has a vote and not an office, a notebook in a coat with a morning in it, a keeper of records four hundred miles away who is not going to ask him anything, and **a brother whose yard took a four-hundred-mile contract and did not tell him, because a yard's book holds things set.**
- **The Office of Provincial Registration** wants **a complete register of oaths.** It has no file on the Marches, it has a form with a column headed *Keeper* and a marginal instruction it printed itself, and it has discovered in the last eleven months that a **count of persons** is lawful, names nobody, needs no court, and cannot be refused without refusing a figure. **It is an office. It is doing its job. It is the most dangerous thing in this volume and it never appears.**

**The volume question:** *what does a promise owe the people who keep it, when keeping it is the harm?*

### 3.1 Aren Kest

Thirty-two. **Stage 2, the Second Door, by qualification and not by award, four hundred miles off, in a shed, with a witness, on the terms printed in the Office of Provincial Registration's hand on the back of a form in an unlocked drawer.** Two routes held and the holding of one not the holding of the other: **Bellweather-Living, and the County of Ash, which does not open on a schedule and whose far end has never been asked and never volunteered.** Two carried over and the carrier not able to say which path gave him which — a hand that reads a handrail in the dark, and a way of standing, weight a little back, hands loose, that he cannot source and that a woman in a fen noticed before he did. **A return prepared by two hands, a date, and a witness at each end; if the witness is not there the route does not open and does not fail.** One message, and **it must be a list, and a list can only be checked**; one object under a pound, **two half tiles**, one of which went north in another woman's bag and one of which he made himself behind a kiln with a saw mark on the back, and the one in the press is the account of nobody. **A permanent road-marked left palm, warm at about the fourth hour on the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth and not needed.**

**On the roll of the Merefen bridge guild, with a vote, not holding the crossing, not voting, having said in a hall in Frostmonth that he would come when he was wanted; and he came on the twenty-fourth and has been on the deck every working morning since.** **A copying post at three shillings a week, taken on the sixteenth of Fallowmonth, gone since Thawmonth, filled by somebody else, sixteen weeks and two days unclaimed.** A cutting contract at eight pence a day, two marks and three quarters, carried if the cutting fails. **Eleven marks and fourpence owed by a trust in abeyance for eleven days' crew pay.** **Fifty marks one shilling and sixpence in a trust's account that nobody can pay, with a line in it carrying no figure at all, and the line now has six pence of report against it.** And two habits he has never told anybody about: he counts load points, and **he looks at the bottom of a form**, which is how he found a blank that had been sitting in a county for ninety-four years.

### 3.2 Mara Vey

Twenty-nine, four years and a half keeper of the Bellweather Claims Archive, **now a body corporate with a door, a number and the capacity to sue and to be sued since the ninth of Greenmonth**, in one room over a weigh-house at the north end with a door on a lane, four pence a year, two rooms' work in one. **Keeper is employment and not jurisdiction.** The office's rule is on the wall in a fair copy, and its second rule is that **no combined figure is entered in this office.** **The day-book is the only book in that office nobody can serve.** **Two unserved books in the press and a half tile in it that weighs a shade over ten ounces and is the account of nobody.** Two claims against nobody this year and nine claim numbers in four years, of which one is a name out of a copy.

**Eight entries of the not writing in the month of Greenmonth**, and the sentence has not changed in four years and a half and she is at the end of what it can do. **The eighth ruled line in the served schedule of the eighth of Fallowmonth is still empty**, looked at three times and not filled, and the reason for leaving it empty is copied in the hand of a county boy of nineteen who asked whether it would still be on the sheet in a year, **and it is.** **Ferris Oat's brine weights are cited and not filed**, two day-book lines four lines apart and five days apart in one week of Fallowmonth, read together every time the book is opened; the reason for not building the roll out of them has changed and is written out in full: **a ledger is a claim, and this office has learned this year, from a county four hundred miles off, that a claim about people is not a record of them.** The roll is Chapter 892's and nothing in this volume may begin it.

**A line in a fair hand about a man at a kiln four hundred miles off, read four times, entered against no person with the hour, not built into a roll, not joined to anything, and not asked about in four months.** **A column of eleven counties entered as a question and not as finding, with the hour, against nobody, no opinion, on the twenty-seventh of Greenmonth** — and the eighth of the eleven have a boundary oath renewed in public on a stone in a year anybody there can name, and the other three have nothing in the leaf at all. **She looked at it four times in four minutes and refused to write the answer down, and the reason is in four words: *I am not going to be the person who notices one.***

**A body corporate can be sued. A keeper can only be dismissed. Nobody in the room said it and the chapter did not say it and a yard said it three times, in the same words, to one person at a time.**

### 3.4 Sabin Dain

## 4. Starting power state, and the shape of holding Stage 2

**Stage 2 going in, qualified in a shed, with a witness.** Two routes held, two carryovers of untraceable origin, one message that must be a list, one object under a pound, a return prepared by two hands, a date, and a witness at each end. **The second route does not answer when it is called, and there is no procedure for a far side that has not decided.**

**The decision this volume makes, and the close that made it.** `outline/series.md`'s power ladder gives **the Second Door to Volumes 2–3 and Threadkeeper to Volumes 4–6**, and `bible/power-system.md` names Threadkeeper's third ability as **a limited forced-connection cut**. **A cut is an instrument and not a document, and it is Volume 06's beat, and it is not available in this volume.** Therefore:

- **Stage 3 is not reached in Volume 03 and must not be.** No third path is *held*. No lineage is read. **No cut is attempted, offered as a plan, priced, or described by anybody as a thing he could do if he chose.**
- **The volume's cost for not reaching it is the volume's climax.** The Marches' water route is failing, and a forced-connection cut is exactly the sort of thing a man is told to use on a failing water route, and **somebody says so to him in a room, in Block 3, and he agrees that it is not his to do, and the reason given is not a rule and is a date: the last time somebody cut one of these, the anchor tore and a community lost contact for ever, and the man who cut it was told afterwards that the witness had been forged.** That is Volume 06's plant, held. **What he does instead is hold a failing span with his body in Block 5 and come up with a permanent limp, acquired before the return stitch closes, which `outline/series.md` fixes as the Volume 3 cost and which is the only new bodily cost in the volume.**
- **The qualification's second clause is met, in Block 4, and it is the volume's most expensive act.** *Deliberately close one route without losing it.* He holds three routes for the middle of the volume: Bellweather-Living, the County of Ash, and the Hearth Marches. **The County of Ash closes — by a four-line notice down the line, on a scheduled day, itemised, checkable, sent by the woman who keeps the road at that end, and asked for by the county's own bench, and agreed to by him in writing, and the closing is done at that end and not by him.** The notice arrives signed, and its fourth line is the county's second term, forty-one words long, **and it is a refusal of him, and it is correct, and he has known since the first of Embermonth of the year before that the second route does not answer when it is called.** He calls it once, in the same chapter, and the answer is the notice. **At the close of the volume he holds two routes, and the Second Door is stable enough to keep two open under pressure, which is the series' resolution line, and the third is gone and is not lost, and the county that closed it has a roll in three hands and a rule with no office behind it and does not need him.**

- **The provincial town, two hundred miles off** — the Office of Provincial Registration, the fourth press, the third press, the provincial court, the press registers, and **the file in which a *Keeper* column on a village's paper has been relied upon.** Name the office and the form. Do not name the people in the office, with one exception: **Sabin Dain, who is a named person with a want and not a clerk, and who is the only one of them who appears on a page.**

### 5.2 The Hearth Marches (the path)

Rolling farm country on a river the Marches call the Water, in eleven villages and a market town, on household fields and commons, **with seasonal oath-stones: a long field, a boundary, a season, a stone renewed in public in a year anybody there can name, by men who are still alive.**

- **A boundary in the Marches is made of water.** The ditch is the boundary and the stone is the record of the boundary, and the man who holds the ditch is called **the bound** and the man who holds the water is called **the ditchman**, and the two words have been one word for a hundred and forty years because a boundary here is a channel. **A road in the Marches is a causeway and the people who keep it are the bridge guild, and the guild's word for its first hand is *the hand*, as in Ash, and the two counties arrived at the same word from opposite ends of the world and neither of them has ever heard of the other.**
- **The Water.** A river in spate in the wrong month. **Two low waters a year, and the Marches' year is thirteen rollings**, counted from the first sowing, and **no main-world month name and no weekday of this world is spoken in a Marches scene, and no character in one converts anything.** The gauge column: a woman of thirty-eight has read the gauges for eleven years and has a column of her own in a book in a hut at a weir, and **nobody has ever asked to see it, and the council has never once looked at it, and that is the volume's first fact about how a county dies.**
- **The seed vault** — a stone room behind a chapel, and a woman of sixty-six who keeps it, and inside it nine sealed jars of a wheat that has not been grown in this county for forty years and a wall in which two earlier answers have been built up. **The cooperative forms here and not anywhere else, and the Marches' plan is a plan about seed and about water and not about a bridge.**
- **The council chamber** — a room over a tithe barn, about eleven people, a bench that argues about a boundary, and **no writ to answer and no bell to ring and no Crown's schedule carrying this county.** Its minute is a county's own book and its entries are facts and not decisions, and its standing rule since the last volume of the previous county is a rule with no office behind it.
- **The four villages going white.** Sallow Marsh, and three others, on the same ditch system, on the same stone, renewed in the same year. **The soil is not poisoned and nobody has poisoned it: the water table has risen because a ditch has been moved a hand's width to the left every year for a hundred and forty years, and the salt has come up out of the ground it was always in.** That is the volume's mechanism and it is a mechanism a bridgewright can see in a day, and it must be said in trade terms by a person with a gauge column and not by a Crown's man.

## 6. Major factions

Each has a want that survives Aren leaving the room. None is a monster and three are better than he is.

- **The Marches council.** Wants **the stone to stand.** It has no writ, no bell, and no schedule, and it is the only body in the county that meets, because a county that has been cut off still has to argue about a boundary. **It is not a rescue and must not be written as one.** It argues about the price of a ditch.
- **The Marches bridge guild.** Wants **the water to be somebody's job and not a county's mood.** It is the only body in the county that can re-cut a ditch, it has about nineteen men, it has been refused a wage twice by a landlord and once by a bench, and **it knows the waterworks better than the main world and will say so in the first ten minutes.**
- **The ditchmen and the bound.** About forty men whose job is a length of water and whose wages have been paid out of a rent roll that has been shrinking for nine years. They want **the length of water they are responsible for to be written down on something they can be shown**, and their want is a record and not a wage, and **they are the volume's mirror of the struck in Ash and the book must not say so.**
- **The struck of the Marches** — about sixty households on the four white villages, alive, on the roll, and **not on the oath**, because the oath is renewed by the people who hold the ditch and the people who hold the ditch are the people who were not struck. **Their want is to be asked what they swore, and they have never been asked, because the stone is renewed by the men who hold the water and the men who hold the water are the ones with no standing in the fields.**
- **The salt on the water.** Not a faction. **A fact, and the villain of the volume, and it is not named by anybody as a villain because it is not a person.**

## 7. The mechanics this volume runs on

1. **The road is a line and two hands.** A return must be prepared by two hands, a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping the road, or the road does not open. **If the witness is not there the road does not open and it does not fail, and nobody is to say afterwards that it failed.** In Volume 03 this is the engine of two separate closes: the Marches' road is closed by the far end in writing, and the Ash road's **sixth term — the line is not to be opened on a day the Marches have not named** — is the term that makes the Marches' refusal possible.
2. **A record is a person who is not present.** Volume 02's distinction, carried forward whole: a day-book is not a file, a file is not a record, a record is not a roll, and a **gauge column in a hut at a weir is not a record of the county's water because nobody has ever been asked to see it and a column nobody is shown is a claim.**
3. **A promise that can only be kept by breaking something else is a claim, not a record.** The volume's own extension of Volume 02's argument, and it is arrived at by a ditchman in a shed with a rule on it, in the same register as the struck's sentence and the rollkeeper's sentence and the physician's fourth page. **And it is arrived at by nobody in Merefen.**
4. **A form is a machine for making an office out of a word — and the machine is already running.** Form R.C. 4, Return of Contact. ## 9. Volume climax

**Chapters 145–147: the span, and it is not a bridge.**

The last structural act of the volume is a form being filled in by the wrong person for the right reason, and it is the Marches' own form, and it has no column for a road warden's vote. He has the road, the hands, the two of them, the plan he wrote, and the plan the county wrote before he arrived, and **the council has not asked him for anything and cannot be asked to give him an authority it has not got**, so the only move available to him is the one the Sundering was built out of and the one the council refused him in Chapter 137, and he makes it anyway in a different direction: **he gives the road away, in writing, to his brother and to a bridge guild in a county he has been in for thirteen months, and the county's minute carries the hour and no name in it, and neither of the two men uses the word brother in the scene.**

Then the river comes back up over the re-cut boundaries in a night, which is the point of the whole volume and the reason the plan was tested in the same season it was made. The plan holds. ## 17. Time budget

**Main world: the thirtieth of Greenmonth, YR 313, to the twenty-fourth of Hearthmonth, YR 313.** **Two hundred and thirty-four days elapsed, two hundred and thirty-five inclusive — just under eight months**, matching the Volume 3 row of the ledger. **Path time: nine days and then thirteen months in the Hearth Marches, the longest single interval in the manuscript so far.** The Second Door amplifies drift; dated market days are preserved throughout, because every market day in this volume is a line in a yard's book or a guild's roll.

**Rainmonth's length is fixed by the Volume 02 close, at thirty days**, from the thirty-day month the whole calendar has been built on and from the audited chain in continuity item 147, because this volume runs forward past it and would otherwise have to guess. **Every month in this stretch is thirty days and that is now binding on every later volume.** Mudmonth's length is still not fixed and this volume does not need it.

**The weekday chain**, built forward from Chapter 100's own last line — **the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth, YR 313, a Monday** — and from the thirty-day month and nothing else, so that any later writer can check any single figure in it by counting. **Greenmonth 30 Tue** · **Longlight 1 Wed, 4 Sat (market), 9 Thu (market), 30 Thu** · **Rainmonth 1 Fri, 4 Mon, 8 Fri, 9 Sat (market), 11 Mon, 13 Wed, 15 Fri, 16 Sat (market), 18 Mon, 19 Tue, 20 Wed, 21 Thu, 22 Fri, 24 Sun, 29 Sun, 30 Sat (market)** · **Harvestmonth 1 Sun, 5 Thu (market), 7 Sat (market), 12 Thu (market), 20 Mon, 30 Fri** · **Fallowmonth 1 Tue, 5 Sat (market), 30 Thu (market)** · **Embermonth 1 Thu, 8 Thu (market), 9 Sat (market)** · **Wolfmonth 1 Sat, 6 Thu (market), 8 Sat (market), 15 Sat (market), 22 Sat (market), 29 Sat (market)** · **Frostmonth 1 Mon, 4 Thu (market), 6 Sat (market), 13 Sat (market), 20 Sat (market), 27 Sat (market)** · **Hearthmonth 1 Wed, 2 Thu, 4 Sat (market), 5 Sun, 9 Thu (market), 20 Mon, 24 Fri (market, the volume's last day).**

**The one consequence a writer must not miss.** The volume's first day is a Tuesday and its last day is a Friday, and **the ninth of Rainmonth is a Saturday and the fifth of Fallowmonth is a Saturday, and both crossings are made at the fourth hour of a market day, which is the manuscript's habit and not a preference.** **A market day falls on whatever weekday the chain gives it; a chapter does not get to want a day.** The county's market days are Saturday and Thursday, and the chapter opens with both facts and does not reconcile them.

**Path-side weekdays are the path's own** and are not carried into Merefen without being wrong about the day and right about the night, which is what happened in Chapter 44 of Volume 01 and has happened in this series ever since. **The Marches keep a year of thirteen rollings counted from the first sowing, every rolling is four weeks, and the year is a year plus a rolling, so a man is gone a year and a rolling and the county has had a sowing, a flood and a harvest inside it and the second route did not tell him.** **No main-world month name, no main-world weekday, and no conversion of a Marches rolling into a day of this calendar may be spoken or written anywhere in Chapters 118–147.** The one conversion attempted is refused in Chapter 130, in his own notebook, and the refusal is the plant.

**Do not drift further, and do not pretend to.** Volume 01's third crossing was six days of absence against a printed twenty-one, an error of minus fifteen. **Volume 02's widest was sixty-seven days at eighty-eight days of absence, about three quarters. This volume's first crossing is ten days of absence against a printed twenty-one — an error of minus eleven, inside the warning for the first time in this case, and the only figure in the volume a man can check — and this volume's second is ninety-six days at one hundred and seventeen days of absence, about four fifths.** The two crossings are the volume's proof and they are in one table. **The ratio, not the figure, is the thing: a good result teaches a man nothing and a bad one teaches him everything.** The ledger allows the ratio to widen with each volume and the warning itself is not going to change. The warning has been three weeks in a Crown office's hand since Chapter 51 and **the Crown cannot say whose figure it is, and no character in this volume describes it as having failed.** What this volume does with the arithmetic instead of hiding it is put on the page in a notebook and in a fair copy and **in the one sentence the volume's own argument turns on: the error is in him, and it scales with the work, and he wrote that down two volumes ago and has now proved it twice.** A later volume may widen it again. **It may not make a Gatehouse warning wrong by months and call that an accident, and it may not have any character say the word *failed* about a road.**
