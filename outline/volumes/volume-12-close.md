## 1. What the volume was about

**It is about a valley of terraced fruit above a river, four days and about nine of hard going from the nearest made road, where nothing has been decided for twenty-two years because the people who stopped deciding put the reason in the ground, and where a man of thirty-two with a limp was asked to certify it as the one road that is stable, and refused, and the valley then reopened grief, argument and choice on purpose and accepted the instability, at the cost of about nine households' ground for a season.**

It is a finding with three names on a slate, four sets answering yes and no and yes and no, nine days and a morning counted by a woman with a stick and about nine stones, a ditch two paces wide cut by one person where six were to be cut, cloths coming down uncounted, a person who can be refused and is on no roll, a delegate whose first written act refuses thanks, a hearing that stands with no bench, a fourth head still empty, a floor washed by somebody nobody knows, and a half leaf still face down. **It is a woman of about twenty-nine, two hundred miles off, saying that a beautiful place is still an owned place, and the volume's last page is that sentence in a lane, said to a man who does not go on with it, and the key turned over once and not put into the lock.**

**And what the volume did, in fifty chapters, was refuse to fill anything.** It did not name the antagonist in any of its fifty chapters and did not say whose plan the letter is. It did not fill the fourth head, turn the half leaf, lift the bar of wood at the Low Gate, cut the twenty-second notch, send anybody to that coast, reopen the shut road, resolve the three words for one lane, choose between the two readings of a man who published his own mistake, or repair the breach between two people who stood in one lane and said nothing. **It printed no System panel and no stage, ledger or qualification word; a sweep of all fifty returns 0 for every one of them.** It spent two of five names and left three unspent. It restored nothing and the city's three protected numerals are at zero for the whole volume.

**The deepest change in the volume is a want, and neither of them is the protagonist's.** A woman of about thirty-eight who keeps four blocks in the Long Set wants to be able to say that a thing she did was wrong without it also turning out that she is the one who has to move, and she discovers on the way that the sentence comes out in a voice four generations old that she was given at a table when she was small, and she can hear it arrive. A woman of about sixty-four who has gone to the oldest tree at the eighth hour for thirty years to count a ring of stones discovers that she has never once counted them and that the number she says is a number she was given in her first year and copied ever since. **Neither is thanked, and the volume does not notice, and that is its habit and its cost at once — §9.**

**And there is a third blind spot which this close found and which the two warnings on the page do not name.** The page names that the house form cannot read *a hundred and forty-seven*. It also cannot read **a total whose hundreds word is itself a tens word** — *two hundred and one*, *two hundred and thirty-one*, *two hundred and five*, *two hundred and sixteen*, *two hundred and eleven*, *two hundred and nineteen*, *two hundred and twenty-three*, *two hundred and twenty-seven*, *two hundred and twenty-four* — because the phrase before *hundred* is not the word *one*. **Eight of Volume 12's own columns are written in that form and a parser built on the *one hundred* shape returns none of them.** A parser that split on hyphens before parsing and then handed the split token to a function expecting a hyphen also throws on every hyphenated total, and a swallowed exception returns a short list that looks like a clean block. **All three faults produce a shorter set and a longer clean report, and all three are in the direction of reporting that there is nothing wrong.**

**And there is a fourth, and it is the one that survived this document's own repair, and it is the reason §11.2 exists.** Both extractors above are keyed on the **conversion**: the house form reads *A mark is forty-eight pence and X pence is*, and the item-sum reads the item list. **Neither one requires that the printed total be the sum of a run of pence values that sit immediately before it.** A column can therefore be genuinely footed, foot three ways, and still be invisible to both — because it words its conversion differently. `volume-09/chapter-0417.md` and `volume-09/chapter-0420.md` both foot *Sixteen and forty-eight and two and four* to *seventy pence* and convert it as *a mark is forty-eight pence and that is one mark and twenty-two pence*: lower case, with **that is** in the middle, and the over-figure given as *pence* rather than as a bare figure. **That is a real footed column at 70, and no version of the house form reads it.**

**The third instrument, built at §11.2 and used for the count this document now certifies, keys on the footing and not on the conversion.** It locates a total — *is N pence* — and then walks left to recover the maximal run of spelled pence values the total foots, accepting commas, *and*, and a multi-word hundreds phrase, and it keeps the match **only when the run sums to the total**. That last clause is what makes it a footing test rather than a proximity test. **Over the five hundred and fifty chapters before this volume it returns 155 instances, 134 distinct, 111 hyphenated and 44 unhyphenated, 0 unparsed, range 3 to 280, and it reads all three of the seventy-pence columns that the conversion-keyed extractors miss.**

### 2.4 Chapter 0567 names a gate as the first occasion of the warm palm, and two chapters say it was not
