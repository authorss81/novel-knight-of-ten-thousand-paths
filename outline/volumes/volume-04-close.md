## 1. What the volume was about

**It is about a promise that nine generations have kept in a valley where nobody was ever asked what they swore, and about the discovery that a community can put a person in a column at birth exactly as efficiently as an office can, and that this is worse, because an office is at least an office and can be refused.**

A valley under a winter nine generations old is physically stable. The river runs, the stores hold, the births are normal, nobody is hungry, and the whole of its government, its economy and its marriage custom is built out of an unasked question. **Two hundred people were entered in a book on the day they were born by nobody**, and the book is the only roll in the valley, and it is nine generations deep, and it is a book of days, marks and hands with a mark column that holds one mark two hundred times. It is also, and this is the volume's finding, **a form.** It is ruled to the same rule as a printed Return of the Office of Quarters at Grayford, laid down about two hundred years ago in a room four steps below a byre, with four columns taken out of the middle of it and about four kept. The valley did not fill in the column that asked for a figure of men going out. It entered the whole count one line at a time, in its own hand, over a generation, and **nobody ever recognised it as a form, because there is not one form in that valley for it to be recognised against.**

**The volume's most consequential page is a second column with eight names in it and a ninth line that is not ruled across.** It is ruled in a man of fifty-eight's own ink, about the width of two fingers, on the ninth spread of the only roll in a valley of four hundred people, and eight people wrote their own names into it because a name in a book of that valley goes in because the person did it, and the ninth line is the same depth as the other eight and he will not rule it across. **The volume's most consequential figure is a figure of nine that is wrong by one**, made by a man of thirty-four on a step in the cold in about a minute with a rope-belt on, kept in the day column because it is what was said at about nine hundred doors, with a margin note in a man's own hand saying *eight named and one not found*. **The wrong one is a person who was not at a door and cannot be found, and the volume is careful to say so and careful not to say who.**

**And the volume's central finding is stated on the page, in the open, in Chapter 197, by a man of about twenty-eight of the ninth generation to a child of nine, and the volume does not improve on it:** that a community did to itself exactly what an office does, with no office in it at all, and that the difference is not that it is gentler. The rest of the book is that sentence's consequences.

**And the volume's best single act of writing is a man declining to rule a line across an empty space.** Not a triumph and not a failure: a man of fifty-eight ruling a ninth line at the same depth as eight others and not ruling it across, in a book nobody can be served with, and writing the reason in a margin in his own hand, and being asked afterwards to say why he had not filled it and giving a different reason. **It is not a victory over the office and it is not a defeat by it. It is the third thing, and the third thing is the volume's whole subject.**

## 3. The volume's argument, as it landed

**What Volume 03 pressed:** *an oath is a name, and a name is the only thing an office can act on, and therefore a county that keeps its promises in public in a field for a hundred and forty years has been handing its own address to every registry in three countries.*

**What Volume 04 answers:** *a place can do the same thing to itself with no office in it at all, and the difference between the two is not that it is gentler, and the second time round there is nobody left in the room who is able to refuse.*

**Where it is proved.** In two sheets of paper lying side by side on the floor of a room about four feet by six, in Chapter 172, where a printed Return of the Office of Quarters at Grayford and the ninth spread of the winter book turn out to be ruled to the same line within a hair, three columns to the same three widths, with about four columns taken out of the middle. Then in a document read out in a cold room across Chapters 171 to 174, where the finding is that **a form cannot be sent on, and a valley of about four hundred people cannot carry a sheet of paper to Grayford, and an office that builds a form so that the answer has to come back to it has built a form that a valley like this one can never answer, and the office was right to design it that way, and the office has never once been told this valley exists.** Then in Chapter 174, from a man of thirty-two, in a room, in the middle of a morning, not as the last line of anything: **an office is the most durable thing in this book. A county can break an oath in public. A family can burn a roll. An office waits.** And then the sentence that is the volume's answer to itself, said once in Chapter 178, by a man of thirty-four, on a bank: *we have had a hand at every wall in this valley for nine generations, and there has not been one road, and the reason there has not been one road is that about four hundred people have never once in nine generations wanted to be anywhere.*

**And the volume's own sentence about asking, which it says once and does not improve on**, is in Chapter 166, in a market, in about four seconds, from a young man of twenty-two who has been in a book since the day he was born: *I am not refusing the question. I have never once said I did not want to be asked what I swore. I want to be asked what I swore.* And then the second half, which is the whole of the volume's problem and which nobody in that valley and neither the man who said the first half has worked out how to want a thing and not have a page: *Then it happened and you did not have it.*

**Where the volume contradicts itself in its own favour, deliberately and well.** A community that argues itself into asking — on foot, at about nine hundred doors, in eleven days, in a cold, with four men and a woman and the same six things in the same words at every door, and nothing written down at any of them — gets a yes. It is a real yes, given by people who were asked by four of their own in a doorway. It is not unanimous, and the not-unanimous is in a book as a figure with a mark and a hand over it and not as a name. **And then those same people have to live in a valley where about two hundred of the people who said yes are the people a book says are the heirs of a winter.** The asking was right. The record that followed from it is the disease. **The cure and the disease are the same instrument**, exactly as in Volume 03, where the promise-and-release was a door made of the same material as the thing that was killing the county — and the difference is that in Volume 03 a county argued itself into the door, and in Volume 04 nobody argued at all, and the door is the same door.

**What it cost, on the page and in pence.** A winter nine generations old taken out of the ground in four days by about forty people who cannot afterwards say what they did. **A hole in a bank at a ford with a barrow run over the top of it**, and eleven yards of made ground lost every time a thing has to go round something. Four hundred yards of the best ground anybody in that valley had ever seen gone to a slope in nine days. A ninth stone off a coping on its own, and nobody in that valley ever asking a question about a stone. Six byres, four of nine roofs, a fifth roof on a house that is not there, and a mill. Nine bushels of seed in a four-foot aisle that has had no person in it for three generations. **A figure of nine that is wrong by one and a ninth line in a book that is empty and is going to be empty.** A figure of weeks refused, in four words and nine sentences. **About two hundred people leaving a valley of four hundred, and the first three out being the three that said no.** The wages of the winter's work footed on the page at **1,496 pence, which is thirty-one marks and eightpence**, in a county's money, in a valley that has had no coin in nine generations, with the valley's own price not a figure and the two never added.
