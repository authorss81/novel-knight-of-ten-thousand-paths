## 1. What the volume was about

**Volume 02 is a book about a records office and it never once says so.** It opens in a form — five ruled columns, one of them headed *Keeper*, and a marginal instruction on the reverse that manufactures a keeper out of a word and that nobody in fifty chapters mentions — and it closes in an entry in a day-book over a weigh-house, in a room with a door on a lane, at four pence a year, by a woman who has just written down that a number, a county and an hour is all there is against anything. **The two documents are the same document fifty chapters apart.** Between them: a count instead of a list, twice, in three trades, in two countries; a registrar who sells a book that should not exist and is then, for the price of one document, the only man in the world who can be shown to have done it; a county of nine hundred and forty-one hearths that has spent nineteen years being a claim about itself; a bench of nine in a fen that has been asked three times, has said yes three times, and knows the difference; a road that did not open and did not fail; and a bell on a bridge four hundred yards up a road that is nobody's, rung at the fourth hour and a half on a Monday by a boy of sixteen and a carpenter, and, four hundred miles off, by a tile-cutter of forty-one and a person nobody in Merefen has ever seen.

**What that costs a reader is worth stating plainly, because the volume does not state it.** A volume whose subject is a filing system asks a reader to care about a filing system, and this one earns it by never once letting anybody be wrong about a document and by making every competent person pay for being competent. But the cost is a cost of register. Fifty chapters in which a great many of the best pages are rooms with documents in them, in which a bench minute, an endorsement, a bill and a county column each get more page time than a person does. The reader who came for a man holding a bridge in the rain has spent a year with a woman holding a rule. That is a legitimate book and it is a different book, and a close that pretended otherwise would be lying about the manuscript. **The second cost, which this close was told to look for and did, is that a volume which is a book about a records office may have been a book about forms** — §10, item 1, and the finding stands.

## 2. The volume's argument, as it landed

The thesis the book has been pressing since Chapter 4 is that **a man who can cross believes that makes him the person who should decide.**

Volume 01's answer was that he is one of the things other people can hold. **Volume 02's answer is three sentences, none of them his, and he arrives at none of them.**

> **A record a person can contradict is a record of that person. A record that cannot be contradicted is a claim about that person. And what cannot be taken from you is being asked.**

The first two are a bridgewright's, arrived at in a shed in four days, in tons, in a notebook, after he has already written the mistake down twice. The third is a kiln-worker's, said in nineteen seconds to a room of nine men in a firing shed on the eleventh day, and it is the sentence the rest of the book is built on — and he knows in about a second and a half that it is the answer and that it is the most dangerous sentence anybody has said to him in five months, and he does not say the second half of it in that shed, **because once it is in a form in his own head it will get into his own hands, and he had watched his own hands write a list down in front of witnesses once already in another country.** The second half is in Chapter 84, in four days, and it is still in his notebook and not in a form.

It is arrived at a third time, independently, by a keeper of records in a fen in about a minute, over a list in a fair hand. A county four hundred miles off has answered a term in writing, itemised, and there is one line in it she cannot put down, and the line is about a man at a kiln who is alive and employed and correct in three books, and **the record is not asking her anything and there is no instrument in this kingdom that can make it ask her anything.** She reads it four times and does not put it down, and the reason she does not put it down is written in a day-book and is not a sentence about the man.

**Where it is proved.**

1. **In a room with a stove at Kilnholt, on the sixteenth day of a rolling** (Chapter 68). A decent magistrate with a column of figures that goes nine, eleven, nine, thirteen, fifteen, seventeen, twenty-one, twenty-four and never turns asks a stranger what he thinks — the first time in the book anybody has asked him for an opinion instead of a service. **He has nothing, and the reason he has nothing is that the magistrate is right about the fever.** What he says instead is the sentence the volume is built on: *if I tell this room not to burn that list, I am telling a county with a fever in it to keep a list, on my word, and I am the man who puts lists into counties by arguing about them in front of witnesses, and I have been doing this for eleven years and I am not going to do it again in a hat.* Then he asks for seven days, and the asking is the most expensive thing he does in fifty chapters, and it is the first time in the book that he asks a community for time instead of taking it.
2. **In a long room, over nine days, in nine places, by the county's own people, with no office reading anything and no stranger in it** (Chapters 88–89). The plan that saves the county is not his. It is forty minutes a person instead of eleven, because a person has to be found and asked and brought and put back and there is no stove in eight of the nine places. It is slower and it is better and those two things are the same fact. The rollkeeper signs after two days on the volume's most professional sentence — *a roll that can be wrong is not a weaker roll, it is a roll that has to be read twice, and a roll that has to be read twice cannot be read by one man in a morning* — and then says the harder half: *and a roll that cannot be read twice cannot survive a person.*
3. **In a shed at the north end of a bridge, in about four seconds** (Chapter 90). He qualifies for the Second Door and there is no ceremony and nobody to give it, because a stage is a word in a term and the term is printed on the back of a form in a drawer, and the only other person in the county who can be asked is a tile-cutter who says *that was a stage, I do not know what it is and I have witnessed it*, and puts her mark on the page because the rule in that county is that a hand witnesses a thing and does not understand it.
4. **In a merchant's counting house, in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists** (Chapters 95, 99). A creditor breaks all four of his own conditions in four leaves in a fortnight, and the reason he breaks them is not denied but simply not put next to the thing, and he asks for the argument to be minuted and the minute put where a Crown's man will find it in about nine years, and does not know how. It is the first document in the series held on purpose by a person in a path.

**Where the volume's own last ten chapters contradict it.** This is the finding the audit was told to look for and it is a real one, not a technicality.

1. **A body corporate can be sued.** On the ninth of Greenmonth the county council resolves that the Bellweather Claims Archive is a body corporate, with a door, a number, and the capacity to sue and to be sued — on a form whose printed space for a thing's *name* was filled and whose printed space for its *purpose* was left empty, the words in the filled space being the founder's own, of which there are **thirteen**. **Nobody in the room says the sentence and the chapter does not say it, and a yard does, three times, in the same words, to one person at a time: a body corporate can be sued, a keeper can only be dismissed.** That is the volume's own argument, landed on the volume's protagonist's employer by a county council on somebody else's initiative, and the volume lets it stand without a word. It is not a contradiction; it is the argument's own consequence, arriving through a door nobody in the book opened. A record a person can contradict is a record of that person — **and a corporation is a person, and the county has just made one, and a person can be got at.**
2. **A figure is a record about a place and cannot be contradicted by anybody in it.** The second count is answered with **one thousand six hundred and nine**, taken out of a day-book, and it is lawful, and it is checked by nobody, and it is the answer to Edric's question about who else this road warden has spoken to, and it is made by a keeper of records, for the Crown, and nobody in the room notices until a three-line reply proposes a bigger one. **In Chapter 54 Mara Vey said that a list with the names taken off is the one document she has spent four years refusing to make. She made it. It was the right answer. It is how the Crown got further in seven months than in eleven years.**
3. **The Office of Provincial Registration's answer to a figure is a bigger figure, and it is cheaper than names**, and Sabin Dain says so out loud, in daylight, standing up, to about nine people including two clerks, on the twelfth of Greenmonth — *the office is not asking you to tell us anything; the office is asking you to confirm a figure; a figure has no name in it; in four years there will be a number in a file with no name attached to it, and that number will be about this county, and nobody will be able to say whose hand made it — not because the hand has been concealed, because a figure does not need one; and it is not coming because anybody thought of it in a room with a clock on the wall, it is coming because it is cheaper than names and there is not one instrument in this kingdom that can make it more expensive.* **She is right.** She is a witness in a county's minute and not in a register, and she has said that a witness is a figure in a book that answers a question for as long as the book is dry, and that it will be the only name in the file, and that she will have earned it and it will not help her at all.
4. **And a schedule of dispositions whose county column runs to eleven other counties is a column, and a column is not a cause**, and a keeper of records who has been the only person in the book who could see it looked at it four times in four minutes and refused to write the answer down.

So the argument landed, in two countries and five trades, in about four hundred and fifty pages, and it was then contradicted by a county council, by an office, and by a woman with a good filing system, all of whom were doing the right thing. **That is the correct shape for this book, and it is why the volume ends on a man at a kiln rather than on a settlement.** The volume does not resolve its own argument. It establishes that the argument is available to anybody willing to pay for it in a form, that four people in this case have now paid, and that the cheapest way to keep winning is to stop writing names down.

**What it cost.** Two hundred and eight days of main-world calendar, of which **one hundred and fourteen are absence** — the volume is more than half absence. The two absences are twenty-six and eighty-eight days against a printed warning of three weeks in an office's hand that the office cannot say whose figure it is. **The error is five days on the first crossing and sixty-seven days on the second**, the ratio is about three-quarters of the absence and is not a constant, and the volume's whole lesson is one number: **the error is not in the warning, the error is in him, and it scales with the work, exactly as he wrote it down in Greenmonth before he went.** Two fevers of three days. A lost morning. Two hundred and fifty-four fired tiles ground back into clay at a cost of twelve marks one shilling and sevenpence over two years, on a schedule, in a season the county has a name for and this calendar does not. **Fifty marks one shilling and sixpence** in a trust's account that nobody can pay, with a line in it carrying no figure at all. **Twelve marks and tenpence** for a copying post at three shillings a week, unclaimed for sixteen weeks and two days, in the county's hands, with no post for it to be in. Eleven tons of stone and a fortnight with a town's crossing shut to carts. A road warden's vote and not an office. A sister's salary the reeve's office calls a discouragement. And the standing cost, unpaid and unpayable: **the exact sequence of an ordinary morning with his brother — six or seven things in an order, a particular Tuesday, a room over a cooper, a yard on Weir Street, eleven years of it — which he can do and cannot put in order and does not know the order of, and which his brother has said out loud in a yard, in his own words, once, and will not improve on: *I am not going to re-teach you your own brother.***

**The book-sized form of the answer**, in a day-book, and it is not a sentence about the man: *because a keeper of records who asks a question has already decided that the answer is not in the records she keeps, and a keeper who has decided that has stopped being a keeper and started being a person who wants something.*

## 3. The seven-block audit

The volume is five batches of ten chapters. **Batch 0006 has no prompt directory and never had one** — it ran off the generic continuation prompt, which carries no cards — so the only surviving record of the plan for Chapters 51–60 is its entry in `state/batch-summaries.md`, with **six card deviations and an eight-item date-correction paragraph** recorded after that batch closed. The plan for 61–70, 71–80 and 81–90 is the Batch 0007, 0008 and 0009 entries with **seven, eleven and twelve** deviations. The plan for 91–100 is this close's prompt and the Batch 0010 entry, with **twelve** deviations and two review repair passes. **Forty-eight numbered deviations and eight date corrections between them, all in those five entries, none in memory.** The audit below is against the outline's cards, not against the outline's intentions, and every block ends by saying which of its deviations became canon.

### Block 1 · Chapters 51–60 · The form and the registrar

**What the cards promised.** A second Form R.C. 4 comes back and a person comes with it, and the person is Sabin Dain and she is not a member of anything. The marginal instruction is read aloud in a gateway in front of four men. The reverse carries the three-week figure as a standard of measurement, in an office that cannot say whose it is. A count of persons arrives instead of a list. A market-day hall answers the count's own question and gets **eleven and not four**. Halven takes the marshal's shield, sells the *Anchors, schedules and dispositions, Alder Vale*, and discloses it unasked. The second severance's index is read off the front leaf of a creditor's writ register and the reading produces one fact and three refusals. The scheduled crossing on the ninth of Embermonth is **refused by a woman who asks one question.** He goes anyway, on the same morning, from the near end.

**What the page delivered.** All of it, and two things the cards did not ask for. The refusal landed as the best page in the block: she asks *are you the one who sends people*, he says no, she says that the last one said so too, **and the road does not open, and he does not argue, and he stands on a bridge in a market town and lets a woman in another county refuse him**, and the toll-clerk at the Bellweather end says the thing the reader needs — *a road that is refused is worth more than a road that is open, and a county that refuses a road has something to refuse it with, and this county has nothing.* The thing the cards did not ask for is **Dain's refusal to write the word *none* in a box headed *State the office, if any*,** on the ground that a man who writes *none* has conceded that there ought to be one, and her writing instead: *Aren Kest, road warden, in the roll of the Merefen bridge guild, and no other office.* **That sentence is the plant Volume 03 is built on and it was written in Batch 0006.**

## 4. The volume's five blanks, and why they are five

A finding the audit had to make, because it is the volume's best structural trick and it is invisible:

1. **The fifth space headed *struck*, with no sixth** (Chapter 66, Ash). A sheet with four columns and a foot. Somebody ruled five and stopped, ninety-four years ago, with a knife.
2. **The county cell in the ninth line of an index of outgoing writs** (Chapters 58, 92). Ruled, on a line with a number and a date on it, with nothing in it, **and no space on the leaf for a reason, and there never was.**
3. **Four ruled cells at the foot of a provincial shelf register** (Chapter 79). Found in a fair copy of a leaf copied because a letter with no names in it asked for the register.
4. **Six lines in a county's book with nothing against them and no reason on them** (Chapters 79, 80, Ash).
5. **The eighth ruled line in the served schedule of the eighth of Fallowmonth** (Chapters 58, 98). Seven villages by name, `sum not stated`, eight ruled lines, the eighth the city's and empty, with a reason under it copied in the hand of a county boy of nineteen who asked whether it would still be on the sheet in a year. It is still empty, and it is now the oldest document in the case and the one nobody has asked her about.

**No two of the five are in one room, in one chapter or in one sentence anywhere in Volume 02, and no character in either country says that any two of them are the same. A later volume may not put them together.** A sixth blank — the space on a form for a thing's *name*, left empty on the county council's resolution — is not on this list and is a different kind of thing: it was left empty by accident, by a clerk in a hurry, in about a minute.

## 8. The corrections Volume 03 must not undo

These are the ones a reader would notice, and they are on the page, and they are not the next volume's to revisit.

1. **The breach is open and is not to be closed in Volume 03.** Not in a scene, not in an aside, not in a document, and **not by having either of them apologise.** Volume 03 is where they establish *explicit rules* for recording cost, dissent and authority during the Marches crisis — **and a procedure is not a repair.** The rules are a workaround, built around a concealment rather than ending it, and the volume must say on the page that this is what they are, in a document, in a room, without either of them using the word *wound* to the other. **The repair is forbidden in Volume 03, and Volume 04's chosen intimacy is bought with it.** A later writer may not have her ask him about the morning; may not have him tell her; may not have either of them say they have been wrong; and **may not have it turn out to have been a misunderstanding, because it is not a misunderstanding, it is a decision entered eight times in one month in a book nobody can serve.**
2. **The road is a line and two hands, a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping the road. If the witness is not there the road does not open and it does not fail, and nobody is to say afterwards that it failed.** A Volume 03 writer may not improve this into a mechanism, may not make it faster, may not let a road open on a substitute, and **may not let a road that did not open be described afterwards as having failed, by anybody, in any register, including retrospectively and including kindly.**
3. **Bellweather-Living's two stewards are Sera Quen and the man with the mallet, and Aren is neither, and he is not a third thing.** He is a **road warden of the Merefen bridge guild with a vote**, on the roll, not holding the crossing, not voting, having said in a hall in Frostmonth that he would come when he was wanted. **He is not a steward of anything, must not hold a bench seat, and must not stand in for a steward where one is named.** A Volume 03 writer may note — and should — that the word **custodian** is now available and is a different office with a different consent behind it, and that the Marches' word is neither.
4. **The name of Edric Hunn is on a separate sheet under a separate number and is never on the same sheet in the same county as the claim.** And one step further: **his name is not written anywhere in Chapters 91–100 at all, and the join between the ninth line of an index and a signing office is not stated once in fifty chapters.** A Volume 03 writer may not put a name in a column, may not join the two halves, and may not have the marshal appear. **The marshal is told nothing and learns what he learns in a file.**

## 9. The three hooks, in the order §11 gives them

Each stated as a document with a date and an owner, and each with what Volume 03 is required to do with it and what it may not do.

### Hook 1 · The disposition schedule's county column, and eight counties with a stone in a field

*Document:* **the county column of a schedule of dispositions**, copied whole on the fourth leaf of a four-leaf fair copy made for the purpose of a purchase, read in a room over a weigh-house on the **twenty-seventh of Greenmonth, YR 313, a Saturday, a market day, the fourth hour**, and entered in the day-book **as a question and not as a finding, with the hour, against nobody, no opinion.** *Owner:* **Mara Vey**, keeper; and the underlying column is in the possession of **Alis Merrow** in a counting house in a city that does not admit to the Crown that it exists. *What is on it:* **a column of eleven other counties, and eight of the eleven have a boundary oath on a stone — a long field, a boundary, a season, a stone renewed in public, in a year anybody there can name, by men who are still alive and who can be asked what they swore and to what and in whose name — and the other three have nothing in the leaf at all: no stone, no oath, no season, no year.** The disposition word against one of the eleven is a word the County of Ash uses for something else. *Who can see it:* **exactly two people in the world** — a registrar who sold the book, and a keeper who has a copy of a page of it — **and neither of them can say what an oath has to do with a severance.** It is on the page as a column and not as a revelation, and the sentence that refuses it is the volume's last joke at its own expense: *a thing you do arithmetic on is not a thing you have looked at.*

**What Volume 03 is required to do with it.** **Make the arithmetic stop working.** The volume's question is the one §11 hands over — *why are the Culling Order's records concentrated around agricultural oaths?* — and the answer has to be structural, arrived at by two people in a room out of a column, because a column is the only thing any of them has. **The pattern is not a pattern and the volume must say so**: eight counties with a stone are eight counties in which somebody can be asked; an oath renewed in public by men who are still alive is the only durable consent a rural county can produce; **and an oath is also the only document in the countryside that names a body, and a body can be served.** So the records concentrate around oaths because an oath names a body, and because the counties without a stone are the counties where nobody survived to swear one and there is nothing to serve. That is the small, true, worse answer. And the finding that makes the volume a volume: **a boundary in the Marches is made of water, so the person who holds the water and the person who holds the oath have been the same person for a hundred and forty years, and when the water began killing the soil the oath had to be kept by people who did not swear it — and the eight stones are not eight of the same instrument, and a column is not a cause.** *What it may not do:* it may not turn the column into a prophecy; may not have anybody use the phrase *Culling Order* in connection with it; may not have a Crown's man explain it; may not have the eight counties become a list of names of dead communities; and **may not resolve the pattern before Chapter 141.**

### Hook 2 · A form of the Office of Provincial Registration with a marginal instruction that manufactures a keeper, and Sabin Dain

*Document:* **Form R.C. 4, Return of Contact**, of the Office of Provincial Registration; ruled columns *Object*, *Date*, *Person having knowledge*, *Keeper*, *How often*; undated on the copy that came; **and on the reverse, in the office's own hand:** *where no office of keeper is found in the county schedule, the person having knowledge shall be entered under the column headed Keeper, and the entry shall not be construed as the creation of an office.* First in the case on the **first of Embermonth, YR 312, a Wednesday**, and read out loud in a gateway in front of four men by an officer who had filled that column about two hundred times, who had asked twice in writing to have the instruction explained and had been told it was standard. *Owner:* **the Office of Provincial Registration**; the copy is in a shed four hundred miles off in an unlocked drawer. **The woman who read it out loud** is Sabin Dain, 33, aid to the register, not a member of anything, who **refuses to discuss the column in a gateway** and says thank you when a man off a road does not press her, and the frightenedness is not spoken.

**What Volume 03 is required to do with it.** **Learn what it is being read as, and learn it out of a file and not out of a person.** The plant has been in print since Batch 0006 and understood by nobody, and Chapter 94 was written so that it could not be noticed. The volume's beat: the clause is not a law, it is a **practice**, and the practice has already been used — **a name entered in a *Keeper* column on a village's own paper by somebody who is not the office, and an office's marginal instruction cited in a return, and a man thereby made answerable for a road he keeps and cannot refuse.** And the private keeper: **his own entry — *Aren Kest, road warden, in the roll of the Merefen bridge guild, and no other office* — is the sentence being read as a keeper credential in a provincial file**, and the officer who wrote it refused to write the word *none* on purpose. That is the volume's argument landing on its own protagonist, and it is the Volume 3 row of the knowledge-control table: *that his route signature is being misread as a keeper credential.* **Sabin Dain writes the letters, will write more of them, and in Volume 03 offers a road warden's rank and a sealed personal record in exchange for closing the eastern paths** — and the offer is a recruitment maneuver, and a *warden's* rank is a **command rank** in that county's vocabulary and not a custody, and the thing she is buying with it is a man's evidence about his own reading of a column. *What it may not do:* **it may not be put in anybody's mouth as an explanatory sentence.** It may be printed in an office's hand, quoted in a return, cited in a file, and refused about in a gateway. **Sabin Dain may not explain it, may not be asked about it twice, and may not change her want**, which is that no family in this country is ever again removed from a record by a name nobody entered, and which has nothing to do with Aren.

### Hook 3 · A tile on a coping at Ninebridge, a hand's width, with a rope on it and no yoke

*Not a document.* **A fired clay tile, six inches by four, on the coping of the second span of the Ninebridge, with a rope on it in two loops and no yoke, no headstock and no striker, carrying the device of a hand laid flat on a book, and the county's own kiln's mark on the back.** It has been on that coping since before the roll was begun. ## 10. What Volume 02 did badly, and what Volume 03 inherits on purpose

Nine things. The first three are defects of the prose, the next four of the structure, the last two are things to keep.

**1. A volume which is a book about a records office may have been a book about forms.** This is the finding the close was told to make and it holds. The Crown's whole method in Volume 02 is documentary: a form, an instruction, a count, a second count, a resolution on a form, a fair copy, a leaf, a schedule, a purchase, an endorsement, a bill, a minute, a finding, a return, a day-book. **The book's own argument is that this is the correct method and it is working, and that is why the volume is good.** But the consequence for the prose is that **a great many of the best pages in the last block are pages in which documents are read aloud and checked, and the human event in most of them is a woman deciding not to write something.** Volume 02 crossed from a book about records into a book about forms somewhere around Chapter 95 and stayed across the line. **Volume 03 must correct this by putting a body at the centre of its paperwork: a river in spate, a footpath under water, a man in a ditch at four in the morning, a bridge deck in weather, a seed vault with salt in it.** The forms are there. They are not the event.

**2. The same rhythm, about forty times, and the reader learns it by Chapter 23.** The Volume 01 close recorded that approximately thirty chapters end on a line in which somebody corrects him, refuses him, or prices him. **Volume 02 does the same thing, more consistently, in a lower register: a chapter ends on a sentence in which somebody declines to do a thing for a reason nobody expected, and the reason is a craft reason and not a moral one.** Quist's *day and a half of a wedging shed*. The ash-driver's *a number in a week he has not counted*. The kiln clerk's *two hundred and forty on the far wall*. The slate taken off in four seconds. *A thing that cannot be produced is a thing that cannot be corrected.* It is the best rhythm in the book and it is the same rhythm about forty times, and a reader trained by Volume 01 to watch for the refusal is, by Chapter 80, watching for the refusal instead of reading the page. **Volume 03 must break it in the last block: the Marches council refuses him in a form and then, in the same week, the same people ask him for something, and the asking is not a reversal and is not sentimental.** And **at least one refusal in Volume 03 must be accepted without a reason being given**, which this manuscript has never once done.

**3. The meta-language correction held, and the two things that did come back were worse.** Batch 0009's review pass found and removed **thirty-three narrator-voice references to the book** in ten chapters — *this volume* thirty-three times, *the volume's argument*, *this chapter*, *the chapter* — including two passages that addressed the reader about the following forty pages and about a character, and it recorded the rule for the rest of the volume: **the narrator may not use the words this volume, this chapter, the chapter, this book as a book, the argument of the volume, or any phrase that tells a reader what a book is doing.** **The correction held across the last ten chapters.** There is not one instance of narrator-voice reference to the book outside a physical object in Chapters 91–100; the three that exist in the block are diegetic — two in Aren's own hand in Chapter 84 and one a private book in a counting house in Chapter 81 — and a check of all fifty files finds *Culling Order* twice in the whole manuscript, *Gatehouse* spelled once in Volume 02, and *custodian* not at all. **The defect did not come back, and this close is obliged to say so plainly rather than manufacture a finding.** The two things that did come back in the same ten chapters were worse and are named here so that no later phase is surprised: **a real-world month name spoken twice in Chapter 96, and the forbidden sum of the four thousand and the seven hundred and twenty performed in Chapter 98, in the middle of the batch's own guardrail paragraph.** Both were introduced by state files, both were found by a review pass, and **the second review pass then found five sentences that a state file had promoted to canon and the prose did not say.** **The finding is about this project's method and not about the prose: in this repository the state files are the least reliable document, and a phase that takes its facts from them without going and looking at the page will carry an error forward with a footnote saying it is canonical.** Every later phase must take a fact from a state file and then go and look at the page.

**4. Nine months of Ash in ten chapters is a great deal of clay, and the county's texture is thinner than its argument.** Chapters 78–90 are the strongest stretch in the volume and they are also ten chapters of the same room, the same kiln shed, the same long room with a stove, the same shed at the north end of a bridge. **The county has four kilns, eight villages, a market town, a physician, a rollkeeper, a magistrate, a river called the Kiln, ash-woods, a seed economy, a bench, a strike, a road that is tiled, and a boy with a slate, and the volume uses about nine of them.** Four consequential people there are addressed by trade and unnamed — the woman who fires at the Kilnholt kiln, the woman of about sixty on three hearths, the clerk of the counting house, and the man with the ledger, whose name is withheld on the page by design — and the withholding is right in a county that has no column for a person. **Volume 03 inherits the withholding on purpose and corrects the thinness**: the Marches must have a landscape that is doing something, four trades it can lose, and at least one person it does not name who is *load-bearing rather than poignant.*

**5. Nobody is in work in trouble except one yard and one bench, and the bench's money problem is solved by the same answer four times.** Volume 01's close asked for a labour failure in Volume 02 and got it — two kilns closed and about forty men out and thirteen who did not go back — **and it is the only labour failure in fifty chapters, it is in a path, and it is resolved by the protagonist's own method.** The Sedge Reach costs eleven tons and a fortnight and is paid for by a county paying a man six pence out of a box, which is the best beat in the volume and is also the only money in it. **Volume 03 must have a main-world employer problem that the bench cannot vote on** — and the natural one is already on the page: **a yard in a fen town is offered four hundred miles of river work by men from a county it has never been to, and the money is real, and the second crossing is the consequence of a wage.** The yard takes the Marches job because a yard has to eat, not because a bridge failed.

**6. The volume's one reversal is a blank, and a blank is the quietest possible instrument.** The book's thesis — a promise about a stone kills people — was asserted for ninety chapters of Volume 01 and then cashed in a column that somebody stopped ruling ninety-four years ago with a knife. **That is the best structural joke in a hundred chapters and it cannot be repeated, because there is one blank per volume and Volume 02 spent the good one.** Volume 03's reversal must be bodily: **a river that changes course.** A record may be the shape a volume turns on once. Water is the shape the next one turns on.

**7. Two threads were opened in this volume and then left to their own schedules, and both of them are now long.** The **second severance** has been open since Chapter 58 and is not resolved and cannot be until Chapter 894 — that is correct and it is held. The **eastern-counties claimant and her eleven names** were spent in Chapter 54 and not returned to, and a county of ash four hundred miles off has now become the volume's far end, so the eastern counties now have two Ash-adjacent presences and one live question. The volume's own discipline held: **the brine weights are cited and not filed for a second time, and the eighth ruled line was looked at three times and not filled**, and the frequency of the refusals did not drop. **No Volume 03 chapter may build a scene out of the eastern-counties claim; it may be entered in a day-book once, and the eleven names are not to be listed.**

**8. The volume's one deliberate structural lie is now a liability a later writer could trip over.** The Book 0006 date-correction paragraph exists, the Chapter 10 column does not foot, the scour was a washtub for "eight weeks" in one chapter and most of a year in another, and the prompt's "two hundred and twenty-three days" is thirty days out. **Four known defects, three of them arithmetic.** None of them is worth a repair pass and all four are recorded here, and **the rule that follows is the real output of this close: a later phase states a figure, checks it, and records the check. A defect that is recorded is a fact about the book. A defect that is inherited silently becomes a canon with a footnote.**

**9. Inherit on purpose.** The refusal, the "not here," the distinction between a road that does not open and a road that fails, the refusal to total, the refusal to file the brine weights, the refusal to write the one sentence the whole case wants, the four different kinds of holding, the refusal to name what a form is being read as, the ordinary labour everything is built on, and **the sentence pattern that a reason is a thing believed and a minute is a thing set.** Correct on purpose: the register. Volume 02 made its argument about records entirely through people refusing to give things away. **Volume 03 must make the same argument through a record that is given away lawfully, on a form, with a receipt, and read back to the man it is about** — because that is what Dain's offer is, and what a file is, and what an office does.

**Findings this close records, and what it does about each.**

1. **The promise's age is two hundred and twenty-three days, the shape is right, and this close's first statement of this finding was itself wrong and is withdrawn.** Chapter 92 says the promise of the twenty-second of Fallowmonth, YR 312 is **"two hundred and twenty-three days"** old at the fifth of Greenmonth, YR 313, and that figure is carried into five state files. **The figure is correct.** Counted from the thirty-day month the whole calendar is built on and from nothing else: Fallowmonth has eight days after the twenty-second, then **seven** months of thirty — Embermonth, Wolfmonth, Frostmonth, Hearthmonth, Goatmonth, Thawmonth **and Mudmonth** — and then five days of Greenmonth: **two hundred and twenty-three days across seven month boundaries**, which is what the chapter says in the same breath as *it has taken me seven months*. **The shape is canon and the number is canon and no prose change is owed to either.** The review of this close found that the first version of this finding dropped Mudmonth out of the sum, arrived at one hundred and ninety-three, declared correct prose an error, and put that figure into five state files in bold with an instruction that no later phase re-derive it. **It is withdrawn in all five, and the record of what was wrong with it is `reviews/volume-02/volume-02-close.md`.** **What survives of the first finding is the reason it went wrong, and it is the most useful thing in this section: the two weekday labels it used came from two different chains.** The twenty-second of Fallowmonth, YR 312 is a Monday on the Volume 01 and 02 chain and a Saturday on the chain Volume 03 runs on; the fifth of Greenmonth, YR 313 is a Sunday on the first and a Friday on the second. **Pairing a Monday with a Friday is what made a bad sum look like a confirmed one. Two hundred and twenty-three is six days over a week, so any correct pair of endpoints is six days apart in the week, and Monday to Friday is five.** The chains themselves are item 270 and they are not this close's to reconcile. Continuity item 263 as corrected, and **no Volume 03 figure may be derived from the promise's age**, which was true before this finding and is true now.

The corrected figure is on the page twice in the volume's own last ten chapters, in a book of its own, and a repair would mean editing a chapter no phase owns for a fact a reader can only catch by doing arithmetic across a month boundary.

Both belong in this document and neither is going to be improved on, because a close that praises the volume is a bad volume close.**
