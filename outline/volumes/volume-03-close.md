## 1. What the volume was about

**It is about a promise made in a field, and about the fact that in a farm county a promise and a boundary are the same object, and about a man who could see that and had no standing to say so until the last day of the year, when he was asked for nothing and gave something away.**

A boundary in the Hearth Marches is made of water. The man who holds the ditch and the man who holds the oath have been one word for a hundred and forty years, and the oath is renewed in public, on a stone, in a year anybody there can name, by men who are still alive — and every renewal moves the ditch a hand's width to the left, and a hundred and forty years of hand's-width moves have put a water table a mile wide under four villages, and the salt has come up out of the ground it was always in. Nobody poisoned anything. The ditch did what a ditch does. **The volume's most consequential page is a list of twenty-nine thumbs**, and its most consequential figure is a name.

The volume is also about what that arrangement turns out to be *for*, and the answer is worse than the problem. A county that renews a public oath once a year, in a field, in front of people who are still alive, has been producing the only durable consent a rural county can produce — and it has been producing it in the only form the countryside has that **names a body**, and a body can be served. The records gather where the oaths are. **It is not because oaths cause anything. It is because an oath is a name.** And the Marches has known this about itself since the ninth rolling and has entered it in a book that cannot be served, in a county with no office, no writ, no bell and no schedule, and has told nobody, and is now a place a record gathers in.

## 2. The volume's argument, as it landed

**What Volume 02 pressed:** *a record a person can contradict is a record of that person, and a record that cannot be contradicted is a claim about that person, and what cannot be taken from you is being asked.*

**What Volume 03 answers:** *an oath renewed in public by men who are still alive is the only durable consent a rural county can produce, and it is also the only document in the countryside that names a body, and a body can be served, and that is why the records gather where the oaths are.*

**Where it is proved.** In two people in a room, out of a column, in Chapter 140, and out of a leaf and a fair copy across four chapters: Oswin Rill's return of the twenty-ninth of Greenmonth, YR 313 and the whole leaf sent north unasked on the first of Longlight and arriving on the fourth, and the marginal note in an office's hand reading *Fourth column relied upon as the office of record for the object*, and the back of the leaf in a seed-carter's own hand dated two years before the return — *I do not keep this. I have not kept this. I did not put my name in the fourth column.* **A record a person can contradict is a record of that person; the fourth column is a claim; the back of the leaf is a record; and the office that holds the leaf has a column headed *Keeper* and has never turned the leaf over.** That is Volume 02's whole thesis restated inside Volume 03's first block, by a man who was never going to be asked, in a village four hundred miles off.

And then the second half, which is new: **the same public field that produced the durable consent produced the list, and the list is the first time in the volume that the record and the consent are the same object.** Twenty-nine of fifty-eight households put their own thumb on a board with three columns and none of them a name, three weeks before a day named a rolling ahead, and the council entered at the head of its own list that the list is keyed to the roll and that both books are in one room and that a man with both can turn a household into a person — and did it anyway, in the minute-man's hand, where a man who does not like it will find it. **The county volunteered itself and then entered, in advance and in the wrong hand, the exact sentence by which it can be volunteered again.**

**Where the volume contradicts itself in its own favour, deliberately and well.** The promise-and-release is the only door the Marches has, and it is made of the same material as the thing that was killing it. An oath that can be broken, in public, by the men who swore it, on a day named in advance, with a release in it and a consequence and no office behind it — that is a document, and a document is a name, and a name is what a body can be served by. **The cure and the disease are the same substance.** That is not a flaw in the plan; it is the finding, and the volume states it in a stone room and does not improve on it.

**And the volume's question — *what does a promise owe the people who keep it, when keeping it is the harm?* — is answered by a door and not by a cure.** The door is a promise that can be broken on a day named in advance. **A door is a thing you can be killed in.** Nobody in the volume says that sentence and it is the sentence the volume is built out of.

**What it cost, on the page and in pence.** The re-cutting of a mile of boundary in nine days in standing water, **1,752 pence of which the county had none**, and the difference from the same work in the fifth rolling is **1,272**, and Coll Ferrow says out loud in a room that the difference is what it costs to do a thing twice. Four men of a fen yard paid for their last twenty-seven days in **ninety-six bushels of salt, 864 pence, eighteen marks exactly, landing clean**, and the rest of their stay at **none**, in two books, four men and a date and the word *none* in a money column. A **permanent limp** acquired at about the fifth hour of the last night of a year on the far side of a line, before the return stitch closed, in the leg he had chosen to put across the failing bay. A footbridge with the near bay hanging four inches off the far one. And **a breach that is still open, nine not-askings entered and no tenth, and no apology in any form in fifty chapters.**

## 3. The five-block audit

The audit is against the cards and against the recorded deviations, not against the outline's intentions. The five entries in `state/batch-summaries.md` hold **fifty-eight recorded deviations between them — ten for Chapters 101–110, eleven for 111–120, eight for 121–130, twelve for 131–140 and seventeen for 141–150.** That count is on the page and it foots, and this close checked it rather than taking the first printing of its own prompt, which said fifty-one and gave components that came to sixty-two.

### Block 1 · Chapters 101–110 · The Figure and the Stone

*Main world, thirty-one days, from the thirtieth of Greenmonth to the thirtieth of Longlight, YR 313. No crossing, no path scene, no System display, no stage claimed, no mechanism explained.*

**The card promised** a delegation, six terms, a guild's own terms, a standing offer with a sealed record, a form with a *Keeper* column, and three rules in a day-book.

**The page delivered** all of it, and delivered two of them earlier and in a different county than the cards had them. **The six terms are fixed in Chapter 104 and not Chapter 114**, printed in full off a four-foot board of lath and lime in chalk because a board cannot be copied out of and a copy in a book is a thing a court can have; and a later writer may not re-word a line of them. **Coll Ferrow and the woman who renews the stone are not named in this block at all** — §14 holds the volume to seven names and the manuscript has a precedent for keeping a path face to a trade for a whole block — and the delegation is **eleven of the council, of whom nine are householders, plus two of the guild, plus a seed-carter who is not on the council**, which is the detail the block's best scene turns on.

**The deviations, and whether they improved the book.** Ten. Four improved it and are canon: the six terms landing in 104 and fixing the wording; the delegation's composition, because a council of eleven that includes two guild men who came *with* the council and not under it is a council that has already made the distinction the volume is about; the leaf's four dates, which a later writer may not move; and the second Crown instrument, the instruction of the ninth of Longlight with an object nobody in Merefen can find, which the card did not ask for and which the volume needed.

**One damaged the book and was recorded rather than fixed.** Chapter 105 was the block's only attempt at a crossing that does not open, and it is better than the card, but the block also printed the Ash figure nine hundred and forty-one aloud in a room over a weigh-house in Chapter 103, which the block's own repair pass removed. **A Volume 03 figure may not be got from the promise's age, and 941 is a number a reader in this project is going to notice twice**, and it was noticed twice, and once was right.

**And the block's real cost is not on its list of deviations at all, it is in the paragraph under the three rules, in the same ink:** *a rule about what is written down is not a rule about what is done; a procedure built around a thing that is not being said is a procedure and not a cure; and the day this office stops entering the not asking is not the day the rules have done their work.* **The volume says on the page, in a document, that its own central device does not work, in the block that introduces it, and then spends fifty more chapters on it.** That is the most honest page in Volume 03 and it is in Block 1, and almost nobody will remember it.

### Block 2 · Chapters 111–120 · The River Changes Course

*Main world to the twenty-second of Rainmonth, then path time. A count answered on the morning he goes out, a line made ready, a crew that goes in seven days earlier on somebody else's calendar, and nine days in a county that has never refused anything.*

**The card promised** a second count, the six terms, a line made ready, the crew going in, the crossing out, the first sight of the place, and the refusal.

**The page delivered** the block's central fact, which the card did not have: **the road did not open at all inside the ten days, and he cannot tell whether it was refused or merely not kept, and there is no procedure for a far side that has not decided, and nobody in either country says the word.** It is the first crossing in this case that has come back with no answer of any kind, and the whole of the next nine months happens without one. **The first crossing's drift figure — ten days of absence against a printed three weeks, an error of minus eleven — is the one number in the volume he can check**, and what he learns from it is that a good result teaches a man nothing and a bad one teaches him everything.

**The deviations, and whether they improved the book.** Eleven. Eight improved it. The best is the ninth: the drift figure is written **as a projection and not as a measurement**, in the Marches' own register, on faith, because the return is in Chapter 148's country and the page would otherwise have to convert two calendars. And the correction inside the deviation is the volume's whole method in one line — **the first pass subtracted the path interval from the warning and got minus twelve, and the error is the absence minus the printed warning and the path interval is nowhere in the sum.** Ten improved the book. One damaged it and is recorded: Chapter 120's attempt to re-run Chapter 110's failed conversion, now cut, because **a volume may not perform the same failure twice in nine chapters and call it a theme.**

**The block's real achievement is a rhythm it did not know it had.** The refusal in Chapter 120 is one question, asked in about four seconds, by a woman who has never been asked anything, and the answer is that a county that has never refused anything has no word for it and the word it has is the word for a thing that got lost. **The word *refused* is used fifty times in Chapters 101–120 and the block spends it as ordinary vocabulary so that Chapter 124's refusal of a working can land in the same word without anybody noticing the weight.**

### Block 3 · Chapters 121–130 · Thirteen Months, the First Third

*Path time, rollings one to four. A second refusal, the salt, the vault, the court of the Marches, and a man told in a room what a cut would be and agreeing that it is not his.*

**The page delivered** the mechanism exactly once, in trade terms, by Coll Ferrow in Chapter 131 and by Thessa Wend in Chapter 121, and **never again in fifty chapters.** The finding is eight sentences long and is not restated: a boundary is a channel, a ditch has a head, a trough is dug to a level and does not walk, the head has been eleven yards from the trough for a hundred and forty years, a pond against a bank on a mile of boundary is a water table a mile wide, and a water table a mile wide under four villages is the salt. **The volume's one refusal of a working he could do is in Chapter 124, and the reason is not a rule, it is a date, a community that lost contact for ever, and a witness that was forged afterwards — and he does not explain it and may not, and nobody understands the sentence.** That is Volume 06's plant, held for two volumes, in nine words, unexplained.

**The deviations, and whether they improved the book.** Eight. **The batch's own review called its claim that nothing was compressed to get under one unsupported by its own figures, and that judgement was correct**, and the repair added real consequence to four chapters and nothing else. Four improved the book. The best is the seventh: **the volume's central antithesis is spent once, in Chapter 130, and not twice** — the first pass spent the same construction in 125 and 130, five chapters apart, and the Chapter 125 instance is cut. Four improved. Two damaged the book and are recorded: **the block ran at forty per cent of the length of Batches 0011 and 0012 on the first pass and the repair pass did not close that gap** (16,315 words against 29,130 and 31,648, with Chapters 127 and 130 under half the batch median), and the section device was removed entire, which honoured a ceiling and lost a metronome.

**And the block's best page is one the cards did not ask for**, which is Chapter 130's leaf: a correction coming down the line nine days later, in a hand that is not a clerk's, from a woman four hundred miles off who keeps the road at that end, two lines long, one of them *we did not lose our register, we lost our refusal, and a register is what you have instead* — **and he has read that in a fair copy once already and has never been told where it came from, and now he knows, and the county does not know he knows.**

### Block 4 · Chapters 131–140 · Thirteen Months, the Second Third

*The river moves. The crew is on the wrong side of it. He takes command and is refused. The county road closes in writing. And the arithmetic stops working.*

**The card promised** a waterworks better than the north proved on the page, a flood in nine sentences, a boy of fifteen and an unnamed man, the command and the nine seconds and the hand-over of the sheet, the four-line notice, the call, and the arithmetic stopping.

**The page delivered** all of it, and the proof that the guild is better than the north is **not in a stranger's mouth**: no drawing, because a drawing of water is a picture of something that has already gone; three pegs on an old shoulder, because the shoulder is the drawing; and a line that came down two fingers to the left, **the same error the same first hand made eleven years earlier on the same system, written into the book as an expectation with the hour against it, because a thing you expect to be true in a year is worth more in a book than a thing you found out in a day.** The flood is the third answer and not a curse, and Coll Ferrow says so to Aren alone and gives him the date and not the reason, **because a council which expects a flood is a council with a plan, and this county has never had a plan for anything except a boundary.** Nobody blames anybody and nobody dies.

**The deviations, and whether they improved the book.** Twelve. **Nine improved it and are the best material in the volume.** The two that matter most are the fifth and the eighth. The fifth: the marks on the walls are found twice and the flood-time finding is the one that pays, and **they are not read, not copied, not measured into anything and not explained, and the water does not read them, and Ferrow does not go and look and asks one question in a yard and the guild's book has no line about it.** The eighth: the block is over its own prompt's per-chapter ceiling on four chapters, and **a close that trims a chapter because a prompt said 3,800 has misunderstood what the ceiling was for** — the four long ones carry the mechanism, the flood, the rescue and the finding.

**Two damaged the book and are recorded.** The first is the plan's own fault: `outline/volume-03.md` §8 Block 4 Chapter 139 says the notice was sent because the *fifth* of five terms forbids a name in the line's book, and canon is the *second*, and the chapter followed canon and **the outline was not edited by the batch and the discrepancy is recorded rather than smoothed.** The second is the block's cost against its own prompt in one specific place: **Chapter 136 is in the eighth rolling and not the seventh, and the day map absorbed the shift without listing it among the deviations**, which is the Volume 02 close's §10 item 3 happening for the ninth time in this project and the reason the shift is now continuity item 342.

**And the block contains the volume's answer.** Chapter 140, in a room, in the last chapter of the block: the eight stones are not eight of the same instrument, some of them fix a boundary between two fields and some of them fix a *count*, **a boundary about a share of water is not a boundary at all, it is a claim, and it has been renewed in public for a hundred and forty years by men who are still alive and it is killing the soil under a third of the Marches**, and the three counties with nothing in the leaf are the three where nobody survived to swear anything, **and a column of counties is not a cause.** The Marches is the sixth of the eight.

### Block 5 · Chapters 141–150 · Thirteen Months, the Last Third

*Path time, then the return, then twenty-two days in Merefen. The plan, the day named, the oath broken in public, the cost, the hand-over, the span, the limp, the account, the hearing, the last day.*

**The card promised** an oath that can be broken with a release in it, a list that is not a list of persons to be struck, boundaries re-cut in nine days, a name in a yard's book, a hand-over in the county's own form with no column for a road warden's vote, a span that holds a night river, a limp, a Marches account, a cooperative, a delegation, and a last image.

**The page delivered** all of it, and improved the two beats the cards had weakest. **The delegation does not arrive**, because the first of the Marches' six terms is that the road is not to be made to carry a person, so there is no way on earth to get anybody from that county to a fen town — **and a hearing entered for a date the other party cannot reach is the thing this office has done to two other offices in four years and a half.** The Archive resolves to hear it on the Marches' own terms and enters the hearing for **the ninth of Rainmonth of the year following, at the second hour**, and the resolution says the delegation is not to be sent for and that if it does not come the hearing is not adjourned and not refused and it stands. **Better than the card, and a recorded deviation.**

**The five reasons for the Chapter 145 route are on the page and are the answer to the plan's one open question:** the road is not made to carry a person and cannot be; a main-world room cannot be inside a path chapter; therefore the yard is not in the room and the room is in the Marches and **the brother is in a document**; the yard's name is in the guild's book in the twelfth rolling and in the fair copy in the thirteenth and **in the minute nowhere**; and the two men do not use the word brother in the scene, which the page makes a fact rather than an absence — Ferrow raises the two Kest names on one board as the objection he is obliged to raise and then refuses to have either name taken out, and the minute-man asks whether the two of them are anything to each other that the council is entitled to enter, and is answered *No*, and enters it exactly as given.

**The deviations, and whether they improved the book.** Seventeen, the most of any block, and **the two highest of them are the block's own corrections of its own state files.** The fourth — **the limp is acquired at the fifth hour and not the sixth, and the reason is the working and not the flood**, and it happens to the leg he had chosen to put across the failing bay rather than the sound one, and nobody in the block calls it a punishment, a sacrifice, or a cost of the flood — is the difference between a cost and a moral. The eleventh is the plant paid exactly and not as deception: he said in the ninth rolling that in five rollings half of four villages would sign whatever was read out to them, **it has taken eight rollings, and the number is twenty-nine of fifty-eight, and not one of the twenty-nine was deceived, and the thing that produced the marks was a board with three columns and none of them a name and a household's own thumb in wet chalk** — and the county has not learned it was wrong about a list and does not learn it.

**Two damaged the book and are recorded, and both are the same defect.** The block's own entry claimed the fair copy was four hundred and six words when it was one thousand one hundred and seventeen and the prose claimed four hundred and six anyway — **a figure a reader can check and cannot reconcile** — and it took a first pass and a second one to cut the account into a fair copy of **four hundred and eight words and a working on a second sheet**, which is the Chapter 149 model for the whole series' money discipline and the single best structural decision in the block. The second is a repetition of the first at the level of the state files: **the block's entry, three other files and the volume close's own prompt all printed the same wrong total, 34,117, and one of them printed a chapter figure in its own list that disagreed with the page, so that a single item held three mutually inconsistent numbers at once.**

## 5. The Chapter 138 contradiction, decided

**The defect.** `chapters/volume-03/chapter-0138.md` dated the guild's plan to the twelfth rolling at its lines 11, 13 and 43, and `chapter-0149.md` line 31 called the working's fourth column *the plan of the twelfth rolling*, against Chapter 139's ninth rolling with four rollings left in the year. A plan written in the twelfth rolling cannot be on the table in the eighth. The Batch 0015 review removed Chapter 138's two duplications in the same pass and left this alone, correctly, **because a fix has to move a date earlier in four places in two chapters and has to choose a rolling before the Water, and that is a plan-level decision and not a repair pass's.**

**The decision: the plan is of the twelfth rolling of the year before. This is now the page at all five places and it is not revisitable.**

**Why this rolling, and why it is the right one.** `chapter-0131.md` is in the fifth rolling and has Coll Ferrow hold up the book and say, before anybody has sat down: *That is the twelfth rolling. Written before I met any of you. It has been in that press since the spring and I have read it twice this week and I have not altered one line of it, and the reason I have not altered one line of it is that I wrote it in the twelfth rolling and it was wrong in the twelfth rolling, and a man who corrects his own plan before anybody has read it is a man who wants to be agreed with.* **The page already implied the year before, in a character's mouth, for exactly the reason a bridgewright would give: a plan written in a rolling when the water was sitting where it always sits has to carry a stop in it, and the plan is about the Water.** Dating it to the twelfth rolling of the year before therefore resolves the contradiction in the direction the manuscript supports, and it sits before the Water, which is the seventh rolling, which is what the four stops are for.

**What the fix actually was.** Five references in two chapters, and one of them was a fifth place nobody had found, which is the first column in Chapter 145. **The three in Chapter 138 and the one in Chapter 149 now read the twelfth rolling of the year before.** Chapter 131 is left as it is: *That is the twelfth rolling*, said in the fifth rolling by a man talking about a book, and a reader who has Chapter 138 will supply the year, and a man in a guild room does not say *of the year before* about a book he has had in a press since the spring.

**Three consequences, and they are binding.**

- **Aren arrives in the eighth rolling of the current year.** Chapter 138's clause is *the seventh of this year is the rolling before the man with the bar came up the stair*, and the seventh rolling of this year is the one before his arrival. **A later phase that audits the day map may not derive an arrival rolling from this and may not restate it in any other chapter; if it needs his arrival it must go and read Chapter 138.**
- **No arrival rolling may be printed in Chapters 111–140, none has been, and none may be inferred from a chapter that does not say it.** Batch 0015 restated no arrival rolling and touched no outline, and this close has changed nothing about the order of the blocks: **fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, tenth, eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth.** The plan's date moves; the day map does not.
- **The plan is a document written before he existed in that county, and that is the volume's structural spine.** Every time the plan beats his — four stops and no last day, the old banker's shoulder, the two-finger line, the date written in a hand and not in a mouth — **the reason is now visible on the page as a fact about the calendar and not as a slip.** A reader who checks the arithmetic will find it foots.

**One known looseness, recorded and not changed.** `chapter-0131.md` line 9 has *It has been in that press since the spring*, in a county whose year is thirteen rollings of four weeks counted from the first sowing, against a plan dated to the twelfth rolling of the year before. **It is loose. It is not wrong** — the twelfth rolling of a year is late, and late is a season, and a bridgewright who has had a book in a press since the spring does not date it to the rolling. **It is recorded here so that a later phase does not "correct" it and so that nobody calls it a fresh error.** The standing rule is the one the Volume 02 close set: **a defect that is recorded is a fact about the book; a defect that is inherited silently becomes a canon with a footnote.**

## 6. A state-of-the-world ledger at the close of Chapter 150

In the spirit of the Volume 02 close's §6, auditing and expanding the ledger already drafted at the foot of `state/continuity.md`. **The twenty-fourth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, a Friday, not a market day, the fourth hour, day 235 of the volume and the volume's last day.**

**The Hearth Marches, and its year.** Rolling farm country on a river the Marches call the Water, in **eleven villages and a market town**, on household fields and commons, about **four hundred people in the county**. **Thirteen rollings counted from the first sowing, four weeks to a rolling, a year is a year and a rolling, and a year ended inside this volume** — the last day of the thirteenth rolling, with the first sowing of the next within a week of it, noticed in Chapter 141 by the minute-man in the county's own words. **A boundary here is a channel.** The man who holds the boundary is *the bound* and the man who holds the water is *the ditchman* and the two words have been one word for a hundred and forty years, because a boundary here is a channel. **A road in the Marches is a causeway, and the people who keep it are the bridge guild, and the guild's word for its first hand is *the hand*, as in the County of Ash, and the two counties arrived at the same word from opposite ends of the world and neither of them has ever heard of the other.** **No main-world month name, no main-world weekday, and no conversion of a Marches rolling into a day of this calendar was spoken or written anywhere in Chapters 118–147**, and the one conversion attempted was refused in Chapter 130, in his own notebook, and the refusal is the plant.

## 12. The Stage 3 decision, and what it costs

**`outline/series.md`'s power ladder gives the Second Door to Volumes 2–3 and Threadkeeper to Volumes 4–6. `bible/power-system.md` gives Threadkeeper's third ability as a limited forced-connection cut, and its qualification as: *complete one consensual cut while a local bridgekeeper remains present, then help the same community repair the physical scar before the route is declared stable.* `outline/series.md` gives Volume 04 a sixteen-month path interval, which is longer than the Marches' thirteen months and is the longest in the manuscript.**

**Decision: Volume 04 reaches Threadkeeper, and it reaches it by qualification at the end of the volume, and it completes the qualification, because that is what the ladder says and because the volume's problem is the first in the series where a forced connection is genuinely threatening a path and a consensual cut is the trade's answer.** Concretely:

- **Three paths held at the close of Volume 04: Bellweather-Living, the Hearth Marches, and the Ninefold Winter valley.** Two to three is the whole of the movement and it is not a queue of disposable maps.
- **One cut, in Block 5, with a local bridgekeeper present, and the same community repairs the scar before the route is declared stable.** Not on a false witness. Not on a stranger's word. **The Volume 06 row of the ending's failure table — *a cut made on a false witness, the community's own anchor tears, contact ends, sometimes for good* — is Volume 06's and is not scheduled, foreshadowed as an event, or priced here.**
- **The cost, and it is not a limp.** Every cut leaves a visible seam in the current world and gives the affected path a lasting memory of him. **The seam in the current world is in a bridge, and that is why it is visible, and it is the first thing in a hundred and fifty chapters that a stranger can see and not explain.** **The Many-Handed stage's worsening of the limp belongs to Volume 15 and Volume 04 adds no second bodily cost.** The fever after the return is a week, because the stage is Stage 3 and the stage earns it.
- **And the thing that costs most, which is not a stage cost at all: he gives up a fourth route.** A refuge is offered, cheaply, that would open for him alone and cost the offering community nothing, and taking it would mean the valley does not have to be paid for. **He refuses it in writing, and the refusal is a document, and it is the second refusal in a document in two volumes, and Dain's is the first, and the two are in different counties and neither party knows the other exists.**

## 13. The question Chapter 151 opens on

**Not the valley. Not the frozen winter. Not the six names.**

**The twenty-fifth of Hearthmonth, YR 313, a Saturday, a market day, the fourth hour, the day after the volume ends — and a man who was paid in ninety-six bushels of salt comes up two flights of a stair in Weir Street and reads a fair copy off a board under the bench, because anybody coming up two flights may read it, and asks for it to be copied, and nobody has to be asked.**

**And the copy goes four hundred miles to a bench of nine that has been asked four times and has said yes four times and is not a bench, and asks it a fifth question, and the answer is no, and the reason is in the fair copy, and the person who put the fair copy where anybody coming up two flights may read it is not the man who wrote it.**

**The road that opens is made of paper and carries nobody, and the man who cannot open it is the man who built it.**
