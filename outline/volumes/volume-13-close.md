## 1. What the volume was about

**A road is kept open by being named, and naming it requires somebody local to stand up and be wrong in public, and the man who can see the whole of it is the last person who can do that.** A sheet with a name at the top of it and no sender goes out and is obeyed in nine counties for nine days, and the cost of it is a road standing down and a count that reads differently at one end and not at the other and a woman finding out in a market, four days late, from a third party. Seven roads strain one set of works. The volume's question is not which road to shut; it is that **a rule about finding people is not a rule about roads, it is a rule about roads that have people on them at the end**, and that this is discovered in Volume 13 by a person who is not the protagonist and is not argued with.

**The volume's object was an empty fourth head on a form that had been ruled and empty for four and a half years and about seven months of the volume's own span, and a room that had spent four and a half years proving it could not hold a decision and was then asked to hold a rule, and proved it again by not holding it.** The answer was nine hands and no copy, and a public meeting that decided in four minutes to write nothing at all, and one sentence held by about nine people and by nobody, and a reading of that sentence in a room with about nine people in it that nobody thanked anybody for.

## 2. The five-block audit

### Block 1 · Chapters 601–610 · The Cheapest Thing on the List · days 1831–1872

### Block 2 · Chapters 611–620 · Three Blanks and Three Marks · days 1896–1914
