## 1. What the volume was about

**A road which is to be shut for want of a name is not shut by a gate or a bar or a hurdle. It is shut by a roll, and the roll arrives in the ordinary post, and the question a county spent a year on becomes a question with a date on it in ninety days.**

That is the sentence the volume was built on, and the volume says it out loud and then spends three hundred and sixty days showing what it costs to be the man who asks the question. **The thing that is shut a road in this volume is not a person and not the Wound and not a gate. It is the second thing a piece of paper can do, which is the first thing it did, and the method is shown working once in a county the man who wrote it has never heard of: a roll of nine lines with a date on it, nailed to a board in a market street, answered out loud three times in one week by three people who do not agree with each other and of whom none of them is him, not filled in, not torn up, and then a third copy proposing a man by his trade with a name written out in full at the foot of it, and nobody read out loud and nobody asked.**

**And the volume's own real subject is four and a half years of not being asked, which is Volume 15's material and not Volume 16's invention, and the volume says so more than once and blames nobody for it.** A man of about fifty-four has kept a figure out loud every morning for about four and a half years and nobody in nine counties has ever asked him what a sheet weighs, and when he is asked something else he gives that instead, and the something else turns out to be the whole of it. A roadkeeper of about fifty-two has asked that office for nine years to pay him for nine hundred feet of kerb. **And a man of forty-one, who said ten words in eleven years before this volume and has been asked exactly one question in it, on the eleventh of Hearthmonth in a market street in the open and in his own trade, spoke three times afterwards without being asked and said sixty-five words in three scenes: thirty-four on a shoulder on the fourth of Thawmonth to one person, fifteen on a shoulder in Mudmonth, and sixteen on a shoulder in Longlight, and each time he put the spade back in and nobody has asked him anything since.** **All three of those are costs of a habit and not of a fault, and the volume states that in its own words more than once, and it does not cure any of them.**

**And the answer the volume gives is negative, local and has no head. Nine people can hold nine things by saying a number out loud twice at the fourth hour on the five mornings a week that a room over a weigh-house is shut behind its own door, and he is not one of the two, and it holds for eleven mornings without him, and about nine people along eleven miles of made road find out that the gate at the top of a stair about nine hundred yards long came off its hinges when a carter reads a printed sheet out in a room on a Sunday twelve days after it happened. A closed path is not destroyed. Two hundred and fifty-two mornings of practice are written on the back leaf of a day-book in one woman's hand and on no other paper in nine counties. A roll of nine lines has stood on a board for a hundred and forty-three days with three copies of it and nine empty lines on each. And the last line of the volume's narrative is a room and a floor and a lamp and a road that somebody else is holding.**

## 5. The one System panel, and where it was spent

**EXACTLY ONE SYSTEM PANEL IS SPENT IN VOLUME 16 AND IT IS AT `chapter-0776.md`, day 2783, the eighteenth of Longlight, and it is seventeen words.** It stands alone as its own paragraph, it is not spoken aloud, nobody on that floor heard it, he has never told anybody what it was, and it carries no number:

**Blocks 0001, 0002, 0004 and 0005 printed no panel and the volume's cap at `outline/volume-16.md` §4 and §8 is one, and this record counted the string across all fifty files and it stands once and once only.** It is not spent on the reversal, because the reversal is learned off a road and off a person at a shut door in Block 0003; **it is spent on the fact that a practice is said by two and the rest of the room is not holding it, and that the man who began it has been in the room for about four and a half years and has never been one of the two.** The panel is technically true and it conceals the moral consequence, which is that what he needs is other people's mouths and therefore other people's consent, and the volume pays that in two hundred and fifty-two mornings of a number said twice by people who are not him.
