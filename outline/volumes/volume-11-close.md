## 1. What the volume was about

**It is about a line cut into a piece of wood that nobody ever wrote a word under, and about the fact that a room can be handed an empty line and spend five months and nine counties and about four hundred sheets a month refusing to fill it.**

It is a public inquiry sitting in a hired room over a saddler's in a market town of about two thousand people, shut on Sundays, with a chairman whose chain of office is a badge and a clerk of about twenty-two and a copyist of about twenty-six with a knife in her hand and a man of about thirty-four with a sheet and a man of about fifty-three with a barrow and a woman of about thirty-one with a pen two hundred miles off who keeps a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane. **The Crown's form has four heads cut into it: a place, a figure of people, a trade, and a fourth that is ruled and empty. Nobody knows who cut it. It is empty on every return that has come back from nine counties for five months, one county cut its head off clean with a knife and took a quarter inch of paper with it, and a copyist in another county has said out loud that it ought not to be there, has been right, and has been told twice that it is not hers to cut.**

**And what the volume did is refuse, in fifty chapters, to do the one thing every one of those objects was built for.** It did not fill a head. It did not name the four roads that are not roads. It did not restore a city. It did not conduct a crossing and did not schedule one. It did not resolve the three words for one lane. It did not cut the twenty-second notch and did not send anybody to that coast. It did not reopen the shut road four hundred miles off. **And it did something else, which is the volume's actual event and the reason the outline's own summary of it is wrong: it published a record that is true, with a man's own name at the top of it, and the name is the man of thirty-two with a limp, and the volume is careful throughout to say that his being the reason a thing went wrong and his being the reason a record is worth believing are two different credits that are not added together.**

**The last page is a Saturday and a market day and a man in a market who is not upstairs.** Day 1650, the twenty-fifth of Mudmonth YR 320, a Saturday and a market day. He came up the lane at about the third hour because he had decided before the second hour that he would come up the lane, and there was a market in the street, and he was in it, and a man of about twenty-nine with a handcart knew him from a street and said the name out loud twice. **Nobody in that room said anything about where he was and nobody sent for him, and the man of about fifty-eight refused out loud to say what happens to him, on the grounds that a room that says what happens to a man is a room that has started keeping a book of people.** About four hundred people in that town can say where he sleeps and it is a figure of persons and it is not a figure of anybody in the room.

**What the volume asked and did not answer.** The series row's question is *if the First Witness was designed to prevent one-road control, why does it still recognise a custodian*, and the volume comes within one sentence of it in Chapter 0523 and does not say it. The plan's own next-volume question is about a path that offers material prosperity, durable peace, and a warning that peace bought by enforced agreement is not freedom, and **no path is scheduled, no orchard is mentioned, and the volume close does not approach it**, because that question belongs to Volume 12's own first prompt. What the volume actually leaves open is a half leaf. It went down a made road to the landward end of a coast four days off, carried by a man who was not sent and is not nobody-sent, and it came back on the sixth of Mudmonth in a bundle with nine returns, and it went on a table in the room over the saddler's, and it is face down, and it has been face down for nineteen days, and **nobody in that room has turned it over — including the copyist, who has a knife, and the clerk, whose job is to enter things, and the man of about fifty-eight, who put a name in the back of a book of carts this volume and then would not read it out. §10.**

**And the deepest change in the volume is a want, and there are two of them and neither is the protagonist's.** A woman of about thirty-one with a pen has kept a room with a door on a lane for four and a half years and has asked twice about a line cut into wood that is not hers to cut, and was told twice that it is not. A woman of about twenty-six has ruled the fourth line four thousand times and has said once, in another county, that it ought not to be there, and has decided that being right is not a reason to say a thing twice. **Neither is thanked. Nobody in this volume is thanked, and the volume does not notice, and that is the volume's cost and its habit at once — §9.**

**Did a volume with a piece of paper in it hold?** It is the first volume running with a document as its chassis rather than a song, a bar or a bench-board, and what held it was a knife, a straightedge, a sixteenth of an inch, a quarter inch of paper taken away with a head, and a line ruled in a different ink in a different county. **The paper's unit is a head and a line; the room's unit is about nine people; and the two are added nowhere in fifty chapters. The volume is careful never to put a main-world month name or a main-world weekday inside a room over a weigh-house two hundred miles off, and the only two chapters that fall on a Sunday are in the market town and not in that room at all — §2.5.**
