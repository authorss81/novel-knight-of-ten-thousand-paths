## 1. Volume identity, and the question this volume is built on

**`outline/series.md`'s Volume 10 central pressure is a road, and the close of Volume 09 handed on a decision, and the two are the same object seen from the other end.**

The series says: the Morrow Coast's living tide road offers a model of voluntary inter-path trade, while the Crown tries to claim it as a royal road. The close says: **nine people met eight times and then agreed that there would be no ninth meeting, and a thing nobody has to answer to cannot be got at, and a name in a mouth is not a record and is still a handle.**

Put them together and the volume is this: **a road that somebody has to answer for.** The last volume found nine kinds of protection and every one of them was a way of not having a name on a thing. This volume finds a kind of connection that is the first in the book which *works* — a road across a foreshore that carries nine hundred people, eleven houses, salt, glass, grain and preserved fruit twice a day, and belongs to nobody, and every one of its users can walk off it — **and the first thing anybody does with a road that works is ask for somebody to be responsible for it.**

**What Volume 09 asked and answered:** *a name that nobody has to answer to is the only kind of record that cannot be got at.* **What Volume 10 asks and has to earn:** ***that protection and a working road cannot both be had, that a road which nobody can be found about will be taken by the first office that puts a chain on it, and that the honest answer is not a keeper and not no keeper but something neither volume has tried, and the volume has to find it out of tide work and not out of a document.***

**And the ninth word is a road, and the last volume's only volume-level invention was the name of one.** `Coldharbour` is a road nine miles long with a gate at the end of it and a woman on the far side, and it is out, and it is in four counties, and it is said by children. **This volume is about nine hundred people and nine miles of a different road and it may not send a single one of them to that gate, and the two roads are two roads and are not compared on a page.**

**What the volume is not.** It is not a volume about a court and it is not a volume about a villain in a chamber. The antagonist is not on the page in this volume at all in his own person, **and the Crown is not a villain either, it is a road office with a column and a wall and about four men, and every one of the four is doing what a road office does.** It is not a volume about a battle; the fight in it is a fight about a boundary mark on sand. And **it is not a volume in which the protagonist is the person who solves it: he asks a question nobody can answer, he is asked it twice and gives the same answer both times, he carries a request to a shore where it is refused, and the refusal is the volume's first real event.**

## 2. Central pressure

**Two parties want two different things, three of the four people in the middle are right, and the man in the middle wants a third thing that neither side has offered and is the reason it does not happen.**

- **A man of about twenty-nine at a factor's table** wants **a permanent connection.** He has eleven days of road behind him and a council of merchants on three coasts behind him and no money, and what he wants is a road that is there when the tide is in. **He is not a villain and he is not wrong, and the volume spends him in Block 2 and he is still a man of about twenty-nine at the end of it.**
- **A woman of about fifty-one who reads a tide off notches cut in driftwood** has **kept that road in use for about twenty-two years** and has never been asked to keep a day, and the request is a request to keep a day. **Her want is a want about not being asked, and it is the most expensive want in the volume, and it is the one the protagonist costs her in Block 2.**
- **A woman of about sixty-three who boils salt** wants **the road to stay exactly as it is**, which is a want nobody has ever put in a request because nobody has ever asked her. **She is the volume's fourth want and the volume's conscience and she is the one who refuses the permanent connection on her own ground and for a reason of her own, and she is not thanked for it either.**
- **A keeper of records, twenty-nine, in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane** wants **that no copy of the road be in a room she has to answer for**, which is the exact inverse of what a request for a permanent connection produces. **She is the only character asked to do a thing she is good at by people who need it, and she refuses one part of it, and the refusal is the volume's relationship work and not an aside.**
- **Aren Kest** wants **to be the keeper of the connection.** He cannot be, **and Chapter 451 is the chapter in which he says so out loud and is believed, and the reason is a different reason from last volume's and it is not that he is on lists.**

**The volume question, in its operational form:** *a road that works, belongs to nobody, and cannot be got at — how is that road kept in use for thirty years, by whom, on whose word, and what does the office that wants it do with a wall?*

**The answer the volume gives is that the road keeps itself and that the thing that has to be built is not a keeper and not a fence and not a deed, and that the only instrument found anywhere in five blocks is a set of eleven houses that are somebody's job.**

## 7. The mechanics this volume runs on

1. **A road can be a road and not a line.** The tide road has no beginning, because the tide decides which end is the beginning twice a day. **This is the volume's central object and it is not a metaphor.**
2. **A working connection produces a keeper's job whether anybody wants one or not.** The moment nine hundred people use a road, somebody has to clear the shingle, mend the posts, and decide what happens to a wagon that comes off the sand at the wrong hour. **That somebody is a person and a person can be found.**
3. **Memory and a voice are the cost, and neither is reversible by deciding to be careful.** The bleed in this volume is not a symbol. He says a sentence in an accent that is not his, in front of people who have a right to that accent, and cannot afterwards say which of two childhoods a room belongs to.
4. **A wall is a thing you can build on a road that is under water.** The office's claim is not illegal in any way anybody can name, and the sand takes the marks, and the marks stay when the water is out. **The antagonist's plan in this volume is a wall and a deed and a number of men in a yard, and it is on the page in Block 4.**
5. **Ordinary craft is where the volume's arguments live.** A wagon at slack water. A post of driftwood with notches in it. A pan of brine. A chained boy with a rod. A wall footing on wet sand.

## 9. Volume climax

**A wall on a beach, and a salt-store, and a question asked nine miles from where the wall is.**

- The Crown's office sets a footing of stone on the foreshore, which is a legal act, is done properly, and cannot be undone by anybody who owns the sand, because the sand is not owned by anybody either.
- **The climax is not the wall and not a fight. It is that a man of thirty-two with a limp is asked, in a salt-store, by people who have already agreed to a common rule, whether his road and their history can be made to be one account, and that he says no, and that the reason he says no is not a principle and is printed.**

- And the Crown attacks the roadhouses in the last week of the block, **and four of the eleven are damaged and none is lost, and the damage is repaired by eleven people in about nine days, and he is one of the eleven and not the first.**
