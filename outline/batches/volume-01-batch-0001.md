# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline

**Batch:** 0001 — "The Nineteenth of Mudmonth"
**Chapter range:** 1–10
**Volume:** 01 — The Bellweather-Living Road
**Pressure types across the batch:** action/survival 3, investigation/discovery 2, relationship 2, political 1, cost/aftermath 1, threshold event 1
**Gatehouse voice budget:** exactly two short lines in the whole batch, one at the opening in Ch 4 and one at the return in Ch 8. Nothing else. No numbers, no menus, no quest text.

**Day map, main world, Mudmonth YR 312:** Ch 1 the sixth · Ch 2 the eleventh · Ch 3 the twelfth · Ch 4 the night of the eighteenth into the nineteenth · Ch 5–7 path time, no main-world clock · Ch 8 the twenty-seventh · Ch 9 the twenty-seventh to the thirtieth · Ch 10 the thirtieth. The anniversary is the nineteenth. The fever runs three days, the twenty-seventh to the twenty-ninth, and breaks on the thirtieth, which is the day Chapter 10 opens.

**Two institutions, two names:** "The guild" in the main-world chapters — 1 and 2, and any later scene set in Merefen — means the **Merefen bridge guild**, the town's craft body of bridgewrights and road-wardens, which holds the Old Toll crossing in municipal trust and keeps the scrap roll the bell's valuation comes from. The path's institution is the **Bellweather Bridge Guild**, and it never appears in Merefen. Both are given their full name the first time either is named in a scene; the bare word "guild" is fine only after that.

## Batch goal

Give the reader the genre promise, the ordinary life it interrupts, one rule they can use, one relationship with friction, one small victory, one real cost, and the first hard evidence — then end on a decision that hands control of the evidence to someone who does not trust him.

## Starting condition

Aren Kest, thirty-two, road warden and bridge repairer in Merefen, YR 312, Mudmonth, the sixth day. The Sable is at flood stage, Sedge Reach Bridge is holding, and the cracked Bellweather toll bell he bought for two pence at the Crown clearance five days ago is still hung on the Old Toll Bridge, which is why his brother Tovan wants its bronze. The eleventh anniversary falls on the nineteenth, in a storm. No open path, no stitch, no second witness, and a public record that names him as the man who lost a city.

## Starting relationships

- Tovan: estranged, affectionate, useful, and unwilling to be sent anywhere.
- Edric Hunn: unexamined devotion, absent in person, present in every habit Aren still has.
- Mara Vey: not met.
- Merefen: trusts the road and distrusts the man.

## Batch midpoint

**Chapters 5–6.** Aren arrives in a city that is alive, mortgaged, and entirely uninterested in being rescued, and then fails at the one thing he is good at because a ferrywright refuses his method. The reader learns that the promise of the book is not a second world to explore but a society that has to put up with him.

## Batch climax

**Chapters 8–9.** The return he did not prepare for costs him nine days he was told would be three, and he comes back to an empty bell cradle, a closed road, a lost market, a brother who covered his work at a loss, and a fever that takes his voice. The bell is under a watch seal. The batch ends with the aftermath in Ch 10: he puts the only thing he brought back into a public file, along with the part of the story that implicates him.

## Required character changes

- **Aren:** begins as a man who solves things alone and pays for them himself; ends as a man who has discovered that the price of a crossing is paid by other people's labour, and who has voluntarily handed his one piece of evidence to a hostile archivist who will set the terms. He has not become humble. He has become specific about his own guilt, which is more dangerous to him than self-sacrifice was.
- **Tovan:** states his refusal. He will mend the bridge. He will not hold his brother's road, keep his brother's secret, or be the one left holding the span. His loyalty survives the batch and his availability does not.
- **Mara Vey:** goes from an archivist with a stalled claim to a party to a documented contact, on terms she set. Her flaw shows: she makes the grief useful and does not let anyone sit in it.
- **Wenna Koll:** the one person who helps Aren without being asked to, and who charges him for it.
- **Sera Quen:** refuses to be grateful and explains why refusal is more useful than gratitude. She is the volume's first real check on Aren.
- **Rell:** writes down what Aren says because a watch officer has no choice, does not believe it, and is right that it is unbelievable.

## Required power changes

- Stage 0 throughout; **Stage 1 is not reached in this batch.** The qualification needs a second crossing and a local person's informed consent to keep the road visible.
- The roadmark is permanent: a blackened line along the pale seam on Aren’s left palm. It is not a token and it is not a promise.
- Irreversible cost paid at the opening: the remembered **pitch** of one verse of Ilyra's river song. The shape of the melody and most of the words remain. He can still hum the river; he cannot land the verse where it belongs, and he notices the hole every time he tries.
- Cost paid at the return: three days of fever, and a body that somebody else has to run.
- The Old Toll sill takes its first scar. It is usable once and has been marked. It will need anchor-glass before it can carry another crossing.
- The route is nominally open. He has no anchor he may lawfully touch, no consent from Bellweather-Living's side, and no way to send or receive anything.

## Required world reveals

- The Sundering fell on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301. The Crown quotes four thousand and never the seven hundred and twenty of the villages; the Archive keeps the two figures apart.
- The bell is unclassified municipal salvage, cracked through the mouth, sold at a Crown clearance for two pence. It is a liability, not a curiosity, which is why it changed hands four times.
- An oathcraft working has four parts — anchor, bound, counterweight, witness — taught by a working that fails, not by explanation.
- A path is a society: a mortgaged bridge, a guild, toll houses, a ferry cooperative, and people with unfinished business who never stopped.
- The severance writ that reached Bellweather-Living was refused by the bridge guild and withdrawn. The counterfoil proving it survives in the guild's keeping. The register naming its signatory does not, and is in toll-house hands.
- The Crown's "no surviving claim" line is renewed on the anniversary, and a claim entered after the renewal is weaker than one entered before it.
- A private-keeper clause is being applied to Aren in provincial correspondence he has not yet read carefully. Nobody can tell him what a keeper is.

## Open questions after the batch

1. Who wrote the seizure order before the anniversary, and how did the marshal's office know the bell could answer?
2. What exactly did the Bellweather bell remember on the night it refused a severance writ?
3. Where is the register that names the signatory, and what will the toll houses want in exchange for it?
4. Why does the word "keeper" fit Aren in an official document?
5. What did the Gatehouse's warning mean by "three days," and how is it nine?
6. Bellweather-Living has a hamlet on a drowned channel, and the name is familiar. Nobody explains it in this volume.

## The days the cards do not cover

There are three stated cuts in the batch, and each one is deliberate. The writer may spend a paragraph in them and must not put a new fact in them.

- **The sixth to the eleventh, between Ch 1 and Ch 2.** Five days of flood-stage work. Nothing turns: the bell stays where it is, the hold Aren signs in Ch 1 runs to the guild's next sitting on the twenty-fourth, and the notice dated the eleventh is a formality the clerk expects to lose. This is where a reader can be told that the town has started to notice the bell rather than the road. Note that the twenty-fourth is the paper that would have handled this bell in the ordinary way — no scene is required at the sitting, and it matters only later, as one more document the watch's sealed order overrides.
- **The twelfth to the eighteenth, between Ch 3 and Ch 4.** Six days of waiting. Aren has made a promise he has already decided to break, the water is still rising, and the anniversary is coming. Nothing arrives. No second visitor, no new document, no arrest, and no further bell sound. Use it for the physical work of waiting — a man who cannot sleep on a deadline, chalk on a pier, water under a door — and let the promise be the whole content of the interval.
- **Path time, Ch 5–7.** The main-world clock does not run for Aren and must not be narrated as though it does. The reader is told once, in Ch 8, that it moved without him.

No chapter may open with a day the day map does not contain. If a scene seems to need a day between two cards, it belongs to the cut above.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** YR 312, Mudmonth, sixth day, late afternoon. Sedge Reach Bridge, Merefen, at flood stage and rising; grey light, rain going sideways off the west channel.

**Immediate goal:** Get the Sedge Reach scour repair through one more flood without losing the town the fourth-day market, and without the toll-holder closing the road on him.

**Resistance:** A ferryman at the abutment says the river is eleven fingers and rising, and it is, by the measure his trade uses. The surveyor's gauge board on the Sedge pier is three days old, a written table nailed to a post, and it reads lower than the man with the boat, because a written table changes slower than the river. Aren believes the ferryman, because Aren trusts what he can read in the water, and that is the flaw this book is going to charge him for twice. The ferry company wants the span declared unsafe, so the carts go by water and the carriage is theirs; the millers want a boat pass they will not pay for; the market wants the road open and is the only party at the meeting with no vote. Aren is one repairer with one crew and a Tuesday barge contract he will lose if the water keeps climbing.

**Information revealed:** His ordinary life, his actual competence, and what it is worth: a shilling a day, a chalk stub, a notebook of failures, a rented room above a cooper. The cracked bell is still on the Old Toll Bridge and the Merefen bridge guild wants it off the books. He bought it five days ago for two pence and told everyone it was scrap, which is true and not the reason. The anniversary is the nineteenth and the storm is forecast for that night. Ordinary, friendly people tell him the fever took Bellweather and that he lost the bridge, in that order, meaning no harm. He does not correct them. In a short, physical memory, not a scene: his debrief, the word *fever* in his own mouth, and the field notes folded in the breast pocket of a coat he kept.

**Action or decision:** He keeps the road open another day, marks the piers in chalk at flood stage, and signs a hold on the bell in his own name — good until the **Merefen bridge guild's next sitting, the twenty-fourth** — with his tool-chest as surety, because the alternative is losing the bell before he has finished with it. The twenty-fourth is a date he expects to be present for.

**Emotional change:** Competence, from the inside, is indistinguishable from authority, and he does not notice the difference today. He ends the chapter having done his job and having made a private decision that four other people will have to live with.

**Power or resource change:** Journeyman Bind on a batten, nothing more. One day's pay and a spent chalk stub. Six marks of exposure he cannot cover, and a hold on the bell that is good paper for eighteen more days.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Sedge Reach is four timber bays. He was twenty-one at the Sundering, the youngest captain in the Guard, commissioned by brevet. The Crown's figure is four thousand for the city alone; the seven villages are never folded in by him or by anyone who has read the Archive.

**Ending type:** decision.

**Next-chapter pull:** The bell comes down on the eleventh, when the guild's clerk comes for it, and the only man in Merefen who can get it off that cradle is the man who has just put his own name on it.

### Chapter 0002

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** Mudmonth, eleventh day, early morning. The Old Toll Bridge upstream, low light under the arches, a ferry working beneath it.

**Immediate goal:** Get the bell off the abutment and into Tovan's yard before the clerk of the **Merefen bridge guild** arrives, so the problem is somebody else's by noon.

**Resistance:** The bell is grown into its cradle and the split lip rings against the yoke every time it moves. Wenna Koll has eleven people stranded on the wrong bank, because the landward road that feeds her slip is the one everybody is arguing about, and she wants the bridge kept rather than cleared. Tovan is there with three journeymen and the offer is honest: the bronze is worth six marks and the crack is worth the difference, and the guild's roll agrees, and the clerk is already on his way. Tovan is right about the metal. He is also right about the brother who left for the Guard and returned a stranger, and right that nobody asked him to hold a bridge.

**Information revealed:** The Old Toll crossing is out of cart use for two generations and is kept because the bell is on it. Wenna's want is plain and unrelated to Aren: her customers and freight reach the water over the raised road, so a shut bridge is a lost day for her, and she resents anyone who shuts one. The Merefen bridge guild's removal notice is **dated today, the eleventh** — eight days before the anniversary, and before any storm — and the clerk carrying it is already on the road, so no one in the room remarks on the date except Aren, who does not yet know what to make of it. Plant it here and pay it off in Chapter 8. The local custom is that the ferrymen ring for the drowned and the town does not decide out loud whether the drowned are still owed that. On the second lift the bell gives one dull note by itself, from the split and not the mouth, with nobody's hand on it. Everyone hears it. Everyone explains it.

**Action or decision:** He refuses the metal and signs the guild's bond for six marks instead, with his tool-chest as surety, and the reason is the batch's first real piece of character work rather than stubbornness. The six marks would buy the bronze and nothing else: the notice exists whatever the metal is worth, and a bell in a yard is a bell somebody else owns. The bond is a different kind of paper. It puts the bell in his name in front of the guild, and it gives the clerk a man to write the delay against instead of an argument, so the removal stands off against a hold that is already good until the twenty-fourth. He chooses the document that makes him responsible over the money that would make him free, because being answerable is the last thing he has left that nobody gave him. He does not say the reason out loud, then or later, and that omission is one of the two the batch owns. He puts the bell back on its cradle and goes to work the sill on the near abutment, which is his own project and the only thing he has touched on this bridge in two years.

**Emotional change:** Family friction stops being banter. Tovan says the true thing — *you were the one they asked to hold it* — and Aren has no answer that is not either a lie or an apology he has refused to make for eleven years.

**Power or resource change:** A signed six-mark bond he cannot discharge. A day lost. A cut-stone sill chalked and packed by hand, and a private note in the notebook that nobody else will ever read.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The bell's clearance entry reads *unclassified municipal salvage, Bellweather, crack through the mouth*. It is chest-high, green-black bronze, split from lip to shoulder. It is not rare, and its owner is laughed at.

**Ending type:** cost, with an unexplained sound inside it.

**Next-chapter pull:** The bell has rung once with nobody's hand on it, and by noon the whole town has agreed on who is answerable for it: him. And a woman at the salt loft has been asking the ferrymen which repairer signed the sill.

### Chapter 0003

**POV:** Mara Vey.

**Location/time:** Mudmonth, twelfth day, afternoon. The two flood-safe rooms over the Merefen salt merchant, with the river audible through the floor and the salt-dust in everything.

**Immediate goal:** Get the Bellweather bell entered in the Archive's salvage roll as a claim exhibit, with a named anchor and a sworn tradesman's account, before the province renews its line on the anniversary — because a claim entered before the renewal is dated before it, and everything entered after is weaker for a year.

**Resistance:** The tradesman she needs is Aren Kest, the captain the official account says lost Bellweather. The Archive's rule will not let her record an anchor on a clerk's word, and the registrar who holds the Crown's own Bellweather entries has quoted a fee she has already paid once. The salvaged bell is the only surviving object she can put in front of a magistrate. And the man will not work for the sake of the record: he wants the bell rung.

**Information revealed:** Her want, stated without decoration: the names and last wishes of the erased, and a second erasure made legally permanent. The Archive's practice of keeping one set of records outside Crown custody, which is why the office is useful and why it is watched. **Ferris Oat's brine weights** — thirty-one days of Saltbrim brine weights with the arithmetic intact, the single most useful witness document the office holds, because a weight cannot be argued with about quantity. The parish list that splits four thousand back into two hundred at Alder Cross. The Crown's four thousand and the Archive's refusal to print a combined total. And once, coldly, in writing: there is a **Hanna Vey, 9, of Hushwater** in the parish list, the same ordinary river-valley surname as her own. She notes the coincidence, notes that it is nothing, and never writes about it again.

**Action or decision:** She offers a clean exchange and makes it a condition: her custody entry, the claim number, the brine-weight extract, and a sworn account of what the bell is attached to — in return for his promise that the bell will not be rung. She does not qualify it. She wants the promise without a date on it, because a date would tell her he has already chosen a night, and a man who has already chosen a night is a man who will be busy on it. He agrees to the promise, meaning the whole of it, and hears how much of it he has just given away. He does not tell her the bell rings when nobody rings it, because he has already decided what the anniversary storm is for, and because he would rather lose her record than lose the road. Chapter 4 is therefore a broken promise and not a technicality, and the prose must let the reader hold both halves of that without being told which one Aren is doing.

**Emotional change:** She sees the shape of the man she is dealing with and names it to herself precisely: he is not lying to her yet, and he has already chosen what she is allowed to know. Her professional regard goes up and her trust does not.

**Power or resource change:** A claim number, a custody entry, a copied extract of the brine weights, and one written lie of omission that the writer should let the reader notice and Aren not. The omission is to her, and it has two parts: he signs a promise with no date on it, and he does not tell her there is a night in mind. There is a second omission in the batch, made to his brother in Chapter 2, which he has not disclosed to anybody either.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** This main-world office is the Bellweather Claims Archive. It is not the living archive inside Bellweather-Living, which is run by Hollis Dree and refuses to surrender copies. No kinship between Hanna Vey and Mara Vey, ever, in any scene.

**Ending type:** decision, with a lie pending.

**Next-chapter pull:** Seven days to the anniversary, one undated promise standing between them, and a storm forecast for the nineteenth.

### Chapter 0004

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** The night of the eighteenth into the nineteenth of Mudmonth, the ninth hour. The Old Toll Bridge in the anniversary storm, working lanterns, the ferrymen out on the water for the drowned.

**Immediate goal:** Ring the bell once, alone, and hear what it answers with.

**Resistance:** The river is loud enough to cover the sound and busy enough that nobody is watching one man in particular, which is exactly the opportunity. His own promise sits in his teeth, undated, which is the reason it is unbearable and not the reason it is breakable. The bell will not lift clean; the cradle is grown in and the yoke is rusted to the headstock. The bell is nine stone of split green bronze, and he is not lifting the bell — he is lifting the headstock under it with a bar through the loop, which is why every handhold is a decision about where nine stone goes if he drops it, and why the crack is the part he cannot afford to have face up.

**Information revealed:** The rule, in action and not in speech: a road needs a hinge and a living anchor and someone who can name the place. He can name Bellweather, and the bell's own split is the hinge — a bell that was rung to refuse an order and was damaged doing it. Oathlight in the crack, dull red, and the sound arriving through the hand before it arrives through the ear.

**Gatehouse voice:** One line, in his own perception, plain and technically true and concealing everything: *A road needs a living hand to open it.*

**Action or decision:** He rings it. The road opens as a fold in the rain — weather, not light — and the verse of his mother's river song will not land on its note afterward. The shape of the melody and most of the words are still there.

**Emotional change:** The first pure euphoria in the book, and it is on a bridge, in the rain, alone, working. He is thirty-two and for about four seconds he is the man who is going to fix it.

**Power or resource change:** A permanent black roadmark along the pale seam of his left palm. The remembered pitch of one verse. Nothing else has changed yet, and he does not know that the cost of the opening is not the largest one.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** No path is created from nothing. This one was already living, and it is about eleven years further along in its own weather.

**Ending type:** discovery, and a threshold.

**Next-chapter pull:** There is a city on the other side, and it is working.

### Chapter 0005

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** Path time. A cold morning with the smell of hot glass and river mud, at the top of the north-bank steps of a great arched bridge, in a city that should not exist.

**Immediate goal:** Get off the steps, out of the wet, and out of the hands of whoever is asking him questions.

**Resistance:** He has no papers, no trade, and the wrong accent, which is the same accent. A toll-collector wants a toll off him for coming up the steps he did not cross. Children ask him what he is here for. The bridge is standing, working, and load-bearing somebody's laundry. And when he says the name — *Bellweather* — the reaction is not joy. It is fear, and the specific question behind the fear: is the fever back, and did it come from below again?

**Information revealed:** The city is alive, occupied with its own business, and mortgaded to its toll houses for a century, which is why every public decision in it is expensive. The foundry fires are lit. The river is eleven fingers and rising, said by a ferryman and corrected by a surveyor standing next to him, exactly as in the main world. The people here have a night in their memory that the main world's record says killed them, and they have never been told they were saved.

**Action or decision:** He gives his name and the name of the city, and he does not soften it. Then, before anyone asks, he says what he is not here for: he is not here to fetch anyone, and nobody is owed to him. It buys him nothing and it is the first true thing he says in the path.

**Emotional change:** The elapse turns. He came for survivors and the first thing he wants from these people is their story, and he can hear himself doing it. The shame arrives before the understanding does.

**Power or resource change:** No magic, no coin, no authority, wet to the bone, and a working knowledge of the gate, the **Bellweather Bridge Guild's** hall, and one four-bay timber bridge that he is reading the way he reads Sedge Reach at home — which is the only reason he recognises the arch at all. The route is open and he has no idea how to close it.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Nobody asks him for a toll twice, and nobody is a version of a main-world victim. These are their own people with their own dead. The eleven fingers said by the ferryman and corrected by the surveyor in this chapter are the exact argument Aren lost in Chapter 1, heard from the other side of the world: folk measure tells you what a boatman feels, the written table tells you what the river has done. He learns it here from strangers and will still get it wrong at home in Block 4.

**Ending type:** discovery, and a reversal of the promise.

**Next-chapter pull:** The guild sends a ferrywright to fetch him, and the ferrywright has already decided what he is for.

### Chapter 0006

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** Path time, midday. The ferry slips, a hull on the blocks, hot pitch and cold water, half the crew watching a stranger.

**Immediate goal:** Earn the right to stay by being useful, without being given anything to command.

**Resistance:** The ferrywright is **Sera Quen**, and she says out loud that she will not be grateful to him, because a debt owed to a grateful stranger is not a debt, it is a leash. The **Bellweather Bridge Guild** will not take a foreign command and tells him so in front of the crew. He tries his own method — hold the frame, bind the span, get the job done in an afternoon — and Quen refuses it, not because it is wrong but because the bound is too broad and the anchor is the wrong object: his working would put the force into the hull's own planks. He pushes anyway, because he is better than the room, and the working fails the way workings fail: force transfers into the anchor, a plank splits, and a young hull-hand opens his hand on the iron.

**Information revealed:** Oathcraft taught by paying for it. Anchor, bound, counterweight, witness, arrived at through an argument about a hull and not through any lecture. The path's word for the people who maintain their crossing is **steward**, and a steward maintains. A steward is not a keeper, and the difference is the first argument of the volume.

**Action or decision:** He does it her way: a narrow bound, a used anchor-stone, his own time and his cut hand as the counterweight, and the hull-hand as the witness because a working with no witness is a working somebody can deny. The plank holds. He carries back the motion, not the working, and cannot repeat it anywhere else without local glass, a proper anchor, and someone to teach him again.

**Emotional change:** The first time in the book he is not the best person in the room, and it is a relief followed immediately by the recognition that being relieved is not the same as being welcome.

**Power or resource change:** A cut hand that will stiffen. The practice of a narrow Mend, physically and partly learned, unusable as a weapon or a shortcut. A social debt to the guild bench, which is heavier than the iron.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Local custodians and stewards do the work; Aren can translate, witness, and hold still. He is never the mechanism, and the batch must show this at least twice.

**Ending type:** first small victory, paid for in blood and in standing.

**Next-chapter pull:** The bench will answer one question. It will want something other than money, and it will want it before it answers.

### Chapter 0007

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** Path time, evening. The **Bellweather Bridge Guild** hall, the bench, and the annual oath-stone they keep cold-water on until the renewal. Hushwater's crossing book is on the table, in a living hand, with different names in it.

**Immediate goal:** Get one answer: was this city ordered to be severed, and by whom?

**Resistance:** The bench's terms are not secrecy and are not money. They are refusal, theirs, carried back in his hand to whoever sends the next order. The toll-house half of the city wants the northern trade reopened and is using him as its argument in the room; the guild wants the crossing kept open and short of hands; and the newest fact is that the outer arch ring has a crack that moves, and the honest repair is a job the debt forbids. They will answer a question only for a promise to return, and a promise to return is a promise that costs him a season of his body.

**Information revealed:** Their night. A writ arrived on the nineteenth of Mudmonth, YR 301, from the Crown; the guild refused it on the deck of the arch; a severance crew came, waited out a day and a half, and withdrew. The guild kept the **counterfoil** — the stub proving what was sent — and lost everything else. The signature block is not on the stub. The register that names who signed it is kept by the toll houses, who are not the guild and who are currently trying to reopen the northern trade with a foreign stranger's help. The oath and the hinge are the same event: the refusal was made on the deck at night, and the guild's promise to hold the crossing was renewed in the same hour, on the stone that is in the room with cold water on it. Hushwater Reach is on the crossing book's second page, and the cooperative does not count souls — it counts ferry hands and hearths, which is why nobody can put a number on the hamlet and why the number, whatever it is, is near sixty. Do not make it eighty, and do not let a character notice a coincidence.

**Action or decision:** He agrees to carry the refusal and to bring back the register page, and he volunteers for the thaw work without being asked, because that is what he is for and because volunteering is how he keeps control. He signs nothing and takes nothing but the stub, and he knows as he does it that usefulness is going to be used.

**Emotional change:** Investigator to participant in one evening. The first taste of being trusted by people who have no reason to trust him, and the specific discomfort of a man who is being given work he would have taken anyway.

**Power or resource change:** A counterfoil in his coat: dated, sealed, and unsigned on the stub. A debt and a promise. A route that will cost him the better part of a season to keep.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The severance writ is Crown-issued and uses emergency-closure language. It does not mention the Gatehouse, the First Witness, or any mechanism that belongs to a later volume.

**Ending type:** decision, with a bill attached.

**Next-chapter pull:** He has no way home, and the only iron in this city with his work in it is the pin he set in the plank he just repaired.

### Chapter 0008

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** The twenty-seventh of Mudmonth, midday, clear and cold after the storm. The Old Toll Bridge, in the ordinary market-town light of a working Tuesday.

**Immediate goal:** Get off the bridge, get warm, and work out what has happened while he was gone.

**Resistance:** The fever starts in his chest before he has taken ten steps. The cradle is empty, with a clean rectangle in the moss where nine days of green bronze stood. The watch is on the bridge and has been for some time. Tovan has the Sedge Reach deck, a lost barge contract, and a bill. The road is closed and the fourth-day market is gone. And Rell has a sealed order, a fever he believes in, and a duty to write down whatever a witness says to him.

**Information revealed:** Nine days. The Gatehouse's warning said three. He tries to hum his mother's river song and the verse will not land on its note. The town has spent nine days without its repairer, and Rell's order was written and sealed before the anniversary — before the storm, before the crossing, before there was anything to seize. A Crown document calls him the road-keeper of Merefen, holding the Bellweather hinge, and he has no idea what a keeper is.

**Gatehouse voice:** One line, at the return, plain and concealing: *What you carry back will not remain only yours.*

**Action or decision:** Feverish, on his feet, in front of the watch and forty witnesses on a market morning, he says the whole thing: he was at Bellweather, the bridge is standing, and he stood on it. Rell must write it down because a statement to a watch officer is a legal act. Rell does not believe a word of it and writes it down anyway.

**Emotional change:** He has traded a private certainty for a public record he does not control, and he cannot tell, standing there shaking, whether that was courage or panic.

**Power or resource change:** Stage 0 with a roadmark. Three days of fever, from the twenty-seventh to the twenty-ninth, and it breaks on the thirtieth — the same three days the Gatehouse's drift cost him and nothing like enough. A crossed anchor scar on the Old Toll sill. A witness statement in a watch book that will be read upward.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Rell believes the bell caused the fever recorded after Bellweather. He is not secretly cruel, and his unlawful seizure stays in his record for the rest of the series whatever the Crown later says about procedure.

**Ending type:** cost, and a reversal of the victory.

**Next-chapter pull:** He cannot get up the steps. The fever decides the three days it is going to take, and everything he promised to protect is already in someone else's storehouse.

### Chapter 0009

**POV:** Tovan.

**Location/time:** The twenty-seventh to the thirtieth of Mudmonth. The rooms above the cooper on Weir Street, the yard, and the Sedge Reach deck in the rain.

**Immediate goal:** Finish the scour repair his brother started, feed the crew, and get three clients to stop asking where Aren is.

**Resistance:** The work is a fortnight's job compressed into the three days his brother cannot stand up, and Tovan has to absorb the part his brother would have known cold. The fever is ugly, and nobody is paying for a sick man's week. The clients want a date, not an explanation. The bridge bond is six marks he does not have. And his brother wakes up at odd hours and talks about a city with a bridge on it, and Tovan has to decide what to do with a statement like that.

**Information revealed:** What it actually costs to be the one who stays: nine days of cover work, a lost contract, a crew paid out of Tovan's own margin, and a set of people in Merefen who decide for themselves that the Kest brothers are the sort who are not told. The estrangement has a shape — Tovan was seventeen when Aren left for the Guard and did not write for two years, and neither of them has ever said so out loud. And the refusal, in Tovan's own voice, with no appeal in it: he will mend the bridge, he will not hold the road, he will not keep the secret, and he will not be the man who is holding the span when the span goes.

**Action or decision:** He finishes the repair at a loss, tells the clients the truth in the fewest words available, and refuses his brother the one thing his brother is going to ask for: a witness, a cover, and a place to come back to. He gives him a room, water, and a physician's fee he resents.

**Emotional change:** Tovan's anger is clarified into a shape neither brother can deny. He is not asking for a career in his brother's road. He is asking not to be expendable in it.

**Power or resource change:** The Sedge Reach repair is done and unpaid-for in the way that matters: the crew is a week out. Tovan now knows something enormous and has decided what to do with it, which is to tell almost no one.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Tovan refuses to call Aren "sir" in private, and switches to "sir" deliberately when he means to make a point. This chapter is the batch's recovery anchor and the volume's relationship beat; the argument continues unresolved for forty chapters.

**Ending type:** cost, and a relationship beat completed.

**Next-chapter pull:** On the thirtieth the fever breaks, the world has moved, the bell is under a seal, and the Archive has been told about a counterfoil.

### Chapter 0010

**POV:** Aren.

**Location/time:** The thirtieth of Mudmonth, the day the fever breaks, weak and upright and not yet sure of his own legs. The salt-loft rooms, then the Old Toll Bridge.

**Immediate goal:** Get the counterfoil into the Archive's custody as a filed exhibit with a claim number and a second witness, before the province's renewal line is re-issued on a schedule he cannot influence.

**Resistance:** Mara's terms are the whole scene. She will take it only as a filed exhibit with a chain of custody, a date, and a second witness, and she will not file a crossing claim on his word. Lysa Fenn is there in her own right, because a sealed Council order has to be decided by somebody in Merefen, and because she is the Merefen magistrate who took his salvage filing eleven years ago, after the Sundering. She does not say what the filing covered, and neither does anyone else in the room. That filing is a different matter from the bell, and Lysa is careful in front of Mara to say so, because the first thing she was taught is that two documents about the same man are not the same document. The cradle's seal is a Crown seal on a merchant's bell. The town ledger in front of them adds up to a number, and the number is what his nine days cost.

**Information revealed:** Aren's own written account, filed as part of the exhibit, in which he records that he was given a partial order, that he did not reach the inner city, that for three years he repeated the fever account in debriefs, and that he kept his field notes in a coat instead of handing them over. It also names the two people he told nothing. To Mara: the promise carries no date on it, and there is a night in mind she was not told about. To his brother: the six marks were turned into a bond because being answerable was the last thing left to him. A witness who has only accused others is worth nothing on a page, and the second omission is what makes the first one real. The Archive's rule of one set of records outside Crown custody, invoked on a document that is technically Crown salvage. And a provincial letter that uses the title *road-keeper* and speaks of the *Bellweather hinge* as things already in his charge, referring to an office's authority nobody in the room can find.

**Action or decision:** He hands it over. He makes no condition, keeps no copy, and signs the whole account including the parts that are his own fault, because a witness who has only accused others is worth nothing on a page. He then goes and looks at the empty cradle on the Old Toll Bridge, and does not touch the seal.

**Emotional change:** A completed beat. He has handed control of the only thing he brought back to a woman who does not trust him, and the road he has been holding alone for eleven years now belongs to somebody else's filing system, which is either the beginning of his education or the end of his usefulness, and he cannot tell which.

**Power or resource change:** Stage 0, marked, unqualified. One carryover: the motion of a narrow Mend, unusable without local glass, a proper anchor, and a teacher. No stitch prepared for next time — the sill is scarred, the bell is sealed, and the next crossing has to be paid for in glass and money before it can be paid for in time.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The Archive never prints a combined total: the city's four thousand and the villages' seven hundred and twenty stay on separate pages, and nobody adds them in dialogue. Ferris Oat's brine weights are **held and cited** in this batch — the extract Mara trades in Chapter 3, and a weight cannot be argued with about quantity — but they are not filed as an exhibit here. The brine weights are what the Archive's named roll of the dead is built out of in Chapter 892, and the batch must leave them a cited authority rather than a spent payoff. Nobody in this batch learns why Aren can cross, what a keeper is, or what the First Witness is.

**Ending type:** decision, with an earned hook into Block 2.

**Next-chapter pull:** A lawyer is coming from the province to argue about a bell, a magistrate has to decide whether a sealed order is law in Merefen, the first surviving Hushwater claimant has walked into the Archive to be told he is wrong, and the Old Toll sill needs anchor-glass before it can carry another road.
