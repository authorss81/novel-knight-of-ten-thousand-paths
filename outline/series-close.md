## 1. What the eighteen years were about, in the plainest terms the book itself uses

**A man is disgraced and goes into a place that keeps going when he leaves, and the whole book is the discovery that this is not a thing he is allowed to own.** He is a knight because he can cross between worlds other people live in. The worlds are not simulations and they do not reset. Everything he does in them costs somebody on the other side something permanent, and the book's question, asked in Year 01 and not answered until Year 18, is whether a man who can see the cost more clearly than anyone else is the person entitled to pay it.

**THE ANSWER THE BOOK GIVES IS NOT A KEEPER. It is that a road can be owed to people who are not in the room, and that the burden of showing that one ought to be shut belongs to the person proposing that it does.** He spends the last year of it learning how to leave a road in hands that are not his, and the last page of the book is a toll bridge at dawn opened by three other people while he stands four paces off with a tool bag.

**This is not a victory and this is not a defeat, and a record that calls it either is a record about a different book.**
