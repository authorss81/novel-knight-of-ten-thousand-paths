## 1. Volume identity, and the question this volume is built on

**`outline/series.md`'s Volume 5 cell and `outline/volume-04.md` §11 hand on the same question, word for word, and this outline says so rather than reconciling a difference that is not there.** The instruction for the Volume 04 close said the two differed and required a reconciliation. They do not differ. The series' next-volume cell for Volume 5 is *Can a path be alive enough to reject him, and what happens when the main world refuses to recognize that refusal?* and `outline/volume-04.md` §11 closes on *can a path be alive enough to refuse him, and what happens when the main world refuses to recognise that refusal?* **The difference between the two sources is one of framing and not of content.** The series cell gives the question in the abstract. **§11 gives the question and the concrete hand-off that produced it**, and §11 is what this volume is built on, because a volume needs a thing on a table and not only a proposition.

**So: this volume is a path city that has chosen to be unreachable, and its council sends a message only through a child's roadmark, and nobody in it will explain what the roadmark is, and a man who has spent four months being recognised as a carrier in a valley is going to arrive at a city where recognition is the trade, and a city that has been keeping a dawn for longer than a valley has kept a winter has never told anybody what it costs.**

**And the live question is the series' own: *can a path be alive enough to refuse him, and what happens when the main world refuses to recognise that refusal?***

**What Volume 01 asked** and answered *nobody, and here is the sheet with two names on it.* **Volume 02** asked what a record owes the person it describes and answered *a promise is only as wide as the people who have agreed to it, and a hall is not a person.* **Volume 03** asked how you keep a promise that has been kept for a hundred and forty years and is killing everybody who keeps it, and answered *with a door, and the door is made of the same material as the thing that was killing you.* **Volume 04** asked what a promise is when the people who keep it were never asked, and answered *that a community can put a person in a column at birth exactly as efficiently as an office can, and that this is worse, because an office is at least an office and can be refused.*

**Volume 05's answer, which the volume has to earn and does not get for free: a refusal is a person, and a person can be alive about it.** A community can put a person in a column and call it a habit. **A city can refuse a road and be a person refusing, and the difference is that a person can be asked a second time and can change its mind and can say no to the second asking in different words, and none of those three things can happen to a column.** The whole series is about the difference between an office and a person, and Volume 04 found that a place can be an office without one. **Volume 05 finds the other half: a place can be a person.**

**And the cost of finding it is the volume's title.** A city that has been keeping a dawn for longer than a valley has kept a winter has never told anybody what it costs, and the volume's last discovery is that **the cost is a roster and a shift and a man of fifty-two on the third shift for nineteen years, and that the city has never told anybody because telling anybody is contact, and because the first person who asked has not been told and cannot be, and because asking is what the city has refused for four generations.**

## 2. Central pressure

**Four parties want four different things out of a city that is not asking, and three of the four are right, and the volume's engine is that the man in the middle is the only one who cannot have what he wants and the only one who can pay for it.**

- **The city** wants **not to be found.** It lives under a permanent magical dawn and has chosen isolation rather than risk contact with the Crown, and its reason is four hundred years old and is written in a book that cannot be shown to anybody. **It is not a villain and it is not a symbol and it does not want to be rescued and it does not want to be explained.** When it refuses, it refuses in a form, and the form is entered by a keeper whose whole job is receiving refusals, and nobody in the city reads the book.
- **The main-world traders** want **the standing light.** A dawn kept for four hundred years is a stable anchor material, and there are people in this volume who will pay for a sample of it and who have never met anybody who lives under it. **They are not the Culling Order and they are not an office and they are a trade, and Volume 05 must not make them into a villain, because the volume's finding is worse with them in it than without them: ordinary people wanting a thing that would cost somebody else something they cannot see.** A trader is a man who has been up a pass and comes back with a story and a figure on a page, and the volume already has one of those and he is the reason the bridge said no correctly. **`outline/series.md` puts *Culling agents* in this volume's escalation beside the traders, and `outline/volumes/volume-04-close.md` §7 item 20 rules that Volume 05 is not the volume that puts a member to it, and the ruling is the one this document follows. What arrives instead of a member is the pressure the member works for, and it arrives in a form. See §16 item 1.**
- **The Office of Provincial Registration** wants **a complete register of a thing it has never been told exists**, and **it has a refusal in its ordinary post that it has not read.** It made an offer in a previous volume, in a sheet of good paper, and had it refused in four sheets with the word *eastern* in it, and it does not know that a body corporate in a fen county exists. It is an office doing its job. It is dangerous because it is patient, not because it is angry.
- **Aren Kest** wants **to be recognised as somebody who came to a place that was willing to be found**, and he cannot have it, and **the reason he cannot have it is that the last time a person was recognised as a carrier in a valley the recognition became a column within a generation.** He has been called a word in four different ways in one volume. He has decided he does not want to be called anything, and the city has decided it does not want to be found, and these two decisions agree, **and agreeing with somebody is the worst possible outcome for him, because it means he can stay and do nothing and call it respect.**

**The volume question, again, in its operational form:** *a path is asked whether it consents to a contact, and it says no, and the asking party's own file records the answer as a figure of persons because a refusal that comes back from a body corporate rather than from a person does not fit a column headed Keeper — and what does the main world do with a refusal it cannot file?* **The answer the volume gives is that it files it and calls the filing a count, and the count is the thing that will kill the city without anybody deciding to kill it.** The word *eastern* is in a document in a bag and the office's habit of counting is in the same room, and neither of them knows the other exists.

**What this volume may do inside Stage 3, and it is not an advance.** `bible/power-system.md` gives Stage 3 three abilities: maintain three paths, **trace a path's lineage**, and cut a forced connection that is already threatening a path. **The second of those has never been used on the page in two hundred chapters, and Volume 05 uses it once.** The cost of it, per the same file, is that **each additional cut makes the Gatehouse's preferred history feel physically plausible to him** — and Volume 05 makes no cut, so what it spends instead is the other thing the file names: **a path left open always costs something while it is open. ## 7. The mechanics this volume runs on

1. **A road is a line and two hands, a date, and a witness at each end who is not the person keeping it.** Carried forward whole. **In Volume 05 the line and two hands and a date are made three times and the witness is present three times, and the fourth time there is a witness and no line, and that is the volume's climax.**
2. **A record is a person who is not present.** Carried forward whole. **In Volume 05 this is the volume's engine: the city keeps a book of refusals that nobody has ever been shown, and the Archive keeps a book nobody can be served with, and the two are four hundred miles apart and the same.**
3. **A refusal recorded against a person is a list. A refusal recorded about a class of persons is a document.** Carried forward from Volume 04 and from Volume 02. **In Volume 05 the whole plot turns on what happens when an office that wants a class gets a person: a figure can be filed and a person cannot, and the office's answer to a person is to count.**
4. **A form is a machine for making an office out of a word, and in Volume 05 the machine is offered a subject it wants.** The marginal instruction may be printed, quoted in a return, cited in a file and refused about in a gateway. **It may not be explained in a sentence by anybody, and Sabin Dain may not be the person who explains it.**
5. **A count is a list with the names taken off, and the office has learned to prefer the count.** **Volume 05's consequence, not its origin: the count of a city's refusals is a figure, and a figure is the only kind of paper that cannot be refused, and a valley in the last volume said that about a figure of two thousand six hundred and forty and this volume shows what the figure is for.**
6. **A person can be asked a second time and a column cannot.** **This is the volume's new mechanic and it is the whole of Volume 05's answer.** Nine refusals in four generations is a fact about a city. **One refusal from a child who is nine is a fact about a child. The city has nine of the first kind and cannot produce one of the second, and the main world has a form that can only file the first kind, and the two of those are the same shape and neither of them can see the other.**
7. **A promise that can only be kept by breaking something else is a claim, not a record.** Carried forward. **In Volume 05 the claim is the light: a dawn kept for four hundred years by a roster is a promise being kept by nineteen years of one man's shift, and the volume must not resolve it and must not have anybody ask him to stop.**
8. **What is under a thing is not mended by the man who dug it.** Spent, in Chapter 194, and not restated.

## 8. Escalation sequence — five blocks, Chapters 201–250

**Do not drift further, and do not pretend to.** Volume 01's widest was minus fifteen days at six days of absence. **Volume 02's was sixty-seven at eighty-eight, about three quarters. Volume 03's first crossing was minus eleven at ten days, inside the warning for the first time in this case, and its second was ninety-six at one hundred and seventeen, about four fifths. Volume 04's was a hundred and seventy-one at one hundred and ninety-two, about nine tenths. This one is sixty-nine at ninety, about three quarters.** **The ratio in every one of those is error over absence, never error over the warning and never the path interval over the warning, and when a chapter or a state file says *the ratio* it means that one.** The warning has been three weeks in a Crown office's hand since Chapter 51 and **the Crown cannot say whose figure it is, and no character in this volume describes it as having failed.** A later volume may widen the ratio again. **It may not change the printed figure, and it may not have any character say the word *failed* about a road, and it may not widen the ratio by more than the two halves of one crossing between two volumes without a ledger entry that says so.**
