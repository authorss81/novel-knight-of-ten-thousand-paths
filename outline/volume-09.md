## 1. Volume identity, and the question this volume is built on

**`outline/series.md`'s Volume 9 central pressure is a song, and the close of Volume 08 handed on a rule, and the two are the same object seen from the other end.**

The series says: nine old names in a song. The close says: **a rule which names a keeper is a rule which makes a keeper findable**, and the page's own uncomfortable answer to *what is a rule that lets four thousand people in four counties keep a thing each of them cannot be got at* is a clerk of about sixty writing four lines again in a second hand with today's date against it, which helps a man who has to decide and not a person who is not there.

Put them together and the volume is this: **a name that nobody has to answer to.** The last volume learned that a name on a roll is a handle. This volume finds a kind of record that has no handle, and it is not a document and it is not a hiding place — **it is a thing people say out loud in the course of work, in nine words, to keep a rhythm, and the whole of it is in a tune and none of it is in a column.**

**What Volume 08 asked and answered:** *a rule that names a keeper makes a keeper findable.* **What Volume 09 asks and has to earn:** ***there is a form of the same protection that names nothing anybody can be asked about, and it has been used for a very long time by people with no interest in archives at all, and it is the only one of the nine that has survived intact, and it is the one everybody sings.***

**And the ninth word is a place.** Eight of the nine are practices. The ninth is a name of a road, and a road is not a place and is on no map and has no inhabitants and cannot be written on a roll because there is nobody to enter in the fourth column. **That is the volume in one object, and the volume's job is to take it apart, and the volume's first block takes the first piece off it and the last block is still holding it.**

**What the volume is not.** It is not a volume about a chase and it is not a volume about a vault. **Nobody breaks into anything in this volume until Block 4, and when they do it is four men with a crowbar and a warrant and a door that is not locked, and the door is not locked because the last person who kept it died in a winter and nobody appointed anybody after her.** It is not a volume about a villain in a room. The antagonist is on the page in Blocks 2, 3 and 4 and is not confronted, and the volume's antagonist in Block 1 is **a song that cannot be un-sung.** And it is not a volume in which the protagonist is the person who solves it: **he is a witness, a reader of load paths, and the man who is asked the wrong question in four of the fifty chapters.**

## 2. Central pressure

**Four parties want four different things, three of the four are right, and the two men in the middle both want the same thing and are the reason it does not happen.**

- **A woman of about forty-four who runs a kiln in a yard of four** wants **that her work be counted.** She sings nine lines while a kiln is drawn because that is the beat a draw takes, and the song is not hers and she does not know whose it is, and she has never been asked for it and does not want to be. **Her want is a want about being left alone, and it is the most expensive want in the volume, and it is the one the protagonist costs her.**
- **A man of about forty who has held one of the nine since he was eleven years old** wants **that nobody take it off him**, and it is hereditary because his father held it, and nobody asked him either. **He is good at it. It works. It will end with him.**
- **A keeper of records, twenty-nine, four years and a half, in a room over a weigh-house with a door on a lane** wants **that no copy of anything be in a room she has to answer for**, which is the exact inverse of her office and the exact thing she is being asked to give up. **She is the only character in the volume who is asked to do a thing she is good at by people who need it, and she refuses one part of it, and the refusal is the volume's relationship work and not an aside.**
- **Aren Kest** wants **to be the person who holds the ninth word.** He cannot be, **because a man of thirty-two with a limp is on more lists than any other man in nine counties**, and he knows it, and Chapter 404 is the chapter in which he says so out loud and is believed.

**The volume question, in its operational form:** *nine communities each hold one practice, no community holds the whole of anything, and one of the nine is a name that everybody knows and nobody owns. How is that arrangement kept without a register, and what happens to it in a market on a Saturday.*

**The answer the volume gives is that it cannot be kept, and the thing that does not have to be kept is the one that is not a practice, and Block 1 finds that out the hard way and the last three blocks have to build something that does not depend on the song.**

## 7. The mechanics this volume runs on

1. **A practice outlives a document.** Eight of the nine survive because four generations of people did them, and not one of the eight is written down anywhere anybody can be shown.
2. **A name that is said is not a name that can be asked about.** The ninth word is a road. **The moment it is sung in a market in front of strangers it stops being a road and becomes nine people who heard a road.**
3. **A song is public and cannot be recalled.** This is the volume's engine and its cost and it is not fixed.
4. **A person can be inherited.** The third of the nine goes from a man to his son at eleven because a man died, and the son was not asked, and the practice is good, and it is a practice that dies on one death.
5. **Ordinary craft is where the volume's arguments live.** A kiln draw, a voussoir, an oath-stone, a drain, a bar.
6. **Money is a promise.** Every column is added in pence and footed on the page before the total is said, and the two prices of the same thing are never added in any register.
